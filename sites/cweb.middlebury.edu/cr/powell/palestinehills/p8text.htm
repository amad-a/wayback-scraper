This location is not far from Hebron. There may be no one who still calls this place Yanun, but that is the name that I found for this tiny cross-roads on the old British maps. The figure on the road is a schoolgirl with a briefcase.

This little land at the crossroads has always been vulnerable to interference and worse by larger nations. Its population has long been divided into feuding factions that have shared the land for millennia. Instead of dealing justly with each other, setting the past aside, and working together, the people of this land turn to foreign allies to help them defeat their own neighbors. It is usually to the advantage of foreign powers to take sides in this conflict, for a united Levant would be an extraordlinarily vigorous economic competitor. The conflict is also dear to the hearts of religious and political extremists, because their influence would inevitably decline in a nation united, peaceful, and prosperous.
