JANUARY

 

1st        Fatahs anniversary today.1

 

Occupied West Bank

o       Palestinian security officer, Muataz Muhammed Said Saruji, age 37, is shot dead near Tulkarm by Israeli security forces.

o       Palestinian officer, Tareq Ibrahim Ahmad Al-Katu, age 31, is also shot dead here by Israeli forces.2

 

Masked mourners carrying guns march in the funeral of Thabet Thabet, Fatah activist and dentist who was murdered by Israeli undercover soldiers yesterday. The mourners vow revenge.3 

 

Jadallah al-Jabari, a retired Palestinian dustman in his fifties, is shot at an Israeli checkpoint in occupied Hebron. The shooting of Jabari, as he walked unarmed in occupied Hebron during a curfew that has been in place exclusively for Palestinians since the Intifada began, is caught on camera by an AP cameraman, a local Arab, who did not want his name mentioned.  The televised incident shows Jabari talking to an Israeli soldier at a concrete barrier. The film pauses, then resumes and shows Jabari in pain lying on the ground, clutching his leg. The footage also shows Jabaris foot almost severed with exposed bone and tissue.4  The Israeli soldiers try to cover the camera lens and demand to see the cameramans papers.5  AP says that its cameraman did not see the actual shooting but that only a minute elapsed before he heard the shots. Jabari is treated in Israeli Hadassah Hospital for severe leg injuries, and his leg has to be amputated.6  Jabaris brother says that Jabari, who had a permit to walk around in occupied Hebron, was on familiar terms with the Israeli soldiers who man checkpoints in the city. The IDF has launched an investigation into the incident. At this point, the IDF says that the Israeli soldiers at the barricades that day were new to occupied Hebron and fired as a result of misjudgement and were ignorant of the tensions that exist there.7

 

Arafat tells Palestinians to keep resisting Zionist aggression.8

 

During a 45 minute phone call, Arafat and Clinton decide to meet.9

 

At around 7pm, a car bomb explodes in a parked car on Herzl Street in Netanya inside Israel shattering windows and vehicles.10 The bombing comes soon after the announcement that Arafat will go to the US for talks.  According to Israeli radio, 54 people receive treatment for the bombing. Various reports say anywhere from 11 to 50 people were injured in the bombing.11  While there are no claims of responsibility for the bombing, Israeli Police Commissioner, Shlomo Aharonishki, says that the one man who is uncritically wounded in the bomb is allegedly the bomber. Deputy Prime Minister, Benyamin Ben Eliezer, says a retaliation to the bombing should be vigorous and immediate, and that all negotiations with the Palestinians should cease until they stop attacking Israelis.12  Deputy Defense Minister, Ephraim Sneh, says Israel will do its best to hit anyone involved in such incidences.13  Barak uses the bombing as an example of what he says is Arafats encouragement of anti-Israeli violence.14  

 

Israel closes Gaza Strip International airport. (As part of its agreement with the PA, Arafat is still allowed to fly in and out of the airport). It also seals occupied Gaza Strip entirely. Only humanitarian aid is being let through Karni Crossing.15  All Palestinian areas are blockaded, and Israel withdraws a number of VIP cards from PA officials.16

 

Barak says he is thinking of a unilateral separation from the Palestinians. This means that Israel would decide the borders between Israel and Palestine, and then enforce them.17

 

2nd                Occupied Dugit, Gaza StripIsraeli forces use live bullets to shoot dead Sabri Awad Ibrahim Khader, age 50, near an army post.1

 

Occupied Gaza StripThe IDF murders a 70 year old Palestinian farmer as he tends his fields.2

 

A bomb explosion in occupied Gaza Strip wounds an Israeli soldier.3

 

While Arafat meets Clinton in Washington DC today for two meetings, Palestinian negotiators circulate criticisms of Clintons plan.  Palestinian officials say that during the meeting, Arafat went over the issues of Palestinian refugees and Jerusalem.4  Meanwhile, Barak tells army radio, Since Arafat has wasted most of this time in dragging his feetwe have very serious doubts about the seriousness of his intentions to reach an arrangement.5 

           

Barak tells the IDF to prepare for a possible war if the current efforts at a peace agreement fail.6

 

3rd        Arafat gives a preliminary conditional acceptance to the US proposal, which a White House spokesperson says, was clarified. Arafat also agrees that he will try to restrain Palestinians and will cooperate in counter-terrorism efforts.1  Clinton calls for 10-12 days of negotiations to work out the details.2  Barak, now taking a hard-line in view of his upcoming elections, says that he does not intend to go to DC for talks because he does not think that a preliminary acceptance is a breakthrough.  Barak further says that his attention has now shifted from the proposal to Israeli security.3 

In the coming weeks, we will not focus on the negotiations with the Palestinians, which in my assessment have no real chance of producing an agreement in the next few weeks, but on containing terrorismIt is our obligation to provide security for Israeli citizens.4 

Israeli Justice Minister, Yossi Beilin, says that Arafats preliminary acceptance is a positive development, and says that the main question now is whether Arafat will stop the violence.5

 

Fatah military leader, Hussein al-Sheikh, says that Palestinians will continue the Intifada even if peace talks happen.6

 

Natan Sharansky, who quit Baraks cabinet when the latter began negotiating with the Palestinians, says that Israel is negotiating under fire to try and reach an agreement with Arafat before the end of Clintons term. Israel gives more and more, getting more terror and more hatred in return.7

 

Barak speaks with Clinton on the phone and reverses his decision made 24 hours earlier to end all negotiations with the Palestinians.8

 

Founder and dean of Simon Wiesenthal Center in Los Angeles, Rabbi Marvin Hier praises Clintons efforts, but asks him not to give control over Haram al-Sharif to Palestinians. The group, which boasts 400,000 members, said that returning control of the Haram to Palestinians would put Israel in a corner at a time when it was coming under terrorist attacks.9

 

Lebanese Foreign Minister, Mahmoud Hammoud calls the right to return for Palestinian refugees, sacred.10

 

4th            Regardless of the peace talks underway, Palestinians dont show any signs of ending the Intifada.1   Israel says that there must be an end to the violence before talks resume. Albright asks Arafat to keep his promise to restrain Palestinians.2

 

Arafats aides say that he accepted the US proposal with so many reservations that it is tantamount to rejection.3

 

Baraks Chief of Staff, Gilead Sher, speaks with US envoys, Dennis Ross and Aaron Miller, about Arafats conditional acceptance of the US proposal.4

 

Arafat is expected to deliver a final answer to the US proposal after a meeting of Arab Foreign Ministers in Cairo today.5  Arafat briefs Egyptian President, Hosni Mubarak, on his negotiations with the US, then goes to the Arab League Headquarters for discussions with Arab Foreign Ministers who have serious objections over giving up the right of return for Palestinian refugees. Syrian Foreign Minister, Farouk al-Sharaa, begins the summit by saying that the US proposal should not even be discussed. He further insists that the Arab nations should instead concentrate on supporting the Palestinian Intifada.6

 

PAs ambassador to DC, Hassan Abdel-Rahman, states the conditions for acceptance of any proposal:

·        the right to return for all Palestinian refugees to their homes inside the Green Line. Israel completely denies this right to Palestinians.

·        removal of all Israeli forces from the West Bank and Gaza Strip. Israel says that it will keep a military presence in the Jordan Valley as well as annex large areas of land with illegal Jewish settlements.

·        the return of sovereignty over the Western Wall. Israel says that it will not return sovereignty to Palestinians.7 

As Clinton had not presented any maps along with his proposal, Palestinian negotiators have made their own based on Clintons proposal and what Israel says it will return.  Palestinian negotiators say, the maps show that the proposal together with the Israeli return of land, would create a Palestinian state on islands of land in which holy sites and Arab neighbourhoods in Jerusalem would be cut of from one another while the Israeli state would be contiguous. The West Bank would become permanently divided into three parts. Palestinian negotiators also object to Clintons complete compliance with the Israeli view. The maps have been sent to Clinton.8

 

At an election rally, Barak reiterates Israels refusal to comply with the internationally guaranteed right to return for Palestinian refugees. He also says Israel will not return sovereignty of the holy sites to the Palestinians.9  

 

Israeli Foreign Minister, Shlomo Ben-Ami, says that the right to return is outside the parameter of the current US plan.10

 

5th            Occupied Erez, Gaza StripIsraeli forces shoot dead Mahmud Mahmud Abu Hasira, age 37, with live bullets at Erez checkpoint.1

 

Occupied Gaza StripA Palestinian man is shot dead by the IDF after he climbs a fence near an Israeli post. The IDF says that he was shouting Islamic religious slogans, which meant that he would attack them. The mans family says that he was mentally handicapped.2

 

Occupied Hebron, West BankArij Jabili, an 18 year old Palestinian, is murdered today. She was shot dead after bullets came through the walls of her home located near the illegal Jewish settlement of Beit Hagai.3

 

Israel responds to the US proposal in a 6 page report that angers Palestinian negotiators. Israeli newspaper, Yedioth Ahronoth, says that Israel is demanding sovereignty over all of Jerusalems Old City as well as the Mount of Olives, the Arab neighbourhood of Silwan, and a large part of the Western Wall. Israel wants the Haram under third party control.4

 

6th            Occupied Hebron, West Bank The funeral of Arij Jabili shot dead yesterday is held today. Several thousand Palestinians gather in Hebron to march behind her coffin draped with Palestinian and a Hamas flags. The mourners chant, We will revenge the blood of our martyrs! Revenge. Revenge.1

 

Occupied Gaza StripThe funeral for a Palestinian man who was shot dead yesterday after he climbed a fence near an Israeli military post, is held today.2

 

Palestinian negotiator, Yasser Abed Rabbo, issues a statement on the US proposal saying that the Palestinians are not interested in signing any document for the sake of rendering a service to Baraks re-election campaign. He accuses the US of trying to push the Palestinians into a flawed agreement to save Baraks political career and declares the proposal cancelled.3

 

Fatah delivers a critique and rejection of US proposal in the Arab newspaper al-Hayat al-Jadida citing 44 reasons as to why the proposal must be rejected. Among these reasons is that the proposal completely violates UN resolutions 242, 338, and 194 on which any sort of peace should be built.4

 

The National and Islamic Forces, a coalition of various groups, restates its firm rejection of the US proposals. Palestinians march in opposition to the proposals in Tulkarm, Ramallah, and Nablus.5

 

7th            Occupied Gaza Strip38 year old Abd-al Hamid Ahman Musabeh Al-Khurti is shot dead by live bullets to his head by Israeli forces on the Karni-Netzarim road. Khurti was from occupied Gaza Strip.1

 

Occupied Nablus, West Bank20-22 year old Palestinian is murdered by the IDF with live bullets as she drives her car from occupied Nablus to her home village. The IDF says that she was caught in the crossfire. The victims family says there was no provocation.2

 

Occupied West BankIsraeli Colonel, Noam Tibon, confirms that a Palestinian man was shot while handcuffed by Israeli forces near the town of Halhoul. The IDF says that he was suspected in shootings and was shot because he tried to run.3

 

10 year old Palestinian is critically wounded by the IDF in occupied Hebron.4

 

CIA director George Tenet flies to Cairo today to meet with Palestinian and Israeli security officials.5  He delivers a bridging proposal to both sides to help them restart talks.6  Palestinians demand an end to the siege in return for cooperation, but so far, the Israelis refuse. According to Palestinian officials, there is no outcome from the meetings.7

 

Londons chapter of Amnesty International announces today that it is sending a delegation to the region to investigate Israels use of force against Palestinians. The group says that it will concentrate on examining the use of extra-judicial execution. Israeli Defense Minister, Ephraim Sneh, defends Israels role in the assassinations calling it effective, precise, and just. 8

 

Arafat plans to meet with King Abdullah II today.9

 

8th            Occupied West BankIsraeli forces murder Muahmmad Ahmad Abu Suf, age 27, with live shots to his chest on Road No. 5. Suf was from Kharas, Salfit in Gaza Strip.1

 

?            Occupied Gaza StripAccording to Palestinian officials, a 34 year old Palestinian was murdered after Israeli soldiers opened fire on a village neighbouring Netzarim Junction before they carried out arrests there. The IDF says it is checking the report.2

 

PA delivers a statement saying that the proposals are contrary to every Palestinian aspiration.3  Israel blames the Palestinians for the failure of the US proposal. An Israeli government spokesperson says that the proposal was the best offer they are ever likely to receive.4

 

A few hours later, thousands of mostly young, religious, right-wing Israelis march around the Old City in Jerusalem with torches to protest the possibility of returning control of the site to Palestinians.5  Thousands of Israeli police fan out around the Old City to keep order and try to keep the Palestinians near Damascus Gate separated from the Israelis brandishing Star of David flags.6 Full page advertisements, by 32 Jewish organizations, run in US newspapers protesting this point of the US proposal. Letter writing campaigns, vigils, and hunger strikes are planned.7

 

 

9th            Occupied Gaza StripIsraeli forces murder Ibrahim Hasan Abu Mghasib, age 70, with bullets to his abdomen in Kfar Darom. Mghasib was from Dier al-Balah, Gaza Strip.1

 

Occupied Jenin, West Bank27 year old Abd Al-Hamid abd a-Rashim Khanfar, from Jenin, is shot dead with live gunfire to his head by Israeli forces.2

                           

10th      Sharon officially launches his election campaign today. He tells the ultra-orthodox journal Kfar Habad, The Oslo Accord is an agreement which no longer exists. Period.  Sharons platform is as follows:

·        Israeli sovereignty over an undivided Jerusalem

·        No Palestinian sovereignty over Haram al-Sharif

·        No return of the Golan Heights to Syria1

           

11th      The PA and Israel implement the proposal given by CIA director, George Tenet, to both sides on 7th January. As part of the US plan to re-start talks, Israel relaxes its blockade on Palestinian towns, Qalqilya and Jenin, in occupied West Bank and opens Gaza Strip International airport. It also allows travel from occupied West Bank into Jordan and from occupied Gaza Strip into Egypt. In occupied Gaza Strip, IDF bulldozers remove concrete blockades from Netzarim Junction. According to Palestinian security chief, General Abdel Razek Majaida, Israel will end travel restrictions in the next few days under the plan and will pull back from flashpoints. According to the plan, Palestinians and Israelis will draft treaties. US envoy, Dennis Ross, will then come to the region and try to mediate between the two sides.1

 

Sharon says that, if elected, he will not dismantle any of the illegal settlements now dotting occupied Palestine.2

 

The current death toll stands at 313 Palestinians from the Occupied Palestinian Territories, 13 Israeli-Arabs, 37 Israeli Jews, 1 Jordanian citizen killed by Israeli fire, and 1 German citizen also killed by Israeli fire.3

 

12th            Occupied Hebron, West BankIsraeli soldiers murder Palestinian, Shaker Hasona al-Husseini, age 21, with live shots in Ash Sheikh neighbourhood in the H1 area of Hebron.  The IDF claims that he was a gunman. Witnesses say illegal Jewish settlers handed out sweets to soldiers after the murder.1

 

Palestinian and Israeli negotiators meet in occupied Gaza Strip. While the meeting has no outcome, they commit to keep talking.2

 

Arafat confirms the executions of two Palestinian collaborators to be shot tomorrow.3

 

13th      2 Palestinian collaborators who were involved in the killings of Palestinian activists and were tried in Palestinian special security courts, are executed today:

Occupied Nablus, West Bank

o       24 year old, Alan Bani-Odeh, is blindfolded, tied to a post, and publicly shot to death after being found guilty by a Nablus special security court.  Odeh was convicted on charges of being involved in the assassination Hamas activist, Ibrahim Abd Qarim Bani Odeh, age 33, on 23rd November.

Occupied Gaza Strip

o       27 year old Palestinian, Majdi Mikawi, is also publicly executed after being tied to a post and blindfolded. He was tried and found guilty of collaborating with Israelis by a Palestinian special security court.

Director of Palestinian Human Rights Group, Bassem Eid, says that the group favours punishing collaborators but, he says, they should have their right to a fair trial.1

 

Occupied Bethlehem, West BankAli al-Khatib and Husa al-Din Musa are sentenced to executions on charges of collaboration. Their sentences have yet to be confirmed by Arafat. Arafats Justice Minister Freihh Abu-Medain estimates that there are 20,000 collaborators and asks them to surrender.2

 

Arafat and former Israeli Prime Minister, Shimon Peres are to meet in two days to decide on common principles from which they can restart talks.3

 

Palestinian and Israeli negotiators meet for more talks today.4

 

14th            Occupied Gaza StripThe body of Roni Tzalach, an Israeli settler from the illegal settlement of Kfar Yam is found today with a bullet in the head with his own gun.1 According to Israeli radio, Hamas claimed responsibility for the killing. Following the incident, Israeli tanks rolled into Palestinian controlled areas, blockade occupied Gaza Strip separating it into 3-4 isolated sections, and fire tank shells, one of which lands only a mile away from Arafats office. Israeli gunboats patrol offshore.  In response to Israels collective punishment police, Palestinian negotiators cancel a meeting with Israeli negotiators scheduled for today.2

 

15th            Occupied Nablus, West Bank24 year old, Majhdii Ahed Abdullah Shtayeh, is murdered by Israeli forces with live ammunition to his chest in Salem. He was from the same area.1 

 

Fanatic Jewish settlers set Palestinian homes and crops afire after Israeli forces sealed occupied Gaza Strip caging Palestinians. Barak accused Arafat of starting the violence thus, in a remarkable sleight of hand, blaming him for the violence unleashed by racist Israeli settlers. He further described the killing of Israeli settler, Roni Tzalach, yesterday as a terrible blow for the peace process.2  Israeli Deputy Defense Minister, Ephraim Sneh, said that Palestinian security forces and the PA had no knowledge of the killing and cooperated in searching for the body.3  Still, Gaza Strip airport remains closed by Israel as does the border between occupied Gaza Strip and Egypt, and Palestinian workers are again barred from entering Israel.4

 

Parents Circle/ Family Forum, a peace group that has been working a tent in Tel Aviv square, where Yitzhak Rabin was assassinated, to try and raise the consciousness of Israelis in regards to the Palestinians, ends its mission today. 51 year old graphic artist, Rami Elhanan, whose 14 year old daughter was murdered in Jerusalem by a suicide bomber, is part of the group and says that the task has been hopeless.  Our people died because there is no peaceWe have been begging Israel to make peace, because the alternative is unbearable. We did all we can as a group, but the situation is clear. People dont really want peace Elhanan is also worried about the current election between Sharon and Barak, which, he knows, is more complicated than the hawk versus dove dichotomy that is being presented in the Western press. Barak, he says, has made

all the mistakes in the worldYou cannot go on negotiating with the Palestinians on the one hand, and building settlements on the other.You cannot try to gain the confidence of the Palestinians, and then betray them. And you cannot patronize the man you are negotiating with.5

 

Israel postpones talks scheduled for today between Arafat and former Israeli Prime Minister, Shimon Peres.6

 

17th      Three mask men murder Palestinian TV chief, Hisham Miki, age 54, by shooting him repeatedly at a seaside restaurant. The motive of the killing is not clear, and Palestinian officials privately say, that it does not look as though Israel is involved. Hisham, a rich businessman, was a close friend of Arafats who was rumoured to be involved in corruption, and was generally disliked. He led a staff of a dozen people and was the founder of a major broadcasting organization in Palestine. The Israeli government often accused his station of inciting Palestinians against Israel.1

 

Occupied Ramallah, West Bank16 year old Israeli, Ophir Rahum, is shot dead in al-Bira. He was from Ashkelon.2

 

20th      Clinton ends his second and final term as US President.
