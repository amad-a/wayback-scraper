The Infinite Violation of Human Rights

The Palestinian prisoners' cause has become a major part of the daily life of the Palestinian people. It has its distinguished features expressed in its struggle within prison walls for proper human conditions and freedom. The Palestinian and Arab prisoners inside Israeli prisons were able to survive harsh conditions of detention and produce creative cultural, artistic and journalistic works. Prisons have become small factories of producing ornamental and beautiful handicrafts.

After more than six years of signing the Israeli-Palestinian agreements, more than 1500 prisoners are still behind bars regardless of the fact that they were supposed to be released in compliance with singed agreements.

Palestinian detainees face hard living conditions which are in direct violation of international laws and covenants, including torture in interrogation rooms, transporting prisoners to prisons far from their homes or being transferred from one prison to another, crowded conditions within prisons, solitary confinement, prohibition of family visits, administrative detention, poor quality and shortage of rations, medical negligence and delaying surgeries. In its follow-up of prisoners and prisons' conditions ADDAMEER conducted 149 visits to Palestinian and Arab prisoners in detention in 1999.

These organized visits to prisons enabled ADDAMEER to follow up the conditions of the prisoners through interrogation, the way they are treated and the torture committed against them. The visits also kept the Association in contact with new developments inside prisons and kept prisoners in touch with the world outside the prison walls and contributed in solving many difficult issues.

The number of visits to interrogation centers reached 87 visits whereas the regular visits totaled a number of 62 visits. The number of detainees visited and interviewed by the Association in prisons and interrogation centers was 537 detainees.

In the course of our visits to all of the Israeli prisons, we have been able to assess the conditions of arrest and incarceration of Palestinian and Arab prisoners, which we see that they fall under the following four categories:

  • Administrative detainees and the extension of their terms of detention, which is a violation of their rights, by the Israeli Security Force, and matters related to the policy of arbitrary extension. Administrative Detention.

  • Health conditions facing Palestinian and Arab prisoners in Israeli prisons. Prisoners' Health Conditions.

  • Torture by the Israeli authorities and the accompanying harsh methods of interrogation used against Palestinians. Torture.

I. Humiliating and provocative inspection and violent attacks

The prisons' department has been imposing more restrictions on the detainees by carrying out wide search campaigns inside the prison cells in all prisons. It has used special units, such as "Nahshun Unit", equipped with special devices and dogs in some of these search campaigns. All search campaigns are justified by security reasons but the actual reasons are usually something else.

The most prominent of these campaigns are:

  • On January 28 1999 "Nahshun special Unit" broke into section "B" at Asqalan prison and carried out a very precise inspection of the prison cells. The search unit damaged the properties and belongings of the prisoners and confiscated other belongings that the prisoners are allowed to keep.

  • An inspection of Nafha prison on February 3 1999 by special forces that broke into the prison and held a strict inspection campaign and damaged and destroyed prisoners' belongings. As a result of that a confrontation took place between the prison's administration and the prisoners, the following detainees were wounded: Nihad Jundiyi and Nidal Matar.

  • An inspection of Bir Alsabi'a prison on February 14 1999 during which prisoners were forced to take off their clothes and were beaten. Four detainees were put in solitary confinement; they were Mukhlis Burgal, Hafith Qandas, Muhammad Dukhan and Sultan Ilajluni.

  • Al Damon prison administration carried out daily search campaigns, including Saturday, the Jewish Sabbath, for a whole week beginning on March 7 1999, sometimes more than once a day.

  • An inspection of Bir Alsabi'a prison on April 28 1999 where all suitcases were taken for electronic inspection.

  • An inspection of Bir Alsabi'a prison on May 9 1999. The cells were broken into at night and all prisoners were handcuffed while asleep. The inspectors tried to forcefully strip the detainees while they were still tied.

  • A general inspection was carried out in Asqalan prison on June 4 1999 followed by punishing some prisoners by preventing them from visits for two months and reducing the recess time from four hours to one hour.

  • An inspection of Asqalan prison on September 28 1999. The inspection was justified by the presence of wireless communication devices. The inspection was followed by several punishments.

  • A precise inspection of Asqalan prison on October 17 1999 during which prisoners' belongings were damaged. One section, where a police officer was stabbed, was punished by 3 months of no visits and 3 months of prohibiting the use of electrical devices and a reduction of recess time from four hours to one hour, while handcuffed. Fourteen prisoners, who were being held in the place where the stabbing happened, were held in solitary confinement.

It is obvious that the aims of the inspection campaigns are to spread instability amongst prisoners, provoke them and disrupt their life. It is an intentionally humiliating policy when detainees are forced to strip off during inspection. If a prisoner refuses to follow orders during inspection, he will be beaten brutally, placed in solitary confinement, deprived from visits or recess or shopping from the prison's canteen, as in the case of the following detainees:

  • Ali Abu Ilrub was beaten during inspection on December 3 1999 when he refused to be stripped during inspection.

  • A group of detainees at Asqalan prison were beaten because they refused to take off their clothes during inspecting them before being transferred to Nafha prison on November 30 1999. One of the detainees received a chest wound and another was wounded in his foot.

  • Taha Ilshakhshir was beaten at Asqalan prison on November 25 1999 because he refused the humiliating method of searching him while he was naked. Taha was beaten and sprayed with gas and was taken to solitary confinement in Bir Ilsabi'a prison.

  • Mahmoud Jabarin was badly beaten during an inspection campaign at Asqalan prison on September 28 1999.

  • A group of detainees were beaten in their cell where a police officer was stabbed during a prisoner count on October 17 1999. The prisoner who actually did the stabbing was badly beaten.

  • Nihad Jundiyi and Nidal Matar were beaten during an inspection of Nafha prison in February 1999.


II. Other procedures of punishing prisoners

In the last quarter of 1999 the prisons' administrations intensified the procedures of punishing prisoners, including:

  • Preventing the children of the prisoners and their relatives' children from direct contact with the prisoners (from going beyond the fences) as it used to be, justifying this for security reasons.

  • The prisons' administration issued a direct order in which prisoners must take off their clothes during pre-visit searches, and transfer searches. This order also applies to families during visits, including children. These procedures caused tension within the prisons and forced prisoners to take confrontational measures, including:

    • Refusing family visits as long as the procedures are effective.

    • Intermittent hunger strikes.

    • Refusing going to trials.

    • Refusing being taken to other prisons, in particular ill detainees who are usually taken to Al Ramla Prison Hospital.

Moreover, after each search a prison administration would carry out a series of punishments against the detainees, such as:

  1. Confiscating electrical devices and some kitchenware.

  2. Limiting the recess time to one hour instead of five hours.

  3. Canceling visits between sections in some prisons such as Nafha and Asqalan.

  4. Depriving some detainees from their families' visits.

  5. Holding some detainees in solitary confinement for varying periods of time.

  6. Transporting detainees from one prison to another.

  7. Preventing prisoners from buying things from the prison's canteen.

  8. Canceling the transmission of some satellite television channels such as Al Jazira, which transmitted some programs about detainees. Stopping some local newspapers such as Al Ayyam and Alhayat Aljadida from reaching prisoners. This contradicts Article (39) of the regulations of prisoners' treatment under the heading "contacting the outside world":

    "prisoners must have the chance to keep in touch with important events through reading daily newspapers and other publications issued by the prison's administration or to watch television stations or to attend lectures or any other means allowed and supervised by the administration."


III. Crowded conditions inside prisons

Although there has been a decrease in the numbers of detainees inside Israeli prisons, the policy of packing a large number of detainees in a small area continues. The objective is to burden the prisoners in order to make them suffer crowded conditions inside the cells and the recess quarters. Many prisoners are taken from one prison to another, justified by carrying out maintenance operations in some sections such as Nafha, Bir Alsabi'a prisons.

Furthermore, the recess quarters of many prisons are not sufficient to accommodate the numbers of detainees, such as the recess quarter of Telmond prison, which is very low and has an area of 2.5 X 7 meters. The prisoners demanded the administration to enlarge it but their request was rejected.

The prisoners of Asqalan prison suffer similar crowded conditions. They demanded that the administration solve the problem by giving each prisoner 5 meters of area. The prisoners of Megiddo prison, where 20 prisoners are allocated to each tent, requested the prison's administration to reduce the number of detainees in each area, especially in Winter, but their request was rejected.

A detailed description of Nafha desert prison

  • The old prison has four sections:

      Section (1) consists of seven rooms. Each room has 4 beds and each two beds are on top of each other so that each room has eight prisoners. The section has 56 prisoners in an area of 26 meters.
      Section (2) has 8 rooms that has 8 prisoners each totaling 64 prisoners.
      Both sections have a recess quarter of 132 meters area.
      Section (3) consists of 8 rooms that has 10 prisoners each totaling 80 prisoners in an area of 27.5 meters.
      Section (4) consists of 8 rooms that has 10 prisoners each totaling 80 prisoners in an area of 27.5 meters per room.
      Both sections have a recess quarter of 161 meters area.

  • The new prison has three section

      Each section has 12 rooms and each room accommodates 10 prisoners in an area of 21 meters. Each section has a recess quarter of 160 meters area.

  • The solitary confinement section

      This section has four cells without internal lavatories. The prisoners use an external lavatory in the morning. During recess, each prisoner goes out alone in an area of 30 meters, which was originally a kitchen.
This contradicts Article (9) of the regulation of prisoners' treatment, lower division, under the heading "detention areas":

"wherever there are cells or single bedrooms no more than one prisoner should be allocated at night. In exceptional cases, when the central prison's administration has to skip this rule, placing two prisoners in a cell or single bedroom should be avoided."
"All rooms must be equipped with all necessary equipment, taking into consideration the climate, especially the minimal area allocated for every prisoner, lighting, heating and airing."


IV. Providing food, whose responsibility is it?

Article (20) of the regulation of prisoners' treatment, lower division, under the heading "food" says that:

"the administration is to provide each prisoner, during the regular hours, with a sufficient, good quality, well prepared nutritious meal that would keep the prisoners healthy and strong."

Detainees inside Israeli prisons always suffer from a lack of usual good quality food. The problem is that the meals served are not enough and even if they were enough, the preparation of the food is bad. There is always a problem with food during the entire period of imprisonment.

The prison's administration is responsible for feeding prisoners. The prisoners are equal in the right to receive meals. In the past years, Israel has been encouraging receiving and buying food. For instance, each prisoner can buy 1300 grams of meat per month.

The reality shows that there is a difference in the purchasing abilities of the prisoners. Moreover, some prisoners are from outside the country, henceforth, and since prisoners live together but have different purchasing abilities, the Israeli authorities' behavior is immoral since they have the responsibility of providing sufficient and good quality food for the prisoners. It has become clear that it is the prisoners' responsibility to provide more than half of the necessary food, which is a very difficult matter since most prisoners come from poor families.


V. Depriving prisoners' telephone calls

All prisons in the world that have the minimal human living conditions give prisoners the right to make phone calls to their families and lawyers. In Israel, a common criminal has the right to make such phone calls, but Palestinian or Arab security prisoners are deprived from such a right for security reasons. Even in humanitarian situations such as bereavement, prisoners are deprived from the right to pay their respects by phone. Prisoners have demanded this right many times but their requests were rejected.


VI. Transfer and political separation

The policy of the prisons' administration is to provoke instability, disrupt prisoners' lives, deprive them from cultural and educational environments and weaken them all the time. Their method in carrying out this policy is through transferring prisoners to different prisons or different sections within the same prison. The following transfer procedures took place in 1999:

  • In March 1999 fifteen prisoners were transferred from Asqalan to Nafha prison. The prisoners refused to enter Nafha prison so the administration put them in solitary confinement for two months then they were taken to Bir Alsabi' a prison.

  • In April 1999 seventy five prisoners were taken from Asqalan prison to Nafha, Bir Alsabi'a and Al Damon prisons.

  • In August 1999 one hundred and twenty prisoners were transferred from Shata prison to Asqalan and Bir Alsabi'a prisons. Shata was closed for maintenance.

  • At the end of September 1999 forty prisoners were taken from Al Damun prison to Nafha. Al Damun was shut down completely.

  • Fifty prisoners were taken from Asqalan to Nafha at the end of October 1999.

  • Eighty prisoners were taken from Nafha, Asqalan and Bir Alsabi'a prisons to a new prison called Hadarim in November and December 1999. The prisoners are held in solitary confinement in Hadarim prison. 140 prisoners from Jerusalem and the Green Line were taken to Shata when it was reopened. The Islamic movement prisoners were taken from Asqalan to Nafha, whereas, imprisoned PLO supporters were taken to Asqalan prison.

Moreover, new dangerous procedures taken in Israeli prisons were presented in classifying prisoners according to their political belief or their legal and political situation in the eyes of the Israeli authorities. In November 1999 the prisons' administration removed the Islamic movement prisoners to Nafha and Hadarim and removed imprisoned PLO supporters to Asqalan. In a step to isolate them from the rest of the Palestinian prisoners, Jerusalem and the Green Line prisoners were taken to Shata prison.

(These prisoners are from the so-called Israeli Arabs, Palestinians who live in the land occupied in 1948, and who are now considered Israeli citizens.)

The prisoners refused these procedures, which aims at dividing them and considering Jerusalem prisoners as Israeli prisoners or considering Hamas prisoners different from the supporters of the PLO. The prisoners also requested the prisons' administration to terminate the policy of dividing prisoners according to their political beliefs, which divides and weakens the prisoners.


VII. The problem of education

Article (77) of prisoners' treatment regulations, lower division, stipulates that:

"procedures are to be taken to allow all capable prisoners to continue their education, including religious education, wherever possible. Illiterate and young people's education must be compulsory and the administration should pay special care to that."

The prisoners have a problem of the prisons' administration refusal to register prisoners at Arab universities. Many attempts were made by prisoners and associations to exert pressure on the prisons' department to allow prisoners education at Arab universities, as Palestinian universities are well-prepared to take prisoner applicants, but the prisons' department rejected this for security reasons.

There are more than 100 prisoners registered at Israeli universities and there is a larger number of prisoners who try to register at universities but cannot for financial reasons, as well as the problem of language. The prisoners at Megiddo are deprived of registering at Arab or Israeli universities because no prisoner in a prison, which is under Israeli army control, is allowed to register at a university. Many prisoners at Megiddo demanded being transferred to a prison under the control of the prisons' department so that they can receive education but their requests were rejected.

Young prisoners suffer many problems related to education such as few hours of education and the fact that the curriculum taught at prisons is Israeli. The prisoners protested against this and demanded a Palestinian curriculum since it is easier and more appropriate.
