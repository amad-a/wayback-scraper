Detainees from Jerusalem and the Green line

Jerusalem prisoners suffer a situation that reflects Israeli policy. Jerusalem was annexed to Israel in1967; its inhabitants received identity cards, which give them "a leave to remain for an indefinite period".Jerusalem is an occupied city; its residents are Palestinians who fought against occupation legitimately and were subjected to the occupiers' regulations, including detention of children, women and the elderly. The sentences given to detainees of Jerusalem were characterized by being tough; they were also tried at military courts according to emergency regulations.

Treatment of the prisoners from Jerusalem is double-faced and contradictory. The prisoners of Jerusalem are Israeli citizens in the eyes of the Israeli authorities when it comes to prisoners' release based on peace agreements, whereas, in other cases, they are treated like any other Arab or Palestinian prisoner and they do not receive the minimal rights inside prisons.

This double-faced treatment has deprived detainees from Jerusalem of being part of the recent prisoners' release. There are approximately 200 Palestinian security detainees from Jerusalem, many of whom are serving long sentences. Their families have been organizing protests against this unfair and unjust policy.

Israel, which is very strict when it comes to releasing Jerusalem prisoners since it claims that they are Israeli citizens, released prisoners from Jerusalem in 1985 in a deal with the Palestinians. It has recently released 7 detainees from Jerusalem, which contradicts its legal claims.

Israel has attempted on many occasions to classify Jerusalem prisoners as civil prisoners. Therefore, their interrogation has taken place in a section allocated for minorities within the State of Israel; interrogation was not carried out by the Israeli intelligence services, as is the case with the rest of the prisoners. In this case they would be with common criminals, a situation rejected by the prisoners of Jerusalem.

The prison administration at Telmond placed 13 children from Jerusalem in isolation from the prisoners of the West Bank and held them with common criminals for more than two months at the beginning of 1999. The prisoners protested this policy and requested a return to the children's section, which was finally granted after much effort.
