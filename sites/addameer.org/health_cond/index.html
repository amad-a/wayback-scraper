Prisoners' Health Conditions

There are approximately three hundred cases of detainees who suffer from poor health conditions in Israeli prisons. Detainees who suffer from chronic diseases are neglected and delayed treatment from the prisons' authorities, which has led to the death of 29 prisoners in the past years. This year, detainee Shadi Saqir Abu Dahruj, 21, from Jabalia died in the cells of Bir Isabi'a prison on May 17 1999 as a result of negligence of his health condition. Dahruj was arrested on January 5 1999.

ADDAMEER has followed up many cases of prisoners who suffer from poor health conditions this year. It visited them and managed to break the confidentiality of medical reports for the sake of medical committees and human rights committees that inspect the medical reports of the detainees and supervise the treatment they receive from the prisons' authorities. If the Association discovers any medical negligence, it will take the necessary steps by sending letters and filing complaints so that it may exert pressure on the prisons' authorities.


Health Follow-Up

Through its visits, ADDAMEER has learnt that the detainees suffer from many health problems, including:

  • Medical centers at prisons are small clinics with very limited facilities. They have a nurse and a doctor who comes once or twice a week. This contradicts Article (22) of the lower division of the regulations of prisoners' treatment under the heading "medical services" which states:

    "every prison must have services provided by one qualified doctor at least. The doctor should have some knowledge of psychotherapy and the medical services should be arranged in cooperation with the local or national health authority. Prisons should also have a psychotherapy section where cases of mental retardation can be treated if necessary."

  • Delay and postponement in taking prisoners to hospitals. In many cases prisoners are taken to hospitals at the last minute when their health conditions deteriorate, such as:
      - Omar Abu Sarhan who was taken to hospital six months late, suffering from a severe eye infection.
      - Ataya Jabir who suffered a stomach ulcer and was taken to hospital five months late for an X-ray.
      - Jamal Sarahin from Bet Ula, Hebron, was taken to Afula Hospital when his health completely deteriorated and he was about to die. He spent more than two weeks at the hospital, then was taken to Al Ramla Hospital where he spent ten days. He was later taken to Megiddo prison, which led to another deterioration in his health. He did not receive the proper health treatment, as he suffers from liver and spleen hypertrophy, chest infection and an increase in the number of white blood cells.

Prisoners complained about health negligence in prisons, especially lack of immediate transfer to prison clinics. The experience showed that prisoners are usually taken to clinics one or two weeks late.

  • Giving out medicine which has passed its expiry date, as in the case of Tariq Jawabra, who was given out of date headache drugs from the prison's clinic.

  • The dentist problem at Megiddo prison. The dentist is supposed to come to the prison three times a week but, in fact, he comes only once a week and then claims that the medical equipment is out of order.

  • The dentist does not perform his duty properly because he claims that pulling out a tooth requires the patient to be taken to hospital and not at the prison's clinic.

This also contradicts Article 22 of the lower division of the regulations of prisoners' treatment, which states:

"prisoners who need special health care should be taken to special prisons or public hospitals. It is the duty of the prisons' clinics, if they have the health care hospitals, to have sufficient equipment and drugs in order to provide the right health treatment for sick prisoners. They should also have professional and qualified staff."

  • Forcing prisoners in Al Damun prison to have their recess. Before that it was optional to take the recess. The prisoners appealed this decision but they reached a compromise with the prison director to withdraw the appeal for canceling the decision.

  • 90% of prisoners in Telmond suffer from a skin allergy caused by the uniform imposed on them by the prison administration.

  • Skin problems also occur in Megiddo and the prison administrations neglect the problem.

  • The insects and dirt problems in Megiddo prison. The prison is full of various kinds of insects and venomous reptiles such as snakes and venomous insects like scorpions. In one incident a snake managed to enter the room where families see their detained sons during visits.

  • The insecticides used are inefficient.

  • Cleaning materials are scarce. When the prisons' administrations are asked for more they claim that they must stick to the rules.

  • The kitchenware is very old. A health committee from the prisons' department ordered the destruction and replacement of all the kitchenware at Megiddo prison but the prison's administration refused to abide with the order.

  • No doors are available at the bathrooms of Nafha prison -the bathrooms have curtains instead of doors. The prisoners sent a petition regarding this but it was ignored.

 

Some examples of health cases followed up ADDAMEER in 1999:

  1. Juma'a Ismail Ilkayali from Shufat refugee camp, Jerusalem was arrested in 1993 and is serving a life sentence. He suffers from diabetes, high blood pressure, chest infection, hernia and retention of urine. He had surgery in February 1999 for the retention of urine problem. Less than a week after the surgery, Juma'a was taken to Sarafand Hospital when his health deteriorated. He remained unconscious for five days, then was taken back to hospital where he stayed for more than two months without being able to stand on his feet or use a wheelchair. He was unable to move from his bed the entire time.

  2. Sabri Ilmuqadi was arrested in 1994 and received an 11 year sentence and suffers from many health problems such as high blood pressure, spinal injury and pain in the right leg. Sabri cannot walk well and has an eye infection which has caused a loss of 80% of his vision. He also has an infection of the urinary tract.

  3. Imad Ata Za'rab from Khan Yunis was arrested in 1993 and received a life sentence. He has kidney problems and an infection in the lymph glands. Imad was taken to Al Ramla prison Hospital in March in order to be under supervision because he had symptoms of a mysterious disease. Later the disease was found to be Cacutchi disease.

  4. Yassir Ilmuathen from Syria was arrested 11 years ago when he was 17. He was sentenced to 25 years imprisonment. He suffered from a kidney failure and spent time in Al Ramla prison Hospital. In Jan 1999 the Israeli High Court held a session to discuss releasing him because of the deterioration in his health condition and after an intervention from the French government and efforts made by local and international human rights associations, including ADDAMEER, Israel decided to free him by a letter sent to the French ambassador in Tel Aviv. Yassir's release was postponed until the last freeing of Arab prisoners on October 15 1999, he was taken to Gaza Strip and then to Syria.
