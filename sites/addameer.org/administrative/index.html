I. Introduction

The tyrannical nature of administrative detention and its complete violation of human rights are based on confidential materials kept from the detainee and the lawyer. The confidential materials determine the period of detention and its extension thereafter. The administrative detainee is allowed an appeal before a court martial but the confidentiality of the materials on which the detention is based makes the trial nominal or false. Experience has shown that the real decision-maker in the appeal is the intelligence services.

On June 6 1999 an amendment to administrative detention took place after a new Israeli military order was issued (order number 1466: administrative detention (temporary procedures) (the 13th amendment). It stipulates that a detainee should stand before a judge during the first ten days of detention. The order was applied in the Israeli courts on July 6 1999. It stipulates that it is in the judge's power to cancel the administrative detention order or confirm it or reduce its period. Then the detainee has the right to appeal the ruling before a court martial, then before the High Court of Justice.

This amendment in the military order of administrative detention came too late after the other amendment enforced by the Israeli law within the Green Line which stipulates that a detainee should stand before a judge during the first 48 hours of detention.

The first impression created by this amendment is that something positive has happened in the policy of administrative detention. Although there is an increase in the judiciary control, the detainees stand before a judge without a defense lawyer in many cases because it would be hard to contact prisoners in the first ten days of arrest during which, the ruling says, the detainee stands before a judge. Furthermore, intelligence services' officers still have the authority to issue orders that contradict the judge's ruling. There are many cases of extending administrative detention that will be cited later in this report.

The change in the number of the administrative detainees does not stem from a change in the law or from an Israeli intention to change its laws but from having another power that implements the use of administrative detention.

The number of administrative detainees has been decreased on one hand, but on the other hand the number of detainees under interrogation has increased.

At the beginning of 1999 the number of administrative detainees reached a total of one hundred, whereas, it reached fourteen administrative detainees distributed amongst Megiddo and Telmond prisons in the end of 1999. Megiddo prison has 12 detainees and Telmond prison has two detainees. They were kept in isolation from other prisoners, justified by stating that they were in need of special medical attention.



 

II.Administrative detainees who received extended detention sentences

The Israeli Authorities has kept extending the periods of administrative detention for the following prisoners:

  • Abdulqadir Idris who was arrested on December 22 1994 and was sentenced to 30 months imprisonment. He was held in administrative detention on June 14 1997 after finishing his first sentence. His detention was extended six times, the latest was on September 7 1999 for six months.


  • Khalid Jaradat who was arrested on August 21 1998 after a week of his release from a year and a half of administrative detention. His detention was extended five times. He stood before the Israeli High Court of Justice three times, the latest was on July 19 1999. The Court dismissed his appeal after examining the confidential report.

III. Transferring Administrative Detainees to Interrogation

Transferring administrative detainees to interrogation before the end of the detention period has become a regular phenomenon this year. This method has been used by the Israeli intelligence services for some time. They arrest a person and hold him in custody of administrative detention for a long period of time. After receiving strong criticism from judicial authorities or receiving new information the detainee is transferred to interrogation.

IV. Transferring Detainees who finish their sentences to Administrative Detention

The Israeli intelligence services have been using the method of transferring detainees who finish their sentences to administrative detention. In the last few years a number of Palestinian detainees have been transferred to administrative detention after finishing serving their sentences. These detainees served sentences of one to ten years before being place under administrative detention. The intelligence services' have justified this by citing state security.

The detainees remain incarcerated. The detainee Salah Shihadi from Gaza Strip who spent ten years in prison was transferred to administrative detention on September 1 1998 and is still being issued extensions, the latest extension was for six months on December 1 1999. No new evidence of protecting state security was produced in his case, although Salah lives in the Gaza Strip, which is completely isolated from Israel and no one who lives in Gaza can go into Israel without its consent. How could a detainee who spent ten years incarcerated endanger the security of the state of Israel? The contradiction and incredibility of the Israeli policies are obvious, especially in offering Ayman Daragmi a deportation to the Palestinian territories in Gaza.
This applies to many Palestinian detainees from the West Bank such as Abdulqadir Idris who spent 30 months in prison and then transferred to administrative detention on June 14 1999 and still receiving extensions to the periods of detention.

V.Protests against administrative detention

The detainees held protests in Megiddo prison on March 2 1999 against the policy of extending administrative detention for some prisoners only hours or days before their release. They marched inside the prison and were confronted with tear gas from the prison administration. Many prisoners were wounded such as Ibrahim Alsahuri, Kamal Ilaruri, Muhammad Ilamalin, Khalil Salim, Abas Qarain, Gasan Madi, Muntasir Farha and Saji Ilkhatib.

These incidents occurred after the prison's administration's decision of transferring the administrative detainee Imad Ilaruj to interrogation one night before his release and extending the period of administrative detention for Hani Jaradat one day before his release. The prisoners reached an agreement with the administration of Megiddo prison, the prosecutor and the intelligence services not to extend the period of administrative detention for any prisoner within the last ten days of his sentence.
