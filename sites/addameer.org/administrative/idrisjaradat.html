Abdulqadir Idris

In the name of God, Most Gracious, Most Merciful
The detainee Abdulqadir Idris
An administrative detainee

"Janat" the five and a half-year-old child would not accept to go to her father like the rest of the children of the administrative detainees would when the jailer opens the special door for the children to pass to their detained fathers during a visit at the Israeli military prison of Majido.
After big efforts and many questions "Janat" accepted to stand in front of her father but on the opposite side of the fence. Perhaps she was encouraged to do so because her father would not be able to hold her since there was an iron fence separating them.

What is she thinking of? She is too young to understand. Does she ask herself about what brings her and her mother to this place twice a month? Who is the man that meets her, plays with her and makes her smile was she is silent?
For the past five and a half years Janat did not see a man at her home and during the past eleven feasts (Alfitir and Aladha), her father did not come home with a present or a toy in his hand for her like the rest of the children.

Janat and her nine-year-old brother did not have the chance to say the word "dad" in the past nineteen feasts like the rest of the children who see their fathers on the feasts' mornings and every morning.

We are not talking about a world of fantasy; it is a real and part of our Palestinian world; it is something called Administrative Detention. The detainee Abdulqadir Idris, 36, from Hebron is an example that stands before the world to show the size of tyranny and subjugation administrative detainees are subjected to inside the Israeli prisons. Idris is still serving extended administrative detention sentences, month after month and year after year. There isn't a known day when he will finish the hardest and most suffering days of his life.
The suffering of Idris started on September 17 1989 when the Israeli general security men "the Shabak" took him from his home at night to the interrogation center at Hebron central prison to remain in the dungeons of interrogation for forty days during which he was subjected to most brutal and most harsh interrogation.

In the next year, in July 1990, Idris was arrested again for 38 months, which he spent transferring between prisons:Althahriyi, Bitunia and Ilnaqab (Ansar 3)
On February 25 1994 a settler fired at Palestinian worshippers during the Morning Prayer in Alharam Al Ibrahimi mosque "the tomb of the patriarch" in Hebron murdering 29 people including Salim Idris, Abdulqadir's brother and his brother Walid was badly wounded, too.

The third arrest of Abdulqadir Idris was on December 21 1994, he was sentenced to thirty months imprisonment, which was based on a deal between the attorney Jawad Bolus and the prosecutor. After the continuous arrests and suffering Idris was supposed to be released but more episodes of suffering started again; four days before his release from Majido on June 14 1997 the prison administration handed him a piece of paper that said: the general security services the Shabak has decided to transfer him directly to administrative detention for six months without mentioning any reasons for the decision.
Before his release on December 19 1997 another order of extending his sentence by three month was issued, then for another six months, then six months again, then another six months, then for the sixth time his detention was extended for another six months. The pieces of paper came to Idris one after the other carrying between their lines the unfair orders of the Shabak of extending the administrative detention sentences. On February 6 2000 the flood of the pieces of paper stopped and Idris was finally released.
Giving Idris one administrative detention sentence after the other was without carrying out any interrogation or questioning. The only excuse of the Shabak was that Idris was an active member of Hamas with no further details about the activities claimed.

The Israeli military authorities use Article 87 (b) of the Israeli Military Rule number (378) issued in 1970 to legally justify extending the administrative detention. Article 87 (b) stipulates that "if a military officer who is in charge of an area has basis for thinking that for reasons regarding regional and public safety that require keeping a person in custody he may do that by signing an order, from time to time, to extend the original sentence for a period of no more than six months."

The question is since this detainee received a 30-month sentence from the Israeli judiciary system and a deal with the prosecutor. During this period the detainee was behind bars but received new sentences, is there a moral cause that gives the Israeli military authorities the right to continuously extend his administrative detention? Is the phrase "a Hamas activist" a logical explanation to keep him behind bars for many years? Where is the threat that Idris poses to the national Israeli security and the Israeli citizens when he spent in prison eight and a half years, including 36 months in a row, a period of many changes that took place and should have been considered?

Despite having Idris to stand before the military committee on appeals to protest being an administrative detainee, the Shabak had the final word.

Social and Health status:
Idris has a BA degree in the Islamic Sharia (law) from Al Quds University in 1986 and he works in commerce. His work stopped on the day of his arrest. He supports his wife, two children, his mother and his two orphaned brothers. The family did not have any income during the absence of Idris apart from the Social Affairs' benefits of 600 Shekels a month.

Khalid Jaradat

The administrative detainee Khalid Hussein Abdulkarim Jaradat
Nickname: Abu Hadi
From: Silit Ilharthiyi/ district of Jenin
Date of birth: January 1 1960
Education: BA English- Alnajah University- Nablus
Year of graduation: 1984

Khalid was employed at Alyamun High School in 1986-87 for two months. He was arrested and was immediately fired from his job. He was released on November 20 1987.

He was arrested in 1988 and was accused of instigating and participating in demonstrations. A few days later his two-floor house that accommodated more than twenty people was blown up without giving the family the opportunity to appeal the order in the courts of law, they were not even given the chance to evacuate the house from all the things inside. The destruction of the house was unjust and was carried out only for deterrence. Khalid was not interrogated and he did not give out any confessions that justify this barbaric act.

The accusations were fabricated by forcing some teenagers who were less than seventeen to falsely confess that he encouraged demonstrations. Those teenagers told him later that they did not know him and they were forced to do what they did.
Based on these lies and false accusations, Khalid was sentenced to 8 months and a fine of 750 Shekels. He was released at the beginning of November 1988 and got married on January 1989.

He was arrested 9 days after his marriage while he was on his way to visit his sister in Nablus. He was given a six-month administrative detention sentence and before finishing his time he was transferred to interrogation because someone confessed that he sent him to Jordan to bring some money. That person said that he brought 400 Jordanian Dinars but did not hand them to the defendant because he was arrested and received a 40-month actual sentence and a 40-month stay of execution. He was released at the end of 1992.

In October 1993 he was arrested again and was given a six-month administrative detention sentence based on the accusation of being a member in "Islamic Jihad" in Palestine. He was released in April 1994. His wife gave birth to a baby on October 22 1993 when Khalid was in prison and he called her Wafa.

He was arrested again a few days later. New accusations were fabricated against him by forcing a person to confess that Khalid has activities amongst the students at Alnajah University in Nablus. He was not questioned or interrogated and received a 30-month prison actual sentence. He was released in October 1996.

By the end of 1996 Khalid started teaching at a school away from his village in Area "2". He was arrested again in the middle of February 1997 while he was on his way to school and received a six-moth prison sentence; he was accused of being a member of the "Islamic Jihad" in Palestine. He was released in the middle of August 1997.

A week later he was arrested -on August 21 1997 while he was going to Ramallah to attend a national conference called upon by the Palestinian Authority, he received an administrative detention sentence for being a member in the Islamic Jihad movement. He is still serving administrative detention sentences, which have been renewed for five months.
He had another baby on October 16 1997 and he called him "Hadi".

He stood before the courts of appeal many times but in vain because he was unable to defend himself since he did not know the accusations against him and because the system of these courts does not allow the defendant to know what he is accused of. All he heard from the prosecutor is that he was dangerous and threatened the security of the State and that the prosecutor would give the confidential file to the judge. He took his case to the High Court in Jerusalem and was shocked to see that the court session was not more than five minutes and that he was dangerous and that there is a confidential file to show to the judge.

He stood before the high Court three times, the latest was on July 19 1999 and was shocked to hear the prosecutor saying "the defendant is dangerous and still an active terrorist inside the prison because he issued direct orders of carrying out bombings." Khalid was surprised to hear such ridiculous talking and requested from the court to answer back such false accusations.
He said before the judges "the honorable judges I want to direct my speech to your minds and I hope that you would hear me well. If I were that dangerous, why wouldn't you interrogate me or even let me see a security officer for the past ten years? How could I be that dangerous without any connection with any one since hundreds of Islamic Jihad activists were arrested and interrogated and brutally tortured but no one claimed that he knew me or had any relationship with me?" But after seeing the confidential file the court ruled to dismiss the defendant's deposition and ordered to keep him in custody.

Khalid wonders "is this justice? Is this the democracy Israel is proud of? Is it fair to destroy a family for lies and false conclusions and accusations and information given by paid collaborators who have no feelings?
Khalid also argues "I have been deprived more than one time from speaking before the judges now I can say it to the whole world I have not committed any act of violence in my entire life. I confess that I am a strict Muslim who would never betray his country or religion, this is the only accusation I admit. I will not speak so much about the sadness, the pain and despair when my family hears about extending my sentences.

My daughter Wafa is now in her first grade. I would love to be by her side to help her and support her education. I would also love to see her going out of home to school everyday wearing her school uniform.
I would love to be by her side on a feast day even if only once so that she can be joyful like any other child in the world. I would love to hold my son Hadi and play with him at home instead of trying to reach him through the fences in the depressing visit room.
Wafa always asks me about the day I go home and why don't I come home in the evening like the other fathers, please tell me what shall I say to her? Are there any psychotherapists who could answer this question?

The supposed date of released is February 16 2000. He is still in jail.
