Hunger Strikes

Hunger strike is the most powerful and dangerous form of struggle used in times when living conditions in a prison become intolerable; it is also used to demand a release. Hunger strikes have a political nature; they were used after the signing of the peace agreements, which deprived prisoners from the right to be free.

Prisoners used hunger strikes many times in 1999 whether individually, collectively or for political reasons. The following is a list of the most important of these strikes:

Collective hunger strikes

  • On May 1st 2000, pisoners in Hadarim prison begin a hunger strike. After a few days, all detenees inside Israeli prisons joined the hunger strike protesting against their bad conditions of detention. On May 31 2000, all prisoners ended their hunger strike after their demands were met by the Israeli prison administration.

  • On January 23 1999 for one day in Asqalan prison protesting preventing non-close relatives from visits, especially Jerusalem and Arab prisoners.

  • On January 27 1999 for one day at Asqalan prison protesting inspections.

  • On May 9 1999 for one day at Nafha prison because the prison administration delayed solving some problems for a long time.

  • On June 2 1999 for two days at Shata prison protesting searching the detainees rooms on the day of families' visits.

  • From June 5 1999 to June 12 1999 at the solitary confinement section in Asqalan prison protesting the poor living conditions. The strike was halted when the administration of the prison agreed to improve the living conditions, such as giving a recess time of three hours, sending a barber, installing sports equipment, taking recess without cuffs and allowing them to move within the solitary confinement section.

  • On July 15 1999 for one day at Asqalan prison when a conference was held to review the Geneva convention and the extent of Israel's compliance with it. The countries were pressured to not hold the conference.

  • On July 27 for two days at Nafha prison in solidarity with Hasan Salami who was on a hunger strike for 14 days protesting being held in solitary confinement and requesting improving the living conditions inside the prison.

  • On August 22 1999 at Asqalan prison for four days in order to exert pressure on the Israeli and Palestinian negotiators to release the prisoners.

Individual hunger strikes

  • From February 15 to March 14 1999 by Muna Qa'dan protesting being arrested and tortured.

  • From April 2 to May 8 1999 by Ataya Jabir protesting being left in solitary confinement for more than three years.

  • A 35-day-hunger-strike by the Lebanese detainee Mahmoud Qasim Hmud in June 1999 protesting being left in solitary confinement at Bir Ilasabi'a prison and following the deterioration of his health condition.

  • Hasan Salami went on a hunger strike for more than 25 days on July 10 1999 protesting being held at Al Ramli prison.
