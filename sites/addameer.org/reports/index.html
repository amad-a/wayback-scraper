Reports from behind the Bars

As the Palestinian and Israeli negotiating teams are discussing final status negotiations, a blind eye remains turned towards a crucial issue: Palestinian and Arab political prisoners. It is our understanding that as two countries make peace, the first sign of peace is the release of prisoners as a sign of a new beginning.

There continue to be 1,600 Palestinian and Arab prisoners in Israeli jails and tens of political prisoners inside Palestinian prisons. Palestinian as well as other Arab prisoners in Israeli prisons were hoping to be released after the Oslo Agreement, which was signed between the PLO and the Israeli government in 1993. In spite of the fact that seven years have passed since the signing of the agreement, there continue to be 1,600 prisoners in the Israeli prisons, some of whom have been incarcerated for tens of years. One example of such a prisoner is Hamad Jbara, who spend 22 years in Israeli prison, and others who have long imprisonment sentences. The Lebanese prisoner, Samir Qintar spend 23 years in Nafha prison as well as other Arab prisoners from the 1948 area and Jerusalem, also suffer from health conditions.

Each prisoner has his own experience of denial of freedom, health situation, solitary confinement, deprivation of family visits, right to an education, in addition to torture and suffering in prison cells.

The prisoners’ movement inside Israeli prisons has been consistently struggling against all attempts by the Israeli Prison Authorities to dehumanize prisoners. The prisoners’ movement has been struggling to fulfill prisoners’ educational needs and develop their capacities in many areas. This is a fundamental goal, in light of the fact that many of these political prisoners have sacrificed their lives for the sake of freedom and living in dignity and humanity.

ADDAMEER believes in all prisoners’ right to freedom and stresses the necessity for the implementation of all international conventions and agreements, specifically those that are related to prisoners and the treatment of prisoners. Moreover, ADDAMEER is against arbitrary arrest and believes that it is the right of all prisoners to receive a fair and just trial. Furthermore, ADDAMEER works to end torture and ill and inhumane treatment of prisoners, in addition to capital punishment, as every individual has the right to life.

ADDAMEER works towards achieving its aims and objectives through various programs and projects. The four main programs of ADDAMEER are closely related and complement one another.

  1. Legal Program: The legal component primarily seeks to achieve the goal of following up with 326 legal cases regarding prisoners in 1999.

  2. Prisoners’ Visit Program: Through the prisoners’ visit program, in 1999, ADDAMEER made 148 visits to various prisons, where we met with 537 Palestinian and other Arab prisoners.

  3. Lobbying and Advocacy Program: The lobbying and advocacy program aims to raise cases of prisoners locally as well as internationally. The Association participated in carrying out the following campaigns in 1999:

    • Lobbying for a just trial in the case of two Palestinian detainees, Samar Al Alami and Jawad Al Bowatmeh, who are being held in British prisons.
    • Al Khayam Prison- a campaign to close it down and to free its prisoners.
    • Suhaila Andrews- a campaign to free her that went on for years.

  4. Documentation Program: Addameer documents all information that is related to statistics concerning the number of detainees, date and place of arrest, and any violations suffered by detainees.

Part of ADDAMEER’s work is the preparation of its annual report, which requires much effort and most importantly, team work. ADDAMEER staff, volunteers, families of prisoners, and friends work hard in order to expose the Israeli violations against Palestinian prisoners. Together, we work to cast the light on the prisoners’ movements, the suffering and difficult living conditions inside the Israeli prisons, and those in the Palestinian Authority prisons.

Violations committed by the Israeli Government

The Israeli government continued its arrest campaigns against Palestinians throughout the year 1999. Arrests were made through raids on villages and camps under Israeli control, at checkpoints between the Palestinian cities and at internal crossing points between Gaza-West Bank or external crossing points with Jordan and Egypt.

  • Solitary confinement.

  • Hunger Strikes.

  • Violations Committed by the Palestinian Authority.

Conclusion:

It is Addameer’s conviction that the issue of prisoners rights will not be ignored or swallowed up by the peace negotiations, it should be a matter of grave importance to both Palestinian and Israeli negotiators.

Through its three main programs, Addameer hopes to achieve the right to freedom for all Palestinian and Arab political prisoners. By bringing to light, the violations of both the Israeli and Palestinian authorities, we hope to garner increasing support for prisoners rights and secure the release of prisoners of security.

Addameer would like to thank its dedicated team of staff, volunteers, prisoners and their families, and friends whose hard work and perseverance has been instrumental in our work. To you we are grateful.
