Solidarity Campaigns

The Association takes the initiative in organizing campaigns concerning important and sensitive issues within the framework of its programs. It concentrates on certain issues and uses all possible means and methods in supporting and drawing attention to them in order to exert pressure so as to find a solution and remedy a particular malfunction. The Association has succeeded in the past in raising some important issues and has managed to achieve its objectives.

The Association participated in carrying out the following campaigns in 1999:

Samar Al Alami and Jawad Al Bowatmeh, who are being held in British prisons.
 Al Khayam Prison- a campaign to close it down and to free its prisoners.

The case of Palestinian detainees Samar and Jawad held in British jails

Samar Al Alami, 32, is a Palestinian whose family was displaced in 1948. She is a Lebanese national. She has a BA degree in Chemical Engineering from University College London. She has political interests since she was a member of Palestinian-British political groups.

Jawad Al Bowatmeh, 30, is a Palestinian from the village of Battir in Bethlehem. He has a BA degree in Electrical Engineering from Liecester University. Both Jawad and Samar were accused of conspiring to blow up the Israeli Embassy and the Union of Zionist Organizations in London in 1994. Both were sentenced to 20 years imprisonment on 16 December 1996. The court did not charge them directly with the explosion because of lack of evidence. It was proved that they were involved with a third person who introduced himself as Basim Rida Almagribi to the defendants, and disappeared suddenly, with the court and the defense lawyers failing to identify and locate him. Both Jawad and Samar accuse him of being a Mossad agent or an agent for the British police. The defendants deny being involved in the incident of blowing up the Israeli Embassy, no one was killed, and they also emphasized that this kind of action damages the Palestinian cause.

Later on new facts appeared when an MI5 agent, David Sheller, defected and disclosed information about MI5, most importantly that MI5 received a clear warning from a distinguished source about terrorist attacks aimed at Israeli targets in London. By the end of July 1994, and after a short time of receiving the warning, which was ignored by the security services, the Israeli Embassy was blown up.

The confessions of David Sheller draw a new picture for the juries of the court since a warning means having secret information of the security services. This indicates an involvement of a foreign government or an international terrorist organization. It also indicates that the British security services had information about who placed the booby-trapped car close to the Embassy and the Union of Zionist Organizations. In addition to this is the fact that the lawyer Garret Peres provided evidence that Al Alami was, at the time of explosion, at the library of Imperial College in London and made a phone call which was recorded at the library record books.

What makes the trial of Al Alami and Al Bowatmeh suspicious is the prosecution's declaration that they found a map with Samar of the location of the Israeli Embassy but it was a map of one of Samar's relatives' homes in Sidon, Lebanon. Another plan, which was found at Al Bowatmeh's residence, was presented as a plan of flying a remote control plain; it was later found out that it was a plan of a simple alarm device. Furthermore, the Israeli Embassy in London refused to provide a video recording of its cameras outside the Embassy, saying that they were out of order at the time of the explosion.

The Association began its campaign of support for Samar and Jawad in 1998 through founding a committee in Palestine and supporting committees in Britain and Lebanon. The campaign carried out the following activities in 1999:

  • A letter campaign to the British Foreign Office, the British prosecution system and the British jury before the court hearing, which was held on 15th March 1999, of the prosecution's "exceptional testimony for the public good" to support the defense lawyer Garret Peres' request to disclose the evidence and give Samar and Jawad the right to appeal the court ruling. The number of letters sent from Palestine was 2000.

  • Publishing the case of Summer and Jawad in the local newspapers and the local media so as to inform the Palestinian community of the case.

  • A letter was sent to the consul general of the British Consulate General in Jerusalem in November 1999 requesting from him to take ADDAMEER'Srequest of retrying Jawad and Samar and for finding new evidence, to the British government. On 18th November 1999 ADDAMEER received a letter from the British consul general, Mr. Robin Kelly saying that the British High Court has agreed to retry Jawad and Samar but the court hearing has not been determined yet. He also promised to take the letter to the relevant British authorities.

  • A discussion on the Internet was held at Birzeit University about Samar and Jawad.

  • ADDAMEERhas also participated, alongside other associations, and the Palestinian Ministry of Education, in signing a petition in support of Samar and Jawad. The number of signatures was 200,000 signatures in Palestine alone.

Al Khayam Prison- a campaign to close it down

Al Khayam detention center was opened in 1985 after the closing of Ansar prison. It is located on a hilltop overlooking the town of Al Khayam in the "security zone" in South Lebanon occupied by Israel. The prison was built on occupied Lebanese soil where Israel has no right to keep the land and properties of another nation.

The prison does not have the minimum level of human conditions. Its many section are cells and interrogation rooms characterized by harsh living conditions, which has led to the death of 14 Lebanese detainees as a result of torture and medical negligence. A prisoner of Al Khayam is completely isolated from the outside world and lawyers are prevented from seeing him. The Red Cross committee was allowed to visit it for the first time in 1995. The prison holds within its walls women, the elderly and children in difficult conditions without a bill of indictment or a trial.

ADDAMEERinitiated a campaign to close down Al Khayam prison in coordination with a special French committee and a committee on supporting Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons. The campaign began by:

  • Adopting several cases of Lebanese detainees in Al Khayam at the Israeli High Court. There were three cases, defended by French lawyers, for:

      - Karam Mustafa, a Lebanese detainee from the village of Alhula. He was arrested on 10th March 1997. (He was released on 13th January 2000).

      - Bilal Abu Ta'am from the Lebanese village of Taybi. He was arrested on 1st February 1998 and is still in prison.

      - Samir Qasim from Yahmar. He was arrested on 16th April 1988. He lost one of his eyes during detention.


  • ADDAMEER participated in a deposition under oath about the living conditions at Al Khayam in the case taken to the High Court for closing it down.


  • On 28th October 1999, the international day for demanding the closing of Al Khayam prison, ADDAMEER held a campaign of signing a petition in Palestine calling for closing down the prison. Thousands of people signed the petition. A press conference, organized by ADDAMEER Association was held in Gaza on this day.

The militia of South Lebanon, in cooperation with the Israeli forces arrested tens of civilians in 1999. Kuzin Ibrahim, 25, a Lebanese journalist, was kidnapped on 2nd September 1999 with two other people in South Lebanon. She was taken to Al Khayam prison and is still held there.

Gunwa Muhammad Jafar and her brothers Bilal and Muhammad were arrested in October 1999. She was released two weeks later. Gunwa's sister Fatma Muhammad Jafar, 16, was also arrested. She was beaten on her head with pistols. Then she was held in custody in Detention Center 17 controlled by the South Lebanon army in Bint Jbail in the occupied zone "according to the Lebanese follow up committee". Fatma was taken to the emergency room at one of Sidon's hospitals after being subjected to beating and torture.

Samira Atiyi and Shamlakan Asaf from the Lebanese village of Arnon were arrested on 23rd November 1999.

Six Lebanese detainees were released from Al Khayam in October 1999. Thirteen detainees, including the detainee Tuba, who was arrested with his father on 20th October 1999 from Arnon, the youngest prisoner at Al Khayam prison, were released on 8th November 1999.

Suhaila Andraws-a campaign to free her after a journey of suffering Suahaila Andra.
The Palestinian detainee, was released from a Norwegian prison on 31st November 1999 after the Norwegian Minister of Justice's order of 12th November 1999 to free her

Suhaila's case is one of the most important and just cases. The case had local and international dimensions since Suhaila was tried twice in two different countries, first in Somalia and then in Germany. Before her second trial, she was arrested in Norway. She faced illegal procedures and her health was in danger because she is ill and still suffering from receiving ten bullets during the Lufthansa airliner hijacking in Germany in 1977. Many international associations supported Suhaila's case since it was a fair political case. Some of the supporters were lawyers, academics, churches and human rights organizations. They all called for her release on human and legal grounds.

Suhaila was born in Beirut in 1953. She joined the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine. She took part in hijacking the German Lufthansa airliner in 1977 in an attempt to free Palestinian prisoners in Israeli and European prisons. She was wounded during the operation to end the hijacking and received ten bullet wounds, which she still suffers from. A court in Somalia sentenced her to twenty years imprisonment but she was released after one and a half years because her health started deteriorating. After treatment, she finished her university education and worked as a translator and a journalist in Lebanon. Then she left Lebanon to Damascus in 1982 where she was married to Palestinian writer and journalist Dr. Ahmad Abu Matar, and had a baby in 1985.

Suhaila moved to Oslo, Norway where she was arrested again on 13th October 1994 by a request made by Germany. The Norwegian courts ruled out extradition to Germany twice but in the third time, and after changing judges, she was extradited to Germany, where she was sentenced to 12 years in prison in November 1996 for taking part in the hijacking of the German Lufthansa airliner. This was the second sentence after the first sentence in Somalia.

Suhaila went on a hunger strike for more than 20 days, during which her health deteriorated severely, protesting her situation. She fought with the support of her family to be taken back to Norway in order to be close to her child. On 29th July 1997 Suhaila returned to Norway to serve her sentence after spending 613 days in German prisons. The attempt to free her went on until the Norwegian Minister of Justice ordered her release on 31st November 1999.

ADDAMEER and other concerned parties about the case of Suhaila started a campaign in 1997 to free her. The campaign included sending letters of protest to the German government, sit-in strikes, solidarity letters to Suhaila in prison in addition to a media campaign and the production of a poster demanding her release.
