I. Background

On 13th September 1993, the Oslo Agreement "Declaration of Principles: the Arrangements of the Interim Autonomy" was signed between the PLO and Israel. The Palestinian Authority was founded by the Gaza-Jericho Agreement (Cairo Agreement), which was signed on 4th May 1994. On 28th September 1995, the Israeli-Palestinian Agreement on the interim period in Gaza and the West Bank was signed (Oslo 2 or the Taba Agreement), then the Wye River Memorandum in October 1998 and the Sharm Al Sheikh agreement in 1999.

The agreements and memorandums signed by the Israeli side and the Palestinian side disregarded the international laws that consider Israel as an occupying state of the Palestinians. The most prominent of those laws is Lahai 1907 and Geneva's fourth convention of 1949 concerning the protection of civilians during war, which stipulate that the protection of people under occupation cannot be subjected to any compromise by any interim agreement.

The Israeli condition under the title of "security" has led to the sacrificing the rights of Palestinians. The United States of America, the sponsor of the peace process, has joined in the violation of human rights through its direct involvement in the committees (such as forbidding instigation and fighting "terrorism" and the Wye River memorandum), as well as through its continuous financial support of Israel, despite its record of human rights violations, and its increased pressure towards arresting those who oppose the peace process, whether Israelis or Palestinians.

These agreements have not canceled the present status of the West Bank and Gaza as closed military zones, where military rules and regulations still apply. Furthermore, new military orders can be issued and applied to the inhabitants of the areas under Palestinian Authority control. Israel also controls internal movement and the crossings.

The general state of human rights, after the agreements, is an increase of violations. Such violations are presented through increased settlement building, land confiscation and home demolition, restrictions on movement, complete separation of Gaza and the West Bank, continuous administrative arrests (detentions) (administrative detention practiced by Israel), use of torture, and extra-judicial killings. The Palestinian Authority violates human rights by carrying out political arrests without trials, establishing a state security court, enforcing capital punishment and restricting the freedom of speech.

In 1999, an abusive arrest policy, "arrest without trial" continued. The state security court in Palestine issued tens of verdicts, four death penalty verdicts were issued -one was carried out- in addition to arresting tens of people without trial. Hundreds of political prisoners, "prisoners of conscience" were imprisoned without trial. Palestinian journalist Mahir Al Dasuki was arrested in addition to other journalists, including the editor in chief of Al Risala newspaper. Nine other individuals were arrested, including a woman, for signing a manifesto known as the 'Manifesto of Twenty', which condemns the corruption of the Palestinian Authority. House arrests were imposed on some ex-mayors such as Wahid Alhamdalla, ex-mayor of Anabta and Basam Alshaka, ex-mayor of Nablus. Abdul Jawad Salih, a member of the Legislative Council, was attacked and beaten on 16th December 1999.

II. Arbitrary arrests

Political arrests

Political arrest is a form of abusive arrest since it is "an arrest for an opinion, a belief or an ideology." The majority of political prisoners held in prisons of the Palestinian Authority are kept in prison for many years without trial or respect for rule of law. A minority of detainees was taken to the state security court, which represents a violation of the detainees' rights to a fair and just trial, choosing an attorney or appealing.

Abusive arrest contradicts many international laws such as

"each individual has the right to freedom and security. No one should be held in custody without a trial and no one should be deprived of his freedom, except for reasons stipulated in the law and in accordance with appropriate relevant regulations". (International Covenant for Political and Civil Rights, Article 1/9).

Abusive arrest also contradicts the effective law. In the Gaza Strip, the procedures of arrest comply with "the procedures of criminal law (arrest and investigation) of 1924", issued during the British mandate. Article 10/1 of the said law stipulates that:

"A person arrested without an arrest warrant or a person held in custody in accordance with the previous article (which gives officers at police stations the power to hold a person in custody) to stand before a judge within forty-eight hours of his arrest."

In the West Bank the Jordanian jurisdiction of 1961 is in use. It stipulates that it is necessary for people held in custody to stand before the prosecutor within 24 hours of the arrest.

The Palestinian draft law describes in detail the conditions that should be respected when arresting a person. These conditions are:

"The judge or the attorney general should issue an arrest warrant before an arrest is carried out. In special cases, individuals may be arrested without issuing an arrest warrant. In this case, the arrested person should stand before the judge of a magistrate's court immediately to enable the judge to issue an order to hold him in custody."

In the case of remanding people into custody and extending the period of custody the draft law declares that:

"It is necessary to bring those in custody with or without an arrest warrant to stand before the magistrate's court within forty hours of the time of arrest in order to check the legality of the arrest or the need to remand them into custody."

The practices of the Palestinian security apparatuses show that most of those held in custody were arrested without an arrest warrant issued by a judiciary institution. They do not stand before a magistrate's court at any time of custody and the period of custody remains a mystery until the time of release. Any person arrested remains in custody by an order of the head of the apparatus that ordered his arrest.

Arrests of individuals in the opposition reflect the extent of the Palestinian compliance with the agreements signed with the Israelis. The arrests are often carried out after a violent incident against Israeli targets. The Wye River memorandum stipulates, under the section of security acts 'C',

"fighting the organizations of terrorism and considering them illegal" and that "a Palestinian-American committee will convene once every two weeks to revise the steps taken to wipe out the terrorist cells and the parties that finance them. In these meetings, the Palestinian party will inform the American party of the steps taken to stop all organizations of a military terrorist nature and all parties supporting them in the areas under Palestinian control."

Article 'E' of the same states that

"the Palestinian-American committee will meet to review the procedures taken in the trials of the suspected individuals who have committed acts of violence and terrorism."

1999 has witnessed major campaigns of abusive arrests in the West Bank and Gaza strip, although they were less than the campaigns of previous years. The Palestinian security apparatuses carried out arrest campaigns which include:

  • 5th February 1999 where 40 people where arrested in Gaza.

  • March 9 members of Al Khalas party were arrested in Gaza.

  • May another 5 people were arrested in Gaza.

  • July 7 people were arrested in Nablus.

  • August, tens of people were arrested in Hebron and Bethlehem.

  • October, 40 people were arrested in the West Bank after detainees escaped from Jneid prison.

  • November 9, signatories of "the manifesto of twenty" were arrested.

The number of political detainees without trial within Palestinian Authority prisons was 140 in 1999. They are from the West Bank and Gaza. The number of those who are remanded in custody by the state security court order is 8 from the West Bank and 5 from Gaza. There are a number of detainees who have spent various periods of time in custody, periods of one month to two years or more without a bill of indictment. In spite of the many objections of local and international human rights organizations made against this policy, the Palestinian Authority is still practicing this method of arbitrary arrest.

III. Prisoners of conscience (political prisoners)

1999 has also witnessed the arrest of individuals based on their opinions and practicing their freedom of speech. In May, Dr. Gazi Hamad, editor in chief of Al Risala newspaper, was summoned and then held in custody at the bureau of criminal investigation. Other editors of the newspaper were summoned, too.

Journalist Mahir Al Dasuki was arrested on 15th September and remanded in custody for 19 days.

On 28th September 1999, journalist Salih Alnaami of the weekly Al Risala and journalist Munir Abu Rizik of Al Hayat Al Jadida were arrested.

Fathi Sabah, a reporter for Al Hayat of London and Wael Abu Daka, Al Jazeera r eporter were summoned.

On 28th November 1999, 9 of the twenty who signed the "Manifesto of Twenty" were arrested, including Abdulsatar Qasim, Ahmad Qatamish, Adel Samara, Dr. Yassir Abu Safiyi, Abdulrahim Katani, Adnan Odeh, Ahmad Shakir Dudin and Afif Sliman Judi. Dr. Ismat Shakshir was also remanded in custody from 6 o'clock in the evening until 11 o'clock. Then she was released on bail of 50,000 Shekels as security given for her appearance on the next day, when she was held in custody again for twelve hours (9:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m.) She was then released. House arrest was imposed on Nablus' ex-mayor, Basam Alshaka'a and the ex-mayor of Anabta, Wahid Alhamadala.

On 16th December, Abdul Jawad Saleh, a member of the Legislative Council was attacked and beaten by members of the General Intelligence Services in Jericho while attending a solidarity sit-in strike of the families of those arrested because of signing "the manifesto of the twenty."

Confronting arbitrary arrests

The detainees rejected being remanded in custody and the orders of the state security court, considering them illegal and unjust. They also requested to be taken to prisons close to their homes. Their rejection was presented in the following protest strikes:

  • Six detainees went on a hunger strike in Jericho prison for 17 days because they wanted to be taken to prisons closer to their homes. They were Abdullah Tahir Alkawasmi, Isa Albatat, Muhammad Abu Warda, Amjad Alhinawi, Nasim Abu Alrus and Jasser Asad Samaru.

  • On 24th January 1999, 44 Palestinian detainees went on a hunger strike at Jneid prison because they were remanded in custody without trial or due process of law. They also demanded implementing the Legislative Council's resolution of releasing all political prisoners and allowing those who have received a bill of indictment to stand trial. The hunger strike went on for 36 days, after a promise to the prisoners that they would be released. This did not happen.

  • · On 30th January 1999, four political prisoners went on a hunger strike in Jericho prison protesting being remanded in custody without trial or due process of law. The strike continued until 16th March 1999.

  • · Individuals also went on strike, such as Ibrahim Masad in Jericho central prison. Ibrahim received a 15 year sentence at the state security court. He went on strike for 13 days, demanding that he be taken to Ramallah prison closer to his home. Salih Talahmi, detained at the Jericho Preventive Security service since 17th May 1996 without a bill of indictment or trial, went on hunger strike for five days.

  • On 9th September 1999, detainees of Jneid prison went on a warning strike for one day for the second year in detention without trial.

Solidarity and protests by the families of the detainees

The families of the detainees, especially their wives, mother and sisters, did not stop fighting for the rights of their relatives in prison. They held protests, initiated petitions and went on hunger strikes in front of the detention centers. Palestinian human rights associations, national institutions and members of the Legislative Council joined in.

IV. The Legislative Council and the Palestinian Authority's stand towards political detention

The Palestinian Legislative Council has discussed the issue of political prisoners many times, most recently on 7th January 1999. The committee for General Supervision of Human Rights and Freedom produced its report in a special session that discussed the state of the detainees and how political arrests contradict the right to freedom and free speech, as well as violations regarding the use of torture and the state security court. The committee produced the following recommendations:

  • Abolishing the state security court, releasing all prisoners of conscience and closing this file forever.

  • Enforcing the previous resolutions of the Council that call for forbidding political arrests and considering it illegal.

  • Halting all forms of torture, in addition to other recommendations.

The Council's resolution regarding forbidding political arrests and other recommendations was a unanimous resolution. It was significantly noted that the Minister of Justice was absent from the session that discussed this issue.

In a session of the Legislative Council, which was held in Ramallah on 27th January 1999, the Minister of Parliamentary Affairs read his report which summarized the viewpoint of the executive body regarding the prisoners of conscience. It read:

"the issue of the political detainees has received the attention it deserves from the executive body for months. No meeting of the cabinet has not discussed the issue since it is our collective concern to close this file and to work hard towards realizing the great principle to abolish political detention and to enforce the rule of law and the judiciary institutions."

There is unanimous agreement against political arrests and for the freeing of all political prisoners and closing this file. This agreement is clear in the support of the Legislative Council's resolutions and the support of the political parties, national institutions, especially human rights organizations, and public opinion. Despite all of this, the executive body has failed to enforce the resolutions of the Legislative Council and it seems unable to do so. It only provides verbal support, which reflects a clear malfunction.

IV. The Palestinian High State Security Court

This court is in conflict with fair and just trials as it lacks the basis of fair trials before a neutral and special court. It does not give prisoners the opportunity to know the bill of indictment. It does not give the detainees enough time to prepare the proper defense or the right to appeal.

For more details see ADDAMEER'S The State Security Court between Necessity and Legality, ADDAMEER publications, 2000.

The Palestinian state security court was founded by a presidential decree in February 1995 and started functioning in April of the same year. It has issued 250 verdicts since its establishment. On 1st November 1999, the President of the Palestinian Authority issued a charter declaring:

"By the powers vested, and for the public well-being, the following have been determined:

  1. The justice Khalid Abdulhadi Alqudra is herein appointed as an attorney general of the state security courts.

  2. The state security court is to deal with investigating and filing complaints and issuing verdicts in the cases related to the high state security courts that have military judges, and in accordance with the effective Palestinian laws and the orders of his excellency the president of the State.

  3. The resolution will be effective on the day of its issuance and all concerned parties must comply with it.

Gaza, 1st November 1999."

Appointing an attorney general for the first time for the state security courts violates the powers vested in the "civil" attorney general who is the only one who has the right of prosecution. It enforces the state security courts instead of canceling them, since court rulings should be the power of the civil jurisdiction.

This worrying presidential charter followed a series of resolutions considered by human rights organizations as a step towards enforcing the independence of jurisdiction. These included appointing a supreme judge on 11th June 1999 and Attorney General on 19th June 1999 and issuing a law for organizing the lawyer profession on 24th June 1999 and the presidential charter number 28 of 1999 on 19th September 1999 which enforces the powers of the supreme judge.

ADDAMEER'S viewpoint towards the state security court

ADDAMEER criticized the presidential charter of founding a state security court on many occasions and expressed this view in numerous manifestos. It has also published a detailed study entitled "The State Security Court: Between Necessity and Legality".

The following are details from a session attended by the Association, producing the following remarks:

" ADDAMEER Association in the Gaza Strip followed up the sessions of the court and has these remarks:

  1. The prosecution was unable to prove that the one who fired the bullets was the suspect "Raed Alatar". The six witnesses were defense witnesses and not prosecution witnesses, who unanimously agreed that they did not see the suspect "Alatar" shoot captain "Rifat".

  2. No evidence was produced to prove that the bullet that killed captain Rifat is the same bullet fired from Alatar's pistol.

  3. Judge Abdulhai Abdulwahid was absent from the last session. He was replaced by judge Brigadier General Ismail Alshafiei, which is a violation of the law, since the judge must attend four court sessions.

The court's ruling led to acts of violence in Rafah between the Judi family and the families of the three detainees. As a result, Ala Juma Alhams, 17 and Khamis Salamih were killed. Three other people were wounded.

ADDAMEER was gravely concerned about acts of violence provoked by the state security court's ruling and the intensive use of violence by the Palestinian security forces.

Henceforth, we declare the following:

  1. We reject the unjust state security court, which violates all international laws and covenants.

  2. We demand President Arafat not to sign the death warrant.

  3. Abolishing the death penalty from Palestinian law since it is not a deterring punishment.

  4. Immediate cessation of using arms against civilians. Adopting self-control in order to avoid any escalation of violence."

VI.The Palestinian High Court

The Palestinian High Court heard tens of cases related to detention without trial and issued 14 rulings of releasing prisoners of conscience; only three were carried out. The Palestinian security apparatuses did not carry out the court rulings, which reflects their disrespect for the rulings of the highest Palestinian judiciary body and threatens the principle of separation of powers.

In 1999 ADDAMREER adopted the cases of Salih Talahmi, the oldest prisoner of conscience in the prisons of the Preventive Security apparatus, held in custody since 26th September 1996, and Muhammad Jamal Alnatshi, who has been held in custody since 5th October 1998 at the prisons of the same apparatus in Jericho.

Adnan Amr and Ribhi Qatamish, on behalf of ADDAMEER Association, took the case to the Palestinian High Court in April 1999. They presented their declaration before the court on 13th April 1999, case no. 14/99 of detainee Talahmi, which included:

  • Issuing an initiative ruling against the respondents (the head of the preventive security apparatus in the West Bank and the attorney general) requesting them to explain the reasons for not releasing the petitioner.

  • Abolishing the administrative order issued by the head of the preventive security apparatus in the West Bank on 26th May 1996, which rules the indefinite administrative detention of the petitioner.

In case no. 15/99 of the detainee Alnatshi, the declaration included the following demands:

  • Abolishing the order of the head of the intelligence services in the West Bank of detaining the petitioner on 5th October 1998.

  • Abolishing the order of the head of the preventive security apparatus in the West Bank of continuing the detention the petitioner on 5th November 1998.

A second session was held in July 1999 but was postponed to 29th September 1999 because of not having a response from the Deputy Attorney General Mr. Salah Mana. On 29th September 1999 the High Court held a session. The Deputy Attorney General produced an order from the military attorney general for the northern Governorate of transferring 6 defendants, including Salih Talahmi and Jamal Alnatshi to the state security court; the date of the order was 12th June 1999.

The Deputy Attorney General requested court adjournment in order to bring forth more evidence. The court was postponed until 24th October 1999 and the deputy attorney general was satisfied with the order of transferring the defendants to the state security court without producing new evidence. He requested postponing his presentation until 14th November 1999. The court held a session on the same date and the Deputy produced his presentation, which included returning the petition to the petitioner for being insufficient for a court hearing. Adnan Amr and Ribhi Qatamish produced a final presentation on the bill of indictment of the prosecution. It included canceling the arrest order since it does not serve the public well-being and for its violation of the detainees' rights and freedom, which reflects an abuse of power.

Another session was set for 29th November 1999, then was postponed because of the absence of the Deputy Attorney General. Another session was set for 27th December 1999, but was postponed indefinitely.

VII. Capital Punishment

"Abolishing the death penalty helps enforce human dignity and gradual development of human rights," from the opening manifestation of the second protocol of the international covenant of political and civil rights.

The Palestinian Authority has carried out the death penalty against Ahmad Atiyi Abu Mustafa from Gaza after one day of his arrest. This was the second time the Authority carried out a death penalty since its establishment.

Captain Ahmad Atiyi Abu Mustafa was arrested after allegedly raping a child from Khan Yunis refugee camp and a special military court sentenced him to death. The sentence was carried out on the next day of his arrest at two o'clock in the morning.

ADDAMEER expressed its point of view in a press release, which concluded that:

"Despite the association's understanding of the brutality of this type of crime, it does not see the death penalty as a deterrent punishment. It is required to take the necessary procedures to prevent the occurrence or reoccurrence of such crimes. ADDAMEER calls for just and fair trials that enable defendants to defend themselves against the accusations as well as giving them the right to appeal the court orders, instead of issuing a court order and carrying it out within hours."

VIII. ADDAMEER 's follow up of the cases of detainees in Palestinian Authority prisons in 1999


  • The Association conducted five visits to the detainees in the prisons of the Palestinian Authority, in Jericho at the preventive security apparatus and in Jenin prison. The Association's request for regular visits to the detainees was rejected. Some of these requests were letters to Mr. Khalil Karaja, the legal advisor to the preventive security apparatus on the following dates: March 6th 1999, April 8th 1999, May 12th 1999, July 22nd 1999, October 9th 1999. Two meetings were held with Mr. Khalil Karaja regarding visits. Unfortunately, the response was not sufficient and the Association was not able to visit the detainees regularly. The Association also sent a letter to General Ismail Jabir of the national forces on 4th November 1999 to allow it to visit Jneid prison but has not received any response to this date.

  • The Association participated in many sit-in strikes with the families of the detainees, whether before the Palestinian Legislative Council or in front of Jneid prison.

  • The Association attended all the sessions of the Legislative Council regarding political detention.

  • The Association sent many letters to Jerusalem Open University regarding detainees continuation of their education. A meeting was held with the chancellor of the university, Mr. Diab Ayoush on 3rd May 1999, in which he promised to facilitate the continuation of the detainees' education.

  • The Association participated, within the framework of the activities of the Council of Palestinian Human Rights Organizations, in many meetings with concerned parties or with the Commission on Supervision of Human Rights in the Legislative Council to discuss the issue of arbitrary (political) arrests and the various violations of human rights.
