LONDON - Eight British peace activists who were trapped in the West Bank city of Ramallah during the Israeli offensive returned home Sunday to the cheers of about 100 Palestinians at Heathrow Airport.

"Victory to intifada," or Palestinian uprising," the Palestinians shouted. Referring to Israeli leader Ariel Sharon (news - web sites), they also screamed: "Sharon, you will see, Palestine will be free," and "Sharon, terrorist."

At one point, the supporters of the Sussex University students also shouted insults at Orthodox Jews who were passing through the terminal.

Many of the crowd held Palestinian flags, Union Jacks and clutched red roses that they threw toward the activists. The boisterous crowd, monitored by police officers, ignored requests to stop shouting.

The eight activists had joined the Palestinian General Medical Relief Council and been trapped in Ramallah when it was placed under curfew by the Israeli military offensive in the city.
The International Solidarity Movement aims to support the Palestinians by working in a nonviolent fashion to bring about change.

One of the Sussex University students, Salma Karmi, 21, of London, said: "We went out on March 28 as guests of the Palestinian people and the very next morning the city of Ramallah was invaded. We were curfewed, and tanks and snipers watched every move anyone made. The Palestinian people are being treated like animals — if not worse than animals."
Dan Glazebrook, 24, president of Sussex University Students Union, said: "We went there to document the Israeli occupation of Palestinian territories. Nothing short of a campaign of terror is being waged against the Palestinian people by the Israeli armed forces."

Other British peace campaigners still living in the West Bank have said they are determined to remain there, despite warnings from the British Consulate that they may be in danger from Palestinians.

Liz Yates, a 61-year-old member of the International Solidarity Movement from Wateringbury in Kent, is one of an estimated 10 to 20 Britons living in three Palestinian refugee camps in Bethlehem.

The retired nurse said, "We are staying with Palestinians. They are feeding us and looking after us. I don't think there's any danger. I would be absolutely amazed if a Palestinian came up and did anything to me."
