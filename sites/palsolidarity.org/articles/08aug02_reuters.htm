INTERNATIONALS BRUTALIZED AND ARRESTED BY MILITARY FOLLOWING NON-VIOLENT DEMONSTRATION

- Urgent Appeal for Equipment Donations

Last Training Day Changed to Friday 13 Aug.

 

JERUSALEM (Reuters) - Israeli police said on Thursday they had arrested nine foreign activists during a protest against military curfews in Palestinian cities in the West Bank and efforts were under way to deport at least some of them.

"There were nine foreigners arrested near Hawara (in the West Bank). The Interior Ministry is taking care of preparing deportation orders," a police spokesman said.

An army spokesman said the foreigners were among scores of demonstrators who blocked a road out of Hawara near the city of Nablus and refused to leave the site, spurring a violent confrontation with troops who had orders to remove them.

Fellow activists who were at the protest said the army used force to disperse them, including tear gas and stun grenades.

The foreign activists included three Americans, five French nationals and an Irish citizen. They are members of the International Solidarity Movement (ISM), which has staged regular protests against an Israeli military crackdown on a 22-month-old Palestinian uprising. One of the detainees is U.S. citizen Adam Shapiro, who drew international attention when he entered Palestinian President Yasser Arafat ( news - web sites)'s headquarters in the West Bank city of Ramallah last April while it was under siege.

"To the best of my knowledge the deportation orders have been issued. They took them to the airport and revoked their visas," said ISM activist Huwaida Arraf, who is Shapiro's wife.

"We've tried appealing deportation cases before and it hasn't worked," she said. "We will continue to protest this suppression, keeping internationals out from seeing abuses of international law."
