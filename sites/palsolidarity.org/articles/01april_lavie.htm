JERUSALEM - A U.S. activist said Tuesday that she will carry on her protests against the Israeli military campaign in the West Bank despite coming under Israeli fire.

Kate Raphael said was at the front of a group of about 200 pro-Palestinian demonstrators marching from Bethlehem to neighboring Beit Jalla on Monday. She said a soldier in an Israeli armored vehicle approached them and opened fire.

"He fired two shots in the air," she said. "Then he fired into the ground."

Six of the marchers were hurt by flying rocks and debris when the bullets hit the road and walls alongside, she said. All were treated at Beit Jalla hospital and released within a few hours, she said.

Raphael, 42, from Berkeley, California, said the group was organized by the International Solidarity Movement, a Palestinian group. The original plan was to march on Israeli checkpoints, dismantle roadblocks and replant olive trees uprooted by Israelis in the West Bank.

"Now, because there's a war on, what we're doing now is more human shields," she told The Associated Press by telephone from the Aida refugee camp next to Bethlehem, where members of the group spent the night after the gunfire incident.

The group represents eight nations and includes about two dozen Americans, an organizer said. It started its activities on Friday.

Israel embarked on a large-scale offensive in the West Bank after a series of deadly Palestinian suicide bomb attacks, including one on the first night of Passover that killed 22 people celebrating the holiday at a seaside hotel.

Israel said its goal is to stamp out Palestinian terrorism. On Monday, Palestinian Cabinet minister Saeb Erekat called the Israeli actions "state terrorism."

On Monday, Israel ordered the deportation of 11 French activists from another group who defied an Israeli ban and walked into Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat (news - web sites)'s office, besieged since Friday by Israeli tanks and troops.

Raphael's group planned to meet Tuesday and decide on further protests, she said. "My personal feeling is that we have to go back out and have another march and show that we are not going to be intimidated and that the Palestinian people are not going to be intimidated," she said.

Raphael said she was brought up in a pro-Israel Jewish household, but discovered later that what she had learned about the creation of the state of Israel "just wasn't true."

Arabs and Israel dispute the circumstances of the creation of the Jewish state in 1948. The Jews see it as a return to their historic homeland, while Arabs believe they were made to pay the price for the murder of 6 million Jews by the Nazis in World War II by sacrificing their land for a Jewish state.

In a two-year war that followed, about 700,000 Palestinians fled or were evicted from their homes in Israel. The Palestinians demand the right of the refugees and their descendants to return to their original homes; the Israelis reject that, offering compensation instead.

Because of her Jewish Zionist upbringing, Raphael said, "I feel a direct responsibility for the horrible situation that the Palestinians are in because I feel like, unwittingly, I was part of the problem."
