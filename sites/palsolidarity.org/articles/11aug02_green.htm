"It is the sound of a multifloor cement home being reduced to rubble.

When you wake up to this sound your heart sinks and seems to merge with a the fear that is rolling around in your stomach."

Chris and Carl Williams

- Urgent Appeal for Equipment Donations

 

The news of the Jerusalem suicide bombing that claimed seven lives last week was splashed across the headlines of American newspapers. The fact that five of the dead at Hebrew University were American makes it particularly easy
for U.S. readers to grasp the human dimensions of the tragedy. President Bush responded by saying, "I'm furious that innocent life has been lost," before offering Israel a green light for commensurate bloodletting. ("Israel must defend herself.")

This contrasts with the mild disapproval expressed by Bush spokesman Ari Fleischer when Israel destroyed an apartment building in Gaza last month, killing 15 Palestinians, including eight children, to assassinate a Hamas military leader. Bush has now framed U.S. support for Israel in terms of his global war on terror.

I suppose it can be said that the citizens of Israel, unlike their Palestinian counterparts in the occupied territories, are victims of terror, because "terror" implies political violence that maximizes its effect through uncertainty and surprise. By that definition, the Israeli Defense Forces are not terrorists because the military violence that Palestinians are subjected to is relentless and inescapable.

In many ways, I identify with the sense of solidarity and commitment that galvanized those five Americans killed at Hebrew University, while my sympathies lie in a much different place. I traveled to the Middle East last month to join the International Solidarity Movement, an organization that supports the Palestinian struggle against Israel's 35-year occupation of the West Bank and Gaza, through non-violent action -- a struggle whose legitimacy is reinforced by U.N. Resolution 37/43.

Along with other internationals from the United States, Western Europe and Japan, I put my body in front of Israeli soldiers to discourage attacks on unarmed Palestinians. As a citizen of a country that supplies Israel with some of the most advanced weaponry on earth through a $3-billion-a-year military package, my life is somehow deemed to be more valuable than the lives of my Palestinian peers.

While visiting the West Bank, I spent a week in Beit Umar, a small Palestinian agricultural community north of Hebron, where farmers are coping with a double tragedy of crop spoilage because of Israel's closure policy and land loss due to the expansion of the Jewish settlements.

In that week, my group witnessed two Israeli military exercises in which soldiers backed by armored personnel carriers paraded through town just before dusk, shooting into the air and beating any Palestinians who had the audacity to stand on the street to observe the Israelis' behavior.

Inevitably, there were children, some no more than 10 years old, who responded to this humiliating situation by throwing rocks at the soldiers, who then fired tear gas and live ammunition at the children. In neither case did we observe return fire from Palestinian militias. (It must be mentioned that no suicide bombing operations have been launched from Beit Umar.)

In the aftermath of these exercises, we found bullet holes in the windows of Palestinian homes, punctured water tanks and downed electrical lines. Other international observers confirm that the military conducts similar maneuvers throughout the occupied territories. What are the military objectives of these operations? I do not like to use the word "terrorize" because it is used in a manipulative fashion to control public opinion, though the word comes close to describing the experience of helplessly watching soldiers fire on children armed only with stones, wondering where you will go if those guns turn in your direction. "This is our normal life" was the constant refrain of the Palestinians we met. So it is true that what they experience is nothing like the terror experienced in Israel, where placid, affluent neighborhoods are occasionally interrupted by explosions of violence.

In Beit Umar, we heard countless stories of young Palestinian men being gunned down in front of their shops or walking along settler-only bypass roads, of Palestinian homes being shot into by Israeli soldiers and settlers alike, of homes being demolished by the military for arbitrary and gratuitous reasons, and of Palestinian men being detained by the military, where they were beaten and pressured to become informers. The purpose of these attacks against a largely unarmed and defenseless population can only be to intimidate dissent, to destroy hope for the future -- in effect, to compel submission to a more humiliating occupation.

We must begin to favor justice for all the world's inhabitants over blind support for the domination of the strong over the weak.

We could start by halting the flow of U.S. weapons to Israel, which are grievously put to use to take innocent lives.

* * *

Jordan Green
Columbia School of Journalism
