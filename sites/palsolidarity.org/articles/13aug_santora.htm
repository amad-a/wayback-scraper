A medical-aid worker from Brooklyn who was trapped in Yasir Arafat's compound overnight in April returned home yesterday, after several days in an Israeli prison.

The worker, Adam Shapiro, 30, faces deportation and various charges in Israel as a result of a protest march he participated in. He left Israel on Sunday of his own accord, he said.

When Mr. Shapiro was trapped in the Arafat compound, he shared breakfast with Mr. Arafat, and the story inflamed the divisive debate associated with the Middle East in Brooklyn, where his parents live.

He was detained on Wednesday as he tried to escort Palestinians from their village, Hawara, to the town of Nablus, a few miles to the north, to protest road closings, he said.

"We were physically assaulted by armed gunmen from Israel's paramilitary border police in the heart of the West Bank," Mr. Shapiro said at Kennedy International Airport.

Dr. Raanan Gissin, a spokesman for Prime Minister Ariel Sharon of Israel, denied that Mr. Shapiro had been treated improperly. "The fact that this is still under investigation implies that there was more than just an innocent walk going on."

An Israeli military source, who spoke on the condition of anonymity, said soldiers had to use force to move the protesters after they chained themselves to a checkpoint.

Mr. Shapiro said that he abhorred terrorism and that the group had been walking peacefully.

When his father, Stuart Shapiro, arrived at the airport yesterday, he threw his arms around his son and repeated, his voice cracking, "It's good to have you home."

After receiving a master's degree in Arabic studies at Georgetown University, Mr. Shapiro spent a year at a language school in Yemen. He later went to Israel, where he was a coordinator for the International Solidarity Movement, which organizes nonviolent protests against the Israeli military presence in the West Bank. In April, he talked his way past Israeli soldiers to deliver medical supplies at Mr. Arafat's compound in Ramallah.

While Mr. Shapiro said he wanted to fight the charges he faced in Israel, he said he was starting a doctoral program at American University in Washington in a few weeks.
