INTERNATIONALS BRUTALIZED AND ARRESTED BY MILITARY FOLLOWING NON-VIOLENT DEMONSTRATION

- Urgent Appeal for Equipment Donations

Last Training Day Changed to Friday 13 Aug.

 

Last night about midnight we were out on the terrace of Ali's house, one of the many friends we have made in the short time we have been here Fara camp, when we heard a helicopter hovering about the hillside. It seemed to drop something off, like landing lights. We were intrigued but our Palestinian friends were more interested in getting us to the table to taste the traditional supper of watermelon and goats cheese. Such sinister events are commonplace here and arouse little excitement in the camp where people are striving to carry on their lives in as normal a way as possible in these conditions, as much for themselves as for the thousands of children who have nowhere to play and very little to play with. So we went to sleep about an hour later and awoke in the morning to find that the helicopter had dropped in Special Forces who arrested and took away two young men aged 20 and 22 to god-knows-where.

The very idea of sending in 'Special Forces' to a camp where the only weapons are stones is so preposterous that I fail to find an analogy. The first possible reason for such ‘covert’ and exaggerated military action could be to avoid the incident being filmed or eyewitnessed. The second possible reason as suggested to me today by another ISM activist is much more sickening. She reminded me that Israel must be seen to be fighting a 'war', and as such must behave as countries 'at war' behave - using high-tech military hardware and strategy, as much for its reputation as for the morale of its soldiers. It is laughable, but it is also criminal.

So, feeling very disappointed that we had failed to prevent the arrest of these two young men, we took a short trip to the nearby villages of Tubas and Tammun where the Israeli army had been making its presence very clear in the previous nine days. There are no internationals staying in these villages and I would like to think that if there had been the level of destruction might not have been so intense and of such a humiliating nature. We first visited Tubas, where we saw the bulldozed remains of three Palestinian Authority buildings and where the local school had been used as a 'detention centre' by the invading army. If Sharon is so concerned that Arafat 'controls' his people, why is he systematically destroying the very infrastructure that would facilitates this authority?

In Tammun the invasion lasted two days, during which the village was without water, as they rely upon Tubas for their water supply (invasion implies curfew). During this 'short stay' the IDF broke into the school, town hall and health centre. The severest damage was done to the health centre. During these two days the army used the health centre as a detention centre for interrogation and beatings. The viciousness of the vandalism is repulsive. In addition to the crime of entering a health centre and using it in this way, the compounded the horror by ransacking the building, including the medicine room, where they mixed up tablets and made much of the store unusable, and in the vaccination clinic, where they opened the fridge and left it that way rendering the contents ineffective. They defecated on the bathroom floor, pulled sterile equipment out of cabinets and on to the floor, and broke some of the doors down. In the town hall they broke through the front door in spite of the presence of an employee who offered them the keys. When they left the town they took 9 men with them, once again, to god-knows-where. Tammun is a town of 16,000 people. For this invasion, the army employed twelve tanks. The appropriation of humanitarian centres for military use is as illegal as every other element of this occupation.

The secondary school in Tammun is being used as an exam hall for the local girls' school also. They have a week of exams in the same hall where they must stick a photographic ID card to their desks as part of the regulations. During their occupation of the school the soldiers took all these photos from the desks. The photos are gone and the community fears that the IDF will digitally rework these pictures to make culturally offensive pornographic images with which to blackmail one or more families into collaboration. It has happened before.

For us, one of the most frustrating aspects of this situation is that the media is not allowed to enter what has been classified as a 'closed military zone'. In Nablus yesterday, two Palestinian journalists were 'detained' (arrested) along with one of our fellow ISM activists during a sit-down protest in front of an invading tank. We are doing as much as we can to film the damage, interview eyewitnesses and record events as they happen. We ISM activists are the only international witnesses to these daily crimes.
