INTERNATIONALS BRUTALIZED AND ARRESTED BY MILITARY FOLLOWING NON-VIOLENT DEMONSTRATION

- Urgent Appeal for Equipment Donations


 

Our group of 10 is back in Jerusalem. We were on our way from Ramallah this morning when word came that a nearby refugee camp, Al-Ama'ari, was under siege by the Israeli army. The army was going house to house to detain all men and boys. 15 internationals from ISM – the international solidarity movement- headed out for the camp and were later joined by French, Swiss and Italians from other groups. We encountered soldiers entering homes on the crooked dusty street and broke off into groups, leaving a group of three in a home of an old man, several women and many children that had not yet been entered. Palestinians called to us from windows and down alleys, directing us further on in the camp to where the men were being held. Rounding a corner we saw a large field set up with tents and open space. 200-300 men and boys were seated in groups surrounded by soldiers. In the street were tanks and armored personnel carriers. We identified ourselves as human rights workers concerned about the conditions under which the men were being held. The soldiers tried to get us to leave. We reminded them that the occupation is illegal under international law and that our concern was about their prisoners. One of our group was allowed to go into the field and was able to see that the men sitting in the very hot sun indeed had water. After a soldier fired his gun in the air to frighten us, our group began to retreat back into the camp where children gathering in the streets and alleys motioned for us to cover our ears. Shortly there was a deafening blast as the soldiers blew up a grocery store one street away. More kids came out of their houses to surround us, as did their mothers.
They were speaking all at once, clearly grateful that we were there although it was also clear that we could not do more than just let them know by our presence that they are not alone, that there are people from around the world who are observing and recording. This is apparently the third time the army has come into this camp to round up the men. As we were re-grouping, we saw a boy, maybe age 13 or 14, with his hands tied behind his back being brought into a building by the soldiers. His father was walking beside him, alternately re-assuring the boy and talking to the soldiers. Some of our group and some other internationals managed to enter the house with them. We don't know yet what came of that situation.

The occupation takes a terrible toll in so many ways. Yesterday we broke the curfew to visit a rehabilitation center and heard the stories of children and adults who were shot while going to work, or playing in the yard, or working the fields. The stories are of course heart-breaking. To compound the situation, the staff of the center, including the director, must stay over for days because of the 24-hour curfew which often lasts 3 or 4 days and then lifts for a few hours. Often they don't go home even when curfew is lifted because to do so means waiting at checkpoints so long that they would not make it back in time.

Tomorrow our group of 10 is off to Gaza to lend an international presence to a project to repair a sewer line that has been approved by the local Israeli authority but forbidden by the army. We are hoping that our being there will strengthen the pressure to allow the local Palestinians to repair the sewer.
