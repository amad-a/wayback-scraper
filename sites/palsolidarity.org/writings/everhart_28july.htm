- Urgent Appeal for Equipment Donations

 

On the edge of a long dirt road lies a row of plum trees with rotted plums scattered at the foot of the trees. On the other side of the dirt road lies Karmit Zur, an Israeli settlement in the middle of the West Bank, near the farming town of Beit Ommar. Ibrahim, a Palestinian farmer shows us his land. He points to his land where the split by a freshly made dirt road, “That is my land”, then he turns towards the Settlement across a barber wire fence, “so is that.” Ibrahim’s fruit is rotting on the trees because he cannot come to his land to harvest it. Normally, when he attempts to come to his land, he is chased off by settler security who yells at him and / or shoots at him with their Uzis.

Today about 10 internationals are accompanying Ibrahim to his fields, but he has no real hopes that it will change much. His plums have already rotted and it is too late to spray for the grapes which should be ready by next month. As we stand in the hot sun, we see a soldier start running towards us in the distance. He comes up to us and tells us that we can’t be there. “There is a security area here”, he tells us. “You have to be at least 300 meters away from the security fence”.

Ibrahim steps forward, “But this is my land, can’t I harvest it?”

“No, how do we know you are not a terrorist? You know that someone came in here last month and killed people here, now go back!”

Ibrahim looks at the young soldier, “But this has always been my land and you can see that I have no weapons.”

“Look I think you are right.” The young man finally admits to us, “but I can’t do anything about it. I am just following orders”.

One of the other internationals steps up, “And if you were given orders to shoot these people, would you follow them?”

“Look, I’m not going to shoot these people, but I am gong to enforce the law which says you can’t be here”

Then, Settler security arrives and suddenly things are much more tense. They immediately question the Palestinians and detain one man, separating him from the rest of the group and forcing him to sit in the hot sun. We inquire about the man, but the settlers are less friendly than the soldier and they try to get us away from where the man is. I sit on a rock in eyesight of the man along with a couple other Internationals. A settler comes up to us demanding that we move. “You must go!” The large man with an Uzi tells me. He lifts up his hand as if to slap me across the face, but is soon distracted and walks away. Eventually, after a half an hour of sitting in the sun, the man in released. His crime, not carrying his Identification with him.

We walk away from the fields as fruit falls to the ground and the Palestinian farmers of Beit Ommar still can’t return to their land.
