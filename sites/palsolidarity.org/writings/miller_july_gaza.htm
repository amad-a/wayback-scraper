Hi All,

Today I'm in the mid-part of the Gaza Strip. Yesterday, we were taken to a checkpoint where many hundreds of cars filled with probably 1000 people waited in the broiling sun for an Israeli in a concrete bunker to waive his hand and allow them to pass. They waited in dusty acres, which had recently been a thriving, productive orange grove. Our group of ten left our van at the end of an ever growing line, three vehicles wide, of trucks, buses, private cars, taxis, pickups piled with household contents and goods for sale, an ambulance with a boy obviously ill and his family, and UN vehicles packed with women and men in Muslim dress.

We 10 made our way to the front of the line where we moved forward slowly with our hands and passports stretched over our heads. We addressed the soldiers in the concrete tower telling them our van was at the end of the line and we were on our way to Rafah in the southern part of Gaza. The soldiers shouted that they would allow the cars to go in five minutes. While we waited, we spoke to Palestinians at the front of the line who asked us what we thought of this situation. We were clear that we were opposed to the occupation and that we were trying to do what we could to inform the world about what was happening. We also told them that five of us are Jews. We have been told again and again that Palestinians understand the difference between people and their governments. This time, one man told us he has met with Noam Chomsky and understands that all Jews are not supporters of the Israeli occupation. The soldiers waved a hand and the cars began to go through with no checking of identity cards, no search of vehicles. There seemed to be no reason for people to have been held up in that field for five hours except to degrade and humiliate. This goes on every day and sometimes for days at a time. A nearby Palestinian farmer has put up a tent and made a place for people who have to wait for many hours or sometimes for several nights for the checkpoint to open. After about ten minutes, the hand waived and the cars were halted. We approached the tower with our cameras and the hand waived again.

After I finished cursing, I wept right out there in that hot dusty field, with anger and shame, that people, that Jews can behave in this way, with weapons and ammunition paid for with my taxes. I cannot begin to express how I feel. I am grateful for the chance to be here with people who are humanitarian human rights activists throwing pebbles in the path of Goliath. Somebody has to do something and I am proud to be one of the somebodies.

Gail
