I wanted to start out with some quotes I had with someone I met from Dair El-Balah (a village at the southern end of the middle area of the Gaza strip). He's a teacher and had some things to say which struck me powerfully. His name is Abel.

"We don't ask the moon we just ask peace"

Speaking about Israeli bulldozing of orchards:

"cutting trees, is this normal? When I cut a piece of a tree I feel pain."

"They use tanks against unarmed people but the Palestinian people will never submit."

Referring to an assassination attempt in Khan Yunis two days ago (still not sure if they actually got the person they were aiming for though they definitely killed at least one):

"This morning they destroyed a court of justice. By doing this they destroyed justice."

Obviously I don't agree with his opinion on this next one but I think its a powerful statement nonetheless and totally understandable:

"I don't believe that Hitler killed 6 million Jews because if he did they would not be doing this to us."

Just a few quotes from a random Palestinian I met. As I've said before I want to try to communicate as many direct quotes from Palestinians as I can.

A little bit of updates on my own personal situation:

As I said before I am now the International Solidarity Movement Organizer for Gaza. Pretty crazy, who'd of thought I'd get a new job title while I was here? I changed my ticket today to return September 18th to LA. It was a crazy decision to make but I feel like
there's a lot of important work to be done here and in the end decided that since I've spent so much money to get here (again thank you to all of you who helped to make that possible through your generous support) I might as well ride out my three month visa since I don't know when I'll have that kind of money or time again. I miss LA already but that's okay. Most of my time this summer will obviously be spent in Gaza, I was looking forward to going to Jenin which was what the plan was before I was asked to stay here but I've always said I will go where I'm needed most and there's no questioning the fact that there is a lot of need and desire for internationals in Gaza even if there aren't tanks on every street corner.

I don't remember where I left off in the second to last email update but I'll start with my second trip to Rafah which I'm sure was after that update.

We travelled to Rafah on 7/12. We met again with the Children's Parliament there and I learned a lot more about what the organization does there. They work with children 11-16 years old. They organize letter writing campaigns, provide services to children, particularly psychological services, organize exchange trips to other countries including Germany, Lebanon and Egypt (Children travel from Rafah to these countries and they bring children from these countries to Rafah) to promote contact between children internationally. The main organizer is wanted by Israel for inciting children. He's also not on Arafat's most favored list as Arafat has imprisoned him in the past for refusing to heed Arafat's orders. The children are amazing and are so amazing to interact with. They can't understand how the international community allows them to suffer under occupation the way they do.

Note to YOC folks: I mentioned my role as a youth organizer in Los Angeles to the organization and they are very interested in setting up youth to youth ties with folks in the US. I'll talk to people about this when I return but just something to think about in the meantime.

This night I attempted to stay in the Brazil area of the refugee camp with some of the same friends I had met there earlier in a home threatened with demolition. We walked to the home at night but when we got there they became extremely frightened (more for us than for themselves. We have found that Palestinians put the safety of internationals before there own all the time) The family had left the home and was staying with relatives and so they convinced us not to stay there either as we wouldn't really be helping to be present with anyone anyway. When we were near the house we saw the Israeli's fire two flares in the air. These are used to light up an area. It usually means that some kind of heavy military operation will follow in the night. We walked to one of their friends places to spend the night in the town of Rafah. We passed the night without further event. This really made me angry. I can't imagine living like this, never knowing if your home will be destroyed in the night. Never knowing when bullets will tear through your home. Living in constant fear. Whole families forced to leave their homes even before they are destroyed because their afraid of the house being brought down on top of them. Again I ask how can anyone do this to another human being?
