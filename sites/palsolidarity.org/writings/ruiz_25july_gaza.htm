Archives

When you wake up to this sound your heart sinks and seems to merge with a the fear that is rolling around in your stomach."

- Urgent Appeal for Equipment Donations

 

Hello all,

I wanted to start out today with a description and quotes from a wonderful woman, Hanadi, I met a few days ago doing amazing work here in Rafah. Again, names are not actual.

Hanadi has agreed to help the International Solidarity Movement coordinate our work here in the southern area of the Gaza Strip. Her father & 6 uncles were killed during the war in 1967. After this she was raised in an orphanage in Gaza City. She is actually divorced (really a quite rare thing in this society, particularly because the divorce came because of her unhappiness with the situation). She is an amazing painter, she has shown me paintings dealing with the occupation, the political situation in Palestine and the role of women within Islam. Before being made refugees by the establishment of Israel in 1948 her family was from the village Beshet (not sure of that spelling) now inside green line Israel but close to Ramallah. We talked for several hours the other day and I wanted to again give you some direct quotes from a Palestinian:

"I hope to travel to Jerusalem just one time." (at most she lives 3 hours from Jerusalem by car)

"We live in a great prison"

"I am from Fatah, but the fair & just part of Fatah, not the corrupt part"

"George Bush comes from the wealthy class. He looks down at people from high up and we are the lowest. He does not want the lowest people to live."

She is just one of the amazing Palestinians I have met.

Yesterday, 7/24, the group spent a good portion of the day at an orphanage in Rafah called SOS Village. There are SOS Villages all over the world. They were started by an Austrian during World War II and this model of orphanages has spread widely. There are 3 SOS villages inside green line Israel (read: they know exactly what this orphanage does, it is the same as the institutions in Israel, down to the same logo on the sign).

About 60 orphans live there, there is a school for 100 and a Kindergarten for 360. In meeting with the administration they were extremely careful with us to continuously say they were not a political organization, that they had nothing to say about politics, they were just there to help children. They did not even want to be interviewed by a journalist with us who's doing a story on the mental health of Palestinian children. They didn't even tell us that they had been attacked by the Israeli military, we only figured it out when we saw the bullet holes ourselves. All this is to say they in no way say anything critical of Israel nor do they advocate anything at all on behalf of Palestinians.

After talking with the administration we went outside to look around the grounds. As I said, we began to notice bullet holes in the buildings and asked questions about them. The orphanage had received heavy gunfire the previous night. It wasn't the first night that had happened. We found out the orphanage is located about 50 meters from the Gush Kutif block of settlements. This is the block of settlements that cuts off the southern part of the Gaza Strip from the ocean. They receive gunfire periodically from the North, the West and the South. Only the East is safe from gunfire. There were bullet holes in the buildings where children live, where they eat, in the classrooms(including several fresh bullet holes from the night before which had left plaster splattered all over the classrooms and we even found the bullet still in one of the classrooms) and even in the tower, which says SOS Village and also holds the water tanks (the water tanks had not actually been shot the night before but they had been shot before and had already been replaced). Once again, this exact same institution exists in three places in Israel. Why would they shoot at it? Why would they terrorize these children who have already been so traumatized? What do they think? How do you condition a human being in such a way that they could act like this? I thought
demolishing homes in refugee camps was the worst thing I could think of, but shooting at orphanages and their supplies of water. Once again my own beliefs of there not really being good and evil in the world is sorely put to the test because if anything is evil, this is it!

Today we spent a few hours in the middle of the day at a protest organized by the Children's Parliament in Rafah against the massacre in Gaza City a few days ago. They had asked the three of us still in Gaza to come and support them particularly because we were US citizens and the F-16's and the missile(s) came from the US. The demonstration was amazing. 200+ children chanting, holding up pictures of martyrs, holding up statements denouncing the United States and Israel and generally creating a ruckus for the media that was present. We chanted with them, held our signs denouncing the US and did a few interviews. When I say children I'm talking about like 7-15 or so. Young children pissed off by the way they are being treated by Israel and who understand the relationship between Israel and the United States. Amazing. It was very inspiring.

Great stuff! Keep up the fight! Good luck to the Jewish Group in LA erecting a checkpoint outside the Israeli Consulate later today. We need more of that all over.

Love and Rage from Occupied Palestine,

Garrick
