INTERNATIONALS BRUTALIZED AND ARRESTED BY MILITARY FOLLOWING NON-VIOLENT DEMONSTRATION

- Urgent Appeal for Equipment Donations

Last Training Day Changed to Friday 13 Aug.

 

Jim and I woke before everyone in the flat this morning, and decided to go the market to buy supplies for the day’s cooking. As we tossed garlic and chili peppers into our bag, the now too familiar sounds of machine gun fire filled the air. People began running and vendors quickly packed their fare. We had thought that curfew had been lifted from 8 to 8, but the IDF apparently decided that was too generous.

Jim and I couldn't make passage to the flat, so we hid in the marketplace until we could make phone contact with the rest of our flat mates (there are ten men and myself). We all met at the city center, and were soon confronted by tanks. Young children followed after the tanks throwing small stones. A symbolic gesture of defiance, as a stone couldn't barely begin to dent these dinosaur sized tanks. The soldiers threw out a sound grenade which frightened everyone because it was black instead of the usual bright orange.

As the tank moved on we followed to provide some protection for the children who were still on the streets. I had to struggle to keep up, as we were running quite quickly at some points. Youel looked back at me, "Someone is going to get shot today." He announced. My eyes turned fierce. "Don't say that!" I insisted. As I turned round a corner, the tank opened fired on the street. I saw our men hit the ground and I hid myself. It was chaos, and then the tank rumbled on. Suddenly, I felt my throat and chest burning. Tear gas. But, we only got it in whiffs as the tank rolled away.

Welcome to Jenin. The rules are different here, with the soldiers, with the locals. It's a whole new horror story, but I feel really honored to be here, and maybe get the chance to help some.

Tamara

(I'm leaving right now for the Red Crescent where I hope to get a job on the ambulances or perhaps work at the hospital on the maternity floor.)
