INTERNATIONALS BRUTALIZED AND ARRESTED BY MILITARY FOLLOWING NON-VIOLENT DEMONSTRATION

- Urgent Appeal for Equipment Donations

Last Training Day Changed to Friday 13 Aug.

 

This morning, at the request of organizer Ali Abu Sawish, from the National Association for Services and Development, our ISM delegation went to the Municipality Office of Nusairat. There, we met with the Mayors of Nusairat and Al Buriej, as well as a Minister for Technology in Nusairat. These men explained for us the water situation in the Middle Area of Gaza. While we are still piecing together the story, this is what we've learned (forgive the spelling errors).

The water distribution for the Middle Area (MA) comes from two main wells, with two supplementary wells for the different camps. The three municipalities in the MA rotate control of the main wells. Unfortunately, since the start of this Intifada (about two years) one of the main wells was damaged by an Israeli military bulldozer. They are quite close to the border of the Net Sarim settlement. Workers attempting to perform maintenance on the working well have been shot at consistently. The broken well is 6 meters from the settlement fence and no one has been able to get near it to assess needed repairs. We hear unconfirmed reports of land mines near the well. These main wells not only provide the majority of the water for the MA (2000 cubic meters/day per well), but this is the best water available, pumped from underneath the Net Sarim settlement. The supplementary wells have high salinity, as well as a high nitrate count. (chloride - 1000mg/cubic meter - should be less than 250 nitrates - 100mg/cubic meter - should be less than 50).

At one time, there was a grant from U.S.A.I.D. to build a desalination plant. This grant would have only covered capital costs of building the plant. But the cost of water would have risen above $3.00/cubic meter and the local population can only afford $1.00/cubic meter. (Plus the difficulties of waste disposal, as a result of the desalination process.) The municipalities have also attempted to negotiate with Mikharot, the Israeli water company, for additional water, but Mikharot would not agree to sell adequate quantities for Gaza. The struggle for water in the Occupied Territories is not news, nor are the difficulties of water privatization globally. This, among many, is a concrete demonstration of a people being denied access to the basic human right of adequate water.

We, as a delegation of internationals from the U.S., Canada and Britain, chose to approach the Israeli army, as our first tactic in a campaign to address the problem of this well. 11 of us walked into the entrance of Net Sarim, passports and hands high. We told the soldiers that our plan was to escort the workers to the broken well, but that we wanted to inform the soldiers in order to guarantee the safety of the workers. Not surprisingly, they were unable to do so and referred us to the D.O.C. They refused to give us their names or the names of any commanding officers. We are still attempting to negotiate the web of bureaucracy that has ensued, but do not have much hope for this. We are willing to escort the workers, but without secured permission, they are understandably fearful of taking this step. We see this as a long-term campaign that will most likely outlast our brief stay here.
