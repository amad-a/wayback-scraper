INTERNATIONALS BRUTALIZED AND ARRESTED BY MILITARY FOLLOWING NON-VIOLENT DEMONSTRATION

- Urgent Appeal for Equipment Donations

Last Training Day Changed to Friday 13 Aug.

 

I wanted to start today with what I learned about the colors of the Palestinian flag. Then talk about last night and then go on to other things.

Hopefully everyone has seen the flag, from the top a stripe of black, a stripe of white, a stripe of green with a red triangle cutting through them all from the left. The red symbolizes the revolutionary nature of the struggle, the green stripe represents the peaceful time before occupation, the white stripe represents the desire for peace and the black stripe above the rest represents the darkness that has covered all of Palestine since the Israelis came. Powerful stuff. I had never heard the symbolism before.

Last night was a mess all over as people may have heard. But I'll start out with what was closest to home for me. Last night the International Solidarity Movement group here in Gaza was meeting in the building we have been sleeping in. By 10:00 pm we had been meeting for about two hours. Suddenly we heard fighters streak by overhead. They were most likely F-16's (provided by the United States of course). We've heard fighters overhead a lot since we've been here but these were the lowest flying we'd yet heard. It was really loud. They passed almost directly over us at least 3 or 4 times. On one of the passes we heard a soft pop and I thought I felt the building shake a little bit, though that may have been my imagination. Within 5 minutes Palestinians came up to us and told us that a factory had been bombed very near to us. The Israeli's apparently used something called a silent bomb which doesn't make a loud noise. I'd never heard of this delightful weapon before.

Several of us walked to the area that had been bombed. It was less than a 10-minute walk from where we sleep most nights, maybe 300 meters from us. Some of us were at the site less than 25 minutes after the bombing happened. As we walked up, we could see that
the two buildings that had been hit were just torn apart. It was two factories which shared one wall. One was a factory which cut slate and tile for flooring. The other was a machine repair shop for farming equipment and other machinery. There was machinery spread out over a huge distance blasted there by the bomb, along with pieces of the buildings and still warm clods of dirt from the blast. I've never seen anything like it. When we got inside the
buildings the scene was one of complete destruction. Stacks of tile and slate, piles of damaged pieces of the stuff, partially and totally destroyed machinery spread out all over. The roof was torn through and metal beams were hanging down to the floor. The actual crater was huge. At least 5 meters across and at least that deep. The place was shattered. Luckily no one had been there when it happened so there were no deaths or injuries.

As I said we arrived less than 25 minutes from when it happened. I believe the Israelis are claiming they hit a bomb making factory. I can personally attest we saw no guns, no bombs, nothing that looked remotely like it could be used to make bombs, and there had
been no secondary explosions to suggest that any type of munitions might have been present. There were simply two civilian facilities totally destroyed.

We were immediately on the phone to other people from the ISM in the West Bank. The person we contacted to report to said he couldn't talk then because he had just seen a kid get his head blown off. We later found out that at least two children had been killed by an explosion in the Al-Amari refugee camp near Ramallah which was what he was referring to. Rumors soon began to circulate amongst the crowds at the bombsite about a suicide bombing in Tel Aviv. I'm sure this is what is being played up in the US but it actually happened after the bombing in Gaza. We also heard reports that Israelis were beating Palestinians near the Al-Aqsa Mosque in the Old City of Jerusalem. Last night was a mess.

I just wanted to comment a little bit on Palestinian culture because I don't think I've done much of that up to this point and last night’s horrors really highlight how amazing it is to me in contrast to the forces of repression and occupation. What amazes me is that so many Palestinians do not turn to violence given all the horrors that are seen here on a daily basis. Palestinians are amazingly friendly, warm and welcoming. It is amazing how I am constantly asked into people's homes, given tea or coffee or other drinks, given huge amounts of really good food, asked to just sit and talk with complete strangers just because they want to get to know me, etc. etc. I have learned not to admire things because anything you admire you are sure to be given. Just one example, yesterday I admired a key chain medallion of a Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine (PFLP) supporter. He immediately attempted to pull the medallion off his key chain and give it to me. Luckily it was stuck so he couldn't get it off. It sounds like he is going to give me a few of the PFLP medallions later today though. I feel bad taking these gifts because people are so poor here but people get upset when you refuse their gifts so it’s easiest not to admire things out loud. People here maintain this amazing culture in spite of the way they are treated every day. It blows me away.

So, I have two more months in this beautiful land which suffers from so many hardships. I am looking forward to it.

I want to write something more broadly about the water situation here in Gaza and the whole story about what we have been doing around it. I don't think I will have time today but hopefully I will find time soon. But I wanted to report on one of our meetings over the last two days.

Yesterday we met with a representative of the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine (PFLP) here in Gaza. The PFLP is the second biggest faction within the Palestinian Liberation Organization (PLO), is a Marxist-Leninist party (to some degree though its policies have shifted in unclear ways over the years) and along with Fatah and Hamas is one of the three major players in Palestinian politics. I'm by no means a Marxist but as far as the big factions go it’s probably the one I find myself most closely aligned with on a personal level. Just to be clear, I try not to let my own personal politics get in the way of my solidarity work and I try never to tell Palestinians the correct way to resist or try to shape a society. I figure they have a lot better shot at that that some dolt from half way around the world and a citizen of the power responsible for their suffering to boot.

The former leader of the Popular Front, Ali Abu Mustafah, was assassinated last year by a missile from an Israeli Apache helicopter. In response the PFLP assassinated the Israeli minister of tourism, one of the most right wing members of the Israeli parliament and one who consistently referred to Palestinians as insects and cockroaches. Their response was labeled terrorism but not the Israeli assassination which caused it. Then their elected leader Ahmed Sadat was placed in prison earlier this year in Jericho under British and US military guard. Just a little bit of recent history.

That said I wanted to write some quotes from that conversation. (These are all translated from Arabic)

"We in the Popular Front support the peace process but with an honest 3rd party such as the U.N., not the U.S."

"Suicide bombings are the right of Palestinians to defend themselves. The real terrorism is the occupation. When occupation ends all suicide bombings will end."

"We are not looking for blood and killing or for ourselves to be killed. We are looking for liberation."

"It is our right to fight against Israel until we get rid of the occupation."

Lots of other stuff has been going on but that's enough for now. I'll try to write again soon.

Love and Rage from Occupied Palestine,

Garrick
