The Citizen Rights Center (CRC) was founded in 1997 by the Arab Thought Forum to assist individual citizens, monitor, and provide feed back on public performance.

BACKGROUND:

The idea behind founding this center is based on the principle of facilitating direct contact between citizens and the authority, and helping the citizen to present his/her claims without feeling of inferiority before government officials. In other words, it aimed at consolidating civil society, taking in to consideration that individuals are the basic components of the society. Citizens, who are afraid of defending their rights and willing to accept injustice, will in turn engender a vulnerable society.

In addition to directly helping solve individual issues, one important and comprehensive goal, was to create an impression with both the citizen and authority that individual’s rights must be respected and protected, and that in case a violation occurs, there will indeed be a possibility for follow-up and rehabilitation through democratic means.

In a very short period of time, the Center has managed to face the basic challenge of gaining recognition and respect from citizens who became to realize and appreciate the importance of the services it provided. This was reflected in the analysis and number and nature of cases the Center had received and the number of citizens approaching it for advice.

The Center also proved its ability to receive the attention and recognition of local and national government institutions. This could be easily noticed from the replies it received from these authorities, which to a large extent had maintained a very cautious approach towards similar interventions in the past.

Added to the above, the ability of the Center to maintain a high quality of professional coordination relations with local and international NGOs working in human rights and democracy in a very competitive environment. One can note the election of the Center to join the secretariat of more than eleven NGOs joining efforts to form the Human Rights NGOs Council.

In sum, the past period witnessed laying stable foundations for a promising future for the Center in terms of maintaining to fulfill its mandate. The current state of affairs in the field of citizens’ rights in Palestine warrant a fully fledged citizen center.

The CRC’ experience demonstrated that there exists a real and profound need not only to maintain a fully functional citizens center, but that there exists a potential to expand its capacity to serve a larger portion of the target population.

OBJECTIVES:

  1. To offer legal assistance to citizens in the areas of the legal process, legislation process, civic and political rights and allegations of the abuse or denial of those rights.
  2. To support citizens in pursuing their rights within the democratic process. i.e. in matters of conflict between the citizen and local or national government.
  3. To offer a direct service to the public to test the flexibility of the democratic process and its reaction to criticism and complaint.
  4. To establish working links with local and national representatives and to offer the CRC as a venue for public/representative meetings.
  5. To promote and nurture the idea of “surgeries” based on the model of professional and confidential exchange between citizens and their representatives.
  6. To provide simple language summary and analysis to the draft legislation that average citizens and regular civil society organizations understand, so that people can get an idea about the legislation and what it means to them.

ANALYSIS:

The aim of the Center is to determine and examine cases that reflect a major trend and thus should be dealt with in a comprehensive approach.

Through analyzing the cases received so far, various general trends were noted:

  1. Prisoners’ Cases: which relates to the denial or abuse of civic rights and undue legal process. For example, torture, detention for unlimited periods without due legal process, illegal arrests, banning lawyers from visiting prisoners, failing to notify relatives on detention and detention in remote areas.
  2. Residency Cases: which relate to inability of an individual to obtain residency permit, unresolved family reunion cases pertaining to returnees, improper handling of residency documents.
  3. Civil Service Cases: related to improper handling of civil service regulations or denial of employment rights.

It is necessary to point out that the Center does not intend to make follow-up to the legal cases at courts, because this will be the responsibility of the citizens and their lawyers. The role of the center is to provide information, advice and guidance.

LEGISLATION UNIT:

This Unit is a new addition to the CRC. It was established to provide simple narratives of the main points sited in approved laws and regulations. These narratives are often reproduced and distributed in the form of booklets or pamphlets.

Two publications on legislation to be presented to citizens in a simplified manner were issue. One on the Civil Service Law and the second on Firearms and Ammunition Law. 

A third publication on Local Government Councils and Local Elections Laws were being drafted. The document will be published soon.
