Greetings to all! I just wanted to make a few comments in response to some of the postings I have read. I would like to defend Project Hope from the many criticisms it has received. For those of you who commend and support it, thank you. First, Project Hope is still in its infantile stages. As a result, it has no been perfected. Furthermore, the nature of life itself will prevent the trip from ever being perfect. Second, in response to the comments about planting a few trees and cleaning the street: yes, cleaning two or three streets and planting a few flowers will not erase the pain in Ramallah, but if you think ahead, it is the GESTURE of doing the act and not the act itself that count. Those trees may be dead by the time we return next summer, but that isn't what is important. Third, I admit, the group spent a huge amount of time touring Palestine. The argument has been made that we did not spend enough time working. There is no need for that argument as us participants felt the same. However, an understanding of the unstable social, political, and economic system in Ramallah would help you understand that many factors were out of our control. There are always higher-ups to answer to. Fourth, to the comment about us working with local youths. That was and is a priority for last year and the upcoming trip. Again, understanding the social climate in Ramallah would support Project Hope's claim. We understood the importance of bridging ties between our youth and Ramallah youth, but as the old cliche goes, "you can lead a horse to water but you cannot make him drink." The Federation is working to have a joint effort with the Boy Scouts and local youth in Ramallah and our youth for next year. Have patience and all of these fine details will be ironed out. Fifth, for those of you that are fortunate enough to spend time in Ramallah summer after summer, you should be the first to take the lead in efforts like Project Hope. You know the locals better than us newcomers did and could have been leaders in initiating the bridging of ties. I was very disappointed that those of you that could have helped Project Hope the most dropped out because you didn't feel it was worth your time. Please, for whatever reasons, reserve your judgement until you have participated fully and even then, hold off judgement of other peoples actions. We are not here to judge. I don't think that spending an afternoon or two with Project Hope renders anyone an expert on what went on. To all the Ramallah youth who have hopes to go, please join Project Hope. We need as many people as possible. Even if our efforts fail the first, second, third, or even tenth time, we have to keep going back. We have to make our presence known and we have to get the locals to warm up to us and want to help us. Is is then and only then that we can make a change. If anyone has any suggestions that they would like heard at the next Federation meeting, please email them to me and I will make sure they are brought up. This comment is not intended to offend, I just want to give my point of view from a Project Hope participant standpoint. All of us agree that Project Hope was far from perfect, but we owe it ourselves and our people not to throw this idea away because it is less than perfect. We cannot give up on Project Hope. Thank you all.
