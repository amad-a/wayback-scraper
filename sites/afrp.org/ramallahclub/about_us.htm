The Ramallah American Club of Jacksonville, Florida was founded in 1953 when
52 Arab American men of Ramallah, Palestine chartered the club as a social, cultural,
and charitable organization.  Without a building to call their own, club members
began to meet in the old Salaam Club which is the Syrian American Club of
Jacksonville.  With the help of the late Ziadeh A. Bateh, the club purchased its first
facility in 1959 on Atlantic Blvd.  This unpretentious house located on what was rural
road became the first place we could call home.  In 1962 a club hall was created at
the rear of the house with additional land purchased in 1963 at the same location.
This completed the physical plan of the original club.  As the years progressed, the
membership outgrew the original club so in 1975 five acres of land were purchased
on Parental Home Road for the site of a new facility.  In 1976 the club sold its old
home and soon thereafter construction began at our new location.  On October 29,
1977 the new club was opened and dedicated.

    The club membership has grown from its original 52 charter members to its
current roster of 175 members.  The cumulative effort of the club members and their
families has resulted in the creation of our new club facilities.  These include a
banquet room holding over 500 people in a banquet style dinner, a small hall where
meetings take place, a recreation room for card players, and a full size commercial
kitchen with a walk-in cooler and freezer.

    This new building stands as a symbol of how our fathers came to this country with
the intent to perpetuate the uniqueness of our Palestinian Arab culture.  Through the
hard work of community members, our children will enjoy the richness of our
Ramallah heritage and feeling of belonging to a family with a home to which they will
always be welcomed.  This building also commemorates the charitable aims which
the Ramallah American Club of Jacksonville has pursued through its unity and
fellowship.  It is to these ideals:  unity, fellowship, charity, and interwoven to
maintain our ethnic awareness that the Ramallah Club of Jacksonville was created.
