The US-Iraq Crisis
The Editors of Middle East Report
Weeks of escalating rhetoric against the backdrop of a massive and carefully choreographed military buildup in the Persian Gulf and continued defiance in Baghdad, had brought Washington to the brink of launching a major military strike. The only alternative would have been to acknowledge that it really had no viable policy towards Iraq.
[Middle East Report, Spring 1998]

Legalism and Realism in the Gulf
Sheila Carapico
The Clinton policy seems less concerned with Iraqi compliance or UNSCOM's integrity than with perpetuating the economic embargo, on-going inspections, "no-fly zones," and periodic American air strikes.
[Middle East Report, Spring 1998]

The Impact of Sanctions in Iraq
While the accuracy of statistics demonstrating the impact of United Nations sanctions on Iraq cannot be fully determined, there is no question that their impact has been severe.
[Middle East Report, Spring 1998]

Military Strikes: Ending or Maintaining the Conflict?
Karim El-Gawhary
High-tech bombardments accompanied by stage-managed military briefings may elicit popular support in the US and Britain. They are not likely, however, to change the situation on the ground because Iraq now has little to lose.

Iraqi Sanctions, Human Rights & Humanitarian Law
Roger Normand
Sanctions have had a devastating impact on the most vulnerable sectors of Iraqi society, especially children. Yet there has been an astonishing lack of public debate over the moral and legal implications of a policy that imposes such enormous costs on a civilian population.
