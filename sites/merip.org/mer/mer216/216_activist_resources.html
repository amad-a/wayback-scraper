Resources for Activists

Sustainable Development in the Middle East

Friends of the Earth-Middle East (FoE-ME) acts as an umbrella organization for regional and international NGOs working on the Middle East. A special FoE-ME project currently studies the impact of regional economic integration on the environment and human livelihood. FoE-ME publishes the MFTZ Environment Monitor, a monthly electronic newsletter.
http://www.foeme.org

Greenpeace-Mediterranean campaigns against environmental degradation in Cyprus, Israel, Lebanon, Malta and Turkey, and publishes a quarterly newsletter.
http://www.greenpeacemed.org.mt

The Mediterranean Information Office for Environment, Culture and Sustainable Development links NGOs across the Mediterranean basin, and co-publishes, with the European Environmental Bureau and RAED, the Sustainable Mediterranean newsletter.
http://www.mio-edsc.org

RAED (The Arab Network of NGOs for Environment and Development) works on developing the capacity of Arab environmental NGOs, and co-publishes the Sustainable Mediterranean newsletter. For further information, write to aoye@ritsec1.com.eg.

ENDA Maghreb coordinates public education about sustainable development throughout North Africa.
http://www.enda.org.ma

Water in the Middle East

The Middle East Water Information Network is a clearinghouse of data and scholarship on Middle Eastern water issues.
http://www.sas.upenn.edu/~mewin

Climate Change and Global Environmental Issues

Ross Gelbspan's The Heat Is On (Perseus Books, 1997) offers convincing evidence of the dangers of global warming. Gelbspan continuously updates the information in his book on his website:
http://www.heatisonline.org

Reports on global environmental issues, and environmental profiles of most Middle Eastern countries, are archived on the website of the World Resource Institute.
http://www.wri.org/wri

Climate change, water scarcity, salination of arable land, desertification and other global environmental trends affecting human livelihood are tracked by the Worldwatch Institute.
http://www.worldwatch.org
