Occupation Impacts
 
December Saturday 12 -1998:

"Israel" destroying Lebanon's environment (1)
" As if "Israel's" occupation of southern Lebanon and its daily aggressions against civilians are not enough, Lebanon is suffering environmental degradation directly linked to "Israeli" activities".

" For the past twenty years, Lebanese have been preoccupied with coping with "Israel's" daily attacks in southern Lebanon, and its frequent military adventures and plans throughout the country, not giving much attention to environmental harm caused by "Israeli" activities in the area. This is a recent Greenpeace mission aimed at intercepting the "Israeli" ship Aribel carrying poisonous and toxic wastes to be dumped in international waters off "Israel's" coast. Aribel makes the trip two to three times a week from the port city of Haifa, to dump toxic wastes from a US company operating in Haifa. Three times "Israel" promised to stop the dumping, which runs counter to international law, and three times "Israel" does not carry out its pledge. Greenpeace Mediterranean representative Fouad Hamdan who took part in the interception of the "Israeli" ship, stressed the wastes do have a negative impact on Lebanon's water. Fouad Hamdan told FTV: ""Israel" is the only country along the Mediterranean Sea that is dumping systematically highly toxic waste in international waters.

Greenpeace sampled the water and found that it is highly toxic and has negative impact on fish and on the water". Lebanon also suffered considerable environmental harm after "Israel" stole tons of fertile topsoil from occupied southern Lebanon last month. Lebanon scored an environmental and diplomatic victory when "Israel" admitted to stealing the soil and pledged to stop, under international pressure. Environmentalists said the area may not be suitable for agricultural purposes for years to come. Another direct consequence of "Israel's" occupation of southern Lebanon has been heightened fears among many in Lebanon, including top officials, that "Israel" may be redirecting the rich Litani River to its territory. That amounts to environmental disaster". "There are the more obvious damages to Lebanon's environment. "Israel" has been shelling areas in the south with internationally banned phosphorous bombs, which set ablaze massive areas of forests and farmland. One day "Israel's" occupation will end but the environmental disasters left behind by "Israel" may be with the Lebanese for a long time".

1- Future TV, 11-12-1998.
December Friday 25 -1998:

The true meaning of goodwill to all (1)
Jarjouh, a village in Iqlim al-Touffah just across the edge of the Israeli-occupied zone, is a frequent target of Israeli and SLA shells and has been deserted by most of its residents. But decorating a tree for Christmas continues to be a yearly custom.

Formerly the home of some 5,000 Christians and Muslims, only 300 residents are left in the village, whose mosque is still intact but whose church was partially destroyed in an Israeli air strike.

Seventy-year-old Haikal Habib Elias is a Christian and has refused to leave his village. Every year, he puts up a Christmas tree outside what is left of the St. George Greek Catholic Church. He sees the ritual as a responsibility to his community since he was entrusted with the keys to the church by the last priest to serve there.

On Christmas Eve, Mr. Elias placed a tree in the church courtyard and decorated it with the help of a Muslim neighbor, Zeinab Saade Bitar.

We are steadfast in our village. This serves as a kind of resistance against the occupiers, Mr. Elias said as he placed an icon of the Virgin Mary on top of the tree.

In the past, Muslim villagers helped their Christian neighbors ring the church bells on Christmas Day, he said, adding that most of the remaining Christians these days go to nearby Nabatieh or Sarba to celebrate religious occasions.

Funeral services are carried out outside the village, but burials still take place in the church cemetery.

Mrs. Bitar said Christians in Jarjouh also take part in Muslim festivals celebrated by their neighbors exactly as we did in the good old days when everyone was still here.

On Christmas Eve a joint dinner was held for everyone in the village, she added to a consenting nod from Mr. Elias.

December Tuesday 29 -1998:

"Israeli" bulldozers raze six houses in Arnoun (1)
""Israeli" troops have demolished six houses in the village of Arnoun after claiming to have unearthed an arms cache in one of the buildings".

"About 30 soldiers accompanied by several militiamen of the South Lebanon Army* entered the village, which lies just below Beaufort Castle, on Sunday and bulldozed the buildings without warning.

Some olive trees were also destroyed in the operation".

"Arnoun, 5 kilometers east of Nabatieh, lies in a no-mans land between free Lebanon and the occupation zone. "Israeli" troops regularly enter the village to search house and question and occasionally detain residents.

The owners of the destroyed houses are Ali Mohammed Qatbani, Ahmad Bashir, Afif Nayef Olleiq, Mohammed Salim Qatbani, Hussein Nayef Olleiq and Yassin Sharafeddine".

"The SLA-run Voice of the South radio claimed that the militiamen had discovered an arms cache and food used by Mujahedeen saboteurs in one of the houses."

"But the residents insisted that the destroyed homes were empty".

I was with my family when we heard a loud noise at 9 P.M. We thought that the "Israelis" had come out of Beaufort Castle to patrol the village as usual, said Zeinab Attieh, 55.

But we found out that bulldozers were razing our neighbors houses. Three hours later, the houses were destroyed.

Zeinab Assaf, 60, said the villagers were used to harassment from the "Israelis" and the SLA.
This is not the first time "Israeli" bulldozers have destroyed houses here but nobody seems to care about it, she said. Five of my sons left the village eight years ago, while my sixth son, Qassem has been detained in Khiam prison since 1997.

Arnouns mukhtar, Afif Hamdan, said the demolition of the houses was only the latest in a long list of woes.

Our plight continues but nobody cares. The whole village could become a ruin if this situation continues, he said.
