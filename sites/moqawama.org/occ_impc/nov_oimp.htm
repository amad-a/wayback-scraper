Boueiz protests theft of soil from south
"Foreign Minister Fares Boueiz sent letters Tuesday to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, the Security Council and the European Union asking them to intervene to stop Israels theft of fertile soil from the south."

"Lebanon has accused Israel of stealing large quantities of soil from the al-Marj area, a region between the towns of Khiam and Hasbaya close to the border, and using it in Israeli settlements across the border."

"Mr. Boueiz said the theft would harm the economy of the south, as well as its environment. The theft of fertile soil  is unacceptable and will render the land unfit for agriculture, the major source of income for the Lebanese living in the occupied zone. This theft will lead to an ecological catastrophe, he said.

"Speaker Nabih Berri had also petitioned the UN, Annan and the world community to deter the Israelis."

 Israel is systematically bulldozing hundreds of tons of earth from the al-Marj area and other regions he said.

"Israel" admits stealing soil from south (2)
"After two weeks of escalating diplomatic pressure, the "Israeli" government formally admitted Monday that civilian contractors from northern Galilee have been systematically stealing soil from the occupation zone."

"The disclosure, an abrupt reversal of earlier denials of involvement in the theft, came three days after the "Israeli" army toldd Unifil that tons of fertile earth had been removed from al-Marj just north of the border with "Israel".

Unifil said Monday that the theft appeared to have ended, with no activity in the area reported since Friday."

"The police in Kiryat Shemona have reportedly been ordered to investigate the removal of truckloads of earth.

The admission by the "Israeli" government came two weeks after The Daily Star broke the story, which initially prompted a flurry of denials from the Jewish state."

"After an official complaint by Speaker Nabih Berri last week to the United Nations, the European Union and the Arab League, "Israel's" Coordinator of Lebanese Affairs, Uri Lubrani, described the accusations as nonsense.

I wonder where all that came from. We are used to such accusations as "Israel" bringing the Litani River to Lake Kinneret. Now its soil. Tomorrow they will say were stealing their air, he said.

"Since "Israel's" sudden confession of guilt over stealing the soil, Mr. Lubrani has remained uncharacteristically silent, leaving his deputy, Reuven Ehrlich, to comment. From an initial inquiry it transpires that these are private contractors who have been stealing quantities of topsoil, said Mr. Ehrlich.

In any event it isnt a methodical plundering of Lebanese resources but the theft of some quantity of soil by local contractors, he said.

"A statement by "Israel's" foreign ministry admitted that soil had been transported into "Israel" and that the army and police were looking into the matter."

"Foreign Ministry spokesman Aviv Shiron said an initial investigation showed that soil was taken from an area near the border for private purposes."

"There was no comment on whether the "Israeli" army was aware of the theft. However, bearing in mind that the "Israeli" army has a heavy presence in the area and man the border crossing through which the soil was transported, it is highly unlikely that they were unaware of what was going on. The soil is being used to construct a series of terraces just inside the "Israeli" border."

" The soil of the water-logged al-Marj is arguably the most fertile in south Lebanon having lain fallow for years due to its proximity to the "Israeli" border."

Lebanon asks UN to force "Israel" to return stolen soil (1)
"Lebanese Foreign Minister Fares Bweiz sent a letter Wednesday to UN Secretary General Kofi Annan calling on the United Nations to force "Israel" to return soil stolen from southern Lebanon. Bweiz said his letter called for adopting "measures to force "Israel" to return the stolen soil or pay compensation for damages to the owners and the environment.

"The "Israeli" foreign ministry formally admitted on Tuesday that civilian contractors from "Israel's" northern Galilee were systematically taking soil from the "Israeli"-occupied border zone in southern Lebanon. The "Israeli" army also acknowledged on Friday that "Israeli" civilians were bringing soil from Lebanon for use in "Israel" and said it wanted the practice stopped. Some 20 to 30 "Israeli" civilians, unaccompanied by "Israeli" troops, have been seen since September transporting soil into northern "Israel" where it is being spread over new agricultural terraces, he said. In a previous letter sent to Annan on Thursday, the Lebanese foreign minister had called on the United Nations to intervene with the "Israeli" authorities "to put a stop to a deplorable practice which is becoming more frequent and persistent."

November Tuesday 17 -1998:

Once the countrys garden, the south is being stripped bare (1)
""Israel's" occupation of the south has not only had a disastrous impact on the residents, but is helping to destroy the environment and agricultural land which the local population desperately needs to survive.

"Israeli" phosphorous shells are regularly burning forests that have taken hundreds of years to take hold.

The green area in the south, once known as the garden of Lebanon, constitutes less than 3 percent of land today compared to 3.2 percent two years ago, said Naji Kodeih of the Ministry of Environment.

This is the lowest percentage of all Lebanon, he said.

"Most of the time, it is not possible to extinguish the fires because they involve military zones, said Abu Ali Sweidan, a resident of Yater, the front-line village whose tobacco and wheat crops and olive groves have lately been subject to "Israeli" shelling with phosphorous rounds. If the "Israelis" spot anyone in the forests surrounding front-line villages, they might shoot at them, said Mr. Sweidan, who is also a former member of the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine."

"Olive farmers often need the help of Unifil for protection while harvesting their crops, said Mr. Sweidan. Unifil troops, carrying the distinctive blue U.N. flags, accompany farmers to the fields, allowing them to collect their crops without coming under fire from the occupation forces."

"This cooperation extends to the peacekeepers often requesting that the "Israelis" cease shelling specific areas at harvest time."

"The "Israeli" occupation has also caused a mass migration from the front-line areas which has resulted in large areas of prime agricultural land being left untended."

"Given the absence of accurate statistics, Mr. Sweidan estimates that 50-60 percent of residents from front-line villages have left, which although compounded by the occupation, had begun years before with the traditional government neglect of the region. Mr. Kodeih warned that if the agricultural land were left fallow for too long it would lead to desertification, the process by which an area can permanently its agricultural capacity."

"Among the suggestions to encourage residents to remain in the south and farm their land is the establishment of an agricultural bank which would give farmers funds in the form of soft loans."
"Southerners tend to cultivate tobacco, to generate quick revenue, instead of fruits, which are more beneficial to the environment but require more time and money. Land in the south needs much preparation to make them fertile enough for farmers to plant apple trees, said Mr. Sweidan.

"According to Mr. Kodeih, the fact that farmers are concentrating on one type of agriculture is very damaging to the environment. The occupation zone has similar problems, said Mr. Sweidan, but if you look at the "Israeli" side of the border, you see green orchards and extended water networks.

"They have the same texture of land and weather conditions, but the difference lies in the level of government concern", he said.

"Shelling and unstable security have also affected livestock. Shelling prevents goats, for example, from grazing freely, said Mr. Sweidan.

"Meanwhile, the number of animals such as porcupines and wild pigs that destroy crops have increased. Hunters, who are the wild pigs predators, cannot reach certain places along the front line, which results in an increase in their numbers, leading to the destruction of a large number of trees and consequently the whole environment."

Residents of Arnoun abandon their homes (1)
"Almost the entire population of the southern village of Arnoun early this afternoon left their homes for good, saying life across from an "Israeli" post had become unbearable. Some forty families deserted the village for the nearby town of Nabatiyeh leaving behind only about 20 elderly people who refused to move. "Israel" constantly threatens and harasses them. One woman said the mayor of the village took his belongings and fled yesterday. "We live at the mercy of the "Israelis" who are positioned in the Beauforte Castle a few kilometers away from us," a villager carrying his belongings said. "Whenever the "Israelis" lose a soldier in southern Lebanon they storm our village and arrest people and take them to the Khiyam Jail." Cases of torture and maltreatment have been reported in the prison. One 14-year-old boy was snatched by "Israeli" troops from Arnoun earlier this year and taken to the jail. Suffering is systematic in Arnoun. Arnoun is a front line village in south Lebanon. But unlike other areas that border the zone "Israel" occupies, Arnoun has a different predicament. It is practically besieged and often visited by "Israeli" patrols. We had visited that village 20 days ago and the report shows the situation in Arnoun."

Report: "The last Lebanese army checkpoint is a little bit over a kilometer from the southern village of Arnoun. Arnoun is not technically inside the zone "Israel" occupies in the south but it lies just beneath the Beaufort castle, one of the "Israeli" manned outposts. Cars are not allowed to enter the village forcing residents to cross by foot. Man from village said: Our village was a paradise, this man says. But it is not heaven anymore. It is a ghost town. He was right. We crossed into the village and the tragedy was evident. Most of the homes have been abandoned and it took a while before we came across people. There were some 30,000 people here before "Israel" carved out its occupation zone. Today the village only has some 30 residents and most of them are old. Woman from village said: Most of the people have fled because of the appalling conditions here, she explained. But my husband and I do not have any other choice. We had to stay. But young men cannot stay here. The "Israelis" will take them. At times, "Israeli" occupation forces set up temporary checkpoints right here at the entrance of the village."

"They search homes, check cars and detain residents who they suspect assist the Lebanese resistance before returning to their hilltop position. "Israeli" forces constantly keep a close eye on the village. And its residents have to deal with constant harassment."

"Man in village said: "Israeli" patrols enter the village. They searched my home, this man said. They look through the drawers, the closets, everywhere. They also besiege the village. When night falls, no one is allowed to leave their homes. This family lost their five year old brother. He needed urgent medical treatment but they had to wait for morning to leave the village. And it just was too late. But life has to go on. Arnoun's residents, or to be more accurate, the remaining few, mainly rely on tobacco cultivation and olive harvests for their livelihood. The serenity of this village is deceptive. The people live in fear. And the hardships they face are all too obvious. As this woman put it: the only thing people here want is to live a normal life."

"Fatima Hamdan, 55, said the exodus has gone on for 12 years. "Nobody is aware of us and nobody cares," she said.

"The last shop in the village has closed and the man who delivered drinking water has also left," said Hassan Qatabi, 55, "There's no longer any life in the village.

Hamdan was referring to a five-year-old boy who needed urgent medical treatment but his family had to wait for morning to leave the village, but it was too late, for the child had passed away.

November Tuesday 24 -1998:

"Israeli" repressive actions force Arnoun's residents to dislodge (1)
"A few undernourished cats were the only signs of life in the deserted and silent streets of Arnoun Saturday a day after almost the entire population departed the village."

"The animals paced silently through Arnouns narrow streets, past empty single-story stone houses, past the villages only shop, now closed and abandoned. The only sound was the eerie moan of wind blowing through the electricity lines, a perfect accompaniment to what has become a ghost town.
A week ago, the streets had been filled with people going about their everyday business. But when the mukhtar, Afif Hamdan, left the village last week most of the 250 residents followed him along the single road that links Arnoun with the rest of Lebanon."

"The village is five kilometers southeast of Nabatieh and its untidy sprawl of houses lies scattered along the grassy slopes of a hill dominated by Beaufort Castle."

"The entire village is overshadowed by the remains of this Crusader fortress whose shattered and crumbling walls, overgrown with vegetation, fail to diminish its imposing aura."

"Half the village officially lies in the occupation zone ­ a swing gate denotes the border. But the other half, supposedly in free Lebanon, in fact falls into an uneasy no-mans land.

The Lebanese army has no jurisdiction there because its presence ends one-and-a-half kilometers away at a checkpoint."

"Beaufort castle is an important "Israeli" position. Its ramparts bristle with radio antennae and sandbagged gun emplacements. The blue and white stripes of a Star of David flag flutters from the parapet."

"For 16 years, the villagers have faced almost daily harassment from the "Israeli" soldiers manning the castle. The troops enter Arnoun at will to conduct house-to-house searches, question villagers and even detain some of them in Khiam prison."

"Five residents, Abbas Attieh, Ali Tawbe, Assem Assef, Mohammed Qaid-Bey and Mustafa Tawbe have spent more than a year in Khiam."

"When Mr. Hamdan left the village he took with him his tractor which had been the only means of delivering tanks of water to the residents."

"The prospect of no more water was the final straw for the long-suffering residents."

The last supply arrived on Friday. When its finished thats it. No more water. How can anyone stay in the village then? asked a 50-year-old resident who requested anonymity.

There are only five or six families left in Arnoun, although two of them were planning to leave soon. "() He added that he no longer lived in the village but had returned to harvest his tobacco.
Once thats done Im leaving for good, he said.

A divorced mother of three, who also refused to be named, spoke bitterly of the lack of support from the government."

"()  We are threatened with death every day here. I want to leave but theres nowhere for me to go. I have no money and no husband. Whats the point of talking. Were finished, she said as tears streamed down her cheeks."

"Also trapped in the village was Khadija Khawaja, 40, and her family. She had packed all her household goods in preparation to depart but admitted she had no place to go."

I asked how much it was to rent a house in Maifadoun or Chouqine (a few kilometers from Arnoun) and was told it would be LL250,000 a month. We cant afford that so we have to stay here, she said.

The familys clothing and blankets had been tied into bundles and stacked by the front door along with kitchen implements and jars of olives. In another room lay 600 kilograms of tightly wrapped tobacco awaiting delivery to Nabatieh.

This is our income but I cant get it out of the village, Mrs. Khawaja lamented.
No cars are allowed inside Arnoun. They are parked beside a dried up pond at the entrance to the village.

"Indicating two wheelbarrows, Mrs. Khawaja joked, these are our cars in Arnoun.

As she prepared glasses of steaming tea, Mrs. Khawaja admitted that her outwardly cheerful demeanor was an attempt to bolster the spirits of her family. My father is very depressed at whats happening here and I dont want him to be sad, she said.

Her father, Ali, 75, lost his left leg in a land mine explosion ().

At least one villager stubbornly refuses to leave Arnoun.

I was born here, Ive lived here and I want to die here. I am 82 years old and Im staying, said Mohammed Marouni.

He said all the village needed was a regular supply of water.

There is a very real fear that if the last of the few remaining residents decide to leave, the "Israelis" may dynamite the village to prevent the resistance from using it as cover to stage attacks on the fortress.

Weve been hoping every year since 1992 that this would be the year the "Israelis" leave, said Mr. Khawaja. Now it looks like 1998 is the year that we leave.
