  June Tuesday 2-1998:

The Israelis assault on the Monitoring Committee (1):
No sooner had the French President Jacques Chirac ended his visit to Lebanon than the convoy of Frances Ambassador and Deputy Chief of Monitoring Committee Joseph Raban came under a light heavy machine guns of the Israeli occupation forces and their agents. It is note worthy that Chirac had called during his visit for an unconditional withdrawal from South Lebanon in accordance with the Resolution 425.

The incident occurred as the convoy was passing across Hamra-Bayada crossing point towards Naqoura town in order to participate with the meeting of Five-Nation Monitoring Committee.

A UN officer clarified that as the convoy tried to pass through the crossing point between the liberated and occupied regions, the members stationed there attempted to search it. The French delegation and UN officers refused the step. Then several machine gun rounds fired in the air. In the meantime, a hot debate happened between the officers and the Israeli agents after which the French delegation continued traveling without searching.

In Tyre city, security sources described the step as provocative. Also, they expected that this issue will subject to discussions, even if it is not scheduled, in order to prevent its recurrence.
It should be noted that this incident is considered as a precedent since establishing the Monitoring Committee in August 1996.

1- As-Safir, 3-6-1998, p:7.

   June Thursday 4-1998:

Press freedom is an Israeli hoax (1):
Sharq El-Awsat newspaper published an article in which it wondered about the press freedom in Israel. To answer this question,it is to be pointed out that there is an agreement between Israels media and the military censorship.

This agreement stated that the Israeli Press officials had become able to object, via the Supreme Court, the rules issued by the security apparatus.

It is worth noting that the censorship steps had been imposed for more than thirty years, but they are subjected now to certain amendments.

The Israeli authorities outlined the core of this mission as follows: Any security issues that can really endanger states stability are forbidden. The attracting point, in this regard, is about the nature and the flexibility of the restrictions imposed on all foreign journalists operating in Israel and the occupied territories.

One of these restrictions is that the reporter should sign a special commitment issued by the Official Press Office. Any article or reportage that may endanger the state security should be exposed to the military censorship, the commitment mentioned.

So, the commitment seems to involve several serious meanings, for it suggests that the reporter is perpetrating a crime or an offense upon presenting his press materials to the military censorship.
This situation raises the following question:

How would this decision be applied with its apparent flexibility while in its essence endangers the foreign journalists?

Finally, we can say it is so strange that the western press didnt comment on these developments in a state which is still insisting that it is the only democratic one in the Middle East.

1- Al-Sharq Al-Awsat, 5-6-1998, p:22.

  June Thursday 18-1998:

Green Peace and Israels Toxic Materials (1):
Green Peace announced that two of its rubber boats caught the Israeli ship Ariel, owned by the American Haifa Chemicals Company, casting industrial liquid wastes out of Haifa bay. It noticed that these wastes contains heavy toxic minerals such as cadmium, mercury and zinc elements.

Green Peace activators lifted posters demanding that burying the materials must be stopped and that the ship has to come back to Haifa port, but its crew didnt reply at all. The burial continued throughout the night, it clarified.

Mario Damato, Executive Manager of Green Peace Office, quoted as saying:  It is the first time in which burying operations of the block toxic liquid had been watched and filmed.

He wondered:  How can these practices be accepted in the Mediterranean Sea while at the same time are prohibited along the American Coast?  He, also, demanded Israel to abide by Year of Oceans Declaration issued by the United Nations, knowing that it had promised two times that it will halt these infractions.

Damato reiterated: We call on the countries of Mediterranean Sea to support us so as to prevent this organized burial and limit the pollution impacts caused be their industries.

It should be noted that Green Peaces activators had sailed with their boats from Cyprus the past Sunday and waited in the international water. No sooner had they knew that the Israeli ship Ariel left Haifa port on Tuesday evening than they rushed to intercept it peacefully raising a placard on which they wrote: Danger  Toxic Burial Area.

Under an American protection and international ignorance Israel may think that it will be in safe by polluting the area with poisons that, certainly, harm the man and environment. But it willnt take too longtime, for Israel to be affected by its environmental crimes beginning with the nuclear experiments and ending with the toxic wastes.

1- Al-Nahar, 19-6-1998, p:12.

  June Friday 26-1998:

What did the hostages say? (1):

Militant Adel Tormos:
Q: As a liberated hostage, how do you feel?
A: First of all, I greet the venerated martyrs and I wish for the live martyrs who are still in the concentration camps to be released. By the way two days ago, 62-year-old internee had died as a result of the Zionisms barbarian and savage, after he had spent 28 years in the prison God willing, we will do our best in order to release all detainees.

Q: Did you expect inside the prison that in a certain day you may be released?
A: No, we have known about the exchange operation when we were inside the jail. As a result of the Israeli obstinacy, I felt that there is no possibility for the hostage detained in Israels jails to be released but, praise be to God!

Q: What about the detention conditions?
A: I had been interned in 1986 during Kounine operation, through which Hizbullah captured two Jews, as our group was retreating from the scene near Baraasheit village. Of the internees also was Mohammad Yassine.

Q: How do you outline your suffering inside the Jail?
A: I had sustained a solitary confinement for about six months. After I had been tried and sentenced to 25 years imprisonment, I had transported to Asqalan Central Prison where I suffered various kinds of physical and psychological torture. But praise be to God, the hostages morale were always very high. Were it not for my disease, I would prefer that another internee be released

Q: From what did you suffer?
A: I had been sustained the meningitis. Over the past year, I had moved between three hospitals for treatment.

Q: But to a certain extent, you seem as if you are in a better state than your inmates?
A: This isnt normal state. I am sustaining an inflation state because the cortisone medicaments that  I had taken them for about last six months!

Q: Did you easily acquaint with your family members?
A: Yes, I had known all my family members even my sister, 13, who had been born one month before I was arrested. This because they were sending their photographs with the International Committee for the Red Cross.

Q: Will you resume again the way of struggle?
A: Of course, it is a pledge I made it by myself that I will continue the way of struggle until the last inch of our occupied lands be liberated. God willing, we will overcome Israel and release the remaining internees whom I promise to do everything in order to release them.

Nabih Awada:
He was apprehended on September 1988 during a military operation at Deir Siryan area. He is one of 42 internees still captured inside the Israeli jails such as Asqalan, Nafha, Ramla and others.

Their morale are very high and I dont wish to talk about their sufferance which are very tough. Moreover, some are undergoing exceptional and arduous conditions such as Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani who had been put in solitary confinement since the date of their detention, he said.

Awada added: I had heard the news about the exchange operation via radios and the internees lawyers who were coordinating with the Follow-up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in Israels jails. At the beginning, I didnt believe that I will among the released hostages. This is because we had been excluded from several past exchange operations.

Awada is so glad for it is extremely great for one to return his home, family and comrades, adding at the same time, I am missing, at these moments, my inmates. I willnt feel deep happiness unless the other internees be released.

He talked about the internee Anwar Yassine saying: He rushed to applaud so high. This made us to feel with comfort and happiness on getting out of the capture.

He, finally, asserted that the resistance should continue as long as the Israeli forces occupy our land.
