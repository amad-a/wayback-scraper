Military tribunal convenes more collaborators trials

A military court resumed marathon sessions related to occupation-related crimes on Wednesday, hearing some 150 cases, most against former members of Israels defunct proxy militia.
The court, which heard the cases of 115 collaborators and 35 others who reportedly entered Israel, was due to hand down its verdicts and sentences around midnight.
The most prominent defendants were Elias Eid, the chief guard of the Israeli-allied militias TV station; Manal Sader, the private secretary of the militias second-in-command, Aql Hashem, who was killed in a resistance attack in March; and George Mansour, who controlled Lahdi artillery positions that shelled villages along the edge of the former occupation zone.
The tribunal, which is meeting three times a week, has sentenced 250 people since the first session on June 5. The two harshest penalties handed down were 15 years in prison and a 15-year ban from liberated areas.
Sentences of five to 10 years are reserved for those who performed intelligence work for the militia or the Israeli Army.
Those who enlisted in the militia have generally been sentenced to one year in prison and those who entered enemy territory to one or two months. The penalties, which are sometimes supplemented by fines, have been doubled when they were handed down in absentia.
Those arrested after Israels withdrawal last month have been held at the army barracks in Beirut or in the nearby suburbs.

The Daily Star, 22-6-2000.
