October Wednesday 7-1998:

Introduction

The Islamic Resistance's (IR) qualitative military operations, launched two days ago, are still resounding inside the "Israeli" military and security establishments. The "Israeli" Army's command, astonished by the high losses among the "Israeli" soldiers, opened a prompt inquiry over the circumstances surrounding IR's killing of two "Israeli" soldiers and wounding six others.

Elsewhere, a trial of 78 "Israeli" agents, who have been charged with leaking information, had begun at the military tribunal.

Occupation Forces are still investigating into IR's infiltration into Hasbaya

"Israeli" media are still trying to trace the reasons behind the occupation army's repeated failures in south Lebanon."

"A more pressing problem facing the (Israeli) Northern Command, however, is how to deal with the threat posed by Hizbullah's roadside bombs in general and the hollow charge that was used in Sunday's attack in particular ", "Israeli" daily Jerusalem Post said.

"The hollow-charge device is designed to penetrate armor and then explode, which is apparently what happened to the protected jeep in shingles in which the soldiers killed were traveling, it added.

The Israeli army has invested a great deal of resources in beefing up the armor and other forms of protection for outposts in the (occupied) "security zone" and the vehicles used there. The armor on the lighter vehicles, including the three jeeps and the "Abir" command car that were in the convoy that was hit on Monday, does not offer complete protection against hollow-charge devices", the daily read.

"The Jerusalem Post acknowledged that Hizbullah has the upper hand when it comes to intelligence.

Another problem appears to be with Hizbullah's intelligence-gathering. The attack on Monday indicated that the organization was aware of the "Israeli" army's movements and planted the devices accordingly. It seems likely the devices were detonated by remote control", the "Israeli" daily said.

"One of the wounded soldiers told the "Israeli" entity's president, Ezer Weizman, "It happened inside Hasbaya, something we had not anticipated because we had been told it was friendly [there]. But there is a war there as well."

"Ha'aretz talked about the underway investigation."

"The "Israeli" army's Northern Command is continuing its investigation into the circumstances of the roadside bombing. The leading hypothesis seems to be that the Hizbullah cell that planted the bombs infiltrated the area only a short while before the detonation", the daily said.

'Hizbullah representatives disseminated video footage that they said shows the bombs exploding. The footage was aired on Lebanese television stations. "Israeli" army sources said they believed the footage was authentic since it matched the testimony of (Israeli) soldiers who witnessed the explosions", it added.

"Ha'aretz quoted the "Israeli" entity's so-called coordinator of government activities in Lebanon, Uri Lubrani, as telling "Israel" Radio that the question of the "Israeli" occupation army's withdrawing from Lebanon is still on the table. There is no absolute solution, he said, "and every solution so far suggested contains a great deal of uncertainty"… (1)

However, The Daily Star reported that the roadside bomb attack in Hasbaya appears to have caused genuine concern in the "Israeli" military, which had perceived the mainly Druze-town as a safe area.
The attack has led to some lurid speculation in the "Israeli" press as to the type of explosive device employed by Hizbullah."
"The Jerusalem Post claimed that the weapon was a “hollow-charge device designed to penetrate armour and then explode”. The newspaper said that the two vehicles destroyed in the explosion were only lightly armoured. "
"The bombs were probably Hizbullah’s standard ­ and effective ­ version of the US-made Claymore anti-personnel mine, which is placed above-ground and discharges dozens of ball bearings. Witnesses said that both vehicles were peppered with holes from the small steel balls. These devices are generally hidden under a hollow fibre-glass “rock” and detonated by remote control."
"It would seem that the Post muddled up the “hollow charge” with the hollow rock bomb ­ which is also deployed by the "Israeli" army along resistance infiltration trails."
"Judging from Hizbullah’s video of the attack, the bombs would have been detonated by remote control, probably by a guerrilla lying beside the cameraman. The first explosion destroyed the second vehicle in the convoy. The guerrilla then waited for almost a minute until two of the "Israeli" vehicles had reversed up the road ­ standard "Israeli" procedure in a roadside bomb attack ­ before exploding the second bomb."

"The "Israelis", apparently convinced that Hizbullah had help from local residents in planting the bombs, have placed restrictions on movement by the town’s residents and have reportedly tapped telephone lines."
"The crackdown was condemned by Tyre MP Ali Khalil."
“I call on the government to take the immediate initiative and expose "Israel’s' terrorist activities that are taking place in the south, the latest being in Hasbaya,” he said.
"Meanwhile, Western Beqaa-Rashaya MP Faisal Daoud called for a sit-in at the Druze religious headquarters in Beirut to protest the recent deportation of two elderly Druze couples by the South Lebanon Army*."

“I appeal to foreign minister Fares Boueiz to work quickly with the concerned authorities to secure the return of those who were deported from their homes. I also appeal to the Red Cross to take care of those who were deported,” he said.
"Two of the deportees were the parents of Raja Ward, the SLA militiamen who defected to the government in July, leading to the arrest of 19 collaborators and the dismantling of their intelligence ring"... (2)

Trial of 78 "Israeli" ‘informers’ gets under way

78 "Israeli" informers have been presented to the military tribunal today on charges of collaborating with the "Israeli" enemy. The Daily Star covered the trial and reported the following:

"The hearings, which began at 3pm, were still continuing when The Daily Star went to press with defence lawyers still arguing after the 19 defendants in custody had testified."
"The defendants have been charged with leaking information about positions of the Lebanese army and anti-Israeli resistance groups to "Israeli" intelligence officials, as well as informing them of the Syrian army’s military activities in Lebanon and Syria."
"They are also charged with having entered the territories of an enemy country and violating the official boycott on Israeli products by importing cars from the Jewish state."
"Those in custody include four women, a Sûreté Générale agent and a customs employee, who were arrested in Beirut and the south in June."
"Police are searching for the others, who are still at large and believed to be hiding in the Jezzine enclave which is garrisoned by the "Israeli"-backed SLA militia."
"The ring was uncovered on the basis of confessions from Ward, the former deputy chief of the SLA’s intelligence branch who fled Jezzine and handed himself over to the army in June."
"Ward, a 37-year-old Druze, told interrogators that Druze "Israeli" army officers had played a key role in recruiting many of the ring’s predominantly Druze and Shiite members", judicial sources said.
"The trial proceeded after the presiding judge, Brig Hussein Shaheen, overruled a request by defence lawyers for Ward’s statements to investigators to be read out loud, as the defendants’ statements were."
"The judge argued that the statement could not be made public “for security reasons”.
"One defendant, Ghaleb Wadih Massoud, claimed that he had agreed to hand over information on Syrian army positions in Lebanon after bowing to “immense pressures” from Ward."
“But knowing that this was a criminal offence and because I needed the money to settle some debts, I fabricated information and delivered it to Ward,” he added, claiming that he had not received more than $7,300 from the former SLA officer."
"Another defendant, Nader Wahib Abdel-Samad, admitted that he had been collaborating with Israel since 1995, saying he was pressured into doing so by Nidal Jamal, a defendant who is still at large."
“When he found out that a cousin of mine, Nahed Ulba, was Syrian and living in Soweida in southwest Syria, he asked me to arrange a meeting between them, something which I eventually did,” he said.
“He later took my cousin to "Israel" where he introduced him to an "Israeli" army officer,” the defendant said. “The officer convinced my cousin to join the Syrian army and leak to "Israel" information about the army’s positions inside Syria,” he added.
"Abdel-Samad denied having been subjected to any pressure when he was questioned by Lebanese army intelligence investigators… (4)
