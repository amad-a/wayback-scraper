"Israeli" fury at escape of Hizbullah guerrilla
Fighter strolls around Sojod base, raises flag, punches soldier and gets away(1)

An embarrassed "Israeli" army has launched an investigation to find out how a Hizbullah guerrilla was able to enter the formidable Sojod position on Sunday and wander around at will before escaping from a squad of elite paratroopers.

The incident occurred during heavy fighting on Sunday night between Hizbullah guerrillas and "Israeli" troops near Sojod in the Iqleem Al-Tuffah.

The guerrilla, armed with two grenades and an M-16 assault rifle, apparently scrambled over the compound's fortifications and strolled around the interior of the hilltop bunker undetected.

According to one account in yesterday's "Israeli" press, the fighter raised a Hizbullah flag in the base and may have stolen classified communications equipment.

The guerrilla bumped into a single "Israeli" paratrooper and opened fire at him from close range but missed. The two combatants had a fistfight before another soldier came to his colleague's aid. The guerrilla was shot by the second soldier but was still able to escape, leaving behind his flak jacket and rifle.

An "Israeli" army spokesman said an investigation had been opened but declined to provide further details until the probe was completed.

The incident clearly shocked the "Israeli army, particularly as it had involved an elite paratrooper unit.

"A paratrooper force cannot be surprised in such a way, this could have been a terrible catastrophe," one senior officer from "Israel's" northern command told the daily Yediot Ahronot newspaper.

"It is unacceptable that a unit of this level and size was unable to capture or kill a terrorist who infiltrated its sector," another high-ranking commander in south Lebanon told the daily Maariv.

The Sojod position, on the heights above Arab Salim and one of the most heavily attacked compounds in the zone, is nominally manned by South Lebanon Army militiamen. But "Israeli" troops are often temporarily deployed there, in particular Special Forces units who ambush infiltrating guerrillas on the wooded slopes of Jabal Rafih.

According to the military correspondent for Yediot Ahronot, "Israeli" army investigators were questioning all of the paratroop unit present in the compound at the time to find out how the guerrilla penetrated the base in broad daylight, for how long, why the first soldier to see him did not open fire and how he then managed to escape.

The investigation comes days after a probe was opened into a deadly Hizbullah attack on the same post on July 30 in which a 20-strong patrol was targeted by a road side bomb while on rifle shooting practice. One "Israeli" soldier was killed and four wounded in the blast. The "Israelis" fear that Hizbullah may have had prior intelligence on the unit's movements.

The breach of security in Sojod last Sunday will have seriously alarmed the "Israeli" army. It believes Hizbullah plans to kidnap a soldier to use as a bargaining chip to win the release of Lebanese detainees.

Haaretz daily said the guerrilla fired 15 rounds at a distance of two metres. No trained guerrilla or soldier misses from that range and he may have hoped to subdue the "Israeli" with the aim of kidnapping him.

However, the Jerusalem Post quoted in its August 12,1998 edition as saying (2):
It is still not clear, however, why the paratrooper, despite the apparent suddenness of the encounter, did not immediately fire his own weapon at the gunman, who was also armed with an automatic rifle as well as at least two grenades.

According to some reports, it was only when other soldiers hearing the commotion reached the scene that shots were fired at the Hizbullah gunman.

Despite being wounded he still managed to evade the paratroopers after throwing down his weapons.

The inquiry being led by Brig -Gen Erez Gerstein, head of what so-called the IDFs Lebanese Liaison Unit, is also concentrating on how the small Hizbullah squad managed to reach the area without being seen.

One of the gunman climbed a short but steep rock face to reach the ramparts of the stronghold and then either cut through or clambered over barbed wire fencing surrounding the position, also without being observed.

The gunman had reached the trench encircling the ramparts before being suddenly discovered by the patrolling paratrooper.

Gerstein yesterday met and debriefed the soldiers and officers who were at post at the time of the incident and his findings will be submitted to OC Northern Command Maj-Gen Gaby Ashkenazi, who will turn them over to Chief of Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaol Mofaz.

The incident was dubbed a debacle in some Hebrew newspapers. According to one report, the soldiers may have been complacent, and a few suffered from low motivation.

AUMG held a meeting (4):

The April Understanding Monitoring Group (AUMG) held its 57th meeting this morning at the UNIFIL command HQ in Naqoura. However, it was another ordinary day of "Israeli" aggressions against Lebanese civilians.

The meeting discussed two Lebanese complaints and 2 "Israeli" claims of understanding violations by the Resistance.

The first Lebanese complaint was over the injury of two civilians, one of them is Mohammad Khalil Merhi (76 years) who was wounded when his house in the town of Majdal Zoun was hit on Sunday by an "Israeli" shell.

The second complaint lodged by Lebanon stemmed from the damage of more than 15 houses and the electricity network in the town of Ain Qana yesterday by "Israeli" shelling.

As usual, the occupation command claimed in its complaints that the resistance launched rounds on "Israeli" outposts from residential areas.

"Israel" plans more construction in the occupied territories (8):
The "Israeli" government is calling for tenders for the construction of dozens of housing units in the occupied Golan Heights and Gaza Strip, "Israel" Radio reported Tuesday. The specifications, for the construction of additional housing units in "Israeli" settlements in the two regions, have been published in the "Israeli" press in the last few days, the radio said.

A spokesman for "Israel's" Peace Now protest movement said the tender invitations showed that the right-wing government of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu refused to continue the peace process with Syria and with the Palestinians. However, "Israel's" deputy minister of housing and construction Menahem Porush, said the government was merely fulfilling its basic guidelines. The tenders were not part of an "Israeli" construction boost in the territories, he said. "Israel" captured the Golan Heights from Syria in the 1967 war. Negotiations for the return of the territory to Syria were suspended in 1996, and have not resumed since then. Although the Palestinian Authority controls most of Gaza, "Israel" still retains control over several "Israeli" settlements in the strip.
