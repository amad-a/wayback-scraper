August Thursday 20 -1998:
 

Enemy Military Command Opens Inquiry in the Recent Sojod Operation(1)
The Islamic Resistance (IR) kept the "Israeli" military institution amidst confusion and embarrassment as the Mujahedeen proceeded with dealing qualitative painful blows to the occupation army and curbing its desperate attempts to revive its deteriorating morale. All the enemy's security measures to protect the occupation troops have been repeatedly intercepted by the IR men.

IR's qualitative operation in the Sojod area yesterday was carried out in spite of the maximum alert and emergency preparedness in and around the outpost after the recent IR penetration which "Israeli" media calls the "Sojod Fiasco".

Questions were raised regarding the participation of the occupation's military intelligence in the patrol, an indication that the enemy was planning terrorist attacks. Moreover, the enemy's command is still puzzled about the way IR found out where the patrol positioned itself and planted a bomb there.

Furthermore, the operation was carried out in broad daylight and the IR men were able to return safely to the liberated areas.

"Israeli" Sgt. Ori Samuelov, 21, of Mevasseret Zion, was killed and four other soldiers were wounded yesterday morning in IR's ambush in the northeastern sector of the occupied zone.

Samuelov, who served in a combat intelligence unit, was buried at 4 p.m. today at Mount Herzl Military Cemetery in occupied Palestine.

The incident occurred around 8:30 a.m. when a combined paratroop/combat intelligence squad was moving in the area. Initial inquiries by the Northern Command found that a large explosive device, probably consisting of two Claymore anti-personnel mines, was detonated, the "Israeli" daily Jerusalem Post said.

The paratroopers involved in yesterday's incident were from the same brigade as the soldiers who were criticized for not preventing an Hizbullah gunman from penetrating the Sojod outpost on August 9 and allowing him to escape alive, it added.

A frustrated and confused OC Northern Command Maj.-Oen. Gabi Ashkenazi resorted to announcing the usual step: a new inquiry.

"What's clear from the inquiries so far is that a small part of the force went to the position and, while they were a few meters away from the position, an explosive device, or possibly two, detonated," he said.

Ashkenazi told reporters at a briefing at the so-called Northern Command headquarters last night that the soldiers were on a mission to "locate identity, and hit" Resistance men in the eastern sector.

Asked how Hizbullah knew where the unit is to be positioned and how the resistance could plant a bomb there, Ashkenazi gave a contradicting answer.

"We don't have any information that Hizbullah knew of the unit's operations - although the fact remains that there was an explosive device at the position," he said.

"The army knows the area and operates there, and the unit itself is acquainted with the spot and had been there several days before," he added. "During the inquiry we will examine how the explosive device came to be in the area and the spot itself."

Ashkenazi was asked about the public debate in "Israel" and those who are calling for unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon. He stressed that the debate is legitimate, but that the army is not a party to it, Jerusalem Post said.

The incident occurred amid discussions in Northern Command over the "Israeli" army's activities deep in the occupied region and especially in the northeastern sector, where Hizbullah has been very active, it added.

Enemy Leaders Resort to Threats after Repeated Fiascoes in the Occupied Zone.
In a fresh expression of their helplessness before the Resistance, enemy leaders resorted to threatening Lebanon and its people with further terrorist acts.

Enemy Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu reiterated his rejection to withdraw from occupied south Lebanon unless his entity reaches security arrangements with Lebanon.

We are looking for a way out of Lebanon. This is one of our goals, but we can't do that without security arrangements, Netanyahu said (2).

"Israel's" internal security minister Avigdor Kahalani repeated his old recommendation of bombing Lebanon's infrastructure whenever "Israeli" troops are killed in the south.

"We should attack bridges, the electricity grid, the water system in Lebanon each time one of our soldiers is killed in the country," he said while touring northern "Israel".

His comments came a day after an "Israeli" soldier was killed and four others were wounded in a roadside bomb blast.

"The Lebanese government must understand that it is its responsibility to deploy forces in the south and re-establish its authority over the region," Kahalani's spokeswoman quoted him as saving.

His comments were echoed by Uzi Landau, who heads the knesset's foreign affairs and defense committee.

"We must strike the economic interests and infrastructure of Lebanon, especially its electrical and transport networks, every time one of our soldiers is killed in the country," he told "Israeli" radio. Landau's threats extended to the Syrian troops deployed in Lebanon.

"If our soldiers are blown to bits by mines or remote-controlled bombs, Syrian jeeps will be blown up in the same way," he said.

Kahalani's comments on south Lebanon over the past year have shifted from one extreme to another.

On September 14, following the deaths of two soldiers, he advocated an unconditional withdrawal from the south, claiming that "Israel" could reach an agreement with Hizbullah. He did not say how that agreement could be reached.

The next day, he called for the wholesale bombing of Beirut and Lebanon's infrastructure immediately after any attack on "Israeli" forces (3)

Amnesty raises fears of detainees' torture (7)
The inmates of Khiam were allowed to receive visits from their relatives for the first time in three weeks.

The "Israelis" allowed 23 family members to pass through the Kfar Tibbnit crossing to meet ten detainees.

The families were accompanied by members of the International Committee for the Red Cross and spent four hours at the prison.

The human rights watchdog, Amnesty International, said it suspected that a captured guerrilla may face torture and ill-treatment at the hands of the (so-called) SLA.

Amnesty said that Ali Banjak, who was released by "Israel" in May after two years imprisonment, was beaten "all over his body, including his testicles, with clubs and hands".

He was also raised and dropped onto a vertical stick "causing terrible pain to his lower body" and interrogated while tied to a 10cm high chair and forced to look into a bright light.

"Those tortured by the (so-called) SLA have described being beaten or whipped while doused with hot or cold water and (subjected to) electric shocks," the statement said.
