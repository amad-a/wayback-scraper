Daily Report
 
 
August Thursday 27 -1998:
 
Introduction
Uneasy calm reigned over the South Lebanon after "Israel" escalated its aggressions on towns and villages on the edge of the occupied region.

Mounting "Israeli" casualties and an increasingly unpopular occupation of South Lebanon among the "Israelis", prompted harsh criticism among "Israel's" inner cabinet.

However, the Five-Nation Monitoring Cease-Fire Committee in South will meet on Monday to discuss Lebanese and "Israeli" complaints over charges of April 1996 Understanding contraventions.

"Israeli" Aggressions:
"Israeli" occupation forces have violated the cautious calm which prevailed in the southern areas with sporadic shelling from the occupation's outposts in Sojod, Zaffateh, and Rihan on the Mount Sojod, Rihan woods, Aqmata Plain and Nabeh al-Tasseh River banks.

Meanwhile, enemy warplanes and espionage drones continually circled in the Lebanese skies reaching, in the morning, the capital Beirut and the city of Baalbek in Beqaa.

Enemy warplanes staged mock air raids in the morning over villages in the areas of Nabatieh, Iqleem Al-Tuffah and the western Beqaa.

"Israeli" occupation outpost in Baraasheit raked the town's residential neighborhoods.
In the afternoon, the occupation artillery in Rihan bombarded the Loweizeh - Jarjoua - Mount Sojod road (Iqleem Al-Tuffah).

The shelling coincided with enemy warplanes circling over Nabatieh and Iqleem Al-Tuffah.
After 17:00 p.m. emplacements in Dhoor Meis fired 10 155mm rounds on the outskirts of Haddatha.

Netanyahu aides charge: Mordechai to blame for shelters snafu
In an indication of "Israel's" top leaders puzzling over the Katyusha rocket attack launched by Islamic Resistance (IR) militants, Yerah Tal, the "Israeli" daily Ha'aretz's correspondent, reported that (2):

Defense Minister Yitzhak Mordechai is responsible for the fact that residents of the north were not sent to bomb shelters before Katyusha rockets rained down on the entire area, aides close to Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu charged. In a meeting of the inner cabinet Netanyahu had harsh words about the episode and demanded an explanation from Mordechai. He also ordered an investigation of what close aides afterward branded a "blunder," using the Hebrew word mehdal, which ever since the Yom Kippur War 25 years ago has had a special resonance implying that the heads of those responsible should roll.

There was also anger in circles close to Netanyahu that Mordechai had "volunteered" too much information to the media about the liquidation of Amal military commander Hossam al-Amin on Tuesday. These sources said that Mordechai was out to glorify his reputation but that his talk had not served "Israel's" security interests.

In this regard, Amos Harel, Ha'aretz's military correspondent said in a relatively long article (3) signaling that "Israel's" Army thought Katyusha attack was unlikely. The article shows clearly "Israel's" lack of information about Hizbullah fighters. Here are the excerpts:

The decision not to instruct the residents of the communities along the northern border to enter public bomb shelters or the security rooms in their homes on Tuesday was made after a situation appraisal by Northern Command concluded that there was "low probability" that the Shi'ite organizations in Lebanon would fire Katyusha rockets into "Israel".

But as opposed to the Northern Command, Military Intelligence (MI) at General Staff HQ on Tuesday evening said they thought the probability of a rocket attack on the north was quite high after the successful assassination of the Amal commander from the western sector of South Lebanon.
But Maj. Gen. Gabi Ashkenazi, who heads the Northern Command, said yesterday that "we made a decision in accordance with the information we had, according to our best judgment, and when all is said and done we acted correctly."

The deliberations in Northern Command began shortly after a single missile fired by an air force helicopter killed al-Amin in his car. Northern Command expected that Shi'ite groups would make do with firing at "Israel" Defense Forces (IDF) outposts along the northern border (the "Purple Line").

That thinking derived from the accumulated experience of the past year in the wake of several episodes of high tension in the north. IDF intelligence noted that Amal and Hizbullah had opened fire at outposts near population centers but not at them.

Armed with this assessment, Ashkenzi briefed senior officials from northern communities but did not issue instructions about getting the population into shelters. In the early evening the decision was made to order security coordinators in the communities to open the public shelters "just in case," but again nothing was said about getting people into them. Shortly after the order was given to open the shelters the Katyushas began to fall on Kiryat Shmona and in Western Galilee.

Sources in Northern Command explained yesterday that the intention was to avoid damaging tourism to the north, and the effort to try keeping life running normally as long as the probability of a Katyusha barrage was considered to be low.

Military sources were willing to say that "since the attack ended with only a few people being lightly wounded, the calculated risk paid off in retrospect. If we send the population to the shelters every time the danger of a Katyusha attack looms, the impact on these communities will be extremely harmful."

Ashkenazi noted that "from our point of view the easiest thing is to get the population into the shelters time after time, but that will be very difficult for them... in my best judgment we did not take a risk," Ashkenazi added. "We acted based on the information that was in our possession." Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz gave Ashkenazi full backing.

Sheikh Qawooq: Occupation is now Prisoner of Red Lines (4)
Hizbullah official in the south area Sheikh Nabil Qawooq said the Islamic Resistance's targeting of "Israeli" colonies (in northern occupied Palestine) was "an affirmation that we will not allow the occupation to vent its anger on our people."

The message was addressed to the public opinion in the Zionist entity after ("Israeli" prime minister Benjamin) Netanyahu tried to exploit an international and regional critical time to establish a new equation that would annul the usefulness of the April Understanding and put the future before the unknown, Qawooq said.

The resistance has restricted the margin of "Israeli" options, and the occupation is currently prisoner of red lines drawn by the Resistance's sacrifices. The attempts to overstep these lines have missed the mark, he added.

The Hizbullah official stressed that "the Islamic Resistance will go to any length to defend its people."
The Islamic Resistance is ready to confront any emergency, although it will safeguard establishing the April Understanding, he said.
Boueiz: Bombardment of Colonies was Retaliation for "Israeli" Aggressions (5)
Bombardment of "Israeli" settlements (in Northern occupied Palestine) was a reaction to the occupation forces' recent aggressions which constituted violation of the April Understanding, Foreign Minister Fares Boaez said today.

Speaking at a press conference he held at the foreign ministry before leaving for South Africa to take part in the Nonaligned States' Conference, Boaez said Lebanon is clinging to the April Understanding.

The "Israeli" government is using south to launch maneuvers. Through the ongoing aggressions, the "Israelis" are trying to justify their occupation of the area or to cover up secret talks which are underway between them and the Palestinian authority that may lead to redeployment in the West Bank according to "Israeli" conditions, Boaez said.

The foreign minister left Beirut tonight to attend the Conference which is to start on September 2.  He is expected to ask for the Nonaligned States' backing of Lebanon's positions against the "Israeli" occupation, especially rejection of "Israeli" booby-trapped proposals for conditional withdrawal from Lebanon.  He will also explain the inhumane practices of the "Israeli" occupation against Lebanese detainees.

Damage from rockets pegged at NIS 1 mil
Two days earlier, Hizbullah said in a statement that "Israeli" aggressions will no longer go unpunished and that IR will retaliate in kind. Each time we suffer human or material losses "Israel" should inflict the same. In this regard, Shomuel Meiry and Sharon Gal, Ha'aretz correspondents, made a survey about the "Israeli" losses. The following are some excerpts (6):

Government property assessors say that direct damage of at least NIS 1 million was caused by more than two dozen Katyusha rockets that slammed into northern "Israel" late Tuesday night in retaliation for "Israel's" killing of an Amal militia commander earlier in the day.

A preliminary estimate by government property assessors who visited the region yesterday found that about 100 apartments, 4 public institutions and 10 vehicles were damaged, and that minor damage was caused to farming areas in the Galilee Panhandle and in Western Galilee. Few buildings took direct hits. Most of the damage was due to the shockwaves that shattered windows.

Less easy to gauge is the scale of indirect damage, such as lost workdays and cancellations by tourists who had planned to spend the final weekend of the summer relaxing up north.

One hotel manager, Reuven Weinberg, said it was unfortunate that the army did not wait until the end of the tourist season.

Two volleys of Katyusha rockets landed on the northern edge of Nahariya, a coastal resort town, and to the north, about 20 minutes apart. Vacationers who were just bedding down for the night in hundreds of rural "simmers" threw their belongings together and headed south.

A few minutes' drive north of Nahariya, Katyushas hit the farming community of Lehman, named for U.S. Senator Herbert Lehman, where many residents rent out rooms during the summer. The rebuilt home of Moshe Cohen - his former house was destroyed by a Katyusha four years ago - was struck by fragments of rockets again yesterday.

Another Lehman couple who rent out rooms, Yardena and Yair ben Eliezer, said they told their guests, who were unfamiliar with the characteristic whistle of an incoming Katyusha, to leave at once. "Of course in all the hullabaloo we did not sit down to work out their bill," said Yardena, adding that she hoped the guests would contact her to pay what they owe.

Residents who were forced to scramble for bomb shelters after the rockets had already landed were critical of the security authorities for not sounding the alert earlier.

AUMG to Meet on Monday (7)
The April Understanding Monitoring Group is to convene next Monday at the Unifil HQ in Naqoura to discuss six complaints, three filed by Lebanon and three by the "Israeli" enemy.

The Lebanese complaints are over the "Israeli" aggressions on the towns of Jarjoua and Arab Salim last week which wounded a civilian and heavily damaged houses; and the "Israeli" indiscriminate bombardment and air raids which targeted on Tuesday Ain Al-Tineh and Mashghara (western Beqaa) and resulted in wounding six civilians, including a six-year-old child, besides damaging several houses and cars.

The "Israeli" complaints are over the Islamic Resistance's retaliation for "Israeli" aggressions against Lebanese civilians which took the form of pounding colonies in Northern Palestine with Katyusha rockets. Another "Israeli" complaint claims that the Resistance fired two rounds on an "Israeli" occupation outpost from residential areas.
