Daily Report
 
 
December Thursday 10 -1998:
 
Introduction
Yesterday, "Israel" had escalated the situation as its warplanes swooped low over Beirut causing two massive sonic booms and frightening the people everywhere. The warplanes drew anti-aircraft fire from the Lebanese Army deployed in the capital.

However, on the eve of the 50th anniversary of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, mothers, relatives and friends of fifteen Lebanese children detained by the "Israeli" occupation troops and their collaborators gathered at the UN Headquarter in Beirut demanding the release of their children.

In "Israel", the father of an "Israeli" officer killed in the aborted naval commando operation said he trusts Hizbullah more than his government.

"Israel" full of sound and fury, signifying nothing

Mock air raids all day as sonic booms over Beirut crack a few windows (1)
""Israel" chose to flex its military muscles over Beirut and the south Wednesday with warplanes breaking the sound barrier over the capital and staging mock air raids, panicking residents and drawing anti-aircraft fire from Army positions".

"In the evening warplanes dropped flares over Khalde, Damour and Jiye, capping a day which witnessed the heaviest air activity over Beirut in years".

"Warplanes swooped low over Beirut at 9 A.M. at supersonic speed causing two massive sonic booms which rattled windows and set off car alarms all over the city.

Cars ground to a halt and residents ran for shelter in shops and apartment blocks fearing the explosions were an air raid".

"It was the first time in three-and-a-half years that warplanes had broken the sound barrier at such a low level over the capital."

"Initial reports that missiles had been fired at a base for the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine-General Command in Naame were later denied".

"The Army, which has orders to automatically respond to air raids at any time, deployed armored personnel carriers equipped with anti-aircraft guns at major junctions in the city.

The planes returned at 11 A.M. and 3 P.M. to circle the city above cloud level, the rumble of the jets clearly audible".

"Warplanes also conducted mock raids throughout the day over Sidon, Tyre and other areas of the south. "

"Premier-designate Salim Hoss reacted angrily to the mock raids, accusing "Israel" of deliberately provoking tensions. "Israel" is trying to intimidate us and I deplore that. We ask the April Understanding committee to look into what the "Israelis" did today. Trouble is not what we need right now, he said.

"There was no apparent reason for the mock raids particularly as the south is enjoying a period of relative calm following the violent escalation at the end of November".

"However, air activity has increased noticeably since Saturday, with warplanes flying continual low-altitude combat air patrols over the south."

"With the "Israeli" government in a quandary over how to prevent Hizbullah guerrillas from killing its soldiers in the south, observers believe that the mock raids amounted to little more than a muscle-flexing exercise".

""Israeli" artillery pounded the Iqlim al-Touffah, including the villages of Jbaa, Ain Buswar and Louaize. Yohmor and Arnoun also came in for heavy bombardments during the day.

In the evening, the vicinity of Yater in the western sector was hit by around 18 shells. No casualties were reported"

However, the Islamic Resistance (IR) issued a statement in which it recorded the following aggressions (2):

20:05, three 120mm rounds fell on the perimeters of Jibaa town.
20:40, Litani River watercourse was bombarded by fifty 120, 155 and 175mm missiles.

Crude messages (3)
"The paucity of the "Israeli" political intellect was on full display Wednesday when the might of its air force managed to frighten some schoolchildren, unnerve a few of the elderly and send others, young and old, scurrying for shelters in the belief that an air raid was under way. It failed, however, to move the Lebanese political establishment or the resistance a millimeter from their appointed course".

"Sonic booms from the Jewish states warplanes as they swooped over breakfasting Beirut at the lowest altitude for more than three years rattled a lot of windows and even broke a few. Warplanes circled the capital twice more and flew up to Damour in the evening. The south was subjected to mock air raids throughout the day".

"It is easy to understand "Israeli" Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahus frustration and the need to make a big noise. At home he is facing the humiliation of defeat in a Knesset vote of confidence, early elections and the possibility of losing his job. Polls show that even lackluster Labor Party leader Ehud Barak would beat him at the moment by 44 percent to 41 percent. If Amnon Lipkin-Shahak, a former army chief of staff who is said to be considering entering politics, were to run against Mr. Netanyahu, the premier would be trounced by a 52 percent to 35 percent defeat".

"He faces desertion by his so-called allies in the Knesset. The Labor Party will head for home if Mr. Netanyahu fails to implement troop withdrawals from the West Bank as agreed under the Wye Plantation Accord and the extremist religious parties will run away if he does. In south Lebanon most of his once-feared occupation Army is holed up in concrete bunkers while Hizbullah ensures that increasing numbers of those that do venture out are despatched back to "Israel" feet first".

"What is certain is that Mr. Netanyahus habit of trying to be all things to all men by adapting his political statements according to the composition of the audience has run its course. Even the catch-all lets try to scare the hell out of the Lebanese is not very effective any more. Prime Minister Salim Hoss described the actions as provocative, not what we need right now and attempts at intimidation before adding: We uphold our attitude toward liberating our territory from the "Israeli" occupation. The Lebanese Army did its duty and manned anti-aircraft guns south of Beirut and in the south itself, and a Hizbullah official said with masterly understatement: Theyre trying to scare us but we are not afraid".

Insariyeh still haunts the "Israelis"; the father of an "Israeli" killed in Lebanon: I trust Hezbollah more than my government
"Israeli chief of staff Shaul Mofaz has decided to form a panel to investigate into Hezbollah's ambush which had been set for a naval commando unit near the town of Insariyeh (south Lebanon) last September", an "Israeli" newspaper said.

"The new committee which is to investigate how 12 "Israeli" troops were killed will be headed by reserve Gen Giora Zorieh, a former senior intelligence officer. It will include Gen Rami Ivrani and Gen Ehud Gal", the daily Ma'ariv said.

"On the other hand, the father of an "Israeli" officer killed in the fiasco said he trusted Hezbollah more than his government".

"Nehtsun Tifi told the "Israeli" military radio Hezbollah has returned the remains of four "Israeli" troops killed in the fiasco, not three as claimed by the ("Israeli") government".

"The "Israeli" army and Netanyahu's government deal with the bodies of killed "Israelis" as if they were the remains of military equipment damaged in battlefield, Nehtsun said.

Families of the "Israeli" troops killed in Insariyeh have repeatedly affirmed that the figure of "Israelis" killed in the fiasco was much more than that acknowledged by the "Israeli" government" (4)

However, "a Knesset subcommittee that carried out a secret, independent investigation for over a year into a disaster in which 12 naval commandos were killed in South Lebanon in 1997 was not able to find any cause for the incident".

"Uzi Landau, chair of the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee, said last night that the subcommittee carefully studied every detail of the incident but could not reach unequivocal conclusions about the causes. "

"It was clearly a unique and complex operation, and we might never find the answers to our questions," Landau said.

"The subcommittee, headed by Moledet's Rehavam Ze'evi and including MKs Uzi Landau and Dan Meridor (Likud), Ori Orr (Labor) and Ran Cohen (Meretz), held dozens of meetings with everybody involved in planning the operation, visited the base, and met with three commandos who participated in the operation and survived."

"Members of the subcommittee refused to reveal any details about their findings, but noted that the investigation raised several new issues that were not known to previous committees looking into the incident"... (5)

"Israeli" soldier plays with explosives, sets guard post on fire?! (6)
" An "Israeli" soldier injured a sleeping comrade and destroyed his guard post in southern Lebanon by playing with explosives, a military spokesman said on Tuesday. The soldier set off the explosives in a room next to where his comrades were sleeping. One man suffered burns, and a large amount of weapons and ammunition was destroyed. The other soldiers managed to put out the flames. "Their action was nothing short of a miracle. The incident could have ended in disaster," the spokesman said. An inquiry has been opened. The troops involved belonged to an elite unit, on duty in the eastern sector of the strip of southern Lebanon occupied by "Israel". "Israel" has occupied Lebanese territory in the south and west Bekaa since 1978 in clear breach of international laws".

Families protest detention of children in Khiam camp
"Relatives of young detainees held in "Israeli" prisons gathered in front of UN House Wednesday to protest "Israel's" imprisonment of children in Khiam and deliver a message for Secretary-General Kofi Annan."

At this moment, our children are still being held in solitary confinement inside Khiam, said Souad Chaayeb, the mother of 17-year-old Khanjar who has been imprisoned for the past 18 months.
Prisoners in Khiam are held without charge and are systematically tortured.

Citing articles from the Convention on the Rights of the Child and reading a letter addressed to UN Secretary-General, Kofi Annan, Ms. Chaayeb accused "Israel" of violating the fundamental rights of children. "Israel" as the occupying power should ensure that these children are charged and tried in accordance with international standards or released immediately.

Among the group of protesters were several women who had made the two-hour trip from the southern village of Arnoun. All had either sons or husbands in Khiam.

They held pictures of 14-year-old Ali Taoube who has been held along with his father for 18 months. Only six families remain in Arnoun ­ a village technically outside the occupation zone but frequently infiltrated by "Israel" or its proxy militia, the South Lebanon Army.

A year and a half ago, they came and took our children and husbands, said Samira Katbay. They havent released them since then.

Zeinab Assaf, 75, was being supported by the other women. They took the only son I had left, she cried out. My husband is dead and my other children left the country a long time ago. I am all alone now. Hes all I have. I am so old now and I need him.

Her son, 25-year-old Assem, was among the men jailed from Arnoun.

The women are allowed to visit their sons and husbands every three months, but they have to stand more than a meter apart and are not allowed to touch each other.

All we can say to each other is, how are you? how are you?, said Mrs. Assem. What else can we say with the "Israelis" or the SLA militia all around us, listening to every word and keeping us apart?

This is not the first demonstration in front of UN house, said Nabil Abu Dargham, UN information officer. There have been many before held either here or at UN headquarters at Bir Hassan. The letter you have here addressed to the UN Secretary-General will be sent to him immediately.

There are 135 prisoners being held at the Khiam detention center, among them are 12 children, all under 18. Another 45 prisoners are being held in "Israeli" jails" (7)

"Mira Alamedine, school teacher and activist said: "we came here simply to say that detainees are simply not someone else's brother or son, they are everybody's children. We came to support the protest and detainees should be freed unconditionally. Children have rights."

"The Convention of the Rights of the Child clearly states that no child shall be subjected to torture or other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment. UNICEF regional director, Ibrahimma Fall told Future Television:" the UN must call on governments or parties involved to honor their commitments to fulfill the rights of the child especially when dealing with governments who have ratified the Convention on the Rights of the Child. "Israel" is a signatory state to the international document. The international human rights watchdog, Amnesty International has issued this pamphlet entitled Juvenile Justice every child's basic human right. It highlights Ali's plight - a schoolboy detained without charge or trial and tortured. Amnesty has issued many reports that explain the torture detainees undergo at the hands of the "Israelis". Ali is among 15 other Lebanese children and over 150 adults held by "Israel" in clear breach of human rights. An "Israeli" rights group leader Eitan Felner said that "Israel" lacks quote a culture of human rights. His group investigates human rights in territories occupied by "Israel". Felner said torture still exists as a systematic device" (8)

An hostage explains his suffer in Khiam detention comp (9)
"Abdel-Karim does not like to talk about his 11 years in captivity. I was taken underground, a bag put over my head, my hands and legs were tied and I was dragged to a cell. For the first month, they put me in a one-meter-square cell with a bright light burning all the time. I was beaten. They kept me alone for the first six- and-a-half months.

Now 27, he is trying to rebuild his life after only knowing an existence as a boy-soldier or as a prisoner of the South Lebanon Army and the "Israelis".

I had a very patriotic upbringing from my mother, he says. Im proud of what I did. But I cant forget.

 We began to train. The guns were bigger than some of us. But we thought we were very big. I was convinced about what we were doing but not by the street war. In the south it was the real war. I went there at 15 and fought for two years before I was captured, he continues.

The favored interrogation techniques of the "Israelis" are well documented by Amnesty and other human rights groups.

The most common torture is shabeh: forcing the hooded victim to remain in a painful position ­ such as squatting or balancing on the toes ­ for hours, alternating with being doused with cold or hot water, and having raucous music played to deprive the detainee of sleep.

Electric shock treatment ­ to the genitals and fingers ­ is frequently cited, as is being suspended from a pole while being beaten. One prisoner in an "Israeli" jail has described being forced into a box just big enough for his body for such long periods that only watching an insect crawling around the box stopped him losing his sanity.

With the SLA, says Abdel-Karim, it was just violence. But the "Israelis" tortured to interrogate me about infiltration routes, arms and supporters.

"After seven months, he became just another of the many Lebanese hostages ­ and thousands of Palestinians ­ held in Ashqelon prison in "Israel".

Surprisingly, perhaps, he feels this was the worst period of his captivity. It was the routine. I always felt weak, they gave us just enough food to survive, he says.
