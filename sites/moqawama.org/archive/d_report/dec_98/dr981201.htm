Daily Report
 
 
December Tuesday 1 -1998:
 
Introduction
The "Israeli" enemy government remains divided over its policy in South Lebanon following the Islamic Resistance (IR) successful attacks which left eight "Israeli" soldiers dead in less than two weeks. Sunday's meeting of "Israel's" security cabinet broke off its discussions without reaching any firm conclusions.

"Israelis" delay reply to deadly offensive
"With the "Israeli" government in a quandary over how to address Hizbullahs deadly offensive in the south, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu sought to play for time Monday, reaffirming that there would be no unilateral withdrawal."

"But he refused to speculate about how the "Israeli" Army would deal with Hizbullah guerrillas, who, judging from the past two weeks, are able to penetrate the occupation zone and kill occupation troops at will."

"The Jewish states inner security Cabinet is expected to reconvene Wednesday for a second round of discussions with Army chiefs in which military tactics in the occupation zone will be re-assessed.
The stalled response from "Israel" is leading observers to suggest that the crisis will soon blow over and that the simmering conflict in the south will continue as usual."

"Referring to the first session of the 11-man inner security Cabinet Sunday, Netanyahu described the talks as very substantive. There were interesting proposals laid on the table but a unilateral withdrawal was not one of them, he told "Israel" Radio. I didnt hear any minister in the government say that we should unilaterally withdraw from Lebanon. No one suggested that we should get up and leave, he added.

"Sundays meeting was primarily an opportunity for the "Israeli" general staff to apprise the Cabinet of the latest tactical developments in the zone and unveil their proposals".

"Ministers are expected to air their views Wednesday, although the opinion of the "Israeli" Army is likely to hold sway"... (1)

"A fiery exchange erupted yesterday in the Knesset's Foreign Affairs and (War) Committee when Defense Minister Yitzhak Mordechai charged MK Yossi Beilin (Labor), who heads the movement for withdrawal from Lebanon, with "selling illusions" about a unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon".

"The stormy argument began after Mordechai charged that "selling illusions and false solutions is neither right nor desirable. I for one am not willing to gamble on security issues," extracting an emotional response from Beilin."

"Aren't you gambling there every day, every minute, with the lives of our soldiers? You know that soldiers can be killed in Lebanon any day. Get out of there! If you get out of Lebanon, there is a chance of a settlement. You think we are some bunch of casino gamblers who do not care about what happens in Lebanon, and in the meantime the march of folly goes on."

"The defense minister responded with no less anger, that Beilin was "gambling by thinking that you have a solution, when this is not a solution. You are misled and trying to mislead others. I recommend that you ask those members of your party who understand defense issues. Ask former chief of staff Ehud Barak, former head of the northern command Ori Orr, and former commander of the liaison unit for Lebanon Ephraim Sneh, what they think of your solutions for withdrawal from Lebanon. As far as I know, there is today no better alternative than the existing one."

"Mordechai's statements caused the usually mild-mannered Beilin to blow his top and storm out of the committee meeting, facing a battery of microphones and television cameras outside committee room"

"The defense minister sat in the meeting all smug and pleased with himself and said that he is not gambling," charged Beilin, saying that "Mordechai is personally responsible for the government's casino in Lebanon."

"MK Haim Ramon (Labor) said that during the late Yitzhak Rabin's term of office the government discussed withdrawing from Lebanon and even made a decision to that effect. The government was presented with four alternatives, but the plan was to withdraw to the border while guaranteeing the safety of the South Lebanese Army*"

"Mordechai responded: "I do not remember any such decision. I was a major-general in the army then, and I remember the military presenting several alternatives, but deciding to prefer the current one."

"In his review of the situation in Lebanon, Mordechai stressed that the army's main problem is in dealing with road-side bombs, which have killed 16 of the 21 IDF** casualties in Lebanon since the beginning of the year".

"At a meeting of the Labor Knesset faction party Chair Ehud Barak called for a gradual IDF withdrawal from Lebanon, in the framework of either a security or a diplomatic settlement with Lebanon. Peres said that the best way to solve the Lebanese question was by clinching a deal with Syria".

Monitoring group looks into 15 complaints (5)
"The five-nation cease-fire panel in charge of monitoring the April 1996 Understanding this morning met at the UNIFIL's headquarters in Naqoura to look into 15 complaints filed by Lebanon and "Israel" over alleged violations of the truce agreement. Lebanon filed six complaints and "Israel" lodged nine others".

"The Lebanese complaints center on "Israeli" shelling of the areas of Harees, Kfar Tibnit, Nabatieh Al-Faouqa, Jarjoua, and Baraasheet which resulted in the injury of three Lebanese civilians and the damage of a large number of civilian homes."

"For its part, "Israel" lodged nine complaints alleging the Lebanese Resistance shelled "Israeli" outposts from civilian areas in southern villages".
