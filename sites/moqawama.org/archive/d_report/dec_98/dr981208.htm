Lebanese children detained by "Israel's" proxy militia (2)
"Sabah Touba, six, proudly shows her grandfather the string of beads her 15-year-old brother Ali sent her, then turns to her brother Taan, who is 10: Ali loves me most because he sent me all these necklaces and didnt send you any.

"Their mother, Zeinab Nasser, tries in vain to hold back tears. Her son Ali is among 15 other children who have been detained in the Khiam detention center for more than a year now".

"Ali still sends his sister strings of beads every month, along with a letter to his mother.

Mrs. Nassers husband, Mustafa Touba, was detained a week before his son. The South Lebanon Army* soldiers banged on our door at 6 A.M. Ali opened the door and fetched his father, Mrs. Nasser said. A week later, Ali was dragged out of the house. It was cold and raining outside but they would not let him put a sweater on. They hit him, blindfolded him and took him away, she said.

"The detention experience did not escape Mrs. Nasser. She spent two months in Khiam, four days after Ali was taken, also accused by the SLA of being affiliated with the resistance".

We moved back to Arnoun four years ago because my husband couldnt find any work in Beirut, she said. We had no other choice, we couldnt afford rent and we have a house in Arnoun, Mrs. Nasser added.

"She is now staying at her parents house in Yohmor, less than 1 kilometer from the occupation zone.

The International Committee for the Red Cross takes Mrs. Nasser and other families to visit their relatives in Khiam every three months. Even then, they dont allow me to hug Ali. A grill separates us and I have to stand one meter away from him, Mrs. Nasser said.

"Visits are monitored by the SLA and detainees rarely speak of their experience inside, but speak of horrors once released. Reports issued by Amnesty International claim that detainees in "Israeli" prisons are subject to flogging, electrocution, beatings, and cold and hot water thrown at them simultaneously.

Mrs. Nasser saw her son last in October. Hes lost weight and he cries every time he sees me. Hes very pale and keeps asking me if people are campaigning for his release. What can I say? I tell him yes, she said.

"The National Association for Lebanese Detainees in "Israeli" Prisons is organizing a campaign Wednesday urging for the immediate release of children from Khiam detention center, one day before the 50th anniversary of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights".

"Association member Jamal Mahroum said that the first step toward the campaign was the associations participation in the International Book Fair Nov. 27. We displayed pictures of detained children, information about their detention and letters of appeal to the United Nations, Mr. Mahroum said.

"Thomas Ekval, representative of the United Nations Childrens Fund in Lebanon, said that "Israel" was a signatory to the Convention on the Rights of the Child and should abide by it".

"Article 37 of the convention, which deals with a childs torture and deprivation of liberty stipulates that no child shall be subjected to torture, cruel treatment or punishment, unlawful arrest or deprivation of liberty. Both capital punishment and life imprisonment without the possibility of release are prohibited for offenses committed by people under 18 years old. A child who is detained shall have legal and other assistance as well as contact with the family.

"Khanjar Shoueib, 17, was detained in Khiam 15 months ago. His mother, who went to protest about her sons detention with the SLA, was detained for 12 days. I had to do something. When they were impolite to me, I screamed back at them, Mrs. Shoueib said.

She saw her son for the first time after his detention in July. I was scared to see him after 11 months. I had missed him so much and when I saw him I started crying, she said. His beard was growing and he acted like a grown up. I asked him if he was being tortured and he said: Forget it but dont forget to remind people that we exist.

Parents of perished soldiers claim: Unauthorized explosives caused helicopter crash (5)
"One of the two IDF** helicopters which crashed in February 1997 near the northern border crashed because explosives which should not have been on board went off, parents of the 73 soldiers killed in the disaster said yesterday".

"The parents are demanding the attorney-general order an investigation into the disaster so that those responsible can be prosecuted".

"At a press conference yesterday, the parents' representatives rejected the findings of the Ivry Commission, which the defense minister appointed to investigate the accident, and accused the committee of hiding the truth. The parents have just finished reviewing the commission's protocols, which they obtained following an petition to the High Court of Justice".

"We demand the investigation of the disaster and the prosecution of the persons responsible for it, persons with blood on their hands who got away scot-free. These are the people who killed my son, and some of them weren't even questioned," Yoram Alper, one of the representatives of the bereaved parents, said yesterday.

"The parents said that the crash on February 4, 1997, actually consisted of two different accidents: one helicopter fell because it hit the other, but the second one, bound for Dla'at, crashed when explosives went off on board".

"Half of the soldiers killed that night could still be alive if the procedures had been kept. The air force investigated itself, and those to blame are walking around free," Alper said.

"According to documents and testimonies, the Dla'at-bound helicopter did not crash immediately after being hit by the other but continued to She'ar Yashuv, where its landing lights went on and it started circling and lowering for landing, the parents said. When the helicopter was between 25 and 50 meters from the ground, an explosion, which eyewitnesses testified was "louder than a Katyusha," ripped the helicopter in half."

"Sources from the Ivry Commission told Channel 1 the possibility of an explosion causing the crash of one of the helicopters was looked into and ruled out by the commission, as no sign was found to substantiate it."

"Another parent, Yehuda Rothenberg, said pathologist Yehuda Hiss demanded that autopsies be conducted on all the soldiers killed on the helicopter to Dla'at that night, but the air force forbade it".

"This would have revealed the cause of death as the explosion inside the helicopter, not its fall," he said.

"The Ivry Commission ignored all the causes of the accident, and accepted all the conclusions handed down by the air force. They hid the fact - discovered later by the parents - that the explosives weren't taken off the helicopter before the flight as required by regulations, and that the regulations had been violated, as they had been on other, previous flights," Alper said.

"The parents accused the committee of regarding the killed soldiers as "inventory to be struck off the roster" and of contriving to put the entire blame for the disaster on the helicopters' pilots, who also perished, as the easiest way out, with no consideration of their families."

"The parents further charged IDF Judge Advocate-General Brig.-Gen. Uri Shoham with acting as both adviser for the supposedly independent inquiry committee and for the investigated parties (the army and air force), in a blatant conflict of interests".

"[Shoham], who was representing the army, directed and steered the investigation, which should have been independent. He passed on material from commission meetings to the air force and the IDF top brass, so they could prepare themselves in advance for the commission hearings. He and the commission collaborated to close the affair and prevent the investigation from exposing the truth," said Ya'acov Gottlieb, one of the parents.

Gottlieb said the commission's debate focused on how to cover things up and close the affair quickly.

"They never imagined we'd get hold of the protocols, with their arguments with [Shoham] and their fears that someone might appeal to the High Court of Justice. The protocols show they forgot all about the disaster and the victims. All they thought about was how to close it, quickly," Gottlieb said.
"Shoham rejected the parents' charges and said he had never expressed an opinion to the committee in favor of conclusions of any kind, Channel 1 reported".

"The protocols make it clear the investigation was carried out by the air force rather than the Ivry Commission, Alper said, adding that the commission "did everything within its power to shift responsibility downwards, as low as the graves of the pilots."

"They cast false blame on the dead pilots, telling lies about the way they functioned, regardless of the feelings of their families. In reality, the pilots acted as experienced, veteran pilots should and did everything to save their passengers, despite a system of negligence and obvious shortcomings of those who today continue leading their lives as though nothing had happened," Alper said.

  Third IDF panel to probe failed naval raid (6)
 "A new inquiry committee will probe the failed naval command operation in which 12 IDF soldiers were killed in a raid by a Shi'ite force shortly after they landed on the Lebanese coast, Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz has decided."

"It will be the third investigation of the September 1997 disaster conducted by the army, and will focus on a question that has deeply troubled the families of the dead soldiers as well as many senior officers in the IDF: Was the ambush that the soldiers walked into set up by accident or was it pre-planned?"

"The decision to hold a new inquiry was announced yesterday at a meeting with the families of the soldiers held by the deputy chief of operations, Major General Dan Halutz, the head of the Human Resources Branch, Major General Yehuda Segev, and the military advocate general, Brigadier Uri Shoham"

"Justice Minister Tzachi Hanegbi yesterday recommended that (War) Minister Yitzhak Mordechai, with the concurrence of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, set up a panel to conduct an indepth inquiry into the families' complaints about the manner in which body parts of the commandos were buried. Hanegbi acted after meeting with the fathers of Sergeant Raz Tabi and Staff Sergeant Guy Golan, two of the soldiers who were killed in the raid. They told Hanegbi that no one informed them about the burial of the body parts and that when they asked questions, the head of Human Resources in the IDF told them, "You are problematic families."

"The first panel to examine the abortive operation, headed by Major General Gabi Ophir, found a "high probability" that information about the raid had not leaked out, but said it could "not totally rule out that possibility."

The new panel will be headed by Brigadier General (Res.) Giora Zorea, a former chief intelligence officer. Military sources said last night that "even after the Zorea committee completes its work, it is doubtful that we will have an unequivocal answer."

"Also discussed at the meeting yesterday was the appointment of a "trustee" for the families who will be able to examine all the classified material in connection with the failed raid. Yoram Golan, Guy's father, told Ha'aretz yesterday that the appointment of a new panel "is an admission by the IDF that it does not have all the information about the episode. The Pandora's Box is beginning to open."

Greenpeace demonstrates at Haifa Chemicals (7)
" Greenpeace activists blocked the entrance to the Haifa Chemicals plant in the bayside suburbs industrial zone area early yesterday morning by chaining themselves to the main gates".

"Two other demonstrators - all of them dressed in white overalls and wearing black gas masks - climbed a chimney stack inside the compound where they unfurled a banner denouncing dumping of toxic waste into the sea".

"The protest ended after nearly an hour when police arrived and dispersed the demonstration without having to resort to force. Fourteen demonstrators were detained for questioning and later released."
"The activists were protesting against the decision of a special inter-ministerial committee to allow Haifa Chemicals to put a further 2,000 tons of waste a month into the sea until the end of March".

"Ofer Ben-Dov, a leading member of Greenpeace, said the US-owned Haifa Chemicals, which produces fertilizer for agriculture, had for years been using the Mediterranean as a waste bin for its toxic sewage."

"The government should have symbolized the end of the current United Nations-declared 'Year of the Oceans' by halting this polluting business once and for all," Ben-Dov said. "Instead it is again insulting residents along the shores of the Mediterranean basin, especially those in "Israel", Cyprus, Egypt and Lebanon, by allowing the dumping of Haifa Chemicals' poisons in international waters."
Haifa Chemicals spokesman Eytan Loewenstein said, however, that "Haifa Chemicals has complied with all its obligations that were agreed with the Environment Ministry to cease dumping waste into the sea by the end of December."

"We are now awaiting authorization from the Environment Ministry regarding the site where the treated phosphate waste is to be sent. The moment they tell us the site we will stop dumping in the sea."

"Loewenstein stressed, however, that even if it proved necessary to dump the sewage at sea, there was no danger because all of the toxic materials, including heavy metal, would have been neutralized by the new treatment plant at the factory."

"We can't leave it at the factory, because we are talking about large quantities that have to be removed. In effect we were given formal permission that if a suitable land-based disposal site is not found by January we will be able to deposit the treated waste at sea," he added.

The Environment Ministry stressed that it had been instrumental in forcing Haifa Chemicals to develop the treatment plant and that once a suitable disposal site is found the treated waste would be sent there".
