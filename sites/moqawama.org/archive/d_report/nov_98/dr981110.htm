UN Experts: "Israeli" theft of Lebanese soil and water cause dangerous environmental and economic problems (3)
"Top world experts warn "Israeli" theft of Lebanese soil and water will inflict environmental and ecological problems. Top world experts are warning "Israel's" theft of fertile top soil and water from occupied southern Lebanon will inflict dangerous environmental and economic problems. The warning was made on the sidelines of a geology conference in Beirut, where experts are discussing the future of mineral resources in the Middle East."

"UN House in Beirut is hosting a four-day international conference on geology in the Middle East. World experts and delegates from forty-one countries, including the thirteen member states of the UN's Economic and Social Commission for Western Asia, are discussing challenges related to sustaining the Middle East's rich mineral resources including petroleum, and in some places like Lebanon, water." "The conference is taking place at a time when "Israel" is admitting its' occupation forces in the south are stealing fertile top soil and there is mounting evidence that "Israel" is doing the same to Lebanese water". "The National Council for Scientific Research confirmed "Israel" has been stealing Lebanese natural resources."

"Dr. Mohammed Khawlie, Director of National Council for Scientific Research, told FTV: "this is adding to the environmental deterioration that "Israel" has already inflicted on very large extent in south Lebanon in terms of forest fires destruction of natural resources, and now of course we do not forget the stealing of water resources and now we have fertile soil. What we can do as the scientific community is making use of platforms of international platform like this one and make sure that international presence and aware of what is going on in the south, and aware of the negative deeds of "Israel"". That seems necessary, since leading experts in the field of geology and mineral wealth seemed surprised that was going on. Dr. William Fyfe, Department of Earth Science, University of Western Ontario, told FTV: Lebanon is suffering from a problem which is there has been confirmation that "Israel" is stealing fertile top soil from the south. Dr. Fyfe responded: "dreadful, it reduces your capacity to grow food, it reduces the capacity of the soil to hold water I have seen this in many places this should not be happening. "Israel" has admitted to the theft of fertile soil from occupied south Lebanon, but has blamed its citizens of hauling the soil to the northern Galilee region. But that is highly unlikely for "Israelis" to transfer the soil across the border without the knowledge of the "Israeli" army."

"Israel" admits stealing soil from south (4)
"After two weeks of escalating diplomatic pressure, the "Israeli" government formally admitted Monday that civilian contractors from northern Galilee have been systematically stealing soil from the occupation zone."

"The disclosure, an abrupt reversal of earlier denials of involvement in the theft, came three days after the "Israeli" army toldd Unifil that tons of fertile earth had been removed from al-Marj just north of the border with "Israel".

Unifil said Monday that the theft appeared to have ended, with no activity in the area reported since Friday."

"The police in Kiryat Shemona have reportedly been ordered to investigate the removal of truckloads of earth.

The admission by the "Israeli" government came two weeks after The Daily Star broke the story, which initially prompted a flurry of denials from the Jewish state."

"After an official complaint by Speaker Nabih Berri last week to the United Nations, the European Union and the Arab League, "Israel's" Coordinator of Lebanese Affairs, Uri Lubrani, described the accusations as nonsense.

I wonder where all that came from. We are used to such accusations as "Israel" bringing the Litani River to Lake Kinneret. Now its soil. Tomorrow they will say were stealing their air, he said.

"Since "Israel's" sudden confession of guilt over stealing the soil, Mr. Lubrani has remained uncharacteristically silent, leaving his deputy, Reuven Ehrlich, to comment. From an initial inquiry it transpires that these are private contractors who have been stealing quantities of topsoil, said Mr. Ehrlich.

In any event it isnt a methodical plundering of Lebanese resources but the theft of some quantity of soil by local contractors, he said.

"A statement by "Israel's" foreign ministry admitted that soil had been transported into "Israel" and that the army and police were looking into the matter."

"Foreign Ministry spokesman Aviv Shiron said an initial investigation showed that soil was taken from an area near the border for private purposes."

"There was no comment on whether the "Israeli" army was aware of the theft. However, bearing in mind that the "Israeli" army has a heavy presence in the area and man the border crossing through which the soil was transported, it is highly unlikely that they were unaware of what was going on. The soil is being used to construct a series of terraces just inside the "Israeli" border."

" The soil of the water-logged al-Marj is arguably the most fertile in south Lebanon having lain fallow for years due to its proximity to the "Israeli" border."

Hizbullah keeps up steady pressure as "Israeli" TV offers glimpse of zone duty (7)
""Israel's" Channel One was planning to show a film Monday night in which a unit of Golani Brigade paratroopers spent a few fraught hours trapped inside a Hizbullah minefield in the occupation zone."

"The incident, which occurred a few months ago, was captured on film by a television reporter accompanying the paratroopers, who were on a mission to ambush Hizbullah guerrillas, according to the Maariv newspaper."

"The commander of the team suddenly realized that they had entered a booby-trapped area laced with Hizbullahs home-made Claymore-style anti-personnel mines. The mines were interlinked with trip wires and the paratroopers were forced to stand completely still while the commander informed his headquarters of their predicament. The beleaguered soldiers took several hours to inch their way out of the danger area, probing the ground with their bayonets."

"A senior "Israeli" officer was quoted as saying that if one of the mines had been tripped off it could have been even worse than the naval commando disaster at Insariyeh last year in which 12 soldiers were killed."

"When activated, the Claymore mine discharges hundreds of ball bearings and has a frontal killing range of about 50 meters."

"The mine is the deadliest weapon in Hizbullahs arsenal and is often placed beneath hollow fiber-glass rocks beside roads and tracks used by "Israeli" troops and South Lebanon Army militiamen*."

Cyprus warns spy case threatens ties with "Israel" (8)
"Cyprus Defense Minister Yiannakis Omirou warned Monday that the case of two "Israelis" detained on suspicion of spying could have a serious impact on relations as officials ruled out any possibility of a negotiated release. "If they are proven to be agents of ("Israel's" foreign intelligence service) the Mossad, then there is a certainty that there will be consequences for relations between the two countries," Omirou said in an interview with Cyprus public radio from Athens. "Certainly this is something which worries us especially in the context of the "Israeli"-Turkish military alliance which provides for the exchange of intelligence between the general staffs of the two countries," he said. Greek-Cypriot politicians have expressed great concern about a series of military cooperation agreements "Israel" has signed since February 1996 with Turkey, which controls the north of the divided east Mediterranean island. The leader of Omirou's EDEK socialist party, Vassos Lyssarides, a prominent hawk behind the government, was more direct in his accusations against the Jewish state".

"I believe this is a spying case -- what else could it be for heaven's sake -- and that those for whom the "Israeli" agents were working was certainly "Israel" and as a consequence Turkey," he told reporters. Udi Argov, 37, and Igal Damari, 49, were arrested after a midnight raid Friday on a flat in the village of Zygi on the south coast of Cyprus. They were remanded in custody for eight days on Saturday on suspicion of spying against the country's National Guard. Cypriot officials ruled out the possibility of a negotiated release for the two men as promised by "Israeli" Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu over the weekend. "The position is that the file must be completed and I don't think that the police have the possibility in this case of releasing anybody without instructions from the proper authorities that is (myself)," said Attorney General Alecos Markides. Omirou vowed that "in no circumstances will political considerations be taken into account. "And Justice Minister Nicos Koshis denied that "Israel" had even made any representations for the men's release."

"No-one has said they should be released and none has come to the ministry to intervene on their behalf because there is justice in Cyprus and there is an investigation," he said. "We have equipment of new technology and the law will take its course and if they are guilty then there is a due legal process." The "Israeli" prime minister had promised to secure the men's release and the Hebrew press reported that "Israeli" Attorney General Eliyakim Rubinstein was personally directing diplomatic efforts. "Every "Israeli" citizen is important to us. We will look into this and will certainly bring them home," Netanyahu said. The comments sparked anger in Cyprus with the island's second largest party describing them as "provocative and unacceptable." "They are an indication of the mentality which characterizes the "Israeli" government," said the communist party AKEL which regularly wins 33 percent of the vote and holds 19 seats in the 56-member parliament.
