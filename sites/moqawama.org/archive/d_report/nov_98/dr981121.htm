Daily Report

Introduction
The "Israeli" occupying troops have stepped up aggressions against the Southern Lebanon. The "Israeli" jets yet again carried out sorties on the South and shelling targeted the outskirts of villages facing the occupation region.

However, the Islamic Resistance (IR) uncovered new details over the Markaba qualitative operation launched by its militants on Monday near a border main "Israeli" post in which three "Israeli" sergeants were killed and four others wounded.

"Israeli" air raids in the south reach 100 mark for year
"The "Israeli" Air Forces 12-day blitz of south Lebanon reached a grim milestone Friday ­ the 100th air raid of the year."

"That is the highest figure since 1985 and reflects "Israel's"" growing desire to limit the involvement of ground troops in the fighting as much as possible."

Warplanes fired missiles at a wadi between the villages of Qabrikha and Touline, facing the central sector of the zone."

""Israel's"" greater use of its Air Force has increased steadily over the past few years from 31 raids in 1993 to 90 last year. The figures exclude the July 1993 and April 1996 air and artillery blitzes on the south, during which, in the latter, over 523 air raids were recorded over 16 days."

"The "Israelis" have launched three air campaigns this year. The first was in February, peaking on Feb. 22 when warplanes fired 40 air-to-surface missiles in 16 sorties."

"Toward the end of June, the "Israelis" resumed combat air patrols, which involve a pair of warplanes circling at high altitude over the south at any one time. The aim was to cut down on the time required to respond to guerrilla attacks against "Israeli" positions."

"The usual reaction time for AH-1 Super Cobra helicopter gunships to arrive from their bases in northern "Israel" to the scene of an operation is 10 minutes, while aircraft can reply inside three minutes.

But the effectiveness of the air raids is open to question. While "Israel's"" F-16 fighter-bombers can deliver a lethal payload of ordinance, pinpointing a mobile and camouflaged guerrilla unit is no easy task. "

"Hizbullah mortar squads are highly adept at firing several rounds quickly and accurately before melting away into the dense undergrowth and wadis of the south. The warplanes can loose a pair of missiles into the vicinity but the chances of causing casualties are remote."

"Helicopters, on the other hand, particularly the fearsome AH-64 Apache, can provide a very real threat to retreating guerrillas. Their thermal imaging equipment allows gunners to detect targets at night or in fog for attack with TOW and Hellfire missiles and 30mm chain guns."

"Other than sparing ground forces, the most plausible reason why the "Israelis" have chosen to increase air operations is that they provide good combat training for the air crews."

Most of these air raids do nothing more than break rocks, said a military observer. For the "Israelis", however, its preferable to break rocks in south Lebanon rather than in their training grounds in the Negev desert.

"Perhaps a final reason for the raids is the morale-boosting effect on the "Israeli" army as well as the public at large".

"The "Israeli" army spokesman trots out the same mantra following every air raid into Lebanon, in which warplanes attack terrorist bases and all our aircraft returned safely after hitting their targets. The inference being that the aircrews have flown through a storm of anti-aircraft fire to destroy Hizbullahs fortified compounds."

"The reality, as "Israel" knows very well, is that Hizbullah has no bases in the south and the danger to the warplanes is minimal ­ the anti-aircraft guns deployed by the army and the resistance have little chance of hitting fast-moving aircraft".

"Nonetheless, the present spate of air raids cannot continue indefinetly as the cost of keeping aircraft aloft for long periods of time is prohibitive" (1)

IR discloses more details of the Markaba operation (4)
"The Islamic Resistance (IR) yesterday disclosed new details regarding the qualitative operation carried out by its Mujahedeen on Monday near the "Israeli" occupation's outpost in Markaba in which three occupation sergeants were killed and four others were wounded."

"One of the operation commanders told al-Safir daily that the Mujahedeen were able to incapacitate the enemy's surveillance equipment planted around the military compound."

"The enemy's radar and infra rouge devices were disabled by using counter means, he said.
"The bomb planted by the IR men had a guided explosive wave which suits the target, especially concerning personnel armoring", he added.

"He affirmed that the bomb achieved its goals with extreme efficiency, noting that there were two resistance groups, one for execution and the other for protection."

"The Mujahedeen were positioned at a place allowing them to have clear view of what was going on, he said, adding that the power of the blow completely confused the enemy, "as reinforcement and ambulances arrived at the operation site 40 minutes after the attack for fear of other bombs."

The IR commander stated that "The resistance men withdrew calmly and swiftly after detonating the bomb, and they were able to deliver the videotape in which the operation had been filmed. The footage was distributed to Lebanese media at night."

"IR selects the sensitive spots which hurt the enemy at human, security, political, and morale levels. The Hasbayya operation followed the same pattern, and so did the Markaba attack, he told the newspaper."

"The enemy's military targets are under constant surveillance by IR around the clock, even after the ground-movement modifications made by the occupation to curb its losses, he added, stressing that IR had information regarding the time when the troops will go to the nearby training field."

"The enemy did not take precautions for two reasons; first, they did not expect the Mujahedeen to reach the threshold of a border post; second, the training field was so close to the outpost that the troops supposed that there was no need for any precautionary measures, the IR commander concluded".

Amnesty says "Israel", allies holding Lebanon cleric (7)
"Human rights watchdog Amnesty International said on Friday "Israel" and its local proxy had arrested a Lebanese religious leader last month and called for the cleric's release if he is not put on trial soon. "Amnesty International has just received news of the arrest of religious leader Abbas Muhsen Fadlallah whom the organization fears could be at risk of torture or ill-treatment and prolonged detention without trial," the London-based organization said in a statement. "We call for his release, if he is not to be charged with criminal offences and tried according to international standards for a fair trial," it added. Amnesty said Fadlallah, was arrested by "Israeli" occupation forces and their local militia on October 27 at his home in Kfar Kila, inside the Jewish state's south Lebanon occupation zone. He was taken to the "Israeli"-run Khiam prison inside the 15-km (nine-mile) deep zone. The statement said around 140 detainees were believed to be held at Khiam jail, some without trial for up to 13 years."

"Israeli" soldier's mother tried to commit suicide (8)
"The mother of an "Israeli" soldier due to "serve" in Lebanon tried to put an end to her life, because she feared her son would be harmed in Lebanon", an "Israeli" internet news wire said.

"The mother, around 60-years-old, tried to set her house on fire with a pile of newspapers, but was stopped by her husband and son", "Israel" Wire reported.

"The woman explained to the police in her area of residence in the north of occupied Palestine that she feared what could happen to her son in Lebanon, and that she would prefer to die before him than be told that he had been killed".

"When the police arrived at the house full of smoke, the woman was outside in an agitated state, but she refused hospitalization. She has no previous record of emotional problems. The soldier is the youngest of her four children. Further responsibility for the matter was transferred to local social services and the "Israeli" army", the news wire concluded.

Residents of Arnoun abandon their homes (10)
"Almost the entire population of the southern village of Arnoun early this afternoon left their homes for good, saying life across from an "Israeli" post had become unbearable. Some forty families deserted the village for the nearby town of Nabatiyeh leaving behind only about 20 elderly people who refused to move. "Israel" constantly threatens and harasses them. One woman said the mayor of the village took his belongings and fled yesterday. "We live at the mercy of the "Israelis" who are positioned in the Beauforte Castle a few kilometers away from us," a villager carrying his belongings said. "Whenever the "Israelis" lose a soldier in southern Lebanon they storm our village and arrest people and take them to the Khiyam Jail." Cases of torture and maltreatment have been reported in the prison. One 14-year-old boy was snatched by "Israeli" troops from Arnoun earlier this year and taken to the jail. Suffering is systematic in Arnoun. Arnoun is a front line village in south Lebanon. But unlike other areas that border the zone "Israel" occupies, Arnoun has a different predicament. It is practically besieged and often visited by "Israeli" patrols. We had visited that village 20 days ago and the report shows the situation in Arnoun."

Report: "The last Lebanese army checkpoint is a little bit over a kilometer from the southern village of Arnoun. Arnoun is not technically inside the zone "Israel" occupies in the south but it lies just beneath the Beaufort castle, one of the "Israeli" manned outposts. Cars are not allowed to enter the village forcing residents to cross by foot. Man from village said: Our village was a paradise, this man says. But it is not heaven anymore. It is a ghost town. He was right. We crossed into the village and the tragedy was evident. Most of the homes have been abandoned and it took a while before we came across people. There were some 30,000 people here before "Israel" carved out its occupation zone. Today the village only has some 30 residents and most of them are old. Woman from village said: Most of the people have fled because of the appalling conditions here, she explained. But my husband and I do not have any other choice. We had to stay. But young men cannot stay here. The "Israelis" will take them. At times, "Israeli" occupation forces set up temporary checkpoints right here at the entrance of the village."

"They search homes, check cars and detain residents who they suspect assist the Lebanese resistance before returning to their hilltop position. "Israeli" forces constantly keep a close eye on the village. And its residents have to deal with constant harassment."

"Man in village said: "Israeli" patrols enter the village. They searched my home, this man said. They look through the drawers, the closets, everywhere. They also besiege the village. When night falls, no one is allowed to leave their homes. This family lost their five year old brother. He needed urgent medical treatment but they had to wait for morning to leave the village. And it just was too late. But life has to go on. Arnoun's residents, or to be more accurate, the remaining few, mainly rely on tobacco cultivation and olive harvests for their livelihood. The serenity of this village is deceptive. The people live in fear. And the hardships they face are all too obvious. As this woman put it: the only thing people here want is to live a normal life."

"Israelis" charged with spying on Cyprus (11)
("Cyprus on Friday formally charged two "Israeli" nationals with spying for an unnamed foreign country on the militarily divided eastern Mediterranean island. In a case which has strained Cyprus-"Israel" relations, Udi Hargov, 37, and Igal Damary, 49, were accused of conspiracy to spy and possession of banned listening equipment. The two countries enjoy close relations, but, in an indication of how determined Cyprus is to clear the case up, the court in the southern port of Larnaca on Friday rejected an official request from the "Israeli" government to release the suspects on bail in the care of the "Israeli" embassy in Nicosia. "With the intent of harming the safety or the interests of the Cyprus Republic, the accused collected information on the defense of the republic and passed this information on to a foreign country," an investigating officer told the court. Defense lawyers had brought the consul at the "Israeli" embassy, Michel Harel, to vouch for the two suspects. "As a representative of the "Israeli" embassy and the state of "Israel", we undertake the obligation that the two suspects appear in the court whenever requested," Harel told the court. The request was hotly contested by public prosecutors, who expressed concern that the accused could take flight (). Judge Tefkros Economou sided with prosecutors. "Under the circumstances and with all respect to the consul of "Israel" and his good intent, the purpose can be secured only through detention by Cyprus authorities," he said. "Israel" has denied the two men were working against Cyprus or for Turkey, which invaded northern Cyprus in 1974 after a brief Greek-inspired coup. The two suspects were arrested at a village on the island's southern coast on November 7 after reports of suspect "Israeli" activity in the area. Police said high-tech listening equipment was found at their rented apartment, tuned in to police radio frequencies."

"Spying carries a maximum sentence upon conviction of 10 years in prison under Cyprus law. The two suspects were not obliged to answer the charges, which were translated to them in Hebrew from Greek by an interpreter. Standing at the dock in court on Friday, they just nodded when asked if they understood the accusations. Police said the alleged offences were committed between October 15 and November 6. The first date refers to another visit of the pair to Cyprus, during a large-scale military exercise with Greek and Cypriot armed forces. The region is said to be militarily sensitive and is suspected of being the site of a new Cypriot naval base. The case has fanned imaginations on the island, with media speculation ranging from the "Israelis" being spies for Turkey, to them snooping on the military in an attempt to gauge when the island will take delivery of a purchase of surface-to-air missiles from Russia. Turkey has vowed to prevent deployment of the missiles, the arrival date of which is a closely guarded secret."
