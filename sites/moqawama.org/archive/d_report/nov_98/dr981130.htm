"Israel" splits over response to its losses (1)
"With "Israel's" Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu vowing revenge against Hizbullah, a divided "Israeli" security Cabinet convened for a crisis meeting Sunday to discuss how to reverse the soaring military losses over the past two weeks".

"The meeting, which included the 11 ministers of the security Cabinet as well as top military officials, broke up after four hours with no decision announced. The Cabinet is expected to continue discussions Monday".

Its a complex debate which is going to take a long time, Internal Security Minister Avigdor Kahalani said afterward. Whatever decision is taken, it will influence the future shape of the northern border and discussions on such an issue cannot be completed in a day or two, he told "Israel's" Channel 2 television.

"Israeli" ministers are reportedly split over how to respond to Hizbullahs deadly offensive in which seven soldiers have been killed in the past two weeks. Some advocate air strikes against Lebanons infrastructure while others support Foreign Minister Ariel Sharons plan for a staged withdrawal.

"Hizbullah warned Sunday that large-scale attacks against civilian targets would trigger a heavy response and that the guerrillas were ready to face any "Israeli" escalation"

"Nonetheless, the "Israeli" army is reportedly considering abandoning some of the more isolated posts in the zone, which could indicate a commitment to withdraw from the northern central sector between Kfar Houne and the Litani river valley".

"A senior security source argued that the "Israelis" will choose not to escalate the situation"...  They have no choice but to issue a few angry words and half-hearted threats then nothing. I think Mordechais view, which appears to be that of the "Israeli" army, will prevail and the situation will de-escalate.

"For "Israel's" inner security Cabinet, the choices they face are much the same as during the last crisis in the south in August"...

Then, like now, the "Israeli" army is itching to retaliate directly against Hizbullah but, due to the guerrillas strict security procedures, it cannot find any targets to strike.

"This frustration is compounded by the fact that Hizbullahs three daring and resourceful operations in the past two weeks have remained completely within the rules of the April understanding".

"Therefore, not only would retaliatory air strikes almost certainly provoke revenge Katyusha assaults on northern Galilee, but smashing Lebanons infrastructure would probably garner little sympathy from Netanyahus domestic audience and would certainly be hard to justify to the international community".

Moms liken Lebanon protest to Vietnam War vigils
 
By HERB KEINON
" Sixteen years ago, soon after the outbreak of the War in Lebanon, Gail Auslander came home from her job teaching social work at the Hebrew University, looked in at her sleeping baby, and cried that he wasn't born a girl".

"That boy is now 17, and soon to go into the IDF*

"She wants to stop him - not by standing in his way, but by rousing public opinion to force the government to remove the troops from Lebanon. That way, she reasons, boys like her son - "young, naive, and ignorant enough to believe that nothing bad will happen to them" - will have no need to serve in the security zone".

"Auslander was one of about 100 people, mostly women from three different organizations, who demonstrated yesterday opposite the Prime Minister's Office, calling for an immediate IDF withdrawal from Lebanon".

"Among the MKs present were Labor's Yossi Beilin and Yael Dayan, and Meretz's Dedi Zucker.
Auslander said she is well aware of the dangers of pulling out of the zone, of how this may be interpreted as an act of weakness, and of the threats to the northern settlements. But as the number of soldiers being killed in the zone mounts, it is painfully obvious to her that the current situation is not working either".

"Get out..." a husky-voiced male protester signalled the crowd through a megaphone, "...from Lebanon!" the protesters yelled back. "From Lebanon..." the leader shouted again, reversing the order, "...Get out!" the protesters yelled back.

One woman took the megaphone and began a chant of her own: "The children are dying, and you are silent; The children are dying, and you are silent."

"That's powerful," the man with the megaphone said, "but not rhythmic enough."

"Get out..." he shouted again, "...from Lebanon!" the crowd yelled back.

Auslander, who immigrated from Washington, DC, 23 years ago, likened yesterday's protests to some of the anti-Vietnam War vigils of the Sixties and Seventies. Perhaps her memory was jogged by a man singing in Hebrew, "Where have all the flowers gone," one of the anthems of the anti-Vietnam War demonstrations.

"Indeed, yesterday's protest passed without incident. At one time, the group surged into the street, blocking traffic for a few minutes, but were pushed by police back onto the sidewalk".

"Another former American at the protest, Lynn Griver, who immigrated from New York 22 years ago, also reflexively compared yesterday's protest to the anti-Vietnam War demonstrations that she took part in".

Griver is now a mother of two living in Gedera. She has one son in the IDF, slated to go to Lebanon in a few months. The placard she held read, "Keep the soldiers on our side."

Griver said she probably would have joined the protest even if her children were done with the army, but the fact that her son is in uniform and due to serve in Lebanon has given her added incentive.

"I think it is wrong to be in the security zone," she said. Too many have been killed there to justify the cost. It is time to rethink the policy."

Getting out of Lebanon (5)
 "The deaths of seven "Israeli" soldiers killed in action within only ten days by Hezbollah ambushes in southern Lebanon have generated feelings of profound grief and great frustration. The "Israeli" public identifies with the bereaved families and prays for the safety of those soldiers who are serving in the outposts beyond our northern border. It is only natural that the need for our continued presence in Lebanon and our present policies regarding Lebanon have once again become the chief topics of discussion in our national agenda."

""Israel" has no territorial claims on any part of Lebanese soil, so the question is what is the best way, from the standpoint of national security, for "Israel" to get out of Lebanon. Previous proposals such as widening the security zone or intensifying the "Israeli" occupation of southern Lebanon have no relevance in the present discussion. Nor should we allow ourselves to even contemplate such suggestions as broadening the confrontation to include Syria (which is assisting Hezbollah in its war with "Israel") or a massive attack on Lebanon's economic infrastructures".

"An immediate solution would be a unilateral withdrawal in the hope that the guerrilla forces won't transfer their struggle against "Israel" to our border with Lebanon in order to strike out against the communities there. Should this hope prove to be a pipe-dream and we are unable to reach any agreement with a responsible government, the price of the withdrawal could be civilian casualties and the total disruption of life in all of northern "Israel". Another possibility, which involves a slow and difficult process, would be to carry out a withdrawal within the context of negotiations and treaties with both Lebanon and Syria. It is obvious, however, that the second option would involve a withdrawal from the Golan Heights as well, and not only from southern Lebanon"

"A peace treaty is unquestionably preferable to an unpredictable unilateral withdrawal. But the prospects for a treaty are dependent on the good will of the Syrians as well as the determination of the present government to continue the path to peace that was begun by the Rabin government. The current regime, which so far has not worked very hard at advancing the negotiations with the Syrians, will now have to focus considerable energy in order to renew those talks".

"A unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon is a solution based on a balance of fear, in which either side could use excessive force in the event of a flare-up, even one of local dimensions. That kind of solution could be a last resort, if concerted attempts to reach an agreement with Syria and Lebanon fail."
