Daily Report
 
 
November Friday 27 -1998:
 
Introduction
The Islamic Resistance (IR) militants leveled yesterday a new qualitative and panic strike at the "Israeli" occupying forces deep into the occupied region of South Lebanon, at Markaba itself, following last week's powerful operation which killed three soldiers and wounded four others. Yesterday, "Israel" lost two soldiers whereas another two were wounded as an "Israeli" patrol fell under an ambush carried out by the IR 100 meters only from the Markaba post.

This military operation has galvanized an already simmering debate in "Israel" over its presence in the South.

Netanyahu flounders
"Israeli" patrols now have a new drill before setting foot in south Lebanon. The soldiers each have a number written in felt-tip pen on the back of their hands and shout it out in sequence. The routine apparently comes in useful when they are under attack by the Lebanese resistance as a swift method of determining who is alive and free of wounds, and who doesnt reply because he may be dead or too badly wounded.

Meanwhile their political leader, Benjamin Netanyahu scurried back home Friday from Europe two days earlier than planned, after uttering a variety of confusing, contradictory and illogical statements.
According to "Israel" press reports, Mr. Netanyahu is considering a plan by his foreign minister, Ariel Sharon, for a phased unilateral withdrawal from the south. Mr. Sharon has suggested the idea because he has come to the conclusion that the "Israelis" have failed in their mission to prevent cross-border guerrilla attacks into northern "Israel". If that is how the "Israelis" want to camouflage the real reason for considering a withdrawal ­ the high number of casualties ­ so be it. The facts on the ground are somewhat different.

Since the PLO left Lebanon in 1983 the number of attempts to attack "Israel" from Lebanese soil has been minimal and, other than retaliatory Katushya strikes after "Israeli" shelling of Lebanese civilian areas, none has involved the Lebanese resistance.

At a press conference in London Mr. Netanyahu said Thursday: As soon as we find a way to withdraw our forces without abandoning the need and the ability to defend our northern villages and towns  we shall withdraw from Lebanon.

And very reasonable it must have sounded to a British audience ignorant of the fact that "Israel's" northern towns and villages are not now under attack and essentially havent been for the past 15 years.

Speaking to a domestic audience on "Israel" Radio a day later, Mr. Netanyahu said: The various ways to implement Resolution 425 will be examined by the Cabinet on Sunday. Certainly we have to think that as long as we dont have an alternative path, we will remain in Lebanon to protect northern "Israel".

It would be interesting to discover if even the "Israeli" prime minister understood the whole of that one. The truth is that Mr. Netanyahu is floundering. Domestically he is at the mercy of the Labor Party, whose leader, Ehud Barak, would rather have Mr. Netanyahus job than sustain the present incumbent for too long. On the Palestinian front, the prime minister faces the humiliating prospect of a Clinton-driven advance in the peace process which Mr. Netanyahus non-stop rhetoric of fear actively impeded. And on Lebanon his best advice comes from the father of one of the seven soldiers killed in the past 11 days. We must defend the countrys borders from within "Israeli" territory and not from any other country, said Moshe Cohen, whose son, Staff Sergeant Ayal Cohen, was killed Thursday. We dont have anything to look for in Lebanon, added Mr. Cohen Sr. Precisely.

IR brings down enemy's measures, strikes again in Markaba; 2 occupation troops killed (1)
"The Islamic Resistance (IR) has dealt a fresh blow to the "Israeli" occupation forces deep into the occupied zone, in Markaba itself, after last week's qualitative operation which killed or wounded 7 "Israeli" troops."

An IR communiqué read:
"In spite of the field and technical measures undertaken by the enemy and the inquiry committees to "deduce lessons", the Mujahedeen have established their superiority carrying out another operation only tens of meters away from the site of the previous one.

"At 22:50 (Wednesday), Martyr Ghassan Zaatar's in IR's Special Forces ambushed an "Israeli" foot patrol at the al-Qobaa outpost road, 100 meters away from the "Israeli" 70th battalion HQ, and 400 meters away from the border with occupied Palestine.

"When the patrol reached the ambushed spot, the Mujahedeen detonated a huge bomb, which was followed by a shower of machine-gun fire and rocket propelled grenades, killing or wounding the enemy troops. The Mujahedeen heard the loud screams of the "Israeli" occupation soldiers afterwards.

"In the meantime, IR's Artillery Reinforcement Unit (ARU) pounded the operation site, inflicting more casualties in the ranks of the enemy troops.

"The occupation forces heavily bombarded the vicinity of the attack site, while enemy helicopters had to intervene to evacuate the "Israeli" casualties under flare shells' light.

"Meanwhile, the Mujahedeen were able to retreat under heavy fire cover by the ARU".

An "Israeli" army spokesman announced this morning that two soldiers of the Golani Infantry Brigade were killed in the central sector of the occupied zone last night when an explosive device was detonated while they were operating in the area.

The names of the fallen are: Lt. Uriel Peretz, 22, from Jerusalem, and Sgt. Nitzan Bladran, 19, of Kibbutz Lotam.

A spokesman for the U.N. Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) in the occupied zone said a big blast occurred at 10.55 p.m. (2055 GMT) near Markaba. He did not elaborate.

In Marjayoun, the headquarters of "Israeli" occupation troops in south Lebanon, a pro-"Israeli" militia source said that after the explosion "Israeli" helicopters flew over the area and there was heavy shelling of areas to the north of the zone.

Moreover, IR at 10:30 a.m. today (Thursday) spotted an "Israeli" force in the vicinity of the occupation's outpost in Kfarhona.

IR's squad of Martyr Mohsen Zreiq targeted the enemy force with the appropriate weapons scoring direct hits.

"IR's squad of Martyr Salah Sh'aito at 16:30 targeted an "Israeli" motorized force which was fortifying the al-Qobaa outpost with the appropriate weapons scoring direct hits.

In the meantime, IR's squad of Martyr Khalil Rmaiti targeted the Qobaa outpost itself, while squad of Martyr Jihad attacked the occupation proxy militia's outpost in the same area".

"Furthermore, an IR squad at 20:35 (Thursday) detonated a large roadside bomb against an "Israeli" motorized patrol near the Blatt outpost, destroying a Merkava tank. The enemy troops inside the tank were either killed or wounded in the attack".

"Wednesday night's attack brought to 22, according to "Israeli" acknowledgment, the number of "Israeli" soldiers killed in Resistance operations in south Lebanon since the beginning of the year, 102 were injured. The operation raised to five the number of "Israeli" occupation troops killed in IR attacks in south Lebanon over the past 10 days."

"Two "Israeli" soldiers remain in hospital in serious conditions after they were wounded in a heavy exchange of fire with IR men Monday night."

The Daily Star commented on the new Markaba operation as saying:

"The boldness of the operation, coming just 10 days after an identical bomb attack, neatly underlined the inability of the "Israeli" Army to counter the devastating effect of Hizbullahs roadside bombs."

"To rub salt into the wounds, Hizbullah guerrillas again targeted the same post Thursday afternoon with mortars as "Israeli" soldiers were re-fortifying the barricades."

"Netanyahus options in the zone would appear to be limited. The offensive tactics of last year resulted in the Insariyeh debacle, in which 12 elite naval commandos were killed. "Israeli" War Minister Yitzhak Mordechai then adopted a defensive strategy of boosting the defenses of compounds in the zone and limiting troop movements to the minimum."

"While this affords soldiers better protection sitting in their reinforced-concrete bunkers, it allows Hizbullah guerrillas greater freedom to roam the zone and plant their roadside bombs."

Netanyahu is talking for domestic consumption, said a senior security source. Theres not much he can do and his comments dont herald a sudden withdrawal.

"The next likely initiative by the "Israeli" government is to dust off Mordechais proposal to withdraw in return for security guarantees. "Israel" is expected to seek the help of Washington and the UN to re-present the offer to Beirut. Lebanon has dismissed all offers, insisting on an unconditional pull-out under Security Council Resolution 425" (2)

Debate over continued occupation of south Lebanon flares up again in "Israel" (3)
"The recent heavy casualties in the ranks of the "Israeli" occupation troops heated up the "Israeli" public debate over whether occupation troops should continue to stay in the south Lebanon."

"In an attempt to ease growing outrage among "Israelis" owing to the increasing fatalities in the ranks of the occupation army, "Israeli" enemy prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu Thursday promised a review of "security arrangements" on the border with Lebanon following the killing of five "Israeli" soldiers in south Lebanon in the past 10 days."

"Speaking to reporters after talks with British Foreign Secretary Robin Cook, Netanyahu repeated that he would be willing to contemplate the eventual withdrawal of "Israeli" occupation troops from south Lebanon if a way could be found to meet ""Israel's" security needs" and protect its militia allies there."

"As soon as we find a way to withdraw forces without abandoning the need, and the ability, to defend our northern villages and towns, and without abandoning our allies in the south (of Lebanon)... we shall withdraw from Lebanon,'' Netanyahu said.

"When I return to "Israel", I will initiate a process which will take several weeks for a reassessment... of our current methods and tactics in the north (of occupied Palestine) in order to at once increase protection of our own forces there and at the same time accelerate the process of achieving the goals that I have just outlined,'' he added.

"Following the killing of five occupation soldiers in the past two weeks there appears to be an increase in the number of "Israelis" calling for the "Israeli" entity to pull out of the occupied zone."

""Israeli" Labor party lawmaker and former Prime Minister Shimon Peres said the government should abide by United Nations Security Council Resolution 425 and unilaterally pull forces back to the "Israeli" border."

"It must be implemented," Peres told "Israel" Radio. "There is no other way."

"But he said "Israel" would retaliate heavily in Lebanon if launched cross-border attacks are launched after a unilateral withdrawal."

"Peres stated that it would have been preferable to obtain Syrian guarantees prior to an "Israeli" withdrawal from the occupied zone, but it is obvious that this is not possible with the current political climate."

""Israeli" occupation army's chief of staff, Lt-Gen Shaul Mofaz".

"Acknowledged that in the past year, Hezbollah's activities against "Israeli" forces in the zone have increased significantly and the Hezbollah fighters are now capable of more sophisticated combat than seen in the past."

"Moreover, the "Four Mothers" organization, established by four women whose sons are among the occupation troops in the zone, is mounting more and more pressure against the "Israeli" political and military establishment to leave the area and bring an immediate end to the daily fighting which results in the death and injuring of "Israeli" soldiers."

""Israeli" proponents of a retreat from southern Lebanon vow to increase demonstrations, lobbying and other activities to make the move a reality as soon as possible."

"Israel's" media: "Israel" stuck between a rock and a had place:
"Lieutenant Uriel Peretz and Staff Sergeant Nitzan Balderan, from the 51st Battalion of the Golani infantry brigade, were killed a few minutes before 11 P.M. on Wednesday in South Lebanon while on their way to set an ambush for Hezbollah guerrillas in the "Israeli"-controlled security zone.

The force set out from the "Tsivoni" outpost, led by platoon commander Peretz. On Nov. 16 three Golani soldiers from the Tel Kab'a outpost were killed in the same area when they hit a Hezbollah bomb.

About two kilometers west of Tel Kab'a, and three kilometers north of the Upper Galilee city of Kiryat Shmona, Peretz's force also triggered a bomb - actually four explosive devices attached to one another. Peretz, who was at the head of the unit, was killed instantly; Balderan, the machine gunner who was by his side, suffered critical injuries that caused his death within minutes.

The platoon commander's order to the soldiers to maintain the regulation distance between one another prevented additional deaths. The platoon sergeant, Tomer Reichman, from Jerusalem, took command, organizing the evacuation of the casualties and calling for reinforcements".

"The lethal bomb was a relatively simple device, in contrast to the sophisticated explosives that Hezbollah has used before this year. The device may have been planted at the site a few weeks ago. The investigation of the incident will determine whether it was activated by remote control or was set to go off when stepped on.

One of the soldiers in the force said he had dimly seen what he thought were people running from the site of the blast, but this was still being investigated.

A senior military source said yesterday that the number of incidents in Lebanon this year was nearly double the figure for 1997 - more than 1,100, as opposed to 651 last year. This year 20 soldiers were killed in Lebanon, compared with 39 last year.

"If anyone thinks that an army can fulfill its mission in the face of more than a thousand attacks at a lower price in casualties, let them show us how," the senior source said. "People who think it is possible to seal hermetically 310 kilometers of wadis and brush simply have no idea what they are talking about."

"As "Israeli" casualties mount in Lebanon, the IDF* will soon examine the possibility of effecting changes in its operational activity there. One possibility under consideration is to reduce the number of "Israeli" outposts in the security zone."

"Next week, "Defense Minister" Yitzhak Mordechai will chair the semiannual discussions on the IDF's modes of operation in Lebanon. The IDF high command has already concluded its deliberations on the subject ahead of the discussions in the broader forum under Mordechai."

"A senior IDF officer said yesterday that "we will reduce our presence in Lebanon in those places where we think it is operationally correct to do so." A security official who advises the IDF in this sphere said yesterday that "the IDF is holding part of southern Lebanon until a settlement is reached, but there is no comprehensive perception of the goals of the fighting or of the military achievement that is necessary."

"The army, this source charged, "is suffering from a conceptual fixation; a new offensive doctrine is needed in Lebanon."

"After the discussion in the Defense Ministry, the inner cabinet is expected to discuss the IDF's operational activity in Lebanon and the government's initiative to withdraw from South Lebanon in accordance with United Nations Security Council Resolution 425."

"Defense Minister Mordechai said that he is "aware of the feelings of mothers whose sons are serving in Lebanon."

"The IDF will intensify its activity in the central sector of the security zone, which has been targeted intensively by Hezbollah. A number of residents in two villages of the area have been arrested by the IDF on suspicion of collaborating with Hezbollah."

"Deputy Minister in the Prime Minister's Office Michael Eitan (Likud) said the security establishment should find ways to protect northern "Israel" without an IDF presence in South Lebanon. He said that he believes Netanyahu is more flexible than the security establishment on this issue. "If we could find a formula, I am convinced the Prime Minister will support it," Eitan said.

"The death over the past 10 days of five "Israeli" soldiers in Lebanon - more than a quarter of the 17 "Israelis" killed there this year - has sparked a fresh renewal of the domestic debate on whether to unilaterally withdraw from the security zone in Lebanon" (4)

"Since the beginning of the year, 20 IDF soldiers, as well as 31 South Lebanese Army** soldiers have died."

"Most have been killed in Hizbullah bombings, and others by long-range firing at outposts, patrols and convoys"

"The government plans to discuss the Lebanese situation on Sunday, Education Minister Yitzhak Levy said yesterday."

"Netanyahu said that an evaluation of the situation in Lebanon was being carried out on two levels, with one level being a serious assessment of IDF tactics and strategies in southern Lebanon, and the other, work on reaching a long-term solution involving a withdrawal."

He said increased attention would be given to both levels in the coming weeks.

"We have said that we will leave Lebanon only with the assurance that with our departure, the Hizbullah will not be able to strike out at our northern settlements, and that our southern Lebanese allies will be protected," Netanyahu said.

"OC Northern Command Maj.-Gen. Gabi Ashkenazi said that the possibility of the involvement of residents of the Markabeh area in the recent wave of incidents was being investigated."

"Members of the SLA's security apparatus, backed by "Israeli" security forces, conducted wide searches yesterday in Markabeh and other villages in the security zone near the Tel Kabah position."

"Reports from Lebanon said dozens of residents were interviewed and that several people had been detained for questioning on suspicion of collaborating with Hizbullah."

"In a briefing with reporters at Northern Command headquarters yesterday, Ashkenazi said there had been over 1,000 incidents so far this year, almost double the number in 1997."

"There's no doubt Hizbullah has significantly increased its attempts to hit the IDF through a variety of means - counter fire, attempts to plant explosive devices and the shooting attacks of the the kind we saw at Dla'at," Ashkenazi said.

"Victories on the battlefield, however, require planning, training and the development of new methods to combat the counter-measures developed by the IDF and the SLA."

"At a certain stage, it appeared that the commanders decided to call "time-out" to devise new methods of operation, especially in the field of explosive devices, and to gather better intelligence to pinpoint IDF weak spots. This latter aspect has apparently been carried out with the aid of villagers in the south and almost certainly the collaboration of some residents of the security zone."

"During the interim, Hizbullah maintained its pressure on the IDF mainly by long-range attacks and hitting the South Lebanese Army, especially in the Jezzine enclave north of the zone."

"The upsurge in more accurate shooting earlier this month and the attacks since then are an indication that Hizbullah has done its homework - only too well - and has managed to infiltrate at least one bomb-planting squad into the security zone"

"The Lebanese battlefield has its own dynamics and not even the wisest of analysts can hope to predict what will happen - other than that the fighting will continue in one form or another" (5)
