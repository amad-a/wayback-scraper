Daily Report
 
 
November Tuesday 24 -1998:
 
Introduction
A few days following the Markaba operation in which three "Israeli" soldiers were killed and four others wounded, the Islamic Resistance (IR) militants leveled yesterday another powerful strike against the "Israeli" occupying troops. One "Israeli" was killed and two others injured as the Mujahedeen hit Merkava-3 tank destroying it promptly at the entrance of Dabsheh outpost. In another IR attack at Ali Taher outpost, the militants wounded two collaborator militiamen.

In the meantime, Markaba operation is still resounding throughout the "Israeli" circles. Haaretz, an "Israeli" daily newspaper, commented that those who can plant a bomb in Markaba can do so in "Israel".

In another article, Ha'aretz expanded in criticizing the continuous changes of "Israeli" tactics adopted in the occupied region to confront the successful methods of IR men.

One occupation soldier killed, two wounded in IR operations
"The Islamic Resistance (IR) dealt a fresh blow to the "Israeli" occupation forces as one "Israeli" soldier was killed two others were wounded besides the destruction of Merkava tank at the entrance of the Dabshe outpost, along with the wounding of two collaborator militiamen at Ali al-Taher outpost.

IR's Tank Marksmen Unit (TMU) at 13:45 today was able to destroy a Merkava tank at the entrance of the "Israeli" occupation's outpost of al-Dabsheh."

"Reuters and Agence France Presse affirmed that an "Israeli" soldier was killed and two others were wounded in the tank which was targeted by IR's appropriate missiles."

"Moreover, IR's Artillery Reinforcement Unit (ARU) targeted the occupation's outpost in Zaffateh, Beaufort Castle, Ali al-Taher, and al-Dabsheh with the adequate arms scoring direct hits.

AFP said 21 "Israeli" troops have been killed and 100 others have been wounded since the beginning of the year, most of them were hit in IR operations."

"Resistance men have also killed 31 collaborator militiamen and wounded 40 since January 1998.
IR issued communiqués explaining the occurrences of today's operations."

"On the occasions of Master of all Martyrs, Imam Hussein's Birth Day and Independence Day, IR dealt a new painful blow to the "Israeli" occupation forces and their collaborators, inflicting more casualties in their ranks and defeating the occupation's military industry by being able to destroy a Merkava tank at the al-Dabsheh outpost entrance.

"At exactly 13:55 today, IR's squad of the Nabatieh Massacre Martyrs' in the TMU infiltrated through the enemy's security measures and sophisticated surveillance equipment into a spot close to the entrance of the Dabshe outpost. The Mujahedeen attacked a Merkava 3 tank with the appropriate missiles hitting it directly and killing or wounding those in it.

"Meanwhile, IR's squad of Ghassan Zaatar attacked the Dabshe outpost from close range with machine guns and rocket propelled grenades, scoring definite hits. Smoke billowed from the outpost.

"In the meantime, IR's squad of Martyr Bilal al-Akhras blitzed the collaborator militia's outposts in Ali al-Taher destroying its main northern and western fortifications. Definite casualties were scored.
"IR's squad of Martyr Abdallah Fneish attacked the Beaufort Castle with the adequate arms preventing it from backing up the other targeted outpost.

"When the enemy emplacements tried to provide fire cover for the enemy to evacuate its casualties from the operation site, IR's squad of Martyr Hussein Mansour in the Artillery Reinforcement Unit (ARU) targeted the Zaffateh emplacement for 90 minutes scoring definite hits. The "Israeli" warplanes and helicopters had to intervene and stage several raids on the Habboush River banks, the outskirts of Upper Nabatieh and Tibneet.

"IR's ARU intercepted the raiding aircraft with the appropriate weapons", obstructing its activity.
Another IR communiqué read (1):

 At 16:40, the IR men carried out a series of attacks at the "Israeli" forces and their collaborators. The TMU squad of martyrs Mohammad Ali Ghazi El-Husseini and Ali Kawtharani penetrated all the defensive measures and attacked an "Israeli" force stationed at Rihan hilltops with the rockets.

The Mujahedeen managed to destroy another Merkava tank and inflict all its crew. A blaze was seen erupting in the tank.

Meanwhile, the group of two martyrs Hussein Merhi and Mustafa Chokair was targeting from a close range Bir Kallab outpost with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades. Direct goals were scored whereas the post was prevented from reinforcing the attacked force.

In the meantime, the squad of both martyrs Rabih Wehbi and Hassan Mreish targeted Aramta position with the rockets, destroying one of its fortification. Also, the group of martyrs Youssef Ammar and Ali Abdel-Wahed attacked Kassaret Al-Oroush position with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades chalking up direct goals.

Moreover, the squad of two martyrs Samir Mattout and Hassan Shouman targeted Sojod outpost with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades scoring direct hits (2)

"Israeli" Aggressions:
The "Israeli" jets and helicopter gunships fired several rockets at the area between Habboush and Arab-Salim as artillery shells pounded the area facing Dabsheh and Ali Taher compounds on the hills of Nabatieh at 2:05p.m.

Also, the "Israeli" warplanes carried out another sortie in the early evening firing missiles into the Iqleem Al-Toffah near the village of Jibaa town.

Three civilians were wounded when "Israeli" shells hit houses in Kfar Tibbnit, near Nabatieh. Mohammad Yassine, 73, his son Hussein, 30, and Ali Yassine, 8, are now recovering in the hospital.
"Israeli" warplanes, also, staged an air raid on a wadi near Zibqeen. No casualties were reported.

During an artillery bombardment of the Western Sector, a house in Haris was struck by a shell. Lebanon protested this shelling to the five-nation monitoring group (3)

"Israeli" debate over Markaba continues; Ha'aretz: those who can plant a bomb in Markaba can do so in "Israel" (5)
"The "Israeli" daily Ha'aretz affirmed that the artillery shelling by the resistance on "Israeli" outposts, along with the Markaba operation, fall into line with the April Understanding, describing these attacks as "authorized or allowed" as they "took place inside the (occupied) zone, and did not harm civilians."

Hizbullah sources underlined that its fighters knew the Tel Qobaa outpost very well, having observed it continuously and become acquainted with the ways of living and activity in it, the "Israeli" daily said.

What confirms this is that they did not rush to detonate the bomb, but waited until the soldiers walked close to one another to the training field, the daily added.

"One can imagine that the Hezbollah fighters are at this moment observing their next target; filming it with a video camera and gathering information from the villages or the (collaborator) militiamen", Ha'aretz said.

"The daily bitterly noted that "Hezbollah media no longer need to brag about their successes, as they are largely quoting what "Israeli" television reporters, officers, soldiers and Knesset members are saying, for the success of Hezbollah's media does not need more than this."

"No militia inquiry panel can change the current facts in Lebanon. Moreover, instruction letters are about to explode due to the amount of directions in them", the daily said.

"Those who want to know how such operations take place must ask Hezbollah, for its fighters can explain what is known: every "Israeli" solider in Lebanon is a target", Ha'aretz added.
"The newspaper said "Those who can plant a bomb 300 meters from the border (with occupied Palestine) can also plant it in ""Israel""; but efforts are not needed as long as achievements are possible inside the Lebanese court and as Hizbullah's ambition is just to expel the "Israeli" army."

"On the other hand, enemy Deputy Prime Minister Moshe Ketsav called for "Israeli" immediate withdrawal from south lebanon, saying that "there are no political motives preventing such a step if the "Israeli" army say the border can be protected from within".

"On the other hand, "Israeli" war minister Yitzhak Mordechai said his entity is renewing its proposal to reach a security agreement so it can withdraw its occupation troops from south Lebanon."

"Popular pressure has mounted within ""Israel"" to withdraw the occupation soldiers following heavy losses in their ranks in resistance operations."

"Mordechai who made his remarks as he toured the occupied Syrian Golan Heights said efforts had been put to a halt because of the Lebanese elections and the US focusing on the Palestinian-"Israeli" track."

"In another related development ""Israel" Wire", an enemy internet news site, disclosed that the occupation's army commander of the sector of the occupied zone in which three soldiers lost their lives last week "reportedly requested to be relieved of his assignment one month ago amidst heavy "Israeli" army casualties in the area of his responsibility".

"The officer, identified as Colonel "Z", submitted his transfer request to "Israeli" Chief of Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaul Mofaz about one-month prior to last week's incident according to veteran military reported Carmella Menashe, of "Israel" Radio", "Israel" Wire said.

"Following the deaths of soldiers in October, the battalion commander told Mofaz that he felt responsible for the incidents and requested to step down from his assignment, it added, noting that the commander is responsible for the eastern sector of the occupied zone, where fighting (between resistance men and the occupation troops) takes place on almost a daily basis."

"Israeli" repressive actions force Arnoun's residents to dislodge (6)
"A few undernourished cats were the only signs of life in the deserted and silent streets of Arnoun Saturday a day after almost the entire population departed the village."

"The animals paced silently through Arnouns narrow streets, past empty single-story stone houses, past the villages only shop, now closed and abandoned. The only sound was the eerie moan of wind blowing through the electricity lines, a perfect accompaniment to what has become a ghost town.
A week ago, the streets had been filled with people going about their everyday business. But when the mukhtar, Afif Hamdan, left the village last week most of the 250 residents followed him along the single road that links Arnoun with the rest of Lebanon."

"The village is five kilometers southeast of Nabatieh and its untidy sprawl of houses lies scattered along the grassy slopes of a hill dominated by Beaufort Castle."

"The entire village is overshadowed by the remains of this Crusader fortress whose shattered and crumbling walls, overgrown with vegetation, fail to diminish its imposing aura."

"Half the village officially lies in the occupation zone ­ a swing gate denotes the border. But the other half, supposedly in free Lebanon, in fact falls into an uneasy no-mans land.

The Lebanese army has no jurisdiction there because its presence ends one-and-a-half kilometers away at a checkpoint."

"Beaufort castle is an important "Israeli" position. Its ramparts bristle with radio antennae and sandbagged gun emplacements. The blue and white stripes of a Star of David flag flutters from the parapet."

"For 16 years, the villagers have faced almost daily harassment from the "Israeli" soldiers manning the castle. The troops enter Arnoun at will to conduct house-to-house searches, question villagers and even detain some of them in Khiam prison."

"Five residents, Abbas Attieh, Ali Tawbe, Assem Assef, Mohammed Qaid-Bey and Mustafa Tawbe have spent more than a year in Khiam."

"When Mr. Hamdan left the village he took with him his tractor which had been the only means of delivering tanks of water to the residents."

"The prospect of no more water was the final straw for the long-suffering residents."

The last supply arrived on Friday. When its finished thats it. No more water. How can anyone stay in the village then? asked a 50-year-old resident who requested anonymity.

There are only five or six families left in Arnoun, although two of them were planning to leave soon. "() He added that he no longer lived in the village but had returned to harvest his tobacco.
Once thats done Im leaving for good, he said.

A divorced mother of three, who also refused to be named, spoke bitterly of the lack of support from the government."

"()  We are threatened with death every day here. I want to leave but theres nowhere for me to go. I have no money and no husband. Whats the point of talking. Were finished, she said as tears streamed down her cheeks."

"Also trapped in the village was Khadija Khawaja, 40, and her family. She had packed all her household goods in preparation to depart but admitted she had no place to go."

I asked how much it was to rent a house in Maifadoun or Chouqine (a few kilometers from Arnoun) and was told it would be LL250,000 a month. We cant afford that so we have to stay here, she said.

The familys clothing and blankets had been tied into bundles and stacked by the front door along with kitchen implements and jars of olives. In another room lay 600 kilograms of tightly wrapped tobacco awaiting delivery to Nabatieh.

This is our income but I cant get it out of the village, Mrs. Khawaja lamented.
No cars are allowed inside Arnoun. They are parked beside a dried up pond at the entrance to the village.

"Indicating two wheelbarrows, Mrs. Khawaja joked, these are our cars in Arnoun.

As she prepared glasses of steaming tea, Mrs. Khawaja admitted that her outwardly cheerful demeanor was an attempt to bolster the spirits of her family. My father is very depressed at whats happening here and I dont want him to be sad, she said.

Her father, Ali, 75, lost his left leg in a land mine explosion ().

At least one villager stubbornly refuses to leave Arnoun.

I was born here, Ive lived here and I want to die here. I am 82 years old and Im staying, said Mohammed Marouni.

He said all the village needed was a regular supply of water.

There is a very real fear that if the last of the few remaining residents decide to leave, the "Israelis" may dynamite the village to prevent the resistance from using it as cover to stage attacks on the fortress.

Weve been hoping every year since 1992 that this would be the year the "Israelis" leave, said Mr. Khawaja. Now it looks like 1998 is the year that we leave.

In South Lebanon, another change in tactics
"Entrenched by the northwestern edge of the south Lebanon security zone, the Rotem outpost is now completely protected. A reinforced concrete roof covers the space between the rooms. The mortar fire, coming every few days from Hezbollah guns in an adjacent wadi, hardly make an impact. This, in a cement-shell, is the IDF's* situation in Lebanon in late 1998: a protected, jacketed army conducting missions in the decreasing maneuvering room left by the political echelon."

"Officers don't know what to do with the space created underneath. Until a few years ago, soldiers played basketball here. Today no one ventures out without helmet and flak jacket."

"Installing additional protective measures is currently the main project in Lebanon. Without them, IDF casualties would have been much higher this year."

"Since early this year, there have been over one thousand incidents of enemy fire (some anti-tank weapons, mainly mortars), which is five times more than two years ago. Thus, these measures are a function of change in fighting methods. As usual, when the army identifies a problem (often, too late) it goes all out to solve it. Millions have been invested in reinforcing outposts, making them nearly impenetrable."

"But it's just another vicious cycle characteristic of the conflict in Lebanon. In the end, Hezbollah, because of the IDF's defensive actions, will have to seek newer, deadlier, fighting methods and then the IDF will seek new solutions and so on."

"The familiar rituals recur with tragic regularity. Three Golani soldiers killed last week by a Hezbollah landmine some 30 meters away from Tel Kaba'a outpost in the central sector, or yesterday's three soldiers were wounded by mortar fire."

"Each time, the incident briefly reopens public debate over "Israel's" presence in Lebanon. But up north, they aren't so sure that the public elsewhere understands what's really going on in the security zone".

"What Granot doesn't know is that the army is actually to blame for the remote media coverage of Lebanon. Since the beginning of this year, correspondents' visits to the security zone have been sharply curtailed."

"But, this is approach is misguided. Hezbollah is using the "Israeli" media vacuum for its own purposes, and the IDF, perhaps unjustly, is perceived as having something to hide. Sometimes the "Four Mothers" movement, which wants an immediate, unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon, seems to have more effective public relations than the IDF spokesperson in Lebanon."

"A casual survey of soldiers in various places inside and out of Lebanon indicates that most 19 and 20-year-olds in the security zone are clear about their mission and have no reservations."

"Nahal soldiers manning the Rotem outpost say the problem is at home. "Most parents know we are in Lebanon. It's better that they know. But I know soldiers who would rather not tell them," says Ramat Gan resident Amir Lev. ().

"How is victory measured in Lebanon? Amiram Levine's analysis referred to the status quo, not to a decisive result. The IDF is ostensibly there to protect residents of the north, he argued in internal army discussions, but the fact that the Shiite organizations are not firing katyushas is not necessarily because we are preventing them, but because we are not annoying them too much."

"If we kill too many of them they will respond with katyushas. A kind of balance of fear has been created whereby neither side is interested in making its opponents' lives too miserable. Levine felt that the way to maintain low IDF casualties required an ongoing offensive that would keep Hezbollah away from the security zone. The current method favored by the defense minister and chief of staff's is to reinforce outposts. But, will this lead to an abandonment of the offensive initiative, which would in turn create a new vacuum and renew Hezbollah efforts?"

"We get many attacks here," says western brigade commander Colonel Alon, "for every mortar bomb fired at us, I can fire 100 back, but if the 99th hits a house in the village and costs us katyushas on Nahariya, that's dangerous. There are days when we respond aggressively and days when I give an order to swallow it ()."

"Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee chair, Likud MK Uzi Landau, has another solution. "The real problem is the policy imposing controlled rules of engagement on the IDF. We don't even ask ourselves how the opposite side freely dispatches organizations unhindered. We need to change the rules in Lebanon.

"Earlier this year, the IDF saw some improvement. Dozens of Amal and Hezbollah fighters were injured after the army made tactical adjustments in its assault patterns. The volume of operations was huge. This was urged by Amiram Levine in pre-retirement interviews. But lately, the IDF has been striking less frequently at the guerrilla fighters. Some army officials note extreme caution in operations and fear its impact on tactics and morale: "It's easier to explain the death of a comrade in a clash in which terrorists were killed than a death from a roadside charge or mortar. The soldiers cannot be allowed to feel as though they are punching bags."

But a senior officer says, "we haven't changed anything, but when you send soldiers on a mission, you calculate very carefully the necessity and risk entailed. This approach applies to every security zone operation, not just to those beyond the dark mountains."

"Behind these remarks lies the IDF's usual trap in Lebanon. Northern Command is in an impossible bind. IDF-initiated operations may entail heavy casualties, but staying holed up in the outposts gives Hezbollah greater freedom of operation and lead to more losses."

"Meanwhile, the document advocating departure from Lebanon, prepared by the previous head of the Command staff, Brigadier General Shai Avital, is practically forgotten and the prime minister's withdrawal initiative according to resolution 425 is more frozen than his smile when meeting with Yasser Arafat."

"Last week, military intelligence chief Amos Malka reiterated the mantra that Hezbollah will not be satisfied with a unilateral IDF withdrawal to the international border and that whoever thinks otherwise is "naive or fantasizing." But this week several ministers said that if the IDF says us unilateral withdrawal is possible, it must be considered."
