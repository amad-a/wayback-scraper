Lebanon asks UN if it will disarm Palestinian camps(1)

Lahoud: It is Resistance that forced "Israel" to withdraw

"Lebanese President Emile Lahoud Wednesday set out to the United Nations his country's concerns about its security in the aftermath of an "Israeli" withdrawal from the southern border strip.

In a "presidential memorandum" addressed to UN Secretary General Kofi Annan he asked if UN peacekeepers would be prepared to disarm the Palestinian camps in Lebanon before deploying on the border, and how they would prevent further "Israeli" attacks.

The three-page document contained eight points dealing with the proposed international force that is to help the Lebanese government restore its authority in the border strip that has been occupied by "Israel" for 22 years.

Lahoud made it clear that Lebanon wants all the questions answered before it is ready to discuss in detail the conditions for the pullback which "Israel" has pledged to carry out by July.

"If certain Palestinian groups tried to carry out operations across the border in the context of their right of return and in the absence of any solution for their future, do you think the international force will be able to carry out daily small-scale military operations at the border???" the document asked, underlining Lebanese concern by using three question marks.

It said that the 1978 "Israeli" invasion was "provoked" by Palestinian refugees, and asked whether, in the light of "previous experience," Annan would not consider it in Lebanon's interest "for the international force to disarm the Palestinian camps, or to take part on the ground in such an operation ... before deploying at the border."

Some 350,000 Palestinians live in Lebanon, nearly half of them in the 12 camps scattered through the country. The Lebanese constitution forbids their permanent settlement.

The document pointed out that UN resolution 425, which calls for an "Israeli" withdrawal, also states that the task of the international force is to "assist the government of Lebanon in ensuring the return of its effective authority in the area."

"Is it possible to recover this authority without disarming the camps?" it asked.

The document also raised the issue of possible future "Israeli" "aggression" against Lebanon, or violations by it of UN resolutions, and asked whether the UN force will have the means to deter "Israel" attacks by land, sea or air.

The document also put the case for war reparations, which Lebanon has estimated at 70 billion dollars for loss of life and moral and material damage.

"Who has decided to exempt "Israel" from the compensation due to Lebanon and its people? ... On what grounds has it been forgiven?" it asked, pointing out that "Israel" has only decided to withdraw in the face of attacks by the Lebanese resistance forces.

Lahoud presented the memorandum to Annan's envoy Terje Roed-Larsen, who came to Beirut to brief him about Annan's meeting in Geneva Tuesday with "Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levy.

Copies of the memorandum were also distributed to the press.

Roed-Larsen described his meeting with Lahoud as "excellent." He said he had given the president a written message from Annan about his talks with Levy.

He is scheduled to meet parliamentary speaker Nabih Berri and Prime Minister Salim Hoss Thursday before going on to Damascus.

"Mr. Annan wanted to sent Mr. Roed-Larsen quickly not only to inform Beirut and Damascus about "Israel's" position on its withdrawal from southern Lebanon, but also to find out the Lebanese and Syrian reactions to the question," a top UN official told AFP, asking not to be named.


"Israel" frees Lebanese held for 13 years(2)

"A Lebanese civilian held in detention in "Israel" for several years, had an emotional reunion with his family here Wednesday, before being taken away for debriefing by the Lebanese army.

Ghassan Dirani, 35, burst into sobs as he fell into his parents' arms, saying: "I've missed you so much!"

He scarcely recognised his brother Ali.

"Is it really you?" he asked.

The "Israeli" authorities released Dirani earlier Wednesday, handing him over to a delegation of the International Committee for the Red Cross at the Fatima Gate, the crossing point at the Lebanese-Palestinian international borders.

He was immediately whisked in an ICRC car across the "Israeli"-occupied border zone in southern Lebanon toward the Kfar Falous passageway where he was due to be handed over to the Lebanese army.

"I was kidnapped at the age of 22 in the eastern sector during the days of Samir Geagea while I was trying to get to the United States via Cyprus, and they handed me over to the "Israelis"," Dirani told AFP at the handover.

Geagea was the head of the now disbanded pro-"Israeli" Christian Lebanese Forces militia which controlled east Beirut and surrounding mountains during the country's 1975-1990 civil war.

"The "Israelis" took me because I am related to Mustapha Dirani, who is my father's cousin, but I never saw him in the "Israeli" prisons where I was held, and I don't know anything about him except that we have the same lawyer," he said.

Mustapha Dirani, a militant leader, was abducted from Lebanon in 1994 and is one of several leading figures being held by "Israel" as bargaining chips to secure the return of missing "Israeli" servicemen, including air force navigator Ron Arad who was captured in Lebanon in 1986.

Ghassan's father, Faris, told AFP that the ICRC had informed him of his son's imminent release the previous evening.

He said his son had no political affiliations, and had been a bank employee in the southern suburbs of Beirut when he was kidnapped. He was held in a Lebanese Forces prison for 28 months before being handed over to the "Israelis", he added.

A spokeswoman for the Tel Aviv district court, Anat Naor, told AFP in "Israel" earlier Wednesday that Dirani was being released from administrative detention because he was in poor health, she said.

"Israel" is still holding a number of Lebanese civilians without trial or beyond the expiry of their sentences.

They include Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid, abducted in 1989 and held incommunicado in "Israel" ever since.

Mustafa Dirani claims to have been tortured during his captivity and last month his lawyer Tzvi Rish filed a 1.5-million-dollar law suit against "Israel", saying his client had been systematically beaten and raped by his "Israeli" jailers.


Demonstration against lack of preparation for "Israeli" pullout(3)

"Dozens of "Israeli" villagers on the country's northern border demonstrated Wednesday against the "unpreparedness" of their area for the aftermath of "Israel's" planned pullout from southern Lebanon.

The demonstrators blocked the road leading to the village of Margaliot, waving banners saying: "We want shelters ... The government isn't listening to our needs."

The "Israeli" government has committed itself to pulling its troops out of southern Lebanon by July, whether or not it has reached a deal with Lebanon or Syria, the main powerbroker in the country".

We have grown used to see treachery from "Israel's" side, he said.

Poor man's Masada(5)
"South Lebanon Army* commander General Antoine Lahad's Masada-type speech, in which he vowed to fight to the bitter end, touched Lieutenant Colonel (res.) Danny Reshef, a former intelligence officer who lives at Moshav Hosen in the Upper Galilee. Reshef, an outspoken advocate of a Lebanon withdrawal, sent an angry letter to Ha'aretz. Upon examination, its facts hold true: "[Presumably,] the first ones to join Lahad's call to fight to the end would be the 80,000 residents of the security strip [in Lebanon] who have fled since it was established," Reshef writes.
Sarcastically, he continues: "Escaping from the warfare, these refugees left behind homes, fields, and other assets. The prospect that they would be able to return and cultivate their farms and restore their homes is for them so terrifying, that most of them would make haste to enlist in this [Lahad-led] 'Last Bullet' operation. Next behind them would be 85,000 residents of South Lebanon, who are thrilled to live in a war zone. These residents, who are cut off from their loved ones and from business elsewhere in Lebanon, are compelled to deal with the SLA whenever they wish to visit their family and pay special 'fees,' and go through exhausting check-points en route to the North."
Reshev writes with bitter scorn, alluding to SLA conscripts, most of them Shiites, whom Lahad incorporated in his call for collective suicide. Reshev sarcastically describes SLA top brass, officers who (Lahad claims) would relinquish monetary claims, and forfeit the chance to save their own skin and re-build their families. Reshef's ridicule for Lahad is especially acute: "Here, just like the captain who is the last to jump off a sinking ship, he's giving up his money, leaving his apartment in Paris, where he lives most days of the year, and arriving in Lebanon to serve as a personal example, a role model, ready to fire the last bullet. Nobody dies from this - it's all a lot of noise."
