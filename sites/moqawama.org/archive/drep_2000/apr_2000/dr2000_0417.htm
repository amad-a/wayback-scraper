Daily Report

April Monday 17-2000:

 

Introduction

Hizbullah's Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah warned Saturday that the "Israeli" occupation troops withdrawal would not end problems for "Israel" or its Lebanese militia ally. He listed remaining problems: the Lebanese hostages held in "Israel", war reparations, the fate of the "Israeli"-proxy militia, continuing aggression by "Israel" after it pulls out of South Lebanon and the problem of around 350.000 Palestinian refugees in Lebanon.

Meanwhile, the release of thirteen Lebanese hostages, scheduled for Monday, has been delayed because of an alleged appeal to the Supreme Court to keep them in detention, the "Israeli" prisons department said.

However, "Israel" on Sunday officially withdraw from the April Understanding Monitoring Group, effectively terminating the international body which has overseen the Understanding since August 1996.


We will not leave detainees in prison
Nasrallah warns "Israel": occupation is one justification for the resistance(1)

"Hizbullahs secretary-general said on Saturday that "Israel's" pullout from the south would not be enough if the Jewish state wanted to end its problems with Lebanon.
"Israel" cheats Lebanon and the whole world when it says that its withdrawal will solve all of its problems with Lebanon, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah told thousands gathered in Bir al-Abed to mark Ashoura.
When "Israel" withdraws its troops to the border, only one problem between "Israel" and Lebanon will be solved, he said.
Before the pullout is completed, all detainees must be released  I say it very clearly, that we will not leave our detainees in prison. It is our right to do anything we can to secure their release.
Also, the enemy must pay compensation for all human and material damage and losses it caused in Lebanon, he said.
Nasrallah also said Hizbullah would not tolerate the presence of "Israel's" proxy militia in the occupied zone after "Israel" withdrew.
The collaborators must leave the area with the enemy, surrender to the Lebanese authorities or be killed by our bullets, he said.
He called, however, on the areas civilian residents not to believe rumors and lies that they would come under Hizbullah attack in the event of a pullout.
The areas residents ­ I address the Christians before the Muslims ­ should remember what happened in Jezzine. Nothing has happened since the collaborators left the area (last June), he said.
Nasrallah warned "Israel" that any attack on Lebanon after the withdrawal will not be tolerated The world must know and the enemy must know that the Lebanese have the right to defend their country against any attack in the future.
With respect to the Palestinian refugees, he said: The Palestinians here, as well as the Palestinians anywhere in the world, have the right to return to their homes. Arab and Islamic governments must help the refugees to return.
A statement from the Presidential Palace on Saturday said President Emile Lahoud had praised the important views voiced by Nasrallah.
He also stressed the importance of Lebanons unity vis-a-vis the Zionist enemy and its plans and plots, it said.


Lawsuit delays "Israel's" release of 13 Lebanese detainees(2)

" The release of 13 Lebanese prisoners, scheduled for Monday, has been delayed because of an appeal to the Supreme Court to keep them in detention, the "Israeli" prisons department said.

"The release has been suspended following an appeal by the family of aircraft navigator Ron Arad," prisons spokeswoman Orit Messer told AFP.

"Israeli" pilot Arad was taken prisoner in Lebanon in 1986 after his plane went down there.

"The Supreme Court will begin considering the appeal at 9:00 a.m.and until it decides, the release process will be frozen," said Messer.

But legal experts said the delay would probably be a matter of hours, because it would be difficult for the court, to reverse its earlier order to release the prisoners.

The "Israeli" military announced earlier Sunday that 13 of its 15 Lebanese "administrative" detainees would be released and sent back to Lebanon.

They had all been seized years ago as bargaining chips for Arad and three "Israeli" soldiers missing in action.

The 13 prisoners had been due to Ayalon prison around 4:00 a.m.for Kishon, near Haifa, where they would be handed over to the military which would release them, said Messer.

The "Israeli" Supreme Court ruled April 12 that holding them simply as bargaining chips and not because they threatened the country's security was illegal.

The justice ministry nevertheless said Sunday that the two most important Lebanese in this form of administrative detention, renewable every six months, would stay there because they represented a terrorist threat.

They are Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid, kidnapped in 1989, and Mustafa Dirani, a Lebanese leader abducted in 1994. "Israel" says it was Dirani who held Arad prisoner.

Justice Minister Yossi Beilin said Saturday that Obeid and Dirani were a threat to "Israeli" security.

Dirani, a militant Muslim leader claims to have suffered torture during his time in "Israeli" custody.

Last month, his lawyer filed a 1.5-million-dollar law suit against "Israel", saying Dirani had been systematically beaten and raped by his "Israeli" jailers.

The court's ruling over releasing the prisoners has been strongly criticized by government and opposition politicians, security officials and families of the missing "Israelis".

Lawyers for by 15 other Lebanese prisoners seeking release from the Khiam detention center in "Israeli"-occupied southern Lebanon, said the Supreme Court would consider their appeal on Monday.

The detainees are also asking that other prisoners at Khiam also be freed.

"The prisoners have been locked up without being tried," the lawyers said in a news release. Some of them had been seriously mistreated during their incarceration, the statement alleged.


"Israel" officially withdraws from Monitoring Group(3)

""Israel" officially withdrew from the five-nation Monitoring Group on Sunday, effectively terminating the international body which has overseen the April Understanding since August 1996.
The "Israeli" Army said the delegates to the group would only return after a two-week ceasefire from the resistance. "Israel" decided it will weigh participating... only after calm is maintained in south Lebanon for at least two weeks, the "Israeli" Army said.
""Israel" says Hizbullah and other resistance groups flout the April Understanding by staging attacks from populated areas. The "Israeli" Armys argues that the understanding hampers its ability to respond to the source of resistance fire. If Lebanese civilians are killed or wounded, Hizbullah feels it is justified to respond with a cross-border attack into northern "Israel".
The government said "Israel" could not alter the terms of the April Understanding. Lebanon remains committed to the arrangements of April 1996, in which five parties were involved, and "Israel" cannot unilaterally decide on their status, a statement said.
The activities of the Monitoring Group, consisting of delegates from Lebanon, Syria, France, "Israel" and the United States, have remained frozen since February when the "Israeli" delegation walked out of a session following the killing of a soldier in Beaufort castle.
The remaining delegations have all expressed readiness to resume periodic meetings but the "Israelis" have so far refused to attend. Nonetheless, the "Israelis" have continued to file complaints to the group since February ­ the total number presently standing at around 50. The resistance has been known to stage operations from within the 500 meter cordon sanitaire around all populated areas, but not as often as the "Israelis" declare. "Israel" has periodically called for ceasefires in the south ­ without success. It is unlikely that this latest request will be heeded, particularly given the deadlock in talks with Syria and the imminent "Israeli" pullout.
" If "Israel" begins treating the frontline areas as a free-fire zone and civilians are killed, Hizbullah will respond with cross-border attacks.
"Two civilians were wounded on Saturday in Kfar Romman and on Habboush, near Nabatieh, by "Israeli" shelling. Abbas Atwi, 22, and Shadi Kiwan, 21, a Syrian, suffered shrapnel wounds".


Lebanon insists on threat-free, unconditional "Israeli" pullout(4)

"Lebanese President Emile Lahoud called Sunday for "Israel" to pull out of south Lebanon "without conditions or threats" in line with UN Security Council resolution 425.

"Lebanon supports implementation of resolution 425 but to the letter and without conditions or threats from "Israel", which is threatening to destroy the (Lebanese) infrastructure and to hit Lebanon's and Syria's armies," he said.

Lahoud, quoted by the official Saudi news agency SPA during a visit to the kingdom, said the purpose of his current Gulf tour was "to clarify Lebanon's position" on "Israel's" planned withdrawal in July.

On Saturday, the Lebanese president met King Fahd on the first leg of his Gulf tour and then traveled on to Kuwait.

Quoted in the Kuwaiti newspaper Al-Rai Al-Aam on Sunday, Lahoud said the Jewish state had decided to pull out because of the casualties which its army had suffered in south Lebanon.

"It's clear that the "Israeli" withdrawal comes as a consequence of the losses and not in line with UN resolution 425, which "Israel" has refused to implement since 1978," he said ahead of his first visit to the emirate.

"The Lebanese president also told Al-Rai Al-Aam that it was his country's right to raise the issue of the 350,000 Palestinian refugees in its territory ahead of the planned withdrawal.

"The Palestinian problem in Lebanon is not a problem of refugees like in other Arab countries. It is primarily a security-military problem which has accumulated over dozens of years," he said.


Nabatieh relives Ashouras memory(5)

"During the Ashoura commemoration in Nabatieh in 1983, an "Israeli" convoy tried to force its way through the thick crowds, a move considered a gross insult to the and sparked a huge wave of anger and resentment. The incident subsequently led to the mobilization of the citizens in the south and galvanized the resistance against the "Israeli" occupation.
Seventeen years later, Nabatieh residents on Saturday staged their traditionally commemoration of Husseins martyrdom at the battle of Karbala ­ perhaps for the last time under the watching eyes of the "Israelis" on Dabsheh hill above the town. In clear view of Dabsheh, thousands of black-clad Hizbullah supporters filled the Zibdeen road leading to the town center, rhythmically slapping their chests. Elaborate floats and vivid paintings of Hizbullah leaders led the procession, which waited patiently on the edge of the town while thousands of chanting, blood-soaked men jogged around the main square beating their foreheads in unison.
"But the bloodletting is not popular among all the towns Muslims.
Irans spiritual leader Ali Khameini and leading cleric Sayyed Mohammed Hussein Fadlallah have both declared the spilling of blood during Ashoura haram.
Last year, Hizbullahs leader, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, suggested that those commemorating Ashoura donate blood rather than spill it on the streets.
Heeding Nasrallahs advice, the Hizbullah-run al-Janoub hospital on Saturday set up four blood donor tents around the main square.
Were trying to change the culture here. It contradicts religious teaching, said hospital director Hajj Fouad.
Its very dangerous, he said. They spread disease all over the place, AIDS, hepatitis ... blood sprays in all directions when they beat their foreheads.
"On the edge of Nabatieh, Hizbullah commanders marshaled the disciplined ranks of partisans prior to their march into the town square.
Watching the parade, Ihlam Hajj Ali recalled that fateful 1983 Ashoura. The "Israelis" came along this road and our men attacked, she said. My son was a baby at the time and he hit them with a stick.
"A hand grenade destroyed the lone jeep in the convoy. "Israeli" soldiers, panicked and opened fire, killing at least two".
