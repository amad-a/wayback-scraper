"Israelis" hold up hostage release

Occupation to "try" Sheikh Obeid and Hajj Dirani to bar release
""Israeli" daily Ha'aretz said "The defense establishment began preparing yesterday for anticipated petitions to the High Court of justice for the release of (Hajj Mustafa) Dirani and (Sheikh Abdel-Karim) Obeid. The state and military prosecutors believe that, unlike the other detainees, it will be possible to convince the court that the continued detention of Dirani and Obeid is necessary to the effort to find (missing "Israeli" pilot Ron) Arad."

The daily said discussions will be held in the next few days in which the occupation's premier Ehud Barak and other senior officials will take part.

President of the Zionist supreme court justice Aharon Barak commented on yesterday's decision by saying that he had made a mistake when he barred the release of the Lebanese detainees.

You cannot give legitimacy to a means that is considered necessary for the state's security, he said in reference to the political pressures that barred the release of the Lebanese detainees"(1)

Meanwhile, "Israeli" officials said on Thursday that 13 Lebanese held as bargaining chips for more than decade would be freed on Friday ­ then said the army had requested a delay until next week for operational reasons.
But judicial sources in "Jerusalem" said that the Justice Ministry was considering laying specific charges against them so that they could be kept behind bars while "Israel" continues its efforts to determine the fate of missing air force navigator Captain Ron Arad.
Initially, the Prisons Authority announced that the 13 Lebanese, whose release was ordered by "Israel's" Supreme Court in a landmark ruling on Wednesday, would be taken from Ayalon prison in northern "Israel" early on Friday for handover to the Red Cross, which would bring them home.
But several hours later it said the release would not be until next week, when it had originally been scheduled.
The decision to free Lebanese prisoners ­ kidnapped by "Israeli" forces or by wartime Christian militias who handed them over to the "Israelis" ­ was part of an "Israeli" effort to clear the decks in advance of the expected withdrawal from the south

"The Supreme Courts ruling ­ which reversed its own decision in 1997 that upheld the practice of holding Lebanese without charge or trial simply as bargaining counters to locate missing servicemen ­ followed another landmark ruling by the court last September that physical and psychological pressure by the Shin Bet security service against Palestinian suspects was illegal.
Releasing the Lebanese hostages and outlawing what human-rights groups have long maintained was torture by Shin Bet reflects a growing sentiment among many "Israelis" that such practices, conducted in the name of national security, can no longer be condoned if "Israel" is to consider itself a democracy.
There has been intense pressure on "Israel" to conform with international human-rights conventions and even Baraks coalition Cabinet has been split on the legal and moral issue, with Beilin leading those who believe "Israel" can no longer hide behind the national defense argument if it wants to make peace with its neighbors.

The Supreme Courts ruling nonetheless sparked a storm of protest among hard-liners in "Israel", and on Thursday the official responsible for government efforts to track the fate of missing servicemen resigned.
Yackov Perry, a former senior Shin Bet official, said he told Barak that the ruling made an already complex, difficult mission impossible.
The closure of Khiam, a former French Foreign Legion fort, would mark the end of one of the most horrifying chapters in the "Israeli" occupation.
News agencies quoted the "Israeli" allied militia sources as saying that some of the 150 prisoners in Khiam would be transferred to "Israel" for interrogation, while others would be released.
Amnesty International called on Thursday for the immediate release of all prisoners, many of whom have been held for years without charge or trial. Many of the detainees are held on suspicion they planned attacks on "Israel" and its allies.
Human rights groups have repeatedly said that detainees are systematically tortured at Khiam and prisoners who have been freed have told gruesome tales of the savage brutality that has prevailed there.
"Israel" has long denied any connection to the prison. But last September it acknowledged for the first time that its agents help train the "Israeli"-proxy militia personnel in Khiam. A court affidavit said that the agents were involved in everything to do with the gathering of intelligence and questioning suspects.
Abramowitz noted that after the withdrawal, it would not be possible for the militia to maintain the prison at Khiam"(2)


Amnesty International: "Israeli" government should release all Lebanese hostages(3)

"In defiance of international law "Israel" has been holding Lebanese nationals for years as hostages to barter for information about "Israeli" servicemen who went missing in action in Lebanon," Amnesty International said. "We regret that it has taken the Supreme Court six years to come to the decision that such detention is illegal."

"Israeli" Army radio reported that only 13 of the 15 detainees would be released, suggesting that freedom would be denied to Mustafa al-Dirani, kidnapped in 1994, and Shaikh 'Abd al-Karim 'Ubayd, abducted in 1989.

In contravention of international law they have been held in a secret place of detention without access to the International Committee of the Red Cross or their families. Mustafa al-Dirani is at present suing the "Israeli" Government for torture, including rape, allegedly inflicted after his abduction.

"We consider all 15 detainees -- including Shaikh 'Abd al-Karim 'Ubayd and Mustafa al-Dirani, held for 11 and six years without trial -- as hostages who should be immediately released," urged Amnesty International.

The organization is also urging the "Israeli" Government to compensate all the hostages for the years of suffering cut off from the outside world.

Background

Ten Lebanese detainees, who continued to be held for up to six years after their prison sentences expired, launched an appeal in 1994 against their continued administrative detention.

In 1997 the Supreme Court ruled that it was legal to hold these detainees as "bargaining chips", a decision believed to be unprecedented in the world. But the Supreme Court decided to allow the appeal to be reheard by an enlarged panel of nine judges.

By May 1999 the Supreme Court had completed rehearing the arguments and announced its intention to rule on the case. However, the State applied on four occasions for the Supreme Court to postpone delivering judgement and presented secret evidence to the court to argue for adjournments.

The Lebanese detainees' lawyer was consistently denied access to this material on security grounds.

Hoss: border demarcation is a ploy(4)

"Prime Minister Salim Hoss shrugged off "Israel's" decision to let the United Nations define the border to which "Israel" should withdraw as an "Israeli" ploy on Thursday.
The tug-of-war over the border issue was coupled with a new escalation in violence in which two Lebanese civilians and an "Israeli"-allied militiaman were wounded on Thursday.
This "Israeli" talk is just another publicity stunt meant to confuse the international community and public opinion, Hoss said.
An senior "Israeli" official was quoted on Wednesday as saying that "Israel" would approve the UNs decision on the delineation of the border.
The issue has recently been heavily debated, particularly after "Israel's" deputy defense minister, Ephraim Sneh, claimed there were four separate borders to which "Israel" could withdraw.
Already the United Nations has replied to "Israel" by telling leaders that all UN actions await a written "Israeli" pledge for the implementation of a withdrawal, Hoss said.
He was referring to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annans demand that the "Israeli" government send the UN a written commitment to implement UN Resolution 425 before the UN would begin preparations for an "Israeli" withdrawal from south Lebanon.
It is crystal clear that "Israel", whether in its words or deeds, is still bent on setting up ploys". Hoss added.
He reiterated that Lebanon had one internationally recognized border, clearly spelled out in a Franco-British document. The UN Security Council has already approved the 1923 border under UN Resolution 425. Consequently, there is no need to demarcate the border, Hoss said. All these border scenarios are figments of "Israel's" imagination.

"Israeli"-allied militiaman wounded

The militiaman was hurt when shells slammed on his position in Houla in the central sector of the border zone in southern Lebanon, he said.

The Islamic Resistance, Hezbollah's military wing, took credit for the attack against Houla, "claiming victims among the ranks of the pro-"Israeli" agents."

Earlier Thursday, two Lebanese civilians were wounded in an "Israeli" bombardment that also left two houses damaged in the village of Majdal Silm, just outside the "Israeli"-occupied border zone, police here said.

"Since the start of the year, some 16 militiamen have been killed and 30 wounded in southern Lebanon, according to an AFP count, while three civilians have been killed and 62 others wounded.

Funny things on the way to withdrawal(7)

"An "Israeli" delegation that included the deputy director-general of the Ministry of Defense, Moshe Kochanovsky, and the head of the cartography department of military intelligence, Colonel Haim Sarbiv, left last week for Washington on a secret mission regarding "Israel's" post-withdrawal border with Lebanon.

Naturally, secrets like this cannot be kept under wraps for long, and when they're disclosed, internal disputes are sometimes revealed along with them. It turns out that the story of the National Security Council, whose heads have twice resigned and then withdrawn their resignations, is repeating itself with the team negotiating with the Syrians, which is not being informed about what is happening on the Syrian track. The secret delegation returned feeling that it had convinced Washington that a withdrawal to the 1978 borders from before the Litani operation would actually be a withdrawal to the international border between "Israel" and Lebanon. The Americans are not likely to be so naive. Just as there is no recognized border for June 4, 1967 between "Israel" and Syria - only the military position lines from that date - there is also no recognized international boundary from June 14, 1978 (the eve of the Litani operation) between "Israel" and Lebanon. The "Israel" Defense Forces* were not located on the international border in 1978 - which will quickly become obvious when an international team is summoned to assess the facts.
The irony of it all is that we are to go back to the Lebanese and repeat the Syrian explanation of the June 4, 1967 lines, which is nothing but a diplomatic ruse. The Syrians argue that their claim is based on UN Security Council Resolution 242, which mentions the return of lands conquered in the Six Day War, and "Israel" claims that the issue of the Lebanese border is based on UN Security Council Resolution 425. Resolution 425, however, does not dictate a return to the lines as they were on the eve of the Litani operation, but rather calls on "Israel" to "withdraw its forces from all Lebanese territory," and "to respect Lebanese sovereignty over its recognized international boundaries."
When Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz says in a television interview that "Israel" will return to the 1978 borders from the eve of the Litani operation, it is impossible to say that "Israel" is withdrawing to the international border. And when officials say that "Israel" plans to honor Resolution 425 and withdraw to the 1978 lines, they do not mean that we will be fulfilling the resolution in its entirety - but only that we will appear to do so. And it's still unclear what we mean when we say that we will withdraw to the lines from the eve of the Litani operation. At that time Major Hadad's militiamen in southern Lebanon held three enclaves: Marjayoun-Kliya, the Bint Jbail-Maron el Ras and Nakoura. Do we expect to supply the South Lebanese Army** with arms and ammunition so it can reclaim these enclaves? What's the real decision? What's the real assessment?
"Israel's" declared policy is to follow the letter of Security Council Resolution 425, and withdraw completely from Lebanese territory. So "Israel" seeks to win international support for this step, which is part and parcel with the right to make defensive strikes should Hezbollah, or any other group, Palestinian or not, harm "Israel" from Lebanese territory. This is intended to make it more difficult for Syria and Iran if they attempt to instigate a war against "Israel" from Lebanese territory. It is the right policy if "Israel" really wants to carry out the government's decision to withdraw from Lebanon.
