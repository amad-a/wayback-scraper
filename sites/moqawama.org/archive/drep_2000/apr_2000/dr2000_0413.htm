Lebanon's Islamic Resistance paving the way for removal of "Israel", Hizbullah(1)

"Hizbullah's Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said the Yazied of this era is the usurping entity of ""Israel"".

"The silence with which ""Israel"" was met in 1948 allowed it to expand and take what it has taken, he said.

"Had the Lebanese done nothing after the 1982 invasion, Lebanon would have been today a Zionist settlement, he added.

Sayyed Nasrallah underlined that those who "set out to resist in 1982 wanted to say we will fight, kill and get martyred in order to revive our people and the peoples of our Ummah so that this Ummah recovers its self-conficence and ability to face the challenge."

"The resistance's experience in Lebanon fought not only to defeat "Israel" and expel it from Lebanon, but also to pave the way for the removal of "Israel" from existence through the culture, option and logic of resistance, he concluded.


Hizbullah responds to occupation's threats(2)
"Hizbullah Deputy Secretary General his Eminence Sheikh Nahim Qassem dismissed the conception of reciprocal concession with the Zionist enemy that is proposed by the international community.

Are we the ones who had assaulted this artificial entity to give up our rights, dignity, existence, and land? he asked.

"We will never accept this fait accompli policy."

Speaking at an Ashura rally in Timneen al-Tahta, Qassem warned of the enemy's attempts to sow seeds of unrest among the Lebanese following the withdrawal.

"Harmony among the Lebanese is what annoys the enemy."

The "Israelis" know that they will pay highly for any aggression against Lebanon, he said.


One militiaman killed, another wounded in IR operations(3)
"The "Israeli" occupation's proxy militia acknowledged that one of its militiamen, agent Tony Ephraim Nasr (from Aishieh), was killed and another, agent George Antoine Darios (from Libaa), was wounded in an Islamic Resistance (IR) operation.

IR's squad of Martyrs Bilal Darnouh and Mohsen Zreiq at 15:35 Wednesday targeted an enemy force inside the occupation's outpost in Rihan (the tenth regiment command HQ) scoring accurate hits, an IR communiqué read.

Earlier, squad of Martyrs Haitham Mohammad Faqih and Ali Hussein Swaidan at 6:30 a.m. Wednesday blitzed the "Israeli" occupation's outpost in Baraasheit with machine guns and rocket propelled grenades scoring direct hits.

In the meantime, squad of Martyrs Sami Hassan Mailadan and Hussein Ali Mazloum targeted the enemy's outpost in Haddatha with the appropriate weapons scoring accurate hits.

Moreover, IR's squad of Martyrs Abdel-Hassan Fahs and Moussa Diab Yahia at 11:45 a.m. attacked a Lahdi force that was fortifying the occupation's outpost in Dhour al-Kassara.

IR men used machine guns and rocket propelled grenades in the attack scoring direct hits and barring the enemy force from accomplishing its mission.

Also, the IR team of martyrs Ahmad Mohammad Olayan and Hussein Mustafa Salman attacked, at 17:05, Dohour El-Kassara position with the rocket-propelled grenades scoring and certain hits.

Furthermore, the IR group of martyrs Naji Fawzi Abou Daya and Ahmad Abdel-Jalil Sherri targeted, at 19:35, enemy movements inside Rshaf position. Using the appropriate arms, the militants managed to chalk up direct goals.


"Israel" to free Lebanese held as bargaining chips after court acknowledgment(4)

""Israel's" prisons authority said Wednesday it would free by Monday 13 Lebanese held without trial for years as bargaining chips, following the acknowledgment of the supreme court admitted that they were being detained illegally.

"All 13 Lebanese held by the prisons authority will be released and returned to Lebanon as soon as the technical arrangements have been finalized" with the International Committee of the Red Cross, the authority's spokeswoman told AFP.

"We are implementing a decision by the court which ordered the release of eight prisoners who had submitted an appeal and five others whose case is the same," Orit Meser-Arel added.

However, she said the two best known Lebanese detainees, Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid and leader Mustafa Dirani, kidnapped by "Israeli" commandos in southern Lebanon in 1989 and 1994 respectively, were not covered.

Both are held by the army and do not come within the competence of the prisons authority.

Prime Minister Ehud Barak, who is also war minister, and who heard of the decision as he traveled back to "Israel" from Washington, said he "would respect it and would act in accordance with the order of the court," a war ministry statement said.

Nevertheless, he said he thought it would have been better "if "Israel" had kept the prisoners for a certain time."

Deputy War Minister Ephraim Sneh, however, lashed out at the court ruling.

The judges "took no account of the fact that we are not living in an area where Scandinavian democracy reigns," he said.

"We have lost one of the few cards we had to get the release or information about our "Israeli" soldiers," he said.

The head of the "Israeli" armed forces, General Shaul Mofaz, who earlier told state radio that the military would comply with the ruling, said the imprisonment of the Lebanese had been judged necessary in an attempt to secure the release of "Israeli" air force navigator Ron Arad, who was shot down over Lebanon in 1986 and who the "Israelis" believe is still alive.

Also missing are three soldiers thought to have been captured in 1982 during a tank battle between "Israeli" and Syrian forces.

Relatives of those missing greeted the court ruling with bitterness, with Arad's mother Batia telling the radio, "We have been betrayed."

The mother of one of the soldiers, Miriam Baumel, said she was very disappointed. "They give us promises all the time but they don't keep them," she said.

The court's decision, by a majority of six to three, that the Lebanese concerned by its ruling posed no threat to "Israeli" security, caused some surprise here as on previous occasions it had dismissed objections to such detention without trial.

The development followed an appeal to the court by lawyer Zvi Rish, who also represents Mustafa Dirani in a 1.5-million-dollar law suit against "Israel", alleging his client had been systematically beaten and raped by his "Israeli" jailers.

Rish told AFP he expected Dirani and Sheikh Obeid to be released soon as "they are not a security threat."

In Beirut a prisoners' support committee welcomed the supreme court's ruling, calling it a "Lebanese and international victory."

The sister of one of the men to be released told AFP she was "delirious with delight" to learn her brother would be freed.

"Although he was innocent, my brother Ali (Ammar) was sentenced to four years in 1986 on suspicion of helping the anti-"Israeli" resistance ... And the "Israelis" then kept him unfairly and quite arbitrarily in prison for another 10 years on top of that," she said.

There was a constant swarm of visitors to Ali Ammar's house in the Beirut district of Basta after the news broke, coming to congratulate the family.


Hoss: We denounce "Israeli threats of reprisal(5)

"Prime Minister Salim Hoss said that it was practically impossible for any country to guarantee an absolutely trouble-free border.
We totally reject and denounce "Israeli" threats of reprisal if stability along the common border is jeopardized. No country in the world can guarantee a trouble-free border, the prime minister maintained.
We further reject "Israeli" warnings because UN Security Council Resolution 425 does not leave room for retaliatory threats in the event of border disturbances.
The premier reiterated that his government was looking forward to the day when "Israel" withdrew unconditionally from south Lebanon under UN resolutions.
We eagerly wait for the day when "Israel" vacates all of the Lebanese territories in line with UN Resolution 425. This has been our demand for the past 22 years, Hoss said.
