Daily Report

April Thursday 20-2000:

 

Introduction

Thirteen Lebanese hostages held for years as bargaining chips by the "Israeli" enemy arrived back home Wednesday to a hero's welcome from relatives, Hizbullah and a crowd of people.

"All Lebanese held in "Israel" and in the Khiam detention centre must be released before the "Israeli" pullback, including Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid and Hajj Mustafa Dirani", Hizbullah's Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said in greeting the returning hostages.


Nasrallah: All hostages must be released before "Israeli" retreat(1)

"Thirteen Lebanese detainees held for years as bargaining chips by "Israel" were welcomed back to Beirut by Hizbullah leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah after their release Wednesday.

Nasrallah emerged into the street patrolled by Hizbullah activists to greet the returning prisoners, and called for the release of all Lebanese detainees before "Israel" withdraws from southern Lebanon as it has promised to do by July.

"All Lebanese held in "Israel" and in the Khiam detention centre (in "Israeli"-occupied southern Lebanon) must be released before the "Israeli" pullback, including Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid and Hajj Mustafa Dirani," he said.

"Israel" has refused to release Sheikh Obeid and Dirani, who were kidnapped in southern Lebanon in 1989, and 1994 respectively, to put pressure on Hizbullah to provide information about "Israeli" servicemen missing in action.

"Israel" now says the two are a danger to its security, and is preparing to change the law to legalise their detention without charge or trial, by defining them as "non-legal fighters".

"They will all return, I promise you, and we will do all we can to obtain their freedom," Nasrallah thundered to the 200 of so people longing to greet the ex-prisoners, including many of their relatives, who had waited since dawn at the Kfar Tibnite gate, linking the occupied zone with the rest of Lebanon.

About 20 more Lebanese are serving sentences in "Israeli" jails after being tried and sentenced, some of them to life imprisonment.

Another 150 or so are being held in Khiam without trial, some of them for nearly 15 years.

The "Israeli" supreme court last week ordered the release of the 13 who were all being held either without trial, or beyond the expiry of their sentences, after ruling their detention illegal.

Like Dirani and Sheikh Obeid, they were being held as part of "Israel's" effort to obtain news of its servicemen.


Lebanese hostages freed by "Israel" receive hero's welcome

Hizbullah head receives the hostages(2)

"After being held as bargaining chips for up to 14 years, 13 Lebanese detainees had to wait another nine excruciating hours on Wednesday before they finally returned home from "Israel's" Ayalon Prison.
The 13 drove into Lebanon in an International Committee of the Red Cross bus at the Kfar Tibnite crossing near Nabatieh. More than 2,000 family members, Hizbullah supporters, villagers and journalists gathered at the crossing, waiting for the prisoners release amid a sea of Hizbullah flags and loudspeakers blaring patriotic songs. The bus did not stop for families but drove slowly through the crowd as it made its way to Beiruts southern suburbs, where Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah waited to welcome the detainees.
Some relatives, however, climbed atop the roof of the bus to get closer to loved ones they had not seen in years.
Hawiyyeh Yassin, 65, whose son Ahmad was among those released, stood among relatives clutching a picture of her son, who was seized by "Israeli" troops in 1986 at the age of 23.
It has been torture every day since the "Israelis" took my son away, she said. It feels longer than 400 years of waiting. But I have been waiting for this day for 14 years, she said.
Since she heard the news that Ahmad was to be released she hasnt eaten, hasnt slept, said her daughter, Zeinab.
Amid shouts of Allahu Akbar and God protect Nasrallah, the detainees, toting Hizbullah flags, were carried toward Nasrallah at the partys Shura Council headquarters in Haret Hreik.
After hugging each of the 13 detainees, Nasrallah tried to calm the growing crowd.
We bless the families of the detainees for this victory. It is natural for us to stand here among our liberated brothers, he said. Our land is going to be liberated and all other detainees will be released. This is the natural result of a nation that sacrifices and causes the enemy all these losses.
Huddled next to Hizbullahs southern commander, Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, Abbas Srour, 31, spoke briefly of the minutes prior to his release.
We were about to explode. They told us our release had been postponed three times as we waited, blindfolded, at the border, said Srour, who was detained in 1987 along with three of his brothers. But the most difficult period I spent was the last three years in Ayalon because we were completely isolated from everything.
His brother, 29-year-old Abdel-Hassan, also released on Wednesday, said the torture he and other detainees suffered from was beyond imagination.
Abdel-Hassan added, however, that political detainees Sheikh Adel-Karim Obeid and Hajj Mustafa Dirani were subjected to the most horrific torture.
While welcoming the release of the detainees, Prime Minister Salim Hoss condemned "Israel" for refusing to release Dirani and Obeid. We cannot but condemn "Israel" for refusing to release Sheikh Obeid and Hajj Dirani and other Lebanese detainees, and we urge the international community to exert pressure on "Israel" to release them, Hoss said.
Today is a partial victory, agreed Mohammed Safa, head of the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in "Israeli" Prisons.

"The detainees were released in spite of numerous "Israeli" appeals to the countrys Supreme Court, which determined that they could not be held as bargaining chips in exchange for information on Ron Arad, an "Israeli" Air Force navigator shot down over Lebanon in 1986.


Lebanese mothers' dreams keep hope alive through long years of detention(3)

"Even when a son is far away in jail in a foreign land, he is never far from his mother's thoughts and haunts her dreams, as Lebanese mothers recounted Wednesday as they waited here for their loved ones to arrive from "Israel".

"I thought about him all the time, what he was eating, what he was drinking, how he was sleeping. I remember his face exactly," said Fatima Wehbeh, 65, whose son, Hussein Ramiti, was kidnapped in 1986 by pro-"Israeli" forces in Lebanon, and handed over to "Israel" in 1990, where he was held for 10 years without trial.

He was 23 then, and now, at the age of 37, Ramiti and 12 other Lebanese were freed earlier Wednesday after "Israel's" supreme court ruled their detention illegal.

All were being held as bargaining chips to pressurize the Hizbullah to provide information about "Israeli" servicemen missing in action in Lebanon.

Mohammad Yassin is also now 37. He was arrested when he was 23 when he was taking part in a Hizbullah attack against "Israeli" troops in Lebanon.

"When I think of Mohammad at night, I weep," said his mother, Zeinab. "I dream about him. He talks to me, and I cheer him up and tell him he is going to get out, I'm going to see him. He opens his arms and I hug him close, but when I wake up he is not there," she said, choking back tears.

"In one of my dreams, I went to see him in "Israel" in spite of its being forbidden. I begged the prison governor to let me see him, and she pointed me down roads that all turned out to be dead ends. Then suddenly I saw him coming out of a cloud and shouting: 'Here I am, mother!'"

Dawiyyeh, the mother of Ahmad Ammar, carries a picture next to her heart of her son when he was 19, showing the way he looked when he was arrested and sentenced to four years in jail, a full 14 years ago.

"At first, I didn't think he would be held for long, and I kept hoping he would come in through the door at any moment. I often dreamed that he had come home, and those dreams calmed me," she said.

She said that after Ahmad's arrest in the "Israel"-occupied border zone of southern Lebanon, no-one came to visit her any more, since her son was accused of being a member of the resistance forces.

"So I came to Beirut," she explained.

"I have never had any regrets, and have never felt ashamed. Just the opposite, I am proud of my son, who is a hero," she said.

Jamileh, the sister of Hassan Hijazi, who was arrested in 1986 at the age of 15 and sentenced to three years in jail, recalled how she once dreamed of her brother coming towards her with open arms, when suddenly armed "Israelis" jumped on him and dragged him off before she could hug him.

"Once I dreamed about a huge pit with crowds of prisoners coming out of it. But every time my brother tried to haul himself out, "Israelis" would push him back in," she said.

"Another time, I dreamed that waves and waves of freed prisoners were coming towards me, but my brother was going the other way," she said.

Some of the women waiting in the crowd knew that their dreams were not going to be fulfilled.

Khadijah Hamames carried the photo of her son, Kamel Ahmad Shehadeh, whose name did not appear on the list of those being released.

"He disappeared when he was carrying out an attack with the Amal movement. The International Committee of the Red Cross failed to find him either in "Israel" or in Khiam," the notorious detention centre in the "Israeli"-occupied zone, she said.

"I have come to share the joy of the other relatives. I feel that their sons are my sons, and perhaps they will recognise him if they see his photo. He was 22 when he went missing. He must be 33 today," she said.


Qassem warns against complacency(4)

"Hizbullahs Deputy secretary-general, Nahim Qassem, cautioned that "Israel" would remain aggressive even if it withdrew from south Lebanon.
There are two possibilities  "Israel" will either launch an aggression before its withdrawal or it will do so after it, he said on Wednesday.
We have to be ready for either possibility instead of living in a tense atmosphere, he said, adding that the situation called for unity and cooperation".
