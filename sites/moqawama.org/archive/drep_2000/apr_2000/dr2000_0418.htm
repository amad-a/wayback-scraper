Detainees families protest delayed release(1)

"The Families of 13 Lebanese detainees, who were expected to be released by "Israel" on Monday, staged a sit-in on the Kfar Tibnite-Nabatieh crossing in protest against "Israel's" decision to postpone their release to Wednesday.
Ive been living in anticipation since "Israel's" announcement that it would release the prisoners a few days ago but the new decision has devastated me, said Dawiya Ammar, the mother of detainee Ahmad Ammar.
Ammar is one of 15 Lebanese held without trial by "Israel" as bargaining chips in exchange for information on the fate of "Israeli" Air Force navigator Ron Arad, whose plane crashed over Lebanon in 1986.
Several people, including many who were elderly, stood at the Kfar Tibnite crossing, which separates the occupied zone from the rest of the south and which is the first point where those released are allowed to meet their relatives.
"Israel's" Supreme Court rejected an appeal on Monday by an extreme-right activist group challenging an earlier decision to release 13 out of 15 detainees and ordered that they be freed on Wednesday.
Arads family and "Israeli" relatives of those MIA, have been pressuring the jewish state to find a way to keep the prisoners in custody.
Ammars mother appealed to Arads mother, telling her, your son was shelling women, children and civilians from his plane when he went missing in Lebanon. My son was taken from his bed because he was defending his country inside Lebanon.
Mohammed Safa, secretary-general of the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in "Israeli" Prisons, who took part in the sit-in, called for stronger government participation in the drive. I appeal to the government to stop being indifferent. It must stop acting as if no Lebanese are being detained at a time when it should be campaigning against "Israel's" maneuvering, Safa said.
He added that the "Israeli" judiciary and court were merely tools in the hands of the "Israeli" government which was trying to play a dirty game by showing it as a democratic country abiding by the courts decision.
Safa argued that it was the same court which held the same prisoners 14 years ago while renewing their sentences every six months. He also pointed out that it was the same court which released five detainees two months ago, following an initiative by Germany, without a decision from the Supreme Court.
We hope this sit-in will gain enough momentum for all Lebanese to take part in it. "Israeli" human rights organizations are condemning what is happening while Lebanon has not taken any action, he said.
NGOs campaigning for the release of Lebanese detainees from "Israeli" prisons have been dissatisfied with the governments handling of the issue.


Barak looks for legal means to thwart Supreme Court ruling on detainees(2)

""Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak Monday set about looking for ways to get round a supreme court ruling ordering the release of 13 Lebanese detainees held as bargaining chips in "Israeli" jails.

Speaking on television, Barak made it clear that he wanted to keep at least seven of the prisoners as bargaining chips in the hope of obtaining information on "Israeli" servicemen missing in action in Lebanon.

A total of 15 Lebanese are being held either without trial, or beyond the expiry of their sentences, after being kidnapped by "Israeli" commandos or pro-"Israeli" groups in Lebanon.

Eight of them appealed to the supreme court, which Wednesday ruled that their detention was illegal.

Most "Israeli" legal experts considered that another five, who had not appealed but who were being held in identical circumstances, should be freed at the same time.

The supreme court Monday rejected a petition against their release submitted by an extreme right-wing group, and said they would be freed by Wednesday.

"I do not think that the eight whose release was ordered by the supreme court will stay in "Israel", but as far as the others are concerned, we shall try to hang on to them," Barak told "Israeli" television.

"In any case, we must do everything possible to ensure Abdel Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani remain in our hands, and on Tuesday we shall examine the case of the other five at a meeting of the security cabinet," he said.

Sheikh Obeid was kidnapped by "Israeli" commandos from his home in southern Lebanon in 1989, and leader Mustafa Dirani was seized in 1994. They are regarded as the two most important detainees, and the "Israeli" authorities say they are "a danger to the security of "Israel"."

Barak said he had called a meeting of the security cabinet -- consisting of the ministers concerned with defense matters -- for Tuesday morning, "to discuss a bill to allow us to hold people who could help to get our troops back."

"Their continuing detention must depend on the government and the prime minister, not on the courts," he said.

His announcement came after he discussed the ramifications of the supreme court decision with Justice Minister Yossi Beilin and Attorney General Eliyakim Rubinstein.

Officials in Barak's office said it was unclear whether the legislation would affect the decision to free 13 of the 15 Lebanese imprisoned here.

"It was aimed at the very last at the two remaining detainees," said one of them, who spoke on condition of anonymity.

Barak admitted that "holding prisoners as we have done for more than 13 years is not very humane, but we are conducting a very tough war in the Middle East."

The "Israeli" parliament could convene Tuesday after the security cabinet's meeting.

Danny Naveh, a leader of the main Likud opposition party, and another MP from the ruling Labor party, Avi Yehezkel, were trying to call an emergency parliamentary session to rush through a law preventing the men's release.

The law would enable the state to keep "administrative detainees" in prison and would block any future appeals for them to be freed, Naveh and Yehezkel said in a news release.

Government leaders, security officials, the right-wing opposition and families of the missing "Israelis" have strongly criticized the court's decision to release the 13 Lebanese while four "Israelis" remained missing in Lebanon.

Three "Israeli" tank troops disappeared in Sultan Yakub, Lebanon during a 1982 battle against the Syrians. The fourth missing "Israeli", air navigator Ron Arad, was shot down over Lebanon in 1986.


Lahoud rules out security guarantees for "Israel"(3)

"Lebanese President Emile Lahoud said Monday that Beirut would not guarantee "Israel's" security after its withdrawal planned for July.

"We will not agree to guarantee the security of "Israel" and bear the responsibility of problems resulting from forcing Palestinian refugees out" of their country, he told representatives of the 50,000-strong Lebanese community in Kuwait.

""Israel" is trying to spread the word that we are against the "Israeli" withdrawal from our territories. In fact, we support the implementation of all international resolutions linked to the recovery of Arab and Lebanese rights," he added.

Lahoud has called for "Israel" to pull out of south Lebanon "without conditions or threats," in line with UN Security Council resolution 425.

Earlier this month he submitted a memorandum to the United Nations in which he expressed concern lest "certain Palestinian groups," forced to leave their homes in what is now "Israel" when the "Israeli" state was established, might try to carry out operations across the border "in the context of their right of return."


Hoss and Sharaa discuss regional situation(4)

"Prime Minister Dr Salim al-Hoss on Monday discussed the situation and the recent developments in the region during a phone call with Syrian Foreign Minister Dr Farouq al-Sharaa.

PM al-Hoss held the enemy's entity responsible for any border disorder following withdrawal from Lebanon, stressing Lebanon's invariable principles in the settlement process.

"It is known that Lebanon is for the unconditional implementation of resolution 425. This means complete and unconditional withdrawal from the Lebanese territories to the internationally recognized borders," al-Hoss said.

Anything other than this is redeployment of the "Israeli" occupation forces and not withdrawal in line with resolution 425, he added.

The premier said Lebanon can never be held responsible for the security of the border with ""Israel"", adding, "the security in that region is related to an overwhelming atmosphere of stability therefore, "Israel" is alone responsible for any disorder at the borders."

What is required is an organized pullout through a settlement which can solve the explosive unresolved issues such as that of the Palestinian refugees", al-Hoss concluded.


"Israeli" War Ministry will urge its militia to safeguard El-Khiam prisoners(5)

" The Defense Ministry yesterday promised to urge the South Lebanese Army* to safeguard the Lebanese prisoners in its El-Khiam Prison after "Israel" withdraws from southern Lebanon.

The statement came at the end of a hearing in the High Court of Justice regarding seven petitions claiming that "Israel" is in charge of the security zone, including El-Khiam Prison, and should release the prisoners who are being held under extremely harsh conditions.

The government says it does not control the security zone, the SLA, or El-Khiam Prison.

In response to Leah Tsemel, one of the petitioning attorneys, State Attorney's Office representative Malchiel Blass read out the following statement: "In the framework of the ongoing dialogue between the IDF** and the SLA in preparation for the withdrawal, the IDF will take action to convey to the SLA its position that the SLA must safeguard the well-being and security of the prisoners at El-Khiam Prison and find a reasonable and humanitarian solution in their matter. This subject will remain on the agenda until the withdrawal is executed."

Association for Civil Rights in "Israel" attorney Dan Yakir said the statement "was a step in the right direction, but insufficient. It does not include an unequivocal commitment to safeguard the lives and well-being of the El-Khiam prisoners."

Yakir said the only way to assure their safety was to release them before the IDF withdrawal.

The hearing turned out to be preliminary. Presiding Justice Mishael Cheshin asked the sides whether they thought there was any point in hearing the petition, given the fact that "Israel" intends to withdraw from the security zone in less than three months.

Yakir said it was imperative to hear the petition before the withdrawal because it is not certain what the SLA would do to the prisoners after the IDF was gone.

Blass disagreed. "The entire situation is going to change in a few months," he told the court. "I don't think it is right for the court to intervene and to order the IDF to take action if the SLA refuses to heed the court's ruling. I don't believe an "Israeli" court has ever ordered the IDF to carry out an operation in occupied territory."


French academics urge occupation to close Khiam(6)

"French well-known academics have cabled the Zionist government's "legal advisor" Eliakem Robenstein, demanding the release of around 150 detainees held at al-Khiam detention center.

The French academics also addressed a similar request to the "Israeli" "supreme court".

The cable which was signed by "The University Committee for the Release of Khiam Detainees" said, "The detainees are going through very difficult conditions, besides the fact that they have been held illegally with no trial or charges."
