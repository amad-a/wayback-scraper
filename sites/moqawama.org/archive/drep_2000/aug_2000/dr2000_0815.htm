Introduction

In a fresh violation, the “Israeli” enemy soldiers crossed the fence along the Lebanese – Palestinian borders Monday morning, proceeded to level an embankment in Abbad, raised an “Israeli” flag and erected a barricade of barbed wire.

Elsewhere, the relatives of Lebanese hostages held in the Jewish state’s jails urged UN Secretary General Kofi Annan to help gain their release as soon as possible.

Fresh “Israeli” air and ground violations

i.a.
Al-Manar Television's correspondent in the South Ali Shuaib spotted a fresh “Israeli” violation of Lebanese territories.

The enemy troops have completely annexed the tomb of Sheikh Abbad and encircled it with a barbed wire, Shuaib said.

In a more provocative step, the enemy troops planted their entity's flag on the tomb, he added.

They also loaded their arms when our correspondent neared the site and videotaped the new violation.

Moreover, the occupation's warplanes violated the Lebanese airspace Monday morning. One enemy warplane flew at a medium altitude over the Marjeyoun area…(1)

The “Israeli” soldiers were spotted crossing the Blue Line where it bisects an ancient tomb on Abbad hill ­ along with two earth excavators to a depth of around 20 meters. The earth removers leveled an embankment before retiring.
But the soldiers remained west of the tomb on Lebanese soil, having raised an “Israeli” flag and erected a barricade of barbed wire.
There was no explanation for the incursion from the “Israeli” Army. Abbad hill was the location of one of the most important “Israeli” outposts during the occupation. The hilltop post had commanding views over the central sector of the occupation zone to the west and northern Galilee to the east.
The UN-delineated Blue Line split the outpost into two, granting Lebanon about two-thirds of the position and forcing “Israel” to retreat a few meters to the east.
The hilltop is the site of Boundary Pillar 33 of the original border markers dating from 1923, when the frontier was first demarcated. One of “Israel’s” last remaining violations of the Blue Line, which delayed the deployment of UNIFIL peacekeepers and the joint security force occurred at Abbad.
“Israel” says Abbad is the burial place of Rabbi Ashi, who lived in the fifth century, while Lebanon says Muslim Sheikh Abbad, who lived 500 years ago, is buried there.
Following the “Israeli” withdrawal in May, armed and uniformed Hizbullah fighters took up positions at the former crossing points into “Israel”.
Since UNIFIL troops deployed to nine positions along the Blue Line, the fighters have reduced their presence along the border, abandoning their weapons and dressing in civilian clothes, but continuing to observe the frontier.
Observers believe that Hizbullah’s continued presence along the border is to signal “Israel” that no offers of security can be expected while the Golan Heights remain occupied ­ although prospects of renewed cross-border fighting are remote for the time being.
Hizbullah appears to have been granted considerable leeway in dictating security requirements in the South ­ possibly in acknowledgment of the party’s role in ousting “Israel” from Lebanon.
“Israel’s” latest alleged violation comes three days after an “Israeli” Army excavator reportedly entered the Marj valley opposite Metulla and dumped several loads of sand stretching six meters wide and several hundred meters long. The sand allows “Israel” to spot footprints and vehicle tracks on the Lebanese side of the border.
“Israeli” jets were also observed over parts of the western sector on Monday, for the first time since the withdrawal. Witnesses said the planes were seen at high altitude over Yarin, Teir Harfa and Jibbayn.

Families of Lebanese hostages' hunger strike enters eighth day

hostages

Relatives of 19 Lebanese hostages held by the “Israeli” occupation's entity for more than a decade appealed to the United Nations on Monday to secure their release.

"They have become skeletons in “Israeli” jails,'' said a letter to Secretary General Kofi Annan from the Follow Up Committee campaigning on behalf of the prisoners.

"“Israel” withdrew from Lebanon but did not release the prisoners. How can they return the land but not the human beings? How can you say resolution 425 has been executed?'' the group asked.

The United Nations said last month that "“Israel”" had fulfilled Resolution 425 after it ended its 22-year-old occupation of south Lebanon in May.

But the “Israeli” entity still holds 17 Lebanese who fought the occupation forces. It abducted another two officials from Hizbullah as "bargaining chips" for possible information on an “Israeli” pilot who went missing while bombarding Lebanon in the 1980s.

Human rights groups say the “Israeli” entity is breaking international law by holding all 19 prisoners.

Relatives of the prisoners have gone on hunger strike and staged a sit-in opposite the U.N. regional headquarters in Beirut since last week, drawing international attention to their plight.

Several delegations have arrived to express their support, the latest from the French Green Party.

"We promised the families to come. Their plight cannot remain forgotten with the absence of a formal peace agreement,'' said Patrick Farbiaz, a Green Party official.

Human Rights groups in Australia backed the families action adding that moves will be taken in Australia by the end of August to pressurize the occupation's entity into releasing the Lebanese hostages…(2)

Representatives from the Palestinian movement Hamas and a Syrian delegation from the People's Assembly also joined the sit-in. Relatives reiterated their frustration over last week's deployment of 1,000 army and security forces in the former occupied zone in south Lebanon. They called on the government to pronounce the “Israeli” withdrawal as incomplete until the Jewish state frees all Lebanese hostages. The head of the Follow Up-Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in “Israeli” Prisons Mohammad Safa handed UN officials a letter addressed to Secretary General Kofi Annan. The committee condemned “Israel’s” failure to release the Lebanese, saying the implementation of UN Security Council Resolution 425 remains incomplete. UM Anwar says she will continue her hunger strike. She said her nourishment is when she hears efforts are being stepped up to release the detainees…(3)

BBC will go to court over driver killed by Merkava…(4)

“The BBC plans to force the “Israeli” government to apologize for the killing of a Lebanese employee and win financial compensation for his family through a court in “Israel”.
The decision to pursue a civil case in the Jewish state came after the BBC chose not to file a complaint against “Israel” with the International War Crimes Tribunal. Instead, the BBC felt that a lawsuit within the “Israeli” legal system would reap greater benefits for the family of Abed Taqqoush.
The 54-year-old driver was killed by an “Israeli” tank shell on May 23, less than 24 hours before the “Israeli” occupation ended. Taqqoush, a long-time fixer for the BBC and other foreign news organizations, became the last civilian fatality of the 22-year conflict.
“The BBC’s first priority has been to act in the Taqqoush family’s best interests,” Adrian Wells, who heads the BBC investigation, told The Daily Star. “After meeting with them in London it became clear that securing compensation and a proper apology from the “Israeli” government was the family’s most important concern.”
He said that having taken legal advice, the BBC had started moves within “Israel” to try to achieve both these goals.
“It’s clear any international action against “Israel” would not in itself yield any compensation directly,” Wells said. “The BBC is in regular contact with the “Israeli” authorities and there is no question of our letting the matter drop.”
Taqqoush was waiting for two journalists, a veteran BBC reporter, Jeremy Bowen, and his Lebanese cameraman, Malek Kenaan, to finish filming just 100 meters away when a shell hit his parked Mercedes.
The round was fired by an “Israeli” Merkava tank on the edge of the “Israeli” settlement of Menara on the border. Initially, “Israel” denied firing the round but later admitted the incident when confronted with evidence from a BBC investigation team.
The “Israeli” Army claimed that soldiers opened fire because they thought the driver was a “terrorist.” They stopped short of explaining, however, why a “terrorist” sitting in a parked car was targeted instead of the cameraman and journalist who were pointing their camera, supposedly a rocket launcher, toward “Israel”.
It was also clear to “Israeli” and foreign journalists watching the scene from the “Israeli” side the so-called “terrorists” were civilians. These discrepancies led the BBC to condemn the attack as an “act of malice” which qualifies it to press charges against “Israel” at the International War Crimes Tribunal. The decision to sue the Jewish State within its own legal system came after the BBC flew Taqqoush’s son and nephew to London last month for a series of meetings.
Taqqoush left behind a wife and three children. The eldest, 21-year-old Mohammed has collected all the evidence from the BBC and Amnesty International investigations which will be used against “Israel”.
“I want to know why they killed my father,” he said. “I want the “Israeli” public to know what their government did and I want the “Israeli” soldiers who fired the shell to be punished. I have their names here with me. They were just sitting there having fun by killing Lebanese civilians. This was their entertainment. My father was killed so they could be amused.”
Four other civilians were killed by an “Israeli” tank shelling in almost exactly the same place during the “Israeli” withdrawal.
Taqqoush’s burned-out car still lies at the side of the road between Meiss al-Jabal and Houla, its trunk reduced to a mass of twisted metal, clearly showing where the round struck.
Ironically, the hill at Manara where foreign and “Israeli” journalists witnessed Taqqoush’s death now lies inside Lebanon as a result of the path traced by the UN’s so called Blue Line.
Many foreign journalists have already made their way to the site to pay tribute to the fixer and driver who accompanied them around Lebanon during the civil war. His death came as a shock prompting several foreign journalists to dedicate a column to him in their respective newspapers.
For Taqqoush the end came as he was talking to his son Mohammed on a cellular phone. Mohammed said that his father had been excited at watching the “Israelis” withdraw from Lebanon and wished that his son could see the “beautiful settlement” nearby. Suddenly, the line went dead. It was only later that Mohammed realized that his father had been killed.
