Qassem unveiled name of one of IR’s martyrs…(1)

”Hizbullah Deputy Secretary General his Eminence Sheikh Nahim Qassem unveiled the name of one of the Islamic Resistance's martyrs whose name had remained anonymous since he was martyred due to security necessities and considerations related to the movement of IR men.

Martyr Kamil Hussein Ali Hussein, martyred in the Houla area on July 29, 1991, was a Lahd militia recruit.

"He was a security penetration by our side into the collaborators' ranks for two years and a half. He was martyred by an explosive device after he was uncovered," Sheikh Qassem said, adding that Hussein and two people from the town (Houla) were a security group in the ranks of the occupation's agents.

These security guys, along with others, were one of the information sources and the reason behind a number of the successful operations against the “Israeli” occupation forces, Qassem said at a Hizbullah rally to commemorate the Houla martyrs.

Commenting on remarks made by some who claimed that Hizbullah is good at resistance but a failure in politics, Qassem said, "Those who faced for a long time the strongest enemy in the region, decided when to fire Katyushas to defend civilians and when not to, chose many methods at the appropriate time, and achieved goals, (they) can discuss the Lebanese situation, the internal politics, take the right wise decision, whether some people like it or not, because we have never acted on the grounds of family, village or party spirits."

We will always act according to our legal (religious) responsibility, even if a great deal of people rejected that, for we believe we shoulder the welfare of the people and their future, he said.

Our successful political party performance in the resistance against the “Israeli” occupation and the US, as the whole world has acknowledged, is evidence that we will not lack what is needed to have a skillful internal performance, he added.


Three children killed in south Lebanon explosion…(2)

“ Three children were killed and a fourth was wounded when a metal object blew up near a former “Israeli” military position in southern Lebanon where they were playing, a hospital source said Tuesday.

The sole survivor, 12-year-old Ibrahim Shehab, told AFP that they had been digging a large hole in a vacant area below the former military position in Baraashit when they came upon a metal object that blew up in their hands.

"They're dead, they're all dead," a dazed Ibrahim, who was injured by fragments, kept repeating before being taken into the operating theatre at al-Najm hospital in Tyre.

The Lebanese Red Cross also took the bodies of Ali Hussein Shehab, 10, Rabih Daud Hennawi, 9 and a third child, whose identity is not yet known, to the hospital.

The incident brings to nine the number of civilians killed by mines or shells exploding near former “Israeli” military positions in southern Lebanon since “Israeli” forces withdrew from the border strip on May 24, after 22 years of occupation. About 50 have been injured in the same period.

The UN Interim Force in Lebanon began demining work along the “Israeli”-Lebanese border in late June. But a UN demining expert from Ukraine said August 16 that maps of the mined areas that “Israel” provided were inaccurate.

The United Nations says 130,000 mines are scattered throughout the former “Israeli”-held area. Most were planted by the “Israelis” and their allies while others were laid earlier, at the beginning of the 1975-90 Lebanese civil war, experts say.


Twenty-five Lebanese return from occupation's entity…(3)

Twenty-five Lebanese who had fled to occupied Palestine following the “Israeli” occupation forces' retreat from south Lebanon in May returned home Monday, the police said.

The group included four ex-members of the now-defunct pro-”Israeli” militia.

The nine men, eight women, and eight children, crossed over from occupied Palestine into the coastal town of Naqoura. They were received by representatives of the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) and turned over to Lebanese police.

The nine men, including four who admitted that they had been members of the defunct militia, were held for questioning. The others, women and children, were allowed to return to their villages of Rmaish, Qlaia, Dibel and Marjeyoun.

Some 6,500 Lebanese, mostly former militiamen and their families, followed the “Israeli” occupation army as it retreated from Lebanon in May.

According to police figures, about 555 of those who fled to occupied Palestine have returned to Lebanon.

More than 2,200 Lebanese are in the process of being tried before the military court in Beirut, or have already been convicted, for collaboration with "Israel" or having contact with the “Israeli” enemy.
