Christians set to elect Hizbullah’s candidates in South…(1)

Hizbullah’s even-handed treatment of Christians in the former occupation zone following “Israel’s” withdrawal three months ago looks set to be rewarded at the polls on Sunday.
Many Christians said they would vote for Hizbullah in the hope that the party’s MPs will encourage the return of some of the 6,000 mainly Christian inhabitants of the area who fled to the Jewish state following the “Israeli” troop withdrawal in May.
The surprise backing by Christians for Hizbullah appears to be tacit acknowledgment of the party’s influence in the former occupation zone and the lack of acceptable alternative candidates drawn from the Christian community.
But many Christians, while welcoming the opportunity to vote freely once again in parliamentary elections, doubted that an end to the economic and social malaise gripping the border area was imminent.
Youssef Atallah, a shopkeeper, said he looked forward to voting and hoped that the local economy would pick up soon afterward.
“We’ve had no government here for 25 years. Since Lebanon became a republic, this area has always been neglected,” he said. “Every transitional stage is difficult. Hopefully things will get better.”
About two-thirds of the population of the Maronite village of Qlaya fled to “Israel” during the withdrawal. The village had provided the backbone for the “Israeli”-proxy militia since the it was first founded in 1976. Some of Qlaya’s residents, including former militiamen who opted to remain behind or civilian collaborators, are serving prison sentences.
“Our village is a special case because most of the people have left. We have no faith in the candidates because there is no one helping those in prison or their families,” said a resident in a barber shop.
“Our main concern is to bring back those who left and get the village back on its feet,” said another Qlaya resident. “We should vote for someone who is accountable and to whom we can take our problems.”
For Abdel-Massih Homsi in neighboring Bourj al-Moulouk, that meant voting for Hizbullah’s candidates.
“We used to think of Hizbullah as our enemies but they’ve proven themselves different,” he said. “Many abandoned houses were robbed in Bourj al-Moulouk but none by Hizbullah.”
Homsi said he had attempted to rally the residents of the town to support Hizbullah.
“Some have accepted the idea, others haven’t,” he said. “They’re scared of lending their support to one party and upsetting the other party.”
According to the residents, between 150 and 300 people remain in the village after the bulk of the 1,900 inhabitants headed for “Israel”.
The election-mania that has swept the country in recent weeks has bypassed the tiny Christian village of Dibil, hidden away in the wadis and hills of the western sector.
But voters in the Christian village of Alma Shaab, near the coast, have recognized the dominance of Hizbullah and Amal in the area and the prudence of supporting their candidates.
“Hizbullah is the most popular,” said Ibrahim Sayah. “They didn’t make any problems for us and they were very polite. I’m not sure what will happen afterward, but so far they’ve been good to us.”


Lebanese officer and four soldiers injured by mine in south…(2)

“ Five Lebanese troops were injured by a landmine in southern Lebanon Tuesday, the first casualties the army has suffered since deploying in the southern border strip at the beginning of the month.

The five, an officer and four soldiers, were taken to the government hospital in the nearby town of Nabatiyeh, an official of the army's information service told AFP.

A source at the hospital said the hand of one of the victims had to be amputated, but none of them was in a life-threatening condition.

The Lebanese police said the patrol had been clearing mines when one exploded.

The border strip was occupied by “Israel” for 22 years until it withdrew on May 24.

Nine civilians have been killed and about 50 others injured by exploding mines or shells since the “Israeli” pullback.

In the most recent incident on August 22, three children were killed and one injured when a metal object they found while playing close to a former “Israeli” military position blew up.

The UN Interim Force in Lebanon began demining work along the “Israeli”-Lebanese border in late June. But a UN demining expert from Ukraine said August 16 that maps of the mined areas provided by “Israel” were inaccurate.

The United Nations says 130,000 mines are scattered throughout the former “Israeli”-held area. Most were planted by the “Israelis” and their allies while others were laid earlier, at the beginning of the 1975-90 Lebanese civil war, experts say.


“Israeli” soldiers provoke up beated Lebanese at Fatima Gate…(3)

The Lebanese civilians have been accusing “Israeli” troops of provoking violence at Fatima Gate.
Report: Celebrations continue at Fatima Gate. Lebanese gather at this former crossing point with “Israel” to rejoice the Jewish state's troop withdrawal from south Lebanon. “Israeli” soldiers are stationed a few steps away from these make shift cafes where people from across the country, including Hizbullah fighters, who spearheaded the campaign to end “Israel’s” occupation, sip their morning coffee and talk about the latest cross-border stone throwing incidents. 
(Ali Toku saying: "There is one “Israeli” soldier over there, he provoked us, he said some very bad words to girls, and stripped naked that is when we looked for rocks and threw at them, they provoke us because we try to make a living out of our victory over them and our celebrations.. they should push this observation post back.. all they want is to continue to fight us").
Hussein makes a living from selling images and items depicting Hizbullah's victory over “Israeli” troops.
(Hussein Awada saying: "I was packing my goods at night when a rock hit my head, I started bleeding, they hit me for no reason, they provoke us to respond then they shoot in the air but people here are not scared").
Tensions have been mounting along the “Israeli”-Lebanese border fence. Top “Israeli” officers have threatened they may re-occupy south Lebanon if unrest escalates. Hizbullah has warned “Israel” against any cross-border attacks, saying the resistance remains armed and on alert. A number of Lebanese civilians and tourists have been injured by “Israeli” gunfire here. UN peacekeepers and Lebanese soldiers have been stationed throughout the zone “Israel” occupied for 22 years, but Beirut has yet to give the green light for any troop deployment along the frontier. It is not expected to do so before “Israel” frees at least a dozen Lebanese it still detains and withdraws from some patches of territory that remain under occupation. 
Fatima Gate has become a flashpoint for sporadic border violence. Many fear it could only be a matter of time before major cross border clashes erupt. Random flare-ups have been lasting few minutes. “Israel” and Lebanon, which are not bound by any peace agreement, have not pronounced an end to warfare, despite the calm that has prevailed since occupation troops retreated from the south. 
(Ali Krayem, saying: "They could not withstand our resistance when they occupied our land... They will never return here, they are now fortifying themselves inside their own land, they are even shielding their farms... ").
Clashes had been restricted to Lebanese civilians and “Israeli” troops. But lately settlers of northern “Israeli” cities have also been making the trip to Fatima Gate, according to “Israeli” news reports for the sole purpose of hurling rocks at Lebanese.
