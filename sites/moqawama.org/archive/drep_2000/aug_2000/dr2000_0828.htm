Hezbollah: Lebanon doesn't want conditional funds(1)

Hezbollah Deputy Secretary General his Eminence Sheikh Naem Qassem stressed that the Islamic Resistance maintains its readiness for any potential confrontation with the “Israeli” enemy.

Speaking at a rally in the southern town of Ebba, Sheikh Qassem said Lebanon does not need funds that are granted under political conditions, noting the US administration's announcement that it is considering a $60-million grant to Lebanon.

Qassem warned that the US may try to pressure Beirut into adopting policies favorable to "Israel" in return for financial aid to help reconstruct southern Lebanon.

He also rejected recent “Israeli” threats to reoccupy the former border region if tensions along the frontier escalate.

The Hezbollah official said the resistance is ready to face any confrontation and stressed "Israel" continues to pose a serious danger to Lebanon's stability

 

“Israeli” town near Lebanese border declines after pullout from Lebanon (2)

The settlers of Kiryat Shmona, near the border with Lebanon, has dropped eight percent since “Israeli” troops withdrew from Lebanon and Lebanese Hezbollah guerrillas moved down to the border, public radio said Saturday.

When “Israeli” forces pulled out of what they called their south Lebanon "security zone" May 24, Kiryat Shmona settlers felt they had been left open to attack from Lebanon and many of them went elsewhere.

According to Kiryat Shmona town hall statistics, 1,800 of the 21,000 settlers have since left, and the number of children registered in the town's schools for this school year is 20 percent lower than in 1999.

Since “Israeli” troops ended their 22-year occupation of south Lebanon, no rockets have fallen on northern “Israel”, nor has their been any exchange of fire on the border, at least 10 kilometers (about six miles) from Kiryat Shmona.

But that is not to say that the border is calm. “Israel” has filed 375 complaints with the UN Interim Force in Lebanon about acts of violence on the border by the Lebanese.

The complaints concern stone throwing, molotov cocktails, firecrackers and damage to barbed wire along the border.

For its part, Lebanon has accused “Israel” of encroaching onto its side of the border, dumping sand and flattening embankments.

UN peacekeepers and a mixed force of Lebanese troops and police have take up positions in the border area, but no units have deployed right on the frontier with “Israel”.
