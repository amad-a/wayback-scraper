Meanwhie, Hizbullahs Deputy General Sheikh  Nahim Qassem said that the Israeli withdrawal to the blue line was not complete as Lebanese soil, including the Shebaa farm, is still under occupation.

However, Lebanon has given United Nations troops the green light to deploy along the border with the occupied Palestine after being satisfied that all Israeli encroachments into its territory have been cleared up, Prime Minister Dr. Salim Al-Hoss said Friday.


Khatami calls on all to contribute to reconstruction of S. Lebanon(1)

 President Seyed Mohammad Khatami said Thursday that all should try to reconstruct southern Lebanon as it deserves, and safeguard the great victory against the Zionists through unity and solidarity among people and various tribes.

Speaking to the Speaker of the Lebanese National Assembly Nabih Berri, President Khatami said what happened in southern Lebanon was an important event in the contemporary era because the people of Lebanon had successfully ousted an occupying enemy without giving it any concessions.

True, the victory belongs to all the Lebanese people, the world of Islam and the Arab world, but the Islamic resistance and the brave children of Amal movement did have an important and grave role in the victory, noted Khatami.

Khatami thanked Lebanese government for taking good and principled stances in support for the resistance movement.

Touching on failure of the Camp David-II talks, Khatami said one should be alert to the future conditions of the region and to the plots being hatched against victory of the Lebanese nation.

He said the plan to shift the U.S. embassy to Beit-Ul-Moqaddas (Qods) is a sign that the U.S. sympathizes with Israel for the latter's defeat in the Camp David-II talks. Due to this reason the U.S. has no competency to play a mediation role between Palestinians and Muslims on the one hand and Israel on the other hand, added the president. He said Beit-Ul-Moqaddas belongs to all Palestinian people and the right to decide the fate of Palestine rests with Palestinians, including Muslims, Christians and Jews, he added.

The Iranian President said the world of Islam and Christianity and even the freedom seeking Jews who disclaim Zionism should join hands to restore the usurped historical rights.

Berri said that national unity, cohesion among all the Lebanese people, including Muslims and Christians, as well as consensus between the Resistance Movement and Lebanese government had served as basis for the great victory against the Zionist regime.

He praised Iran for its "fundamental'' role in the resistance against the Zionist regime and in the attainment of the great victory and thanked Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, government and nation for their spiritual support to the people and government
of Lebanon.

Berri said unity and strategic cooperation among Iran, Syria and Lebanon served as important factor in victory of against the Zionist regime.

He said Camp David-II talks show that the enemies of Lebanon hate to see more victories by the Lebanese nation, and were trying to prevent the progress of the people of Lebanon to their further goals.

He commented that the Lebanese would certainly maintain their integrity as a nation and would stick fast to the ideals that brought them their recent victory.


Hizbullah: Lebanese soil is still occupied(2)

The withdrawal of the Israeli occupation forces to the blue line was not complete pullout as Lebanese soil, including the Shebaa farms, is still under occupation, Hizbullah Deputy Secretary General his Eminence Sheikh Nahim Qassem said.

Speaking at a rally to commemorate the martyrs of Bourj el-Barajneh (southern suburbs of Beirut), Sheikh Qassem underlined that the Resistance is ready to perform its task once again.

The Resistance is the one that decides when to launch its efforts and jihad, he told thousands of Hizbullah supporters who gathered to mark the martyrdom of 18 Islamic Resistance men who had been martyred in operations against the Israeli occupation forces.

His Eminence called for mass turnout at the forthcoming general elections in order to back the march of the Resistance.


Lahoud gives UNIFIL the green light
800 peacekeepers begin moving into liberated areas(3)

UNIFIL was given the green light by the government on Friday to begin deploying throughout the former occupation zone.
Around 800 peacekeeping troops are scheduled to begin moving at dawn into the towns and villages of the liberated areas, an operation that is expected to take several days to complete.
All the troops are ready on the ground and we are raring to go, said acting UNIFIL spokesman Daljeet Bagga.
The final approval was relayed Friday morning to UNIFIL commander Major General Seth Kofi Obeng by President Emile Lahoud.
Prime Minister Salim Hoss told The Daily Star that the decision to deploy UNIFIL troops was a landmark moment in the troubled history of the South.
We hope this will be the way to re-establish Lebanese authority in the areas that were under occupation and reintegrate it into the rest of the country. I am certain that security in the South will be as strong as it is in the rest of the country, Hoss said.
The premier admitted that Lebanon was still unhappy with the location of an Israeli outpost on the border, 2 kilometers south of Yaroun, the last suspected violation of the so-called Blue Line of withdrawal.
There was a little misunderstanding over the system of measurements, he said. We reserved our right to disagree with this point but it has not stopped us giving the green light to UNIFIL, he said.
Asked when the 1,000-strong security force of military police and Internal Security Forces would deploy into the liberated areas, Hoss said that the move could come 24 hours after UNIFIL had settled into its new sector.
We are ready for it now but we want to see where UNIFIL deploys so that our special force knows where to go, he said.
UN Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen welcomed the deployment of UNIFIL, saying it was another milestone... on the path to the full implementation of Resolution 425.
The deployment... is a testament to Lebanons political leadership and the Lebanese people who, for 22 years, have been determined to see the fulfillment of Security Council Resolutions 425 and 426, Larsen said in a statement.
The path has not always been easy, but todays deployment is the clearest demonstration possible of the excellent co-operation between the United Nations and the government of Lebanon, he added.
Larsen telephoned Lahoud, Hoss and Speaker Nabih Berri to congratulate them personally.
UNIFIL said that the main aim of the peacekeepers was to instill confidence among the local inhabitants. They would achieve this by occupying positions, conducting armoured mobile and static patrols, observing and reporting on all relevant occurrences and activities, including any future violations of the Blue Line, a statement said.
Civilians were also warned against approaching any minefields and asked to report the presence of unexploded ordinance.
UNIFIL occupied six new posts along the Blue Line a week ago. Some 20 new positions had been earmarked for the peacekeepers, but the number was reduced to 12 Friday evening.
The decision is to establish 12 new posts now and augment around eight old positions (located in the former occupation zone), Bagga said.
The Fijian battalion is to send three platoons of around 35 soldiers each to Alma Shaab, Mazraat Jibayu and Mazraat Arab near the coast in the western sector.
The Nepalese will deploy two platoons to Ramieh and near Rmaish. The battalion already has one platoon on the border south of Rmaish.
The Irish are moving to Aitaroun and to another position due south of the village, opposite an Israeli outpost and the settlement of Avivim.
The Ghanaian battalion, which already has a platoon located beside the Israeli settlement of Manara, will dispatch troops to nearby Meiss al-Jabal.
The Finns are deploying to two posts in and near Addaisseh. The Indian battalion is sending two platoons to Meri, 2 kilometers east of Khiam, and Qlaya.


Invited by Hizbullah, Damascus University delegation visits south(4)

A large delegation from the Damascus University, including its president Dr Hani Mortada besides faculty deans and directors, toured the liberated southern Lebanon and the border area.

President of the Lebanese University Dr Asaad Diab, Head of Hizbullah's Educational Mobilization Unit (HEMU) Dr Bilal Nheim, and Public Relations' Director in the Supreme Lebanese-Syrian Council Ahmed el-Hajj Hassan took part in the tour.

The tour, at an invitation from HEMU, began early in the morning at the notorious al-Khiam detention center where the delegation inspected the brutality of the Israeli occupation and its collaborators, toured the prison's cells and saw with their own eyes the way the occupation used to torture the detainees.

The delegation then moved to the Fatma Gate where they met a number of the residents who talked about their suffering under occupation.


Arab intellectuals urge Palestinians to fight force with force(5)

Participants in a conference of Arab intellectuals from all over the Arab World on Friday called on the Palestinians to follow in Lebanons footsteps and gain their demands through violence.
Several dozen people, including the 23 researchers who took part in the Democratic Revolutionary Arab Dialogue Forum, gathered at Khiam prison
The communiqué of the four-day conference, which opened on Tuesday at the Bristol Hotel, hailed the resistance movements victory over the Zionist enemy and its agents, a victory that revived the hope of all Arabs.
It urged the Palestinians to follow in the footsteps of the Lebanese, arguing that the Zionist enemy does not understand any language other than the language of force and that resistance is the only means for liberating land and retrieving rights and dignity.
The intellectuals lashed out at Palestinian President Yasser Arafat and Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak, saying that last months Camp David summit in the United States sought to liquidate the Palestinian cause according to the wasteful and humiliating process started by the (1993 Palestinian-Israeli) Oslo Agreement.
It also called for developing a new Arab discourse that supports resistance movements, combats normalization attempts between Arab countries and Israel, and opposes cultural invasion by information media disrespectful of our Arab-Islamic principles.
Participant Ali Oqla Orsan, who heads the Union of Arab Writers and who read out the recommendations, warned against attempts by the United States to impose a settlement for the Arab-Israeli conflict.
The participants, who were accompanied by Baalbek-Hermel MP and Hizbullah official Ammar Musawi, and Mohammed Said Khansa, the mayor of Ghobeiri and a Hizbullah official, then moved to the Fatima Gate on the Lebanese-Israeli border where they threw stones across the barbed-wire fence.
