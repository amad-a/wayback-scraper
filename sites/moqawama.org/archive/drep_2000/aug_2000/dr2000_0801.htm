Hizbullah: prisoners are responsibility of all, US' moving embassy to al-Quds will cost it a lot(1)

Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah called on all the Lebanese to assume their responsibilities pertaining to the issue of the Lebanese hostages in the Israeli occupation's prisons.

Speaking at a Hizbullah rally to mark the 11th anniversary of "Israels" abduction of Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid in his hometown of Jibsheet (Nabatieh area), Sayyed Nasrallah stressed that the Islamic Resistance has vowed to its Lord that these prisoners will not be left in the occupation's prisons.

You know that when the Resistance promises, it fulfills and acts, he said recalling that the Resistance "has done a lot in the past to free the prisoners and offered martyrs to secure this, the last of these being Hajj Karbala (from the Beqaa town of Bidnayel) and Martyr Jalal Rammal (from the town of Nabatieh), his Eminence said.

As he reminded his audience of the crimes perpetrated by the Israeli occupation in Lebanon, he mocked the claims made by the Americans and the Israelis regarding democracy and human rights.

Do they have the right to detain civilians in order to pressurize to secure the release of a prisoner pilot whom we know nothing about? he asked.

As for us, in their logic, we don't have the right to do anything to recover our oppressed prisoners and detainees.  And if we do anything, and we will - Allah willing, the terrorism charge is waiting for us, not only in Washington, but also in all the world's capitals, he said.

His Eminence talked about the American foreign policy.

The US has no policy in the Middle East; it has an Israeli policy, and it uses all its influence to achieve the demands of the Zionists and pressurize the Palestinian negotiator to give "Israel" everything, he said.

If he (the Palestinian negotiator) does not submit, the US will move its embassy to al-Quds (Jerusalem) and will stop all aid and tell the whole world, "Don't pay anything to the Palestinians!" he added.

The secretary-general commented on this logic addressing the Palestinians by saying, "Reject these funds before they stop giving them to you.  The price of these funds is al-Quds. Return to your Ummah, honor, Resistance, and option.. to the option of Jihad."

The people of Palestine's answer to the Americans must be: ""You (Americans) can move your embassy to al-Quds and send your diplomats there. And the honorable people can turn your embassy to rubble and return your diplomats in coffins,'' Sayyed Nasrallah said.

"This is the only logic that can be understood by the United States and Israel," he added.

"A US embassy in al-Quds, similar to the usurping Zionist entity, has no future in the region as long as there are people willing to launch a war of jihad (holy struggle) and be martyred in such a war," the secretary-general said.

In his speech, his Eminence announced the names of the Faithfulness to the Resistance's  (FTR) candidates to the parliamentary elections calling on all to observe the electoral alliances.

Concerning the Israeli occupation's violations of Lebanese territories, the Hizbullah secretary-general reiterated the party's position.

Hizbullah does not acknowledge a blue or red line.  What we understand is that every occupied land must return to the homeland with the jihad and the pure bloods of the martyrs who are waiting, his Eminence concluded.


President: liberation was fruit of steadfastness and confrontation, not begging(2)

Liberating the south and the Western Beqaa was the fruit of the residents' steadfastness, the Resistance's sacrifices, the army's support, Syria's assistance, and the state's adoption of a clear invariable policy of confrontation and realistic practical diplomacy, President Gen Emile Lahoud said on Sunday.

Marking Army Day, Lahoud addressed the armed forces, saying that Lebanon's "rejection of pressures bore the fruit of our reaching the final stage of removing the Israeli violations, and having a complete withdrawal."

The president called for a good employment and investment of this victory, besides never trusting "Israel". This requires "continued maximum preparedness by the defense forces and the Resistance as long as the horizons of just and comprehensive peace are not close.

Guaranteeing stability in the South would require a three-pronged effort by the army, the security forces and the resistance, he said.

"The army's role will be defined according to the state's responsibility, which stems from a strategic vision of peace and the rejection of the settlement of Palestinian refugees (in Lebanon)," the president's statement read.


UNIFIL blasts a path toward full deployment in the border zone(3)

The second stage of UNIFILs deployment to some 20 positions in the former occupation zone received preliminary approval on Monday but final authorization will come from the president and prime minister.
The decision to defer the final say to the political authority on UNIFILs continued move into the liberated areas came as a team of Ukrainian engineers finished clearing a path through a 300-meter deep minefield on the border south of Yaroun.
The move will allow Lebanese Army officers to examine the last remaining suspected Israeli violation of the so-called Blue Line on Tuesday afternoon.
Brigadier General Maher Toufeili, the governments coordinator to UNIFIL, and the peacekeepers commander, Major General Seth Kofi Obeng, met at the Defense Ministry in Yarze to discuss coordinating the deployment of UN troops with the 1,000-strong joint army and Internal Security Force unit.
A UNIFIL officer said further talks would be held between the peacekeepers and the Lebanese Army. He added that there would be no further UNIFIL deployment until talks had concluded.
According to sources close to the meeting, the Lebanese unit will consist of 500 ISF personnel, 200 military police and 300 military intelligence officials and will be under the command of the ISF, rather than the army.
Almost 200 UNIFIL soldiers have deployed along the border since Friday in six separate locations. The remaining troops are expected to be dispatched to villages and towns in the former occupation zone.
While the two generals in Beirut discussed UNIFILs deployment, a 30-strong team of Ukrainian engineers on Monday completed the arduous task of removing dozens of Israeli land mines beside the border, 2 kilometers south of Yaroun.
The team, working in blazing heat, has removed 101 Israeli M4 shoebox anti-personnel mines since Saturday, clearing a path 5 meters wide.
The Ukrainians are as professional an outfit as I have ever seen and extremely brave, said Irish Colonel Frank Kelly, UNIFILs deputy chief operations officer. They have been face to face with these mines, working in 45 degrees of heat.
The newly cleared path will let the Lebanese border inspection team reach Boundary Pillar 18, where it is suspected an Israeli outpost encroaches on Lebanese territory.
UNIFIL has made measurements and believes the outpost is fully inside Israel. If the Lebanese team agrees, then the last of Israels violations along the border should have been rectified, paving the way for stage two of the UNIFIL deployment.
The Ukrainians efforts along the border will also allow direct resupplying of a lone platoon from the Irish battalion, which deployed beside the Israeli outpost at BP 18 on Friday.
Due to the almost unbroken chain of minefields along the border, the Irish soldiers were forced to travel through Israel to reach their new position.
Colonel Oleg Khavroniuk, the Ukraine battalions commanding officer, confirmed that the mine clearing was complete and Tuesday morning would be spent widening the track and clearing a bulldozed earth barricade.
Israeli trucks and military jeeps drove along the border kicking up clouds of powdery white dust. The squeal of caterpillar tracks and the clank of heavy machinery could be heard as the Israelis continued to build their new border fence.
Some of the Ukrainian soldiers, stripped to the waist and  with bandannas tied around their foreheads, lounged in the shade beneath a canvas sheet tied between two trucks. Their mine-clearing gear ­ helmets, flak jackets and trowels ­ lay scattered on the ground beside dozens of empty water bottles.
Khavroniuk said the task began by firing a Bangalore torpedo ­ a rocket, trailing explosive rope ­ across the minefield. The rope explodes when it hits the ground, detonating any mines in the immediate vicinity.
We destroyed six mines this way, he said. The explosion also sparked a small brush fire which detonated several other land mines and exposed several anti-tank mines in an adjacent field. We have found 11 anti-tank mines in this small stretch alone, Kelly said.
The Israelis told us that they only planted 100 anti-tank mines in the whole of Lebanon, he added with a wry smile.


Foreign diplomats throw their weight behind bid to take Israel to court(4)

Arab and foreign diplomats meeting with Prime Minister Salim Hoss on Monday supported the countrys intention to file a complaint against Israel with the International Court of Justice (ICJ) at The Hague.
The diplomats, who asked not to be identified, said Lebanons decision to file the complaint with the International Court was better than filing a complaint with the United Nations, because it could lead to a practical solution during a shorter period of time.
The diplomats, who met with Hoss to discuss the legal aspects of the complaint, also expected Israel to use all means to counter the move, with pressure from diaspora Jews.
They also quoted Hoss as saying that Lebanon had no choice but to file a complaint against Israel for compensation because it was impossible to ignore the Israeli atrocities.
The diplomats warned the government not to let fears about its chances of winning the case keep it from acting promptly on the issue.
The benefits of filing a complaint are not only financial. Winning the case would reveal Israels aggressiveness against Lebanon through its 22 years of occupation and its refusal to implement international resolutions that led to losses of up to $40 billion, said a diplomatic source familiar with the meeting.
Moreover, the diplomats stressed the importance of the fact that decisions made by the ICJ were binding.
Another advantage of filing a complaint, they added, was that it would represent item for negotiation in case peace talks with Israel were resumed.
Hoss discussed the legal aspects of the complaints with Justice Minister Joseph Shaoul and Michel Tueini, an international law expert, the diplomats said.
They added that members of the meeting also discussed Israels legal history in filing complaints, particularly what it requested from Germany following World War II and from Iraq after the Gulf War.
A memo summarizing the discussions will be presented it to the Cabinet.


Tribunal continues prosecution of collaborator(5)

In its 20th round of trials against Israeli collaborators, the Military Tribunal issued sentences against 39 people on Monday, the maximum being a five-year prison term. The trials were presided over by Military Judge Maher Safieddine. All those accused were punished for collaborating with and giving information to the enemy.
Thirteen people accused of joining the former Israeli-proxy militia were also interrogated. Sentences varied from a three-week prison term to fines ranging from LL1 million-LL2 million for entering Israel. Among them was a physician who supervised a militia infirmary in Yarin and contacted Israeli officers. One man was acquitted for lack of evidence. Last week, 23 people were also questioned about joining the collaborators. The minimum sentence issued was one year in prison, while all received fines of up to LL400,000. They were also prohibited from living in villages where they were born ­ mainly Bint Jbeil, Marjayoun, Hasbaya and Tyre.
First Investigating Magistrate Riad Talih issued 21 arrest warrants against former militiamen who, after questioning, will stand trial on charges of collaboration with Israel.


Ex-Israeli-allied militiamen go to Germany(6)

Former members of the disbanded Israeli-proxy militia who fled their country  arrived in Germany on Monday, where they will be given refuge.
The militiamen members were among 12 Lebanese nationals from three families who arrived at Berlins Schoenefeld airport en route to resettlement in the nothern port city of Hamburg.
 The collaborators men were initially given refuge in Israel, which has, however, asked other nations like Germany, Canada, Chile, France and the United States to accept some of the Lebanese refugees. ­
