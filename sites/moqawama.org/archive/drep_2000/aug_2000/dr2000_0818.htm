Sightseers armed with cameras storm Abbad Hill as “Israelis” retreat…(1)

““Israel” on Thursday rectified a violation they had committed a day earlier by removing a defensive fence they had set up at the top of the Abbad Hill near Houla. “Israeli” soldiers retreated to their own side of the site after removing equipment they placed on the Lebanese end of the hill.
A Ghanaian UNIFIL unit took positions around the hill where it installed an observation point. “Israel’s” evacuation of the site prompted dozens of Lebanese to visit the religious shrine and start to take photographs.


Beirut court sentences 41 more collaborators with “Israel”…(2)

“A Beirut military court sentenced another 41 Lebanese to prison terms for "collaborating with “Israel”," bring to 1,016 the number of Lebanese sentenced since the court began mass trials of 2,200 people on June 5.

One was judged in absentia and sentenced to 15 years in prison.

Two of the others received five year jail terms and a third was sentenced to four years prison, with a ban on visiting their villages in the former “Israeli”-occupied zone for collaborating with the “Israeli” intelligence services.

The sentences for people accused of being members of the pro-”Israeli” militia or of visiting “Israel” varied from three weeks to five years, with some also barred from living in the former occupied zone.

This was the 22nd group of people to be judged since the start of the mass trials of Lebanese suspected of collaborating with the Jewish state, following the May 24 withdrawal of “Israeli” troops from southern Lebanon after 22 years of occupation.

The military court is continuing with the hearings despite court holidays.


NIS 60M wasted in Lebanon pullback…(3)

The NIS 60 million cost of repairing and partially moving the fence on the Lebanese border, as demanded by the United Nations at the behest of the Lebanese government, was far above the original budget for the redeployment.
According to experts, this was a sheer waste of money that could have been avoided had Prime Minister Ehud Barak rejected the proposal to build the fence along the line of the 1978 border and accepted the advice of those who said it should follow the line of the 1923 international border.
It now emerges that from the outset, the defense establishment was divided on how to proceed. One group of experts, under Maj. Gen. (Res.), Uri Saguy, the chief negotiator on the Syrian track, argued that “Israel” should stick to the old international border and that any deviation from it would eventually be detected by UN or Lebanese cartographers, forcing “Israel” to carry out additional mini-withdrawals.
The other group, which was backed by Maj. Gen. (Res.) Danny Yatom, Barak's top adviser, said the border fence should follow the line that was formed at the conclusion of Operation Litani in 1978, when UNIFIL (UN Interim Force in Lebanon) confirmed the “Israeli” Army’s withdrawal from Lebanon.
Barak accepted the Yatom option and forced the other group of experts to leave the negotiating team.
The result was that according to Lebanese perception, the “Israeli” Army did not withdraw to the international border, but to a different line. Beirut, therefore, demanded that “Israel” make numerous adjustments in the positioning of its army outposts and positions. In the wake of this, “Israel” had to demolish some of its outposts, build new roads and carry out other changes, adding NIS 60 million to the original budget for the redeployment.
Speaking at one of the sessions held to sum up the redeployment, Barak admitted that he had been warned about this and said it was unfortunate that he had not taken the advice of the other group of experts.
