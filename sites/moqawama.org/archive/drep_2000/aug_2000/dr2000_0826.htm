Introduction

Hizbullah’s Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah threatened to fire rockets into Galilee if the “Israeli” enemy sent troops back into Lebanon stressing that the Zionist settlements are now at the mercy of the Islamic Resistance (IR) fighters.

For his part, Hizbullah’s Deputy Secretary General H.E. Sheikh  Nahim Qassem said that “we are at a temporary stage which could very easily break out into warfare because the enemy has an aggressive nature”.

However, Lebanese Speaker of Parliament asserted that Lebanon could never be assured of “Israeli” intention and that the “resistance and its weapon will stay as long as “Israel” remains on the Lebanese border”.

Hizbullah comments on occupation's threats: enemy is afraid, knows we will retaliate

”Hizbullah Secretary-General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah has warned the “Israeli” occupation's entity against any military adventure against Lebanon, stressing that the Zionist settlements in occupied northern Palestine are now "under the feet of the (Hizbullah) Mujahedeen."

Sayyed Nasrallah said during a rally to honor families of martyrs, prisoners and the wounded of the Islamic Resistance men in southern Lebanon late Thursday that Lebanon remains "within the circle of “Israeli” threats and ambitions and we will continue to hear more threats of ground and air invasions."

His Eminence was replying to renewed threats by the commander of the “Israeli” occupation's army ground forces, Maj. Gen. Moshe Ivri Sukenik, who said Wednesday that “Israel” would have to reoccupy parts of southern Lebanon if fighting resumes there.

“Israeli” troops withdrew from the southern region in May, ending a 22-year occupation.

Sukenik said it would be impossible to defend “Israeli” border towns and villages from within those settlements.

"Does this General imagine that an aggression on Lebanon, its south or any of its villages will be a promenade?" Sayyed Nasrallah asked.  "Do this General and his state imagine that the aggression against Lebanon would remain without retaliation or confrontation?"

The Hizbullah chief said that when the Jewish settlements in occupied northern Palestine "were dozens of kilometers away from us" Hizbullah men used to bombard them when civilians in south Lebanon were hurt by “Israeli” shelling.

"Today, they (settlements)  are under the feet of our Mujahedeen," Sayyed Nasrallah said, adding that the renewed “Israeli” threats indicate "a weakness because the “Israeli” forces cannot enter Lebanon as they would be aggressing and confronting people who refuse humiliation and prefer to die as martyrs instead of dying in their beds"…(1)

Nasrallah threatened to fire rockets into Galilee if “Israel” sent troops back into Lebanon. “When their settlements were only kilometers away from us they were never immune from our danger. What about now ­ when the settlements are at the mercy of our resistance fighters?” Nasrallah said in Maaroub.
“Does this “Israeli” general think that attacking Lebanon or its South or any Lebanese village is a picnic? Does he think we’re still living in 1978 or 1982? After May 24, 2000, we became something completely different,” he said referring to the date of the “Israeli” pullout.
Nasrallah mocked what he said was “Israel’s” obsession with Hizbullah. “You’re going to hear lots of talk and threats because the biggest nightmare of the Zionists is Hizbullah,” he said. “They’re obsessed with Hizbullah. They’re arresting Palestinians on the pretext that they belong to Lebanon’s Hizbullah.”
Ivry-Sukenik’s threats to send troops into the South was directed more at the “Israeli” Finance Ministry than at Lebanon. The “Israeli” military is locked in its annual battle with the Finance Ministry over the defense budget, which faces further cuts now that the expense of maintaining the occupation is over…(2)

“Hizbullah has been maintaining observation posts close to the border with occupied Palestine to prevent any infiltration attempts by the “Israeli” forces or their agents.

President Gen Emile Lahoud also rebuffed the “Israeli” threats.

"I think that after what happened “Israel” will think one hundred times before embarking on such a move," the president said.

Lahoud was referring to the Hizbullah and other resistance men who fought “Israeli” occupying troops and forced them to withdraw from south Lebanon on May 24.

Lebanon is no longer the passage it used to be in the past, the president said…(3)

Hizbullah warns “Israelis”: ‘We’re still ready to fight’…(4)

Hizbullah considers the present calm in the South temporary and is ready to fight “Israel” at any time, the party’s deputy secretary-general said on Friday.
“We’re at a temporary stage which could very easily break out into warfare because the enemy has an aggressive nature,” Sheikh Naim Qassem said.
Qassem was responding to comments by “Israel’s” ground forces commander, Major General Moshe Ivry-Sukenik, earlier in the week in which he warned that “Israeli” troops would return temporarily to Lebanon if the settlements along the border came under attack.
Qassem told participants at a memorial service in Nabatieh that “due to these threats … the only solution is to be ready to face the enemy.”
Speaker: Resistance's arms to stay, “Israel” supplied wrong mine maps…(5)

Speaker of parliament said Thursday that “Israel” had provided the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) with wrong maps for locating land mines left behind when “Israeli” occupation forces pulled out of the region last May.

"Do you know that the maps presented by “Israel” are wrong maps? And I am responsible for what I am saying," Nabih Berri said during a rally in southern Lebanon.

He said the “Israeli” maps referred to "land where there are no mines and kept the lands where there are mines."

He said U.N. Secretary-General Kofi Annan could ask the UNIFIL about the "camouflage maps, which were provided in order to inflict casualties like what happened in Baraasheet."

He was referring to the killing of three Lebanese children Tuesday night by a landmine left over by “Israeli” occupation forces in the village of Baraasheet in southern Lebanon. A fourth child, a boy, was severely injured in the explosion.

Berri said Lebanon could never be assured of “Israeli” intentions and that the "resistance and its weapons will stay as long as “Israel” remains on the Lebanese border."

“Israel” pulled out its troops from south Lebanon on May 24, ending 22 years of occupation but leaving behind thousands of mines.

Nine people, including five children, were killed and 25 others were wounded, when they stepped on mines or played with unexploded shells since the “Israeli” withdrawal.

Ukrainian members of the U.N. peacekeeping mission have begun a de-mining operation, dismantling 330 mines so far.

U.N. mission spokesman Timor Goksel said it was difficult to determine the scope of the mine problem in southern Lebanon and noted that the UNIFIL troops were de-mining only along the border with occupied Palestine, although thousands of mines are well within Lebanese territory, near the edge of what used to be the “Israeli”-occupied zone.

The “Israeli” occupation forces left behind over 138,000 mines. Lebanon has appealed to other nations to help finance the de-mining of the former occupied zone, a project estimated
to cost $8.5 million.
