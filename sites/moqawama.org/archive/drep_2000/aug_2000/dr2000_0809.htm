Introduction

A convoy of 1000 soldiers and policemen is expected to complete Wednesday the first deployment of Lebanese forces in South Lebanon in more than two decades.

However, relatives of Lebanese detainees still held in Israeli prisons continued their hunger strike for the second day on Tuesday in front of UN House in downtown Beirut.

In the meantime, Amnesty International said Tuesday Israels detention of Lebanese prisoners without trial was a serious breach of international law and expressed support for relatives of detainees who have started a hunger strike.


Lebanese security force to deploy in liberated south Wednesday(1)

The deployment of Lebanese security forces in the newly-liberated areas will begin before dawn on Wednesday, according to Interior Minister Michel Murr.
A force of 500 soldiers and an equal number of policemen will deploy in the former Israeli occupation zone at 3am, Murr said in a brief statement.
The force will be based in Marjayoun, but it will also have positions in Bint Jbeil and many other main towns, a government source said.
UN peacekeepers deployed in the areas on Saturday ­ a step that Lebanon made the prerequisite for its own deployment ­ but tension in the area continued to simmer.
Murr has said the 1,000-strong force will be under the Interior Ministrys command and their job will be to maintain law and order in the zone. They will be joining a few hundred policemen deployed shortly after the Israeli withdrawal.
The minister said Lebanese security forces will not deploy along the border, saying that job is for the UN peacekeepers. Lebanon has repeatedly refused to send troops to its southern frontier, saying it does not want to serve as Israels border guard.
After weeks of planning, the decision to deploy was made Tuesday following a meeting at the Defense Ministry between senior Lebanese Army officers and the UN peacekeeping force.


Hunger strike gains growing sympathy(2)

Relatives of Lebanese detainees still held in Israeli prisons continued their hunger strike for the second day on Tuesday in front of UN House in downtown Beirut.
Some 600 Palestinian, Lebanese and other Arab detainees in Israeli prisons have already declared a hunger strike supporting the cause.
The protesters were joined by Josee Lambert, Canadas Amnesty International co-ordinator for Lebanon, who emphasized Amnestys support for those families who still have detainees in Israel.
The hunger strike was organized by the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in Israeli Prisons.
Lambert and other protesters spoke at the so-called tent for freedom which was pitched outside UN House on Monday.
Abbas Hammoud, whose brother Mustafa is among the 20 detainees Lebanon claims remain in Israeli prisons, gave an emotional speech urging others to show solidarity with the cause.
Perhaps the cries of mothers and children will pierce the ears of silence in Lebanon, he said.


Amnesty International supports Lebanese relatives of Israeli-held prisoners(3)

 The international human rights group Amnesty International said Tuesday Israels detention of Lebanese prisoners without trial was a "serious breach" of international law, and expressed support for relatives of detainees who have started a hunger strike here.

Amnesty "continues to demand the release of the Lebanese detainees... since Israel is obliged in the context of its withdrawal and in accordance with article 77 of the Fourth Geneva Convention to release immediately the Lebanese still in its jails," said Josee Lambert, Lebanon coordinator for the francophone Canadian section of Amnesty.

The convention, drawn up in 1949 to protect civilians in war, states that inhabitants of occupied areas "who have been accused of offences or convicted by the courts in occupied territory shall be handed over at the close of occupation ... to the authorities of the liberated territory."

Lambert was visiting the hunger strikers who have put up a tent outside the UN headquarters in Beirut.

"Amnesty considers that the detention of Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid and Mustapha Dirani are a grave breach of the Fourth Geneva Convention and believes that in holding these Lebanese, the Israeli courts are violating the basic principles of humanitarian law," she said.

Although Israel withdrew from southern Lebanon on May 24 after 22 years of occupation, 19 Lebanese nationals are still held in Israeli prisons. They include Sheikh Obeid and Dirani, who were seized by Israeli commandos 11 and six years ago respectively, and have been held ever since without charge or trial for use as bargaining chips for information about Israeli servicemen missing in action.

The other Lebanese prisoners have been tried and received sentences ranging up to life in prison. But some of those who have served their time have seen their sentences arbitrarily extended on a six-month basis.

The Israeli Supreme Court earlier this year ruled illegal the practice of keeping prisoners as bargaining chips.

However, an Israeli parliamentary commission has approved a bill that would let the Israeli military keep foreigners in administrative detention -- without due process of law -- if they are deemed to pose a danger to Israel. The bill has not yet come before a vote in the Israeli parliament.


Israeli-allied militia returnees accused of collaboration(4)

Eleven men who returned to Lebanon after fleeing to Israel were on Tuesday accused of collaborating with the Jewish state.
The men were among the approximately 6,000 Lebanese who moved into Israel during the withdrawal of its troops from the South in May.
They were among at least 400 others who returned, complaining about poor living conditions in Israel.
Many of those who fled were militiamen with Israels now-defunct militia and members of their families.
The men among the returnees turned themselves in to the Lebanese authorities, while women and children were allowed to return to their homes.
The latest 11, due to be tried at a later date to be set by a military court, were accused of dealing with Israel, fighting on the side of an enemy army, entering an enemy country and violating the law providing for boycotting Israel, said Assistant Military Prosecutor Abdullah Hajj.
One suspect, whose name was not disclosed, was also accused of shelling civilian homes and individuals in Habboush (near Nabatieh) in 1998, Hajj said in his list of accusations, which still needs the approval of Chief Military Prosecutor Nasri Lahoud.
While all accusations were made under a Penal Code article that carries maximum imprisonment sentences of 15 years with hard labor, the man accused of shelling civilians faces execution if convicted.
Hajj also accused the militiamen Kamal Mohammed Shreim and Ali Abdel-Raouf Ayoub of collaborating with Israel and detaining Lebanese Army First Sergeant Izzat Yassin for six years.
Suspected of arresting Yassin at his home village of Houla in the former occupation zone in 1994, the two men will face life imprisonment if convicted.
While Shreim fled to Israel and is due to be tried in absentia, Ayoub was among 2,200 collaborators who turned themselves in to the Lebanese authorities after the Israeli pullout.
The military court has so far issued sentences ranging between three weeks and 15 years imprisonment. One person has received a life sentence in absentia; dozens have been acquitted.
