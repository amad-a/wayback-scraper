Introduction

Forgetting the disgraced “Israeli” defeat in South Lebanon, a high-ranking “Israeli” military official threatened Wednesday that “Israel” would reoccupy Lebanon if hostilities erupt on the two countries’ border.

Meanwhile, the houses and people of Baraasheit were shrouded in black on Wednesday. The village was mourning three children who died when they set off an abandoned “Israeli” mortal shell.

However, the Lebanese Cabinet formed a committee to pursue  the government’s plans to sue “Israel” in the International Court of Justice to recoup damages caused by its attacks and 22-year occupation.


“Israeli” officer warns of renewed invasion of Lebanon…(1)

A top “Israeli” general warned on Wednesday that “Israel” is prepared to move back into Lebanon if the border does not remain peaceful ­ but that it would only be to restore order, and not to re-establish an occupation.
“The new line in the north is a border of peace,” said Major General Moshe Ivry-Sukenik, the commander of ground forces.
If fighting breaks out, Ivry-Sukenik said, “we won’t be able to protect the residents of the north … and it will obligate a strong reaction from the “Israeli” Army including a land action.”
But this time the army has no plans for a long-term stay, he said. “If there’s something like this it would only be temporary in order to create a new situation,” he said. “We don’t have any intention of history repeating itself. But during a battle we’ll have to take over temporarily.”


Baraasheit buries boys killed by shell…(2)

”The houses and people of Baraasheit were shrouded in black on Wednesday. The village, in the newly liberated Bint Jbeil area, was mourning three children who died when they set off an abandoned “Israeli” mortal shell.
Shops did not open until Hassan Chehab, 9, his 10-year-old brother Ali, and Rabih Hannawi, 12, were buried. During the funeral, Koranic verses were broadcast over loudspeakers throughout the village.
The children died on Tuesday, when the 120mm shell they were playing with exploded.
Two other children, the Chehabs’ 12-year-old brother Ibrahim and Mahmoud Abdel-Nabi, 11, were seriously wounded. They are being treated at the Jabal Amel Hospital in Tyre.
“My brothers and Rabih were digging a hole in the ground when the explosion took place. After that, there was dust and blood every where. I couldn’t move,” said Ibrahim Chehab.
Relatives of the deceased came from Beirut for the funeral, and many fainted when they saw the corpses at the Bint Jbeil hospital. Hizbullah cars transported the bodies to Baraasheit, whereupon they were carried by relatives through the village roads.
Women wailed as Hizbullah’s al-Mehdi Boy Scouts Association band played somber tunes.
Aida Hannawi, Rabih’s paternal aunt, tried to tear through the shroud to see the dead child’s face but was stopped by relatives. “Three children died in our village and not a single official or parliamentary candidate bothered to be with us today or to clean the liberated areas from mines and unexploded bombs,” she said.
“Our children die here and their children are abroad studying and living away from any danger,” she added, choking back tears. “This is a comedy. We’re not going to vote.”
At the Chehabs’ home, the two boys’ grandmother, Umm Hassan, shouted to the crowd: “No one must cry. The boys are going to heaven. We should pray for them.”
Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, Hizbullah’s commander in the South, led the prayers at the funeral. He told the crowd to watch out for mines and shells and called on the authorities to remove the ““Israeli” killing tools.”
Meanwhile, at the hospital, Ibrahim Chehab was being treated for shrapnel wounds all over his body. Doctors said his condition was stable.
Chehab had not been told the fate of his brothers.
He said: “I hope my brothers and Rabih are well.”
Abdel-Nabi had his left leg amputated above the knee, his doctors said, adding that they may have to remove the other leg if gangrene develops. He was also blinded in his right eye.
He could not talk to reporters as he was under sedation. He does not know that he lost a leg.
“The government should remove all mines and shells left by the “Israelis”. If the explosion took place in another country, the prime minister or the concerned minister would have resigned,” said Abdel-Nabi’s father, Hassan.
Six people have died and more than 20 have been wounded in similar explosions since the May 24 “Israeli” withdrawal.


Cabinet sets up committee to sue “Israel”…(3)

“The Cabinet formed a committee on Wednesday to pursue the government’s plans to sue “Israel” in the International Court of Justice to recoup damages caused by its attacks and 22-year occupation.
“Prime Minister Salim Hoss will be the committee’s president, Justice Minister Joseph Shaoul will be the vice-president, and several judges, diplomats, lawyers and academics will be members,” Information Minister Anwar Khalil said.
Lebanon estimates that $7 billion in damage was inflicted on Lebanon since “Israel” launched its first attacks against Lebanon in 1969, then destroying a dozen empty civilian airplanes at Beirut Airport.
Beirut is preparing a lawsuit at the International Court of Justice in The Hague.
During its weekly meeting, Cabinet decided to spend $20 million of a Kuwaiti aid package to build homes in the South.


Court puts off collaboration trials…(4)

The trial of 160 alleged “Israeli” collaborators opened on Wednesday, but 147 cases were postponed until next month at the request of the defense lawyers, who said they needed more time to look into the charges.
Brigadier Maher Safieddine’s court is due to hand down rulings in the 13 remaining cases on Thursday morning. The defendants were all charged with providing security information to “Israeli” agents.
The defendants included two Iraqis, Qais Mahmoud and Akram Khalaf, and Indian national Zaid Khan. They are accused of spying on inmates during their stints in Roumieh Prison.
They allegedly sent reports to “Israeli” agents via intermediaries.
Since June 5, the court has held 24 sessions to try alleged collaborators and former members of “Israel’s” militia. It has ruled in 1,005 cases, while more than 1,500 defendants are awaiting trial.


Three former pro-”Israeli” militiamen flee to “Israel”…(5)

“ Three former members of the pro-”Israeli” militia fled to “Israel” Wednesday, a Lebanese police source said.

The three men, aged from 20 to 25 from the village of Hasbaya in southern Lebanon, crossed the barbed wire fencing on the border in the eastern Abbassiyeh area, he added.

According to locals in the area, their decision was taken after hearing reports that “Israel” had started paying compensation of up to 15,000 dollars to the collaborators.

Three other cases of Lebanese fleeing to “Israel” have been reported since “Israeli” troops' May 24 withdrawal from southern Lebanon, which ended 22 years of occupation.

Some 6,500 Lebanese, mostly former militiamen and their families, followed the “Israeli” army as it withdrew from Lebanon, fearing reprisals from anti-”Israeli” guerrillas or ill treatment from Lebanese authorities.

Meanwhile, mass trials of 2,200 Lebanese accused of collaboration with the enemy have been going on at a military court in Beirut. More than 1,000 have already been sentenced.
