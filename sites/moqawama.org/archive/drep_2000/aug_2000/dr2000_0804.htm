Introduction

Supreme Leader of the Islamic Revolution Ayatollah Ali Khamenei warned Thursday that Israel remains a threat to the Palestinians despite its withdrawal from South Lebanon. Khamenei, speaking during a meeting with visiting  Lebanese parliament speaker Nabih Berri, stressed the need to maintain the spirit of resistance and unity to stand up to the Jewish state.

However, the deployment of UN peacekeeping forces throughout southern Lebanon moved one step closer Thursday, as experts completed their verification of Israeli encroachments into Lebanese territory.


Leader: Zionists' defeat in South Lebanon "unforgettable"(1)

 Supreme Leader of the Islamic Revolution Ayatollah Ali Khamenei told Speaker of the Lebanese National Assembly Nabih Berri here Thursday that the Zionist regime's defeat in southern Lebanon is an unforgettable event.

The Supreme Leader said the great victory is the climax of Islamic-Arab honor against Zionists.

The leader said the big victory was achieved due to three factors, namely spirit of resistance and struggle, solidarity, and perseverance against insinuations of the enemy who tried to portray resistance ineffective.

Despite the victory, great struggles are ahead and victory in the struggle needs safeguarding these three factors, stressed the Supreme Leader.

Ayatollah Khamenei thanked the Lebanese government and parliament for supporting the Islamic resistance and cautioned them against continued greed of Israel in southern Lebanon.

"One should by keeping the spirit of resistance in Lebanon alive and by supporting it and maintaining unity among the resistance forces be alert and prepared against the danger," said the Leader.

The leader stressed that one of the reasons for failure of the Camp David II talks was the repercussions of the victory in southern Lebanon.

With Palestine being still occupied, the danger of Israel will be there and all have the duty to strive for liberation of Palestine, said the Supreme Leader.

The leader said resistance is the sole way of liberating Palestine. One of the serious demands which one should emphasize on is the return of Palestinian refugees to their own land, added the leader.

Berri for his part congratulated the Supreme Leader on the honorable victory of the Islamic Resistance in southern Lebanon and touched on the impact of the spiritual support of Iran for Lebanon on realization of the victory. He called on Iran to continue its support
for Lebanon and help the country reconstruct its southern territory.

In a meeting with Berri on Wednesday, Head of Iran's Expediency Council Ayatollah Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani expressed pleasure over the victory of the Lebanese Resistance Movement over the usurper Zionists in Southern Lebanon while saying that this great
triumph was the Almighty God's promise to Muslims.

Berri also conferred with Iran's Majlis Speaker Hojjatoleslam Mehdi Karrubi.


All over except tiny technicality
UNIFIL to deploy in numbers soon as Hoteit says: Were finished on the ground(2)

After 56 days of frustration, tedium and footslogging, General Amin Hoteit, the head of the Lebanese border team, announced that the process of verifying Israels withdrawal was finally over on Thursday.
But the green light for the long-awaited deployment of UNIFIL troops into the former occupation zone would have to wait a little longer. Hoteit said that some final technical measurements were necessary at the site of an Israeli outpost beside Boundary Pillar 18, two kilometers south of Yaroun.
Were finished on the ground but there are still some technical problems, small calculations, Hoteit told reporters following a meeting in Naqoura with senior UNIFIL staff. The technical problem was not expected to lead to a lengthy delay before the deployment of the peacekeeping troops.
UNIFILs deputy commander, Major General Jim Sreenan, flew by helicopter in the afternoon to the Israeli outpost near Yaroun for a final inspection. Sreenan, who heads the UN verification team, returned in the evening to Naqoura to brief UNIFIL commander Major General Seth Kofi Obeng on his findings.
Obeng was due to relay the information to Brigadier General Maher Toufeili, the governments coordinator with UNIFIL, who, in turn, would brief President Emile Lahoud.
Lahoud is the final authority to sanction the deployment of UNIFIL into the liberated areas.
According to a UNIFIL officer, readings taken with a global positioning system ­ used by the parties to measure the delineation of the so-called Blue Line of withdrawal ­ indicate that there is no Israeli infraction of the line at BP18.
The Israeli post had been suspected of slightly overlapping Lebanese territory.
The Lebanese team conducted measurements beside the Israeli position on Wednesday after UNIFIL engineers cleared a path through a minefield along the border.
Although there has been no official comment on Lebanons opinion of the possible violation, Hoteits reference to technical problem suggested that he was not entirely happy with what he found.
UNIFILs alleged violation of the Blue Line has been cleared up, according to a UNIFIL officer. On Tuesday the Israelis accused the Irish platoon, posted beside BP18, of planting a UN flag inside Israeli territory. The errant flag was reportedly around 1 meter across the line.
Two days after the Israelis leveled the accusation, the UNIFIL officer said on Thursday that the dispute had been rectified. We moved the flag back one meter, he said.


Occupation resumes threatening Lebanon(3)

The Israeli enemy's army announced in an aggressive statement addressed to Lebanon that it is preparing for what it termed as "Hezbollah and Palestinian organizations potential conflagration of the situation in southern Lebanon" in case there were confrontations in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip.

A senior Israeli military source told the enemy radio that "Israel" would retaliate with a heavy hand if there is such a flare-up and would "continue to attack those responsible for what is going in South Lebanon".


Palestinians want emulating Hizbullahs model(4)

Two-thirds of Palestinians believe they should emulate Hizbullah and launch attacks against Israeli targets, according to a poll published on Thursday.
Fifty-two percent of the 1,259 respondents in the West Bank and Gaza said they supported armed attacks against Israeli targets, the highest level since 1994, according to a poll by the Palestinian Center for Policy and Survey Research.
The figure stood at 44 percent in March.
Fifty-six percent of Palestinians supported establishing a state regardless of an accord. And 60 percent said they would support or strongly support confrontations with Israelis if they broke away in the absence of an agreement, with 57 percent saying they thought such clashes would support Palestinian rights in a way the negotiations could not.
