‘Cowboys try to provoke Hizbullah’
“Israeli” troops play violation games at Abbad Hill…(1)

“UNIFIL confirmed on Wednesday that “Israel” has committed a new violation of the UN-delineated Blue Line by constructing a defensive barrier on Lebanese soil at the top of Sheikh Abbad hill near Houla.
It is the first confirmed “Israeli” violation of the Blue Line since the 1,000-strong Lebanese security taskforce deployed in the former occupation zone nine days ago. Security sources believe that the reason for the continued violations, lies in a combination of muscle-flexing and provocation undertaken by local military commanders.
UNIFIL spokesman Timur Goksel said that a team from the UN’s Observer Group Lebanon had inspected the site and confirmed that “Israeli” soldiers had erected a barbed wire barricade on the Lebanese side of an ancient tomb on the summit of the hill.
“Basically it’s a violation,” Goksel told The Daily Star.
He added that “a small presence” of UNIFIL troops would deploy on Abbad hill “until the “Israelis” pull back.”
Asked why he thought the “Israeli” Army was still transgressing the Blue Line, the veteran UNIFIL spokesman said “I’ve given up trying to analyze all these violations.”
A unit of Ghanaian peacekeepers and two armored personnel carriers were stationed on the hilltop Wednesday morning. The summit of the hill is marked by the site of an ancient tomb which rests on a small concrete platform surrounded by a raised ledge.
The Blue Line runs through the middle of the platform, bisecting the tomb. On Monday, the “Israelis” bulldozed earth to form an embankment and strung barbed wire from the edge of the platform on the Lebanese side. Since the new barricade was erected, soldiers have been stepping past the tomb onto the Lebanese end of the platform to gain a better view of the approach to the hilltop.
Wednesday morning, several soldiers were leaning over the Lebanese edge of the platform as a group of civilians hurled insults at them in Arabic and English.
The abuse was given an added dimension by the broad New York accent in which the colorful English insults were delivered.
Sharin Saraya and Tanya Shamma, both Americans, said they had brought a Palestinian family from Shatila camp in Beirut to the border to show them their homeland.
Asked why she was yelling insults at the “Israelis”, Saraya, who has worked with Palestinian refugees in Jerusalem and Gaza, said that the soldiers were “pigs.”
“They have been standing over there making taunts and its very aggravating,” she said.
As the two women continued their tirade, oblivious of the smirks of local reporters, the “Israeli” soldiers laughed and responded in a similar manner.
Four Hizbullah members stood in the background, one of them scanning the “Israelis” through a pair of binoculars.
A young Ghanaian lieutenant in charge of the UNIFIL squad watched the two groups trading insults with apprehension. “So far it’s been calm here,” he said.
Sheikh Abbad hill was one of “Israel’s” most important outposts during the occupation. The “Israelis” were forced to abandon the strategic post, however, when the UN determined that the Blue Line ran through the center of the compound. A new fortification is in the process of being constructed on the “Israeli” side of the hill. But the new post is situated just below the brow of the hill and lacks the sweeping views into Lebanon enjoyed by the former position.
Despite the coils of barbed wire stretching either side of the platform, the tomb can still be reached from the Lebanese side by scrambling up the earth embankment. As a handful of reporters approached the tomb, the “Israelis” soldiers simply moved back to their side of the platform. Hopes that “Israel’s” violations would end with the deployment of UNIFIL troops to the border and the Lebanese taskforce elsewhere in the south appear premature.
A US-based security source, familiar with the “Israeli” Army, suggested that the violations were a localized form of muscle-flexing.
“There has always been a cowboy element or two on the “Israeli” side,” the source said. “The (“Israeli”) northern commanders’ game, I bet, is to provoke Hizbullah, knowing the international consensus will support “Israel”. They are convinced, I am sure, that it is only a matter of time before the Hizbullah campaign turns southward.”
Supporting this view, “Israel’s” chief of staff, Lieutenant General Shaul Mofaz, has predicted Hizbullah will launch attacks against “Israeli” targets following the parliamentary elections.
“We know that after the elections Hizbullah is planning both to attack “Israeli” targets abroad and perhaps to send terrorists into “Israel”,” he told Channel 1 television Tuesday night.


“Israel” gave wrong maps on Lebanese mines: UN official…(2)

“ “Israel” gave the United Nations incorrect maps on the location of mines set up during its 22-year occupation of southern Lebanon, a peacekeeping official said Wednesday.

"The maps supplied by “Israel” are not correct and we are counting on our own demining experience to pursue our work," said Ouli Kovriniok, a colonel in the Ukranian specialist unit here.

Kovriniok told reporters that 83 mines have been removed from the Lebanese border town of Manara, but declined to say how many mines the UN force has unearthed since it started its work.

The Ukranian peacekeeping unit, which specializes in demining and logistical support, began its work on the “Israeli”-Lebanese border in late June. Last month it was reinforced by 500 Ukranian soldiers with special equipment.

The United Nations estimates that 130,000 mines are scattered over some 900 square kilometers (360 square miles) in the zone “Israel” occupied from 1978 until May.

Mines have killed six civilians and injured another 50 since the “Israeli” withdrawal, according to the Lebanese branch of the Red Cross.


Relatives suspend protest over Lebanese held by occupation…(3)

A 10-day hunger strike protesting “Israel’s” detention of Lebanese ended Wednesday with protesters claiming success.
Mohammed Safa, secretary-general of the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in “Israeli” Prisons, said the strike was called off after France’s Green Party asked the 19 mothers of Lebanese prisoners to stop their strike in front of United Nations House in Riad Solh Square.
Members of the French party also took part in the strike and gave their support from abroad.
“We never expected the strike to lead to the detainees’ release,” he said. “But it was necessary to encourage the UN, the international community and Lebanon’s government to act quickly to save our hostages.”
Despite Safa’s upbeat evaluation, there was concern about lack of support.
Up to 150 people picketed with the hunger strikers, fewer than the number of Lebanese mothers participating in a Paris demonstration for detainees last month, according to Green Party Middle East representative Ahmed Bouzeid.
Safa claimed international success, citing statements by UN special Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen in Oslo on Aug. 11. Larsen said the UN was inclined to “address the issue of the detainees in a more effective way.”
“This is why we can say that the strike has achieved its goals. What helped this victory, in addition to the international support campaign, was the resistance of detainees’ families in ‘The Freedom Tent’ for 10 days of hunger and thirst.”
Safa said the protest reactivated local and international support for the detainees and boosted the families’ morales.
“Our strike succeeded in encouraging the detainees,” Safa reported.
But he said the state missed an “historic opportunity” when it agreed to deploy UN and army troops before prisoners were freed.
“Lebanese authorities should have engaged in a tough diplomatic battle that would have made the release of detainees a precondition for UNIFIL’s deployment,” he said.


Lebanese community in Germany rejects collaborators…(4)

Sources of the Lebanese community in Germany rejected the German authorities' granting political asylum to hundreds of the “Israeli” occupation's collaborators who had fled to the occupation's entity along with the “Israeli” troops on May 24.

The 400 collaborators had left occupied Palestine for Germany where they stayed in a camp in Dresden.

The Lebanese immigrants in Germany affirmed that Lebanese and Arab communities have refused to deal with the occupation's collaborators.

The collaborators tried to seek assistance as they do not speak German, but to no avail as all the Arab residents refused to have any ties with them because they regard them as traitors who had served the occupation and conspired against their country and people.
