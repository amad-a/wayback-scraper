Hizbullah remembers remaining detainees, asserts cooperation with all…(1)

”Hizbullah Secretary-General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah called on all the Lebanese to take interest in the freed detainees besides remembering those who are still in captivity.

He was speaking at the Victory and Liberation Festival which was held on Saturday in the Islamic Resistance Martyrs' Stadium in the liberated town of al-Khiam.

Tens of thousand of Hizbullah supporters attended the rally to honor the former detainees in the al-Khiam jail. Among the crowd were 234 former detainees, besides a number of ministers, MPs and political figures.

Nineteen chairs were left empty near the podium for the remaining detainees held in “Israel”.

Sayyed Nasrallah, who earlier toured the notorious prison of al-Khiam along with Sheikh Nabil Qaouq, Hizbullah's southern official, said the center was one of the pillars of steadfastness and resistance in the country.

"The Khiam detention center should become a national historic landmark and this is the government's responsibility," the secretary-general said.

Referring to the 19 detainees who are still held by the “Israeli” occupation, including Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, Mustafa Dirani, Samir Qantar and Anwar Yassin, Sayyed Nasrallah said the party is willing to do "anything and everything" to recover them.

"Let the (“Israeli”) enemy and the whole world hear us: We will do anything and everything to fill these chairs... no matter what the price or sacrifice," he warned.

"“Israel” and the entire world should know that the liberation of our land from the “Israeli” occupier will only be complete after we have secured the return of our brothers, who include Sheikh Obeid, Mustafa Dirani (both leading Hizbullah figures), Samir Qantar and Anwar Yassin (from left-wing parties)," Nasrallah said.

"We, the Lebanese, don't leave our prisoners in prisons and detention centers."

Sayyed Nasrallah urged the government to compensate former detainees for the years they spent in al-Khiam and to find jobs for them. He also suggested that they be integrated into the Lebanese army.

"The least the government should do is consider them (former detainees) soldiers in the Lebanese Army. The government can count the years of detention, rank them accordingly and give them salaries until they are employed," he said.

As he unveiled the names of some martyrdom resistance fighters who had carried operations against the “Israeli” occupation forces, the Hizbullah leader stressed that the Resistance knows the enemy's weaknesses.

The illusion of a big lie called “Israel” has fallen apart, along with its terror and legend. The truth is now clear and is expressed day and night by our children, men, women and elderly at the Fatima Gate, he said.

Sayyed Nasrallah called on the Palestinians to rise up and learn from Hizbullah's experience.

"The Palestinian people should benefit from this historic and golden opportunity to recover their land with blood and not through negotiations," he said.

His Eminence also commented on statements by US secretary of state Madeleine Albright indicating that Yasser Arafat had authorization from Arab countries to negotiate over al-Quds (Jerusalem).

"This is a frightful statement. We tell Albright from here, from al-Khiam and at the threshold of Palestine, that no one in this world holds authorization over al-Quds or Palestine, whether from the Arab nation or from Muslims or Christians."

Regarding the general elections in Lebanon, the secretary-general called on all the Lebanese to cooperate.

"We don't want a political life based on fights, competition, or provoking tribe spirits. We say development and removing dispossession requires cooperation from all."

"The Hizbullah-Amal alliance was not imposed by anyone, neither local nor regional forces. This alliance was made because of our feeling of responsibility toward the victory that we have offered thousands of martyrs to achieve. This is the beginning of a new stage of cooperation," Sayyed Nasrallah said.


Plan to export suspected “Israeli”-proxy militia war criminals foiled…(2)

“The Canadian government has drawn up a secret list of former “Israeli” allied militiamen suspected of war crimes.
The list, containing the names of 35 former militiamen, has been quietly circulated among a handful of countries, mainly European, which “Israel” has asked to accept militia refugees and their families.
The secret document, a copy of which was obtained by The Daily Star, lists the names of the staff at Khiam prison, including the heads of the jail, those in charge of interrogations and the guards.
The list was compiled by Canada’s Royal Mounted Police and drew upon Lebanese “community sources.”
“These persons are alleged to have been operating in Khiam prison in southern Lebanon, responsible for investigating and interrogating Hizbullah activities in that region,” the document said. “The prison is associated with extensive and serious human rights abuses, including torture, executions, and arbitrary detentions.”
The list has reinforced the reluctance of some European governments to take in large numbers of former militiamen due to their activities in the militia and because they may be the target of revenge attacks.
“It’s fair to say that generally most countries that have been approached by “Israel” are very reluctant to accept the collaborators,” one European diplomat said.
The bulk of the names are from the villages of Qlayaa, Marjeyoun and Kfar Kila. One former guard, originally from Qlayaa, is already living in Sweden, according to the document.
Some 6,000 militiamen and their families fled to “Israel” when the occupation collapsed, fearing revenge at the hands of advancing resistance fighters.
The militiamen have been temporarily housed in 40 different locations in Galilee. The “Israelis” pay a basic stipend to each family as well as paying for their accommodation.
Around 3,000 of the refugees are expected to resettle abroad.
Denmark, Belgium, France, the Netherlands, Germany, Canada, Sweden, Switzerland, Australia, New Zealand, the United Kingdom and the United States have all been asked by “Israel” to accept groups of refugees. Each group consists of between 20 to 50 former militiamen and their families.
So far, only Germany has agreed to take in the former militiamen. Forty collaborators and their families left “Israel” for Germany at the end of July.
Guido Hildner, charge d’affaires at the German Embassy, said the decision to accept the refugees was based solely on “humanitarian considerations.”
“Of course, if there are any allegations of them having engaged in war crimes or human rights abuses, they will be prosecuted. German courts are entitled to try war criminals and they will do so,” Hildner said.
He added that the refugees’ stay in Germany had been limited to one year, after which their status will be reassessed.
The UN High Commission on Refugees has ruled that there is no case for the immediate relocation of the collaborators from “Israel” as the “Israeli” government has granted them one-year residence visas and promised to view with favor any subsequent requests for citizenship.
Holland and Switzerland are the only countries to have openly refused the collaborators.
“There is a feeling that they don’t meet the criteria (as refugees) because they are already in a country that can give them residency,” a Western diplomat said.
The Dutch government said that “Israel” is the most appropriate home for the militiamen for the time being.
“The collaborators have nothing to fear in “Israel”. According to international statutes, there is no reason to expect other countries to take them in,” said Michel Rentenaar, the Dutch charge d’affaires.
A statement from the Dutch Foreign Ministry said that ““Israel” is responsible for the former militiamen, who in fact have worked for “Israel” for many years.”
Some of the former militiamen have undertaken their own visa applications to other countries, especially those with relatives living abroad.
For the rest, “Israel” reportedly is offering groups of the collaborators to various countries without naming the individuals, creating concerns that suspected war criminals are in the mix.
“Everyone points out the danger of war criminals slipping through, that’s why it’s utterly important to investigate every militiaman who wants to come into your country,” a European diplomat said.
“There is an issue of public safety here: who will safeguard them from vendettas,” he added.
Many of the countries earmarked by “Israel” to take in the agents have sizable lebanese communities ­ Germany has accepted 60,000 Lebanese from the South over the past two decades, most of whom are unlikely to view the collaborators arrives with much sympathy.
“If a nephew wants to avenge his uncle’s death and he has a few thousand dollars then it wouldn’t be hard to achieve. Although it’s hard to assess the risk, it’s there nonetheless. No country wants to have to deal with that,” the European diplomat said.
Several diplomats raised the possibility that Lebanon could block “Israel’s” efforts to transfer the agents to other countries by demanding the return of the militiamen to stand trial.
The government could issue international arrest warrants through Interpol, the international police authority, for individuals wanted by the authorities in Lebanon, they said.
Although it is unlikely that the wanted militiamen would find themselves in a situation whereby they could be extradited to Lebanon, the existence of the arrest warrants would frustrate “Israel’s” attempts to resettle the refugees.
But Prime Minister Salim Hoss said that the government had no plans to issue arrest warrants for the agents.
“There’s been no effort in this direction,” he said, adding that any effort to seek the refugees’ return “depends on the future.”
One European diplomat said that “Israel” would have to arrange deals with countries with relatively open immigration policies ­ Canada, Australia, New Zealand and some South American countries ­ but not most European nations.
“If they arrive in somewhere like Bolivia or Paraguay, nobody will ask many questions especially if they are carrying a suitcase full of dollars,” a Western diplomat said.


Occupation reorganizes fleeing collaborators for "future use"…(3)

Three months after the retreat of the “Israeli” occupation forces from south Lebanon and the fleeing of a number of the Lahdi militiamen into occupied Palestine where they have become a heavy burden on the “Israeli” society, the “Israeli” occupation army and the concerned bodies began a process to reorganize the human presence of the Lahdi collaborators in “Israel” besides preparing them to be on demand for any sabotage acts in Lebanon.

Security sources unveiled that a special committee affiliated with the occupation's secret service has classified the fleeing collaborators into several categories including security (those who had worked with the occupation's secret service), press, social and other groups.

Those who had been classified as security agents were moved along with their families to houses close to the border with Lebanon, in specific prefabricated houses near the Metulla colony (one kilometer from the border), the sources added, noting that the occupation army has cordoned off the collaborators’ houses barring anyone from approaching unless they have special permits from the occupation command.


New UN envoy tours southern Lebanon for first time…(4)

“ New UN envoy Rolf Knutsson toured southern Lebanon on Monday for the first time since he arrived in the country to oversee implementation of UN resolutions on “Israel’s” pullout from the area.

"My mandate is to make sure that UN Resolutions 425 and 426 are respected," Knutsson told reporters at Abbad, one of the two border points where his convoy stopped for a field verification.

Knutsson flew by helicopter earlier Monday from Beirut to the headquarters of the United Nations Interim Forces in south Lebanon (UNIFIL) at the coastal town of Naqoura, near the border with “Israel”.

He held a meeting with the UNIFIL commander General Seth Kofi Obeng at Naqoura before visiting Abbad and Marwaheen, also a border point.

On August 16, Knutsson arrived in Beirut where he set up an office to coordinate day-to-day issues with the Lebanese government.

The Swedish diplomat had been appointed by UN Secretary General Kofi Annan as a "diplomatic and political liaison with the Lebanese government on the implementation of Security Council Resolutions 425 and 426," a recent UN statement said.

The two UN resolutions, passed after “Israel’s” invasion of Lebanon in 1978, called on a UN force to deploy after “Israel’s” withdrawal to help restore Beirut's authority over the zone.

“Israel” withdrew on May 24 after 22 years of occupation and UNIFIL began deployment in the area earlier this month.

Knutsson will work under UN envoy Terje Roed-Larsen, who has shuttled around the Middle East since “Israel’s” withdrawal.

Knutsson, who serves as director of political affairs in Annan's office, will serve as a special envoy for south Lebanon for an initial period of six months during which he will also supervise reconstruction efforts in south Lebanon.
