Lebanon's Hezbollah chants "Death to “Israel”" at border(1)

Members of the militant Shiite Muslim movement Hezbollah ended Friday prayers at a shrine here by chanting for “Israel's” destruction, just inches away from “Israeli” soldiers on the other side of the border.

The half-dozen “Israeli” troops stood motionless in front of sandbags marking the border as the Islamic militants prayed and then shouted slogans praising Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, the founder of Iran's Islamic republic, and Sheikh Hassan Nasrallah, the leader of Hezbollah.

"Let's all march on Jerusalem! War until victory! Death to “Israel”!" the Hezbollah members chanted, under the eyes of Ghanaian UN peacekeepers who have been posted near the spot since Wednesday.

The shrine, one-fifth of which is on “Israeli” territory, is believed to be the burial spot of Sheikh Al-Aabbad, who died 500 years ago.

 

“Israel” will be made to pay, in court(2)

When the Lebanese Resistance was first launched in the aftermath of the 1982 invasion, its declared target of ousting “Israel” from the South was greeted with skepticism, even ridicule. Eighteen years later, Lebanon is celebrating victory over the formidable enemy.
Today, an enthusiastic lawyer is launching what he calls “Legal Resistance,” convinced that Lebanon could ultimately bring “Israel” to pay billions of dollars in war reparations.
“We’re seeking equity and justice. This is a national cause,” Michel Tueni said in a recent interview at his office in Achrafieh. “We need the Lebanese to support and believe in this legal resistance.”
He explained how at first, the Lebanese were skeptical of armed resistance. But when the fighters began dealing painful blows to the Israelis, the government offered tacit support.
“Under President Emile Lahoud, there was open support for the resistance, and what happened? The “Israelis” were ousted,” he said confidently.
He expects his campaign to evolve in the minds of citizens in the same way.
Tueni cited Iraq as an example of how the international community could pressure an aggressor to pay war reparations to the victim. Some 30 percent of Iraq’s restricted oil sales are channeled to a fund in Geneva which is entrusted with paying compensation to victims of Saddam Hussein’s 1990 invasion of Kuwait.
But why has “Israel” escaped such international pressure? The answer for Tueni is simple. “No one has ever petitioned them before.”
He says it was the “Jews who first introduced the principle of compensation,” when after World War II, Germany was made to pay for the atrocities committed by the Nazi regime.
Tueni was encouraged by a series of rulings by the US District Court in Washington in anti-terrorism cases. The verdicts demanded that Middle East states pay compensations of about $250 million for each kidnap or murder victim.
“If the US says a human being is worth some $250 million, it’s our right to immediately demand almost $30 billion for Qana victims alone,” Tueni said, referring to the 1996 attack on the Fijian UNIFIL headquarters in the village, in which 107 civilians perished.
“No one can claim Lebanon was not attacked by “Israel” and was not devastated over more than 20 years of systematic aggression. Now “Israel” has withdrawn. Are we supposed to say, let bygones be bygones?
The extent of devastation that became apparent after the “Israeli” withdrawal in May, coupled with the economic recession ­ also partly due to 22 years of occupation ­ persuaded Tueni that he had to accelerate his drive to put together a case against “Israel”.
But before approaching the government with an abstract idea, Tueni enlisted the help of international legal experts to determine the viability of such a complaint, and the international forum best suited to handle the case. After consultations with British experts, including the renowned Ian Brownley, it became apparent that there was solid claim for compensation.
“The choice was either the UN Security Council or the International Court of Justice. But we all know what happens at the Security Council,” he said, referring to the possibility of the US vetoing any decision in Lebanon’s favor.
“Besides, the ICJ is the best arbiter in inter-state conflicts.”
Tueni sent his findings to various government bodies, and found that former Justice Minister Bahij Tabbara had launched a similar campaign in 1996.
He received the support and encouragement of Prime Minister Salim Hoss, who urged him to prepare the necessary files.
At this point, any suggestions concerning an appropriate figure for compensation would be guesswork, which Tueni categorically refused to discuss.
“The legal documents are almost ready,” Tueni said.
But hard work of completing the “economic file” lies ahead, which will include case-by-case evidence to support claims.
Completing the economic file is a two-fold process, involving an assessment of direct and indirect losses, Tueni said.
The former would include human and material losses that are being assessed from the testimonies of citizens, media reports and security agency files. The latter would include the impact of “Israel’s” aggression on Lebanon’s economy. “This,” he said, “must be evaluated by international experts.”
“Lebanon was put in the freezer for more than 20 years because of “Israeli” attacks. Someone has to pay for its revival,” said Tueni.
“The technical tools for this case are there. But there has to be a political decision.”

 

Dissention grows within “Israeli” military over border ‘inaction’ (3)

In a further sign of dissension among members of the “Israeli” Army posted on the border with Lebanon, senior officers on Friday criticized the “Israeli” government for not adopting a tougher response to stone-throwing across the frontier.
“The army hopes for a little bit more energetic action, but the government holds back,” one officer told Agence France Presse, speaking on condition of anonymity. “There should be a stronger reaction.”
Other “Israeli” officers in the area have expressed similar sentiments and have ordered their troops to open fire if they feel they are in danger.
Despite the relative calm along the border since “Israel’s” withdrawal from the South in May, there has been no let-up in stone-throwing from the Lebanese side, particularly at the Fatima gate in Kfar Kila.
“Israeli” soldiers have retaliated several times by opening fire, wounding civilians with plastic bullets and once with live ammunition. More generally, the soldiers respond by hurling stones back across the border and yelling insults.
On Thursday, The Daily Star watched a Lebanese civilian throw a well-aimed stone at an observation booth at the entrance to the Fatima gate. The stone just missed the booth’s narrow window, causing the soldier inside to flinch.
Annoyed, the soldier stepped out and wagged his finger in admonishment. He then raised the middle finger of both hands at the crowd on the Lebanese side of the fence before ducking back into his bunker. The incident was typical of the tit-for-tat provocations found each day along the border.
On Friday, a group of Hibullah members prayed at the top of the hill and then chanted pro-party slogans and cursed the “Israeli” soldiers stationed just a few meters opposite.
Although “Israel” has withdrawn back to its side of the UN-delineated Blue Line at the summit of the hill, and thereby rectifying a violation, Abbad remains a potentially serious flashpoint.
The ease of access by civilians to the hilltop, the lack of a security fence across the summit and the presence of an “Israeli” Army post just below the brow of the hill make Abbad potentially a more dangerous hotspot than the Fatima gate.
“Israeli” frustrations at having to turn the other cheek are believed to be behind many of the repeated violations of the Blue Line. One still remains: a former patrol track that creeps onto Lebanese soil near Ramieh is being used again.
According to a UNIFIL officer, the Israelis have told the peacekeepers that they need to access the road to build their new security fence on the Blue Line. There was little fence-building under way on Thursday when The Daily Star drove past, although two “Israeli” military jeeps were parked on the patrol track.
The concerns of local “Israeli” commanders are compounded by the belief in “Israel” that Hizbullah will shortly resume guerrilla operations.
“Hizbullah is likely to avoid escalation until after the elections during the first week of September, wrote Gerald Steinberg, a professor at “Israel’s” Bar Ilan University in Friday’s Jerusalem Post.
These fears were exacerbated last week by the ease with which two Lebanese were able to jump the border fence and enter “Israel”. Two former members of the “Israeli”-proxy militia even managed to borrow a jeep from an “Israeli” outpost and drive 45 kilometers south of the frontier before being apprehended.
As a result, the “Israeli” Army dismissed an officer.
“An investigation into the incident led the army to dismiss a captain of the parachute unit in the area because he did not keep his soldiers on the required state of alert,” a military spokesperson said. ­

 

“Israeli” army dismisses officer after two Lebanese infiltrate border(4)

The “Israeli” army said it dismissed an officer Friday after an investigation into how two militiamen of “Israel's” former ally, sneaked across the border.

"An investigation into the incident led the army to dismiss a captain of the (elite) parachute unit in the area because he did not keep his soldiers on the required state of alert," a military spokesperson said.

The two leaped over the fence from Lebanon into “Israel” Wednesday.

"The two men were not armed, they jumped over the border fence with Lebanon near Kfar Sold, the army chased them and arrested them," another army official said.

The two men are being detained.

Kfar Sold is some 10 kilometers (seven miles) east of Metulla.

This was the first incident of its kind since “Israel” withdrew its troops in May from southern Lebanon, ending a 22-year presence there.

Several thousand “Israeli”-allied militiamen and their families sought asylum in “Israel” after the “Israeli” withdrawal from their country.

 

Lebanese refugees return home from “Israel” (5)

Eighteen Lebanese who had fled to “Israel” following that country's withdrawal from south Lebanon in May returned home Friday, the police said.

The group included three ex-members of the now-defunct pro-”Israeli” militia.

All formerly residents of Christian and Druze villages in south Lebanon, the 18 crossed over into this town on the Mediterranean coast. They were received by representatives of the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) and turned over to Lebanese police.

Eight men, including the former militiamen, were held for questioning. The others, women and children, were allowed to return to their villages.

Some 6,500 Lebanese, mostly former militiamen and their families, followed the “Israeli” army as it withdrew from Lebanon in May. According to police figures, about 530 of those who fled to “Israel” have returned to Lebanon.

More than 2,200 Lebanese are in the process of being tried before the military court in Beirut, or have already been convicted, for collaboration with “Israel” or simply having "contact with the “Israeli” enemy."
