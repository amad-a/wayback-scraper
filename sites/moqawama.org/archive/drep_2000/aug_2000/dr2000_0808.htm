Introduction

The Israeli enemy soldiers shot and wounded four Lebanese civilians, two of them journalists, at the Fatima Gate along the international border.

In the meantime, Interiour Minister Michel Murr said the 1000-strong joint force of army and Internal Security Forces will deploy in liberated areas to maintain internal security as soon as the UN will officially inform us that it has completed its deployment.

However, more than hundred relatives of Lebanese detainees held by Israel went on hunger strike Monday in front of the UN headquarters pledging to fast until the Jewish state releases hostages it took during its 22-year occupation of South Lebanon.


Shootings raise border tensions
Four civilians, including two journalists, injured at Fatima Gate(1)

Tensions remained high at the Fatima Gate on Monday, a day after four civilians, including two journalists, were shot and wounded by Israeli soldiers.
Several dozen civilians had gathered at the flashpoint crossing, singing patriotic chants in praise of Hizbullah.
On the other side of the border, Israeli troops shouted insults in Arabic, in particular targeting Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah. An Israeli soldier occasionally unbuckled his pants to moon the Lebanese crowd.
But no stones were thrown as the crowd stayed a dozen meters from the border post.
The tense atmosphere was sparked by the wounding of four civilians in two separate incidents on Sunday.
Katia Jarjoura, 25, a freelance journalist for Radio Canada from Montreal, was hit in the abdomen by shrapnel from a bullet and Ali Dia, 50, a correspondent with Agence France-Presse, received wounds to both legs.
Tareq Hussein, 13, was also injured in his left foot and later had two toes amputated.
We were hurling stones at the Israelis when a soldier emerged from the outpost and opened fire, hitting me in my toes, Hussein said from his hospital bed in Nabatieh, where he remained on Monday.
Dia, who had the shrapnel removed from his legs and was discharged before dawn, said youths were throwing stones at the Israeli outpost when he and Jarjoura arrived at the gate.
Two Israeli soldiers emerged from the outpost and, when they saw that the youths were preparing to throw a Molotov cocktail, they fired shots, Dia said. Video footage of the incident showed a man, who was not hurt, throwing the bomb after the soldiers fired.
Jarjoura and Dia said they arrived shortly after midnight, waiting for the possible deployment of Lebanese security forces after UNIFIL peacekeepers fanned out in the area on Saturday.
Jarjoura, who came to Lebanon two months ago, said that doctors had removed three pieces of shrapnel from her abdomen. She hoped to be released on Thursday.
Also, Ali Hayek was wounded earlier on Sunday at Fatima Gate when Israeli soldiers trying to disperse a crowd shot at the ground near the teenager.
Meanwhile, there was still no final confirmation on the deployment of the 1,000-strong joint unit of military police, army intelligence and Internal Security Force personnel. A senior military source said the force was still preparing some logistics.
We need some more time, he said, without elaborating.
Elsewhere, Brigadier General Amin Hoteit presented President Emile Lahoud with a map of the final border.
Lahoud said the professional military work of the Lebanese inspection team will go down in history and be an object of pride for the next generation.


Lebanon: joint security force to be deployed when UNIFIL finishes deployment(2)

Interior Minister Michel Murr said on Sunday the joint force of army and Internal Security Forces would "consist of 500 soldiers and 500 policemen who will deploy in liberated areas to maintain internal security based on a Cabinet decree as soon as the UN will officially inform us that it has completed its deployment."

He was speaking after a meeting with senior army and ISF officers to discuss preparations.

Murr added that "nothing is delaying the deployment of the joint force." He added that all officers would continue to discuss the preparations in a joint meeting "to distribute mission and roles in preparation to discover the region and decide where to locate the headquarters and its points."

According to the Cabinet decree, the ISF's Brigadier Nazih Abi Nader has been appointed commander of the joint force with two senior officers drawn from the army and the ISF as his aides. An operations room will be set up in the Interior Ministry and the force will have direct access to Murr.

"The mission of the force is to preserve security and order in liberated areas by conducting patrols and setting up mobile checkpoints in these areas," the decree said.

Security at the border fence was the job of the UN troops, Murr said.

Asked about the fate of Hizbullah's border observation posts after the area becomes in the hands of UNIFIL and the Lebanese force, the minister said, "The Resistance has been acting positively with the security forces since the beginning of the liberation."

The Resistance will facilitate the task of the joint force, he added.

 

Detainees families threaten hunger strike until we die(3)

More than 100 relatives of detainees still held in Israeli prisons began a hunger strike outside UN House in downtown Beirut on Monday morning.
The protesters ­ who fear that the issue of detainees will be forgotten following Israels withdrawal and the deployment in south Lebanon of UNIFIL and Lebanese military police and Internal Security Forces ­ said liberation would remain incomplete until all Lebanese detainees had returned home.
Receiving no initial response from either the government or local United Nations representatives, president of the Follow-up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in Israeli Prisons Mohammed Safa said plans to escalate the protest would be announced on Tuesday.
Well be on hunger strike until we die, said Abbas Hammoud, brother of Mustafa Hammoud, who was only 16 when he was detained in 1987. What happened to the governments claim that there is no complete liberation before all detainees are released? he added, referring to comments by Prime Minister Salim Hoss and others.
We are demanding that the government complain to the UN and present the detainees issue as an obstacle to border security, said Walid Salim, brother-in-law of Samir Qantar, who was captured in 1979. Qantar is the longest-serving Lebanese detainee in Israel, serving a 542-year sentence.
According to statistics posted by the committee at the protest, the official number of detainees held in Israeli prisons is 19. One of them was imprisoned in 1979, six in the late 1980s and seven throughout the 1990s. The circumstances of the five remaining detainees are unknown.
Mustafa Dirani and Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid are being detained without trial, while two others, Jawad Qasfi and Ali Balhas, have received life sentences.
What does liberated land mean to us if our people are still held captive? asked 67-year old Jamileh Yassin, mother of Anwar Yassin, who was detained in 1987. Jamileh said she had not heard any direct news from her son in two years.
Meanwhile, banners strung up outside a nearby tent read 425=Release and Lebanese detainees are not and never will be bargaining chips.
We always hear that the detainees are in good spirits and that theyre holding out, but we are here to remind the government that the victory is not complete before all the detainees are released, Salim said.


Hundreds of Lebanese militia members want compensation from Israel(4)

 Hundreds of members of the defunct pro-Israeli militia are seeking compensation for their service to the Jewish state, Israeli public radio reported Monday.

The militiamen, who have been in Israel since they fled Lebanon amid Israeli troops' withdrawal from the country, have asked Yaacov Weinroth, one of the country's leading lawyers, to represent them, the radio said.

Weinroth told the radio his 300 or so clients "plan to demand compensation from the state for the years during which they served Israel, for the goods and assets they left behind in their country of origin and for the emotional and material damage they have suffered."

He added that he was going to demand that a judicial committee be set up "with the job of fixing the criteria for receiving compensation."

More than 6,000 Lebanese, mostly militiamen and their families, have settled in Israel since the Jewish state completed its 22-year occupation of Lebanon on May 24.

Lebanese military courts have since June 5 judged some 2,200 Lebanese on charges of collaborating with Israel, giving them jail terms ranging from a few months to 15 years.


Human rights watch urges investigations into former Israeli allied militia activities(5)

Human Rights Watch has urged Germany and Lebanon to investigate war crimes and abuses committed by Israeli allied militiamen in the former occupied zone as the first group of former fighters and their families arrived for resettlement in Germany. Hanny Megally, the Executive Director of the Middle East and North Africa division in the New York-based human rights watchdog warned that militiamen who committed war crimes should not be granted impunity abroad. In letters addressed to German and Lebanese officials, Human Rights Watch called for the trial of militiamen who committed serious violations of human rights during Israels occupation. Six thousand militiamen and their families fled to the Jewish state following Israels lightening withdrawal from south Lebanon in May. Many of them are seeking refuge in Western nations. Some 2,200 ex-militiamen are standing trial in Beirut. Sentences handed down to collaborators have so far ranged from three weeks to 15 years and the death sentence in absentia. 


Dutch asked to take in Collaborators(6)

The Israeli government has asked the Netherlands to take in around 300 former Israeli-allied militiamen and their families, the Dutch Foreign Ministry said on Monday.
Israel has welcomed a large number of Lebanese since its pull-out from south Lebanon and has asked several countries, including the Netherlands, to take some of them in, a ministry spokesman told AFP.
The Dutch government is to make a decision shortly on the request, which was made at the end of June, he said.
Some 6,000 former militiamen and relatives fled to Israel after the Jewish state withdrew its forces from Lebanon on May 24. Israel had promised to take them in and provide work permits.
Some Lebanese have asked to emigrate to other countries where they have family. the spokesman said. Israeli public radio reported on Monday that hundreds of militiamen are seeking compensation for their service to the Jewish state.


Hajj issues warrants for 10 Collaborators(7)

Military Examining Magistrate Abdullah Hajj interrogated 10 former members of the defunct militia on Monday and issued warrants for their arrest.
Hajj sent the cases to Military Prosecutor Nasri Lahoud to issue recommendations for prison terms. Hajj said two collaborators should receive up to 15 years with hard labor on charges of collaborating with a wartime enemy.
Brigadier Maher Safieddine, who presides over the permanent Military Tribunal, is scheduled to prosecute 70 former militiamen on Wednesday in his 21st trial proceeding for the collaborators.


More members of pro-Israel militia return to Lebanon(8)

 Twenty-seven Lebanese, including six members of the former pro-Israeli militia who fled with retreating Israeli forces on May 24 returned to Lebanon Monday to surrender to the authorities, an AFP correspondent saw.

The militiamen, accompanied by 12 women and nine children, crossed the border at the coastal town of Naqoura, where UN peacekeepers took charge of them and handed them over to Lebanese gendarmes and members of the Lebanese security services.

The men were held for questioning, while their family members were allowed to proceed to their homes in villages of southern Lebanon.

Of the 6,000 or so Lebanese who followed the Israeli troops out of the country, for fear of reprisals or ill treatment, some 400 have now returned.

The former militiamen face trial for collaboration with the enemy.

Since the withdrawal, more than 2,200 Lebanese have been detained on charges arising from the 22-year Israeli occupation of the border strip.

The Beirut military court has so far issued sentences ranging between three weeks and 15 years imprisonment. One person has received a life sentence in absentia, while dozens have been acquitted.
