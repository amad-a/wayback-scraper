Daily Report

May Wednesday 17-2000:


Introduction

The "Israeli" occupation troops continued handing over the frontline outposts to their allied militia, while the Islamic Resistance (IR) fighters exert increasing pressure by staging a large number of military operations against the enemy positions and sentencing an "Israeli" agent at Markaba village.

In the meantime, twenty Lebanese students were treated for shock after shells fired by the "Israeli" enemy and its agents hit a school at Arab Salim town.

However, Syrian Foreign Minister Faruq Al-Sharaa told UN Secretary General Kofi Annan that the "Israeli"-occupied farms of Shibaa are rightfully Lebanese, Syria's official SANA news agency reported.


"Israelis" under Hizbullah pressure to step up pullout
IR kiled a collobrater near border post.

""Israel" continued transferring key frontline outposts to its militia allies on Tuesday as Hizbullah maintained pressure on the occupation forces by pounding border posts and killing a militiaman in Markaba.
The "Israelis" handed over the clifftop radar station at Bayada early on Tuesday morning to the collaborators, a day after transferring the Taibeh outpost in the central sector to the militia. "Israeli" troops continued to remove non-essential equipment from the Qobaa position near Markaba and several 155mm cannons from the Shraifeh artillery position between Marjeyoun and Ibl es-Saqi.
The "Israelis" are apparently on the verge of leaving the Blat outpost in the western sector, the most important "Israeli" outpost in the area and traditionally a favorite target of the resistance. The militiamen could have taken control of the hilltop outpost by Wednesday. Other than the positions straddling the border, "Israeli" troops remain in nine outposts in the occupation zone: Dimashkieh, Dabsheh, Beaufort Castle, Rihan and Aishieh in the northern sector, Izzieh, Hamamis and Shraifeh in the east and Blat in the west. "Israeli" soldiers also share headquarters with the militia in Marjeyoun and Bint Jbeil.
The militia mans around 40 positions throughout the zone.
The "Israeli" Army has stepped up its withdrawal in the past week, apparently intending to evacuate all non-essential equipment and personnel by June. From that point on the remaining "Israeli" troops will be ready to leave at a moments notice. But they may have a longer wait than generally believed.
Meanwhile, the militia appears to have been assigned the perilous task of protecting the "Israeli" pullback. The "Israeli" intention appears to be that the bulk, if not all, the exposed frontline outposts will have been transferred to the collaborators by the beginning of June. The "Israelis" will have redeployed to border outposts.
The militia has already borne the brunt of fighting this year. So far, 23 militiamen have died compared to 20 for all of 1999.
The morale of the militia is already low and it remains to be seen how the militiamen will react to the sight of their "Israeli" allies heading for the border crossings leaving them to the mercy of Hizbullahs fighters.
The vulnerability of the Israeli agents was underlined in deadly fashion on Tuesday when two squads of Hizbullah fighters crept into Markaba in the central sector and shot dead Azar Shahla, a 22-year-old member of the militias security wing. The killing was identical to that of another militiaman, also from the Shahla family in Markaba in March.
The Hizbullah teams set up a checkpoint on the edge of the village and pulled over a car driven by Shahla, who was accompanied by his fiancée. The fighters ordered the woman to leave and then shot Shahla dead. (1)
Azar Shahla, who had returned to civilian life more than a year ago, was shot dead at point-blank range on the village square.

Villagers told AFP that three armed men had arrived in the village in a van. They fired into the air to discourage gawpers, then went up to Shahla and shot him dead, before driving off.

Hizbullah issued a statement claiming responsibility for the killing, saying one of its commandos had "carried out the death sentence on the collaborator Azar Shahla."

An "Israeli" military source in the northern "Israeli" town of Kiryat Shmona said Shahla's death was the third such incident in recent months.

Lebanese Prime Minister Salim Hoss last week rejected an amnesty request from militia leader Antoine Lahd for his men, who face charges of "treason" for their activities, while Lahd himself has been sentenced to death in absentia for "collaboration with the "Israeli" enemy."

However, Hizbullah said Sunday it would seek a pardon for any militiaman who killed an "Israeli" or a collaborators official.

The promise came after a warning last month by Hizbullah chief Sheikh Hassan Nasrallah that his group would "liquidate" the militiamen who did not hand themselves in to the authorities after "Israel" pulls out, which it has pledged to do by July.

Lebanese security services put the number of militiamen at between 2,400 and 2,500 today, where in 1999 the membership was estimated at between 2,700 and 2,800. (2)


Hizbullah responds after Israeli shells school again

"Tensions remained at a steady boil in the south on Tuesday when more than 20 students were treated for shock after shells fired by the Israeli-proxy militia hit a school in Arab Salim.
Hizbullah responded by firing Katyusha rockets at the border positions of Ramieh and Berkat Risha in the western sector, wounding two "Israelis"  working on the new defenses along the frontier.
The school was hit at 10.30am when two rounds fired from the Soweida position at the southern end of the village hit the buildings upper floor, with one round exploding inside a classroom. The students were in the playground for the morning break. The explosion shattered windows and destroyed desks and chairs.
At the same time the school was shelled, the same post fired shells at a nearby Health Ministry infirmary, causing major damage. The bombardment echoed a similar incident in December in which 18 children were wounded.
"An ambulance transferred five students to the Nabatieh Public Hospital for treatment"(3)

Sharaa backs Lebanon on Shibaa Farms(5)

"Syrian Foreign Minister Faruk al-Sharaa told UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan on Tuesday that the "Israel"-occupied farms of Shibaa are rightfully Lebanese.
Syrias official SANA news agency reported that during a telephone conversation initiated by Annan, Sharaa said Damascus supported Lebanons claims on all Lebanese occupied territories, including the farms of Shibaa.
Sharaa told Annan the hamlets were never a point of contention between Syria and Lebanon, SANA reported.
Under international law and UN resolutions on the Arab-"Israeli" conflict, the farms of Shibaa are occupied lands and do not belong to "Israel", Sharaa said, asking Annan for UN help to guarantee "Israel's" complete departure from south Lebanon.
"Sharaas declaration was the first public comment on the Shibaa Farms debate from Damascus. "Israel" claims that the hamlets are part of Syria and their fate is tied to "Israel's" peace negotiations with Damascus".

Fifa will expel "Israel" if it attacks Lebanese sports facilities: Velapan(6)

"The secretary-general of the Asian Football Confederation, Peter Velapan, confirmed in Kuwait that the sport's world governing body, FIFA, will expel "Israel" if it ever thought of threatening Lebanon's sports facilities ahead of the Asian Cup 2000 hosts in that country. He told the Kuwaiti newspaper Al-Qabas Tuesday: ""Israel" won't be so stupid as to attack Lebanon during the Asian Cup or try to destroy its sporting facilities. "But if this happens "Israel" must know that FIFA will punish it and suspend it from the international scene." He added: "This issue was the main talking-point during my last meeting with Joseph Blatter in Zurich. Mr Blatter spoke to the UN to prevent "Israel" from destroying Lebanese facilities." Velapan said that if the Lebanese situation deteriorates four countries - Malaysia, Thailand, China and Japan - are ready to stage the finals in October with just four weeks' notice. "Israel's" air force recently attacked and destroyed electricity facilities in Lebanon".


"Israeli" occupation forces mobile phone networks to shut down(7)
"A spokesman for the Lebanese phone company LibanCell said Tuesday that the "Israeli" occupation's proxy militia, acting under "Israeli" orders, had cut off transmitters and relays for mobile phones in the area Tuesday.

The phone service would be cut every morning from 9 a.m. to midday, the spokesman said.

While no motivation was given, it likely shows that ""Israel"" wants as much privacy as possible as it moves troops and military equipment, said Agence France Presse that reported the news.

LibanCell telephones are the only ones that work in occupied southern Lebanon, where no traditional telephones have been installed since 1976, the second year of Lebanon's civil war.

"AFP said two militiamen on Tuesday came to two LibanCell transmission stations in Ain Ibel and Naqoura and ordered the technicians to close the stations before noon everyday until further notice".
