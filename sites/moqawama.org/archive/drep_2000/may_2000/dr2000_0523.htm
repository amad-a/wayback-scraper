Defections gut Israels militia ahead of "Israeli" pullout
Five civilians slaughtered by "Israeli" shelling(1)(IA)

"Israel's" plans for an orderly withdrawal from the occupation zone took a sharp turn toward collapse on Monday as Israeli-allied militiamen defected in droves and jubilant villagers and resistance fighters surged into liberated villages to return to their homes.
But even as the 22-year occupation neared its end, a series of "Israeli" and militia attacks by helicopter gunships and artillery guns killed at least five civilians and wounded 21.
Fearful that Hizbullah would retaliate for the civilian casualties by launching cross-border rocket strikes, "Israeli" authorities ordered residents of northern communities into bomb shelters.
The collaborators completely collapsed in the central sector and was on the brink of total disintegration in the western sector after 200 militiamen handed themselves in to the authorities and resistance groups.
By Monday evening, the occupation zone was split in two with jubilant flag-waving Hizbullah supporters standing at the "Israeli" border.
The stunningly rapid developments began on Monday morning, when about 500 Houla residents gathered at the edge of the frontline village of Shaqra, 3 kilometers to the west. They ignored warnings from UN peacekeepers and streamed to Houla along a rutted track that wound through Wadi Slouqi, the scene of countless gunbattles between resistance fighters and "Israeli" troops.
The militiamen manning the position on the edge of Houla had fled overnight in the wake of Sundays liberation of the neighboring villages of Qantara and Taibeh.
The sound of heavy machine-gun fire could be heard coming from Markaba as the Israeli agents hurriedly abandoned their outpost and checkpoint on the edge of the village. The militiamen regrouped at the southern approach to Odaisseh, blocking the road with mounds of earth.
The liberation of Houla paved the way for the extraordinary scene of enthusiastic Hizbullah supporters streaming along the border road, just dozens of meters from a string of "Israeli" Army outposts. With Hizbullah flags fluttering from car windows, the first few vehicles passed directly beneath the "Israeli" troops and entered Markaba to receive another rapturous welcome from the residents.
A group of Hizbullah fighters raced into the abandoned headquarters of the militias 70th battalion next to Markaba and emerged minutes later with grenades, helmets, flak jackets, and belts of ammunition.
Tensions mounted around noon, however, when five "Israeli" Apache helicopters took up position, hovering high above the border fence between Houla and Markaba. Two teenagers raced by on scooters heading toward Markaba when one of the helicopters opened fire at the road 20 meters ahead of them. The hail of shells kicked up fountains of dust and stones, causing both teenagers to fall off their scooters. They hurriedly remounted and returned to Houla.
Two "Israeli" Merkava tanks emerged on the edge of a position on the border, their barrels pointed ominously at the road into Markaba. Another Merkava sidled into position beside the Abbad outpost on the border opposite Houla. "Israeli" artillery units near Meis al-Jabal opened up on various areas in the central and western sectors. The Apaches continued strafing the road north of Markaba to deter anyone from approaching the new militia lines at the entrance to Odaisseh. Two Merkavas also shelled the sides of the road, while a temporary artillery emplacement just inside "Israel" made its presence known every few seconds.
The Taibeh outpost, abandoned by the collaborators on Sunday, was destroyed in a deafening explosion that sent an enormous cloud of gray smoke billowing into the sky. A second massive blast further to the south indicated that another outpost had been abandoned and destroyed. It was unclear what caused the blasts but appeared to have been from guided missiles fired by high-flying "Israeli" warplanes.
A Merkava lay burning by the side of the road just south of Houla, having been abandoned by its crew after it broke down earlier in the morning. In the afternoon, an "Israeli" helicopter fired a missile into the tank, setting it ablaze. A crowd gathered 200 meters from the tank, hoping to continue on the road to Meis al-Jabal. But the "Israelis" were determined to block the route.
A tank round exploded just 5 meters from this reporter. Seconds later, heavy machine-gun fire cracked overhead, followed by the hiss and blast of an anti-tank missile, which exploded in a cloud of white smoke.
Two civilians were killed a little further down the road by shelling from the same tank. A pickup truck braved the bombardment to recover the mangled corpses from the side of the road. Three more civilians were killed by machine-gun fire and artillery shells in various villages, while at least 21 were wounded.
By late afternoon, heavy fighting erupted around the village of Srobbine, just outside Tyre, as Hizbullah fighters battled retreating the militiamen.
Resistance fighters gathered at the entrance to Srobbine as an ambulance drove down a narrow lane to recover four civilians wounded by shelling. An "Israeli" tank opened fire on the village, sending residents ducking into doorways. Over 140 shells exploded in the nearby wadis. By the evening, the Baraasheit, Beit Yahoun, Rshaf and Haddatha compounds had all been evacuated, along with much of Bint Jbeil.
UNIFIL spokesman Timur Goksel said that the disintegration of the collaborators had been inevitable. Its the collapse of the fallacy that the militia could stand on its own as the "Israelis" leave, he said.
Security sources believe that the western sector could cease to exist by Tuesday, with the rest of the occupation zone being dismantled in the next few days.


Lebanese villagers rejoice to be home (2)

" Trills of joy re-echoed through this south Lebanon village Monday as its inhabitants regained their long-lost homes, drowning out the distress of those who suddenly found themselves on the wrong side after 22 years of "Israeli" occupation.

Among the thousands of triumphant villagers pouring back into Houla a few dozen men stood out, dressed in fatigues, bristling with weapons and ammunition.

As the returnees entered the village, just three kilometers (one mile) from the "Israeli" border, to be showered with rice and roses by the few hundred who had remained throughout the long years of occupation, the Hizbullah fighters went methodically round the houses with their list of names, tracking down any member of "Israel's" proxy militia who had not fled.

"Give me back my son!" sobbed one mother, who collapsed on her doorstep as the Hizbullah men led him away.

But she got short shrift from black-clad Ghaziya Ali.

"My son fell as martyr defending his country, yours collaborated with the occupation forces, and you don't want him to be put on trial?" she said, waving a photo of her dead son.

The exchange cast a fleeting shadow over the general joy, but villagers hastened to reassure the weeping woman.

"Don't worry, your son is in good hands. Any punishment he gets will be fair," they said.

The Hizbullah squad picked up a dozen or so militiamen in Houla, to be packed off northwards in twos and threes in ordinary cars.

"We are carrying out a cleansing operation in the liberated areas and will hand over all collaborators to the relevant state services," Hizbullah's political head for south Lebanon, Sheikh Abdel Magid Ammar, who was going round with them, told AFP.

The holiday mood soon returned. Villagers who had stayed behind throughout the occupation offered water and milk to the new arrivals, some of whom had been born since 1978 and had never seen Houla before.

Nahed Daher, 24, who works in a clothes shop in Beirut, came with a bunch of school friends.

"'They' arrested my brother 11 years ago, and we had to flee. Now I am back I can feel my heart is going to burst with joy," she said.

Zeinab Qtaish could top anyone's story.

"I fled in my wedding dress with my fiance who wanted to escape compulsory military service with the Israeli agents. Now I've come back with four children aged between six and 12," she said.

Maha Zaraqit could not contain her impatience, laughing and crying at the same time. She was desperate to return to her own village of Markaba, but knew she still had a few hours to wait.

Old Hassan Daher, leant on his cane as he sat next to his wife in the shade of an oak tree, his furrowed face transfigured with happiness.

"I've lived alone with my wife all these years. Soon my three sons will come back. They married in Beirut, and I have not even set eyes on my grand-children yet," he smiled.

"God be praised, we have been delivered from those thugs who used to attack our homes and prevented us from sleeping at night by firing their guns into the air all the time," he added.

Mohammed Zaayter, 32, had tears in his eyes but was laughing out loud as he wandered round the village square.

"I can't believe it. Tell me, am I dreaming, or am I back in Houla?" he yelled.

"It's true then, what (Egyptian leader Gamal Abdel) Nasser said: what was seized by force can only be liberated by force," he said.

 

Hundreds of "Israeli" troops leave Lebanon as pullout forges ahead(3)

" Hundreds of "Israeli" troops left south Lebanon under the cover of darkness early Tuesday morning amid reports the Jewish state will end its 22-year occupation of its northern neighbour within days.

An army official confirmed that "Israel" had evacuated 120 troops from its Bint Jbail headquarters in the central sector of its self-declared "security zone" and described the movement of several hundred more overnight as "technical changes".

However, security sources in Lebanon said withdrawal affected some three-quarters of "Israel's" forces in the zone.

"Israeli" public radio reported that security cabinet decided an emergency session chaired by Prime Minister Ehud Barak to complete its withdrawal from Lebanon "within days", public radio said.

"Israel" has been widely expected to carry out a swift withdrawal after its grip on its occupied zone in Lebanon was shaken by the flight of its allied militia on Monday from several border villages in the face of Hizbullah advances.

"The army is preparing itself in the security zone according to the new situation that has evolved," it said in a statement.

Travelling in armored vehicles including troop carriers, tanks and jeeps, jubilant soldiers rolled back into "Israeli" territory through a border gate near this small northern town, many of them dancing and singing and waving their guns in the air.

An AFP photographer saw a first group of up to 600 soldiers cross from the central sector of the zone into "Israel" near Kibbutz Baram shortly before 2:00 a.m. (2300 GMT Monday) but "Israeli" military censors had imposed a media blackout on reporting of the troop movements until some five hours later.

At the gate near Metullah, an AFP correspondent saw at least 370 troops pass through a high metal and barbed wire fence into "Israeli" territory in five separate convoys from around 3:00 a.m. (0000 GMT) until dawn.

"It feels great," said one soldier, as his comrades hugged each other in joy and several pulled out mobile phones to call their families.

"It got pretty hot in there. We are so happy to be out. I guess some things didn't go right," he told AFP.

The operation in Metullah occurred without incident, with no sign of any attacks by Hizbullah although the preceding hours were marked by a series of "Israeli" air and artillery bombardments that reverberated through this valley.

A total of five Lebanese civilians were killed and 35 injured during the day by "Israeli" shelling aimed at the outskirts of villages abandoned by its proxy militia police and hospital sources said.

"Israelis" living on the last active Arab-"Israeli" frontline were ordered into bomb shelters because of the chaos, fearing revenge attacks after the deaths.

The returning soldiers brought with them sniper guns, radio equipment, armoured bulldozers, mine-clearing tanks, while one truck was loaded up with office furniture, computers and a fridge.

Barak had long pledged to end what he termed the "tragedy" of "Israel's" bloody occupation by July, the anniversary of his first term in office.

At an emergency late-night session on Monday, the "Israeli" cabinet empowered Barak to set a new date for the pullback after agreeing in March to the July 7 target date.

Hundreds of militiamen streamed across the border seeking asylum here after fleeing their posts, while scores more surrendered to Hizbullah or to the Lebanese army.

Many are coming reluctantly to the Jewish state, but fear reprisals or prosecution by Beirut as "collaborators with the enemy" after "Israel" ends its occupation of its northern neighbour.

In the end, they just skulked away. But can the war really be over?(12)

By Robert Fisk in Beirut

23 May 2000

So what was it all for? The almost quarter century of occupation, the two decades of guerrilla war, the dispossession and the thousands of deaths? In just 24 hours, the whole history of southern Lebanon has been shown up as a fraud.

"Israel's" "security zone" was not secure. Its existence never protected "Israel"  when one- third of "Israel's" 10 per cent of Lebanon collapsed yesterday, not a single Katyusha rocket was fired across the border.

And when "Israel's" proxy "South Lebanon Army" militia  once referred to by an "Israeli" general as "the shield of Galilee"  simply ran away, the tens of thousands of villagers who had fled their homes or been driven from them by the "Israelis" over 22 years streamed back with babies and children who had turned into Hizbullah fighters during the years of war.

It was the endgame, final proof that the whole cynical, vicious war in southern Lebanon was a lie.

Three of the returning villagers were killed when an "Israeli" helicopter pilot opened fire on a convoy of civilians, another of "Israel's" notorious south Lebanon "mistakes"  like the massacre at Qana, the attack on the Mansouri ambulance, the destruction of an entire family in Nabatieh in 1996, the slaughter of a shepherd's family two years ago.

But the village graveyards are already full of "martyrs" and death is a familiar character in the stony hills of southern Lebanon.

Death has come to Hizbullah guerrillas and "Israeli" soldiers with equal ferocity over the past 18 years. The Hizbullah welcomed death, the "Israelis" feared it. Which is why the Hizbullah have won and the "Israelis" have lost.

And it was all so predictable. The yellow and green Hizbullah banners on the abandoned SLA positions, the dire warnings of "severe reprisals" from the "Israeli" Prime Minister, Ehud Barak, the claims  all too real  of victory by the guerrillas who have been fighting the "Israelis" since 1982.

Never would "Israel" retreat without a prior peace agreement, successive "Israeli" prime ministers have promised, bleeding their soldiers in the wadis and gun batteries of southern Lebanon. But in the end, there was no agreement, no peace, no treaty.

Just the sight of the greatest army in the Middle East skulking away and its militiamen surrendering in droves.

In just four days, 375 SLA men, perhaps 18 per cent of the active SLA force that has bombarded its fellow citizens, threatened them, cajoled them and  in Khiam prison  tortured them, have defected to the Hizbullah or handed themselves in to the Lebanese authorities.

Those who tried to flee into "Israel" met a familiar response: go home.

In 1983, it was the same when the "Israelis" left the Chouf mountains. In 1985, it was the same when they left Sidon. Their collaborators were abandoned to the wolves, tried, imprisoned, sometimes executed or bundled into the trunks of cars for torture.

Yet yesterday, oddly for such a Lebanese war, there was a kind of tolerance about it all. No SLA men were murdered. No Hizbullah army moved en masse into the Shia villages liberated from the "Israelis". The guerrillas were there, of course, often with their guns.

But most were men born in the newly freed hamlets of Houla and Taibeh and Markaba and Talloussa and Beit Yahoun, men who could not remember the day their parents carried them on shoulders from these same villages more than two decades ago.

Some say only a handful of "Israeli" soldiers remain; perhaps 140, perhaps 500. The great keep of Beaufort Castle, besieged by Saladin during the crusades, besieged by the Hizbullah for the past 15 years, was rumoured to have been abandoned.

Had the "Israelis" flown out their artillery by helicopter overnight? Some of them had walked home over the fields to "Israel", in silence and in fear. While their ex-comrades in the SLA  some of whom had been working for the Hizbullah all along  walked north into the arms of their own people. In all 100 SLA men gave themselves up yesterday.

There was gunfire, of course; "Israeli" tanks vainly firing into the wadis and on to the roads to stop the army of civilians from returning to long-lost homes, helicopter gunships firing into the wadis. And three more "martyrs" to the cause of south Lebanon, as innocent as the dead of Qana.

But by last night, almost every Shia Muslim village in southern Lebanon had been liberated from "Israel's" occupation. Only the Christian villages remained.

And there lies the rub. Will they be "freed" as easily as the Muslim hamlets? Will their Christian SLA men be so keen to give themselves up? Is the war really over?
