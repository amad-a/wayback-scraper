Daily Report

 

May Saturday 27-2000:


Introduction

"Blood has defeated the sword and broken chain", Hizbullah Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah told around 200 thousand people who had gathered to meet him in the newly liberated town of Bint Jbeil. "We are here thanks to the martyrs and the bloods of Karbalaa, not as a result of a favor from the UN, the US or the cabinet of Barak who had no choice other than pulling out his troops from Lebanon, he said.

Sayyed Nasrallah called on the Palestinian people to follow the lead of Hizbullah and adopt the option of resistance to liberate their land from the Zionist occupation.

Meanwhile, Lebanese President Emile Lahoud stressed that the "Israelis" were paying a very high price as the resistance was going strong", adding that "for the first time in Lebanon everyone was with the resistance because of the united Lebanon being the national army, the resistance and the population".

Elsewhere, Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk Sharaa said UN force's actions must not biased against the Lebanese, as this would immediately make the force lose any credibility, adding that "nor was it the role of the United Nations to disband Hizbullah".


Nasrallah: World is amazed at this ideal victory Palestinian must use Lebanese model to liberate their land…(1)

"Hizbullah’s secretary-general urged south Lebanon’s Christians to abandon their fears on Friday, stressing that inter-faith coexistence was a top national priority now that the "Israeli" occupation was over.
Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah spoke at a rally in Bint Jbeil, only 3 kilometers away from the "Israeli" border, on his first trip to the South since "Israel's" withdrawal on Wednesday.
“We have to prove to everybody that we’re up to this responsibility,” Nasrallah said. “Don’t allow anybody to interfere because we’re neighbors to an enemy that cannot bear to see us happy after getting used to seeing us suffer all this time.”
He spoke from a recently erected platform engraved with the figure 1,276 ­ a reference to the number of Hizbullah fighters who martyred in the long struggle against the occupation.
Nasrallah said that the absence of violence between people was a true victory for the Lebanese.
“We don't want the enemy to take advantage of small issues. I call on local Christian and Muslim clergymen, politicians and notables in the area to work on healing the wounds,” he said. “The whole world is amazed at this ideal victory, which is even more important than the military victory we achieved.”
Referring to fears expressed by some Christians of the liberated area, Nasrallah said: “I say to some Lebanese, abandon your fears ... be true Lebanese and true nationals during this historic moment.”
Throughout his speech, Nasrallah was repeatedly dismissive of the United Nations and its role, saying that Hizbullah was not concerned with decisions the Security Council made.
Earlier in the day, UN envoy Terje Roed-Larsen said that the United Nations expected everyone ­ including Hizbullah ­ to abide by decisions of the Security Council.
“We in the resistance don’t understand this jargon,” Nasrallah said. “What we understand is that there is Lebanese land, whether or not its part of Resolution 425 or Resolution 5,700, this land must be returned to Lebanon.”
Nasrallah also reiterated that Hizbullah was not in any way responsible for the security in the South and did not at all consider itself an alternative to the Lebanese government.
“It is the government which is responsible for security and should assume that,” he said. “There is also another responsibility that the government should handle, that of development and reconstruction.”
Hizbullah, he added, would offer all the services it could, but he also stressed that it was still the government that was responsible for the development of the area.
Nasrallah also said that "Israel" would soon have to give back the Shibaa Farms to Lebanon and release all detainees in "Israeli" prisons, including Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, Mustafa Dirani and Samir Qantar. There are still 19 Lebanese detainees in prisons inside "Israel".
“Every captive in "Israeli" jails will return to you soon, God willing. Shibaa Farms will return to Lebanon, God willing,” Nasrallah told the crowd waving a sea of yellow, green and red Hizbullah flags.
"Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak “and his government have no choice. I advise him to pull out of Shibaa. He has no choice, and the coming days will prove that he has no other choice,” he added.
Nasrallah interrupted on several occasions with chants of “Oh God, preserve for us Nasrallah,” also urged the Palestinian people to use the Lebanese example to liberate "Israeli"-occupied territories and end the peace process.
Nasrallah said that the "Israeli"-allied militiamen who handed themselves over to the Lebanese Army should be
harshly punished so that serve as a lesson for the future. “It’s time that the Lebanese benefit from all their mistakes,” he said.
Nasrallah also said that apart from the South, there was another area in Lebanon that was suffering from neglect and was in dire need of development. “Allow me, from Bint Jbeil, to talk about an area which we are greatly indebted to in terms of resistance, which is Baalback-Hermel,” he said.
Nasrallah urged the government to start a balanced development in both the liberated area and the Baalback-Hermel area.
Earlier, he said that Hizbullah was not intent on a rough electoral battle in the south . This, he said, was because the priority in the area was internal unity and “fierce electoral competition could jeopardize this unity during such a critical stage.”
On Friday, Nasrallah repeated ­ but not in as many words ­ that Hizbullah would not take advantage of the victory it achieved and “it will be shown how modest we can be.”
Addressing the Arab nation, Nasrallah said that the Arabs had now moved into a new stage. “Defeat is something of the past,” the cleric declared. “This victory is the base of a new historic era. In the name of the Lebanese martyrs, I call on you to stop normalization and relations with "Israel".”


Lahoud: In Lebanon everyone was with resistance…(2)

"Speaking in a television interview with CNN, Lahoud replied to worries over internal strife and revenge among different Lebanese factions, saying: "We believe when all the population is united there is no fear that there will be repercussions or revenge between the Lebanese".

Of the "Israeli" decision to pull out from southern Lebanon after 22 years of occupation, he said: "I can say that they could not stay any more in Lebanon because they were paying a very high price and as you know the resistance was going strong."

"For the first time in Lebanon everyone was with the resistance because of the united Lebanon being the national army, the resistance, the population even the opposition and the loyalists, all worked together as one" to stop the "Israelis" staying in Lebanon, he said.

"This is why they had to leave," he added.

He praised the role of the United Nations in Lebanon, saying they had been helping for 22 years and "we hope it will continue like that."

There were meetings nearly every day and "the cooperation will be even more close between the United Nations and the Lebanese."

But he stressed that any peace settlement had to cover all the areas in dispute with "Israel".

"To have a really global comprehensive peace, there are other problems that should be faced.

"One of them as you know we have many Palestinian camps in Lebanon and it is the only place in the world where there are refugees who are armed and this makes a problem for Lebanon," he said, adding they had a right to go back to their homeland.

"I think it is time to find a way for them to go back home," he said.

He said the Golan Heights, taken by "Israel" from Syria in 1967, was another problem "without a peaceful solution on the Golan Heights there will not be peace, a global comprehensive peace."

He dismissed concerns about left-over weaponry falling into the hands of the various militias in Lebanon, saying it was being handed over to the Lebanese army.


Sharaa says UN mission in South welcome, but don’t disarm Hizbullah…(3)

Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk Sharaa said on Friday that it was a “hypothetical question” to ask whether Syria would accept a United Nations finding that "Israel" had indeed ended its occupation of south Lebanon.
“In principle, I don’t think the United Nations Secretary-General, Kofi Annan, would give such a statement unless he is 100 percent sure that "Israel" has already left all the Lebanese national territory, in accordance with Resolution 425 and (including) the Shibaa Farms,” he told a news conference in Lisbon.
He was speaking during a meeting of ministers and officials from 27 European Union and Mediterranean countries, including "Israel" and a number of Arab countries.
Economy Minister Nasser Saidi, who like Sharaa is taking part in Euro-Med partnership talks in the Portuguese capital, also said the Shibaa Farms must be returned for the "Israeli" withdrawal to be complete.
“Until the Shibaa Farms have been returned, we will not consider that Resolution 425 has been fully implemented,” Saidi told a news conference.
Sharaa said that, in general, Syria supported a UN role in south Lebanon, but he was less definitive when asked if he backed proposals to almost double a UN peacekeeping force to 7,900. He said he could not comment without knowing what role such a force would play.
A UN force’s actions must not be biased against the Lebanese, as this would immediately make the force lose any creditability, he said. Nor was it the role of the United Nations to disband Hizbullah guerrillas.
“It’s up to the Lebanese government to decide the future of this movement from a military point of view,” he said.

Runaway militiamen should return for trials…(4)

Prime Minister Salim Hoss called on the 7,000 Lebanese on Friday who sought refuge in "Israel" following the withdrawal of the "Israeli" occupation forces to return to Lebanon.
“I urge those who left as a result of the liberation to return to their homeland and turn themselves in to the Lebanese judiciary, whose proceedings are just, fair and transparent,” said the prime minister.
More than 1,250 militiamen serving in "Israel's" proxy militia crossed into "Israel" with their families before the pullout was completed on Wednesday.
Some have already returned here, complaining of the squalid living conditions at a summer resort near Lake Tiberias where the refugees were being housed.
Many others wish to return, the UN and the Red Cross have indicated. “We’re receiving a lot of requests from people who want to come back,” UNIFIL spokesman Timur Goksel said.
Henri Fournier, the head of the International Committee for the Red Cross in Lebanon, said that “the ICRC is acquainted with this problem. We’re in touch with the population concerned and are working on a solution.”
Addressing the trial of some 1,250 militiamen who turned themselves in, Hoss said: “Special measures have been taken to speed up the questioning and the prosecution of those who surrendered to the authorities.”
Prosecution of some 500 collaborators has already begun, an unnamed judicial source said, while another 750 are due to be prosecuted within a few days. “So far, only 60 suspects have been questioned by the chief military prosecutor, Riad Talih, who is in charge of the questioning process,” the source added.
Special military courts are expected to be created to handle the cases to ensure a speedy prosecution, the source said. “The legal framework for doing this was discussed in meetings by Justice Minister Joseph Shaoul and State Prosecutor Adnan Addoum and between Addoum and Chief Military Prosecutor Nasri Lahoud.”


Lahd: "Israelis" treated us like animals…(5)

The chief of the defunct "Israeli" occupation's proxy militia Antoine Lahd talked about the miserable situation he and his fleeing militiamen are living and the bad treatment they received in the "Israeli" entity.

Lahd told the "Israeli" newspaper Yediot Aharonot that the "Israelis" have deceived him and dragged him behind them "like animals".

The fleeing militiamen and their families staged a demonstration today to protest the treatment they are receiving at the hands of the "Israeli" officials. They burned some of the clothes that the "Israelis" had given to them.


"Israeli" withdrawal will be verified in a matter of days: Annan…(6)

" The United Nations expects to finish verifying the withdrawal of "Israeli" forces from Lebanon "in the next few days," UN Secretary General Kofi Annan said Friday.

"I expect the certification in the next few days," Annan told journalists, adding that the United Nations had already begun the certification task.

A team of UN cartographers started working on the border Friday in conformance with UN resolution 425, the first stage in verification that "Israeli" troops have returned to the international border.

Annan said he believes "things are going quite well," saying that there is calm at the border and that first talks between UN special envoy Terje Roed-Larsen and Lebanese authorities had gone "extremely, extremely well."

He said he had been in contact with "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak and Lebanese President Emile Lahoud, and the two countries' leaders were cooperating with the United Nations.

Annan has asked the UN Security Council to increase the size of the UN peacekeeping mission in South Lebanon from 4,500 troops to 7,800 soldiers in order to help Lebanon maintain security in the zone vacated by "Israel".

He said he hoped UN reinforcements could begin moving into Lebanon as soon as the certification process was complete.


Spoils left by retreating occupation army arrive in Baalback…(7)

The 80-kilometers journey of the spoils left by the "Israeli" occupation forces in the western sector ended this morning.

The 15-hour journey from Sohmor to Baalback stopped at around 20 spots as people gathered on sidewalks to see what the "invincible" army left before it retreated from Lebanon with its tail between its legs.

The convoy included a T-54 tank, two Armored Personnel Carriers, a number of military vehicles and a car that used to belong to the eastern sector commander of the occupation's militia Nabih Abu Rafeh who has fled to occupied Palestine.

Beqaa Governor Milad al-Qareh mounted one of the convoy cars along with Faithfulness to the Resistance (FTR) member MP Sayyed Ammar al-Musawi.

This the first time ""Israel"" resorts to destroying its arms or leaving them in the battlefield, al-Musawi said.

This is a real independence day for Lebanon. What happened represents the victory of the Resistance's values and culture as towns were liberated without bloodshed even among the collaborators, he added.

MP Musawi said the UN's position on the Shibaa farms is a fallacy that may lead to the UN losing what is left of its credibility.

Resolution 425 talks about withdrawal from all Lebanese territories, not only about territories occupied in 1978, he concluded..


Hizbullah destroys "Israeli" munitions

Hizbullah on Friday blew up munitions left behind by the "Israeli" Army during its hasty withdrawal from the south, security sources said.
Three loud explosions shook the newly liberated south as a former "Israeli" artillery position was dynamited at Dimachqiye in the central sector of the former occupied zone, they said…(8)

One civilian killed and two wounded…(9)

Police reported that Hassan Salman, 17, was killed while handling a grenade he found on Thursday.
Two other young people were wounded when a land mine exploded near the "Israeli" position at Beaufort Castle, which was evacuated in the final hours of the retreat on Wednesday. One lost his foot in the blast.


Barak rules out release of Lebanese hostages now…(10)

"Prime Minister Ehud Barak said there are no plans as of yet to release 20 Lebanese detainees held in "Israel". Lebanon has been demanding their freedom as a precondition to confirming "Israel's" pullout from the south. Barak said the hostages, including resistance figures Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani, will only be released after "Israel" receives information on airman Ron Arad, who went missing when his plane crashed over Lebanon in 1983. Various militias who operated across the country at the time have denied any knowledge on the whereabouts of Arad's body. UN envoy Terje Larsen has promised to do his utmost to help solve the issue. Diplomatic sources told Future Television, Beirut will hand Larsen a list of the detainees' names. They added, Larsen will deliver the list to "Israeli" justice ministry officials and work for their release".


“No one felt safe ­ the "Israelis" used to see a shadow move and shoot at it’…(11)

Life in the small town of Odaisseh following liberation will never be the same. Its people are still close enough to the "Israeli" border to shout to inhabitants on the other side but now they dare to do so.
“This is the most traffic this area has ever seen,” said Mariam Asmar, a native of the village. “It was such a sight to come into the village at midnight and see the streets full of people.”
For the last 22 years, the inhabitants of Odaisseh have been careful to be home by 6pm every night. “No one felt safe,” said Asmar. “The "Israelis" used to see a shadow move and shoot at it.”
On Wednesday, she sat on her terrace until 2am without fear.
“It’s like a huge weight has been lifted,” said her neighbor, Raqaya Ashmar, sipping juice at her side under the shade of a willow tree. “We feel so relieved, like we can breathe again.”
Until a few days ago, children were repeatedly warned not to even “stick a finger past the road” separating the adversaries, or attract too much attention. Today, hundreds of families stop to wave flags and take pictures with "Israeli" soldiers standing in the background.
Before liberation, villagers who had not openly aligned themselves with Antoine Lahd’s militia lived under the constant threat of being taken to the Khiam detention center or having their homes demolished because of suspected collaboration with the resistance.
Raqaya’s cousin, 70-year-old Zarifeh Abboud, who has lived here since moving from Africa when she was 18, said: “All our young men are in Beirut. If they’d stayed, they would’ve had to do obligatory military service with Lahd.”
Many left for Beirut because of a lack of work, although some went into "Israel" to bring in a steady income through sewing or agriculture.

With all these factors at play, only a handful of the several thousand residents remained. But Raqaya’s husband Ahmad made it a point to make his presence in the occupied zone felt. “I knew that with every tree I planted in front of my house,” he said, “they’d die a little because I was refusing to leave.”
But while the Jewish settlement of Misgav Am has sat opposite the Ashmar home since 1948, there has been little interaction between them. “We’ve been neighbors for over 50 years,” said Ahmad. “But yesterday I saw something that made my hair stand on end ­ Amal, Hizbullah and "Israeli" soldiers all in one corner.”
There is also a language barrier, said Asmar ­ who unlike her Palestinian neighbors living under occupation, does not speak a word of Hebrew. “The "Israelis" didn’t dare mix with us,” she said. “They’d only drive their tanks back and forth on our roads. We’d only see Lahd’s men around here. ”
Even though "Israel" is their “closest neighbor,” literally a stone’s throw away, Odaisseh people would trek to unoccupied Lebanon for their daily provisions. “It would’ve taken me five minutes to get into "Israel", but I insisted on making the three-day trip to Sidon and Nabatieh to get my supplies,” said Ahmad, who brings in medicines to distribute to the area’s pharmacies.
The trip would not have even taken so long had the militia not closed the border crossing at Kfar Tibnite at noon every day. As it was, newspapers arrived a day late because the village drivers were unable to make it back before noon after picking them up. And not all papers were allowed in.
Sending or receiving mail in the occupied zone was impossible. Any such correspondence had to be done through the drivers leaving the area.
“We often sent yogurt to family in Beirut and they’d send us coffee beans with the drivers,” Abboud recalled.
All goods, except oil and foodstuffs that they produced themselves, were also brought in from cities outside the occupation zone. “Nothing was brought in from "Israel",” Abboud said. “Water, electricity, schools, all came from Lebanon.”
Of course, the impact of shortages during the war were felt down south as well, and many depended on generators, well water, and stored rain-water. Phone lines, as in many other parts of Lebanon, have still not been installed.
But government officials who had access to the zone, set up street lights in Odaisseh two years ago. Civilians originally from the area could get permission from the Lebanese Army in Sidon to enter. It was a simple process, Asmar said.
For healthcare, Adaissians would either travel to the hospital in Marjeyoun or go into northern "Israel", depending on whether they were collaborators or not.
All car license plates carried the old Lebanese numbers. “You might see many without plates because they weren’t registered,” said Ahmad. “Those were the ones illegally imported from Europe through Naqoura port because Customs wouldn’t allow pre-1992 models in. But all that should change now under the Lebanese government.”
And now that Lebanese troops have made an appearance on the scene, these two families are breathing easier.

Khiam’s future may be a remembrance site…(12)
The National Audiovisual Media Council wants to convert Khiam into a museum to keep the memory of the war’s brutality for generations to come.
Nasser Qandil, head of the council, proposed the idea at Khiam on Thursday during a media tour of the prison, where more than 144 detainees were liberated on Tuesday following the "Israeli" withdrawal and the "Israeli"-proxy militia collapse.
The idea of turning the formerly militia-run prison ­ the site of numerous human rights violations ­ into a place of remembrance has been backed by activists like Mohammed Safa, secretary-general of the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in "Israeli" Prisons.

The prison has turned into the most sought-after tourist destination in the South as thousands of Lebanese and foreigners flock to see it with their own eyes.
The rooms of the former Lebanese Army barracks, were all open. Most were kicked in after Khiam residents stormed the center shortly after the agents left it on Monday in order to free the prisoners.
Mattresses, clothes, food and handicrafts made by detainees were left scattered in haste as prisoners rushed to freedom.
Horror and disgust were evident on the faces of visitors who entered each cell.
Ahlam Awada, released from four years of detention in 1993, was among them. For the first time she was able to move about the facility freely, examining rooms that had once imprisoned her.
“I can’t put my feelings into words,” she said. “I’m just so happy to see all this horror come to an end.”
Two of her brothers and her sister had also been imprisoned there.
Torture, beatings and insults were the daily pattern of the first six months of her detention, but later, as Awada said: “They forgot about me and were busy torturing newcomers.
“The only wish I have is that those who tortured me can be detained here and tortured the same way.”


Former detainees recount brutal treatment by collaborators…(13)

Suleiman Ramadan was reluctant to be in the spotlight on Friday, arguing that even though he had been the longest-held prisoner at the Khiam detention center, his stories were no more important than those of any other detainee.
Still, the 100 people who gathered at the Press Federation to listen to a dozen Khiam prisoners fell silent as he recounted his 15 years as a prisoner.
Ramadan was the first prisoner to be held in the notorious facility, which opened in 1985.
He was put there after traveling through the South and taking a curious look at some "Israeli" soldiers. The "Israeli"-proxy militia immediately took him into custody, and during the first part of his detention when he was being investigated, he was left on a stretcher in a prison corridor for 73 days. That was his worst experience as a detainee, Ramadan said.
“It was January, and I was wearing nothing but shorts, with both my hands and my left foot in handcuffs, and with my head stuffed into a black bag, on a stretcher,” he recalled.
Beatings were common. The collaborators wanted to find out if his jaunt through the area was political and if he belonged to any group.

When he was captured, he was shot at and lost his foot. The wound was still fresh when "Israeli"-agents kicked him during his 73 agonizing days, re-opening the wound and causing blood to spurt from every part of his body, including his eyes and his ears.
“My wounds were still hurting, but after a while I couldn’t feel anything any more,” he said. “For 73 days, I wasn’t taken to the bathroom, and for 73 days I wasn’t cleaned up. My dirt caked on me.”
Ramadan said that food and water were very scarce. “I’d consider it a festivity when I was given the yolk or the white of an egg,” he said. “Hunger is more difficult and more humiliating than death.”
He said that the man in charge of his interrogation was an "Israeli", even though "Israel" maintains that the prison was purely  a militia operation. The officer was ready to release him, Ramadan said, convinced that he knew nothing. That was when someone who knew him was detained, and the man snitched that Ramadan was a Communist.
“And then they decided to keep me for another 15 years,” he said.
According to Ramadan, Khiam passed through three different stages.
“The hell stage lasted from 1985 until the inmates’ revolution in 1989. Then there was the arid stage between 1989 and 1995. Finally, the heaven stage was when the Red Cross entered the prison,” after 1995, he said.
Ramadan lives with his mother and his brothers in one room of the Baalback home they share with his uncles and aunts.
Cosette Ibrahim, a reporter who was detained for nine months, said her experience was nothing compared to Ramadan’s.
Ibrahim said that while she was not severely beaten, she did suffer humiliation. “All the interrogations had to be done while I was on my knees, and with handcuffs on my wrists,” she said.
According to Ibrahim, the agents had many methods of torture. For females, guards tried to destroy their reputations by telling military doctors that they were not virgins. They were also regularly subjected to sexual harassment, she said.
Another released detainee, Ghassan Saadi, said that with the possibility of some of the collaborators receiving mercy, he feared that he might have to face militiamen again ­ not in a military situation, but in normal life. “There shouldn’t be any kind of mercy toward those who were traitors to their country and to their identity,” he said.
Mohammed Safa, head of the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in "Israeli" Prisons, said that the liberation would only be complete with the release of detainees who are still held in the "Israeli" prisons of Nafaa, Sarafand and Ramle. He is organizing a sit-in at Khiam on Thursday to demand their release.
“The return of these detainees is a priority,” he said. “They’re even more important than the Shibaa Farms.”
Safa urged the government to officially ask the United Nations to look into the matter.
He also suggested that the prison be turned into a national site of remembrance. He note that in Germany, World War II concentration camps still displayed the spoons that were used to preserve every detail.


Sojod is on ground zero…(14)

"The end of "Israel's" occupation has meant the return of thousands of villagers to their homes in south Lebanon. One group of villagers from the former front-line village of Sojod has returned to an alarming reality. They found their village on ground zero.

" Little did they know what they were returning to. The village of Sojod was a regular target of "Israeli" gunners. It now lies in ruin. It is a ghost town. Like many people from south Lebanon, villagers enthusiastically flocked to their homes after "Israel" retreated from the region. Their homecoming was bittersweet, after years of forced absence. A total of four hundred homes have been demolished by "Israel". The village's mosque, clinic and school were also razed. The villagers are not disheartened -life is beginning to resume in this tiny place, which for years played host to one of "Israel's" most notorious encampments in Iqleem al-Toffah ridges. Villagers want the government to draft a reconstruction plan for Sojod. This man said when he left in 1984 his home was standing- now it is a pile of rubble. (Man telling FTV: "All the people from the south are rejoicing except for the people of Sojod- we are crying for our loss"). Villagers believe it was the price they paid for resisting "Israel's" bloody occupation. Their homes were leveled, but the people of Sojod have returned despite harsh memories of occupation. They have settled for tents, instead of their homes that once stood on this hilltop south Lebanon village. There is also severe destruction in the nearby village of Mlikh. Lebanese living on the border with "Israel" are getting accustomed to a new way of life. "Israel" withdrew from the area, but there is no peace treaty between Lebanon and the Jewish state. The region remains potentially dangerous. But streams of villagers have returned to their homes in south Lebanon after more than two decades of "Israeli" occupation. Residents of the village of Mlikh-on the frontline with "Israel", returned late Thursday after twenty four years of displacement. People went straight to their deserted homes to check them out. An old woman found her husbands jacket-I t had hung untouched for more than two decades. [Woman saying: This is my husbands jacket. Its been hanging here for 24 years.] Church bells rang for the first time in more than two decades, and bulldozers started clearing the main road into the town from the weed that grew wildly and uncontrollably from lack of use. Many houses badly need renovation- nevertheless, people are happy to be back.


Time to pay the piper…(15)

Ehud Barak says his country is ready to turn a new page in its relations with Lebanon, but it will take far more than talk to undo the damage wrought by 22 years of occupation.
His troops may be gone, but the effects of their presence will take many years to dissipate, so the "Israeli" prime minister should take responsibility for his predecessors’ actions and agree to pay compensation for the many thousands of Lebanese whose lives have been ruined ­ or ended ­ by "Israeli" aggression. Having made Lebanon its whipping boy for more than a generation, "Israel" cannot simply walk away and hope to enjoy anything like normal relations with this country.
People have been deprived of their loved ones, their homes, their livelihoods, and most of their human and civil rights. The physical destruction has been immense, the economic ruin almost total, and the psychological impact devastating. How does one answer a 5-year-old whose only comment on the withdrawal is a question: “When will "Israel" be back?”
Try as he might, if at all, Barak will never be able to make full restitution for all of the trouble that his country has caused here. He could start, however, by addressing the most easily quantifiable aspect: the destruction of property both private and public.
What was, until this week, the occupation zone is quite literally filled with evidence that "Israeli" actions cost this country billions of dollars over the years in ruined homes, shops, roads, and infrastructure. This is not to mention the damage inflicted on priceless assets like Beaufort Castle, or the bill for air strikes on bridges, power stations, and communications installations.
From the "Israeli" perspective, the best part about paying compensation is that it would signal the Jewish state’s willingness to live by the very rules that it has sought to have others obey. The "Israeli" government has put much weight behind the efforts of Holocaust survivors to reclaim what was wrongfully taken from them during World War II, and it has promised to seek compensation for Jews whose property was confiscated by Arab governments in 1948. If it does not pay reparations to Lebanon, how can the Lebanese listen to the words of Barak and his compatriots with anything but doubt and hostility?
"Israeli" officials and citizens alike can often be heard to complain that many governments treat theirs like an international outlaw, but in fact the Jewish state has benefited from uncommon leniency, thanks largely to protection afforded it by the United States. Other countries are not simply denied a few perks at the United Nations when they defy international law: they are slapped with sanctions and/or bombed into submission. Iraq, for example, killed nowhere near as many Kuwaitis as "Israel" killed Lebanese, but Baghdad is still paying the price.
It will take years for Lebanon to get back on its feet, and while much of the damage was our own doing until 1990, "Israel's" “contribution” has been relentless.
