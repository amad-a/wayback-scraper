Israel back off from new Lebanon strikes (3)

Israel backed off from further aggression against Lebanon Friday despite a new Hezbollah rocket strike on the north of the country.

Israel's public radio said the security cabinet had decided not to launch further attacks on Lebanon following the raids early Friday, but reserved the right to hit back at the appropriate moment.

 

Israel did not want to unleash a military escalation in Lebanon, after its air attacks on power facilities in the wake of a Hezbollah rocket strike Thursday, an unnamed senior official was quoted as saying.

 

The radio said members of the inner cabinet chaired by Prime Minister Ehud Barak were satisfied with the air raids, considering they had served their purpose of "punishment and dissuasion."

Thursday's Hezbollah rocket attack, itself in retaliation for deaths and injuries of civilians in southern Lebanon in air raids and shelling by Israel and its militia allies, killed one soldier and wounded another and injured 26 civilians in the northern town of Kiryat Shmona.

 

Friday's strike, which targeted Kiryat Shmona and the northwestern locality of Shlomi, caused one slight injury in the latter place.

Vital infrastructure destroyed again in latest Israeli air aggressions (8)

People across the country were woken by thunderous shelling in the early hours of Friday morning as Israeli jets blasted two power relay stations, plunging large sections of Beirut, the Bekaa and the north into darkness.

No casualties were reported but a watchman at one of the power stations was wounded in the arm by broken glass.

The raids were the third against power plants in a year.

Three transformers at the Bsalim power station, northeast of Beirut, were completely destroyed while three others sustained serious damage but might be repairable pending further reports, according to Electricite du Liban.

Meanwhile, Israeli bombers blasted the Deir Ammar power station in Baddawi, north of Tripoli, to rubble, leaving Akkar, Baalbek and Hermel in total darkness.

Its still premature to estimate the extent and cost of damage before the submission of a comprehensive report (of Bsalim and Deir Ammar), Electricity and Water Resources Minister Suleiman Traboulsi said during an inspection of the Bsalim plant. The station is now non-operational and has been isolated from the network, he added.

Meanwhile, Public Works Minister Najib Mikati confirmed that as in the past, reconstruction will take place immediately, bringing things back to normal.

He also said that generators would be supplied to ensure the distribution of drinking water through the networks.

Even by midday, smoke and steam jetted out of the wrecked transformers, leaving repairmen wary of starting their work.

They could go up in flames at any moment, said one, pointing out that the wreckage still contained boiling oil.

The EDL board called an emergency session at noon to assess the situation and make appropriate decisions, said Rebecca Abi-Daher, media advisor to EDL director-general George Mouawad.

Jalal Halawi, heading an Egyptian delegation of electrical engineers, said he would report what he saw to the president of the Electricity of Egypt and discuss possibilities for aid.

The Israeli bombers also struck near Syrian headquarters along the Damascus Highway near Chtaura, creating a crater 10 meters deep and 20 meters wide, severing the road.

A power rationing schedule has still not been worked out, pending final assessments of the damage incurred.

A senior official at the Qadisha Electricity Company said the company could provide up to six hours of power to the northern areas hit by the raids.

Meanwhile, residents near the Bsalim station had a restless night, traumatized by the tremendous explosions caused by the Israeli shelling.

I cant even describe to you how the thunderous booms sent vibrations through the building, said Elie George, a resident who lives about 300 meters from the facility.

It felt as though we were being exposed to a gush of high pressure, he added.

Our two-month-old baby daughter cried non-stop for three hours from the trauma. We felt helpless, unable to do anything for her except hold her close and comfort her.

George, whose house does not have a bomb shelter, said he would not be going anywhere in the event of resumed shelling. We have no choice.

A hairdressing salon located across from the plant suffered serious damage as a result of Fridays raids.

Fouad Rashdiki, the salons owner, estimated that the three raids over the past year had caused damage valued at least $10,000 ­ with broken windows, mirrors and twisted aluminum.

Fridays five raids in the north also caused damage to Deir Ammars vocational institute and sparked panic among residents, who fled to neighboring fields and orchards until the shelling stopped.

At about 11am, Israeli jets broke the sound barrier over the power plants of Deir Nbouh and al-Hreisheh, but no additional air raids were carried out.

Larsen: there are many complex issues to be discussed

Lebanon is not putting conditions on Israeli pullout.(13)

Terje Roed-Larsen made the following statement at the UN House on Friday,  summing up his talks with Lebanese officials:

My delegation and I have now concluded the present round of consultations with the government of Lebanon on the implementation of Resolutions 425 and 426.

As I said when I commenced my visit here, my mission is to consult with the parties and interested member states in the region on how these resolutions will be implemented. In approving these resolutions the Security Council gave us our destination. It is up to us to determine how we will get there.

We have held three extensive rounds of talks with President Lahoud and Prime Minister Hoss. We have also met with Speaker of Parliament, Mr. Berri. In these talks we have covered a very wide range of issues related to 425 and 426. The fact that these talks went much longer than planned is an indicator of the very positive and open welcome that we have received here in Beirut.

As I have said several times since my arrival, there are many complex issues to be discussed and decided upon.

I will not go into any details concerning the substance of our talks. I will only say that we have agreed on important principles which provide a basis for the United Nations to implement its responsibilities under 425 and 426. There are issues over which we have yet to reach agreement.

However, I have no sense that the government of Lebanon is putting forward conditions for the implementation of 425. On the contrary, I found the president and prime minister open-minded, and we will continue to look for common understandings that will enable the United Nations to undertake its responsibilities.

We and the government of Lebanon are in complete agreement that the full and unconditional implementation of Resolution 425 is a vital necessity for Lebanon and the wider prospects for Middle East peace.

The government of Lebanon has assured us of its full cooperation. Without such cooperation, which has existed for over two decades, the United Nations would have no peacekeeping presence in this country.

We are here at the request of the government of Lebanon, and our continued presence in the future would also be at the government's behest. Our talks over the past two days are evidence of the government's spirit of cooperation.

Under Resolution 425, the United Nations has three main responsibilities: to confirm an Israeli withdrawal from all Lebanese territory to the internationally recognized boundaries; to help restore international peace and security; to assist the government of Lebanon in the return of its effective authority in the area.

For the purpose of confirming an Israeli withdrawal from Lebanon, the United Nations must identify a line on the ground beyond which we can say that a withdrawal has occurred. Our discussions on this issue have by now been widely reported in the media with varying degrees of accuracy.

I would like to clarify what we are doing and why we are doing this. But first I must emphasize that we are not defining borders. This is a matter for states, not us.

Whatever line is endorsed by the Security Council for the purpose of verifying a withdrawal is completely without prejudice to any future border agreements between concerned states.

The 1923 line, with mutually agreed adjustments following the 1949 Armistice Agreement, constitutes an internationally recognized boundary for that portion of Lebanon's border that it shares with Israel. This does not mean that Lebanon and Israel have ever agreed on that border. Rather, this was a line drawn and agreed prior to the existence of both Lebanon and Israel.

We have encountered no fundamental disagreement that this portion of Lebanons border constitutes the line that the UN will use for the purpose of confirming a withdrawal. At some stage the United Nations will have to physically identify relevant parts of this line on the ground in order that we will be able to carry out our verification responsibilities.

But we also know that in addition to its border with Israel, Lebanon also has a border with Syria. For the purpose of confirming whether a withdrawal from all Lebanese territory has taken place, we need to identify where this line lies as well. Since my arrival here in Lebanon, I have been informed of the existence of territory which does not lie in the area of operations of UNIFIL as defined since 1978 but which the government of Lebanon considers Lebanese. We are looking very carefully into this matter, together with the government of Lebanon.

On the question of how UNIFIL proposes to undertake its responsibilities for confirming the withdrawal and for helping restore international peace and security, I explained that the full redeployment of UNIFIL could only take place once a full withdrawal had been verified.

However, I also explained that throughout the withdrawal process for the purpose of verifying the withdrawal, United Nations military teams would be deployed. These teams will be fully supported for their safety and security thought the area of operations as required.

I reiterated that the United Nations reserved the right to decide how to operationalize the military elements of its mandate, based on the judgment of the force commander and the decision the secretary-general.

We are in full agreement that dismantling the Israeli proxy militia is a requirement of the withdrawal. We also agree that detainees in Khiam prison as well as those Lebanese held inside Israel in connection with Israel's occupation of Lebanon must be released.  I conveyed this to the representatives of the government of Israel prior to coming to Lebanon. The UN is very concerned with the well-being of these detainees.

The UN is also concerned about the future of the inhabitants of the occupied zone. We conveyed this concern to the representatives of Lebanon during our meetings. My hosts cited the example of Jezzine, which I found encouraging, and reassured me that the inhabitants would be treated fairly as citizens of Lebanon.

As you have heard, we have covered a lot of ground over the past few days. I am encouraged by the progress that we have made. I look forward to continuing our consultations on these matters throughout this preparatory phase.  Even more, it is my deep hope that the day will soon come when the United Nations will be able to report to the Security Council that Resolution 425 has finally been implemented.

The people of Lebanon have had to wait too long for this and have suffered far too much in the waiting. It is my sincere hope that we will receive the full cooperation of all parties concerned to help ensure that this day will not be far off.

As you know, I will be meeting in Damascus with Foreign Minister Sharaa on Sunday. I very much welcome the Palmyra statement issued jointly yesterday by the foreign ministers of Syria, Egypt and Saudi Arabia. In particular, I appreciated the support expressed for the role of the United Nations in carrying out its responsibilities in Lebanon. Such support is essential to us throughout this period in which we are defining how we will implement our responsibilities under 425 .

Against the background of this positive statement, I am looking forward very much to my consultations with Foreign Minister Sharaa, which I have every reason to feel will be as constructive and productive as my consultations so far.

 

Lebanon rejects Annan's requests for wide UN role in south: press (14)

Lebanon has rejected a plan of UN Secretary General Kofi Annan for the deployment of an international force after Israel's withdrawal from the southern part of the country, the pro-Syrian Lebanese daily As-Safir said Friday.

Annan proposed his plan through UN special envoy Terje Roed-Larsen, who met twice Thursday with Lebanese President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss, As-Safir said.

"Lebanon rejects seven UN propositions because they violate sovereignty and the spirit of Resolution 425 and they interfere in its internal affairs," the newspaper said on its front page.

Resolution 425 calls for Israel's unconditional retreat from southern Lebanon, which it has held since 1978. Prime Minister Ehud Barak said April 17 it would comply with the resolution by July.

As-Safir said Annan proposed an increase in UN forces with an operational role in the area vacated by Israel as a way to assure a complete withdrawal.

He asked for Beirut to assure the UN troops' security -- without which, he said, it would be difficult to convince countries to contribute soldiers.

Annan also proposed sending in human rights observers in the border area and creating a Lebanese-Israeli-UN commission to replace the one created by a 1949 armistice between the two countries.

The secretary general, according to the newspaper, hinted that if Lebanon did not accept the proposals, the UN Security Council would be hesitant to renew the mandate for UN troops in Lebanon, which is set to expire July 31.

Lebanon also said UN forces should not be deployed until Israel withdraws completely, including from the so-called Farms of Shebaa area on Mount Hermon, which the Israelis have held since 1967.

"Lebanon will consider the application of Resolution 425 incomplete until the Israeli army evacuates Shebaa," As-Safir said.

The United Nations told Lebanon to present proof of its claim to the area and said the Security Council could take up the matter as a last resort, the newspaper said.

Lebanese and UN officials contacted by AFP declined to comment on the report in As-Safir, which is usually well-informed.

The UN Interim Forces in Lebanon (UNIFIL) have been deployed on the border for 22 years, with the stated mission of assuring an Israeli retreat, re-establishing peace and helping the Lebanese government restore its authority over the area.

The force includes 4,500 troops from nine countries -- Fiji, Finland, France, Ghana, India, Ireland, Italy, Nepal and Poland.

On Thursday Lebanese officials handed Roed-Larsen an eight-point letter vowing Beirut's full cooperation with the UN for an "accurate and honest" implementation of the UN resolutions for the Israeli pullout.

Roed-Larsen said after his talks with Lebanese President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss that there was good reason to feel that the future looks far brighter.

But he admitted that "we have many complicated issues to address and many details to work through in the coming weeks."

 

Beirut makes case for Shebaa (15)

Lebanon submitted documents and maps on Friday to prove to UN experts that the Shebaa Farms were part of Lebanese territory before they were captured piecemeal by Israel over the past two decades.

The second half of the meeting between UN experts and government officials at Baabda Palace was attended by President  Emile Lahoud, Premier Salim Hoss and UN Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen. The presidents participation was said to have contributed highly to consolidating the Lebanese stand.

As Israel plans to withdraw its troops from the south, Beirut is demanding that the Jewish state relinquish the fertile Shebaa Farms.

The Lebanese delegation included the head of the Surete Generale, Major General Jamil al-Sayyed, and one of Hoss foreign-affairs advisers, Yehya Mahmasani. Among the UN delegates was topography expert Miklos Pinther, to whom the Lebanese presented seven arguments, supported by documents, to counter UN claims:

All ownership deeds in the various Shebaa Farms are Lebanese, issued and stamped by the Lebanese government.

A Cabinet decision was issued in 1948 saying that the state owned 3 million square meters in the farm of Machhad al-Tayr. The decision was made following judicial discord between Lebanon and the Sunni religious court. The decree contradicted a decision by the Sunni religious authority that described the land as religious endowment property. Proving that such a rift actually existed, the mufti of the republic, Sheikh Mohammed Qabbani, asserted on Friday that the Islamic authorities own 3 million square meters in the Shebaa Farms.

The Lebanese delegation refuted the Israeli argument that Shebaa was occupied in 1967 ­ that is, during the Arab-Israeli war ­ which means that a settlement on the issue would have to be part of UN Resolution 242. Beirut argued that the area of Bastara, within the Shebaa Farms, was invaded much later, in 1989, leading to the displacement of more than 30 families.

Lebanese officials asked that if the Shebaa Farms were considered Syrian or were the subject of conflict between Syria and Lebanon, why hadnt UN experts questioned Syria about it?

According to political sources, Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa asserted to Hoss that Syria was convinced that the Shebaa Farms were Lebanese territory. However, Syria would only express this stance if asked about the ownership of the disputed farms, the sources said.

The Lebanese delegation argued that even though Beirut did not make its claim until recently, that fact did not alter the fact that it was Lebanese land.

The Lebanese officials said that during the occupation, Lebanon avoided raising matters related to the Israeli border. These issues were left for when Israel would plan to fully withdraw, which did not occur until this year. When Israel started saying that it would pull out in accordance with Resolution 425, it became Beiruts right and duty to ask for every inch of Lebanese territory, the delegation argued.

This point was raised following remarks made by a senior official from UN forces in the Golan, who attended the meeting and argued that UN forces had always dealt with the Shebaa Farms as Syrian territory.

The delegation presented a military map in which the Shebaa Farms were included, contradicting maps UN experts have submitted that did not mention the farms. The military map was printed in 1966 on the basis of an earlier one published in 1945.

The delegation argued that a Lebanese-Syrian committee, formed in 1949, had officially recognized the Shebaa Farms as Lebanese territory. The delegation supported its account of the committees findings with official notes from the Syrian Foreign Ministry at that time.

Lebanon has warned that a failure to give back the farms, located in 15 villages, will be considered an incomplete withdrawal. Marching to a background of patriotic songs, drum beats and taped accounts of Shebaas annexation, protesters demanded that the United Nations force Israel to return the farms, which the Lebanese government claims were fenced off with barbed wire by Israel.

UN Resolution 425 means a complete withdrawal from Shebaa, said one of the banners brandished by Boy Scouts from Arqoub, one of the areas seized by Israel in 1967.

Human rights are raped in Arqoub. So, where are you America? another banner read, held high between a blanket of Lebanese flags.

The hour-long march, which was organized by the Committee for Descendants from Arqoub, set-off from the busy Barbir thoroughfare and proceeded to the UN House downtown, where Larsen was holding a press conference. The envoy did not meet the protesters, the majority of whom  represented the Arqoub Committee. Instead, UN representative, Saeed Shamseddine, was handed a memo addressed to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, in which the displaced citizens of Arqoub and Shebaa demanded that the international body observe Lebanons right to regain the Farms.

The United Nations, which is currently scrambling to implement Security Council Resolutions 425 and 426, is duty-bound to dislodge all Israeli occupation forces from all Lebanese occupied land, and in particular the Shebaa Farms and hamlets on Mount Hermon, said a statement by the committee.

Any implementation of the two UN Resolutions which overlooks our demands will only be interpreted as an Israeli troop redeployment, the statement added. A truck accompanied the demonstrators, its loudspeakers blaring an account of Israels seizure of Shebaa, which was reportedly turned into a Jewish colony housing the Falasha immigrants from Ethiopia in 1985.

This is the land of my ancestors. I am here to demand the return of my land, said Ali Wehbeh, 64, from Arqoub as he waved a copy of the title deed to his farm.

Marjayoun-Hasbaya MP Nazih Mansour affirmed that Shebaa would be regained by force if the international community fails to convince Israeli to hand back the territory.

What is taken by force, should be liberated by force, he told the gathering which chanted Allah Akbar as he praised the resistance movement.

 

IR martyrs laid to rest (17)

Hezbollah on Friday escorted two of its martyrs to their final resting place.  The Islamic Resistance had issued a statement announcing the martyrdom of Jalal Hassan Rammal and Hussein Mahdi Mohammed Ali who were killed during the clashes with the Israeli occupation forces in the evening of Wednesday May 3, 2000.

The enemy did not dare to confront the two Resistance men although they were in the territory it occupies.  Therefore, the occupation used its helicopters and warplanes to stage raids against them, the IR communiqué read.

 

Martyr Ali (Karbala), 32, born in Bidnayel (Beqaa), married with two children, joined IR in 1988, took part in several high military courses, progressed in several Jihad responsibilities, participated in scores of surveillance and qualitative operations against the occupation forces, was wounded several times, obtained encomium from Hezbollah's Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah.

 

Martyr Rammal, 35, born in Dwair (South), married with two children, joined IR when it first set out, took part in several high military courses, participated in scores of Jihad operations against the occupation forces, was wounded several times.
