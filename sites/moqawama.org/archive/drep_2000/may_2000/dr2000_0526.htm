Hizbullah chief lauds party discipline(1)
After fighting "Israel's" occupation for 15 years, Hizbullahs secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, was among the many who were surprised by the Jewish states abrupt withdrawal.
We expected the withdrawal in a month, not now, he told a group of journalists on Thursday.
Smiling proudly, the resistance leader insisted that the struggle was not yet over, citing the need for "Israel" to return the Shibaa Farms.
We do not consider the withdrawal to be a full withdrawal as long as there is Lebanese land occupied and as long as there are still prisoners in "Israel", he said, adding that Hizbullahs policy of retaliating against "Israel" with Katyushas for Lebanese civilian deaths would not change.
Nasrallah explained that the decision to storm the southern villages, a move that accelerated the collapse of the Israeli-allied militia and the hasty withdrawal, had been Hizbullahs.
We consult with others but were the ones to take the decision, he said.
Nasrallah also said that the Lebanese government was responsible for security. We do not take control of any area. The members of Hizbullah who are in the area are from that area and they have returned to their homes, he said, reiterating that the party had no intention of replacing the government in security or judiciary matters.
Weapons left behind at the border by the retreating "Israelis" would be gathered and displayed in a museum honoring the resistance movements victory, he said. We dont intend to keep the arms we collected from the area. We dont need them.
Nasrallah also played down reports of looting in the villages. While he admitted that there had been a few isolated incidents, he said the reports had been widely exaggerated.
The thefts, he quipped, probably matched that of a United States city in one day.
The secretary-general praised the conduct of people during the liberation, saying that the worlds fears of violence were misplaced. Nobody was even slapped in the face, he said.
Nasrallah also said that Lebanons victory should serve as a model for the Palestinians, revealing the advantages of resistance rather than negotiation and capitulation.
Asked about the future of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon and whether he would support their fighting for their homeland, Nasrallah said: They can do whatever they want, no one can stop them, whether Ill encourage them, I havent (had time to) think about it yet.

Jubilation as Iran's FM tours Lebanese-"Israeli" border(4)

"Iranian Foreign Minister Kamal Kharazi used a visit to the Lebanese-"Israeli" border Thursday to stress the importance of unity between Muslims and Christians after the hasty end of "Israel's" 22-year occupation of southern Lebanon.

Thousands of people in this border town gave Kharazi a jubilant welcome as he made what was virtually a victory tour through the south.

They brandished the flag of Lebanon's Hizbullah movement, which spearheaded efforts to force "Israel" out, chanted slogans and invoked blessings on the Islamic Republic's founder, Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, Kharazi was driven along a road running parallel with the border, which was marked by coils of barbed wire, and got out of the car several times to do some sightseeing.

His guide was Hizbullah's chief official for southern Lebanon, Sheikh Nabil Qauq, who pointed out landmarks and identified "Israeli" villages, just a few kilometers (miles) away.

It was the first visit to south Lebanon by a top official from the Islamic republic of Iran, founded in 1979 a year after the occupation began. Escalating attacks by Hizbullah prompted the collapse of "Israel's" allies in southern Lebanon and a final pullback across the border early Wednesday.

Speaking to journalists later, Kharazi stressed the importance of "solidarity between Christians and Muslims, Shiites and Sunni," which "will consolidate the victory of the Lebanese people."

"I visited Muslim and Christian villages ... which lived under occupation to show the importance of this unity," he said.

He had expressed the same theme in a statement on arrival in Beirut, saying: "the Lebanese people achieved its victory through unity, and it must preserve unity between the various religious faiths."

Iran, he promised, would pursue "its efforts to consolidate this solidarity."

Qauq, addressing a crowd in Bent Jbeil, praised Iran for the help it had given Hizbullah over the years. Tehran, along with Damascus, has been the group's main backer.

"This is a victory for Lebanon, Syria and Iran, which has been a full partner in particularly difficult circumstances," he said.

"Our victory is not the last and the defeat of the enemy ("Israel") is not the last," he said.

"Other victories await us. The resistance will only end with the total liberation of Shibaa," he said, referring to an area on the cusp of Lebanon, Syria and "Israel", which "Israel" says it seized from Syria in 1967 and is not covered by the 1978 UN resolution which called for "Israel" to withdraw from the whole of Lebanon.

Kharazi passed on the congratulations of Iran's leaders and people, and paid homage to those who had given their lives in the resistance.

He praised Lebanese President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss, who, he said, had given "full approval" to the resistance, and also praised Syria for "backing the resistance of the Lebanese people."

Kharazi later returned to Beirut, where he had talks with Lahoud, which he described as "excellent."

Hizbullah guerrillas swept through south Lebanon after the precipitate "Israeli" withdrawal but have repeatedly insisted they have no plans to control the region, saying that is the responsibility of the Lebanese government.

Kharazi will head for Damascus on Friday for talks with Syrian leaders, the Iranian foreign ministry said.

Hizbullah takes its war booty towards Baalback(8)

Hizbullah moved some of its war booty to Baalback in the Syrian-controlled east of the country Thursday, including some 15 armoured cars abandoned in the south by the "Israeli" army and its ally.

Roads jammed as thousands flock to south
Villages gridlocked but holiday atmosphere permeates the air(9)

The heat did little to deter curious Lebanese from exploring the former occupation zone on Thursday, as tens of thousands of people took advantage of the national holiday to mark liberation, pouring into the south and creating miles of gridlocked traffic.
Vehicles crammed village roads leading into the zone while the country celebrated its liberation and resistance holiday. Those same roads only a few days ago were highly dangerous areas.
Former Israeli-proxy militia and positions stood beyond. For the first time in 22 years, they were empty.
No guns threatened villagers any more. Many stood at their doorsteps watching the traffic with amusement. Some yelled out their welcomes, never having seen their area so crowded. Still, the small villages were not equipped to cope with so many cars and a few harried-looking policemen tried in vain to put order to the chaos.
But nobody seemed to mind, as peoples shouts of joy remained strong. Hizbullah and Amal fighters flew their flags high, both getting along amiably.
Once in the zone, many visitors got lost. People yelled out to each other, asking for directions. A few had maps. Finally, some Hizbullah and Amal fighters stood on various cross points giving directions.
Abandoned militia tanks along the road attracted much attention as visitors, young and old, climbed on top and insisted on having their pictures taken. Very few seemed to mind the numerous pot holes in the roads.
In one section, it seemed like the militia had been in the process of fixing a huge hole in one street. A bulldozer stood abandoned.
In Debbine, in the qaza of Marjeyoun, a few people were searching the cemetery for the graves of long-buried relatives. Several graves already had fresh flowers on them. Three young women were reading the Koran beside one grave.
Our grandfather died 11 years ago, said 21-year-old Suzanne Yassine. We just arrived today to lay flowers on his grave. The women were originally from Debbine.
Now we feel like a bird who was in cage and its door has been opened, said Sabah Yassine.
Nearby, 52-year-old Abed Rashid had just located his parents graves. Hes been waiting to lay flowers on them for 23 years. Two years ago, my brother passed away in Beirut, he said. And I couldnt even attend his funeral. The Red Cross brought him to the village.
Rashids excitement seemed somewhat subdued. Im happy of course, he said. But Ive found that my house was demolished by the "Israelis". Not much is left there.
Not too far away, in the village of Khiam, an elderly man was welcoming his son home. Hassan Ibrahim, 48, had not been back to the family house in 24 years.
We fled in 1976 when collaborators first came to the area. More than 70 people were killed here. Later, the "Israelis" took people to the village square and shot them, he said.
My parents went back, but I didnt. I was scared for myself and my sons. His 70-year-old father, Mohsen, stood near him, his eyes bright and gleaming.
Already a small handwritten sign in Khiam pointed the way to the notorious prison

 Its been 15 years since we set foot in our village, said Ali Jouny, 28. The men were lawyers from Beirut and part of the resistance. Is that surprising? asked Jouny. There are also doctors with us.
The weapons in their hands had been left behind by the militia at the prison. Well turn them over to the government, said Ashraf Kallash, 31. This is nothing. We have tanks and much heavier weapons that we found.
They were on their way to free the Khiam prisoners on Tuesday but had to go through Rashaya to reach here.
But the residents went to the prison and freed everybody. They didnt even have weapons. We arrived later, said Jouny.
In the village of Taibeh, Hassan Farhat had his 16-year old daughter in tow. Were looking for our olive grove, he said. Farhat himself planted the trees 24 years ago. I want to see what happened to them.
The grove was soon found. Look, Sukainah, he called out to his daughter. There it is. The trees had survived and had blossomed.

Shibaa residents hope "Israelis" will give them their land back(12)

" Residents of the Lebanese village of Shibaa looked expectantly over to the nearby "Israeli"-occupied area known as the farms Thursday, hoping "Israel's" destruction of one of its fortifications in the disputed zone was a sign that a pullout there too was imminent.

A mass of large blackened debris spread over a hundred square metres (yards) was all that remained of Astra position dynamited late Wednesday in the mountainous area of the Shibaa farms, claimed by Lebanon.

The remains of the "Israeli" fort at an altitude of 1,600 metres (5,300 feet) on the slopes of Mount Hermon could be clearly seen from the position of the Indian contingent of the United Nations interim force in Lebanon, UNIFIL.

A road leads from the bunker, destroyed then abandoned by the "Israelis" who have occupied the area since the 1967 war, and then disappears into the grey, rocky and barren hillside, where a cold breeze blows, despite the hot sun.

Behind it is a fence sealing off the rest of the captured territory.

Hassan Farhat, a resident of Shibaa, which was evacuated by the "Israelis" Wednesday in their hurried departure from southern Lebanon, said he was woken up around 9:30 p.m. by a "very loud explosion," coming from the position just four kilometres (2.5 miles) away as the crow flies.

"We thought Hizbullah artillery was shelling the "Israelis"," as had happened Sunday and Monday, when it bombarded Astra and a nearby spy station for the first time.

The shelling came as "Israel's" proxy militia in southern Lebanon cracked and then collapsed, forcing the "Israeli" occupation forces to quit the territory in a little over 24 hours.

As Hassan spoke, dozens of armed Hizbullah militants trailed through the streets of Shibaa, waving flags to celebrate victory.

"This morning," said Hassan, "we discovered that the "Israelis" had blown up their position, perhaps the first to be set up in 1967 in the occupied farms."

"Israel" intimated Thursday that the destruction of Astra heralded the handover to Lebanon of parts of the Shibaa farms designated on UN maps as Lebanese territory. It said it would also abandon two other positions.

The fence is likely to be moved back, as according to an officer of the UN observer corps monitoring the truce which ended fighting between "Israel" and its Arab neighbours in 1949, it encroaches in parts on Lebanese territory.

But the rest of the land claimed by Lebanon will remain prohibited and inaccessible, as "Israel" says it was seized from Syria and its fate must be decided in negotiations with Damascus.

The mayor of Shibaa, Assad Farhat, who owns several hectares (acres) of olive groves in the disputed area, told AFP that "the "Israelis" have burned a million olive groves in the farm area, destroyed 500 houses and buried sources of water."

He said "these destructions are intended to keep us from our lands."

"But today," he said, "we keep a ray of hope of being able to return there after hearing on the radio that ("Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud) Barak is ready to give up part."

The area claimed by Beirut covers some 250 square kilometres (100 square miles) contains the equivalent of some 14 farms, and has four important water sources that feed into the Jordan basin and the Sea of Galilee.

From a promontory next to Shibaa, the local people can see the biggest military spy station in the Middle East set up by the "Israelis" at an altitude of 2,000 metres. It can "see" right up to Syria's border with Turkey.

Next to the spy station the "Israeli" army has set up an artillery position on the land of another Lebanese farm, according to the mayor of Shibaa.

Lower down "Israeli" bulldozers could be seen piling up earthworks.

The Lebanese claim is being looked into by UN envoy Terje Roed-Larsen, currently in Beirut on a mission which includes determining if the "Israeli" military pullout of southern Lebanon is complete.

"Israeli" withdrawal from Lebanon not enough for security: Syrian official(17)

" A Syrian official said Thursday it was up to the Hizbullah whether it pursues their war against "Israel" over land they say is still to be recovered, while warning that the "Israeli" withdrawal from southern Lebanon was not enough.
