Daily Report

May Friday 12-2000:


Introduction

President Emile Lahoud reiterated that "there will be no amnesty for those who collaborated with the "Israeli" enemy against their people and homeland".

In the meantime, Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch warned Thursday of "serious fears" for the fate of the Lebanese unlawfully detained by "Israel" after the Jewish state's planned pullout from southern Lebanon.

However, the "Israeli" settlers occupying the northern Palestine staged a general strike in light of the plans to bring the border fence very close to their homes calling upon enemy's Prime Minister Ehud Barak to step down for his failure to provide them with adequate security.


Lahoud: no amnesty for the collaborators(1)

"President Emile Lahoud on Thursday made a distinction between the treatment of ordinary residents of the "Israeli"-occupied zone and the collaborators once "Israel" withdraws from the south.
The overwhelming majority of the occupied zones residents tolerated with steadfastness the tragic circumstances imposed by the occupation, he said. These people will receive special attention from the government.
On the other hand, a small minority of residents fought on the side of the occupation, targeting citizens inside and outside the occupied zone. They violated the law and will face trial, he said, according to a statement from the Presidential Palace.
There will be no amnesty for those who collaborated with the enemy against their people and homeland, Lahoud warned.
Visitors who met the president on Thursday quoted him as saying that the appeal from clemency for the "Israeli"-allied militiamen which their commander, Antoine Lahd, made earlier this week was instigated by "Israel".
However, Lahoud noted that the militiamen who handed themselves over to the government after the withdrawal from Jezzine had received fair trials and were being released after the completion of their sentences.
Lahoud warned "Israel" against creating crises following a pullout, because this would jeopardize the stability of its northern border, something that would run contrary to "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Baraks assurances to his people that the withdrawal would bring an end to their problems in Lebanon.

Alarm raised over Khiam

Amnesty International and Human Rights Watchdog, HRW fear for Lebanese detainees after "Israel" pullout warned on Thursday that the imminent withdrawal of "Israeli" forces from the south could put detainees in Khiam prison in grave danger.
Were also at a critical point in this cause because of legislation submitted a few days ago by "Israel's" Ministry of Interior to the attorney-general that would name Dirani and Obeid illegal combatants and keep them in "Israel", Virginia Sherry, associate director of Human Rights Watch, told families of detainees at a press conference.
Human Rights Watch is scared the legislation will be used to legalize the taking of Khiam prisoners into "Israel", she said.
Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, detained in 1989, and Mustafa Dirani, detained in 1994, are being held to pressure Hizbullah to provide information about "Israeli" airman Ron Arad, who disappeared after being shot down over Lebanon in 1986.
Sources told The Daily Star that while an attempt may be made to transfer these prisoners to "Israel", the International Committee of the Red Cross is playing a major role in high-level negotiations to ensure the release of Khiam detainees ahead of the withdrawal. This is expected to take place in stages over the next month.
The coming month will also see a pivotal decision handed down by the "Israeli" High Court on a case filed more than a year ago by "Israeli" human rights lawyers for 25 Khiam prisoners.
The key issue is  who holds legal responsibility for the detainees, said Sherry.
While the Barak government has argued that they are the "Israeli"-proxy militia's responsibility, HRW argues that "Israel", as the occupying power, is responsible under international law for the illegal detention and torture of the detainees, as well as for their protection during the pullout.
Also speaking at the conference, Elizabeth Hodgkin of Amnesty International stressed the importance of publicizing human-rights violations(2)
"We have serious fears for the fate of the Lebanese detainees after the "Israeli" withdrawal," Elizabeth Hodgkin, representative of the London-based Amnesty International, said in a press conference here.

"We call for the release of all Lebanese detained in concern with the conflict, whether they were sentenced or not and whether they are held in jails in "Israel" or in the Khiam prison in the occupied border zone in south Lebanon," she said.

Hodgkin was speaking in a joint conference with Virginia Sherry.

Hodgkin warned against plans by the "Israeli" interior ministry to draft a law to legalise the detention of prisoners without charge or trial, by defining them as "non-legal fighters".

"This is against all international laws and conventions. It does not exist anywhere else in the world," said Hodgkin.

The draft law was mainly meant to maintain the detention of Islamic leaders Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani.

"The administrative detention of Dirani and Obeid will expire later this month, and we are afraid that this draft law would be passed before then in order to maintain their detention," said Hodgkin.

Sherry said HRW was "afraid" that such a law could also permit the transfer of detainees from the Khiam jail into "Israel".

She also voiced fears that the detainees in Khiam could be left in "chaos or in a military vacuum" when "Israel" withdraws from the occupied zone and if its allied militia remains there.

There are 20 Lebanese detained in "Israel" and 144 others in the Khiam jail, run by the militia under "Israeli" supervision. Most have been held without charge or trial for several years.

Both HRW and Amnesty have repeatedly denounced cases of torture and ill-treatment of Lebanese in "Israeli" jails(3)

"Umm Ali Yassin, the mother of Anwar Yassin who has been detained in "Israel" on a 30-year sentence since 1987, thanked both representatives of the international organizations for caring and continuously lobbying for her son. We ask you to complete our happiness by helping us release Dirani, Obeid and all others still in prison.
Meanwhile on Thursday, a 61-year-old taxi driver was arrested by militiamen and taken to Khiam.
Mohammed Ali Kharroubi, who worked between Nabatieh and his hometown Arnoun, was summoned by the militia as he waited for fares near his village.
He was detained for two hours before being taken to Khiam"(4)


New weapons delivery puts "Israeli" militia disarmament in doubt(5)

""Israel" handed the "Israeli"-proxy militia another long-range 160mm mortar on Thursday, defying speculation that the militias heavy weapons would be reclaimed by the withdrawing "Israeli" troops.
The mortar was strapped to a tank chassis and carried by a transporter vehicle from the border to a frontline position in Zimraya in the western Beqaa, witnesses said.
The militia is believed to have 20-25 160mm mortars, along with dozens of 120mm and 81mm mortars, which are kept in frontline outposts.
The larger-caliber 160mm versions used by the collaborators are usually fixed to tanks which serve as makeshift mobile artillery platforms, according to sources inside the zone. The militia is also equipped with about six 130mm artillery guns in four batteries located at Mohaibib in the central sector, Marjeyoun and Hasbaya in the eastern sector and Tair Harfa in the western sector.
"Israel" has been charged by the United Nations with ensuring that these weapons do not remain in the hands of the militia following the withdrawal. UNIFILs plans to deploy up to the border could be jeopardized if the area continues to be controlled by a well-armed militia.
The "Israeli" agent commander for the eastern sector, Nabih Abu Rifai, reportedly said on Thursday that the militia was expecting to receive further supplies of heavy weapons and ammunition in coming weeks. "The armaments would help the militia defend the border zone following the departure of the "Israelis"", he said.

Hoss praises Aramta residents(6)

"Prime Minister Salim Hoss congratulated residents of the newly liberated southern village of Aramta on Thursday for regaining their freedom after 18 years of "Israeli" occupation.
I extend my congratulations to the people of Aramta who withstood the occupation and endured persecution at the hands of the "Israeli" troops for 18 years, Hoss said in a statement.
The collaborators abandoned and demolished the Aramta outpost on Tuesday after Hizbullah forces briefly seized the position in an April 28 offensive that left three militiamen dead.
We await the day when our happiness will be complete after a total "Israeli" withdrawal takes place to the internationally recognized border, including the Shibaa Farms, Hoss added.
The Aramta outpost is the second to go down this week after the militia vacated the Shayar Azour position in the early hours of Monday.
The "Israeli" agent withdrew from the strategic Sojod outpost in February after successful resistance hit-and-run attacks.
Hoss, however, cautioned Aramtas residents against succumbing to dissenting voices that were trying to dampen the joy of liberation.
We urge you to stop listening to troublemakers who want to stir up dissension among your ranks and distract you from savoring the joy of victory, he said.
"The premier assured the residents that the government would secure stability in the region and help them in whatever way it can".


Workers demo becomes voice of unity(7)

"Some 400 people took to the capitals streets on Thursday to denounce the latest "Israeli" air raid attacks on Lebanon, changing the theme of a demonstration which was originally planned to protest socio-economic conditions.
The march, organized by the General Labor Confederation, was attended by loyalists from all of the countrys political parties. The GLC had originally called for a demonstration on May 16 to protest the countrys growing economic difficulties. But after last weeks "Israeli" air strikes on power plants in Metn and north Lebanon the theme was transformed to express solidarity with the government during this critical phase.
The protesters gathered at Barbir at 4pm and marched to the UN House in Riyad Solh, 3 kilometers from the starting point, chanting slogans, waving Lebanese flags and banners that represented the political parties.
The road from Barbir to Riyad Solh was closed by policemen who escorted protesters amid patriotic songs that blared from loudspeakers.
Representatives from most Lebanese parties took part in the demonstration, in addition to union groups who arrived from around the country. The Communist Party, the Lebanese Resistance Brigades and Al-Jamaa Al-Islamia fielded that largest groups of participants.
Chouf MP Zaher Khatib and Beirut MP Jamil Shammas joined the protest, marching in the front row alongside GLC President Elias Abu Rizk and other union leaders.
Public drivers drove their buses and mini-vans to the UN house to express their support for the protest.
Banners praised the resistance. One of them said that the resistance proved that "Israel" was a paper tiger, and another held by members of al-Jamaa al-Islamia read in Hebrew and Arabic: We are coming to kill you, in reference to the "Israelis".
Phalange Party Vice-President Rashad Salameh called on all Lebanese parties to declare the day when "Israel" withdraws from the occupied south a national day to be celebrated every year. He suggested naming the occasion Lebanons victory through the resistance and the resistances victory for Lebanon.
Salameh, who spoke on behalf of all parties, praised the GLCs decision to replace the labor demonstration with a national protest against the "Israeli" attacks.
He said the step reflected the utmost of awareness especially during this time.
 Abu Rizk praised the resistance, which he said drove "Israel" to withdraw because of the heavy losses incurred by the Jewish state.
But he said that victory would not be complete unless the Shibaa Farms were returned to the Lebanese government.
We wont be afraid from now on. Neither the bombs nor the "Israeli"-American weapons will scare us, he said.


"Israeli" settlers stage protests over Lebanon withdrawal

" Settlers of the northern "Israeli" border town set tyres ablaze to protest at the government's planned withdrawal from Lebanon which they fear will leave them vulnerable to attack.

Dozens of people joined a demonstration in the streets of Metullah, which abuts the border with Lebanon, some of them blocking army bulldozers making preparations for the pullout.

"The border will pass only metres from us, putting our security at risk," Koby Katz, mayor of the town of Metullah, which abuts the border, told "Israeli" radio.

The town's protest followed a flare-up of cross border violence last week, including Hizbullah rocket attacks on northern "Israel" which killed one soldier and wounded several dozen other settlers.

"If the border passes through here we'll be right in the line of fire of light weapons," one unidentified woman protestor said.

"Israel" has pledged to pull out from southern Lebanon by July after a 22-year occupation which has cost the lives of hundreds of its soldiers, but settlers of the northern border have expressed fears for their safety.

In particular, settlers of Metullah are angry at the installation of a new army base on land that had been earmarked for a new housing development.

But Deputy War Minister Ephraim Sneh defended the plans, saying the site was chosen for strategic reasons and that compensation had been offered.

"We will not accept any hostile elements taking up positions in the evacuated sectors," he said.

Prime Minister Ehud Barak has proposed a 450 million dollar plan to strengthen the frontline communities which is due to be discussed by his cabinet on Sunday"(8)

"The protest also took a political tone, with protesters calling upon Prime Minister/War Minister Ehud Barak to step down for his failure to provide them with adequate security.

Metulla settlers fear Hizbullah guerillas will have an easy time firing rockets at them from across the border following the troop withdrawal. They also fear fighters will be able to infiltrate directly into "Israel" and they are also afraid they will be injured even when Hizbullah fires at military targets.

Hizbullah said they will bomb each military target and in town, we have three military targets. It cannot be that our children will wake up in the morning as a target of Hizbullah, stated Amir Belzer, Metullas deputy mayor(9)

Military Operations(11)

The IR team of martyrs Zoheir Ali Tabatabai and Ali Noureddine attacked, at 16:00, Bir Kallab outpost with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades. Direct and certain hits were scored, the mujahedeen reported.

Also, the IR squad of martyrs Suleiman Atef Yehia and Nasser Ahmad Al-Laqis targeted in a violent and concentrated manner, at 23:15, a Lahdi engineering force as it was beefing up Mashaaroun outpost. The militants, who used the rocket-propelled grenades, were able to chalk up direct goals.

Fifteen minutes later, the IR group of martyrs Hussein Ali Mefleh and Hussein Mahmoud Al-Shehaini rushed upon a Lahdi motorized force as it was evacuating Mashaaroun outpost. The mujahedeen, who used the rocket-propelled grenades, managed to score direct goals.

For its part, Amal Movement attacked, at 14:40, 19:15 and 21:55, the positions of Baraasheit, Taibeh and Mohaisbat respectively with the 107mm rocket-propelled grenades and artillery shells.
