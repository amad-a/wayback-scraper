Lebanese family kicked out of israel-held zone: police (AFP)
SIDON, Lebanon, April 30 (AFP) - A Lebanese couple and their four young children were expelled Sunday from the Israeli-occupied zone in southern Lebanon, police said.
Yussef Mussa Raslan, 35, his wife Ward and their children Mohammed, 5, Fatima, 3, Zeinab, 2, and Mussa, two months, went to the Kfar Tebnit gate, one of five checkpoints linking the zone with the rest of Lebanon, police said.
Members of IsraelsSouth Lebanon Army militia ally had burst into the family's Taibeh village home and ordered them to leave the zone, police added.
Hundreds of people have been expelled from the zone since 1995, according to the US-based Human Rights Watch.
The group says israel is violating the Geneva conventions and committing a war crime by expelling civilians from their villages in southern Lebanon. 

Syria says israel trying to deceive with Lebanon withdrawal  (AFP)
Radio Damascus charged Saturday that israel was trying to pull the wool over the eyes of the United Nations and major powers by vowing to withdraw unconditionally from southern Lebanon by July 7.
"By raising the implementation of (UN) Security Council Resolution 425, israel wants to lead the great powers and the United Nations astray," the official Syrian radio station said in its daily editorial.
The resolution, approved in 1978, calls for israel to withdraw immediately and unconditionally from Lebanon, where it occupies a "security zone" just north of their border.
"Discussing the deceptive pull out enables israel to sneak out of peace... because that way israel covers up its rejection of a just and comprehensive peace, UN resolutions and the principle of trading land for peace," it said.
Syria's own price for peace with israel is the return of the Golan Heights, which the Jewish state captured during the 1967 Arab-Israeli war. But the two sides have been unable to agree on where their border should lie.
The official Ath-Thawra newspaper said that Israelsdecision to pull out of Lebanon is a "new attempt to implement the 'Lebanon first' proposal made four years ago by former Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu in order to separate the Syrian and Lebanese tracks."
The paper also predicted that israel "will not withdraw unconditionally to the international border" with Lebanon.
Syrian-Israeli negotiations broke off in January. And the Israeli government decided in March to withdraw from Lebanon with or without accords with Beirut and Damascus.
