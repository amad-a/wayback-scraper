Beirut sends new Shibaa file to Annan
Hoss says maps and documents support land claim(1)

"Lebanon sent the United Nations new documents on Friday in a bid to prove the Lebanese identity of the Shibaa Farms ahead of a Security Council meeting on "Israel's" planned withdrawal from the south.
Among the documents were three memorandums received by the Lebanese authorities from the Syrian government between 1944 and 1972.
These include a 1961 Foreign Ministry statement containing excerpts from a report issued by a joint Lebanese-Syrian committee that studied the situat-
ion of the borders between the two countries.
They also include an official complaint filed by Beirut with Damascus in 1944, after a Syrian team surveyed the land of several farms in Shibaa and included them on Syrian territory. The joint Syrian-Lebanese committee contended at the time that the misunderstanding was due to a printing mistake on the map used by the Syrian team.
Syrias Foreign Ministry replied in 1946, saying that the work of the team was purely technical and did not aim at modifying the borders or annexing farms.
These documents include a confirmation from Syrias Foreign Ministry to its Lebanese counterpart that these farms are Lebanese, Prime Minister Salim Hoss said in a statement.
According to the statement, Syrian officials recognize that a mistake had occurred and that the Shibaa Farms were not Syrian territory, but were in fact on Lebanese soil.
Hoss, who is also minister for foreign affairs, sent the new documents to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, along with a map which shows that the Shibaa Farms are unequivocally on Lebanese territory. He then followed up his submission with a telephone call to Annan.
The map is different from one presented to the media by Speaker Nabih Berri last Wednesday. According to government sources, Hoss map is clearer and unchallengable.
The map presented by Berri was greeted with skepticism in political and diplomatic circles as it left room for interpretation as to the exact location of the farms. Parliamentary sources said Washington admitted that the map was a US document issued by the Pentagon, but argued that it was not clear whether the Shibaa Farms were inside Lebanons border or on Syrian land.
The new documents will be added to a long list presented previously by Lebanese authorities to the United Nations.
"The list includes ownership deeds of the Shibaa Farms issued by the Lebanese government, as well an exchange of notes between the Syrian and Lebanese governments, in which the Lebanese identity of the farms is clearly stated".


Obeid: "Israeli" pullout must include Shibaa
Prime minister heads home after supporting Beirut(2)

"Lebanon and Egypt issued a joint call on Friday for a full "Israeli" pullout from the south.
After the fourth annual meeting of the Higher Lebanese-Egyptian Joint Commission, Prime Minister Salim Hoss and his Egyptian counterpart, Atef Obeid, said in a communiqué that the "Israeli" retreat must be fully in line with United Nations Security Council Resolution 425 and must include the Shibaa Farms.
Shibaa, located on the slopes of Mount Hermon, was occupied by "Israel" in 1967, unlike south Lebanon which has been held since 1978, but Beirut says it must be abandoned as well, although "Israel" claims the area is Syrian.
The withdrawal is an important development, a victory for Lebanon which sees its land liberated and a step which serves Arab interests, the communiqué said.
On Wednesday, Egyptian Information Minister Safwat Sharif, a member of Obeids delegation, said that the dispute over Shibaa should be settled in Lebanese-"Israeli" negotiations following the Jewish states proposed pullout.
"But Obeid said on Thursday that Egypt supports Lebanons efforts to regain its sovereignty over all of its territories, including the Shibaa Farms.

Desertions from pro-"Israeli" militia Gather Pace(3)

" Eight members of the pro-"Israeli" militia deserted the militia Friday and handed themselves in to anti-"Israeli" groups, bringing the number of desertions to 19 since Monday.

An agent for Lahdi intelligence and two former militia members turned themselves into Hizbullah, said in communiqué.

It said they were the "informer" Ali Mahmoud Ollaik has worked since 1987 "under orders of the Zionist officer known as Badr," the former militia member Shafic Aillayq, who was demobilized in 1999 at the age of 19 after serving six years at the heart of the militia, and Ali Ahmad Shahla, demobilized in 1993 after losing his right hand in an attack on his patrol.

Another agent of militia intelligence, Shawqi Karim, from Deir Siriane in the occupied zone, turned himself into locals on the edge of the eastern sector of the zone, according to the official news agency ANI.

Another militiaman from the Mashaaroun position dismantled last week turned himself in to Amal Thursday, seven surrendered to Amal and Hizbullah Wednesday, and three others abandoned their posts Monday and gave themselves up to the two groups fighting to end 22 years of "Israeli" occupation of southern Lebanon.

Four militiamen, who all come from the village of Ribbit Thlathine in the occupied zone, surrendered to a position of Amal movement on the edge of the central sector of the zone occupied by "Israel", Amal said in a communiqué.

Nayef, Ali and Hussein Barakat, and Ahmad Faqih, aged from 18 to 30, have served two and a quarter years in the "Israeli" agents positions at Markaba, Mohaisbat and Qssair, said the communiqué, which also gave the service numbers of the four.

At the beginning of April, six militiamen deserted in less than a week.

Interior Minister Michel Murr said Friday that deserters should be handed over to the Lebanese army's intelligence services.

They will contact Hizbullah and Amal with that aim, and will place the deserters into the hands of Lebanese justice, Murr said in statements appearing in the press.

The increasing speed of desertions at the heart of the collaborators coincides with the start of the "Israeli" pullout due to be completed by July 7, with the breakup of the militia now a serious possibility.

According to figures from Lebanon's security services, the "Israeli"-proxy militia, created, financed and trained by "Israel", today numbers between 2,400 and 2,500 militiamen against 2,800 in 1999.

The reduction in its influence is due to desertions and emigration, particularly to Canada and Australia.

Militia spokesman Raymond Abu Mrad told AFP Wednesday that the militia will stay on in southern Lebanon after "Israel's" withdrawal, braving attacks by Hizbullah guerrillas until Beirut agrees they are not traitors.

Abu Mrad said the 2,500 strong force would still hold a strip along the border with "Israel", smaller than the so-called "security zone" which "Israel" says it will abandon by July after a 22-year occupation".


Militia's losses in two days: 2 killed, 6 wounded(4)

Two militiamen were killed and six others were wounded over Thursday and Friday.

Lebanon will not deploy army in south after "Israeli" retreat: Minister(5)

" Lebanese Interior Minister Michel Murr said Friday that Lebanon will not send its army into the southern part of the country when the "Israelis" leave and that Beirut's main presence in the region will be the police and administrators.

In a published statement, Murr said Beirut would treat southern Lebanon the same way it dealt with Jezzine, which until last June was controlled by the militia allied with "Israel".

"The state will be present in the localities of southern Lebanon like it was in Jezzine, with internal security forces, prefects, under-prefects and technical services," Murr said. "Administration offices that are already there will be reopened."

The minister met Thursday evening with Lebanon's Central Security Council, which includes the heads of Lebanon's various security services. The meeting was attended by Defense Minister Ghazi Zaaiter and General Roustom Ghazalay, a representative of the Syrian army in Lebanon.

"The militia, under "Israeli" supervision, has for 14 years controlled an 850 square kilometer (340 square mile) area of southern Lebanon, which "Israel" says it will leave by July 7."


"Israel's" militia must be disbanded if south to have peace after withdrawal(6)

"The "Israeli"-proxy militia must be disbanded ­ by force if necessary ­ to avert further bloodshed in the south following the departure of the "Israelis".
The militia appears determined to continue functioning as a military entity after their "Israeli" allies have retreated behind the border due to fears of a violent aftermath to the occupation.
Many militiamen are prepared to accept the penalty for being on the losing side and would turn themselves over to the authorities, stand trial, and spend a period of time in jail.
" "Israel's" withdrawal is almost over and the next act in the south Lebanon drama is about to begin. There are apparently only 120 "Israeli" soldiers remaining in Lebanon. Half of them are located in the "Israeli" Armys forward posts at Beaufort Castle, Dabsheh, Aishieh and Rihan. The rest are stationed in outposts along the border.
With the departure of the "Israelis", the militia is becoming more assertive. New checkpoints on the edges of villages are springing up and ­ in what could be a foretaste of future relations between the militia and UNIFIL ­ the militia has restricted access to all UN vehicles at the Hamra crossing south of Tyre.
On Thursday, the "Israeli" agent informed UNIFIL that they would shoot at any vehicle attempting to pass through the Hamra crossing. The crossing was partially opened to UN traffic on Friday, with vehicles allowed to pass in either direction at three separate times during the day.
The collaborators cannot continue to hold the present perimeters of the occupation zone without "Israeli" support. The Shiite element of the militia will probably collapse in the wake of the withdrawal, as indicated by the growing number of defections.
In this event, the militia is expected to fall back to the lines of the so-called Haddad enclave, the area under the control of the militia's founder in the 1970s. The enclave centered on three clusters of Christian villages: Alma Shaab in the western sector; Rmeish, Dibil and Ain Ibl in the same sector; Marjeyoun and Qlayaa in the eastern sector. To these three original enclaves will be added a fourth: Hasbaya.
"Hizbullah would continue to launch attacks on the new village-based militias. Without the restraining influence of "Israel", the militia will have few qualms about retaliating with artillery fire into populated areas. UNIFIL and the Lebanese Army would stay out of the area.
Continued fighting in south Lebanon would almost certainly spill over into "Israel", thus inviting "Israeli" warplanes to return to the skies above Beirut.
This gloomy scenario can be thwarted, however, if there is a determined effort to dismantle the "Israeli" agents. With no militia in place, Hizbullah has no one left to fight. Contrary to the fears of the zones residents, Hizbullah is not going to jeopardize the prestige it has gained spearheading the resistance by slaughtering anyone perceived to have been a collaborator with the "Israelis". Instead, Hizbullah is more likely to declare victory and prepare for the parliamentary elections slated for July and August.
The absence of the collaborators will permit a reinforced UNIFIL to deploy up to the border. The government is unlikely to exacerbate the fears of the residents by treating those former militiamen that remain in Lebanon with undue severity. Some ministers have hinted that the militiamen will receive the same treatment as their comrades in Jezzine.
The dilemma over the militiamen has already forced the UN to postpone to next week a briefing by UN special envoy Terje Roed-Larsen to the Security Council.
"Israel" has hoped that the Lebanese government or the UN would shoulder the problem, having apparently planned nothing for the collaborators other than to offer vague promises.
"The Security Council is expected to formally demand that "Israel" dismantle the militia. "Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak seems determined to ensure that the withdrawal is coordinated with the UN. But whether Barak will order his troops to strip their allies of their weapons before retiring remains to be seen".


"Israeli" air raid, naval attack and artillery exchanges in southern Lebanon

"The "Israeli" air force bombarded the Iqleem al-Toffah in southern Lebanon, which also came under artillery attack from the "Israeli"-occupied zone, Lebanese police reported.

"Israeli" fighter bombers fired two air-to-surface missiles mid-afternoon on a valley separating Iqleem al-Toffah from the neighboring area of Nabatieh, they said.

Around 100 155mm artillery and tank shells fell on valleys along the edge of the occupied zone, the police said, without reporting casualties.

Separately, the "Israeli" navy fired at four fishing boats off the coast near Tyre, around 80 kilometres (50 miles) south of Beirut, with again no casualties in the police report.

Khiam jail, where torture is routine and by remote control(10)

Robert Fisk

"Khiam is an awful place. Electrical wires attached to the penis and feet, constant whipping, cold nights attached to a pole while pails of freezing water are thrown over near-naked bodies. Are the "Israelis" likely to leave this dreadful institution behind when they withdraw from Lebanon? Will they leave the last 160 prisoners  hostages of "Israel" for the return of missing "Israeli" soldiers  as witnesses to this shameful episode in history? Or take them away?

Officially run by "Israel's" collapsing "South Lebanon Army" militia, the interrogators have been obsessive as well as cruel men. The questions come from "Israel" tapped on e-mail from Metulla to a computer screen in the little torture room in Khiam. And, not so long ago, this question came over the computer screen: "When he came to your village, did Fisk talk about forged $100 bills? Did he mention Lubrani's name?"

The man who received these two questions was stunned  so was I when I heard about them. I had written an article in The Independent on the circulation of fake $100 bills in southern Lebanon, many of them among the salaries paid to SLA by their "Israeli" masters. Lebanese banks think these bills are forged in "Israel". But I had never connected this with Uri Lubrani, "Israel's" so-called adviser to Lebanon. So why did the "Israeli" interrogators ask this? And why did they also ask  their computerized questions translated into Arabic by the torturers of Khiam  if I had been to the village of Jibsheit in southern Lebanon and what were my contacts with Hizbullah?

Stupidity, of course, plays a part in all intelligence wars. My own book on Lebanon's war is in the Khiam jail library  sent there by the International Red Cross  and the "Israelis" had only to glance through its pages to read that I spent three months in Jibsheit during "Israel's" 1985 occupation, that as Middle East correspondent of The Independent I had met the Hizbullah leadership.

Indeed, I knew Abbas Moussawim, the Hizbullah chairman who was assassinated by an "Israeli" helicopter pilot who fired an air-to-ground missile into his car, killing him and his family as they drove back from southern Lebanon after visiting the village of Jibsheit. Hizbullah leadership? Jibsheit? What did those computerized questions really mean?

I still don't know. Nor did the prisoner who was asked them. He was questioned about other things  if there were spies in his village from the Lebanese Army, if the Hizbullah used mobile phones to explode bombs. Then he was taken to a courtyard, stripped, tied to a stake and had freezing water thrown over him. All night. Then he was locked in one of three rooms  the "black rooms', the inmates call them  with just enough space to squat with his knees to his face for day after day. It is routine in Khiam.

I met one inmate just 10 days after his release, a man who had spent more than a year in Khiam. "When they interrogated me, they hit me on the head, then on the back with a Kalashnikov rifle. I fell down. The man put his boot in my face and broke part of my jaw. I have lost the hearing in part of my right ear. The ear-drum is broken. He said, 'You are working with Hizbullah.' I said no. I run a café that sells beer. How could I be Hizbullah? Now I have bad breathing problems and the doctor says there is no medicine for it. That this problem will stay with me all my life."

It is all true. The Red Cross, Amnesty and Human Rights Watch have all concluded that these stories are true. The case of Suleiman Ramadan, his arm amputated after beatings, still imprisoned after 16 years, is among the best known. What will these men, and a few women, receive by way of compensation? Nothing. Two hundred dollars a month from the Lebanese government but nothing from "Israel". The prisoners of Khiam will, of course, get nothing.

Some of them are former Hizbullah guerrillas but many are held because their families refuse to join the SLA or work for "Israeli" intelligence or because brothers and sisters are believed to work for the guerrillas.

Another ex-inmate told me: "They said to me I would be tortured in Khiam and held there for years if I did not renounce my brother." He refused to make the denunciation. He was beaten insensible and kept in Khiam for more than a year.

Needless to say, the world does not call these men hostages  which they are in reality and also according to the "Israeli" courts. The world's press and television, anxious, no doubt, not to offend the "Israelis", call them "bargaining chips", as if the tortured and the untried of Khiam are players in a large and amusing chess game. We never called the Western hostages in Lebanon "bargaining chips". Hostages is what they were. And hostages is what the last Khiam prisoners remain.

Will they come home when "Israel" ends its almost a quarter-century of occupation? Or will they be taken off across the border into "Israel" for further incarceration? When will Mr Ramadan, who has spent more than twice Anderson's time in captivity, at last be freed? Khiam is an old French Mandate fort with its walls still scarred by the tank fire of the Australian Division in the Second World War. It may have more shrapnel marks in the days to come.

But at least there will be no more questions about Uri Lubrani. Or Robert Fisk.
