Nasrallah and Bashar Assad call for evacuation of all mercenaries(1)

"Hizbullah and Syria stressed on Wednesday that an "Israeli" withdrawal from south Lebanon should include the evacuation of allied militiamen and "Israeli" troops from all Lebanese territories, including the Shibaa Farms.
Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah and Colonel Bashar Assad, son of Syrian President Hafez Assad, issued a joint statement after a meeting in Damascus outlining their demands for the looming "Israeli" withdrawal.
Both sides affirmed that a withdrawal should be complete, That is, including the pullback of mercenaries in the Israeli-proxy militia and from every inch of the Lebanese territories, including the Shibaa Farms, a Hizbullah statement said.
The enemy will have to foot the repercussions of the continued presence of occupation forces if the withdrawal does not take place according to this scenario, the statement added.
Nasrallah, in a separate meeting, lashed out at international envoys who had appealed for clemency for militiamen.
Foreign emissaries who proposed they be granted amnesty are vilifying Lebanon and conspiring against it, he told a 19-man delegation from the Arab National Forum, an independent think-tank.
Nasrallah was referring to statements made by Terje Roed-Larsen, special envoy to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, and Peter Hain, British Minister of State for Foreign Affairs. Both called on the Lebanese government to be lenient on the militiamen once "Israeli" soldiers vacate the south.
At this stage, we should not be easy on collaborators lest they turn from being an "Israeli" problem to an "Israeli" land mine implanted on Lebanese territory, Nasrallah said.
He also praised residents in the occupation zone and the sectors straddling the "Israeli"-held strip for aiding Hizbullahs resistance fighters mount their operations against "Israeli" troops.
We consider that Hizbullah has been the vanguard in scoring this victory, but others have lent us a hand, Nasrallah said. Residents in the occupied strip have long cooperated with us. Most of them were honest people who showered our fighters with the best aid there is.
Touching on the recent bloody Palestinian uprising against "Israeli" troops in Palestinian self-ruled areas, Nasrallah urged the Palestinians to step up their rebellion and emulate Hizbullahs military victory over "Israel".
We call on you to escalate your uprising  and benefit from the great Lebanese victory, he said. He was referring to the recent bloody showdown between stone-throwing Palestinians and "Israeli" soldiers which left three Palestinians dead on Tuesday.
I assure the people of the new uprising that theyre capable of imposing their demands, including the release of Palestinian detainees in "Israeli" prisons, he said.


"Israel's" proxy militia detains two doctors and a pharmacist.

The Order of Physicians and the Association of Pharmacists have called for governmental and international support to free two physicians, a teenager and a pharmacist kidnapped by the Israeli-allied militia in the occupation zone and transferred to Khiam prison on Tuesday.
The two doctors, Ibrahim Harb, his 19-year-old daughter, Malak, and Sateh Bazzi were seized at night by the militia from their homes in Bint Jbeil.
Hussein Awada, a pharmacist from Naqoura, was forcibly taken from his pharmacy in broad daylight and transferred to Khiam prison.
The assault on the physicians is considered a violation of the whole medical body and goes against all international human rights laws that protect doctors in times of war or occupation, said Ghattas Khoury, the president of the Order of Physicians, at the unions headquarters in Hazmieh.
Harb is a Health Ministry employee and Bazzi is an emergency room physician and anaesthetist at the Bint Jbeil Hospital.
Reuters quoted a militia source as saying that Harb and his daughter were detained on suspicion of dealing with Hizbullah.
When asked whether he knew the reason behind the kidnappings, Khoury said that there werent any and that the two doctors pursued their humanitarian duties as physicians and served the community, and their country, by remaining in the occupied zone regardless of the threats to their lives.
Khoury referred to the issue as a national and human rights cause that must be dealt with immediately with the support of local and international human rights groups(2)

The militia generally gives no details as to why it detains suspects, other than accusing them of working with the Lebanese government or guerrilla groups fighting the "Israeli" occupation.

The heads of the doctors' and pharmacists' professional bodies, Ghattas Khoury and Leila Khoury, issued appeals Wednesday for the "immediate release" of their colleagues at a joint press conference in Beirut.

"This is an attack on the medical corps and a flagrant violation of human rights," said Ghattas Khoury.

The two also called for the release of about 140 Lebanese being held without charge or trial in Khiam jail, run by the militia under "Israeli" supervision in the occupied zone.

Human rights groups, including the London-based Amnesty International and the Washington-based Human Rights Watch, have repeatedly denounced cases of torture and ill-treatment of detainees in Khiam.

"Israel's" supreme court ordered the government Wednesday to settle the fate of the Khiam prisoners before it withdraws from the region as it has pledged to do by July"(3)


"Israel" pulls out military material from its HQ in southern Lebanon(4)

" The "Israeli" army started pulling out military material from its headquarters in southern Lebanon overnight Tuesday, a security source reported.

Trucks carried spare parts for armoured cars and transport vehicles which had been stored at the HQ back to "Israel", the source said.

The movement was part of piecemeal preparations for the complete withdrawal of "Israeli" troops from southern Lebanon after 22 years of occupation, scheduled to be completed by July 7 at the latest.

Similar convoys, carrying tanks, prefabricated cement buildings used as barracks or shelters for troops, and other equipment began appearing on the roads of the occupied zone at the beginning of May.

The "Israeli" military this week transferred two of its forts to its militia allies, Taibeh in the central zone, and "Radar" in the western sector near the Mediterranean coast.

"Israel" had already let go of another seven positions, following the government's decision in March to abandon southern Lebanon.

 

Israeli militia spoils for more fighting after pullout(5)

"There are growing indications that "Israel" will not fully disarm or disband its militia before it withdraws.
While "Israel" continues to explore possible ways of resolving the fate of their Lebanese allies, the militia is maintaining an increasingly defiant tone. The Lahdi militia staged a series of minor redeployments on Wednesday in the occupation zone in what could be preparations for the creation of several village-based militias.
UN personnel have expressed pessimism over the prospects of a peaceful aftermath to the withdrawal, raising the possibility of an internecine conflict.
The collaborators spokesman Raymond Abu Mrad told Agence France Presse on Wednesday that the militia would stay on in the south after the pullout, braving attacks by Hizbullah until the government agrees they are not traitors. Well  control a smaller region that would enable us to protect our villages and people, he said, Were carrying out changes in our military positions, but well continue to maintain dozens of them.
UN spokesman Timur Goksel told AFP that a move like this is a recipe for the revival of sectarian violence  and nobody should expect the United Nations to be involved in that.
Goksel is in New York for discussions on the future of UNIFIL with the UN Security Council. According to UN sources, members of the Security Council grilled "Israeli" representatives on Tuesday over their intentions toward the militia. The "Israeli" responses were apparently less than satisfactory in the UN view.
The "Israelis" want the UN would shoulder the problem. The UN, however, has insisted that the militia must be disarmed, dismantled and all ties severed.
UNIFIL will almost certainly not deploy in liberated areas if a well-armed militia force remains behind.
Abu Mrads bold words were backed by the emergence of two new Lahdi checkpoints on Wednesday. They dont look like a militia that is about to disband, noted a UN officer who passed through one of the checkpoints.
The moves suggest the militia is preparing to break down into village militias, possibly along the lines of the original Haddad enclave.

War-weary Lebanese suspect "Israel's" pullout(10)

" "Israeli" missiles have regularly seared Khadija Moussa's village for the past 22 years and every day the elderly woman prays that an earthquake will bury the residents of "Israel" alive. Since "Israel" invaded south Lebanon in 1978, Moussa has lost family and friends in Habboush, one of the hamlets bordering the Middle East's last active warfront. The olive groves and tobacco farms have been scorched while countless homes have been razed in the almost daily barrage of air and artillery fire. Days ago, Moussa's two-month-old great-grandson and 11 relatives were hurt in an air raid. Like many residents of the south, she has seen too much to greet "Israel's" plans to withdraw from Lebanon with anything but suspicion. Prime Minister Ehud Barak, under pressure to quit what many "Israelis" call the south Lebanon "mud", has vowed to pull out by July 7. The pullout dominates talk in the south, where fear of "Israel" runs deep. Many residents believe the Jewish state is setting them up. Others say it will never leave. "We want to live in peace, but do you think "Israel" will let us?" asked Samih Halan, director of Habboush municipality. ""Israel" has never been honest and it has no credibility. How can they expect us to believe that they will no longer kill us?" South Lebanon is the last active Arab-"Israeli" front, where resistance fighters are battling to end "Israel's" occupation. Hundreds of civilians have lost lives and limbs in the conflict, which often spills over to the villages fringing the occupation zone. Last year, 15 children were hurt when mortars struck their school in Arab Salim, a village just outside the zone. "Israel" apologized, but the hatred and mistrust the attack stoked have not diminished -- especially when mortar bombs hit the school again this month. "There will never be peace as long as "Israel" is in the region," said headmaster Afif Noureddine, who bricked up all classroom windows as a precaution against more assaults. "There can never be peace with the Jews. They won't leave us be." Mohammad Farahat, a teacher at the school, said "Israel" would find any pretext to hit Lebanon. "If an "Israeli" anywhere in the world is hurt, they'll strike us. They're like that," he said, morosely sipping black coffee. Further north, within firing range of "Israel's" allied militia, grocer Abdallah Mushawrab in Jarjou adamantly refuses to believe "Israel" will withdraw. "This is a ruse, a game they are playing with us. They wont go." "Israel" has threatened to blitz Lebanon if resistance fighters attack over the border after the withdrawal, but violence seems likely if the pullout is not accompanied by a peace deal. "In "Israel", when our men hit them, they run to bomb shelters. We don't even have those. I am longing for the day this nightmare is over," said Nadia Karambash from her frontline home in Zawtar es-Sharkiya, a sleepy village with roses blooming from every garden. A 1996 accord on rules of engagement in the south bans Lebanese fighters and "Israel" from launching strikes from or on civilian areas".


UN observers face tough time verifying "Israel's" withdrawal(11)

"They have been quietly and diligently observing the border between Lebanon and "Israel" for over 50 years, but in the coming weeks, the UNs 51-strong Observer Group Lebanon (OGL) will be playing a crucial role in verifying "Israel's" withdrawal from Lebanon.
The OGL is part of the UN Truce Supervision Organization has been patrolling the border since the 1949 Armistice Agreement, patiently logging all breaches of the accord from overflights by warplanes to full-scale invasions.
OGL personnel man five observation posts in the occupation zone at Labboune, Marouahine, Maroun er-Ras, Markaba and Khiam, all of them approximately 15 kilometers apart and within eyeshot of each other.
Captain Duncan McMillan, the Canadian commander of the Markaba post, explained that the OGL would participate in a number of tasks following the "Israeli" withdrawal.
Security will have to be provided for UN teams, and for engineers involved in mine clearing, he said. Well also have to help ensure that all outposts are no longer occupied.
The initial task for the UN is to confirm that the "Israelis" have genuinely pulled all their forces from Lebanon to behind the armistice demarcation line, which follows the original 1923 boundary.
The teams observation post near Markaba is located beside one of "Israel's" incursion zones where the border fence was pushed into Lebanon a few hundred meters.
The "Israeli" Armys original withdrawal plan recommended retaining the strategic incursion zones, the so-called Purple Line, claiming they were necessary for the defense of Galilee.
The "Israeli" government was warned, however, that keeping the incursions would provide justification for further resistance attacks. After much procrastination, the "Israelis" finally agreed to pull back to the 1923 border.
There are few better places to observe "Israel's" preparations for the withdrawal than from McMillans post on the border beside Markaba.
To the north, an outpost on the "Israeli" side of the border is being reinforced with bulldozed ramparts and concrete T-walls. Three-meter-high poles line the front of the position, waiting to be fitted with protective wire mesh anti-missile screens.
To the south, a crane looms above the Abbad position. Half the position, recognizable by its distinctive twin radio masts, lies inside Lebanon. The "Israelis" are dragging their equipment a few meters to the west and re-establishing the outpost to ensure that the withdrawal complies with UN requirements.
In a deep wadi below the observation post, "Israeli" soldiers milled around a self-propelled 155mm gun, one of five established as a temporary artillery post. Some of the soldiers sunbathed on armored personnel carriers, others lounged in the grass outside a row of canvas tents.
The artillery emplacement is sited in the Odaisseh incursion, the original border fence could be clearly seen as could the existing fence which follows the Purple Line.
You can see the "Israelis" are beefing up their positions just inside "Israel" and dismantling the positions just inside Lebanon, said Major Brendan OShea, OGLs operations officer, as he drove along a pot-holed road adjacent to the frontier in the western sector of the occupation zone. He indicated the Berkat Risha outpost on Lebanese soil near Yarin. The "Israelis" have abandoned that post and built another position 1 kilometer south of the border, he said.
However, Hizbullah is not allowing the construction activity to proceed unmolested.
As OShea approached the Abbad position in the central sector, a terse warning was broadcast over the UN radio.
Warning, warning, warning. Operational activity is taking place in Irishbatt.
Several mortar rounds exploded against the Abbad position, sending plumes of dust and smoke into the sky. OShea pulled over to the side of the road to sit out the attack.
Moments later came the thunderclap of outgoing "Israeli" artillery fire from a position near Meis al-Jabal.
This is typical of whats going on here. They (Hizbullah) are keeping up the pressure, OShea said.
Because the OGL teams operate in the occupation zone, they are often caught up in the conflict. The night before, Hizbullah mortar teams had pounded the positions along the border, wounding two "Israelis.
McMillan and two of his colleagues had a narrow escape during the fighting when a mortar round exploded near their car. Were unarmed and stuck in the middle trying to navigate our way through as best we can, said OShea".


Hizbollahville is scenting victory (12)

By Robert Fisk in Jibsheit, southern Lebanon

18 May 2000

"If Hizbollahville exists, it is Jibsheit. Its "martyrs cemetery" holds the bodies of 72 fighters, including that of Sheikh Ragheb Harb, the village imam hated by the "Israelis" and shot in the back in 1985, at the age of 32. When "Israel" bombarded Jibsheit in 1993, a helicopter fired a missile straight into his grave.

On the other side of the stubborn hill on which Jibsheit stands, is the home of another priest much loathed by "Israel": Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, kidnapped by "Israeli" occupation troops in 1989 and now languishing as a hostage  against all international law  in "Israel".

And when "Israel" withdraws from southern Lebanon, there are few here who believe that peace will bless the streets of Jibsheit. Celebrations there may be; the Hizbullah are promising victory flags atop "Israel's" abandoned artillery batteries and a few captured pro-"Israeli" militia tanks to show to the people of southern Lebanon. But as the late Sheikh Ragheb Harb's brother Ismail says, Jibsheit is a special place.

"The "Israelis" despise us specifically because of our fighters and our martyrs," he says. "We were the first to hurt them so it is natural that they will seek revenge. There could be intelligence and covert operations: the history of Zionism shows us that "Israelis" use assassinations, the killing of civilians with explosives.

"But for the rest of Lebanon, I think there will be some tranquillity after a total "Israeli" withdrawal because the resistance that has defeated the "Israelis" will remain in place here.

"The "Israelis" will have concerns for the safety of their settlements and their civilians [in northern "Israel"] and will not want them harmed."

Talking to the resistance organisation in Jibsheit, it's obvious that it scents victory. I've never seen so many Hizbullah men joking and smiling, promising that Christian villages in the occupied zone will be protected and Obeid's son Sajed, 18 years old and a student at the Lebanese University in Beirut, believes he will see his father very soon. "We have spent 11 years waiting to see him and hoping every day that we would see the sheikh. Now it doesn't matter if it's before or after the withdrawal."

In fact, there is no agreement to allow Obeid his freedom. Indeed there is no agreement by the Arabs for an "Israeli" withdrawal. Obeid is a hostage, held for the release of a captured "Israeli" airman, Ron Arad. Why should the "Israelis" let him go now?

Sajed Obeid is plastering his website with the story of his father's abduction and imprisonment in "Israel". The website, www.geocities.com/sajedz, is his little resistance cell on the internet, a technology far removed from the old winding roads around the hill villages of southern Lebanon  and from the dire warnings that the Hizbullah issues daily against "Israel's" proxy militia, led by Antoine Lahd. "They have now been given one last chance by Hizbullah," Sajed Obeid says. "They must kill an "Israeli" or a Lahd officer  but this is the last chance. What they do after that is their concern."

Privately some Hizbullah members say they are worried that car bombs may explode in the aftermath of "Israel's" withdrawal in an attempt to provoke the Hizbullah into a war across the border. "Israel" might be blamed for this even if they were not responsible. Syria has already insisted that 18 farms around the village of Chebaa - farmland which "Israel" refuses to consider part of its occupation area  are in Lebanon. So does the war really end if Lebanon and Syria claim "Israel" has not withdrawn from all Lebanese territory?

And does hatred end? A conversation with Sheikh Ismail Harb produces some chilling remarks; quotations from that ghastly old Tsarist forgery, The Protocols of the Elders of Zion, and blatant racist material about the "horrific nature of the Jews" and "their desire to sacrifice children's blood". When I challenge the sheikh and tell him that this is nonsense, he merely repeats the same terrible statements over and over.

Several Hizbullah men who caught my eye were clearlydisturbed. But Sheikh Harb is the imam of Jibsheit. And who in Jibsheit dare challenge an imam on the eve of victory?


No mercy for "Israel's" allies in South Lebanon(13)

By Robert Fisk in Taibeh

17 May 2000

"Waiting for the "Israelis" to leave occupied southern Leb-anon is a fearful business. As the sun burns the long grass around the big artillery batteries, "Israel's"  militia are on the run.

In droves, they are defecting to the Hizbullah or the Lebanese army  nine ran through a minefield to give themselves up at Shaqra in just one month  and the Hizbullah guerrillas have made an offer they hope the militiaman cannot refuse: face liquidation or kill one of your own officers.

A militia officer. An "Israeli" soldier. Either will do. Otherwise the militia can expect no mercy. Even the United Nations fears that the Hizbullah are going to chase the "Israelis" all the way to their border when they stage their last retreat from Lebanon. And the UN will not move south to the frontier when they do. First, the 4,500 army of peace-keepers  note that word for future use  will demand verification that every "Israeli" soldier has withdrawn behind the international border. And then confirmation that the militia has been deprived of all its tanks and artillery pieces and is not being resupplied by the "Israelis".

So who would want to be a militia man now? On a hilltop at Khirbe, they have their own war memorial to the 652 pro-"Israeli" militiamen and collaborators who have been shot dead or blown to pieces in this vicious guerrilla war. It is a sleek place with landscaped gardens, the chiselled name of every "martyr" for "Israel's" occupation cause and a cross and crescent for Christian and Muslim on the concrete walls. Among the latest names is 35-year old Abbas Tormas, who died on an anti-bomb patrol, a father of four who boasted of his own survival until one of the Hizbullah bombs he was looking for exploded 60 feet away. "Nice enough guy," a UN man said. "He loved his kids." Too late.

It is the same all over "Israel's" occupation zone. The militiamen are mostly local but can no longer go to their homes at night, no longer run over the border into "Israel" for cover, no longer trust each other. In Taibeh village, the breeze bangs the wooden doors of long-abandoned houses, two old men play backgammon by a fetid pond. Tank tracks have torn up the little square. "An Israeli drove his tank in yesterday and went round the town taking photographs," a middle-aged villager muttered. "I guess he wanted some tourist snaps before he left."

Everyone knows they are leaving. The collaborators men from Taibeh already have five dead and they will no longer patrol the village. Only one of the villagers still works in "Israel"  the so-called "Good Fence" is closing on them and the Lebanese who worked on construction projects and olive picking inside "Israel" have been fired from their jobs. The "Israelis" are promising severance pay to their collaborators, last payments to the men who have secured their occupation over almost one-quarter of a century.

The "important" men, the killers and torturers, are expected to receive visas to Europe and the United States since they will be murdered or executed if they stay behind. "It's an irony," a villager told me bitterly, "that the worst guys are going to end up in the United States or France or England but the little men will be left to their fate."

In Sidon in 1985, the "Israelis" left most of their collaborators behind. I remember watching them being collared by resistance fighters or bundled into the trunks of cars for execution. The leader of the collaborators then, Abu Arrida, now languishes in an "Israeli" prison, convicted for ultimate collaboration with the Lebanese army.

So visa officers in Britain and other European countries  and in the US  may be looking out for names such as Raymond Daher and Hassan Abdulatif and Hussein Kdouah, an intelligence officer in the notorious torture prison at Khiam inside the occupied zone. There are only 160 prisoners left there and their fate, despite requests for information from the International Red Cross, remains unknown. Will these hostages, held for the return of missing "Israeli" troops in Lebanon, be freed or taken across the border into "Israel"?

There are men in the jail who have been held without charge for more than 10 years. Sulieman Ramadan, for example, has been in Khiam for 16 years, one of four men who tried to dig a tunnel out of the walls last year but who were punished most severely for their attempts at freedom, their food and letters and family visits suspended. Ramadan's leg was stamped on and burnt during interrogation. It was later amputated. He will be in no mood to offer compassion or clemency to his Lebanese collaborator torturers.

"I would like revenge of some kind," a former Khiam inmate told me in suppressed fury as we looked across the village at another massive "Israeli" compound, its minefields and wire and cement bunkers baking under the early summer sun. "I don't want to kill them, but I would like to do something. I don't know what. Slap them, beat them maybe."

The same man told me he has a fast car ready for the "Israeli" withdrawal. "If the resistance attack the "Israelis" when they leave, the "Israelis" will fire on all of us and we will want to leave at once.Yes, we want liberation, we want the Lebanese army here. But we are all afraid," he said.
