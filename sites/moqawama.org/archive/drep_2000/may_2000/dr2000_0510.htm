Daily Report

May Wednesday 10-2000:

Introduction

The Islamic Resistance's (IR) seizure of the main "Israeli" allied militia post in the village of Aramta last month had effectively hastened and forced the "Israeli" occupation troops to retreat to areas deep inside the "Israeli" occupied zone. Joy has overwhelmed the villagers who lived under "Israeli" occupation for 17 years.

Meanwhile, Lebanese Prime Minister Salim Hoss rejected a demand for amnesty by the "Israeli"-proxy militia's head insisting that he should surrender to Lebanese justice.

However, the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in "Israeli" Prisons appealed to President Emile Lahoud and UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan to exert intensive pressure on "Israel" to secure the release of the hostages Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, Hajj Mustafa Dirani and Samir Qentar from the "Israeli" concentration detention centers.

 

"Liberated" southern Lebanese village welcomes visitors(1)

"The hundred or so residents of the southern Lebanese village of Aramta wept at the sight of their first visitors Tuesday after "Israel" and its allies abandoned the area they had held for 22 years.

Local authorities reclaimed the village, sending a tractor to dismantle a road block of earth and concrete slabs erected on the approach road by "Israel's" proxy militia.

And regional governor Nabih Hammud went and made a speech, before returning under police escort to his offices in the nearby town of Jezzine.

The militia lost its fortified position in Aramta on April 28 when it was seized by Hizbullah, who planted explosive devices before withdrawing, killing three militiamen and injuring four others.

The scene in the village Tuesday was just a foretaste of what can be expected in the rest of the occupied border strip when "Israel" finally pulls out, which it has promised to do by July.

Past the sign in black paint on a sheet of metal warning of shelling, the 12-kilometre (eight-mile) road to Aramta was deserted, as was the entrance to the village, lying in a valley 1,200 metres (4,000 feet) above sea level.

Weeds grew in shell-holes along the road, and the ruins of four buildings marked the "Israeli"-proxy militia fortifications blown up by Hizbullah or by the militia in its retreat.

Finally, after a row of gutted houses covered in moss and shops with their iron shutters blown out by blast, the inhabitants were waiting, gathered on the village square.

"Welcome, welcome, it's so long since anyone came from outside," they chorused.

The cries and cheers grew with the arrival of one of their own, Jaafar Hussein, 44, a teacher expelled by the "Israelis" in 1985.

"It's the first time I have seen my sister and my parents, who didn't want to leave Aramta", he said, freeing himself from the embrace of his elderly mother.

Mohammad Tirani, 65, told AFP that they had been informed late Monday by the International Committee of the Red Cross, of the militia's departure.

"We had heard the noise of explosions and firing, and it was only from the radio that we learned that the collaborator position outside Aramta had been destroyed by Hizbullah," he said.

It was also from the radio that they knew the militia had given up Shayar Azour, the next hamlet in the occupied zone, on Sunday night, making access to Aramta possible.

Tirani said that in the last few months, under shellfire from the opposing sides, they had lived three or four families to a house, in the buildings which seemed to be the safest.

Aramta had a population of 5,000 people when it became part of the "Israeli"-held "security zone" in 1978. A steady drain of departing residents left only 110 today.

Youngsters were supposed to go to school in Jezzine before the militia gave up the town in June last year after holding the salient of the same name for 14 years.

But they told AFP it was too dangerous to make the journey because of the war.

 

Beirut takes back control of village in "Israeli"-occupied zone(2)

" Lebanon Tuesday took back control of the village of Aramta in the "Israeli"-occupied zone, after it was abandoned by "Israel's" proxy militia, officials here announced.

The local authorities sent a tractor which dismantled the road block of earth and concrete slabs which the "Israeli" agents had erected at the entrance to the village, officials in the office of Prime Minister Salim Hoss said.

The militia lost its position in Aramta on April 28 when it was seized by Hizbullah, who planted explosive devices before withdrawing, killing three militiamen and injuring four others.

The extension of Lebanese authority to Aramta comes after the collaborator gave up its post in Shayar Azour, the next hamlet in the zone on Sunday night.

The militia abandoned another position, Kfar Houna, in the same central sector, in January, leaving Aramta as the first point of access into the occupied zone.

In February it withdrew from Sojod which security sources described as an "important strategic position" controlling the Hizbullah stronghold of the Iqleem al-Toffah hills.

In June last year the militia withdrew from the whole of the Jezzine salient which it had controlled with "Israeli" army backing for 14 years".

 

Lebanon rejects "Israeli" ally amnesty demand(3)

" Lebanese Prime Minister Salim Hoss rejected Tuesday a demand for amnesty by the head of "Israel's" allied militia, saying he should surrender to Lebanese justice.

"It is bizarre that Antoine Lahd is asking (Lebanese) President Emile Lahoud for a general amnesty, while he is still carrying out aggressions by the side of his nation's enemy against his own civilian people and sometimes doesn't even spare his nation's army," said Hoss.

"He first has to surrender to Lebanese justice before demanding anything," Hoss said in a statement.

Hoss said if Lahd surrendered, "he knows that ... he would only meet justice, transparency and the rule of the law, and there won't be any place for revenge in the (Lebanese) officials' policy toward anyone."

"As for the steadfast citizens under the oppression of the occupation and the coercion of the collaborating militias, they are our dear people and we value their steadfastness," said Hoss.

"They will all return to the open arms of the nation and the state with the mere withdrawal of the occupation forces and their collaborators from Lebanese soil and their joy at the liberation will be as ours," he said.

The "Israeli" agent Antoine Lahd, whose militia helps control the "Israeli"-occupied border zone in southern Lebanon, sent an open letter to Lahoud on Monday asking for amnesty for his troops, excluding himself.

Lahd was requesting a general amnesty similar to that promulgated in 1991 after the end of Lebanon's 1975-1990 civil war. All militias were then disarmed, except for those like Hizbullah combating "Israel's" occupation of southern Lebanon.

The fate of the roughly 2,500-strong "Israeli"-proxy militia has been up in the air since "Israel" announced it would end its 22-year occupation of southern Lebanon by July.

Lahd has himself been sentenced to death in absentia by a Lebanese military court and many members of his militia have also been prosecuted for "collaboration with the enemy."

The United Nations has also called for the dismantling of the militia, while Hizbullah gave them three options: surrender, emigration or death at the hands of the group's.

In "Israel" Tuesday the daily Ha'aretz said the "Israeli" army was becoming increasingly worried about morale in the militiamen, and "fears it is gradually losing control of the militia."

The paper's military correspondent added: "New attacks by Hizbullah could bring about the collapse of the militia and substantially move up the withdrawal."

Monday the daily Maariv quoted a military source as saying that "Israel" was scaling back its supplies of arms and munitions, notably tank and artillery shells, to the militia ahead of the withdrawal.

The source confirmed that "Israel" could also call in its heavy weapons under an agreement with the United Nations, but would leave the militia with light arms.

 

Militia's unknown future a headache for "Israel" ahead of Lebanon pullout(4)

" Two months before "Israel's" planned departure from the battlefields of south Lebanon, concern mounted here Tuesday about the future of its allied militia.

Beirut's refusal, announced Tuesday, to meet militia commander Antoine Lahd's demands for a general amnesty for the some 2,500 troops who have served alongside "Israeli" troops in Lebanon for two decades will only complicate the withdrawal, analysts said.

Prime Minister Ehud Barak's government is hoping that the militia will quietly disappear after "Israeli" troops leave Lebanon in an operation due to take place no later than July.

"The "Israeli" Army fears total collapse of its militia," said the front-page headline in "Israel's" leading Ha'aretz newspaper on Tuesday.

"New attacks by Hizbullah could bring about the collapse of the militia and substantially move up the withdrawal," its military correspondent said.

But a refusal by the collaborator to disarm could be used by Lebanon and Syria to accuse "Israel" of failing to abide fully by the 1978 UN resolutions calling for its withdrawal.

The United Nations warned "Israel" in April that leaving behind the militia would not mean a "complete withdrawal."

"Israel" has expressed concern that the militiamen and their families could be victims of reprisal attacks after it quits the self-declared "security zone" that covers a swathe of land in southern Lebanon.

While saying it owes a moral debt to the "Israeli" agents, "Israel" has made no concrete promises to the militia amid reports that several thousand fighters and their families are seeking asylum in the Jewish state.

"If anything happens to them, it will be a black mark for "Israel"," said Mark Heller of the Jaffee Centre of Strategic Studies at Tel Aviv university.

Lahd, whose men help control the "Israeli"-occupied zone, sent an open letter to Lahoud on Monday asking for amnesty for his troops, excluding himself.

But Lebanese Prime Minister Salim Hoss rejected the demand, saying he should surrender to Lebanese justice.

Lahd has been sentenced to death in absentia by a Lebanese military court and many members of the militia prosecuted for "collaboration with the enemy."

The Hizbullah movement which spearheads the guerrilla war to evict "Israel" from Lebanese soil, has also warned that it would give no security guarantees after a pullout -- although stopping just short of explicitly threatening to attack.

"It's going to be very difficult to disarm the "Israeli"-proxy militia if not impossible unless there is some kind of understanding with the UN," Heller said.

 

Rights group appeals for release of Lebanese detained by "Israel"(5)

" A Lebanese rights group appealed Tuesday to UN chief Kofi Annan and other humanitarian agencies for the release of three Lebanese held by "Israel" as hostages to swap them for missing "Israeli" servicemen.

The group called on Annan, the Human Rights Commission and the International Committee for the Red Cross to "exert intensive pressure on "Israel" for the release of the Lebanese detainees."

"Israel's" withdrawal from occupied southern Lebanon planned by July will not be complete "if one single detainee remains in "Israeli" jails," said a statement by the Follow Up Committee for the Support of the Lebanese Detainees in the "Israeli" Prisons.

The appeal was made for the release of Islamic leaders Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani as well as Samir Qentar, a Druze held since 1979 and sentenced to 522 years in jail for taking part in an anti-"Israeli" operation.

"Israel" has refused to release Sheikh Obeid and Dirani, who were kidnapped in southern Lebanon in 1989, and 1994 respectively, to put pressure on Hizbullah to provide information about "Israeli" servicemen missing in action.

"Israel" said the two are a danger to its security, and is preparing to change the law to legalise their detention without charge or trial, by defining them as "non-legal fighters".

""Israel's" continued detention of the three Lebanese nationals as hostages is among the worst violations of human rights and all international laws which prohibit hostage-taking," the support group said.

Another 144 are being held at the notorious Khiam prison in the "Israeli"-occupied border zone in southern Lebanon, without trial or charges, some of them for nearly 15 years.

The London-based Amnesty International and the Washington-based Human Rights Watch repeatedly denounce cases of torture and illtreatment of Lebanese detainees in "Israeli" jails.

 

Militia unveils occupation's post-withdrawal plan(6)

Less than 24 hours after he begged for the Lebanese government's pardon to his militiamen and his threat to create unrest if his requests are denied, agent Antoine Lahd said the occupation's militia will maintain a border strip in south Lebanon after the "Israeli" pullout.

The strip will extend along the border between Mount Hermon and Naqoura, the chief of the occupation's collaborators told Reuters, adding that he would maintain the borders with the "Israeli" entity open.

Agent Lahd said he would return to the "Israeli" occupation the heavy arms it has given to his militia as he would not need them since he "does not intend to attack anyone".

Asked about a potential confrontation between his militia and the UNIFIL troops, Lahd said he did not believe disarming is among the responsibilities of the international force.

 

Iran promises aid after Israeli raids

Lebanon thanks Iran for support of resistance against "Israel": IRNA

"The Iranian ambassador to Lebanon said on Tuesday that his country was planning to help Lebanon reconstruct the power stations devastated by last Fridays "Israeli" air raids but did not specify what type of aid Iran would offer.
Mohammed Ali Sobhani made the announcement following talks with Finance Minister George Corm which focused on the latest regional developments as well as different fields of cooperation between the two countries.
Were seriously looking into how we can help in the rebuilding of the power stations and we will soon announce it, Sobhani said.
He stressed that his country would always stand beside Lebanon in the face of all pressure from the Jewish state.
Iran has always been concerned with providing whatever possible help for Lebanon, Sobhani said.
Iran helped fund the rebuilding of power stations destroyed by "Israeli" air raids last February and in June 1999.(7)

Meanwhile, " Lebanon's Prime Minister Salim Hoss thanked Iran for its support of the Lebanese resistance against the "Israeli" occupation in a call to Tehran officials, Iran's state IRNA news agency said Tuesday.

"Lebanon pays homage to Iran for its support of the resistance as well as the Lebanese government," Hoss told Foreign Minister Kamal Kharazi in a telephone convseration, according to IRNA.

Hoss also reiterated "the determination of the Lebanese people to reconstruct its damaged installations as soon as possible," referring to power sites bombed by "Israel" last week.

Kharazi for his part expressed "the serious concern of Iran, as head of the Organisation of the Islamic Conference, about the raids."

The "Israeli" air strikes that knocked out two electricity relay stations followed the death of an "Israeli" soldier and injuries to some 30 settlers in rocket strikes on northern "Israel" by Hizbullah guerrillas, launched in reprisal for civilian casualties under "Israeli" fire in southern Lebanon.

Kharazi said the attacks were a "flagrant violation" of a 1996 accord not to target civilians in the hostilities in south Lebanon.

The Iranian minister met Monday with Hoss's predecessor Rafiq Hariri, who is making a private visit to Tehran"(8)
