Daily Report

May Friday 05-2000:


Introduction

In retaliation for the repeated "Israeli" enemy aggressions against the Lebanese civilians, the Islamic Resistance (IR) fired its largest salvo of Katyusha rockets in almost a year against the "Israeli" settlements of northern occupied Palestine. Forty Katyushas were sent into the Jewish state after the "Israeli" artillery shells killed an 82-year- old woman and her 40-year-old daughter at Qotrani village of South Lebanon. The IR bombardment resulted in killing one "Israeli" soldier and wounding 26 settlers and another soldier.

Meanwhile, "Israel" proceeded with aggressions where its warplanes launched air strikes against two power stations, Bsalim and Deir Ammar (Biddawi), and the main highway between Beirut and Damascus.

However, the "Israeli" occupying troops and their proxy militia released six of the Lebanese hostages detained at Al-Khiam detention center.


IR retaliated for "Israeli" aggressions

One "Israeli" soldier killed, 25 settlers and another soldier wounded

The Islamic Resistance (IR) released a communiqué in which it reported the following:

In the name of God, the Merciful the compassionate.

"If you should turn back, We will go back too and turn Hell into a confinement".

In retaliation for the Zionist enemy's going too far in its repeated aggressions against our unarmed people in South Lebanon and Western Beqaa, following our long-lived patience, after the continued aggressions against our villages and towns of Arab Salim (its school), Mansouri, Majdal Selem, Majdal Zoun, Shaqra, Kfar Romman, Libbaya and others reaching to Habboush, yesterday, and Qotrani today, where a lot of civilians were inflicted and a number of houses were damaged bearing in mind the "Israeli" destroying of Lebanon's infrastructure in the near past. The last aggression occurred today (yesterday) when the "Israeli" shelling of Qotrani resulted in killing two women.

Accordingly, the IR was compelled to practice the right to defend its people and deter the aggression by any available means. So, the IR bombarded on Thursday 4/5/2000 the "Israeli" settlement of Kiryat Shmona with barrages of Katyusha rockets pledging its people to sacrifice the souls for the sake of protecting them(1)

AFP reported that "Twenty-six people were injured in a blaze of Hizbullah rocket fire on this northern "Israeli" settlement Thursday following an upsurge of deadly violence across the border in south Lebanon, the "Israeli" army said.

"An "Israeli" soldier was killed by a rocket fired on the northern "Israeli" settlement from the Lebanese border.

Sergeant Major Shaked Ozeri, 24, was killed and another soldier injured when the vehicle they were travelling in was hit by a Katyusha rocket near an "Israeli" army post close to the border, an "Israeli" military spokesman said.

"Hizbullah, the group spearheading the armed campaign against the "Israeli" occupation of southern Lebanon, claimed responsibility for the barrage against northern "Israel", which followed the death of two Lebanese women and the wounding of 14 in separate "Israeli" attacks over 24 hours.

Thousands of residents of northern "Israel" were ordered into bomb shelters following the barrage, which comes just two months before "Israeli" troops are due to leave Lebanon after 22 years of bloody occupation.

Five of those hurt were hospitalised, the army said. Medical officials had previously reported that four people had been wounded by shrapnel and that 16 people, several of them elderly were in a state of shock.

Hizbullah's military wing, the Islamic Resistance, said in a statement that its guerrillas fired the Katyushas "in retaliation for repeated "Israeli" aggressions against Lebanese civilians."

"Israeli" public television broadcast footage from the Kyriat Shmona, the largest settlement in the region, showing a man with a bandaged head and spattered with blood being taken to hospital, panicked residents in the streets, cars in flames, and columns of black smoke rising into the afternoon sky(2)

For its part, Jerusalem Post mentioned that in fact, a number of rockets hit the heart of the northern "front-line" city, scoring a direct hit on some buildings, including an old-age home.

Cars parked outside houses and apartment blocks were also hit and set alight by the blasts. The head of Magen David Adom in Kiryat Shmona, Shimon Abutbul, said paramedics and doctors had treated nearly 20 people at the emergency ward in the town, including those who had been brought in by ambulances after MDA had received reports of casualties.

He said most of those hurt were suffering from light wounds from shrapnel and others from shock. Five more seriously wounded were taken to Rebecca Sieff Government Hospital in Safed.

Panic-stricken mothers clutching their children raced for the bomb shelters and security rooms in their homes after the first salvos struck. Within a few minutes the streets of Kiryat Shmona were empty.

After an hour or so of quiet, some residents could be seen packing belongings into cars and driving to traditionally non-target areas in the region. Others brought mattresses, bed linens, food, drinks, and children's games from their homes to the bomb shelters in preparation for a long night underground.

Residents of all the border communities, from Nahariya and Rosh Hanikra on the coast to Kiryat Shmona and Metulla in the east, were ordered to stay in bomb shelters and security rooms until further notice"(3)

""Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak held a four-hour emergency session with his security cabinet at the war ministry in Tel Aviv to debate "Israel's" response to the rocket attack, the bloodiest in the region since June last year.

After the meeting, Barak visited Kiryat Shmona whose inhabitants he assured of his government's "firm decision" to "react" to the Hizbullah attack.

Tensions have remained high in the region despite "Israel's" decision to pull out of southern Lebanon by July 7 with or without an agreement with Beirut and Damascus.

The latest round of violence came during a two-week mission to the region by a UN special envoy to discuss the role of UN peacekeepers when "Israel" pulls out of Lebanon, where hundreds of soldiers have lost their lives.

""Israeli" radio reported that the rockets hit Kyriat Shmona as well as the neighbouring villages of Margaliyyot, Misgav Am and Kfar Giladi. An AFP correspondent in the region said several hundred settlers were fleeing for fear of further attack"(4)


"Israeli" jets raid power stations near Beirut, in north Lebanon

""Israeli" jets raided power stations near the capital and in northern Lebanon early Friday, while firing a missile near a Syrian intelligence post in the east, police said.

An AFP photographer said an electricity worker was slightly injured in the raids which came after the death of an "Israeli" soldier in a Hizbullah attack into northern "Israel" meant to avenge the killing of two Lebanese civilian women in an "Israeli" shelling.

"Israeli" jets struck a power station in the mountainous region of Bsalim, northeast of Beirut, at around 2:45 a.m. (2345 GMT), abruptly cutting electricity supply in many parts of the capital and its nearby regions, they said.

Many residents woke up in panic at the sound of the jets breaking the sound barrier over Beirut.

Witnesses told AFP that fire broke out in the Bsalim station.

The fighter-bombers also struck a power station in the Beddawi region, further up from the northern port city of Tripoli, the police said.

The worker who was wounded there, 28-year-old Daoud Fawaz, was hit in the arm by broken glass. Two of the station's three transformers were destroyed, the photographer said.

The warplanes returned about an hour later to strike again at Bsalim and Beddawi, they said.

"Israeli" warplanes also fired a missile in front of a position of the Syrian intelligence forces on the main highway linking eastern Lebanon to Syria, they said.

The fighter-bombers fired at around 3:20 a.m. (0020 GMT) a missile some 10 meters (33 feet) away from a building where Syrian intelligence forces are based, they said.

"The explosion of the missile has damaged the building and provoked a crater four meters (13 feet) deep, cutting the Beirut-Damascus international road in the village of Taanayel," the police said.

"Israeli" jets had earlier raided in a residential area on the main road between the cities of Zahleh and Baalback, at around 2:20 a.m. (2320 GMT), they said.

Detonations were heard for several minutes in the area, they said.

"Five other "Israeli" raids were also staged into valleys near the villages of Khorbet Selem, Majdal Selem, Wadi al-Qaissieh and Burj Qalaway -- all east of the southern port city of Tyre", they said(5)

Larsen hints that "Israel" can keep Shibaa Farms(7)

"In Lebanon to help define the area "Israeli" troops would have to relinquish for its imminent withdrawal to meet the conditions of Resolution 425, Larsen publicly spelled out the UN views.
The United Nations will be using the 1923 line, with mutually agreed-upon amendments after the Armistice Agreement in 1949, Larsen said after the second set of talks with Lahoud and Hoss in Baabda.
In his public statement, he focused only on the border issue. But in private, he relayed some of "Israel's" viewpoints to Lahoud and Hoss, according to sources who were briefed on the talks.
He said that "Israeli" leaders made it clear to him that there would be no violation of Lebanese airspace or naval and territorial sovereignty after the pull-out.
According to the source, Larsen also said that the "Israelis" planned to release all Lebanese detainees except for three ­ Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, Hajj Mustafa Dirani, and Samir Qantar, who are being held as bargaining chips in exchange for information on a missing "Israeli" airman, Ron Arad.
As for the future of "Israeli"-allied militiamen, the envoy said the "Israelis" would provide a solution for the more prominent officers of the militia, but that Beirut would have to deal with the rest.
The two rounds of talks in Baabda were adjourned to allow both sides to undertake internal consultations, according to a UN statement.
During the morning meeting, Lahoud and Hoss presented Larsen with a letter to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan expressing Lebanons position concerning the "Israeli" withdrawal.
The eight-point letter pledged the countrys full cooperation with the UN. Lebanon is ready to cooperate with the United Nations secretariat-general for an accurate and honest implementation, the letter said.
The implementation of the international resolutions requires a complete and unconditional withdrawal from Lebanese territory up to the internationally recognized borders. This border was defined in 1923 with Palestine, it added.
The letter also described a complete withdrawal as including a pull out from the highly fertile Shibaa farms.
Larsen delayed an expected visit to UNIFIL in the south on Friday to continue talks with Lahoud and Hoss.
But the UN envoy stressed that the border issue was a technical exercise that we are carrying out in consultation with the parties.
Any deviation from this border will signify that "Israel" did not pull out in conformity with Resolution 425 and that its retrieval actually constitutes a redeployment and not a withdrawal, the letter said.
 "In the letter to Annan, Lebanon expressed its rejection of "Israel's" vengeful threats against Lebanon if any incident were to occur on the border.
If such events were to take place, they should be dealt with in line with international legitimacy and not by bombarding civilian targets in the heart of Lebanon. Moreover, if "Israel" ­ which is the strongest military force in the region ­ cannot protect its border, how would Lebanon be able to do so? it asked.
The letter said any "Israeli" failure to withdraw from various parts of Lebanese territory will be considered as an obstacle hindering the implementation of the UNs mission, which stipulates that the responsibility of UNIFIL is a two-stage operation.
In the first stage, UNIFIL will confirm the withdrawal of "Israeli" forces from Lebanese territory to the international border. Once this is achieved, it will establish and maintain an area of operation, the letter said, quoting Article 6 of Resolution 426.
In other words, the letter added, the implementation of the second phase, that is, the establishment of an area of operation, does not begin before the first phase ­ a full "Israeli" withdrawal ­ is completed.
The letter also said that a full "Israel" withdrawal requires the release of all detainees in "Israeli" prisons and Khiam detention center.
In reference to Lahouds memorandum to Annan last month, the letter said that the position expressed was not distinct from the memorandum but is rather a detailed document complementing it.
A UN statement issued after the talks said the meeting took place in a warm, cordial and constructive atmosphere.
In the afternoon, Larsen met with Speaker Nabih Berri who insisted that the UN obtain the proper maps that prove Shibaa was part of Lebanese territory.
According to sources close to the speaker, Berri cautioned against a premature enlargement of the 4,500-strong UN force in south Lebanon, arguing that if "Israel" failed to implement a complete withdrawal UNIFIL would turn into de facto guards of the Jewish state.
Earlier, the speaker said that Lebanon will never be a guardian for "Israel's" borders.
Wed like to address a message to the United Nations and its delegate who is among us so that itll understand our stand to the letter, Berri said.
Berri was commenting on a recent statement by "Israel's" Prime Minister Ehud Barak in which he said that "Israel" would respect Lebanons airspace.
Lebanon would like to stress that it is not a guardian for "Israel's" borders, he added.


Six detainees released from Khiam(8)

"Six Lebanese were freed from the Khiam detention center on Thursday, but the family members of one of the freed detainees were booted out of their village.
Ali Hammoud of Markaba, Jamal Qassem of Debbine, Isaam Qlit of Rihane and Mohammed Sweidan of Aadshit were released and allowed to return to their villages in the occupation zone.
Abbas Qawsan, 33, from Taibeh, who suffers from heart disease and Elie Yunan, 40, from Maghdousheh, were transferred through the Kfar Tibnite crossing in an International Red Cross car.
Qawsan was immediately whisked from the crossing point to a hospital in Nabatieh for treatment.
The "Israeli"-proxy militia also forced Qawsans wife, Iman Sweid, and his three children to leave their village of Taibeh on Thursday afternoon.
Qawsan, who looked pale and tired, said he was abducted from his village two years ago by the militiamen .
"Last month, the Jewish state released 13 Lebanese detainees held in "Israeli" prisons but held on to two leaders as bargaining chips".
