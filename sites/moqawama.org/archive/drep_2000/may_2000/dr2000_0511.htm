Daily Report

May Thursday 11-2000:


Introduction

Hizbullah's Deputy Secretary General H.E. Sheikh Nahim Qassem casted doubt on agent Antoine Lahd's ability to carry out the threat he had launched on Tuesday regarding his intention to maintain a border strip along the border with occupied Palestine.

Despite the assurances from the "Israeli" government towards its allied militiamen, the lack of any concrete plans at this late stage suggests that the "Israelis" are facing a real dilemma in finding an appropriate solution for their longtime allies.

Meanwhile, Lebanon's President Emile Lahoud said that the "Israeli" withdrawal will be a victory for us reiterating the Lebanese insistence on an "Israeli" pullout from Shibaa farms.

 

Hizbullah: agent Lahd is extorting, his strip means continuation of occupation(1)
"Hizbullah Deputy Secretary General his Eminence Sheikh Nahim Qassem casted doubt on agent Antoine Lahd's ability to carry out the threat he had launched on Tuesday regarding his intention to maintain a border strip along the border with occupied Palestine.

Lahd's threat is a way to have the UN intervene and say to Lebanon: "We have a problem here, help us solve it and give these people (the collaborators) something, Sheikh Qassem told as-Safir daily.

Concerning the liberation of Aramta yesterday, Qassem said, "Evacuations will be repeated over the next few days; they may be part of an "Israeli" plan to partition the pullout steps in order to curb the big withdrawal in the last day."

What Lahd is saying aims at political extortion. The strip he intends to establish is an "Israeli" occupation, and we will deal with it as a strip whose responsibility is completely shouldered by ""Israel"", and not restricted to Lahd's group. Any explanation that might be given in this regard is rejected, the deputy secretary-general said.

"I think the UN envoy, Terje Roed-Larsen, heard the Lebanese officials reject that the collaborators get any special treatment, be given guarantees or that they do not undertake full responsibility for their acts".

 

Lahoud salutes youths withdrawal will be victory for us(2)

President Emile Lahoud said that he had high expectations of Lebanese youth and national unity on Wednesday, stressing that the "Israeli" withdrawal was a victory for Lebanon and a prelude to economic prosperity.
Our young people have proved their strength. Theyre strong because they suffered during the war and paid dearly to become adults, he told visitors. They used to ignore shelling and go to school without knowing if theyd be back. One example of their strength is their liberation of Arnoun.
"Israeli" troops annexed Arnoun into the occupation zone last year but it was reclaimed by unarmed students who tore down the barbed-wire barricades. "Israel", however, re-occupied the village weeks later and has retained control ever since.
In peacetime, our young people will continue to build up their strength, said Lahoud, who has decorated the reception hall at the Presidential Palace with photographs of young people liberating Arnoun.
The president hailed Lebanons unity, saying: In the past, people used to shoot each other for any trivial reason. Today, we have a truly national army and a resistance movement. Sectarianism has no place in the army or the resistance movement. He said that thanks to Syria, he managed to rebuild the 75,000-strong army while he was its commander between 1989 and 1998.
On "Israel's" expect withdrawal, he said: We want the withdrawal to take place according to international resolutions. It is "Israel", not Lebanon, which has a long history of violating international law. They say they want to leave before their self-imposed deadline of July. Let them leave today. The withdrawal will be a victory for us.
Lahoud warned of "Israeli" plans to destabilize Lebanon, hinting that former army commander General Michel Aoun had mobilized his supporters to call for Syrias withdrawal to serve "Israel's" interests.
The young people who demonstrated are not collaborators with "Israel", he said. Those who mobilized them are.
He reiterated Lebanons insistence on an "Israeli" pullout from Shibaa, saying that only Lebanon and Syria had the right to delineate their common border.
The United Nations doesnt have the authority to interfere in such matters. An expert in international law agreed with this when I met a UN delegation last week, he said.
He also said that Beirut had turned down a request to have human-rights groups attend the trials of the Israeli-proxy militiamen, saying that their presence would violate Lebanons sovereignty.
Lahoud, who received several MPs, including Chouf MP Walid Jumblatt, accused "Israel" of launching air strikes  to discourage foreign businessmen from investing here.
The "Israelis" want Lebanon to remain at their mercy, he argued, because they dont want it to become an economic threat to them.

 

Hoss: Lahd should turn himself in(3)
"Prime Minister Salim Hoss reiterated his call on Wednesday for the Israeli-ellied militia commander Antoine Lahd to turn himself in to Lebanese authorities.
I said it two days ago and I repeat it now: Antoine Lahd must turn himself in to the judiciary instead of asking for amnesty for his militiamen who continue to aggress Lebanese civilians and army, Hoss said.
He was addressing a delegation of Mediterranean news agencies directors who had called on him with head of the National News Agency, Khalil Khoury. He said it was normal for the authorities to look into the militias case without revenge or acrimony but within the context of the legal texts that were currently applicable.
Hoss hoped that once "Israel" withdrew, the foreign media would deal with the news on Lebanon in a positive way so that Arab and foreign investors would be encouraged to come. The foreign media usually portrays Lebanon in a negative way because of the "Israeli" aggressions that take place on a daily basis, which gives the impression that the situation in Lebanon is not stable, he said.
Hoss said the Shibaa farms were 100 percent Lebanese based on the ownership documents released by the Lebanese authorities and court rulings.
If these farms werent Lebanese, the real estate authorities would not have been able to issue relevant documents and the judiciary wouldnt have ruled in conflicts surrounding them based on their ownership by Lebanese citizens, he said.
"He added that Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa had confirmed to UN envoy Terje Roed-Larsen that the land was Lebanese during their meeting on Sunday".

 

Israeli-proxy militia rank and file set to be hung out to dry by "Israel"(4)
"Despite assurances from the "Israeli" government that the well-being of the Israeli-ellied militia militiamen is being taken into account, the lack of any concrete plans at this late stage suggests that the "Israelis" still have no clear idea what do with their longtime allies.
The uncertainty and the looming deadline for the last phase of the withdrawal led one seasoned military source to suspect that the "Israelis" will have no alternative but to dump the militiamen. The "Israelis" are hoping that the UN could step in to offer some degree of protection for the militiamen following the withdrawal.
During his trip to Beirut last week, Terje Roed-Larsen, the UN Middle East peace coordinator, raised the possibility of deploying UN human-rights observers in the South to monitor events in the post-withdrawal phase. The observers could also attend future trials of militiamen, he suggested. Larsen apparently received no prior authorization from the UN to make such a request. Instead, he had been encouraged to raise the "Israeli" suggestion, made during his meetings in the Jewish state.
The government rejected Larsens proposal immediately. The "Israelis" are trying to get the UN to handle the collaborators but if nothing works then the militia is going to be dumped very badly, the source said.
The "Israelis" had considered allowing the militia to continue functioning as a single military entity complete with tanks and artillery. The UN has told "Israel", however, that it is obliged to disarm the militia if Resolution 425 is to be fulfilled.
The "Israelis" have yet to formally decide whether to comply but it is unlikely they will refuse the demand and risk losing UN support for withdrawal.
On Tuesday, the Israeli agents Commander Antoine Lahd vowed that his militia would keep their own weapons and continue manning the zone after the "Israelis" withdraw.
I wont impose any obligatory military service, he said. Everybody will fight to defend his dignity and not to be humiliated in prison as a traitor for his homeland.
Despite Lahds defiant tone, the most likely prospect for the militia is that it will fragment into village-based civil guard units ­ a process that has already begun. The intention behind establishing the civil guards has more to do with protecting former militiamen from reprisals than maintaining the zone.
Any serious attempt to maintain the collaborators after the "Israelis" have withdrawn would be doomed to failure. Not only would the militia lose its air and artillery support, but the militiamen would no longer receive a pay packet at the end of each month .Senior collaborators and the militias security personnel are expected to leave with the "Israelis". Nabih Abu Rifai, the commander of the eastern sector, reportedly has been packing and transporting his household goods from Hasbaya to a new home in Metulla.
Those left behind will consist of low-ranking militiamen.
"But the greatest risk is during the volatile period before the final departure of the "Israelis", when the militia may seek to disrupt the process by deliberately targeting civilians. The scenario is not without precedent. The killing of two Christian women in Qatrani last Thursday by Lahdi tank fire provided the spark for the sudden cross-border escalation.
On March 13, a Lebanese soldier and a civilian were killed and two wounded when a Lahdi tank deliberately shelled a checkpoint in Mansouri. On that occasion, Hizbullah refrained from responding.
Hizbullahs decision last Friday to ignore "Israeli" threats and fire a second volley of Katyushas into "Israel" demonstrated that the resistance is prepared for a final showdown. The militia ­ deciding it has nothing to lose ­ may choose to provide the spark that will result in a bloody conclusion to "Israel's" occupation".

 

Israelis proxy militia hard put to hold front-line outposts(5)
" The militia has in recent weeks encountered difficulties in holding onto its front-line outposts on the border of the security zone, an area often attacked by the Hizbullah. Only a small number of soldiers now man the front-line outposts, where most of the militia's casualties have been sustained.
These difficulties, according to "Israeli" sources, is one of the reasons for the militias dismantling of four front-line outposts since February. All the abandoned outposts were in the same area - the northern tip of the eastern sector of the security zone.
As a result of the abandonment of these outposts, the area controlled by the collaborators has narrowed by several kilometers.
The Israeli Army is extremely concerned over the militias performance in upcoming weeks. Already at the beginning of the year, senior Israeli officers speculated that the "moment of truth" for the militia would be during the month of May, when its commanders realize that "Israel" is seriously planning to withdraw from South Lebanon in the beginning of July, or earlier.
Sources in the security zone told Ha'aretz that they believe the only reason that most of the militiamen "continue to show up for work" is the anticipation of financial compensation from "Israel". "This is a poor area," the sources say. "The rumor here is that "Israel" will give each militiamen some $15,000 before the withdrawal. That is a lot of money in Lebanese terms, enough to buy a small house. Most of the fighters are not leaving the militia, for the time being, because they are afraid of risking this money."

 Lebanese villagers look for family graves as they return to old home(6)

" In the cemetery of Aramta, villagers peer at the overgrown tombstones they had been unable to tend for the 17 years of enforced absence which ended Tuesday when the Lebanese authorities took back control of the village from "Israel's" proxy militia.

Ismail Hajjeh can only rely on his intuition to find the graves of his parents who died while he was away.

"Perhaps it's these here. Someone said they are close to our neighbour Subhiyeh," says Mariam, 60, who has put on her glasses to help her make out the names written in ink on the tombstones, which are all piled up and leaning crazily against each other.

"No, they are too recent. My father died in 1984, and my mother followed him the next year," replies Ismail, continuing his desperate search.

"Come and look over here," shouts old Khadijeh, kneeling by two unnamed tombs, in the shade of a huge walnut tree whose branches have been shattered by shells.

Ismail runs over.

"My heart tells me it isn't them. But even so I shall recite the prayer for the repose of their souls," he says.

In the end, Ismail did manage to track down the right graves, thanks to Abu Siraj, one of the 110 villagers who remained behind in Aramta out of the 5,000 who lived there when "Israel" occupied it in 1978.

He cleaned up the stones and laid flowers on them in a long-delayed act of filial piety.

Mariam was looking for the final resting places of her mother and brother-in-law.

"I left Aramta exactly 15 years and six months ago. I was counting the days, and now I have come back, I hope for good," she said.

Fatima Mansur had gathered a huge bunch of blood-red anemones among the graves of the tumbledown cemetery. Dressed in black from head to foot, she had come with her daughter Huda, born "in exile" 18 years ago, to visit for the first time the grave of her husband who died in 1998.

"His body was brought back by the International Committee of the Red Cross and Lahad's men only allowed one person to go with it," she said, referring to "Israel's" proxy militia.

Abdel Latif Jahjah, who never left, chimed in: "They sent us the bodies by registered post, with no-one to accompany them to the grave."

He said he expected hundreds of people to pour into the village Sunday to take part in ceremonies for Ayub al-Ali, who died just before the militia withdrew on Monday.

Dozens of villagers managed to take time off work Wednesday to celebrate the liberation of their village, which was decked out with the colours of Hizbullah, which has spearheaded efforts to force "Israel" out of Lebanese territory.

Red and yellow flags bearing Hizbullah's emblem fluttered from telegraph poles along the road leading to the village and from the shell-shattered houses.

"Congratulations to Aramta, congratulations to the motherland" read a huge banner emblazoned with the same emblem, stretched out across the square where the village imam, Sheikh Ibrahim Husseini, a Hizbullah supporter, led open-air prayers for some 30 of the faithful.

"After 18 years of exile I have come to share with those villagers who resisted the joy of liberation and of the victory achieved by the Islamic Resistance," Hizbullah's armed branch, he told AFP.

Hizbullah forced the Israeli agents out of Aramta on April 28 when they planted explosive devices which killed three militiamen and injured four others.

"The local authorities Tuesday dismantled the road block of earth and concrete slabs which the militia had erected at the entrance to the village, and took back control".

The occupation's shells that landed in Kfar Romman damaged the houses of Hassan Ali Ahmed and Hussein Ali Ahmed besides killing some cattle.

The enemy's shelling also concentrated on the Habboush-Arab Salim road which is usually crowded in the morning by school buses. Pupils had to go back to their homes in order to avoid the risk, especially the Iqleem al-Toffah students.

Metulla settlers launch strike, demand harsher reprisal measures(10)
"Lt.-Col. (res) Dubi Amitai, a Metulla setler and one of the organizers of the protest, said it was the first time he could recall citizens of the town, which abuts the Lebanese border, taking such action.

"We are demanding that Prime Minister [Ehud] Barak come to us and make a public commitment that any attacks on northern communities will be met with the kind of response that will deter any future breaches of the peace - even if this means wiping out the village from which the attacks originated," Amitai said.

"At the moment we have no guarantees whatsoever that we will be protected after the proposed withdrawal, particularly after the ineffectual response to Hizbullah's recent Katyusha rocket attacks."

St.-Sgt. Maj. Shaked Ozeri, 24, of the village of Elyachin near Hadera, was killed in one of the rocket attacks last Thursday and Friday. More than 30 settlers of Kiryat Shmona and Shlomi, in Western Galilee - were lightly wounded or had to be treated for shock.

"Metulla's Amitai, who said he served on numerous occasions in south Lebanon as a battalion commander and reserve officer, charged that the Israeli Armys hands were being tied in the face of Hizbullah attack.

He stressed that settlers of Metulla, Kiryat Shmona, Shlomi, and other communities along the northern border were no longer prepared to be cannon fodder for fighters operating from Lebanon.

He added that the relocation of the security fence to the 1923 international border would bring the line to within several meters of homes in Metulla and endanger city settlers. In such circumstances, the army would not have enough time to respond to infiltration attempts before fighters reached the nearest houses, Amitai said.

Metulla settlers are demanding that the fence be constructed at least a few hundred meters from their homes. They also demand appropriate defensive measures and a clear-cut directive from the government for the Israeli Army to retaliate with all its force to attacks emanating from Lebanon on "Israeli" territory or its settlers.

"We are also demanding a meeting in Metulla with the prime minister before any decisions are implemented regarding our future security, safety, and welfare," Amitai said.

The Metulla settlerss strike is being supported by the local council headed by Ya'acov Katz, who also has expressed concern over what the future holds for the region in light of the withdrawal plans.

Amitai, who is chairman of the farmers' association in Metulla, said that as a family man with a wife and three children, whose home could be in the future firing line, he was more than just concerned".
