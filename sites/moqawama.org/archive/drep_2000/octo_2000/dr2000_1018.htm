“Israel” close to admitting army colonel was captured in Lebanon…(1)

”London's Daily Telegraph newspaper said the “Israeli” army colonel captured by Hizbullah may be Hanan Tannenbaum who was among five “Israeli” agents arrested in Lausanne, Switzerland in 1998 while trying to bug the flat of a man suspected of belonging to Hizbullah. 

“Israeli” officials have admitted Tannenbaum may have been captured in Lebanon after initially claiming the reserve colonel had gone missing in Switzerland and was kidnapped in Europe. An advisor to Prime Minister Ehud Barak said Hizbullah Secretary General Sayyid Hassan Nasrallah is very honest and does not lie so the incident has to be carefully checked. “Israel’s” military censors deleted parts of a Reuters report in which Lieutenant-Colonel Udi Levy, Barak's advisor, refused to reject Nasrallah's description of how Tannenbaum was captured in Lebanon. The “Israeli” government has insisted that Tannenbaum had no connection with the security services and claimed he was a businessman in Switzerland. But “Israeli” officials have been hinting Tannenbaum may have been captured in Lebanon. Swiss police have so far said they have no information on a missing “Israeli”. Nasrallah said Tannenbaum was working for the Mossad intelligence agency and had been trying to recruit a high-level Hizbullah member. He stressed, the “Israeli” had flown to Beirut from Brussels recently with a forged foreign passport to meet the Hizbullah member with whom he had established contact through a mediator. Nasrallah told reporters only Hizbullah is responsible for carrying out the operation and stressed the capture was legal since Tannenbaum was caught spying for “Israel” inside Lebanese territory. The news of his capture first broke on Sunday, when Nasrallah announced resistance fighters had taken prisoner an “Israeli” colonel in the intelligence services involved in a "complex operation". The news stunned “Israel”, still shell-shocked by the capture of three of its soldiers by Hizbullah on October 7 from the occupied Shibaa Farms. “Israeli” press said Nasrallah's announcement "amazed and embarrassed “Israel’s” security establishment which did not know anything about the incident". “Israel” launched a frantic investigation. A Al-Quds court then slapped a ban on the publication of any information about Tannenbaum's activities abroad and the capture itself, a blackout recommended by the internal security agency Shin Beth that set off a flurry of speculation. Nasrallah said Tannenbaum was an artillery officer in the “Israeli” army who had taken part in “Israel’s” invasion of Lebanon in 1982 and the siege of Beirut, and lived with his family in Tel Aviv. Barak raised the incident with UN Secretary General Kofi Annan at the Sharm el-Sheikh summit in Egypt, saying he expects decisive action from the international body for Tannenbaum's release. Annan had been mediating a possible prisoner swap, but Hizbullah accused “Israel” of not being serious in efforts to release the “Israeli” soldiers. Nasrallah said the UN and Russia have been spearheading efforts to swap the three “Israelis”, now four, with Lebanese and other detainees held by the Jewish state. “Israel” holds at least nineteen Lebanese as hostage, many of them for more than a decade without trial or charge, in clear violation of international law. 

Warrants for collaborators…(2)

Two military investigating magistrates issued arrest warrants for nine collaborators Tuesday on charges of joining the enemy’s army, spying, and entering enemy territory. Riad Talih and Antoine Suleiman issued the warrants after completing interrogations.

 

Widespread disappointment regarding Sharm el-Sheikh …(3)

The conclusion of the Sharm el-Sheikh summit in Egypt on Tuesday spurred negative reactions from Lebanese and Palestinian politicians in the run-up to a two-day Arab summit beginning on Saturday.
Caretaker Premier Salim Hoss expressed disappointment that the summit failed to take a clear stand on all the pending issues related to the Palestinian track of the peace process.
“We had hoped the participants would reach a clear declaration of principles to resolve all the unsettled national issues that caused the Palestinian intifada, including Al-Quds, the Palestinian refugees’ right of return and “Israeli” existence on Arab land,” Hoss said in a statement.
“The problem in Palestine is not a mere security issue solved by security measures,” he added, “but rather the cause of a people with the right to live.”
Hoss will leave for Cairo on Wednesday to participate in a two-day meeting of Arab foreign ministers starting on Thursday. The meeting will discuss the uprising that has swept through the occupied Palestinian territories, as well as the outcome of the Sharm el-Sheikh summit.
The ministers are expected to set the agenda for the emergency Arab summit on Oct. 21-22.
President Emile Lahoud will head Lebanon’s delegation to the Arab summit.
Progressive Socialist Party leader Walid Jumblatt said: “The Sharm el-Sheikh summit decisively buried the Oslo Accords.”
Speaking after Parliament’s inaugural session, Jumblatt called on the summit’s participants to secure UN Resolution 242, which calls for “Israel” to withdraw from all territories it occupied after the 1967 war, and urged the establishment of a Palestinian state.
He said Palestinians could not regain their dignity unless Jewish settlements were eliminated and Al-Quds was recovered.
“I believe there is a new Arab current,” he said, adding that if this segment of the population wants war, Arabs have the means to wage one.
“Why be afraid,” Jumblatt asked, “when a small unit of Hizbullah members can destroy the “Israeli” military machine?”
Meanwhile, a visiting Palestinian leader said that regardless of the results of the Sharm el-Sheikh summit, Palestinian resistance will persist as long as “Israeli” aggression continues.
Asaad Abdel-Rahman, a member of the Palestine Liberation Organization’s Executive Committee, spoke to reporters following a tour of the South during which he visited Sidon and inspected the Rashidieh refugee camp in Tyre.
Speaking of the accord reached in Egypt, Abdel-Rahman, who is also refugee affairs minister, said that what had been described as an agreement was in fact a “diplomatic expression of failure.”
“Whoever carefully scrutinizes the details can see this clearly, since the summit did not deal with the root of the problem.
“Accordingly,” he warned, “tension will continue and will probably escalate.”
Abdel-Rahman echoed Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak’s remarks that the gathering failed to meet Arab aspirations.

 

Palestinian refugees decry crisis summit…(4)

“For the second consecutive day, thousands of Palestinian refugees staged demonstrations across the country on Tuesday to support the Palestinian uprising and protest the Sharm al-Sheikh crisis summit in Egypt.
Some 3,000 refugees marched through the streets of the Beddawi refugee camp chanting anti-”Israeli” slogans.
Protesters slammed the US-brokered summit, claiming its aim was to abort the Palestinian uprising. “The Sharm al-Sheikh summit is a plot to strike the intifada,” the crowd chanted. “Al-Quds is burning in front of the world. Where is the conscience of the people?”
At the  Nahr al-Bared refugee camp, a simultaneous demonstration was staged by 4,000 refugees holding banners protesting the summit: “Jews we are coming to get you from every house or street.”
The Palestinian factions alliance in the North denounced the summit and renewed its support for the intifada.
“We condemn the Sharm al-Sheikh summit which is preparing to slaughter and abort the intifada politically ... The uprising of our people will continue, and we will not be intimidated by the “Israeli” enemy’s planes, tanks or bullets,” it said.
In Tyre, Palestinian doctors gathered outside the headquarters of the United Nations Interim Forces in southern Lebanon (UNIFIL) to denounce the “barbaric “Israeli” Army.”
“It is totally inconceivable that “Israeli” soldiers fire on children and on ambulances in the Palestinian territories,” said one of doctors, Mohammed Ataya.
The doctors delivered a letter for UN Secretary General Kofi Annan to a UNIFIL officer.
“We ask for the international protection of our people and for the children of Palestine, who are the victims of a war of extermination on their own land,” the document said.

 

Human rights groups condemn the use of 'excessive and deadly force'…(5)

Two of the world's leading human rights organisations accused “Israel” yesterday of using excessive force to control the uprising across the occupied territories and in Arab towns.

The reports, published by Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch on the eve of an emergency meeting of the UN Human Rights Commission, both said many lives had been lost unnecessarily because of the methods used by “Israeli” forces. Amnesty also pointed to a failure on the part of both sides to investigate the events of the past three weeks.

Elizabeth Hodgkin, one of two members of the Amnesty team that visited “Israel” and the occupied territories between 4 and 13 October, said: "There may have been one or two points where “Israeli” soldiers had the right to return fire because they were fired on. But really there was practically no occasion that we visited that could not have been solved by other policing methods ... without the loss of life."

Ms Hodgkin said the team "found the mindset of the policing of demonstrations and riots in the occupied territories and in “Israel” was that of using military methods; that is to say, of eliminating an enemy, rather than policing methods of serving the community and preserving lives". She said: "On almost every occasion we saw an extremely rapid escalation to the use of firearms", and the scenes of demonstrations showed evidence of random shooting.

In a more strongly worded assessment, Human Rights Watch said it had found "a pattern of repeated “Israeli” use of excessive lethal force ... in situations where demonstrators were unarmed and posed no threat of death or serious injury to the security forces or to others". It added: "Where gunfire by Palestinian security forces or armed protesters was a factor, use of lethal force by the “Israeli” Army was indiscriminate and not directed at the source of the threat, in violation of international law enforcement standards.

"In “Israel”, the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, the “Israeli” Army regularly used rubber bullets and plastic-coated metal bullets as well as live ammunition in an excessive or indiscriminate manner.

"A particularly egregious example of such unlawful fire is the Army’s use of mediumcalibre bullets against unarmed demonstrators in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, and in some instances ... against medical personnel. These military weapons, which inflict massive trauma when striking flesh, are normally used to penetrate concrete and are not appropriate for crowd control."

Today, the 53-nation UN Human Rights Commission will convene in emergency session – only the fifth such meeting in its history – to discuss the violence. “Israel” has described the meeting, requested by the Arab League, as "inopportune".

Arab members are expected to push for a mandate for UN investigators to travel to the region and for the session to determine the "initial responsibility" of the Likud party leader, Ariel Sharon, for the cycle of violence. His visit to the Haram Al-Sharif  is widely regarded as the catalyst for the violence.

Responding to the Amnesty report yesterday, Tuvia “Israeli”, “Israel’s” deputy permanent representative to the UN in Geneva, said: "We tried to control an eruption of violence ... at the beginning trying to be restricted. Once it went out of control and there was shooting and Molotov cocktails, bottles thrown at civilians and soldiers, we had to react ... otherwise the violence would have gone further."

 

Jewish settlers kill Palestinian…(6)

Palestinian President Yasser Arafat a short while ago warned that “Israeli” violence contradicts the Sharm el-Sheikh accord. But anti-Palestinian attacks have continued unabated. A Palestinian man was shot dead by Jewish settlers in a village near Nablus in the North West Bank. Residents of the village of Beit Furiq said twenty-eight year old Farid Moussa was shot by settlers while picking olives. His death brought to more than 105 the number of Palestinians martyred by “Israelis” since unrest swept across the occupied territories late last month. Jewish settlers also injured six Palestinians near Nablus. Settlers had gone on a rampage near the West Bank town of Hebron last night, shooting and wounding three Palestinians as “Israeli” helicopter gunships attacked the Palestinian town. Ten Palestinians were also injured by live “Israeli” gunfire at the Erez crossing point linking the Gaza Strip to “Israel” and in Bethlehem. One of the men is in serious condition.
