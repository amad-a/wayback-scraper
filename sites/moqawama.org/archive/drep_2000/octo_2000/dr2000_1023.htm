Imam Khamenei's message to the Arab leaders: "History is watching and muslims await a lot from you"…(1)

Leader of the Islamic Nation Ayatollah Sayyed Ali Khamenei reminded the Arab leaders, participating in the Summit, of the great responsibility they carry towards the Palestinian cause, and that the muslim peoples await a lot from them.

Addressing 110,000 affiliates of the Basiji Force in Tehran on Friday, Ayatollah Khamenei affirmed that the Palestinian question will not be solved in the Summit.

"However, such a Summit can answer the calls and match the aspiration of the Palestinians", said Imam Khamenei.

The Islamic leader also maintained that any decision taken by the Arab leaders in the Summit will be judged by history. He added, "Arab leaders should free themselves from the American influence, and gain honor by taking the right measures". Ayatollah Khamenei also called for the establishment of an Arab Islamic Court whose mission would be to put on trial the “Israelis” who committed crimes against the Palestinians.

 

Intifada could lead to wider struggle
Resistance says ‘failure’ of summit makes cause stronger…(2)

Hizbullah said on Sunday that the results of the Arab summit would strengthen the intifada, resulting in more powerful armed confrontations with “Israel”.
“We expect the insufficient decisions made by Arab leaders to help in propelling the intifada to a stronger level, leading to forceful and better armed confrontations,” Mahmoud Qmati, deputy head of the party’s politburo, told The Daily Star.
Hizbullah denounced the Arab leaders’ failure to make firm decisions to boycott “Israel” during the Cairo summit, and said the party had low expectations of the outcome.
Such results only strengthened the conviction that the only way to face “Israel” was through fighting, Qmati said.
“The least we expected was that Arab leaders cut trade relations with “Israel” and close “Israeli” offices and embassies in Arab countries that have relations with the enemy,” he said.
Hizbullah had hoped the decisions would “do justice” for the dozens of Palestinians who sacrificed their lives over the past three weeks during the intifada.
“We wanted Arab countries to open their borders to allow for Palestinians to defend their land or at least express their support for the rising intifada and take action to encourage it,” he added.
Describing the decisions made by the leaders as “ineffective on the political level,” Qmati said the strongest evidence of this was “Israeli” Prime Minister Ehud Barak’s praise for Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak maintaining a “balanced approach” during the summit.
“What stronger evidence do we need?” Qmati asked.
The Hizbullah official said he hoped the next Arab summit, in March, would achieve “more serious results.”
“We don’t expect “Israeli” aggressiveness against the Palestinians to stop. Therefore, we hope by that time that the Arab people are able to exert even more pressure on their governments,” he said.

Lahoud has mixed take on summit…(3)

President Emile Lahoud said Sunday that the Arab summit set the ground for “a new phase of collective Arab work” but argued that resolutions adopted by leaders were below their publics’ expectations.
“The decision that the summit will convene annually raises the hope for firmer joint Arab action on a more solid and dynamic basis,” Lahoud told reporters en route to Beirut from Cairo following the end of the two-day summit on Sunday.
“Arab people were perhaps expecting firmer stands or a more applicable plan of action … but the summit succeeded in unifying the Arab stand and in warning “Israel” that Arabs might adopt substantial measures against it as an aggressor,” the president said.
Regarding public criticism over the failure to cut ties with “Israel”, Lahoud said: “The final statement on normalization with “Israel” was not in harmony with the stands and aspirations of the Arab people. But Lebanon did not object to the final statement, as the Arabs consented to it.”
While reading Lebanon’s address at the summit, Lahoud cautioned the leaders that their citizens would react badly if they failed to adopt “a strong and united” stand against “Israel”.
“I also stressed that “Israel” must pull back to the June 4, 1967 line, that Palestinian refugees must return to their homeland, that the Shibaa Farms must be liberated and that all Lebanese detainees held by “Israel” liberated. “Israel” must also compensate Lebanon.”
Lahoud added that though the summit was devoted to
Palestinian developments, he addressed the need for Lebanon to receive financial assistance  promised by Arabs to help the South. Lahoud said he broached this subject during his talks with Arab leaders and during the summit’s closed meeting.
“Arab leaders told me they were committed to extending this aid and that there was no need for new decisions in that respect since the decisions made were still valid,” he said.
Lahoud met with Syrian President Bashar Assad on three occasions during the summit, adding that “Lebanon and Syria have many joint interests that require permanent coordination and consultation.”
Arab leaders Lahoud met on the sidelines of the summit included Saudi Crown Prince Abdullah Bin Abdel Aziz and Jordan’s King Abdullah II.
He also discussed bilateral relations with Algerian President Abdel-Aziz Bouteflika.
In a letter to Hosni Mubarak prior to departure, Lahoud thanked the Egyptian president for his hospitality and efforts “to give collective Arab action a new dimension.”

 

President: Arabs have a lot to learn from Lebanon…(4)

President Emile Lahoud addressed the Arab summit in Cairo on Saturday by citing the need to learn from Lebanon’s experience of national unity in confronting “Israeli” occupation.
The following is the full text of the speech, as conveyed by the National News Agency.
“I begin my brief and unprepared speech by thanking President Mubarak for giving me the opportunity to speak in the opening session.
“Lebanon suffered tremendously from the “Israeli” occupation and paid a high price.
“But the Lebanese people, after having rallied around their strong state, managed to realize the greatest achievement in their modern history, liberating their land from “Israeli” occupation.
“I am certain that, when Arab countries take a unified stand to liberate all occupied Arab territories and ensure the Palestinian people’s right of return to their land, Arab countries will succeed.
“In Lebanon, we suffered harshly for 20 years of “Israeli” occupation and its practices.
“Today, we know what the Palestinian people are suffering and what aggressions and troubles they endure. But we also know that unless Arab leaders are up to the responsibilities that their people charge them with, relations between the people and their leaders will be shaken.
“That is why we should have a united and firm stand concerning the events in Palestine and face the crisis with the responsibility imposed by this sensitive stage.
“I urge you to take bold decisions capable of solving the continuing tragedy in Palestine. We can say to our peoples that we are with them on the way to liberation and comprehensive and permanent peace.
“I also say from this lofty Arab forum that some Arab territories are still occupied. Consequently, peace cannot be achieved unless all lands are liberated. The quicker we take bold decisions, the quicker we achieve peace.
“Mister President, I thank you for giving me the opportunity to deliver this speech, which springs from my conscience. I say to you and to Arab leaders that Lebanon feels today, as always, united in solidarity with you and committed to a unified Arab decision, which we hope will achieve everything that we wish.”

 

Arab leaders slap “Israelis” on the wrist
Final statement stops short of all-out diplomatic freeze…(5)

“Arab leaders deployed their political and financial clout in support of the Palestinian intifada on Sunday, but failed to rupture ties with “Israel”, ignoring the anger in the streets to give the peace process a last chance.
Still, the Arab summit’s stern warning to “Israel” to end the onslaught against the Palestinians or risk a stringent boycott enraged “Israeli” Prime Minister Ehud Barak, who called for a “time-out” in the peace process. Palestinian President Yasser Arafat lashed back ­ “let him go to hell” ­ as clashes raged on in the West Bank and Gaza.
Despite clear signs of disappointments from a number of participants, the two-day emergency summit in Cairo scored a major breakthrough, bringing together leaders who had been at odds since the Iraqi invasion of Kuwait for the first time in 10 years. It also signaled Iraq’s return to the Arab fold.
Syrian President Bashar Assad made an impressive first stride onto the Arab stage, cautioning in a speech on Saturday against “empty decisions” that would not be fulfilled.
President Emile Lahoud, also making his first appearance at an Arab summit, implored participants to take decisions that “match the anger in the streets.”
Arab leaders “risked their own people abandoning them if they failed to meet their expectations,” Lahoud warned.
Although severing relations with “Israel” was widely supported, host Egypt finally triumphed, with President Hosni Mubarak repeatedly reminding participants that they were there to try to salvage the peace process, not pour oil on the fire.
Libya walked out in protest, but the rest of the 21  members all sealed the final communique, albeit some of them reluctantly. Iraq recorded reservations, insisting that the Arabs should have mobilized all their resources for a jihad, or Holy War, against “Israel”.
“The Arab leaders stress their commitment to firmly challenge “Israeli” attempts to infiltrate the Arab world in any form, and from now on to stop opening relations with “Israel”,” the summit’s final communique said. “They hold “Israel” responsible for any steps and decisions made by Arab states regarding relations with “Israel”, including their cancellation.
“The Arab people were expecting firmer resolutions from the summit,” Lahoud said on his return to Beirut Sunday. Still, he said, “the summit succeeded in adopting a unified position that warned “Israel” against continuing its aggressions and opens the way for future concrete action.”
The Palestinians emerged from the two-day summit with a solid $1 billion pledge, initiated by Saudi Arabia ­ which, significantly, had sided with the hard-line calls for ostracizing “Israel”.
The summit demanded that the UN Security Council to “form an international criminal court to try “Israeli” war criminals,” in line with similar tribunals for alleged war criminals in Rwanda and Yugoslavia.
It demanded that Security Council and the UN General Assembly take measures to protect the Palestinians, and requested the establishment of an impartial international committee under UN auspices to investigate the “causes of and responsibility for” the Palestinian-”Israeli” violence. Qatar’s emir, Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al-Thani promised to cover the costs of the inquiry.
The Arab leaders stressed that they would settle for nothing less than total withdrawal from land occupied in 1967, offering full backing for Syria and Lebanon in their quest to recover the Golan Heights and the Shibaa Farms. They also demanded the release of all Arab detainees.
A significant decision, which was all but overshadowed by the emphasis on the plight of the Palestinians, was a written commitment signed separately by the Arab leaders to revive annual summits, with the first scheduled for March in Amman.
The Egyptian president surprised Lahoud, inviting him to take the floor during the inaugural session. Lahoud delivered an unprepared statement in colloquial Arabic, apologizing for not having prepared a speech.
Reuters news agency said Lahoud had given the text of his address Assad, who failed to return it in time. But members of the Lebanese delegation said the president had not counted on addressing the parley, as Premier Salim Hoss had presented Lebanon’s position during the preparatory foreign ministerial meeting on Friday.

Tehran couldn’t influence Hizbullah to help free “Israeli” captives…(6)

“ Iranian President Mohammad Khatami said Sunday that his country could not influence Hizbullah to release three “Israeli” soldiers and a reserve colonel captured by the resistance group.
Many diplomats had hoped that Iran, the main backer of Hizbullah, might be able to help persuade the resistance party to reach a deal on the four men, exchanging them for Hizbullah fighters held in “Israel”.
“Hizbullah is an Arab and Lebanese movement,” Khatami said at a joint news conference with visiting Spanish Premier Jose Maria Aznar. “The Islamic Republic of Iran has no power or influence over Hizbullah.”
Spain and France have been asked by the US to push Iran to use its influence on Hizbullah to free the four “Israelis”.
Hizbullah captured three “Israeli” soldiers Oct. 7 in the Shibaa Farms region and later said it lured a fourth “Israeli” to Lebanon, maintaining that he is a Mossad operative. “Israel” says he is a businessman and a reserve army colonel. The party has said it is willing to swap them for Arabs held in “Israeli” jails.
“What is going on in Palestine must be a lesson to everyone as it shows an explosion of anger by a people that has been held back for 50 years,” Khatami said, adding that “Israel” has killed “more than 100 martyrs … with incredible savagery.”
The president said the uprising would continue unless Palestinians rights were recognized.

 

Hizbullah’s burden of hope
Nasrallah revives hopes of families of missing…(7)
Missing’s mother: Nasrallah to me is as precious as my own son

Khadijeh Wehbi has renewed hope of learning the fate of her son, who was kidnapped 24 years ago.
After being bed-ridden for over two months following a government decision to consider those kidnapped during the war as dead, Wehbi is grateful that a “more credible source” ­ Hizbullah ­ has come to the rescue.
Following the resistance party’s capture of three “Israeli” soldiers two weeks ago, Hizbullah leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said the soldiers, and an “Israeli” colonel whose capture was announced last week, would be swapped in return for Arab detainees in “Israel”, bodies of those captured by “Israel” and information about the fate of the missing and kidnapped during the war.
The announcement revived hope among hundreds of families who have yet to recover from the shock of the government decision, issued late in July, to consider the missing and kidnapped as dead.
The government’s report, which declared the missing and kidnapped dead, based its decision on information received from the families and meetings its members held with head of political parties who participated in the war.
The report said that its efforts to obtain evidence to prove that those missing and kidnapped were dead was impossible, little consolation to Wehbi.
Her son, Ahmad Hirbawi, was 16 when he was taken by the Phalange from Achrafieh as he was crossing to West Beirut with his mother and sister.
“He was taken right in front of me. I kept shouting that he was Lebanese and that I was told that all militiamen were allowing the Lebanese to cross,” she said.
Wehbi looks much older than her 60 years. Her eyes reflect the same pain felt by all parents of the kidnapped and missing.
She continued to recount the story of surviving 24 years since her son’s abduction when she began weeping uncontrollably. Minutes later, as she attempted to calm down, Wehbi spoke: “I haven’t cried for you in awhile, my son.”
But when asked again about the government decision, she became angry.
“I reject every word Hoss said because my heart tells me Ahmad will come back. And I’ll continue to believe this until the last minute of my life,” she said.
Wehbi’s justification for refusing Hoss’ decision was that the government, through the committee appointed to discover the fate of the missing and kidnapped, did not provide any evidence of her son’s death.
Now, she is clutching onto Nasrallah’s words. “You know, I used to hate Nasrallah because I hated political parties. But after what he has done for us, I will listen to nobody but him,” she said.
Wehbi sent Nasrallah a letter on Wednesday telling him about her new hopes following his announcement that he wanted “Israel” to reveal the fate of those missing and kidnapped during the war.
Although Wehbi has no evidence that her son may actually be in “Israel”, she still hopes.
In its final report issued in July, on which the government based its decision, the committee said that 216 kidnapped were believed to be in “Israel”.
The responsibility Hizbullah is bearing by shouldering the issue of the kidnapped, in addition to that of the detainees, is undoubtedly huge.
While Wehbi would only believe a government decision if it provided convincing evidence, she expressed her readiness to believe “whatever Nasrallah says, even if he didn’t give us evidence.”
“I will believe him because I know that he only speaks the truth,” she said. “Nasrallah to me is as precious as my own son Ahmad.”
According to Wadad Halwani, a spokeswoman for the families of those missing and kidnapped during the war, Wehbi’s sentiments reflect those of many other families in the same situation, whose hopes were revived after Nasrallah’s comments.
Although Halwani decided to accept the government decision, by announcing the death of her husband Adnan who has been missing for 18 years, other families continued to reject it.
“I decided to make peace with myself and announce that, but I will continue to stand by these families because I understand their persistence. Evidence should be provided to satisfy their hearts,” Halwani said.
Halwani is still struggling for what she believes the state still owes detainees’ families.
Following the government decision, the Committee of the Families of those Missing and Kidnapped during the War demanded that the government hold an official mass funeral for those it declared dead.
Despite President Lahoud’s promises, the date for such a funeral has not yet been set.
Halwani said she was horrified upon learning that the government was considering organizing a wide-scale festival to honor the war-time kidnapped and dead, an idea that now appears to have been dropped.
“I couldn’t believe it,” she said, angrily. “We wanted a funeral. Imagine us participating in a festival that would most probably include officials who were responsible for our sons’ kidnapping in the first place.”

 

Former detainees get used to life after release from Khiam
Lara Sukhtian finds that help has been slow in coming for those still getting used to freedom…(8)

As he lounged around in his living room, 18-year-old Samer Hijazi was unaware of the events that were about to take place, events that would change his life forever.
A high-school student preparing for his upcoming graduation, he never made it to the ceremony. On that fateful afternoon in March 1994, South Lebanon Army militiamen knocked on Hijazi’s door and asked if they could talk to him for “a few minutes.”
“They didn’t just want to talk,” he said, “they wanted to take me away.” Which they did, and those few minutes stretched into six-and-a-half years.
That same afternoon, the SLA arrested Hijazi’s first cousin, Ziad Ghanawi, also 18. In separate cells, surrounded by cold gray walls, they lived the most important developmental years of their lives in an “Israeli”-controlled prison as their hopes for the future dwindled.
Today, the Byblos Training Center is working to give back part of that hope to Hijazi and Ghanawi, and other former detainees, by providing them with an opportunity to get a free education and ease the transition process from detainee to citizen.
“It’s our duty to provide ex-detainees with the education and training they were denied,” said Khalil Haidar, the director of the Training Center in Haret Hreik where Hijazi and Ghanawi will study.
According to Haidar, working to build and improve Lebanese society is an age-old tradition at Byblos. Since 1982, the center has taken in 273 students, free of charge, most of them detainees, resistance fighters, or children of fallen guerrillas.
“Over the years, these men and women have sacrificed their lives for freedom,” said Haidar. “Now we must support them in their efforts to integrate back into society.”
According to Ibrahim Kallash, vice-president of the National Association for Lebanese Detainees in “Israeli” Prisons, about 8,000 Lebanese men and women have been detained in “Israeli” prisons since 1985.
Today 19 Lebanese citizens remain in “Israeli” prisons. But as Kallash made clear, “indeed there are 19 prisoners in “Israel”, but there are about 65 Lebanese that have been kidnapped by “Israeli” forces over the years, never to be heard of again.”
The real tragedy, said Hijazi, was that most of the detainees hadn’t participated in the struggle. He explained how SLA leader Antoine Lahd and “Israeli” soldiers didn’t need a good reason to arrest people. Simply knowing the wrong people was reason enough to be arrested.
This seemed to be the case with Hijazi and Ghanawi; their crime was being acquainted with members of Amal and Hizbullah, something quite common for most residents of the southern village of Houla.
“There was only one rule of thumb practiced by Lahd and “Israel”,” said Hijazi, “If you’re not with us then you’re against us and thus you’re a threat.”
This unwritten policy lead to the random arrest of hundreds, even thousands of Lebanese over a 15-year period. All were held without trial; most were held without good reason.
With the withdrawal of “Israeli” troops from the South in May, Ghanawi, Hijazi, and hundreds of detainees were released. “We made it. We survived,” said Hijazi. “We were finally free.”
Indeed they were, but there was a question looming over their heads ­ what comes next?
While in prison, Hijazi and his cousin thought about little else but staying alive. “We didn’t exactly give the future much thought,” said Ghanawi.
A few months after their release, having received free medical care and counseling from government-funded agencies, Ghanawi and Hijazi were ready to move on.
Because they were imprisoned at such a young age, they lacked the education and skills necessary to be admitted into the workforce. With an already-struggling Lebanese economy, the path to finding a secure well-paid job seemed grim. But according to Hijazi, “now that Byblos has opened its doors to us, we have a chance.”
Byblos offers an extensive course load ­ from languages and marketing to electro-mechanics and child education. With a wide range of fields and a flexible schedule, Byblos is confident that ex-detainees are sure to find at least one field of interest.
Starting on Oct. 30, Hijazi and Ghanawi will be taking courses in computer language, maintenance and repairs. Hijazi has registered for English-language classes as well.
Although Haidar has informed the Follow-up Committee for Lebanese Detainees about the opportunity for free training at any of the three Byblos Training Centers ­ located in Furn al-Shubbak, Haret Hreik, and Dora ­ Hijazi and Ghanawi were the only two to register for classes.
Haidar is confident that once their offer is better publicized, more detainees will register.
According to Ayyoub Hitti, the general director of Byblos Training Centers, there will be significant efforts, with the help of the media and non-governmental organizations, to inform detainees of the opportunities available to them.
Organizations like the Follow-up Committee and the National Association for Lebanese Detainees are among the NGOs working to spread the news.
The government also has promised support and assistance to ex-detainees. But Hijazi claims that although the government promised them free healthcare, employment, and training, it has not exactly kept its word.
“I’m thankful for the free healthcare I receive from the state, but there’s so much red tape that sometimes I feel it’s not worth it,” said Hijazi.
Frustrated, Hijazi explained how “the amount of paperwork, useless errands, and waiting around in line each and every time I need to see the doctor is ridiculous.”
Hijazi also made it clear that he feels the government has not lived up to expectations with regard to assisting detainees in their search for employment. Both he and Ghanawi understand that the country’s current limited resources and that the difficult economic situation in Lebanon can hinder any efforts in acting upon the promises made. But still, they argue that the government can and should be doing more.
“They’re mostly empty promises,” said Hijazi. “Ask me. I know from experience.”

 

“Israeli” helicopters violate Lebanese airspace…(9)

“Israeli” forces once more violated Lebanese sovereignty when an “Israeli” attack helicopter unleashed missiles on the Majidyyeh post, a former “Israeli” collaborators post, inside Lebanese territory close to the occupied Shibaa Farms.

A spokesman of the “Israeli” army said, "Soldiers had killed one armed man Friday night during an attempt infiltration into '“Israel”'."

The Arab world demonstrates in support of the Palestinians…(10)

While Arab leaders are convening in Cairo, thousands of Arabs in different countries took to the streets calling their leaders to cut all kinds of relations with "“Israel”" and support the holy Intifada.

Yemen witnessed a massive demonstration in which tens of thousands of Yemenis expressed their solidarity for the revolting Palestinians. They called the Arab leaders to take a firm stand in support of the Palestinian cause.
The Popular Conference opposing any normalization of relations with the “Israeli” enemy in the Gulf said, "Either the Arab leaders will adopt efficient resolutions confronting the Zionist arrogance, or they should write the death certificate of the Arab league".

The conference, which is headquarted in Kuwait, also called the Arab Summit to cut all relations with "“Israel”" including diplomatic relations.

People, political forces, and deputies of Jordan condemned Sharm el-Sheikh Summit agreement, confirming that the Intifada is the right option and it should be pursued.
Moreover, Jordanian demonstrators greeted the courage of the Resistance in Lebanon, which was able to liberate their land through military struggle and faith.

In various parts of Lebanon, demonstrations, marches, and sit ins were held in support of the Intifada, also calling for the arming of Palestinians.

Some 3,000 protesters, led by representatives of various Lebanese and palestinian parties, marched from the Martyrs square in downtown Beirut to the Arab League office, carrying Palestinian and Lebanese flags.

"Death to “Israel”", the demonstrators shouted, stamping their feet angrily on an “Israeli” flag burning on the ground.

Palestinians in several  refugee camps in Lebanon, took to the streets shouting: "Palestine is calling for Katyushas" and "we will not forget you, Al-Quds".
