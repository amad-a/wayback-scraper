"Son, from your blood the sun of Palestine" lights southern entrance…(1)

Hizbullah's Press Unit in the South has erected a giant painting at the south's main entrance portraying child Mohammed Rami ad-Dorra who was martyred by “Israeli” bullets while seeking protection behind his father during the confrontations between Palestinians and the “Israeli” occupation forces.

The painting has the child's photo as he was being martyred; opposite is a photo of a Zionist soldier aiming his machine-gun at the child. The background is a picture of the Rock Mosque in Holy al-Quds.

Down the 6-meter high 10-meter wide painting was written in Arabic and English: "Son, from your blood (shines) the sun of Palestine".

Thousands mourn Mohammed

A Palestinian boy who went to throw stones at the troops has become a hero, an Islamic David taking on the Goliath of “Israel”…(2)

“Wrapped in his national flag and placed in a simple wooden box, the bloodied and unwashed corpse of a small Palestinian boy was borne through the narrow alleys of southern Gaza yesterday by an hysterical crowd.

Thousands of people gathered to follow the funeral procession of Mohammed Abu Aasi through the dusty streets and to take part in the grotesque process of turning him, a 13-year-old boy from a poor family of refugees, into a national and religious martyr.

Surrounded by dozens of middle-aged women wrapped in black chadors in a mourning tent on the roof of a nearby house, his mother, Miriam, waited to see her dead son for the last time, and ran through her self-deluding mantra.

Mohammed died in the name of Islam, she told me; she did not regret his death. Stunned and numb, she described how the day before his death, her boy – one of 15 children – had seen television pictures of a Palestinian with his brains blown out by an “Israeli” bullet at Netzarim Junction, Gaza's biggest and most notorious flashpoint. "He went crazy. I couldn't stop him," she said. "He didn't even stay for his lunch. He wanted to go straight there." And that – fatally – is what he did.

By midday yesterday, the myth of Mohammed's death was complete. It was present on the lips of every member of the baying crowd, wildly firing Kalashnikovs into the air. And it was present in the graffiti that had appeared overnight on the walls, showing a boy, stone in hand, with a bullet hole in his chest standing before al-Aqsa mosque in Al-Quds – the holy site at the focus of the current (Intifada).

Mohammed had become an Islamic David taking on the Goliath of “Israel”. The mundane and pathetic reality – that of another Arab boy whose parents, for some reason, failed to stop him from going to a trouble spot to throw stones at an “Israeli” army post while bullets flew all around – was already lost. He was a hero, just like the young man limping behind his open coffin yesterday, with a fresh bullet wound to his foot, a crutch in one hand and an Uzi machine pistol in the other.

 

Please, stop this “Israeli” massacre…(3)

Over the past week, the Palestinian people have lived in a state of war.

At a critical time in the final status negotiations in the peace process, the man behind the massacre of Sabra and Shatila, Ariel Sharon, visited the Haram El-Sahrif in Al-Quds under the protection of “Israeli” policemen and soldiers. The visit and the repeated “Israeli” official stance denying Palestinian

rights in Al-Quds lead to intifada-like demonstrations by Palestinians throughout Palestine.

The “Israeli” Army responded with excessive force to these demonstrations.

They used high-velocity bullets, live ammunition, anti-tank missiles, and helicopter gunships. In 6 days of clashes, 66 Palestinians were killed and over 1,600 injured. The confrontations are still taking place. One 12-year old, Mohammad El-Dura, was killed in cold blood, like many others. “Israeli” soldiers or settlers shot 25 children. One was less than two years old.

Since its establishment, “Israel” has been violating international law and human rights standards. “Israeli” atrocities have been documented by international organizations. The international community has repeatedly condemned “Israel” for these acts.

Nonetheless, the crimes continue to be perpetrated against Palestinian civilians.

The core issue is that “Israel” cannot bring itself to believe that Palestinians are equal human beings. The suppressed guilt of the “Israelis” prevents them from facing themselves and the atrocities they have committed. One “Israeli” woman asked, why do these children put themselves in the line of fire.i She did not want to ask herself why soldiers are killing them.

As stated in international law and UN resolutions, “Israel” is an occupying force. They have no right to be on Palestinian soil.

The Palestinians are guaranteed by international law the right to free themselves from this occupation and defend themselves against oppression. Yet, “Israel” has been the recipient of a great deal of international aid that has allowed it to build the army and security machines that have been oppressing Palestinians.

We call upon the international community to take a firm stand against the outrageous human rights violations perpetrated by the “Israeli” leadership and military establishment. We appeal to individuals and governments to:

- Stop the serious “Israeli” aggression against the Palestinian people.

- Secure international protection to the Palestinian people from the “Israeli” army.

- Mount pressure on the UN Security Council to take concrete measures to protect the Palestinian people.

- Strongly condemn “Israeli” human rights violations.

- Take measures to ensure “Israeli” implementation of UN resolutions on the Palestinian rights including the withdrawal from the occupied land, including Al-Quds, and the right of return.

- Boycott “Israel” on all fronts, economic, political, and diplomatic, until it proves its willingness to implement UN resolutions.

- We call upon the masses in the Arab and Muslim world and all advocates of peace and justice where ever they maybe to organize demonstrations supporting Palestinians rights, and pressure their governments to boycott “Israel” culturally, economically, politically, and militarily.

 

Muslim leaders call faithful to rally in support of intifada
Qabbani demands Arab summit, youths protest at border…(4)

“The highest Sunni religious authority in the country has called for a rally at its premises in Zarif, Beirut, following Friday prayers as a show of support for Palestinians under attack by “Israelis” in Gaza and the West Bank.
“All worshippers are urged to attend the rally, during which the grand mufti, Sheikh Mohammed Rashid Qabbani, will deliver a speech in support of Arab rights in Palestine,” Dar al-Fatwa said in a statement on Thursday.
Gun battles flared between “Israeli” soldiers and Palestinians in the Gaza Strip on Thursday, just hours after a cease-fire agreement was reached.
Also on Thursday, Qabbani held a meeting of regional muftis, religious court judges, religious scholars and mosque imams to express Dar al-Fatwa’s support for Palestinians.
“To face “Israel’s” mass killings and crimes, for which the new world order is held responsible, Palestinians act heroically and make sacrifices that have reaped the world’s admiration,” the clerics said in a statement.
They urged Arab countries to speed up efforts to hold an Arab summit “to unify their stances on the Palestinian cause, devise a plan to liberate Al-Quds and the rest of Palestine and decide to sever the ties that exist between some Arab countries and the “Israeli” entity.”
They denounced calls by world powers for the protection of human rights as “illusions” and accused international organizations of “obeying the orders” of the United States.
A group of young Lebanese staged a protest at the Lebanese part of the border shrine of Abbad in the South in protest against “Israel’s” attacks on Palestinians in Gaza and the West Bank.
The few dozen protesters sat near the steel fence that separates the Lebanese and “Israeli” sections of the shrine, while around 15 “Israeli” soldiers took up positions on the other side.
The youths placed a Palestinian flag at the fence, but an “Israeli” soldier removed it and threw it over to the Lebanese side, triggering calls of derision from the Lebanese, who promptly placed the flag on the Lebanese side.
The shrinehas been the site of daily tension between Lebanese civilians and “Israeli” soldiers since the United Nations verified “Israel’s” withdrawal in July.
The protesters carried placards denouncing “Israel’s” attacks and pictures of the body of 12-year-old Mohammed al-Durra, who was gunned down by “Israeli” soldiers while in his father’s arms in Gaza on Saturday.
Some protesters pitched a tent near the fence, saying they planned to spend the night there.
Ghanaian peacekeepers with the UN Interim Force in Lebanon, who have a position at the site, did not interfere.
“We’re here to express our solidarity with the Palestinian people’s uprising against their “Israeli” aggressors,” said Nelly Yassin, a 24-year-old participant. “We didn’t want them to feel that they’re alone in their struggle.”
Roula Qobeisi, 23, called on young Arabs to stage sit-ins in areas bordering “Israel”.
“There should be the largest possible support for the intifada,” she said.
Khouloud Hamoush, 24, urged the Palestinians to follow in the footsteps of the Lebanese by liberating their land through resistance. “Resistance is the only means for regaining lost rights,” she said.
In Nabatieh, dozens of protesters demonstrated in the streets. After gathering at the main husseiniya (Shiite religious and cultural center), they marched to the town hall where they staged a sit-in.
The local council of Ghobeiri, Baabda, decided Thursday to name a street in the town Al-Quds Street, “in a show of solidarity with the uprising of our people in Palestine,” it said in a statement.
The popular committees in the Palestinian refugee camps of Baddawi and al-Bared in the North called for a strike and a demonstration on Friday.
The committees also decided to indefinitely extend their one-week closure of schools and offices run by the UN Relief and Works Agency and their ban on foreign UNRWA employees from entering the camps.
The Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine sent a message to UN Secretary General Kofi Annan asking for international trials of senior “Israeli” officials, including Prime Minister Ehud Barak.
The Damascus-based group, opposed to the Middle East peace process but holding an intermittent dialogue with Palestinian President Yasser Arafat over the issue, said it “considers that peace talks cannot continue in the backdrop of massacres to avoid letting “Israel” make use of the situation to impose the solutions that suit it.”

International uproar over “Israeli” attacks on Palestinians…(5)

Canada has condemned the violence against civilians and said it would work to care for injured Palestinians. Foreign Minister Lloyd Axworthy told Parliament, Sharon's visit to Al-Aqsa was ill-timed. Axworthy said Canada's office in the West Bank town of Ramallah will provide emergency medical assistance. There has been worldwide condemnation of the “Israeli” killings.

“ A week after uprising erupted in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, demonstrations are picking up around the globe. In Iran, thousands of people gathered in Tehran's Palestinian Square. Chanting anti-”Israeli” slogans and waving banners, the huge crowd listened to a series of speeches condemning the bloodbath by “Israeli” troops. In Cairo hundreds of Egyptian demonstrators marched to the “Israeli” embassy yesterday and set fire to the “Israeli” flags. Thousands of students across the American University of Cairo, Ain Shams, and Cairo University campuses held peaceful demonstrations. For the first time, secondary school pupils also joined the protests. There were unconfirmed reports a campus demonstration turned violent when students allegedly set fire to three cars. In Istanbul, Turkish police broke up a demonstration and detained fifteen people after they tried to unfurl a banner near the office of “Israel’s” El Al airlines. The demonstrations spread to Europe and the United States. At least eight hundred Palestinian and Arab students gathered in Paris last night for a peaceful anti-”Israeli” demonstration. In Denmark, Arab youths clashed with police after a peaceful demonstration erupted into street riots. Five were injured. Protestors burned “Israeli” flags, following a massive demonstration attended by five thousand people. Anti-”Israeli” demonstrations also took place in the Romanian capital, Bucharest, where hundreds of Arabs gathered in the city square and denounced “Israel’s” killing of innocent Palestinians. Similar demonstrations earlier took place in Indonesia. In New York recent attacks in Brooklyn, where large communities of Arabs and Jews live, were linked to the violence in the Palestinian territories, prompting Arab and Jewish community leaders to appeal for calm. Police officers were beefed up around mosques and synagogues. Brandishing photographs of 12-year-old Palestinian Mohammad al-Durra, killed in his father's arms by “Israeli” gunfire, some two hundred demonstrators in Ohio urged Vice President Al Gore to halt US aid to “Israel”. At a campaign rally they urged Washington to adopt "even-handed policy in the Middle East." The Jewish state is the largest recipient of US financial aid. Late this afternoon, Omani students took to the streets of Muscat to protest against “Israeli” killings and show solidarity with Palestinians. Similar gatherings also took place in neighboring United Arab Emirates.

UN agency calls for investigation into “Israeli” killings as Arabs denounce "heinous" crimes…(6)

The U.N. human rights chief Mary Robinson has urged “Israel” to accept an international investigation into the violence. She endorsed calls for an international enquiry to "determine responsibility and accountability" for killings and rights abuses. Robinson urged “Israel” to facilitate a visit by independent U.N. human rights experts. Meanwhile, at a specially convened session of the UN Security Council, Arab and Muslim diplomats denounced “Israeli” attacks on Palestinians. They accused “Israel” of deliberately initiating violence between its soldiers and Palestinians to undermine the peace process. Ambassadors from Pakistan and Jordan were among those accusing “Israel” of violating UN conventions. They accused “Israeli” forces of using excessive force against defenseless Palestinian civilians. Jordan's ambassador charged the Jewish state of committing heinous crimes in occupied East Al-Quds and called for the withdrawal of its forces from the West Bank, Gaza Strip and the Holy City. Several ambassadors also accused “Israel” of provoking the bloodshed by allowing Likud chief Ariel Sharon to visit the al-Aqsa mosque compound, Islam's third holiest site. It was the third and final day of extraordinary deliberations in the Security Council over the latest clashes.

“Israelis” take apart outpost on Sheikh Abbad Hill…(7)

“Israel” on Thursday began dismantling a border outpost located beside a tomb on the summit of Sheikh Abbad Hill near Houla, the scene of increasing tension lately between Lebanese civilians and “Israeli” troops.
It was not immediately clear if the “Israelis” plan to simply reduce the size of the strategically located position, reconstruct it or abandon it altogether.
At least five single-unit accommodation and office blocks were lifted by crane and driven away on the back of transport vehicles during the day, according to Ghanaian UNIFIL soldiers on duty at the top of the hill.
The border with “Israel” bisects the ancient tomb on the summit.
“Israeli” soldiers and Lebanese visitors face each other across the tomb, separated only by a wire-mesh fence. A platoon of Ghanaian UNIFIL peacekeepers patrol the top of the hill in an attempt to prevent violence from flaring.
There were more “Israeli” soldiers on duty than usual on the far side of the tomb on Thursday evening as the crane continued to deposit equipment onto the back of a lorry.
A Merkava tank was parked just on the other side of a length of coiled barbed wire, which presently demarcates the border. The “Israelis” are constructing a more permanent barrier in the shape of a concrete wall,
1-meter thick and perhaps eventually 3 meters high.
The Lebanese side of the hill affords a clear view of the interior of the “Israeli” outpost. As the crane swung to and fro, a group of bored “Israeli” soldiers sat on sandbags and ammunition boxes, eating their rations, sipping coffee and smoking cigarettes. Piled up in one corner of the position were ammunition boxes, stretchers, rucksacks and tables.
During the occupation, the entire summit of the hill was taken up by an “Israeli” outpost. Its distinctive twin antennae were visible for several kilometers. The hill, the highest in the area, was a natural choice for the “Israelis” to set up one of their largest outposts in Lebanon.
The “Israelis” were forced to abandon the strategic post, however, when the UN determined that the Blue Line ran through the center of the compound.
Since the withdrawal in May, “Israeli” engineers have been busy leveling the eastern slopes of the summit, apparently to expand the new outpost. Given its strategic nature and religious significance, it would seem unlikely that the “Israelis” will abandon the site altogether.

 

170 exiles come in from the cold…(8)

“Around 170 Lebanese citizens, including 44 collaborators, who had sought refuge in “Israel” after its May withdrawal, returned home on Thursday.
It was the largest single group to cross the border from “Israel” since the end of the occupation.
Up to 6,000 “Israeli” agents, their families and many other civilians fled their homes during the “Israeli” pullout, fearing bloody reprisals at the hands of the resistance.
More than 200 Lebanese have returned home in the past two weeks alone, encouraged by the calm that has prevailed in the liberated areas since the pullout.
The first convoy of three UNIFIL buses carrying the returnees from the border arrived at Naqoura Port, 4 kilometers away at 11.30am on Thursday.
A fourth bus carried their luggage. Civilian and military cars driven by Internal Security Forces and Army Intelligence personnel led the convoy.
Three more buses arrived an hour later, bringing the total to around 170 men, women and children.
Army troops kept relatives from approaching the port’s gate and also kept photographers and journalists at a distance.
Routine measures were followed to admit the returnees, including a quick baggage search. The collaborators were separated from everyone else.
Three army trucks transported the militiamen to a barracks in nearby Tyre, where they will stay until they are questioned by military investigators.
Separately, a military court on Thursday sentenced four elderly men to six months in jail for collaborating with “Israel”.
The four, all aged over 70, were all “Israeli” agents.
They were already in custody when the court handed down the sentences.
They were the 31st batch of collaborators to appear before the court since it started looking into cases of collaboration with “Israel” on June 5.
The military court, headed by Brigadier Maher Safieddine, has sentenced more than 1,100 people to jail terms ranging between a few months and several years. A few convicts, mostly tried in absentia, were sentenced to 15 years.
