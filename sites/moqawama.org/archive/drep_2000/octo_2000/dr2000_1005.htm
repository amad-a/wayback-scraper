Hizbullah calls for Palestinian armed resistance against “Israel”

“Hizbullah called on the Palestinians Wednesday to turn their stone-throwing uprising into an armed resistance by killing “Israelis” with suicide bombings, bullets and knives.

"You have to develop the Intifada into an armed resistance," Hizbullah Secretary General H.E. sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said in a rally at Sfeir aired live across the Arab world via satellite from the group's al-Manar television station.

"You have to move from the stone-throwing Intifada to the phase of real Jihad (holy war) operations," Nasrallah told thousands at a Hizbullah "rally in solidarity with the Palestinian people" in Beirut's southern suburbs.

Nasrallah, offering the Palestinians "detailed proposals ... for a developed strategy," told them to "save the bullets you are firing into the air or at the walls ... save them for the chests of the Zionists, soldiers and settlers."

"Let a (Palestinian) reach a settler, let him stab him fiercely and let him die afterwards. They (“Israelis”) love worldly matters, we love martyrdom. Hit them in their weak points," he shouted as thousands chanted: "Allahu Akbar," or God is greatest.

Nasrallah said "the rule is: you kill and then you die. You will see that the results will be different"…(1)

“If heavier weapons aren’t available, you can use knives, and stab a settler as he passes by … the time has come for you to stop getting killed … you should kill and then be killed,” Nasrallah said, addressing Palestinians as “ brothers in the struggle.”
“The weak point of the “Israelis” is their love of life, but we in Palestine and Lebanon have a strong point … we love martyrdom,” he added.
Nasrallah called on religious leaders in the Arab and Islamic worlds to issue a fatwa for an armed struggle.
The rally was attended by representatives from Palestinian factions in Lebanon, as as well as Hamas and Islamic Jihad in occupied Palestine…(2)

“Elsewhere, Nasrallah told Orbit Television all Arabs and Muslims should support Palestinians in their struggle against “Israeli” troops in the occupied Palestinian territories. Nasrallah, whose group spearheaded the armed resistance effort to oust “Israel” from south Lebanon, said frustration drove Palestinians to the streets in protest over Ariel Sharon's visit to Al-Aqsa Mosque. The Hizbullah leader said the visit offended more than a billion Muslims worldwide. He called on the Palestinian Authority to abandon peace negotiations with “Israel” and pursue an armed resistance campaign against the Jewish state, saying it is the only way to liberate Gaza, the West Bank and East Quds from “Israeli” occupation. Nasrallah stressed events in Lebanon and the Palestinian territories showed resistance and not negotiations guarantee the return of occupied lands. He criticized international organizations and bodies, including the UN, for failing to condemn “Israeli” attacks on Palestinians. Nasrallah said many Muslims and Arabs are now ready to take quote decisive actions regarding the Arab-”Israeli” conflict but added, they suspect the current clashes will end up serving the interests of peace negotiators. Nasrallah refused to speculate on whether Palestinian refugees in Lebanon will resort to cross-border attacks, saying it is a problem “Israel” will have to deal with since it will not allow the refugees to return home. The Hizbullah chief made clear his group has no presence whatsoever in the Palestinian territories. He stressed, Hizbullah would have helped the Palestinians if it had any infrastructure or members in Palestinian villages and towns. Nasrallah vowed support for the Palestinians. There have been mounting calls in Palestinian refugee camps in Lebanon for the resumption of armed struggle against “Israel”…(3)

 

Palestinians' Jihad further evidence to Resistance option, Hizbullah…(4)

"The Jihad taking place in Palestine is further evidence that the option with which to deal with this (“Israeli”) enemy is Resistance," President of Hizbullah's Executive Council his Eminence Sayyed Hashem Safieddeen said.

Speaking at the annual rally to commemorate the martyrs of Aitaroun Town (South), Sayyed Safieddeen said, "It is not acceptable that Arab and Islamic governments sit in the audience as the enemy is perpetrating massacres."

What is required (by these governments) is to defend and back this cause because all negotiations, promises and slogans are mere lies and claims, he said.

Sayyed Safieddeen talked about the "pressures exerted by the US on Lebanon to force Beirut to abandon its political option and the achievements it has made through expulsion of “Israeli” occupation, liberation of soil, and unity of state and people."

 

Country shows solidarity with Palestinians…(5) 
“Palestinian blood is not cheap”

“Thousands of protesters vowing vengeance marched through the streets of Lebanon’s main cities on Wednesday, chanting anti-”Israeli” slogans and carrying empty coffins symbolizing the Palestinians killed in the week-long confrontations in “Israel” and the occupied territories.
It was the fourth consecutive day protesters took to the streets carrying Lebanese and Palestinian flags and chanting slogans of solidarity with Palestinians.
“Palestinian blood is not cheap” shouted the 20,000 demonstrators gathered at Martyrs’ Square in Sidon before they headed to the city’s Nijmeh Square.
The protestors, carrying Palestinian and Lebanese flags as well as the flags of various parties, called on President Clinton to “come and see how children are being murdered in cold blood.”
The mufti of the South, Mohammed Salim Jalaleddine, urged the Arab League and the Organization of the Islamic Conference to support Palestinians “through all possible means and stop the “Israeli” massacres.”
Students in Sidon’s Ain al-Hilweh refugee camp marched through the narrow lanes holding a makeshift coffin containing a young boy posing as 12-year-old Mohammed al-Durra, who was killed by “Israeli” soldiers on Saturday while trying to seek cover with his father.
Schools remained closed in the camp for the fourth consecutive day in a show of solidarity.
Children stopped cars and handed drivers a picture of Durra, who met his death at the Bureiji refugee camp in Gaza. His father, Jamal, was badly wounded trying to shield his son.
A huge 6x10-meter poster was erected at the northern entrance to Sidon, depicting the killing of the young boy. It was prepared by Hizbullah’s information unit and shows the wounded father and his son screaming, with Quds’s Al-Aqsa Mosque in the background.
Meanwhile, similar protests were staged in Tyre and Nabatieh, while some 5,000 demonstrators marched in Tripoli.
The Tripoli march, organized by religious and political organizations, passed from the Mansouri Mosque to Sahet al-Nour.

Second boy is shot dead as the politicians talk

Middle East: In the place now known as Martyr's Corner, angry crowds step up rioting while “Israeli” gunships unleash attack after attack…(6)

“The bleeding corpse of yet another boy was being borne away from the streets of the Gaza Strip yesterday as diplomatic efforts continued in Paris to end the violence convulsing the Middle East.

Mohammed Yousif Abu Aasi, 13, died from a bullet through the chest just hours after the “Israeli” Prime Minister, Ehud Barak, made another demand for Yasser Arafat to end the violence, only to find his own army being accused again of excessive use of force.

Mohammed, from a village in southern Gaza, was shot at Netzarim Junction, only yards from the spot where French television caught the final moments of another boy, Mohammed al-Durah, 12, who was shot dead huddling at his father's side on Sunday.

“Israel’s” deputy chief of staff, Major-General Moshe Yaalon, said the “Israel” defence forces are "using live fire only if fired upon in a life-threatening manner". This cannot be true.

At least one “Israeli” sniper was at work at the scene of Mohammed's death. When The Independent visited the flashpoint, there was no sound of anyone returning fire.

Netzarim Junction is at the point where Palestinian-controlled territory ("Area A" as it is known in the 1993 Oslo Accord), meets an “Israeli”-controlled road, which leads to a Jewish settlement and conveniently dissects this tiny oblong of overcrowded land along the Mediterranean coast. The “Israeli” army has an outpost at the crossroads but it is heavily fortified and is more like a bunker than a base.

Fed up with the failure of the"peace process", the Gazans clearly want to force the “Israelis” out of their stifled patch of land, securing a victory that they will compare with that of Hizbullah's success in getting “Israel” out of south Lebanon.

The place has come under intense attack for nearly a week, not only from Palestinian youths throwing rocks, Molotov cocktails and pipe bombs, but from Palestinian security men firing their automatic weapons, often wildly.

But to threaten the lives of those soldiers within requires heavy weapons. The sort of weapons, in fact, that “Israel” is using, wholly without regard to the risk to the children who are drawn to this spot.

For the third consecutive day, an “Israeli” Cobra helicopter gunship fired rockets into the surrounding area yesterday. On Tuesday, a Cobra blasted an enormous hole in the side of an apartment building that overlooks the “Israeli” base.

Mohammed Abu Aasi will become another martyr. Within minutes of his death, workers in the morgue were displaying his punctured body – with its gaping wound though the back – to journalists. He will be chalked up here as a further victim of brutal tactics that are intended by Mr Barak, a former general, to send a message to the Palestinians of the terrible consequences of continuing to fight, but which have been having the opposite effect.

You only have to look at Netzarim Junction to see that. The place is now known as Martyr's Corner and is attracting more and more people every day to what has become a macabre circus.

The stronger the emotion on the streets, the more precarious the position of Mr Arafat, the Palestinian leader. And that is the contradiction inherent in “Israel’s” conduct. Mr Barak said that he would not negotiate with the Palestinian leader until he ended the uprising. But “Israel’s” army daily makes that more difficult.

Mr Arafat cannot now stop the uprising outright and he is probably too politically canny to want to try. His huge security structure is littered with men who are as steeped in anger at the “Israelis” as the stone-throwing youths. Their attempts to restrain the crowds have been half-hearted and ineffectual.

The “Israeli” military has blamed the uprising on Tanzim, the youth movement of Fatah. But Gaza is rife with cynicism and despair over the lack of results from the Oslo accord. All the parties – including the Palestinian Authority – have misjudged the depth of feeling. This was succinctly summed up by Jihad al-Wazir, the son of Abu Jihad, the highly influential founder of the PLO's Fatah who was killed in Tunis in 1988 by an “Israeli” hit squad co-ordinated by Mr Barak. "They saw [the people's] silence as acquiescence and not the eye of the storm. Today we are seeing the beginning of the storm. This ushers in the end of the Oslo cycle."

Syrians attack us embassy; saddam expresses readiness to "destroy" “Israel”…(8)

Thousands of Syrians demonstrated outside the US embassy in Damascus, stoning the building in protest against the killing of Palestinians by “Israeli” soldiers. One of the estimated 4,000 demonstrators climbed over the wall round the embassy compound and tore down the American flag while the crowd shouted anti-American and anti-”Israeli” slogans. Hundreds of Syrian riot police intervened and fired tear gas to disperse the protesters. There were also massive demonstrations in Baghdad in support of Palestinians for the second day running. Newspapers in Baghdad reported Iraqi President Saddam Hussein as saying his country could destroy “Israel” if it was given access to a border strip with the Jewish state. Saddam reportedly told university academics Iraq could in a very short time put an end to Zionism. Tens of thousands of Iraqis took to the streets yesterday in support of Palestinians. Iraq opposes the US-sponsored Middle East peace process and has called for a holy war to liberate Quds following clashes in the occupied territories. During the 1991 Gulf War, Saddam's army launched a number of Scud missiles at the Jewish state. Saddam said his country would not need to wait for the lifting of UN sanctions, which have been in force since his 1990 invasion of Kuwait, to strike “Israel”. The UN-led embargo has been crumbling. Tunisia, Algeria and the United Arab Emirates are planning to fly planes to Iraq this week, adding to the rash of aircraft going to Baghdad to protest decade-old UN sanctions. All three filed papers with the Security Council's sanction committee. Council member states, the US and Britain are strongly opposed to the flights, while Russia, France and China say they do not breach the sanctions.

Palestinian opposition groups call on Arafat to resign and abandon talks with “Israel”…(9)

Leaders of Palestinian groups have slammed President Arafat for agreeing to talks with “Israel”. The Damascus-based leaders, who oppose a 1993 interim “Israeli”-Palestinian peace deal, said the Paris meeting was aimed at preventing a new uprising against “Israel”. Colonel Abu Moussa, the leader of the Fatah Uprising who rebelled against Arafat in 1983, urged the Palestinian President to resign. He said Arafat's peace policies have so far failed to allow millions of refugees to return home, stop Jewish settlement construction on Palestinian land, and convince “Israel” to withdraw from the occupied territories. Moussa's group belongs to an eight-member alliance that vowed to oppose and wreck the Oslo agreement which gave Palestinians limited self-rule in parts of Gaza and the West Bank. The Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine and the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine, both opposed to the deal but seeking reconciliation with Arafat, also urged him to end peace talks with “Israel”.

ADC Press Release: Arab Americans Demand U.S. Intervention to Stop the Killing in Palestine…(10)

The American-Arab Anti-Discrimination Committee, the nation's largest Arab-American grassroots organization, today appealed to President Clinton to use his influence with “Israel” to end the shooting of unarmed civilians by the “Israeli” military in occupied Palestine.  ADC strongly condemned the use of extreme and excessive violence by “Israeli” occupation troops against Palestinians, at least 35 of whom have been killed in the past five days.  The indiscriminate and murderous use of live ammunition by “Israeli” troops was clearly demonstrated in the shocking footage of the shooting of 12-year-old Mohammed Al-Durah, gunned down in cold blood by “Israeli” snipers, which horrified millions of television viewers the world over.

The Palestinian demonstrations held over the past few days were instigated by the highly provocative intrusion last Thursday by Likud Party leader Ariel Sharon into one of the holiest Muslim sites, the Haram Ash-Sharif, in occupied East Quds.  Sharon's visit was openly touted as a demonstration of “Israel’s” self-proclaimed sovereignty over all of East Quds. UN Security Council Resolutions 242 and 476, among others, insist “Israel” must withdraw from the 1967 occupied territories in exchange for peace.  The reckless provocation by Sharon, whose involvement in anti-Palestinian atrocities includes responsibility for the massacre of thousands of unarmed Palestinians at the Sabra and Shatila refugee camps in Beirut in 1982, combined with the continued “Israeli” refusal to withdraw from occupied Palestinian lands, sparked this latest expression of determination on the part of the Palestinian people.  The “Israeli” reaction has included indiscriminate use of live ammunition, tanks, helicopter gunships and even anti-tank missiles against unarmed demonstrators, leading to the widespread deaths among Palestinian civilians.  Among the latest victims is an 18-month-old baby shot to death by “Israeli” settlers who have been actively involved in the shooting of unarmed Palestinians.

At a press conference held today with other major Arab-American and American-Muslim organizations at the offices of the Council on American-Islamic Relations, ADC President Hala Maksoud appealed to President Clinton to use the special relationship between “Israel” and the United States to end the killing.  "The United States has an unquestionable ability to convince “Israel” to restrain its troops and end the killing of Palestinian civilians," she said.  "The Palestinians of Quds are guardians of a sacred trust on behalf of the entire Arab and Muslim worlds," Maksoud continued.  "The international community, especially the United States, has serious responsibility to end the killing and provide international protection for the Palestinians of East Quds, the West Bank and Gaza who are living under this brutal occupation," she said.  Maksoud concluded, "International protection is needed at once to secure the lives and safety of the Palestinian people, but their long-term security can only be ensured by the enforcement of UN Security Council Resolution 242, the withdrawal of “Israel” from all the territories occupied in 1967, and the creation of a Palestinian State with its capital in Quds."

 

The International Secretariat of OMCT requests URGENT intervention in “Israel”…(11)

The International Secretariat of OMCT wishes to express its grave concern over the deaths of and injuries caused to Palestinians, including a number of children, by the “Israeli” forces in the last few days.

According to the information received from the Palestinian Society for the Protection for Human Rights and the Environment (LAW), on 29th September 2000, a day after the visit of Ariel Sharon, leader of the Likud party, a large number of “Israeli” security forces estimated at 2000, in addition to special units, were deployed in the early morning in the vicinity of the Haram al Sharif compound.  They installed makeshift checkpoints and a large number were deployed on the roofs of neighbouring houses.  Shooting started a few moment before the end of the prayers.  Initially, “Israeli” soldiers fired rubber-coated bullets and gas bombs but they later fired live ammunition.  This attack led to widespread demonstrations.

The clashes resulted in the deaths of 7 Palestinians.  More than 255 people were injured, 32 of whom sustained serious injuries.  Those killed have been identified as: Mohammad Faraj from Ramallah, Bilal Afaneh from Abu Dis, Mohammad Farrah from Quds and Mohammad Hussein from Um Al Fahem in “Israel”.

Eyewitnesses have stated that ambulances were denied entry to the Haram compound by the “Israeli” security forces.  Furthermore, makeshift checkpoints were installed outside Palestinian hospitals to deny entry to ambulances.

On 30th September 2000, large numbers of “Israeli” soldiers and policemen were deployed in the early morning hours in the vicinities of Palestinian towns.  Vehicles to and from Quds were denied entry at the northern “Israeli” checkpoint near Aram.  Clashes resulted in the death of 12 Palestinians some of whom have been identified as:  Bassam Al Balbesi, 25 from Gaza, an ambulance driver, Khaled Bazyan, 14 from Nablus, Nizar Eideh, 16, from the village of Deir Ammar (a Ramallah resident), Zakariya Al Kilani, 22 from jenin (shot in Nablus), Mohammad Al Atleh, 22, from Gaza, Rami Atarazi, 12, from Gaza, Mahmoud Anbara, 20, from Ruweished, Jordan - shot dead in Nablus, and 3 members of the national Forces (Nabil Mohammad, Rami Aduri and Mohammad Zeidan).

The number of persons injured is estimated at 577, many of which are serious:

In Nablus: Mis'ab Jamil (head injury), Awad Abu Salhieh (Chest injury), Nihad Radi (Abdomen injury), Mahmoud Zahdeh (Chest injury), and Jihad Al Aloul (Head injury).

In Hebron: Thaer Doudine, 22, (head injury), Yousef Al Qadi, 20 (chest  injury) and Issa Abu Hleil, 13 (head injury).

According to LAW, a number of “Israeli” undercover agents have been sighted, disguised in Arab dress, storming the Asuwaneh neighbourhood and randomly shooting at a number of young Palestinians.  The incident resulted in the injury of Yousef Asayad, 22, Tareq Al Hammouri, 23 and Nahla Nusha'she', 42.

According to LAW on 1st October 2000, “Israeli” occupation forces reportedly invaded a number of cities in the West Bank and Gaza with tanks and helicopters bombarding some civilian sites. In Al Bireh, the “Israeli” occupation troops invaded and occupied the City Inn Hotel. In Rafah Gaza, at the Nitsirim junction, “Israeli” troops fired Lao missiles at civilians raising the number of Palestinian killed and wounded. By 7:00 PM Saturday evening, 11 Palestinian were killed and 388 wounded.  18 people are believed to be in very critical condition.

In the City of Nablus the following persons were reported killed and injured: Musatafa Ramadan, 26, from the village of Tal and member of the Palestinian National Forces died near Joseph's tomb in an exchange of gunfire with “Israeli” soldiers. Samer Tabanja, 10, from Nablus was shot to death while on the roof of his house. He was shot with a 500 mm bullet fired from the helicopter hovering above. Husam Bakhit, 18, from Balata refugee camp, was hit in the head during clashes near Joseph's Tomb. The body of Iyad Khashashi, 18, from Nablus, was found in a neighbouring building close to a Palestinian checkpoint near the village of Kufur Qalil. He had been shot the day before, sought refuge in the building but bled to death overnight. Jihad Al Aloul, 23, son of the Nablus Governor died from gunshots wounds. In another incident, one and a half year old, Sarah Hasan from the village of Talfeet, was killed when settlers randomly opened fire, hitting her in the head.

Three people were shot and killed in Ramallah. These are: Mohammad Dahoud, 12, from Al Bireh. Imad Al Anati, 20, from Al Am'ari.  Salah Al Faqih, a member of the National Forces In Gaza, Sami Ataramsi, 20, from Ashate' Refugee Camp, was shot and killed. Three have also been declared clinically dead; these are Raed Al Hams, Talal Al Basouti and Salem Daraweesh. According to LAW's documentation, the number of wounded is as follows:

Inside “Israel”, Palestinian demonstrators clashed with “Israeli” troops resulting in the death of Mohammad Jabarine, 22. Eighty-three people were injured, 50 from the town of Um Al Fahem, 30 from Nazareth and 3 from Jaffa.

OMCT is appalled by these killings and injuries, particularly child victims, and that a large number of injuries were reportedly in the upper parts of the body, particularly the eyes and were caused by live ammunition.  OMCT calls on the “Israeli” authorities to put an immediate end to this use of excessive force against Palestinian demonstrators which violates international human rights standards, in particular Articles 9, 10 and 11 of the United Nations Basic Principles on the Use of Force and Firearms by Law Enforcement Officials.

New flood of collaborators expected to cross border…(12)

“Around 200 former “Israeli”-allied militiamen and their families are expected to return to Lebanon on Thursday, the largest single group to cross the border from “Israel” since the end of the occupation in May.
Although some estimates placed the number of returning Lebanese as high as 680, UNIFIL said that the “Israeli” Army had confirmed 200 would return.
Up to 6,000 collaborators, their families and many other civilians fled to the Jewish state during the “Israeli” troop pullout, fearing bloody reprisals at the hands of the resistance.
But the calm that has prevailed in the liberated areas since May has prompted many low-ranking former militiamen to return to Lebanon to stand trial.
More than 200 Lebanese have returned in the past two weeks alone.
Last week, Mohammed Olayan, a former captain in the militia, was persuaded by his wife, Nada, to come back to Lebanon and turn himself in to the authorities.
Nada, who had fled to “Israel” with her husband, returned to her hometown of Biyada on the coast south of Tyre two weeks earlier with their three young children.
Looking back on her experiences of the past four months, Nada blamed the mass exodus from south Lebanon on unfounded rumors of Hizbullah fighters killing civilians as they entered the occupation zone at the beginning of the withdrawal.
“We heard that Hizbullah was cutting people into pieces when they entered Houla. Biyada was terrified, not just the militia but civilians as well,” she said.
As panic-stricken militiamen began abandoning their outposts in the western sector, the Olayan family hurried to Naqoura, further down the coast.
“All night we could hear cars driving past heading for the border. My husband told me to pack everything and fetch the children and then we joined the river of cars,” she said.
The border crossing at Ras Naqoura was swamped with frightened Lebanese.
“The “Israelis” told us to leave our cars behind with our names and registration information inside. Then they searched us, counted us and took us to Tabariya in “Israel”,” Nada recalled.
She said that all the fleeing Lebanese were brought to Tabariya initially while the “Israelis” attempted to organize them.
“Even the “Israelis” were surprised by how many of us there were. They didn’t know what to do,” she said.
Her second night in “Israel” was spent sleeping on the ground in the open. “When it grew dark, homesick women began wailing and screaming. It felt like a dream,” she said.
The “Israelis” handed the head of each family $700 for expenses and provided milk and diapers for the children before dispatching the refugees to hotels throughout northern “Israel”.
Nada said that she never expected to remain in “Israel” for long and could not imagine one day returning to Lebanon.
“They’re not our society. It was like a jail,” she said.
With little to do, the refugees followed events in Lebanon.
“The “Israelis” bought us a television and we tuned it to Lebanese satellite channels. We spent all our time listening to the news,” she said.
But the rumor mill continued to grind out lurid tales of mass arrests and revenge killings.
“The rumors were put out by people who had no intention of returning to Lebanon,” Nada said.
“We decided enough was enough. Someone had to go back and make sure everything was alright,” Nada added.
Mohammed, who once commanded a militia unit in the western sector, was still receiving his militia-pay of $700 a month. The money was put into a special bank account and he was given a credit card for daily expenses. The “Israelis”, however, were offering one-time payments to all former militiamen willing to return to Lebanon.
Mohammed received $45,000 and Nada was granted $4,000.
“Before we left, we heard rumors that the government was taking all the money from returnees. The rumor mongers made us panic,” she said.
Nada returned to Lebanon 15 days before Mohammed.
“When I saw that everything was fine, I told my husband to come back. I told him that there was no blood on his hands and that he would be treated fairly. I told him that after he was released from prison he could live with his family in peace,” she explained.
Mohammed was among 33 militiamen and civilians to return last week. He handed his money to Nada for safekeeping before being taken into custody.
Nada plans to visit her husband on Thursday for the first time since he was arrested. She said she was confident Mohammed’s eventual prison sentence would not be unduly harsh.
“My husband was well known to everyone,” Nada said. “He has clean hands. When we arrived in “Israel”, people would look at him in astonishment and ask why he had fled.”
She said that she and her children had faced no problems from Hizbullah or any other party since returning to Biyada.

Ukrainians need 37 years to clear South of mines…(13)

The Ukraine battalion of UN peacekeepers in Lebanon will need 37 years to complete its task of removing land mines left over by the “Israeli” occupation, the unit’s commander said on Wednesday.
“We know of about 130,000 land mines in the South but believe that the number is larger. If our battalion is left alone with the demining task, it will need 37 years to finish doing so,” Colonel Oleg Khavroniuk said.
“The Lebanese government is very concerned with the issue of demining. It should ask for another battalion or more to help out the Ukrainians in removing the mines,” he told the National News Agency.
“I urge the NNA and other media to warn civilians against the dangers of land mines and to call on them to avoid suspected mine fields.
“Wherever there are Ukrainian experts working,” he pointed out, “it means there is a mine field that should be avoided.”
Khavroniuk was speaking at the airport upon the arrival of Ukraine’s deputy defense minister, Brigadier Alexander Shabovsky, who is here to inspect his country’s peacekeepers in the South.
“Since the battalion arrived here two months ago, we’ve removed about 800 land mines and provided medical aid to Lebanese children and adults who have been wounded in land mine explosions,” Khavroniuk said.
“In addition to demining experts, our battalion includes civil engineers and paramedics, who help people rebuild their homes and provide them with medication and sometimes food,” he added.
The battalion, which is part of the UN Interim Force in Lebanon, is stationed in Hamra, Qana, Hariss and Ibl al-Saqi.
“We suffer mainly from an intermittent power supply,” Khavroniuk said, “a problem that affects all UNIFIL battalions.”
