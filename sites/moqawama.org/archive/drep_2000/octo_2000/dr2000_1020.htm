New battleground: resistance moves into cyberspace
“Israel’s” supporters try to knock Hizbullah’s site offline…(1)

“Israel” is waging a technological war against Hizbullah by targeting its website with millions of hits in an attempt to crash the server, Hizbullah said Thursday.
Traffic to Hizbullah’s
website (www.hizbollah.org) surged 30 times over its normal rate and crashed the server on Oct. 7, the day that Hizbullah kidnapped three “Israeli” soldiers.
Ali Ayoub, Hizbullah’s webmaster, said that the increase in hits were not due to sudden interest in Hizbullah. Instead, he said it was an attempt by “Israelis” and their sympathizers to sabotage the site.
“For 12 days, we have received up to 9 million hits, 41 percent of which were from “Israel”, rendering our website unreachable to our clients,” Ayoub explained.
Normally, the average daily hits on Hizbullah’s website range between 100,000 and 300,000, depending on circumstances in the region.
Prepared for the possibility that the site could be overloaded, Ayoub immediately launched a back-up website, (www.hizballah.org) which again was targeted but is functioning. The hizbollah.org site is still down and is being rebuilt.
Following the rise of the intifada in Palestine, Hizbullah’s website began providing detailed information of the events, pictures and even live video clips of the “Israeli” attacks against Palestinian civilians.
Although this is not the first time Hizbullah’s website has been targeted, Ayoub admitted that the recent attack has been the toughest.
“Whenever Hizbullah staged a large operation in the past, we’d get more hits. When Hizbullah captured the three “Israeli” soldiers, we received an unbelievable number of hits. It was crazy,” Ayoub said.
Late on Tuesday night, Ayoub said, he was able to launch seven back-up sites.
“In four to five days, our main site will be restored after I enable it to take the number of hits it’s receiving,” he said.
On Wednesday, Ayoub e-mailed his clients explaining the situation.
Addressed to the readers of the Islamic Resistance websites, Ayoub began his e-mail by saying: “Hizbullah’s website is coming under a fierce offensive staged by the Zionist enemy which amassed huge capabilities inside occupied Palestine and throughout the world, especially in the US, to paralyze the website and ban readers from being acquainted with the material and information concerning the struggle against it.”
He added: “We put these facts before the world so they are well informed of the barbarism of the Zionist enemy and the hatred concealed by the many effective forces in the US, especially in the field of the internet, that helps the enemy achieve its goals. These same people claim they support freedom of speech and of the exchange of information among the people.”
Ayoub in particular noted the dominance of “hostile” hits from “fake readers” in “Israel”, the US and Canada. He said there was unusually high traffic from America Online and a group of universities in the US, in addition to abnormal interest from internet users in “Israel”, Canada and South Africa.
Ayoub accused “the people responsible for the internet in the US” of allowing such an attack to continue without stopping it.
“It’s obvious that the Americans are conspiring with “Israel” to cripple our site,” he said.
However, Ayoub said that “no matter what attempts are made, they will not be able to remove us from the internet. The more they target us, the stronger we will make our website.”
He also added that such an overwhelming attack on the site could not be pursued for long because it created burdensome traffic on many other channels.
“Those attacking us are creating problems for themselves because it is crippling their work too,” he said.

Nasrallah addresses mothers of three captured “Israeli” soldiers…(2)

Hizbullah’s Al-Manar Television on Thursday began broadcasting a clip of party leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah addressing the mothers of the three “Israeli” soldiers Hizbullah captured in Shibaa 12 days ago.
The 90-second clip, dubbed in Hebrew and aired every hour, shows Nasrallah during the news conference he held on Monday to reveal the details of Hizbullah’s capture of an “Israeli” colonel.
Nasrallah’s address to the “Israeli” mothers says: “I want to tell you that your government has not worked seriously for the release of your sons. It is still maneuvering and betting to exert pressure on Hizbullah. I tell both the “Israeli” government and the mothers that this is a losing bet and a waste of time. We are not in a hurry and are willing to wait five, 10, even 20 years, and will not give out any information to any party without the release of detainees in return.”

 

Families of the “Israeli” soldiers captured by Hizbullah have doubts about information given by the “Israeli” army…(3)

Acting “Israeli” foreign minister Shlomo ben Ami stated that his government wanted to be sure that the three “Israeli” soldiers and the Colonel, captured by Hizbullah, are still alive before starting negotiations on the exchange of the “Israeli” prisoners between Hizbullah and “Israel”.

Ben Ami revealed that “Israel” has contacts with both the Red Cross and the U.N. Secretary-General Kofi Annan.

He indicated that he held talks with the German foreign minister in the aim of influencing the Lebanese government.

Concerning the captured soldiers, the “Israeli” daily Maarif reported that the families of the captured “Israeli” soldiers called for the formation of an investigation committee to inquire into the exactitude of what the “Israeli” army is saying on the capturing of the soldiers from occupied Shibaa Farms.

The families, doubting that the informations given are not precise, sent a message to Army Commander of the northern region Gabi Ashkenazi telling him that the three soldiers were given clear instructions to go to the point from were they were captured.

However, the “Israeli” army maintains that the “Israeli” soldiers were supposed to stand at another point and that it is not known why the soldiers moved towards the gate were they were abducted.

 

Syrian President rejects to employ any pressure on Lebanon…(4)

U.S. Secretary of State Madelaine Albright asked the Syrian President Bashar al-Assad during a meeting between the two in Riyadh "to curb Hizbullah".  Albright considered Hizbullah's capture of the “Israeli” soldiers as a dangerous provocation. Al-Assad responded to Albright saying that Hizbullah is an independent social force, which gains its popularity from the Arab peoples.

An American official reported that during the meeting Albright tried to obtain a Syrian support for the results of Sharm al-Sheikh Summit.  She also attempted to extract any indication from al-Assad showing that he is interested in resuming  negotiations with the “Israelis”.

On the other hand, the Syrian president conveyed the disappointment and anger of the Arab peoples, stressing that this should be taken into consideration. He maintained that any American proposal should take into account the requirements of a comprehensive peace, in addition to, the expectations of the Palestinian people.

 

Hard labor demanded for collaborators…(5)

Military Chief Investigating Magistrate Riad Talih demanded a minimum of three years imprisonment with hard labor on Thursday for 14 men accused of collaborating with “Israel”.
The men were charged with entering “Israel” and serving in the now-defunct “Israeli”-proxy militia.
The defendants include militia’s artillery gunners, a policeman from the Khiam detention center and a tank commander.
Five defendants were sentenced to three weeks in jail and each fined LL1 million ($670) for entering “Israel”, the court said in a judgment posted on its bulletin board.
Four other defendants were fined LL500,000 for entering “Israel”.
Warrants were also issued for the arrest of 10 former members.
Lebanon, which is technically at war with “Israel”, prohibits any dealings or contacts with the Jewish state.
More than 1,100 “Israeli” agents have been sentenced to jail terms of up to 15 years since the “Israeli” withdrawal.

 

Damascus and Beirut team up at Arab summit
Hoss: To halt normalization and severe all diplomatic ties with “Israel”…(6)

President Emile Lahoud will leave for Cairo on Friday to lead the Lebanese delegation to the summit. He will be accompanied by Finance Minister George Corm, Health Minister Karam Karam and Major General Jamil Sayyed, the director-general of Surete Generale.
They will be joined there by Prime Minister Salim Hoss, who outlined Lebanon’s stand on Thursday, delivering one of the most hard-line statements at the ministerial meeting.
Hoss said the Palestinians were facing a “genocide,” and decried “Israel’s” threats to Lebanon and Syria.
He said the international summit in Sharm el-Sheikh earlier this week projected the Palestinian-”Israeli” conflict “as though it was a security issue, when in fact it is a cause of a people with the right to live.”
“Through terror, “Israel” is trying to impose a fait accompli that violates international legitimacy and undermines a people’s aspirations to establish a state on its national soil with Al-Quds as its capital,” Hoss said.
He said the “painful developments have overshadowed the joy of liberation in Lebanon.”
Hoss insisted that there would be no compromise on Shibaa, the farmlands which “Israel” claims belong to Syria and as such were not included in Resolution 425. He said Lebanon “reserved its right to employ all possible tools to liberate Shibaa and free the detainees in “Israel”.”
He ridiculed the mobilization of international diplomacy for freeing three “Israeli” soldiers captured by Hizbullah in Shibaa on Oct. 7, when Lebanese detainees have been lingering in “Israeli” jails for years with no one moving to secure their release.
He also described any future settlement with “Israel” that did not include the return of Palestinian refugees to their homes as “a time-bomb, threatening the stability of the region,” insisting that Lebanon would not agree to settle Palestinians on its soil.
Hoss said that the regional developments required firm punitive action, suggesting a “halt in normalizing relations with “Israel” and severing all diplomatic ties and all forms of exchanges with it.” He also said the Arabs should suspend their participation in multilateral conferences attended by “Israel”.
Hoss also took the opportunity to firmly remind the Arabs of their financial obligations toward Lebanon. Arab countries, he said, must “fulfill their commitments to help the Lebanese government shoulder the burdens of relief, reconstruction and development in the liberated areas.”
Previous calls for the payment of some $2 billion in aid allocated to Lebanon in 1990 after the civil war have fallen on deaf ears, and this summit is unlikely to produce more than moral and political backing.
Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa said he backed Lebanon’s stand in its entirety.
Moderate Arab states have for long shunned Damascus’ calls for suspending relations with “Israel” until a Middle East peace settlement has been achieved.
Significantly, Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Saud al-Faisal, speaking to reporters after a meeting with Hoss on the sidelines of the conference, seemed to endorse the call for reviving a strict boycott of “Israel”.
“The Arabs have gained nothing from their ties with “Israel”. “Israel” must be made to understand this,” he said, without disclosing whether he felt Arab leaders would call for the rupture of such links.

 

Syria and Lebanon urge Arabs to sever ties with “Israel” as preparations for summit get underway…(7)

“Syria and Lebanon have called on Arabs to stop all kinds of ties with “Israel” as foreign ministers began meeting in preparation for an emergency Arab summit. Palestinian officials said President Yasser Arafat will call on heads of state attending an Arab summit on Saturday for reviving an economic boycott of “Israel” as well as freezing diplomatic ties until peace is concluded. The political director of the Palestine Liberation Organization Farouq Qaddoumi said the Palestinians will make seven demands at the summit including a call for international troops to enter the occupied Palestinian territories as well as sanctions against the Jewish state for its brutal crackdown on Palestinians. 

“ Arab foreign ministers have met in Cairo to prepare the first summit of their leaders in four years and called to forge a strong Arab rank behind the Palestinians. Of the 22 members of the Arab League, the foreign ministers of all countries except Libya were present. Arab ministers started their meeting with a minute of silence for the Palestinians martyred by “Israeli” troops. Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk Sharaa urged Arabs to sever all ties with the Jewish state. But Sharaa said the summit should come up with quote reasonable decisions. Lebanon's outgoing Prime Minister Selim Hoss, who also holds the foreign ministry's portfolio, urged Arabs to stop any normalization of ties with “Israel” and called on “Israel” to withdraw from occupied Lebanese, Syria and Palestinian territories. Egypt's Amr Moussa stressed Al-Quds should come under Palestinian sovereignty, adding “Israel” must withdraw troops from occupied Lebanese and Syrian lands. The Palestine Liberation Organization's representative Farouk Kaddoumi described “Israel’s” recent killing of Palestinians as massacres. Preparations for the summit have been in full swing. Jordan's King Abdullah and Syrian President Bashar al-Assad agreed after talks in Amman that the upcoming summit needed more preparation to ensure its success. They called for a united Arab position. Assad flew into Jordan yesterday from Saudi Arabia. Official Syrian media said that Damascus and Riyadh had agreed on moves to build a tough Arab position against “Israel” at the summit. Syria has been trying to shore up support for a "strong Arab front" ahead of the Cairo meeting. But there is growing skepticism that Arab leaders will take decisions that match the anger of hundreds of thousands of Arabs who took to the streets in protest over “Israel’s” brutal crackdown on Palestinians. Denunciation of “Israeli” actions and calls for the implementation of UN Security Council resolutions have been highlights of almost every Arab summit in the 52 years since the creation of “Israel”. There is concern the next summit, which opens in Egypt on Saturday, will be just the same. Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak called the summit, the first in four years, to discuss three weeks of fierce “Israeli” attacks on Palestinians. Expectations for the summit range from giving “Israel” a slap on the wrist to demands for a halt of all ties between Arab countries and the Jewish state. The stakes for the meeting have risen since the Sharm el-Sheikh summit this week, which many Arabs believe resolved nothing. Egypt and Jordan, the only Arab countries to have diplomatic ties with “Israel”, have indicated that drastic steps would not be taken. Mubarak said on state-run television "war cannot resolve" the “Israeli”-Palestinian crisis, adding "all issues can only be resolved through negotiations". Mubarak warned that the summit cannot fulfill the hopes of all factions. Jordanian officials said the goal of the summit is to mobilize Arab support for the Palestinians but stressed all measures taken at the top level gathering must be "logical and practical". Libyan leader Moamar Qaddhafi first revealed the final draft of the summit. He said it condemns “Israeli” attacks on Palestinians but takes no practical measures to deal with “Israeli” violence. He warned that Arabs have been quote rebelling on the streets while their leaders planned to discuss, among other issues, forging a common Arab market at the Cairo meeting. Fearing the Arab summit might choose hefty anti-”Israeli” measures, US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright traveled to Riyadh, where she pushed for a cool-headed outcome of the summit. Many Arabs have accused Washington of rendering the summit null and void by convening the Sharm el-Sheikh meeting in which Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat agreed to a cease-fire with “Israel”.

Senior Druze spiritual leaders and the confession's Head of court and judges met in Barouk, in answer to the call of the main two Druze spiritual chiefs Sheikh Abu Hassan Aref Hallawi and Sheikh Abou Mohammed Jawad Walieddine.

They discussed the savage “Israeli” aggressions on the Islamic and Christian holy sites and the defenseless Palestinian people.

They stressed in the meeting on the Arab identity of Al-Quds and on the necessity of defending the holy city.

They asserted the right of the Palestinians to have an independent state and that the Palestinian refugees return to their nation.

The attendants also pointed out the importance of a joint Lebanese-Syrian track in confronting Zionist aggressions.

They also hoped that the Arab Summit will result in wise resolutions which will safeguard the rights of Arabs and Palestinians.

Ceasefire forgotten on the Cursed Mountain…(10)

Two days after the Sharm el-Sheikh agreement, a pitched battle erupted yesterday between Jewish settlers and Palestinians on a hill overlooking the city of Nablus, controlled by the latter.

The prolonged firefight, which left one Palestinian and one “Israeli” dead, is further evidence that maintaining the fragile United States-brokered ceasefire does not depend only on Yasser Arafat's efforts to rein in Palestinian militiamen, or on the “Israeli” army pulling back its forces and softening its tactics. Continuing battles between Palestinians and Jewish settlers, who include bitterly opposed hardliners determined to drive one another off the land, could also wreck it.

The clash erupted at Mount Ebal and led to combat so intense that “Israeli” helicopters, trading fire with Palestinians, could not reach all the wounded. “Israel’s” Prime Minister, Ehud Barak, described the battle last night as a very grave incident and a "blatant violation by the Palestinian Authority" of the ceasefire. National radio broadcast a warning to Palestinians to evacuate the area, raising fears of military action.

Forty settlers, accompanied by an army escort, who were on a trip to view a Jewish shrine, Joseph's Tomb, from the hillside, said they were fired upon by Palestinian gunmen from the nearby Askar refugee camp. Palestinians say the settlers descended the hill toward the camp and opened fire on people harvesting olives.

The mood among settlers on the “Israeli”-occupied West Bank has always tended to be hardline, but there is a different edge after three weeks of bloodshed. Yaacov Hayman, of Itamar, a Jewish settlement on a hill south of Nablus, said before the battle: "There comes a time when if you want to have peace you have to make war."

A series of atrocities in the past 10 days has increased tensions between the Arabs of Nablus, a stronghold of opposition to “Israel”, and settlers on nearby hills.

As the bloodshed worsens, the “Israeli” and the Palestinian leadership are edgy and unsure of whether the terms of the Sharm el-Sheikh summit will hold. A deadline for a total ceasefire expires today.

The settlers are uneasy, and there are calls for the “Israeli” army to intervene more forcibly. "We are going in the direction of a big war," said Sarah Gelbard, a London-born settler who lives in Elon Moreh, a settlement of 1,500 people overlooking Nablus. "There is no way we can sign a deal with people who are thirsty for our blood. The Arabs must be made to understand that they must live here on certain conditions, or leave."

There are 200,000 settlers in the occupied territories. They are a civilian occupation force protected by the “Israeli” armed forces. “Israel” has spent vast sums developing fortified towns and building a network of roads skirting Arab areas.

* The United Nations Human Rights Commission voted in Geneva yesterday to condemn “Israel” for "widespread, systematic and gross violations of human rights" and set up an international inquiry into violence in the West Bank and Gaza.

The body voted by 19 votes to 16 to approve a draft resolution put forward by Arab nations, with 17 abstentions and one absentee. The resolution "strongly condemns the disproportionate and indiscriminate use of force... by the “Israeli” occupying power" during recent outbreaks of violence.
