October Thursday 12-2000

 Thurs2ay 29-2000

 

Introduction

Hizbullah stressed that only a prisoner swap would secure the release of three “Israeli” soldiers captured by the Islamic Resistance (IR) men on Saturday at the Lebanese Shibaa Farms. ““Israel” knows very well that there is no solution except through an exchange operation. Let the “Israelis” decide what they see as appropriate, Hizbullah’s Deputy Secretary General H.E. Sheikh Nahim Qassem said.

Meanwhile, President Emile Lahoud said Lebanon will not “bend to pressures at the expense of our national interests and aimed at imposing a security formula in southern Lebanon protecting “Israel”."

Also, Syrian President Bashar Al-Assad underlined “The Shibaa Farms are Lebanese land occupied by “Israel”. The Lebanese people have the right to fight against the occupiers”.

 

Qassem: “Israeli” soldiers won’t be released except through an exchange
Any attack on lebanonf would be very costly…(1)

An exchange of prisoners through acceptable mediators was the only way to win the release of the three “Israelis”, Qassem said.
“The “Israeli” soldiers won’t be released except through an exchange,” Qassem told a score of local and foreign journalists.
He also refused to release any “free” information about the soldiers, going as far as to say that even reports on their identities, which have not yet been confirmed by Hizbullah, may be wrong.
He also rejected United Nations criticism of the ambush that netted the “Israeli” soldiers, which Annan described a day earlier as a violation of Security Council Resolution 425. “We have done what we did. It was our right,” he said.
Qassem told the “Israeli” leadership that any attempt to liberate the soldiers by force would be doomed and warned Prime Minister Ehud Barak that any attack on Lebanon would be very costly for “Israel”.
““Israel” knows very well that there is no solution except through a swap ... The soldiers will not be released except by means of an exchange operation. Let them (the “Israelis”) decide what they see as appropriate,” he said.
Sheikh Mohammed Yazbek, a member of Hizbullah’s Shura Council and a representative of Iranian leader Ayatollah Khamenei, said Annan’s stand was designed to “please” “Israeli” Premier Ehud Barak.
“UN resolutions can’t be realized as long as there are prisoners in the enemy’s jails,” he said during a celebration at a mosque in Baalbek marking the birthday of Imam Ali.
The cleric vowed to continue the struggle against “Israel” and criticized the United States for obstructing aid to Lebanon.
“No one dared to help Lebanon for fear of the American master’s wrath,” he said.
He added: “We will persist in our stand as long as the Shibaa Farms remain occupied and our detainees continue to reside in “Israeli” jails.”

Outside world turns screws to cow Beirut
US, UN, and EU demand answers about captured “Israelis”
Lahoud: Lebanon will not bow to pressures…(2)

The international community turned up the heat on Lebanon Wednesday to provide proof that three captured “Israeli” soldiers were alive and to improve security along the southern border, but Beirut strongly rejected free-of-charge concessions.
“Lebanon will not bow to pressures to find a security formula in the South that would reassure “Israel” at the expense of our national interests which are linked to completing the liberation of our territories, the release of all detainees and the return of all (Palestinian) refugees,” President Emile Lahoud retorted.
The looming dispute with Washington, the United Nations and the European Union coincided with a 24-hour delay in a planned visit by UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan.
Annan rescheduled his trip for Thursday amid a political storm  caused by his denunciation of Hizbullah’s capture of the “Israeli” troops as a violation of Resolution 425 and his suggestion that the government was not doing an adequate job in policing its side of the so-called “Blue Line.”
Beirut categorically rejected the UN chief’s accusations, maintaining that resistance operations were legitimate as long as “Israel” occupied Shibaa Farms, a stretch of farmland still held by “Israel” and claimed by Lebanon.
As Beirut braced for Annan’s visit and the potential conflict in views, a European Union envoy, who is closely coordinating the Middle East trouble-shooting effort with the UN chief, added a new demand of Lebanon.
Javier Solana, the EU’s foreign-policy and security chief, said the international community wanted “proof” that the three “Israelis” captured in Shibaa on Saturday were still alive.
Solana conveyed the request in separate meetings with Lahoud, Speaker Nabih Berri and Prime Minister Salim Hoss.
Asked if he had information about the health of the soldiers, Solana said: “I haven’t got any answers unfortunately for the moment, but I insist that it would be quite an important sign and an important contribution to lower the temperature in the region.”
Solana also said he “agreed” with Annan’s allegation that Hizbullah’s penetration of the Blue Line to capture the “Israeli” soldiers constituted a violation of Resolution 425.
Lahoud, for his part, said he complained to Solana about “daily “Israeli” aggressions against Lebanon which are subjecting civilian Lebanese to danger.”
““Israeli” warplanes are violating Lebanese airspace, in violation of UN Security Council Resolution 425 and a blatant violation of Lebanese sovereignty, and “Israel” bears the sole responsibility for that,” he said.
UN sources said Annan was flying in from “Israel” for a noon meeting with Lahoud. They said he planned to travel later to Naqoura, the headquarters of the UNIFIL, return to Beirut, and return to “Israel” in the evening.
His trip will coincide with that of Iranian Foreign Minister Kamal Kharrazi, and diplomatic sources said the two might meet to discuss a possible exchange of the “Israeli” soldiers for Lebanese and, perhaps, other Arab prisoners.
In Syria on Wednesday, Kharrazi said that Hizbullah would free the “Israelis” in
exchange for “all the prisoners” in “Israel”, without specifying whether that would include Palestinian detainees.
The capture of the three “Israelis”, coupled with a clash on the border between “Israeli” troops and unarmed demonstrators in which two people were killed on Saturday, has threatened to open a new battlefront as “Israeli” clashes with the Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza rage on.
As the Arab and Muslim world rallied behind the Palestinians, the Lebanese and the Syrians, the Western world mobilized its diplomacy to defuse the latest spate of Middle East violence. Where Lebanon is concerned, the United States, the UN and the EU  stepped up pressure on the government to expand the 1,000-strong force it has deployed in the South and to extend the area of operations all the way to the border.
But, Lahoud reiterated on Wednesday, “Israel” has to complete its withdrawal from Shibaa and release the 19 Lebanese detainees from prison before Lebanon will consider Resolution 425 truly implemented.
Berri and Hoss rallied behind the president, issuing separate statements to support his stand.
Annan’s remarks, the speaker warned, could provide a political “cover” for “Israeli” aggression against Lebanon.
“Such statements are unacceptable to us,” Berri declared, “irrespective of our friendship with and respect for the secretary-general.”
Enraged by the international focus on the fate of the three “Israelis”, Berri said: “They’ve turned the entire world upside down over three “Israeli” soldiers snatched in occupied Lebanese territory, even though we’ve made no secret of our intention to maintain the resistance as we will not give up one inch of our territory or single prisoner.
“The entire world has been mobilized to check on the well-being of the three soldiers and we find no one asking about the children being slaughtered in Palestine. What kind of justice are they seeking?
“They’re providing a cover for “Israel” to maintain aggression against the Palestinians and on Lebanon,” he said.
Hoss, in a separate statement, said Annan’s allegations were “out of context.” He reiterated Berri’s argument about the attack taking place on occupied Lebanese land and decried the failure to censure “Israel” for firing on unarmed demonstrators at the border on Saturday.
Information Minister Anwar Khalil defended the right of the Lebanese and the Palestinians to protest at the border, adding that ““Israel’s” disregard for Arab rights is causing the tension.”
“It’s strange that the UN secretary-general should claim that the capture of three “Israelis” on occupied Lebanese territory is a violation of Resolution 425,” he told reporters. “The question is: what are they doing on Lebanese territory?
Khalil also cautioned the Palestinians against turning peaceful protests at the border into violent acts, but insisted that the government would not ban demonstrations, “which is a constitutional right of all citizens.”

 

Lahoud lists complaints to Solana…(3)

”President Emile Lahoud insisted Wednesday that “Israel” alone bears responsibility for its “flagrant violations” of UN Security Council Resolution 425.
Addressing Javier Solana, the European Union’s foreign-policy and security chief who arrived in Beirut earlier in the day from Cairo,  Lahoud affirmed that his country intended to liberate the Shibaa Farms and recover Lebanese detainees and prisoners in “Israeli” jails.
Enumerating “Israeli” violations, Lahoud told Solana ­ who is on the fourth leg of a Middle East  tour designed to pacify the region ­ that “Israel” has persisted in its daily aggression against Lebanon, endangering the lives of peaceful and unarmed Lebanese citizens. The president also told Solana that “Israeli” jets had violated Lebanese airspace.
Solana was accompanied by EU Middle East policy coordinator Miguel Angel Moratinos, French Ambassador Phillipe Lecourtier, and Dimitris Kourkoulas, head of the EU Commission’s delegation in Lebanon.
The tour has already taken the EU team to “Israel”, the Palestinian territories, and Egypt. They are also due to visit Jordan and Syria.
Solana told reporters that he hoped tensions between “Israelis” and Palestinians would subside, adding that UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan’s visit to the region could help.

 

Lebanese have right to fight for Shibaa Farms, says Assad…(4)

“ The Lebanese have the right to "fight" to retake the disputed Shibaa Farms from “Israeli” control, Syrian President Bashar al-Assad said Wednesday.

"The Shibaa Farms are Lebanese land occupied by “Israel”. The Lebanese people have the right to fight against the occupiers," he said in a meeting with Iran's Foreign Minister Kamal Kharazi, the official SANA news agency reported.

Kharazi also met his Russian counterpart Igor Ivanov and leaders of eight Palestinian groups in Damascus opposed to the autonomy deals with “Israel”, before leaving for Lebanon.

“Israel” withdrew its occupation forces from south Lebanon in May, but held on to the Shibaa Farms, located at the meeting point of the Lebanese, Syrian and “Israeli” borders, as they were conquered in 1967 and not in 1978.

Hizbullah captured three “Israeli” soldiers in the disputed border area on October 7, and now wants to swap them for 19 Lebanese held in “Israel”.

“Israel” seized the farmlands from Syria in the 1967 Middle East war but the area is claimed by Lebanon with the support of Damascus.

Lebanese parties and political forces held a meeting at the Baath party office.They hailed the Islamic Resistance for capturing the “Israeli” soldiers and considered that the operation was executed with precision and at the correct timing.

Iran: "we shall not pressure Hizbullah"…(6)

Iranian foreign minister Kamal Kharazi said he doesn't see any instant solution pertaining to the three captured “Israeli” soldiers held by Hizbullah and the Arab detainees in “Israeli” prisons.  "Intensive efforts to free the “Israeli” soldiers and the Arab prisoners are being made, but until now  no solution has been found, "Kharazi said.

He said that the Iranian mediation doesn't mean that it is practicing any pressure.

Hizbullah takes  its decisions independently and the “Israeli” soldiers issue is related essentially with the Zionist entity's readiness to fulfill the legal Lebanese demands.

 

Advice for Annan…(7)

Kofi Annan has the power to do much good in this part of the world, but only if he sticks to the facts and avoids taking sides.
Unfortunately, as it affects the Lebanese-”Israeli” front, the UN secretary-general’s tour of the region has already gotten off to an awkward start because of his public statements to the effect that Hizbullah’s capture of three “Israeli” soldiers was a violation of Resolution 425 and that the troops should be released immediately and unconditionally. These comments can be partly discounted because he was sharing a podium with “Israeli” Premier Ehud Barak, but they must not be allowed to infect behind-the-scenes negotiations.
First of all, the men were snatched on what all sides agree is occupied land: whether it is Lebanese land or Syrian land is largely irrelevant, since Beirut has made it plain that it expects to regain all of its territory before it, as the injured party, considers 425 to have been fully implemented by “Israel”.
Secondly, the Jewish state freely admits that it is holding more than a dozen Lebanese ­ some of whom have languished in “Israeli” prisons for more than two decades ­ and its highest court has ruled that they are being held as bargaining chips. Why has there been no international outcry about “Israel’s” trampling of their rights and international law?
Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, asked Annan to look into the prisoner issue when the two men met in the wake of “Israel’s” May withdrawal. He was advised to be patient. Then Nasrallah presented his case to the UN’s special envoy for the South, Rolf Knutsson. Again, he was told to be patient, to keep the border quiet. The resistance leader promised to be patient, but he also warned that patience has its limits and that his organization was not without other options to bring about the release of the Lebanese detainees.
If “Israel” wants its soldiers back, what it has to do is very clear: send our people back. And if Annan truly wants to defuse tensions rather than apportion blame for the latest dust-up, what he has to do is also very clear: work on arranging a swap.
The UN chief has the prestige to make negotiations work. We have to convince him that we will make good on any deal for an exchange, and he has to convince the “Israelis” that this is a straightforward issue. If he fails, the cycle of violence may be ignited anew, and no thinking person wants that to happen.

 

Trial opens for collaborators…(8)

The trial of 49 people on charges of collaborating with “Israel” opened at a military court on Wednesday, marking the 33rd batch of such hearings.
If found guilty, the defendants, who are mostly members of “Israel’s” now-defunct proxy militia, face various imprisonment terms and fines.
The verdicts and sentences, which can be appealed at the Military Supreme Court, were expected to be handed down around midnight.
The court, headed by Brigadier Maher Safieddine, has sentenced more than 1,100 people to jail terms of up to 15 years since the trials of collaborators began on June 5.
Out of 2,500 “Israeli” agents, some 2,200 have surrendered to Lebanese authorities or were captured after “Israel” withdrew.

 

“Israeli” settlers attack Palestinian school children…(9)

For three days, since it began on Saturday night, large groups of settlers have been rampaging through Palestinian villages and towns in the West Bank and 1948 areas/inside the Green Line, burning down Palestinian homes, vandalising parked Palestinian cars and throwing stones and shooting at Palestinian drivers, and attacking Palestinians. (For a full overview please visit http://www.addameer.org/september2000/media/overview.html or the North American mirror http://nigelparry.com/september2000/media/overview.html)

In a continuation of “Israeli” settler violence against Palestinians, numerous areas throughout the West Bank and Gaza, and 1948 areas reported continued settler attacks throughout the day, and carried on late into the evening. In Jenin, Nablus, Bethlehem, Hebron, Khan Younis and Rafah, “Israeli” settlers attacked school children on their way home from school today. In some instances, “Israeli” settlers shot at children near their schools.

In the village of Silt Al Thaher in the Jenin area, armed “Israeli” settlers attacked three Palestinian schools, in addition to nearby houses. Palestinian cars traveling along the main road of the village, connecting Jenin to Nablus and Tulkarem, were also attacked by the settlers, with a number of injuries reported.

In Ya'bad, also near Jenin, armed “Israeli” settlers, under the protection of the “Israeli” military, attacked school children at their school on the outskirts of the village, shooting at the school and at children attempting to flee to their homes. In order to reach their homes, children had to pass through the settlers and soldiers, after which clashes erupted. Several injuries were reported.

In Hossan village near Bethlehem, another Palestinian school was attacked by “Israeli” settlers under the protection of the “Israeli” military. Clashes followed the assault, which are on going. A number of injuries have been reported, including an ambulance driver. The ambulance driver was attempting to reach those injured when the ambulance was shot at, the driver pulled out of the car and severely beaten by “Israeli” settlers. “Israeli” settlers also attacked a mini-bus carrying disabled children to Bethlehem, smashing the windows of the bus.

In Rafah, Gaza, “Israeli” settlers within an “Israeli” military post shot at school children on their way home from a nearby school. Several injuries were reported. Brief clashes erupted as the children passed the military post in order to reach their homes. One 12 year old child is reported to be clinically dead as a result of live ammunition to his head. At least 2 other injured are said to be in critical condition. Further settler attacks of school children were also reported in Khan Younis, near the Kfar Darom and Gush Katif settlements.

Earlier this morning, a second apartment complex was attacked by “Israeli” rockets and destroyed on Al Shuhada street near Netzarim Junction. The complex had been evacuated three days earlier as it had already witnessed several rocket attacks. On Erez, Carmi and Rafah border crossings, 20 shipping trucks were burnt with their contents, with another 70 trucks smashed and a number of parked cars destroyed by “Israeli” settlers. The vehicles have been unaccessable to their Palestinian owners as they are in an “Israeli” military controlled area.

In the Ramallah area, clashes erupted again this afternoon near the northern entrance of the city, with a number of injuries reported. Ra'ed Hammoudi, currently in Ramallah hospital, is reported to be in extremely critical condition from live ammunition. Recent reports state that “Israeli” settlers have been stoning Palestinian cars at the nearby Jalazone Refugee Camp. Jamal Abdel Hamid, 40 years old, is reported to be in extremely critical condition in Ramallah hospital after his car was attacked by settlers near Ofra settlement. Jamal was dragged out his car, severely beaten, and thrown to the side of the road. His skull was damaged, causing internal bleeding, as a result of the attack. Nearby Birzeit University announced that all of its classes will be shortened to half an hour, ending the university day at 2:00 PM as a result of the danger faced by students traveling home after 5:00 PM.

In Umm Safa, “Israeli” settlers and “Israeli” Police sealed off the entrance of the village and have been preventing anyone from entering or leaving the area after the funeral of Issam Judeh today. Later this evening “Israeli” settlers under the protection of the “Israeli” military invaded the village, attacking houses, smashing windows and destroying cars. Clashes are on-going, with reports of live ammunition fire. No further information is available.

Nablus has been declared a closed military zone by the “Israeli” Authorities. “Israeli” settlers attacked several villages around Nablus, including the villages of Yitmah and Howarrah. Howarrah has been under “Israeli”-imposed curfew for three days. During the curfew today, “Israeli” settlers, under the protection of the “Israeli” military, attacked houses and smashed windows and cars, forcing residents to come out of their homes to defend themselves. Upon leaving their homes, “Israeli” soldiers shot at residents after being charged with breaking the curfew. At least 10 injuries from live ammunition have been reported.

In Al-Quds, settler attacks continue throughout the night. Several roads leading from local neighborhoods to the center of Al-Quds have been sealed off by settlers. Reports of houses being attacked in Beit Hanina and Bir Nabala continue. Attempts by settlers to burn the Latin Patriarchate church during last night's attacks were unsuccessful. However, attempts to attack churches within Al-Quds neighborhoods continue tonight. In the old city of Al-Quds, a Palestinian was severely beaten and injured by a sharp object to his head by Isreali settlers. Makassed Hospital in Al-Quds reported that this was the 17th palestinian to come to the hospital as a result of such attacks within the last three days.

In the 1948 areas/inside the Green Line, a number of cities reported settler attacks on Palestinian workers during the day. In Haifa, Jewish residents attacked Palestinian residents and destroyed three tombs within a nearby Islamic cemetary. Clashes stopped after “Israeli” police interfered.

In Akka, clashes erupted briefly after several hundred Jewish residents of the New City attacked the Old City - in which there are only arab residents - smashing shops and cars in the area. “Israeli” Police closed off the area, after which clashes erupted. Police used live ammunition and tear gas, resulting in a number of injuries, including one Palestinian shot in the mouth with live ammunition.

Sami will soon be this war's latest child martyr…(11)

“The little boy is lying under a pink flowery sheet, his bandaged head tilted to one side and his cheeks still streaked with a mix of blood and Gaza dust.

His pathetically small chest pumps away steadily – up, down, up down – a human bellows driven by an artificial respirator.

His closed eye-lids, sealed by long lashes, are swollen; so are his lips, twisted by the battery of pipes and wires that connect his mouth to the beeping and buzzing life support system at his bedside.

Officially, Sami Abu Jazar – a 12-year-old Palestinian who looks no more than nine – is still alive. His heart pounds doggedly on. But, in every other sense, he is dead – "clinically dead", as the doctors put it – because of the “Israeli” bullet buried in his skull.

He never had a hope. He was brain dead from the moment the bullet had passed through his forehead. Islamic custom forbids the doctors from pulling the plug on Sami; only when his heart stops, will he be pronounced dead and released for burial. "We are just waiting for the end," said the doctor in his intensive care ward at Shifa Hospital in Gaza City.

Only then, will Sami Abu Jazar officially become – by the latest count by the UN's Children's Fund – the 24th child to die in the Palestinian uprising over the last fortnight. Or will he? It could be three or four days before his heart gives up its pointless work. By then, he could be number 25, or 26, or will it be 30?

The violence in the Middle East has many horrible forms, but the killing of children has come to symbolise the tragedy above all else. Even as Sami lay there yesterday – and the region slid into a period of comparative calm after the storm of the last two weeks – news was coming in that another Palestinian youth, 17-year-old Sami Hassan Salim, had been shot dead by “Israeli” soldiers on the West Bank. He was number 23.

The search for a solution has brought the world's diplomatic heavyweights rushing to the region. The UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan held talks with both the “Israelis” and Palestinians yesterday before unexpectedly extending his stay for 24 hours. And Robin Cook, the Foreign Secretary, arrived on a mission to "persuade both sides to step back from the brink".

But that already complex and ambitious goal will never be realised while children go on dying. It forms a vortex: the more bloodshed there is, the more public opinion – in “Israel” and among the Palestinians – hardens against peace.

In Gaza, that process is well under way. Palestinians talk now of peace only on their terms, and that means by securing what they perceive to be their rights – a full “Israeli” withdrawal to the borders of 4 June 1967. "The situation may be quiet now, but for how long?" said Dr Mouaweya Hassanien, director of the casualty unit at Shifa hospital. "A month, perhaps. Maybe two. But the uprising will go on until we have a full resolution." By that, he explained, he meant full withdrawal – a condition to which the “Israelis” will never comply.

If he is right, it means deadlock – and the result will be one tragedy after another. Sami is the third really small – prepubescent – boy to die in Gaza – the first, Mohammed al-Durah, also 12, has become an emblem of the horrors of the conflict after TV cameras caught his final moments, huddled at the side of his terrified father.

Unfortunately, Sami's death was not filmed, so we are left to decide whose version of events to believe. The “Israelis” say that “Israeli” soldiers shot at a crowd of rioters at an outpost at Rafah on Gaza's southern border with Egypt. Their men were being pelted by molotov cocktails, one of which penetrated the outpost. Petrol bombs are regarded by the “Israeli” military as a deadly weapon, so the soldiers fired back – on the grounds that their lives were threatened.

Sami's family tell a different story. They say the boy – one of seven children whose father works as a farm worker in “Israel” – was on his way to see his sister after school when he was shot – out of the blue.

"Sami comes from a farming family in southern Gaza, a long way from the trouble. He is not involved in politics," said Abed Abu Jazar, a family member. "He has no idea of what's been going on. His dream was to make a living growing flowers and sell them. He was just a kid going home from school." The little boy was wearing his school back-pack when he was shot – not, take note, the standard apparel of the average Palestinian molotov cocktail thrower.
