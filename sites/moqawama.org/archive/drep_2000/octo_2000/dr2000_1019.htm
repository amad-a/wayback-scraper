Kuwaiti daily al-Rai: "The Zionist colonel was graduated to a high rank in the Mossad"

Hizbullah smashes “Israeli” spy network in lebanon

Al-Rai Al-Aam Kuwaiti newspaper reported an “Israeli” source in London to have revealed to journalists that Elhanan Tannenbaun, the “Israeli” colonel captured by Hizbullah in Lebanon, had been graduated in the “Israeli” intelligence Mossad to the rank of assistant to the Mossad's chief.

More information indicated that the colonel was implicated in wiretapping Hizbullah fighters for many years, and that he faced trial in Switzerland after the Swiss police laid hand on him.

The colonel's colleagues affirmed his fancy for adventure and described him to be shrewd. But he fell in Hizbullah's trap…(1)

Also, Hizbullah has smashed a number of “Israeli” spy networks following its announcement of the capture of an “Israeli” reserve colonel, the daily said Wednesday.

Quoting unnamed sources, Al-Rai Al-Aam said Hizbullah capitalised on the “Israeli” confusion that followed Sheikh Hassan Nasrallah's announcement Sunday of Elhanan Tannenbaum's capture to uncover a number of “Israeli” intelligence networks.

It said “Israel” activated its spy networks in Lebanon for a few hours to verify Nasrallah's announcement, which enabled Hizbullah to act.

A number of arrests may have been made, said the paper, describing the new operation as equally important to that of the previous capture of three “Israeli” soldiers and of Tannenbaum.

The story first began to unfold on Sunday, when Nasrallah announced that the fiercely anti-”Israeli” Hizbullah had taken prisoner an “Israeli” colonel involved in a "complex operation."

The news stunned “Israel”, after the capture of three of its soldiers by Hizbullah on the border with Lebanon little more than a week earlier.

“Israel” launched a frantic investigation, suspecting a kidnapping on the border, but the army found that no officer on active service was missing and began casting the net further afield.

Later a relative of Tannenbaum reported him missing to the foreign ministry and several hours later it became clear he was the man being held by Hizbullah.

Nasrallah said on Monday that Tannenbaum was arrested in Lebanon…(2)

As you sow

According to press reports, US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright wants Damascus to calm tensions on the Lebanese-”Israeli” border by using its influence to “rein in” Hizbullah. In a nutshell, Albright told Syrian President Bashar Assad what he has already heard from “Israeli” leaders themselves: the Jewish state “will hold both Lebanon and Syria responsible” for the fate of four “Israeli” servicemen captured by the resistance in the past couple of weeks.
The approach that Albright and her boss, President Clinton, have taken to this latest set-to has been an extension of general US policy in the region for decades, one of unthinkingly reiterating “Israel’s” positions while ignoring those of the Arab world.
This time around, the imbalance could have easily been avoided if US policymakers had asked themselves a simple question: why has Hizbullah chosen the course of action in question? The answer is that the White House gave Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah and his advisers almost no choice in the matter because no help was forthcoming when various Lebanese and Arab voices demanded the release of more than a dozen Lebanese whom the Jewish state has held in prison for years.
Nasrallah expressed his grievances in a conventional fashion shortly after the “Israelis” pulled out in May: in talks with UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, he warned that the resistance would not consider Lebanon to have been totally liberated unless and until, among other things, “Israel” set the detainees free. No UN, US, or EU officials came galloping into the Middle East to press for their freedom, so Nasrallah could not help but presume that his strategy had failed.
Unaccustomed to sitting on its hands, Hizbullah’s reaction to this international intransigence could not have been more natural. After all, it is a professional guerrilla organization, so when its attempts to obey the niceties of diplomacy were rebuffed, it reverted to what it knows best: speaking to the enemy ­ and his powerful allies ­ in the only language to which they respond.
Lo and behold, it worked ­ Washington has finally taken notice of Lebanon’s existence again. Now if we can just convince Clinton and Albright of the radical notion that gross human-rights violations committed against Lebanese are matters worthy of their consideration, the situation might actually be resolved satisfactorily for all concerned ­ including the four “Israelis”, whose families now have a tiny taste of what some of their Lebanese counterparts have endured for more than two decades.
Lebanon is a peaceful country. Its people seek no quarrel with others, and its military is too small for anything but a minimum self-defense capability. Now “Israeli” warplanes are plying our skies again, sometimes frightening women and children by blasting through the sound barrier, sometimes lackadaisically ­ and arrogantly ­ over-flying our capital at an altitude that our meager air-defense weapons cannot hope to reach.
This was the last thing that the Lebanese public wanted to see, but the decision was not in its hands, and nor was it in Hizbullah’s: the choice was taken in Washington when America’s leaders decided that their expensive ally did not deserve to be scolded for adopting an approach to the detainee issue that would have made Burma’s ruling junta proud.
This goes to the very core of Washington’s unconditional (and paradoxical) support for whatever boorish policy “Israel’s” government happens to trot out on any given day. When “Israel” does something for which any other government would receive a tongue-lashing from the White House, the Jewish state’s neighbors are accused of “provocation;” when “Israeli” occupation forces treat Palestinian civilians far worse than British colonial troops ever did Americans, the successors to the world’s first popular revolutionaries warn the victims of oppression to stop making trouble.
China and North Korea can rely on America to remind them when they treat human beings like farm animals. Unfortunately for a few “Israeli” families, their sons and husbands are now in captivity because America would not do the same for “Israel”…(3)

 

“Israeli” jets violate airspace

“Israeli” warplanes continued to fly sorties over Lebanon on Wednesday, breaking the sound barrier over Beirut and in the South.
Supersonic booms were first heard over the Western Bekaa district and the Marjayoun region before flying over Beirut and Tripoli.
On Friday, “Israeli” warplanes carried out mock air raids over the southern port city of Tyre and other nearby regions.
The overflights began after Hizbullah captured three “Israeli” soldiers along the border near Shibaa on Oct. 7. Previously, “Israel” had refrained from flying reconnaissance patrols over Lebanon following the withdrawal of its troops in May.
President Emile Lahoud complained of repeated “Israeli” “violations of Lebanese airspace” to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan during his visit here last week.
Meanwhile, “Israel” has warned that protesters in south Lebanon risk coming under fire from its soldiers if they hurl projectiles over the border…(4)

 

Beirut seeks Arab support in confrontation with “Israel”…(5)

“Acting Prime Minsiter Selim Hoss has left for Cairo to take part in preparatory meetings for the Arab summit scheduled to start on Saturday. Hoss said Beirut will ask Arab leaders to sever ties with “Israel” and stop any normalization of relations until a just and comprehensive Mideast peace is concluded. The outgoing prime minister said the Sharm el-Sheikh summit, which ended in Egypt with a Palestinian-”Israeli” accord to stop more than two weeks of the uprising, failed to resolve fundamental Palestinian problems which sparked fighting. President Emile Lahoud will head the Lebanese delegation to the Arab summit. He is expected to call on Arab states to make a clear statement in support of Lebanese demands that “Israel” withdraw from the occupied Shibaa Farms and release some 19 detainees. Lahoud, who discussed the latest developments in south Lebanon with Canadian Prime Minister Jean Chretien in a telephone conversation, will also ask Arab countries to implement pledges of financial aid to reconstruct the zone “Israel” occupied in the south.

 

Murr grants OK for center to help victims from Khiam…(6)

Interior Minister Michel Murr has approved an application for the formation of a non-profit association to be called the “Khiam Center for the Rehabilitation of Torture Victims.”
According to its application, the group was named after the notorious Khiam detention center.
The association stated that it would cooperate with the appropriate quarters in collecting information on the conditions of those once detained in “Israeli” facilities.
It hopes to set up centers for medical and psychological treatment, as well for social and educational rehabilitation.
Other proposed activities include organizing conferences and rehabilitation seminars, as well as publishing books and documents on the psychological and social effects of torture.
The association was founded by outgoing Telecom Minister Issam Naaman; Western Bekaa-Rashaya MP Robert Ghanem; Mohammed Safa, who heads the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in “Israeli” Prisons; Raja Ammar; and Elias Mutran.

 

Warrants issued for collaborators…(7)

Qassem: Coexistance between Palestinians and “Israeli” usurper is impossible

Military Investigator Riad Talih issued warrants on Wednesday for the arrest of 14 men accused of collaborating with “Israel”. The men were charged with serving in the now-defunct “Israeli”-proxy militia and revealing information “serving enemy interests.”
The case is the latest involving ex-militiamen who returned from the Jewish state after fleeing during the “Israeli” withdrawal in May.
Various prison terms and penalties have been imposed on collaborators by military courts examining cases of collaboration with “Israel”.

 

Lebanese parties come out against Sharm el-Sheikh …(8)

Political and religious organizations denounced the recent Sharm el-Sheikh summit Wednesday, urging Arab leaders to support the intifada and announce the immediate establishment of an independent Palestinian state.
On Tuesday, Palestinian, American and “Israeli” leaders agreed to try and halt the wave of violence which has killed over 100 Palestinians and brought the peace process to the brink of collapse.
Hizbullah’s deputy secretary-general, Sheikh Naim Qassem, dismissed the meetings, saying they were designed to undermine the intifada and absolve “Israel” of all its recent crimes.
“Talking about an alleged peace in the Middle East through repression, injustice, criminal acts and occupation is impossible,” he said.
Qassem maintained that the uprising had proved that “coexistence between the owners of the land and the “Israeli” usurper is impossible.”
“We’ve come to the conclusion that the Sharm el-Sheikh and other summits may lead to steps to exert pressure to reach unrealistic and illogical solutions, but they cannot change the intifada’s initiative to recover lost rights,” he declared.
The Progressive Socialist Party said it supported the intifada and denounced the agreement reached at the summit, calling for “protecting and helping (the uprising) by all means.”
The party urged the those leaders attending the upcoming Arab summit, due to be held in Cairo on Saturday, to shoulder their “responsibilities at the present decisive and dangerous phase, so that (the meeting) may not merely be an echo of the Sharm el-Sheikh summit.”
The PSP also issued a statement calling on Arab leaders to adopt “a decisive and effective stand protecting the intifada in Palestine,” and demanding “an immediate and total “Israeli” withdrawal from the West Bank and Gaza.”
The statement urged Arab leaders to demand full implementation of UN resolutions, especially Resolution 242, and keep the whole of Al-Quds under Arab sovereignty. In addition it stressed the right of Palestinians to set up an independent and sovereign state.
In their turn, Druze religious leaders deplored “Israel’s” “barbaric aggression against Islamic and Christian holy shrines, and the unarmed Palestinian people.”
Meeting at the Barouk residence of leading cleric Sheikh Abu Hassan Aref Halawi, leaders from throughout the country called for maintaining the “Arab character” of Al-Quds.
They too declared their support for the right of Palestinians to establish an independent state, and urged the Arab summit to adopt “wise resolutions preserving Arab rights in general, and the Palestinian people’s rights in particular.”
For its part, the Communist Party expressed support for the intifada, and issued a statement urging summit members to “declare the establishment of the independent state of Palestine.” The party said all forms of support should be immediately provided for the uprising and that the announcement of a Palestinian state should be made at the summit itself.
It also urged Libyan leader Moammar Gadhafi not to boycott the meeting, as he had indicated earlier, and to adopt the “maximum demands of the Palestinian people.”
Ali Qanso, leader of the Syrian Social Nationalist Party, said his group rejected the decisions reached at Sharm el-Sheikh. His party called for “transforming the intifada into armed resistance.” It also asked “all parties and popular forces in the Arab world” to exert pressure on their governments to adopt “practical positions supporting the intifada,” and to confront “the ‘“Israeli”’-organized terrorism.”
The party urged Arab summit participants to enforce a total boycott of “Israel”, to abolish existing agreements, withdraw diplomatic representation and sever economic and commercial ties with the Jewish state.
