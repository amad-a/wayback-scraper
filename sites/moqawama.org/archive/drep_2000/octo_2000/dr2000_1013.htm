However, Lebanon’s President Emile Lahoud adamantly rejected international demands for concessions to on implacable foe that is never censured for systematic aggressions on the Arab countries.

 

“Israel” must release all prisoners in exchange for soldiers: Hizbullah…(1)

“Hizbullah said Thursday that “Israel” must free all Arab prisoners it holds to obtain the release of three “Israeli” soldiers captured on October 7.

Hizbullah chief Sheikh Hassan Nasrallah, in an interview with Qatar's Al-Jazira television monitored here, also urged Arab governments to open their borders for a jihad (holy war) against “Israel”.

He demanded “Israel’s” release of "all prisoners -- Palestinians, Lebanese, Arabs and Iranians -- as well as the return of all our fighters listed as missing and the return of the remains of those fallen in combat".

Contacts were continuing "with different parties" to arrange an exchange for the soldiers seized by Hizbullah guerrillas on the Lebanon-”Israel” border, he said, without giving details.

Asked why Hizbullah had so far restrained from firing Katyusha rockets on northern “Israel” in solidarity with the Palestinians, Nasrallah said "we are facing a new and complicated situation, and we should face it wisely."

However, he reserved the right to "fire Katyushas against settlements (in northern “Israel”) when we judge the moment right."

 

Fadlallah dismisses UN claims of Hizbullah violations…(2)

Leading cleric Sayyed Mohammed Hussein Fadlallah Thursday ruled out the possibility of “Israeli” military operations against Lebanon and Syria if Hizbullah does not release three captured “Israeli” soldiers.
““Israel’s” threats against Lebanon and Syria are a way to terrorize the two countries and exert political pressures on them,” he told Syria’s satellite channel.
He brushed aside remarks by UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan that Hizbullah’s capture of the three soldiers in the disputed Shibaa Farms on Saturday violated UN Security Council Resolution 425.
““Israel” violates the resolution by keeping 19 Lebanese prisoners in its jails and maintaining its troops in the Shibaa Farms,” he said. “Why hasn’t anyone called these acts as violations of human rights?”
Fadlallah said the “Israeli” troops “are prisoners of war because they were snatched from an occupied area. (Hizbullah’s) fighters did not violate international resolutions. We don’t even consider “Israel” as a legitimate state.”
Asked about “Israel’s” attacks on Palestinians over the last two weeks, the leading cleric said: “Jews know no God. People who know God must love other people who are God’s creatures. Celebrating their Forgiveness Day (Yom Kippur) by killing women, children, and old people has nothing to do with God’s teachings.”

 

Annan tells Lahoud to rein in stonethrowers…(3)
Lahoud: Lebanon’s Army is not a policef force for “Israel”

“The United Nations sought to pacify the Lebanese front of the Middle East conflict on Thursday, arguing for tighter security along the southern border to avoid a spillover from Palestinian-”Israeli” violence as it slipped out of control.
But President Emile Lahoud adamantly rejected international demands for concessions to an implacable foe that is never censured for systematic assaults on its Arab neighbors.
UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan cut short his visit to Beirut and rushed back to “Israel” after the lynch-mob killing of three soldiers in Ramallah triggered a chain of retaliatory attacks, plunging the Palestinian territories into deeper crisis.
But before his departure, he outlined in a meeting with Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss a role for Lebanon in the international trouble-shooting effort underway in the Middle East ­ rein in stone-throwers pelting “Israeli” soldiers across the southern border and allow a neutral mediator access to the three “Israeli” soldiers captured by Hizbullah to ascertain their wellbeing.
He also modified his outright accusation that Saturday’s capture of the soldiers was a breach of Security Council Resolution 425, describing it instead as a “violation of the Blue Line,” which the UN demarcated for “Israel’s” troop withdrawal in May.
According to a terse Presidential Palace statement, Lahoud insisted during the hour-long meeting that it was “Israel” that was continuously violating Resolution 425.
He said “Israel” must relinquish the occupied Shibaa Farms, which are claimed by Lebanon, release the 19 Lebanese detainees and stop the daily overflights of its warplanes for Beirut to consider 425 fulfilled.
The statement urged Annan to help swiftly end the Palestinian-”Israeli” violence, now in its third week and threatening the entire Middle East with more bloodshed and turmoil.
According to political sources, Lahoud told Annan that “Israel” has only itself to blame for the latest wave of violence.
The UN chief proposed to Lahoud that Lebanon enforce a “security formula” to avert demonstrations on the southern border that could provoke clashes ­ an international demand which Beirut sees as an attempt to turn the Lebanese Army into a police force for “Israel”.
Lahoud argued that “Israel”, with all its military might, has been unable to control the anger of the Arabs, and the government would not push the army into a situation that could ultimately develop into war.
The president also asked why Lebanon and the Arabs were being implored to exercise restraint, when “Israel” is allowed to maintain its aggression and defiance of international resolutions without being censured.
Turning to Rolf Knutsson, Annan’s personal representative to south Lebanon who attended the meeting, Lahoud said the diplomat admitted earlier in the week that the “Israelis” had opened fire without any warning at unarmed demonstrators on Saturday, killing two of them.
The sources, insisting on strict anonymity, said Lahoud stressed that recent events proved that separate deals, such as the Palestinian-”Israeli” Oslo Accords, would not end the conflict and that “Israel”, if it truly wanted peace, should relinquish all territories it occupied in 1967, including the Golan Heights.
On the issue of the “Israeli” soldiers captured by Hizbullah, Lahoud asked why “Israel” would not take the initiative and release the 19 Lebanese detainees.
Annan, according to the sources, replied that he had approached the “Israelis” but found them uncooperative on the issue.
The secretary-general demanded that either UN officials or International Red Cross delegates be allowed access to the prisoners before he could begin a mediation for a prisoner swap. But Hizbullah has already said that information on their wellbeing has a “price.”
Annan took his argument for a sign of life from the soldiers to the Iranian Embassy, where he met Iranian Foreign Minister Kamal Kharrazi.
Later at Hizbullah headquarters, Kharrazi said he relayed to the party’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, a message from Annan, but refused to disclose its contents.
However, a diplomatic source told The Daily Star that Kharrazi informed Nasrallah of Annan’s condition for mediating a prisoner swap. The UN chief asked that the party’s response be relayed to his representative, Knutsson, according to the source.
Earlier at a news conference in Baabda, Annan said that the mandate of the International Committee of the Red Cross provided unconditional access to prisoners of war ­ an argument unlikely to fly with Lebanon, whose detainees in “Israel” are denied such visits.
As for Lebanon’s sovereignty claim over Shibaa, which Syrian President Bashar Assad publicly supported in a statement on Wednesday, he said: “I think we have to base ourselves on the documents and the reports to the Security Council and the exchange of letters that I had with the two governments, Lebanon and “Israel”” before “Israel’s” withdrawal.
The farms, initially Syrian, were occupied piecemeal after the 1967 war. But Damascus and Beirut maintain that in the 1950s, the stretch of fertile land on the western slopes of Wadi Assel was handed over to Lebanon in a bilateral treaty.
Annan said proof of the exclusion of Shibaa from Resolution 425 lay in the fact that it has always been policed by the UN Disengagement and Observation Force, which acts as a buffer between “Israel” and Syria, and not by UNIFIL which was dispatched to south Lebanon under Resolution 425.
“This does not exclude whatever arrangements Lebanon and Syria will make in the future as to the status of that particular territory,” Annan said.
The UN chief flew to Naqoura for lunch at UNIFIL’s command. But halfway into the meal, he received a telephone contact from aides in “Israel”, reporting on the escalating violence.

Iran building Islamic front against “Israel”
Kharrazi denies Tehran has any influence in case of captured soldiers…(4)

Iranian Foreign Minister Kamal Kharrazi announced on Thursday that Iran was trying to form an Islamic front against “Israel” in its capacity as current leader of the Organization of Islamic Countries.
“President Mohammed Khatami has issued special instructions in his capacity as OIC president, to study the possibility of an urgent meeting on the level of foreign ministers,” Kharrazi said after meeting President Emile Lahoud in Baabda Palace. The minister delivered a message from Khatami to Lahoud that included Iran’s expression of support to Lebanon and its people.
The Iranian minister said he had no doubt that such a meeting would produce results, “just as the right and powerful decision taken to hold an Arab summit gave a positive push.”
Kharrazi said it was essential that efforts be mobilized, whether on bilateral or international levels, to pressure “Israel” to stop its threats and provocation against the Palestinians.
“We’re facing urgent and extremely dangerous times and we’ll invest in whatever possible means to solve the problems of the Palestinian people and those of other countries in the region,” he said, adding that Islamic countries supported the new Palestinian uprising.
“The region’s people are beginning to realize that, through their resistance and steadfastness, they can play a role and this is a huge investment for the people,” he said.
Kharrazi also accused the West of duplicity, saying no one reacted over the past two weeks when dozens of unarmed Palestinians were being
killed while “the whole western community reacts when three “Israeli” soldiers are captured.”
“This is a sign of duplicity in standards which we see on the western scene,” Kharrazi declared.
He also criticized the position of UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan who considered Hizbullah’s capture of the “Israeli” soldiers a violation of UN Resolution 425.
The Iranian minister denied his country has a say in the issue of the three “Israeli” soldiers captured by Hizbullah on Saturday.
“We have nothing to do with the issue, it is up to Hizbullah to decide what conditions to agree upon” to release the soldiers.
However, Kharrazi said the solution to the “complicated issue of the three soldiers is in “Israeli” hands.”
Kharrazi, who also met Prime Minister Salim Hoss, held a 40-minute meeting with Annan at the Iranian Embassy in Bir Hassan.
Speaking briefly to reporters, Kharrazi said the meeting was held to find solutions to the current tension in the region.
“The secretary-general is trying to conduct shuttle diplomacy and we hope he’ll be successful,” Kharrazi said.
Annan, meanwhile, said he discussed the regional developments and admitted that the issue of the Iranian diplomats who went missing in the country in 1982 was discussed.
The missing are: Mohsen Musawi, Iran’s charge d’affaires in Beirut at the time; diplomat Ahmad Motevasselian; embassy driver Taghi
Rastegar-Moghaddam; and Kazem Akhava, a photographer for the Islamic Republic News Agency.
Former Iranian Foreign Minister Ali Akbar Velayati asserted in 1997 that Iran had “numerous reasons to believe that the four were alive and in “Israel”.”
The Iranian minister’s last stop was at Hizbullah’s Shoura Council in Haret Hreik, where he met party leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah.
After the meeting, Kharrazi said he delivered a message from Annan to Nasrallah. However, the minister denied that the message from Annan was sent by “Israeli” officials, insisting it was from Annan himself. Sources said the message related to the three “Israeli” soldiers held by Hizbullah.
However, the minister assured journalists that Nasrallah was likely to deliver a response to this message “through his own special channels.”
Kharrazi described the “Israeli” threats as the reason why violence in the region continued, saying he believed the international community should pressure “Israel” to stop its attacks.
Kharrazi, who arrived in Beirut on Wednesday evening following a visit to Syria, left for Tehran early Thursday evening.
He had held talks on Wednesday with leaders of Palestinian groups on how to ensure the continuation of anti-”Israeli” protests in the Palestinian territories.

Another boy martyr inflames Palestinian passions…(9)

Sami Abu Jazar was fatally injured with his school bag still strapped to his back. A photographer captured the moment just after an “Israeli” bullet entered the 12-year-old's skull, leaving a gaping wound in his forehead.

The picture appeared on the front pages of many of the world's newspapers on Wednesday. Later, the pictures were of Sami on his hospital bed, the Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat bending to kiss his tear-stained face.

Yesterday Sami was buried in Gaza in an outpouring of Palestinian grief and anger. After two days on life support, but brain dead, his swollen eyes closed and his bandaged head tilted to one side, Sami's heart stopped beating early in the morning, the machine was switched off – and he became the latest boy martyr of the Palestinian uprising.

Sami was the 24th child victim of the past two weeks of violence. Images of the funeral were shown repeatedly on television across the Occupied Territories throughout the day. Emotions can have been only heightened by the pictures, just as the images of the death of another 12-year-old, Mohammed al-Durah, turned him into an emblem of the conflict.

Mohammed's final moments were captured by television cameras, first as he cowered behind his father, anguish and terror showing on his face, and then slumped on the ground.

Sami was shot on Tuesday. His family say his backpack proved that he was an innocent child returning from school, not part of a Palestinian protesters provoking “Israeli” soldiers into retaliation. The family say he was the little boy from a farming family, his dream to make a living from growing flowers and selling them.

Lies, hatred and the language of force…(12)

By Robert Fisk, Middle East Correspondent

13 October 2000

This is a story about lies, bias, hatred and death. It's about our inability – after more than half a century – to understand the injustice of the Middle East. It's about a part of the world where it seems quite natural, after repeatedly watching on television the funeral of 11-year-old Sami Abu Jezar – who died two days after being shot through the forehead by “Israeli” soldiers – for a crowd to kick two “Israeli” plainclothes agents to death. It's about a nation that claims "purity of arms" but fires missiles at civilian apartment blocks and then claims it is "restoring order". It's about people who are so enraged by the killing of almost a hundred Palestinians that they try to blow up an entire American warship.

It's as simple as that. When I walked into the local photocopy shop yesterday afternoon, the boys there greeted me with ecstatic smiles. "Did you hear that an American ship has been attacked?" one of them asked. "There are Americans dead." All I saw around the room were smiles. In a corner, on a small television screen, an “Israeli” Apache aircraft was firing a missile at Yasser Arafat's headquarters in Gaza.

Seven years ago, CNN showed us the “Israeli” prime minister shaking Yasser Arafat by the hand, live on the White House lawn. Now, live from Gaza, we watch a pilot carrying out an order from the “Israeli” prime minister to kill Arafat by bombing his headquarters.

As usual last night, the television news broadcasts – those most obsequious and deforming of information dispensers – were diverting our minds from the truth. They did not ask why the Palestinians should have lynched two “Israeli” undercover men. Instead, they asked why Palestinian police had not protected them. They did not ask why a suicide bomber in a rubber boat should have bombed the USS Cole.

Instead, they asked who he was, who he worked for, and they interviewed Pentagon officials who denounced "terrorism". Always the "who" or the "what"; never the "why".

It is of course possible that Osama bin Laden, one of the more recent American hate figures, could have inspired – by sermons rather than direct instruction – the attack on the USS Cole. Bin Laden's family originally came from Yemen. And it was Yemen that demanded the right earlier this week to fly arms direct to the Palestinians of the occupied territories – provoked, it seems, by slow-motion footage of yet another boy, a 12-year-old, dying on top of his father in Gaza after being shot by the “Israelis”. Yet many of the attacks on “Israeli” occupation forces in Lebanon were carried out by young men, unconnected with the corrupt Arab political élite but enraged by the injustice of their lot. Maybe it was the same in Yemen.

When Yasser Arafat signed the Oslo agreement seven years ago, only a very few asked how soon this raddled, flawed, hopeless "peace" would collapse. I thought it would end in violence because the Palestinians were being forced by Americans and “Israelis” to sign a peace that would give them neither a state nor an end to Jewish settlements on Arab land, nor a capital in Arab east Al-Quds.

I wrote that Arafat had been turned from "super-terrorist" into a "super-statesman" but could easily be turned into a "super-terrorist" again. And so it came to pass. Yesterday, the “Israeli” spokesman Avi Pasner shared a BBC interview with me – and called Arafat a "terrorist".

Alas, none of it was surprising – none save our continued inability to grasp what happens when a whole society is pressure-cooked to the point of explosion. A Pentagon official was saying last night the US government was trying to find out if the attack on the USS Cole was "related" to "violence" in the Middle East. Come again? Related? Violence? Who can doubt that the attempt to sink the Cole and all her 360 American crew was directed at a nation now held responsible for “Israel’s” killing of scores of Palestinian civilians? The United States – despite all the claptrap from Madeleine Albright about "honest brokers" – is “Israel’s” ally.

Ever since Arafat tried to leave the US ambassador's residence in Paris two weeks ago, the Palestinians have placed this responsibility on America's shoulders. If the US wants to go on supporting an ally that shoots down Palestinians in the streets of the occupied territories, then the United States will be held to account. And will pay for it.

No, of course this does not excuse the bloodthirsty killing of armed “Israeli” agents or the desecration of the Tomb of Joseph in Nablus, or, indeed, the murder of Jewish settlers. But the cruelty of the Palestinians can be explained by the cruelty of the “Israelis”. The death toll among Palestinians now is almost exactly equal to that at Qana in 1996 when “Israeli” gunners butchered 106 Lebanese civilians. We called it a massacre. The “Israelis” said it was a mistake. True, it's scarcely 5 per cent of the death toll at the Sabra and Chatila refugee camps, when “Israel’s” militia allies killed up to 2,000 Palestinian civilians. We called that a massacre. “Israel” said this, too, was a mistake. Like they called the death of two 12-year-old children and a seven-year-old child and Sami Abu Jezar a mistake.

And yesterday – with no institutional memory to guide them – journalists were taking at face value “Israel’s” extraordinary claim that they fired "only at military targets", that the civilian population of Gaza had been "told to evacuate" the areas to be bombed. Do I not seem to remember how the “Israelis” said in 1982 that in Lebanon they "only fired at military targets" – and left more than 17,000 civilians dead in two months? Do I not recall that the “Israelis” ordered the villagers of Mansouri to "evacuate" before they shelled it in 1996, then attacked their cars on the road and fired a missile into the back of an ambulance, killing four children and three women – the missile made, of course, by the Boeing company of America?

And was not the CIA supposed to be training the Palestinian policemen now being derided by Mr Pasner as "terrorists" (his own country having personally vetted which of them should carry arms)? And was not the United States the guarantor and broker of the disastrous Oslo agreement? So is it really surprising that the Palestinians – indeed, the Arabs – blame the United States for the tragedy unfolding in the Holy Land?

And is it any less surprising that the “Israelis” have now turned on the man with whom they thought they would conclude a peace that would turn "Palestine" into a Bantustan? The man who was supposed to "control" the Palestinians, who was supposed to lock up opponents of the "peace process" – whether they be peaceful or violent – is not doing what he was told. He walked out of Camp David because it was a surrender too far. So President Clinton blamed him for the conference's failure – on “Israeli” television, of all places – and ordered Arafat not to declare a state. Or else.

And now, when two US presidential contenders – Messrs Bush and Gore – try to out-do each other in their love and loyalty for “Israel”, can America comprehend what is happening?

I suppose it's the same old story. The “Israelis” only want peace. The unruly, riotous, murderous Palestinians – totally to blame for 95 of their own deaths – understand only violence.

That's what “Israel’s” military spokesman said last night. Force, he said, "will be the only language they understand". Which is about as near to a declaration of war as you can get.

Middle East on the edge of war

 

* “Israeli” soldiers lynched * US sailors die in suicide attack * Gunships strike Arafat HQ * Barak forms government of national unity…(13)

The Middle East was plunged into a deepening crisis yesterday as some of the most shocking violence for 30 years reverberated across the region, reducing the Oslo peace process to ashes and threatening to explode into war.

Ehud Barak, “Israel’s” Prime Minister, was last night moving to form a national emergency government after a day in which “Israeli” helicopters fired rockets into two Palestinian cities – one landing within yards of Yasser Arafat's compound in Gaza while the Palestinian leader was inside – after two “Israeli” soldiers were lynched by protesters in Ramallah.

Mr Barak said he hoped to form a government within days, and said he wanted it to include Ariel Sharon, head of the right-wing Likud party, whose visit to Haram al-Sharif ignited the firestorm that has swept the West Bank, Gaza and “Israel” in the past two weeks.

Such a government ends any chance of new negotiations on peace, as the Arab world is united in its hatred of Mr Sharon, though Egypt offered to hold a four-way summit with America in Cairo if “Israel” halted its attacks.

Fighting continued last night with “Israeli” helicopters attacking the main compound of the Palestinian security forces in Nablus; officers fled into the street before the shooting began. A firefight erupted in the West Bank town of Hebron and helicopters attacked gunmen in the town of Salfit, near Nablus and fire rockets into the Palestinian police academy in Jericho.

Mr Barak's move to form a new government followed a day in which the Middle East was plunged into its worst crisis since the 1991 Gulf War, and which included an attack on an American target, when anexplosives-laden boat detonated alongside a US destroyer in the Yemeni port of Aden, killing five sailors and injuring 36.

By mid-afternoon yesterday, as Mr Barak avenged the soldiers' death in an effort to assuage public fury, helicopters were rocketing targets in Ramallah and Gaza City, while tanks were deployed on the edge of Palestinian zones.

It was the most perilous day to grip the region since the start of the Palestinian uprising two weeks ago and began when the Palestinians killed the “Israeli” soldiers after smashing their way into a police station where they were being held.

Why the soldiers were in Ramallah was not clear. “Israel” said they were reservists who took a wrong turn; the Palestinians said they were spies.

The incident, captured by television cameras, provoked a swift, devastating response. There were waves of helicopter gunship attacks in Gaza and Ramallah but Mr Arafat was unharmed, aides said.

Palestinian spokesmen accused “Israel” of unleashing an all-out war against the Palestinian people and appealed for international intervention. But an “Israeli” army statement described the attacks as merely a "token signal" in response to the soldiers' murder. “Israel” said it was a limited action, and that it had warned the Palestinians three hours in advance.

Diplomats could scarcely believe that a day that had begun with a flurry of diplomatic activity by the UN secretary general, Kofi Annan, had so swiftly descended into chaos. But the deep anger felt by both “Israeli” and Palestinian politicians was unmistakable. "This is a declaration of war - a crazy war ... There is no peace process today," said Saeb Erekat, a Palestinian official.

“Israel’s” Communications Minister, Binyamin Ben Eliezer, said: "The peace process is dead. He added: "Arafat's clear desire is for war, that is what he wants."

In the United States, there was horror at the attack on the Cole in Aden. Officials said last night that in addition to the five fatalities, 36 men had been injured and 12 more were missing. Witnesses said an inflatable craft, which had apparently been helping the Cole to berth in the port for a refuelling, drew up alongside the ship and exploded at 12.15pm. Two men were seen getting to their feet and standing to attention at the moment of detonation. The explosion left a gaping hole, 40ft by 20ft, at the ship's waterline. Efforts were under way to stop it sinking.

Few doubted the attack had been a suicide mission and that it was linked to the violence between “Israel” and the Palestinians. President Bill Clinton ordered US ships in the region to pull out of port. The US also sent medical help to Yemen as well as anti-terrorist agents.
