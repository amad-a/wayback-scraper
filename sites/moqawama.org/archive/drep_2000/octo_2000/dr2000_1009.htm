Introduction

Saturday, the 7th of October, 2000 will be remembered as a good day for the Islamic Resistance (IR) and a bad day for the “Israeli” enemy army. In a wounderful heroic achievement, the IR men managed to capture three “Israeli” soldiers in the Lebanese occupied Shibaa Farms.

Hizbullah’s Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah asserted that the “Israeli” soldiers would be released only in exchange for Arab prisoners in “Israel”.

His Deputy H.E. Sheikh  Nahim Qassem underlined that “we are in full readiness to exercise our right and duty to defend our people. If any harm or damage is done it means that “Israeli” settlements and soldiers will not be safe”.

IR captures three “Israeli” enemy soldiers

The Islamic Resistance (IR) issued a statement in which it reported the following:

Carrying out Hizbullah’s pledge over liberating every detainee and hostage and every inch of our occupied soil and supporting the militant and great Palestinian people in its blessed Intifada, the IR’s heroic mujahedeen staged swift and qualitative attacks against a number of “Israeli” occupation troops’ positions at the Lebanese occupied Shibaa Farms area. The IR managed to capture a number of “Israeli” soldiers who were completely evacuated from the scene to a safe place.

The IR renews its pledge for the honorable hostages and promises them with the freedom. Also, it gives this blessed and qualitative military operation as a present to the spirits of the oppressed boy Mohammad Al-Durra and all of the martyrs of Al-Aqsa Intifada, promising that it will always stand by this noble people and that it will not forget Al-Aqsa Mosque whatever the sacrifices are…(1)

For its part, AFP confirmed that Hizbullah captured three “Israeli” soldiers in an ambush in the occupied Shibaa Farms.

"Our fighters ambushed an “Israeli” patrol at the Shibaa Farms, within the Lebanese occupied territories of Shibaa at 1:40 p.m. (1040 GMT)," said a statement by the Islamic Resistance, Hizbullah's military arm.

"The fighters fired machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades at the patrol and engaged in a clash during which our fighters succeeded in capturing three enemy soldiers and liquidating the rest," it said.

An earlier AFP statement said that Hizbullah fired 50 salvoes of Katyushas, mortars and rocket-propelled grenades from southeast Lebanon Saturday at “Israeli” posts in the Shibaa Farms, a mountainous area occupied by “Israel” on the border with Syria and Lebanon, security sources said.

The shelling targetted “Israeli” posts in the farms of Rowaissat al-Aalam, Rowaissat as-Sammaqa, Ramta, Jall al-Ahmar and the army observation post towering over the mountainous region, they said.

The area hit the headlines earlier this year, when Lebanon demanded its return as part of the “Israeli” withdrawal from its territory in May, but “Israel” says it captured the farms from Syria in 1967 and their future is to be determined in talks with Damascus.

In a statement issued earlier also, AFP reported that three “Israeli” soldiers were wounded in a hail of mortar fire and rockets launched by Hizbullah movement on the “Israeli”-Lebanon border, “Israeli” army radio said.

They are the first soldiers wounded by Hizbullah since the army's May withdrawal from Lebanon after 22 years of occupation, the radio said.

It said it would give other details "when it is able to do so," referring to military censorship on details on the incident.

An army spokesman said a press conference would be held in the next few hours but gave no further information, saying only that "shooting and fighting is continuing at the borde…(2)."

Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, has said only that the soldiers would be released in exchange for Arab prisoners in “Israel”.
Among those expected to be at the heart of any swap are 19 Lebanese held in “Israeli” jails. “We decided to capture a group of “Israeli” soldiers to exchange them with detainees in “Israeli” prisons,” Sheikh Nahim Qassem said. “This is a very suitable opportunity because of the intifada (in the Palestinian territories) and to confirm the importance of the Shibaa Farms as occupied Lebanese territory.”
The three soldiers were captured beside a former border crossing south of the village of Shibaa. The soldiers had been traveling along the border patrol road in a truck.
When they reached the gate in the security fence, they came under fire from guerrillas armed with rockets and machine guns, according to a statement released by Hizbullah.
The ambush was accompanied by a heavy bombardment of neighboring “Israeli” Army outposts located throughout the Shibaa Farms. With “Israeli” reinforcements pinned down, the Hizbullah squad cut through the border fence and grabbed the three soldiers, one of whom was reportedly wounded. The captured soldiers were bundled into a waiting civilian car on the Lebanese side of the border.
They were identified as first sergeants Omar Suwad, 25, and Benyamin Avraham, 20, and Sergeant Adi Avitan, 20.
The “Israeli” Army responded to the bombardment by firing artillery shells into the mountains between Shibaa and the neighboring village of Kfar Shuba. Helicopter gunships hung overhead all afternoon.
The “Israeli” outposts struck by Hizbullah’s rockets were visible from Kfar Shuba. Thick coils of smoke rose from two of them and a small brush fire, sparked by an exploding artillery shell, blazed on a hill above the village…(4)

Speaking in Zefta, Nabatieh, Qassem said: “The heroes of the Palestinian uprising will prove that the settlement can’t be founded on solid ground ... It won’t stand, even if it carries the signatures of (Palestinian) officials. The international community should understand that the peace process is over”…(5)

Moral balance in Hizbullah’s favor…(7)

Capturing “Israeli” soldiers to win the release of Lebanese detainees was perhaps the only viable military operation Hizbullah was able to undertake following “Israel’s” withdrawal from south Lebanon five months ago.
The moral balance, which has often dictated strategic options available to both Hizbullah and “Israel” in their tit-for-tat battles, benefited the resistance on this occasion.
“Israel” admits holding 19 Lebanese without trial as bargaining chips for information on the fate of the three “Israeli” soldiers and an airman who went missing in Lebanon during the 1980s.
The two most prominent detainees are Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, a senior Hizbullah official, and Mustafa Dirani, the head of the Believers Resistance.
Ironically, Hizbullah’s capture of the three “Israeli” soldiers echoed “Israel’s” kidnappings of Obeid in 1987 and Dirani in 1994. Both were snatched by helicopter-borne commandos from their homes and whisked over the border into “Israel”.
The continued detention of the 19 Lebanese flouts international law and has become a cause celebre for human-rights groups around the world.
Although Hizbullah’s kidnapping of the three soldiers has provoked condemnation internationally, the disapproval was not as strident as it could have been. From reactions of some diplomats in Beirut, there is a feeling that the “Israelis” have been given a taste of their own medicine.
More pertinently, “Israel’s” illegal detention of the detainees undermines the option of a heavy-handed military response ­ aside from the risk that such a move could have on the safety of the captive soldiers.
It was this moral principle that allowed Hizbullah to wage its “legitimate resistance” against the “Israeli” forces occupying the South with the tolerance, however reluctant, of the world.

How “Israel’s” trap was sprung using live bait…(8)

Robert Fisk in Houla, Southern Lebanon

Everyone knew that the Hizbollah wanted “Israeli” soldiers as hostages. Now they have three. For weeks, the guerrilla organisation had been claiming that the "liberation" of southern Lebanon would never be complete until Lebanese hostages were released from “Israeli” jails. Sayed Hassan Nasrallah, the Hizbollah chairman – as its former south Lebanon military commander, he is as tactically shrewd as he is politically astute – was demanding freedom only last week for Sheikh Abdul-Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani, linking their return to a few square miles of Lebanese land still occupied by “Israel” in the foothills of the Golan Heights.

And it must have taken weeks to plan. To storm a heavily fortified “Israeli” military position takes days of reconnaisance, weapons training, artillery co-ordination, careful time-targeting and a lot of secrecy. To cross a border wire – albeit into another part of Lebanon – would have to be practised repeatedly. And the word had been around since May: the Hizbollah wanted “Israeli” hostages to exchange for “Israel’s” Lebanese hostages. But they also needed a raison d'etre, something that would force the “Israelis” to acknowledge that the capture – under the rules of engagement which the Hizbollah and “Israelis” have long accepted on the border – was retaliation rather than outright aggression.

These rules meant that “Israel” and Hizbollah must not endanger civilians but could expect retaliation if civilians were killed. Yet no one had died on the Lebanese-”Israeli” border since “Israel’s” withdrawal in May of this year. Not, that is, until 15 bus-loads of Palestinian refugees arrived at the little crossroads above the frontier gate at Marouahine on Saturday morning.

Individual Palestinians have been visiting the scene for weeks, to stare at the land they still call "Palestine" and – in some cases – at the very villages from which their families were driven by the Jewish fighters in the 1948 war which created “Israel”. But on Saturday it was different. For hundreds of Palestinians to leave a Beirut refugee camp en masse needed the tacit approval of Syria and to have staged a border demonstration in which refugees would try to scale the frontier wire needed organisation. Not long before the buses arrived, a few Lebanese policemen were seen touring the area. The “Israelis” would have watched the Palestinians for at least 10 minutes as they marched from the buses down the narrow road with its concrete blocks towards the border.

The first mortars exploded around the “Israeli” soldiers within half an hour of the shooting at Marouahine – the Hizbollah routinely watch the Maraouhine border gate and must have had their men there, ready to radio back reports on the shooting – and within another quarter of an hour, Hizbollah guerrillas were running across the shale and rocks of the hillside to the “Israeli” bunker. According to their own organisation, the three “Israelis” – in a state of shock – surrendered at once.

Ironically – and no doubt deliberately – the attack combined the capture of “Israeli” troops with Nasrallah's other obsession: the Shibaa "farms", the hilly tracts of land that belong to Lebanon but which the United Nations decided to ignore in its "blue line" withdrawal limit for the “Israeli” army last May. The area is owned by Lebanon but was taken over by Syrian police in 1947. When “Israeli” troops captured the Golan Heights from Syria in 1967, they therefore assumed the land was Syrian, not Lebanese. So it was included in “Israel’s” subsequent illegal annexation of the Golan as “Israeli” territory. Lebanon is still demanding its return. But for the Hizbollah, its continued occupation was a godsend.

Yesterday in southern Lebanon was a day like any other. The bougainvillaea bloomed along the border roads, a giant lizard scuttled into the shade and the Nepalese UN soldiers of two armoured vehicles lounged in the sun, squinting through binoculars at a few Palestinians who threw stones over the border fence more out of weariness than desperation. Old men sat in the shade of village squares. Here at Houla, you could look out across the heat haze at the “Israeli” frontier and the red-roofed settlements beyond without hearing a single shell. And why not? The Hizbollah had got what they wanted.

 

Big 3: ‘Don’t tread on us’
President speaker, and premier warn “Israelis” to stay home…(9)

The top three leaders in the country are warning “Israel” against escalating its attacks on Lebanon, amid US efforts to calm the situation on the border.
“Israel” has threatened to attack Lebanon after Hizbullah managed to capture three “Israeli” soldiers in the Shibaa Farms, an area claimed by Lebanon and still under “Israeli” occupation. The operation came shortly after two Palestinian demonstrators were killed by “Israeli” gunfire on the border.
““Israel” is responsible for the escalation of tension on the Lebanese border. It also bears the full responsibility for any reaction that it may carry out against Lebanon,” President Emile Lahoud said on Sunday.
“Lebanon rejects “Israel’s” threats and sees them as a useless means to sort out the new situation, which would not have occurred if “Israel” responded to UN efforts and demands that Lebanese detainees be released,” a statement released by the Presidential Palace quoted him as telling his visitors. “The threats are a clear attempt by “Israel” to overcome the embarrassment it’s facing politically and militarily in the occupied territories.
“Lebanon refuses to become an outlet for “Israel’s” internal dilemmas or a means resorted to by “Israeli” officials to enhance their situation and face their difficulties,” he added. “Thanks to its internal unity and its people’s support of their strong state, Lebanon will be able to face these challenges.”
Lahoud also received Egyptian Ambassador Adel Khodary, who relayed an invitation from President Hosni Mubarak to attend an Arab summit on the Palestinian cause. The summit is scheduled for Oct. 21 and 22 in Cairo.
On Saturday, Lahoud also criticized “Israel” after its soldiers fatally wounded two Palestinians and wounded about 20 others during a demonstration at Kfar Shouba earlier in the day.
““Israel’s” threats will lead nowhere as long as the basic problem is not solved,” he added in a statement, referring to “Israel’s” steadfast denial of Palestinian refugees’ right to return.
US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright telephoned Lahoud as well as UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa, French Foreign Minister Hubert Vedrine and Spanish Foreign Minister Josep Pique in an effort to help curb the rising tension.
Prime Minister Salim Hoss said on Saturday that the clashes “are deplorable and incriminate “Israel” for shooting at unarmed people, keeping Lebanese detainees and maintaining its occupation of the Shibaa Farms.
“As for the Al-Quds cause, everyone knows that it will remain a cause of instability in the entire region until the right thing is done and the Arab character of the city is restored,” he added in a statement.
Hoss, who doubles as the foreign minister, also telephoned Annan, Sharaa, Egyptian Foreign Minister Amr Musa, Satterfield and French Ambassador Philippe Lecourtier on Saturday.
“Prime Minister Hoss explained developments in the South to the officials after “Israel” shot civilians, violated Lebanon’s territory and launched threats against Lebanon,” a statement from the premier’s office said.
“He also asked the officials to work on curbing “Israel” from maintaining its aggression against Lebanon,” it added.
For his part, Speaker Nabih Berri said ““Israel” alone is responsible for the safety of its soldiers, not Lebanon or Syria.”
He was responding to comments by “Israeli” officials holding Beirut and Damascus responsible for the safety of three “Israeli” soldiers captured by Hizbullah in the Shibaa Farms on Saturday.
“The soldiers in question were on land that is not theirs and among people who are being oppressed, killed, displaced and threatened by “Israel”,” he said on Sunday. ““Israel’s” withdrawal from the South does not mean peace. As long as “Israel” maintains its occupation of parts of the Shibaa Farms and keeps Lebanese detainees in its prisons, the Lebanese will have the right to resist the occupation and seek the liberation of the detainees.”
He brushed aside “Israel’s” threats against Lebanon and Syria. ““Israel” is militarily and politically too weak to carry out an adventure, which will definitely be unsuccessful,” he said. ““Israel” knows that a war in Lebanon is no longer a picnic.”

Indian peacekeepers of the UN Interim Forces in southern Lebanon (UNIFIL) helped residents of this mountainous region evacuate their relatives, who had been stranded for several hours on the main road leading to the village after an “Israeli” helicopter raid late Saturday, he said.

Shibaa residents can only access their village from the western side on foot, as they considering the southern road running along the Shibaa Farms too dangerous.

Hizbullah's private war…(13)

In the aftermath of the IDF's* withdrawal from Lebanon, Hizbullah has now set three objectives for its renewed struggle against “Israel”: the return of Havat Shiva (Shibaa Farms) to Lebanon, the release of all Lebanese prisoners imprisoned in “Israel”, and the furnishing of assistance to the Palestinians (not the PA) in their campaign to liberate their lands.
Hizbullah had to put its demand concerning the 10 square kilometer Havat Shiva area on hold when Syria accepted the UN's judgment that “Israel” had withdrawn fully from southern Lebanon. By accepting this UN ruling, Syria agreed that Resolution 425 (concerning withdrawal from Lebanon) does not apply to Havat Shiva; disputes about the area, the Syrians held, would be resolved in negotiations with them held under the terms of Resolution 242 (concerning withdrawal from occupied territories). Hizbullah, which had vowed not to act in contravention of decisions reached by the Lebanese government, was forced to hold its Havat Shiva objectives in abeyance when the Lebanese government assented to Syria's dictates on the matter. Hizbullah ceased its military activity in the area.
Instead of Havat Shiva, the release of Lebanese prisoners held in “Israeli” facilities turned into the main focus of Hizbullah's military planning. Hizbullah's normally strict compartmentalization of information (a secret-keeping tactic which had in the past stopped “Israel” from learning about the organization's military plans in advance) lapsed in this instance. “Israel” received intelligence information about Hizbullah's plans to kidnap IDF soldiers in order to use them as bargaining leverage in a bid to effect the release of the Lebanese prisoners.
Planning for the kidnapping appears to have deviated from Hizbullah's habit of deploying small, compartmentalized cells of operatives. Hizbullah appears to have informed a relatively large military contingent about the plan - and these forces, in turn, carried out the camouflage activities around the IDF's Zarit outpost, along with the military attack against the Har Dov outposts. Using these operations as a smokescreen, the Hizbullah group was able to carry off the kidnapping. Due to the large scale of this kidnap action, and the ploys which accompanied it, the IDF received information about Hizbullah's designs, and disseminated warnings to the public about the possibility of kidnap attempts against soldiers.
What price will Hizbullah try to exact from its success? It could choose to be satisfied with a demand for a swap - the kidnapped soldiers in exchange for Lebanese prisoners held in “Israel”. Or, it could try to pull Lebanon and “Israel” into another military confrontation, a conflict which would resurrect Hizbullah's role as a fighting force. Or, Hizbullah might decide to demand that Palestinian prisoners be released by “Israel” along with Lebanese ones, thereby damaging Arafat's stature (Hizbullah has long loathed the PA Chairman).
Under any of these scenarios, negotiations will be protracted, and Hizbullah is likely to wage a private diplomatic struggle, without the help of Syria, or the Lebanese government. In view of public debates concerning the future of Syria's presence in Lebanon, Syrian President Bashar Assad has been very cautious when it comes to intervening in Lebanese matters. And Lebanon is ruled by a transitional government which could choose to regard the kidnapping affair as a private matter to be resolved between Hizbullah and “Israel”.
Released from pressure applied by Damascus or Beirut, Hizbullah could notch a number of successes - or it could raise the stakes in talks and insert new demands, knowing that Germany, France, Iran, Saudi Arabia and the United States are likely to get involved in the talks.
Well-versed in negotiation dynamics with “Israel”, Hizbullah's Sheikh Nasrallah refrained yesterday from setting a price to be paid in return for the three soldiers. He didn't even say whether he demands the release of Lebanese prisoners only, or whether he's including the release of Palestinians. Maneuvering to ensure that Hizbullah, and not Syria or the Lebanese government, conduct negotiations with “Israel”, Nasrallah is waiting for various international officials to come knocking at his door. Nasrallah has managed to open up a private diplomatic front, one which now buffers his organization against any IDF reprisal actions, against “Israeli” military strikes that could eradicate the success marked by the Party of God this weekend.

International Reaction... (18)

“Israel” was facing growing diplomatic isolation last night as massive demonstrations continued in the Arab world, and its main ally, the US, allowed passage of a United Nations resolution condemning it for using excessive force against the Palestinians in the clashes in the West Bank and Gaza.

After Jordan, Egypt and Sudan, Morocco yesterday became the latest Arab country to witness massive anti-”Israeli” protests when an estimated half million people marched through the capital Rabat, chanting slogans against “Israel” and the US, as well as calling for a jihad, or holy war, to liberate Al-Quds.

The demonstration was all the more striking since Morocco has traditionally been in the moderate Arab camp, and has maintained steady, if discreet, ties with “Israel”.

Jordan, which has had full diplomatic relations with “Israel” since 1994, underlined its displeasure by postponing the departure of its new ambassador to Tel Aviv. But King Abdullah's government has rejected demands by parliament to expel “Israel’s” envoy to Jordan and close down the “Israeli” embassy in Amman.
