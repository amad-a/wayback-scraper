Introduction

An intense western diplomatic effort got under way in Beirut, particularly, at Hizbullah’s Secratariat HQ, to secure the release of three “Israeli” soldiers captured by the Islamic Resistance (IR)

Hizbullah’s Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah met with the UN Secretary General Kofi Annan’s representative in Lebanon Rolf Knutsson and Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Alexanderf Sultanov in order to negotiate a prisoner-swap deal between “Israel” and Hizbullah.

H.E. Nasrallah also held a meeting with a delegation of Palestinian Organization for Human Rights who passed on a letter from Palestinian prisoners asking Hizbullah to act in their favor.

Furthermore, Nasrallah received a letter from the committee of spouses and mothers of Palestinian prisoners in which they expressed “the hope that Hizbullah will succed in concluding a deal in which all the Arab prisoners will be released from “Israeli” prisons”.

 

Negotiations for prisoner exchange quiet but intense…(1)

Nasrallah: Resistance is concerned about the release of all Arab detainees

“Two senior foreign diplomats involved in negotiating a prisoner-swap deal between “Israel” and Hizbullah said Monday that they had “no comment” and “no information” on the three “Israeli” soldiers captured by Hizbullah on Saturday.
Rolf Knutssen, UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan’s representative in Lebanon, and Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Alexander Sultanov told reporters that they would not disclose details of their separate meetings with Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah.
The meetings were held as international efforts to organize a prisoner exchange intensified.
Knutssen, who met with Nasrallah for about 30 minutes at the party’s headquarters in Haret Hreik, seemed optimistic about what he described as “an interesting meeting.”
Knutssen admitted that the “Israeli” soldiers were discussed during the meeting but refused to give details of a possible meeting with them. “I hope they’re in good health,” Knutssen said of the three.
When asked about the possibility of the current negotiations bringing the crisis swiftly to an end, Knutssen simply nodded.
Bearing assurances that he carried no messages to Nasrallah from the “Israelis”, Knutssen said his meeting with the Hizbullah leader had left him with “some very useful thoughts that I’d like to discuss with various parties.” He also said the UN would continue following up on the issue of the three soldiers.
Knutssen was to meet Annan in “Israel” late Monday.
Sultanov, who is accompanying Russian Foreign Minister Igor Ivanov in the region, arrived in Beirut from Syria on Monday. He also refused to comment on his 40-minute meeting with Nasrallah.
Nasrallah also received a delegation from the Palestinian Organization for Human Rights, who handed him letters from Palestinian detainees and their families, in addition to letters from the Palestinian media urging Hizbullah to include Palestinian detainees in any prisoner-exchange negotiations with “Israel”. Abdel-Salam Aql, the head of the organization, said a list with the names of some 130 Palestinians was also handed to Nasrallah.
The list included those captured over the last 20 years, those who were condemned to long prison sentences by “Israeli” courts as well as those who were not included in previous settlements between the Palestinian Authority and “Israel”. These include Palestinian Ammar Zein, who received 26 life sentences.
The list also included five women and 13 Syrian members of the Secret Resistance Organization. According to Aql, Nasrallah received the request favorably.
“He assured us that the Resistance is concerned about the release of all Arab detainees in “Israeli” prisons,” Aql said.
Aql said a separate list containing the names of another 1,400 Arab detainees would also be delivered to Nasrallah.
In the West Bank, the Palestinian Prisoners Club said it had submitted to Hizbullah a list of 1,600 imprisoned Palestinians who it would like to see released.
“This is a unique occasion,” the club said in a statement from Bethlehem, “to put an end to the prisoners’ suffering after “Israel” imposed humiliating conditions on their release.”

Nasrallah received a letter from the committee of spouses and mothers of Palestinian prisoners in which they expressed “the hope that Hizbullah will succeed in concluding a deal in which all the (Arab) prisoners will be released from “Israeli” prisons”.

“The heroic operation in the course of which you captured the three “Israeli” soldiers have raised our hopes to see the return of our sons and our husbands who are suffering the same ordeal as your mujahedeen in “Israel’s” prisons,” said the letter.

Hizbullah deputy secretary-general Sheikh Naem Qassem warned the “Israeli” occupation from executing its threats against Lebanon. He affirmed that Hizbullah will not stay immobile if the “Israelis” attacked ,and that the occupation will bear the consequences of its acts.

He explained that Hizbullah prefers to discuss such cases through the known channels.

Sheikh Qassem reminded that five months have passed since the withdrawal of the occupation forces from Lebanon and until now no Lebanese detainee has been released.

"The international envoys use to come without giving us a glimpse of hope concerning their release. All what they used to say is that they're trying and that they have no answers, while "“Israel”" was applying legal measures to increase the pressure on the detainees..

 

Probe into IDF* kidnappings critical of army's conduct…(3)

” The investigation at the IDF Northern Command surrounding the kidnapping of three IDF soldiers by Hizbullah guerrillas on Saturday in the Har Dov area on the border with Lebanon will continue today in an effort to answer a series of questions which raise doubts about the judgment and behavior of officers and troops during the incident.
At this stage of the investigation, the impact of the conclusions is expected to take a severe toll on the field commanders in the area.
According to the findings, the Hizbullah guerrillas used two road-side bombs against the vehicle carrying the three soldiers. The bombs were set to explode at a sufficient distance from the vehicle in order not to cause the soldiers excessive harm. Initially it was thought that none of the soldiers was injured, but closer investigation revealed blood stains and it is believed that one of them is injured.
The guerrillas then crossed through a gate near the fence, surrounded the vehicle, and pulled the three soldiers out, leaving two of the three weapons behind.
Anti-armor missiles were fired at the vehicle after the soldiers were taken out.
The soldiers and the guerrillas boarded a vehicle which was waiting for them on the Lebanese side of the border and since then the IDF has not been able to track their movements.
Some of the issues currently under investigation at the Northern Command:
The soldiers: It remains unclear whether the soldiers fired their weapons at their attackers. What is certain is that they did not report the attack on the radio. It is also unclear whether they were in motion or had stopped for a break and were not vigilant. Their position was further away from where they should have been and in an area identified by the IDF as "dangerous."
The reaction: IDF spotters at nearby outposts failed to notice the attack. As a result, precious time was lost (as much as 30 minutes in one version), before the kidnapping was reported and the search commenced. Based on previous experience, the IDF believes that the initial minutes of a kidnapping are crucial.
The regional command: The brigade was situated several dozen meters from Hizbullah outposts across the fence. Were the soldiers aware of the danger to which they were exposed from armed guerrillas? Why were there no orders that soldiers move along the border in two vehicles, not one? Was the vehicle used appropriate for such a sensitive area? The type of low, open vehicle used by the soldiers is only in operation in the relatively calm areas along the Egyptian and Jordanian borders.
Early Warnings: The IDF had warnings of plans to kidnap soldiers in the Har Dov area. The head of the Northern Command, Major General Gabi Ashkenazi, spoke with soldiers from the unit of the kidnapped men only two weeks ago, and warned them of the danger of kidnappings. Nonetheless, it appears that the warnings were not adopted in procedures used during patrols.
Commanders: Senior commanders are expected to take the fall, if we are to judge from the behavior of Chief of Staff Lieutenant General Shaul Mofaz, during the Duvdevan fiasco in August, when three IDF soldiers were mortally wounded by friendly fire during an operation near Nablus. As a result of the failure, senior commanders resigned from their posts and Mofaz accepted their resignations.
IDF sources said yesterday that Mofaz was furious at the "horrible failure" which led to the kidnapping.

 

Ferzli: air strikes won’t sap Hizbullah’s support…(4)

Deputy Speaker Elie Ferzli affirmed on Monday that any “Israeli” attacks on Lebanon would not succeed in stripping the resistance movement of the popular support it enjoys.
““Israel” may believe that by attacking civilian infrastructure in Lebanon it can turn the country’s public against the resistance movement or at least split the public over the legitimacy of resistance attacks against “Israel”,” he told the Voice of Lebanon radio. “This is impossible. I also think “Israel” knows that such a scheme will be fruitless and will only intensify the Middle East crisis”. Tension mounted across the Lebanese-”Israeli” border on Saturday after Hizbullah captured three “Israeli” soldiers in the disputed Shebaa Farms.
The operation was launched in retaliation for the killing of two Palestinian demonstrators and the wounding of 20 others by “Israeli” gunfire at Kfar Shouba in the eastern sector of the border area earlier on Saturday.
Tyre MP Ali Khalil, the head of Parliament’s Foreign Affairs Committee, hailed Hizbullah for snatching the three “Israeli” soldiers. “This operation will force “Israel” to consider releasing the Lebanese detainees it holds,” he said. Lebanon says “Israel” is detaining 19 Lebanese, without trial in many cases, while “Israel”, which says it is keeping the detainees as bargaining chips to learn the fate of “Israeli” troops who went missing in Lebanon, puts the number at 15.

Finally: hopeful family awaits Dirani’s release…(5)

It has been a long time since the Dirani family laughed out loud. But they now believe their world is about to come together again.
“It’s a done deal,” Hassan Dirani said with a wide grin.
He has no doubt that the three kidnapped “Israeli” soldiers will be exchanged for his cousin, Mustafa Dirani, one of 19 detainees held by “Israel”.
“Israeli” commandos kidnapped Dirani, the head of the Believer’s Resistance six-and-a-half years ago from his home in the Bekaa village of Qsar Naba.
The family has not heard from him since. They only saw him once, on television in July. He was in an “Israeli” court sitting next to Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, a senior Hizbullah official who was kidnapped in 1989. Dirani, 49, looked thinner, his hair whiter. He touched his forehead with his finger and said: “We are always right and thank God.”
His family, who had not expected to see him, immediately developed the video image into photographs. Posters of the photographs, with the words: “We are always right and thank God” hang around the house. Over 200 posters were printed and banners are already being prepared to welcome the detainee home.
“There will be an exchange of prisoners, for certain,” said Dirani’s wife, Zeinab, 45. “Before we had no hope. But now we have a weapon in our hands. My husband will go free.”
Hizbullah, she said, has done what it promised.
“For six years, we protested, we pleaded and nobody would listen,” she said. “We held sit-ins and still nobody seemed to care or do anything. But now, there’s finally some attention. Hizbullah did promise us to free him.”
Six years ago, Hajj Abu Ali, as Dirani is known, was at home celebrating Eid al-Adha. Well-wishers finally left at midnight and the family retired to their beds. Only 2-year-old Sara was allowed to spend the night in her parents’ room while the rest of the five children went to their own beds.
At 2.30am, the couple were suddenly awoken by bright spotlights shining directly on them. Looking around, they found the room full of “Israeli” soldiers who then grabbed Zeinab, handcuffed her hands and legs and took her to another room.
“Where is Ron Arad?” they asked her, threatening to kill her 6-year-old daughter Tiba if she screamed.
Arad’s plane crashed in 1986 and the “Israeli” pilot was captured by the Believers’ Resistance.
Meanwhile, Dirani was pushed to the floor. He tried to get up twice before his captors dragged him away, warning that explosives had been planted around the house.
“I kept expecting the house to blow up,” said Zeinab.
Dirani was whisked away to “Israel” and never heard from.
“And then last March we heard that he had an “Israeli” lawyer who issued a report that the hajj was severely tortured,” said Zeinab. “But nobody could see him. The “Israelis” wouldn’t even let him have our letters. We didn’t know if he was all right.”
The Red Cross was also denied visitation rights.
Dirani was accused of being a danger to the “Israeli” people. The Jewish state said it would hold Dirani in order to exchange him for Arad.
“But when I heard of the kidnap of the three “Israeli” soldiers,” said Zeinab, “I just knew it was done to bring back the detainees.”
As for the “Israeli” soldiers’ families, “let them taste what we tasted,” she said. “My husband has been detained and tortured for six-and-a-half years, and nobody was allowed to see him. And now after 24 hours, the Red Cross is already negotiating for a prisoner exchange.”
Still, she added, “I can’t compare myself to the “Israelis”. Our faith doesn’t allow us to torture and treat people like they do. We’re above them.”
In the meantime, Dirani’s children continue to remember their father’s advice: “Education is like a weapon in our hands,” recalled Ali, Dirani’s 17-year-old son.
“I’ve applied all of the lessons my father taught me. Especially to be patient.”

Lebanon complains to UN over “Israeli” "violations"…(8)

Beirut complained Monday to the United Nations at “Israeli” "violations" against Lebanon by the shelling of villages, injury to civilians and supersonic bangs in the country's air space.

Lebanese President Emile Lahoud relayed the complaints to Rolf Knutsson, UN Secretary General Kofi Annan's representative for southern Lebanon, during a meeting at the presidential palace, the national news agency (ANI) said.

"Lebanon protests “Israel’s” shelling of Lebanese villages at the Lebanese borders, the injury of civilians, the violations to Lebanon's airspace and the aggressions against Lebanese and Palestinian civilians by firing on them without warning," said ANI.

The agency quoted Lahoud as saying that "these aggressions constitute a flagrant violation of Lebanese sovereignty and a violation of UN Security Council Resolution 425."

The resolution, voted after “Israel’s” 1978 invasion of Lebanon, stipulated “Israel’s” withdrawal from southern Lebanon and the end of hostilities. “Israel” pulled out on May 24 after 22 years of occupation.

Lahoud was referring to violent actions in the last few days at the Lebanese-”Israeli” borders.

Two Palestinians were killed and 17 others wounded Saturday when “Israeli” soldiers opened fire at protestors throwing stones and Molotov coktails at the Lebanese borders.

The same day Hizbullah kidnapped three “Israeli” soldiers in a commando operation in the Shebaa Farms, a mountainous region at the Lebanese-Syrian borders occupied by “Israel” since 1967.

“Israel” retaliated with air and ground strikes on southern Lebanon, wounding a number of civilians, while its warplanes broke the sound barrier over Beirut and other regions.

 

Palestinians demonstrated at Lebanese-Palestinian borders…(9)

Palestinian refugees demonstrating at the border with “Israel” hurled stones at “Israeli” soldiers behind the border’s barbed wire fence on Monday, without inflicting casualties, an AFP correspondent said.
About 20 Palestinian refugees gathered in Ramieh, east of Naqoura and facing the Zarit settlement in northern “Israel”, threw stones at the “Israelis”.
“Israeli” soldiers on the other side ducked, but did not open fire on the protesters.
Two Palestinian refugees were killed and 17 other people were wounded on Saturday at the former Ramieh crossing when “Israeli” soldiers opened fire at protesters hurling stones and Molotov cocktails over the border.
Otherwise, the tense border area was quiet, but “Israeli” troops remained on high alert.
With binoculars, “Israeli” soldiers panned the hilly northern border that on Saturday saw its worst violence since “Israel” pulled out of Lebanon in May.
Although the “Israeli” Army denies it has “reinforced” its northern border, some military hardware has been brought in or repositioned around the disputed Shebaa Farms area, where Hizbullah guerrillas attacked and kidnapped three soldiers on Saturday.
“Israel” has quietly bolstered defenses around the mountainous Shebaa area, stealthily moving in commandos, extra tanks, radio equipment and other tracked hardware.
Outside the hamlet of Sinir on the slopes of the Golan Heights behind Shebaa , a battery of at least five 155mm artillery guns is now stationed, pointing over the hill toward the villages of Kfar Shuba and Shebaa.
The fresh track marks these heavy pieces of mobile artillery dug into the paved roads is testimony to their recent deployment from areas deeper in the Golan, the strategic plateau seized from Syria by “Israel” 33 years ago.
Military roads leading into the Shebaa Farms, which is normally a closed military area, have been blocked with extra soldiers.

Emotions must translate into action…(12)

by David Hirst

As The Daily Star reported on Thursday, Raef Hammoudi has been more than usually busy painting “Israeli” and American flags, so high is the demand from people demonstrating in support of the new Palestinian intifada. He does them on non-absorbent cloth just an hour or so before they are due for ritual burning because “he can’t stand having them in his workshop and they repel visitors.”
Lebanon, as the freest and politically most diverse of Arab countries, is the most eloquent sounding-board of Arab and Muslim emotions. True to form, it was the first to react to the Palestinian drama, with those twin villains, “Israel” and the United States, more automatically linked than ever as the target of the protestors’ wrath.
But, this time, it was far from alone. In fact the protests sweeping the Arab world these days are by far the most persistent and widespread for many years, even reminiscent, some say, of the 1950s and 1960s when Gamal Abdel-Nasser would rouse the masses from the Gulf to the Atlantic.
In Egypt, university students have staged daily protest marches and sit-ins for a week now; in mid-week, secondary school students joined the fray. Some demonstrations, like those in Baghdad, were state-sponsored. But most, like one of particular significance in tightly controlled Syria, were spontaneous; 4,000 people stoned the heavily fortified US Embassy, scaled its walls and tore down its flag, chanting “Al-Quds is ours,” “Down with America, Down with “Israel”.”
There have been demonstrations in Tunisia, Sudan, Libya and Morocco. On Saturday one demonstrator was killed in Jordan. But, most strikingly, the protest has also spread to the Gulf, where there is little tradition of street protest. In Oman, a huge throng of students at Sultan Qabous University shouted “with our souls and blood we shall redeem you, Al-Aqsa.” They demanded the closure of the “Israeli” trade mission in their country. In the United Arab Emirates, schoolgirls in embroidered Palestinian dresses walked side by side with young men in local headgear and flowing white dishdashas.
The protests reached even into Saudi Arabia, heartland of arch-conservative authoritarianism; thousands burned “Israeli” and American flags in the streets of Sakakah in the Kingdom’s remote north.
There is often a religious flavor to the outrage, be it Mohammed Tantawi, Sheikh of Cairo’s Al-Azhar University, decreeing ­ in the name of establishment Islam ­ that “force must now be our only weapon to confront “Israel”,” or the 55 personalities, from Malaysia to Morocco, who ­ in the name of militant, fundamentalist Islam ­ have issued a joint declaration proclaiming that “Israel’s” arrogance would never have reached the dimension it did were it not for “Arab submissiveness and willingness to give in, instead of pursuing the path of the intifada, resistance and struggle to liberate every handspan of Palestine.”
But conventional patriotism, elementary, instinctive fellow-feeling with Palestinian brethren, is the foundation of it all.
It is renewed, dramatic testimony to the abiding centrality of Palestine in the Arab psyche. Yet, though this has been the most impressive such outpouring of solidarity for years, the fact is that the same thing has been seen, in one form or another, time and time again. And, time and again, emotion has never translated into serious action.
Palestine may be a great rallying cry, but, to many Arabs, it is also a badge of shame, a prime symptom of weakness and disarray, of the rottenness and corruption of an Arab world once again reminded, by the spectacle of Palestinian youths dying in unequal combat, that, for all its wealth, numbers and geographic immensity, it is quite unable to do anything to check the excesses of the historic foe.
The anathemas heaped on “Israel”/America may be genuinely felt, but, beneath them, lies another, more significant emotion: self-disgust, a profound exasperation with this Arab impotence, and above all with regimes which are the chief expression of it.
That emotion has long been an intrinsic part of the contemporary Arab condition: but it always breaks surface on occasions like this, not so much in the demonstrations, as in the lamentations of the Arab media.
The fate of Mohammed al-Durra, the 12-year-old boy seen shot to death on the world’s television screens, had a very special resonance. Like other obituarists, Sawsan al-Shaer wrote in the Kuwaiti newspaper Al-Watan that not just “Israeli” soldiers, but the Arabs themselves, had the child’s blood on their hands.
“Do you remember,” she asked, “television footage of “Israeli” soldiers breaking the bones of Palestinian youth during the intifada a few years back? Didn’t we fume and revolt and threaten? What happened afterward? Nothing. Didn’t the sight of Mohammed appal every one of us? What will happen now? Nothing. Look what happened to oil prices. They (the West) made their views known openly, taking no heed of our reaction because they knew there would be none ...
“Mohammed was killed before our eyes, his blood shed in full view of the world. Where they are concerned there is no difference between the price of his blood and that of a barrel of oil ­ both are dirt cheap. But they’re not to blame. We are ­ because we sell ourselves cheap.”
If, as now seems likely, the Arab leaders do finally manage to convene a summit conference to deal with the crisis, will it yield any results? Arab commentators are almost unanimously saying that this time it had better ­ or else.
The growing anger on the Arab street, the passionate rhetoric of the intelligentsia, seems to have begun to seriously alarm Arab leaders. President Hosni Mubarak, who, to general derision, first proposed an “emergency” summit to be held no sooner than January, has now brought it forward to this month.
Popular pressure did it. He himself has come under rare, direct criticism in Egypt’s so-called “opposition” newspapers. “The Egyptian people are angry, Mr. President,” wrote the editor of Al-Wafd. “We demand the breaking of relations with “Israel”. It is the minimum that the people will accept.”
Even the “official,” state-controlled press is sounding militant. Ahmad Ragab, perhaps Egypt’s most popular columnist, said in Al-Akhbar that the Arabs should and could use “the oil weapon” to free Al-Quds, especially with an energy crisis looming in the West.
Instead, he added sarcastically, they have “placed their oil under the protection of William Cohen, the US secretary for the defense of “Israel”.”
Saudi Arabia has agreed to attend the summit, thereby defying the US pressures which are a key reason for its traditional reluctance to do so. But, more dramatically, Iraq is to go too. Following the competitive stampede of Arab civilian aircraft converging on Baghdad, that represents another breakthrough in Saddam Hussein’s quest for global rehabilitation.
He is in an exultant, belligerent mood. In language not far removed from his notorious “I-will-burn-half-of-”Israel”” speech on the eve of his invasion of Kuwait, he says: “You see how many Arab kings and presidents we have, yet 5 million worthless people (the “Israelis”) oppress our people in Palestine and slaughter our children. Let them give Iraq a small adjacent piece of land and they’ll see how quickly we finish off Zionism.”
There was a time when Palestine, as the Arab cause par excellence, had the power to topple regimes and foment revolution. Some now openly hope it could do so again. In the London-based Al-Quds al-Arabi newspaper, leading platform of Arab opposition movements, Abed al-Bari al-Atwan wrote: “Mrs. Albright is doing her best to restore calm for the sake of “Israeli” and US interests. She knows that the countdown to the end of US hegemony over the region has started and that the spread of disturbances to Arab streets could shake Arab regimes that take orders from Washington and lead to a worldwide energy crisis ...
“We pray to God to prolong the intifada, turn it into the trigger that will stir the Arab street to give vent to its accumulated frustrations ­ for there is more than one Milosevic in the Arab world.”

David Hirst, a veteran observer of Middle Eastern affairs, wrote this commentary for The Daily Star
