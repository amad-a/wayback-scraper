Hizbullah SG: conflict with occupation not over yet; Intifada is result of our victory in Lebanon…(1)

Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah underlined that the conflict with the “Israeli” occupation is not over yet.

His Eminence told the Kuwaiti daily ar-Rai el-Am that "“Israel”" would try to take revenge on the Lebanese who have defeated it.

"“Israel” will seek to hold Lebanon and the Lebanese responsible for anything that might happen," he said, adding, "It is impossible to reassure “Israel”, even if we send the army to the South and disarm the Resistance, that won't be enough."

The Lebanese must turn into spies inside Palestine to protect "“Israel”" and its interests in the world from any military action in order to avoid such an act and Lebanon's punishment for that, the secretary-general said.

Regarding supporting the Palestinian people and standing by its side, his Eminence said, "We feel ethical, human, religious, national, and patriotic responsibilities towards the Palestinian people at all criteria."

As for how the support will take place, it is best for the ways to back the intifada not to talk about them, he said, noting that the "intifada was a result of the Lebanese victory and it nourishes on its spirit."

Asked about the situation on Lebanon's southern border, Sayyed Nasrallah said, "No one can say for certain where things in the south will head in the next stage, especially if events inside occupied Palestine develop and the “Israeli” enemy goes too far in violating Lebanese sovereignty and conducting provocative measures."

 

Day of Rage brings new uprising to Heram Al-Sharif

Masked Arabs briefly seize symbolic Lion Gate…(2)

“The Palestinians had promised a Day of Rage, and that is what it was. Fury, stoked by the loss of scores of lives, yesterday extinguished all efforts to try to re-establish peace in “Israel” and its occupied territories.

By mid-afternoon, one of the entrances to Al-Quds's Old City – the 16th-century Lion Gate built by the Ottoman emperor Suleiman the Magnificent – was in Arab hands again.

Thirty-three years ago “Israeli” paratroopers marched through the same gate on their way to conquer the Haram al-Sharif paving the way for the annexation of east Al-Quds days later.

Yesterday it was conquered anew, albeit symbolically and briefly, by a handful of masked Palestinians who set fire to the police post within, causing great gusts of black smoke to billow from the arrow slits.

All afternoon, the youths strutted, shouted, dodged rubber bullets and stun grenades, and threw large rocks at the “Israeli” police in the nearby cemetery outside the city walls. Bleeding and bruised, at least 20 were carted away by ambulance, would-be martyrs in an up-rising that now has a momentum of its own and which, elsewhere in Gaza and the West Bank, claimed four more Palestinian lives yesterday. Those martyrs78 mean more funerals, more crowds, and another turn of the wheel of Intifada.

The confrontations began after Friday prayers on the Haram al-Sharif, the 35-acre plaza crowned by a golden dome in the heart of Al-Quds, which was the single biggest obstacle to what was always, in the long run, a doomed peace deal. Both sides were expecting the worst and appear to have made sincere efforts to avert it.

Unprecedentedly, “Israeli” police were withdrawn from all the entrances. The crowds were less than half the usual size because the “Israelis” closed the occupied territories for four days.

It was a wildly reckless visit to this site – home to Islam's al-Aqsa mosque and Dome of the Rock sacred also to Jews – by Ariel Sharon, chairman of ““Israel’s” Likud party, that ignited the current uprising.

At first the crowd seem subdued. As the Palestinian worshippers left there was a half-hearted flurry of stone-throwing at the Jews worshipping below the mount at the Western Wall but it died after 15 minutes. Then, moments later, the day of rage – ordered by the militant Hamas movement which opposes the Oslo accord – began in earnest. It was sparked when the crowds spotted a solitary “Israeli” policeman.

The sound of battle echoed around the narrow streets. I was standing on a roof with some black-hatted ultra-Orthodox Jewish religious students in the Old City's Jewish quarter. Their reaction was another insight into the affliction of this region. "Who is shooting whom?" said Joseph Ross, 19, from Stamford Hill, London. “Israelis” are shooting Palestinians, we replied. "Good," he said firmly. "They shouldn't have been throwing stones."

On the streets below, a youth of about the same age said the stones were only the beginning. "We haven't achieved much," said Murad, as he stood by the smouldering Lion's Gate on the eastern edge of the Old City, "People will only calm down when we get our land back."

“Israel” has been stung by the attention the world has given to their excessive use of force in this conflict – captured in the slaughter by an “Israeli” bullet of a 12-year-old boy, Mohammed al-Durah, huddling at his father's side in Gaza.

In the past week, “Israeli” snipers, highly trained and with accurate equipment, have shot dead children. “Israeli” helicopter gun ships have fired rockets into apartment buildings. True, their well-fortified bunkers have come under attack by Palestinians firing thousands of rounds Kalishnikovs and hurling Molotov cocktails. But the ratio of the dead reflects the imbalance – 70 Palestinians to three “Israelis”.

Absurdly, much of this has been done in the name of defending tiny pockets of territory and Jewish settlements on occupied land that Ehud Barak knows should not be there, and would have handed back in a peace agreement.

The Palestinians are targeting these places, pounding them daily with rocks and Molotov cocktails. They have concluded that their chances of success are higher by using the confrontation, than by waiting for the peacemakers.

One of the worst trouble spots is a scrap of land in Nablus, where 10 “Israelis” soldiers – mostly Druze Arabs – have been under siege for a week defending a Jewish holy site of doubtful theological history which is coveted only by a handful ultra-Orthodox fanatics.

 

Local clerics lead calls to liberate ‘all of Palestine’…(3)

Demonstrations of support for Palestinians battling “Israeli” forces in the West Bank and Gaza gained momentum across Lebanon on Friday after Muslim clerics called for active support to liberate Palestine.
“I renew my call on the Arab nations to form the jihad (holy war) brigades to liberate Al-Quds and all of Palestine,” Grand Mufti Sheikh Mohammed Rashid Qabbani told an estimated 1,500 worshippers who gathered at his seat in Dar al-Fatwa.
“We must carry on and liberate Palestine with our Arab and Muslim brethren, the same way we liberated Lebanon with the heroic resistance and Jihad,” he added, calling on Arab Christians to join the process. “Our enemy must not rest. There will be no solution in Palestine unless the Western Jews leave. The battle has only started today between us and the Jews occupying Palestine.”
Other Muslim clerics joined Qabbani ’s call and expressed anger and resentment over the reaction of Arab countries to the nine days of bloody clashes.
“The official Arab stand remains hesitant and confused and has not reached the anticipated level,” said the vice-president of the Higher Shiite Council, Sheikh Abdel-Amir Qabalan. “Arabs need to issue a brave and official stand to face ““Israel’s” terrorism and savagery by extending effective field support for demonstrators in Palestine and through a unified Arab policy to pressure the international community to stand by Arabs.”
Qabalan said Arabs should have held an urgent summit and announced their support for the Palestinians and not just settle for issuing condemnation statements. His call for the summit, reiterated by other clerics, came hours before Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak set the date for an Arab Summit to discuss the “Israeli”-Palestinian clashes.
Shiite cleric Sayyed Mohammed Hussein Fadlallah called for a unified Arab stand and for “all organizations and voices” in the Arab world to be one.
“We must plan, for the sake of our holy war, confrontations up to a level where the Palestinian people will be able to continue their resistance,” he said.
He added that Palestinians did not require more stands but rather all kinds of direct and indirect support.
“What is required is a detailed plan that will provide all kinds of support and confrontation through a political, security, and holy struggle mechanism,” Fadlallah said.
Thousands of worshippers across the country took to the streets after Friday prayers to protest the clashes.
Over 25,000 people marched at the Ain al-Hilweh refugee camp in Sidon with a noticeable participation of radical Palestinian and Islamic groups. Around 200 armed members of the Palestinian Islamic Jihad and al-Jamaa al-Islamiya marched in a parade holding their weapons.
“Be prepared to fight the Jews who raped Palestine and expelled its people,” Sheikh Abu Dia told worshippers at the camp’s Abu al-Khatab mosque.
Clad in black and military attire, the men wore makeshift explosives through which they expressed their readiness to die in the struggle. “These people are ready to blow themselves up the moment they are asked to head to Palestine,” said Abu Obeida, a commander of al-Jamaa al-Islamiya.
Meanwhile, Palestinians in the Rashidieh camp in Tyre staged a protest and marched from the camp to the UNIFIL offices, where they raised Palestinian flags and pictures of 12-year-old Mohammed Al-Durra, who was martyred by “Israeli” soldiers last Saturday.
Thousands of Palestinian refugees at the Beddawi and Nahr al-Bared camps in Tripoli also massed in groups following Friday prayers. They went out into the camps’ streets which were filled with smoke from burning tires. A boy dressed in military fatigues and clutching a stone in his hand stood on a jeep leading one of the marches in the Beddawi camp.
Officials and representatives of Palestinian factions and Islamic groups and clerics led the marches.
Other protests organized by Muslim groups were held in Tripoli and merged into a huge demonstration in Mina.
Another protest was held in front of UN House in Beirut, where hundreds of children and representatives of political parties’ students’ unions, non-governmental organizations and Palestinian factions converged. They called on the UN Security Council and the International Court of Justice in the Hague to indict “Israeli” Premier Ehud Barak, hard-line “Israeli” opposition leader Ariel Sharon, and ““Israel’s”, chief of staff, Lieutenant General Shaul Mofaz “for their crimes against Palestinians.”
“We call for suing the assassins Barak, Sharon and Mofaz as war criminals,” a memorandum addressed to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan read.
The memorandum was signed by the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine and the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine, which both oppose peace with “Israel”.
It reiterated its rejection to “any settlement which overlooks the rights of the Palestinian people, including the right of refugees to return and the return of Al-Quds to Palestinian sovereignty.”
“We also stress our rejection to negotiations under way and which only give additional time to the occupation to impose the fait accompli,” the memorandum added.
Islamic groups and Palestinian factions also staged a rally in Baalbek where several clerics addressed the people and called for a united Arab stand to condemn “Israel”. Three separate protests were also staged in Baalbek following Friday prayers.

 

Orthodox Church rips into “Israelis”…(4)

Greek Orthodox clerics on Friday deplored the killing of Palestinian children and what they described as injustice and ugly practices adopted by “Israeli” troops facing “unarmed people defending their land, rights and holy shrines.”
In a communique following five days of deliberations at Balamand Monastery, the clerics also affirmed that Al-Quds did not consist of a mere collection of streets, residential areas and places of worship.
“It’s a holy city and a symbol and history for Christians and Muslims, not only Jews,” the statement said.
“Accordingly, there is no way of giving it up, and it cannot be monopolized through force or political deals.”
The clerics were referring to nine days of violent clashes between Palestinians and “Israelis” in the occupied territories in which around 80 have been martyred and 1,900 wounded, almost all of them Arabs.

Marches backing Palestinians' Jihad spread in Lebanon and world…(5)

Actions in solidarity with the intifada of Palestine and its people continued taking various forms in all Lebanese areas, including Beirut, Baalbek, Sidon, and Tripoli, as thousands of people took to the streets following the Friday prayer calling for Jihad and continuation of the intifada until the liberation of Palestine and al-Quds is achieved.

In Europe, thousands of people from Palestinian, Arab and Moslem communities, along with Swedish supporters, marched through the streets of Stockholm, carrying Palestinian flags besides banners supporting the Palestinian people and condemning the occupation and its crimes.

The German town of Essen also witnessed a massive youth demonstration that was organized in solidarity with the Palestinian people by the German-Lebanese Friendship Association and the Society of the Lebanese Immigrants' Union.

The Arab-European League on Friday organized a sit-in in front of the European Commission's HQ in Brussels to protest the massacres perpetrated against children in occupied Palestine.

In Denmark, thousands of Lebanese and Palestinians staged a massive demonstration in solidarity with the people of Palestine and their intifada against Zionism. They gathered in front of the Parliament's House in Copenhagen chanting anti-”Israeli” slogans.

Furthermore, thousands of Arabs and Moslems demonstrated in front of the British premier's office in London in support of the Palestinian people.

In northern Saudi Arabia around two thousand people gathered Wednesday evening to protest the “Israeli” aggression on the Palestinian people, according to Agence France Presse.

In Tunisia, a popular rally was organized in solidarity with the Palestinian people. The action was attended by representatives of the country's parties that condemned the Zionist aggressions.

Thousands of Sudanese Moslems, including representatives of parties and unions, demonstrated in Khartoum urging Islamic governments and peoples to back the Palestinians stressing that "words are not enough."

Moreover, tens of thousands of Iranians took to the streets in the capital Tehran in support of the Palestinian intifada. Speeches were delivered by representatives of Hamas and other groups stressing support to the Palestinian legitimate struggle.

 

US closes Embassies in 12 Arab countries for fear of demonstrators…(6)

The US state department said it is closing all American embassies and consulates in 12 Arab countries for the next five days for fear of violent demonstrations that may be organized to protest the US bias in favor of the Zionist entity and the massacres it has been perpetrating against the Palestinian people.

The department also ordered its employees in the Arab World to keep their children home from school and said that posts in other regions "may suspend or limit services to the public as necessary.''

"In light of the events in “Israel”, the West Bank and Gaza over the past week and elsewhere in the Middle East region, the department of state is concerned about the potential for further clashes and demonstrations,'' a state department statement said, according to the US daily the Washington Post.

The countries that are to be included in the temporary shut-down are Algiers, Morocco, Tunisia, Bahrain, Egypt, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Oman, Syria, United Arab Emirates, and Yemen.

Most of these countries witnessed demonstrations and actions in solidarity with the Palestinian intifada.

 

Tensions rise in Jordan as protesters clash with police near “Israeli” embassy

In Amman, riot police fired tear gas to push back thousands of Jordanian protesters marching on the “Israeli” embassy. They called on Amman to sever ties with the Jewish state. Scores of riot police blocked roads leading to the “Israeli” embassy forcing some 5,000 demonstrators to retreat. A few hurled stones at police. The incident was the second in as many days in which police clashed with protesters marching in solidarity with Palestinians.

“ Tension grips Palestinian refugee camps in Jordan following a day of protests and clashes with police over “Israeli” attacks on Palestinians in the occupied territories. Jordanian riot police fired tear gas and deployed armored vehicles to control hundreds of protesters at the Wihdat camp. They fought running battles with police for hours in the alleys of the crowded shanty town. Thirty civilians were injured. The clashes began when police blocked the paths of thousands of Palestinians trying to march to a main street that leads out of the camp in defiance of the organizers who had urged the demonstrators to stay inside. Clashes continued into the late hours of the night. Around one hundred youths were arrested. Jordan and “Israel” signed a peace agreement in 1994. Demonstrations have been spreading around the world, including cities Europe and North America. In Jakarta, hundreds of Indonesian students protested outside the United Nations office calling on the world body to be dissolved for failing to condemn “Israel” for "slaughtering" Palestinians. They described “Israeli” attacks on Palestinians as "terrorism". Indonesian President Aburrahman Wahid criticized the UN for not condemning “Israeli” attacks on Palestinian civilians. A group of Canadian and Arab students are planning a major protest outside the “Israeli” embassy in Ottawa. They want the Canadian government to condemn “Israeli” attacks on Palestinians. Similar protests are planned in most major US cities. In Strasbourg, the seat of the European Union parliament, deputies voted through a resolution "firmly deploring the use of disproportionate force by the “Israeli” army" and calling for "an immediate stop to it". The resolution also called for the setting up of a commission of inquiry "to clarify events and determine responsibilities". “Israel” also faced criticism from international organizations. The International Committee for the Red Cross reported a string of attacks on ambulances trying to help casualties. It cited cases in which “Israel” prevented the Palestinian Red Crescent Society from traveling to reach the injured, in violation of the Geneva Conventions.

“Israel” targets journalists in Mideast clashes: report…(8)

At least five international journalists covering the violent clashes between “Israelis” and Palestinians have been wounded, the Committee to Protect Journalists (CPJ) said.   The nongovernmental organization said of the five journalists confirmed to have been injured, three of them were struck by live ammunition or rubber-coated metal bullets fired by “Israeli” troops.   An AFP photographer was among three other members of the press reportedly hit by bullets fired by “Israeli” forces, in attacks that CPJ is still investigating. A French television correspondent was beaten by “Israeli” police after refusing to hand over a rubber bullet he picked up off the ground, CPJ reported. Two cameramen were still in hospital Thursday, receiving treatment for wounds inflicted by rubber bullets.   "It is the professional duty of journalists to report on these events, and the use of violence to impede their work constitutes a grave violation of press freedom," said CPJ Middle East program coordinator Joel Campagna. "We call on “Israeli” authorities to investigate these attacks to determine if they were in fact carried out with the intention of inhibiting the work of the press."

 

It’s a dangerous job but somebody’s got to do it
Nicholas Blanford reports on the Ukranian UNIFIL battalion’s battle to destroy an explosive legacy of occupation…(9)

A short blast on a whistle and a hollow thump of an explosion briefly shatter the placid calm of an afternoon along the “Israeli” border in south Lebanon. A black cloud of smoke, dirt and dust is thrown into the air from the blast, only to be immediately whipped away on the hot breeze.
Ukraine’s defense minister, Lieutenant General Oleksandr Chapovskyi, in Lebanon to inspect his troops, grunts his approval and marches back to his vehicle.
The Ukrainian UNIFIL battalion has just successfully destroyed another handful of “Israeli” land mines. The devices ­ “Israeli”-made No. 4 anti-personnel mines ­ are part of an almost continuous string of minefields stretching along the border from Ras Naqoura on the coast to the foothills of Mount Hermon.
The Ukrainian soldiers are uncertain how many mines were destroyed in the blast. Perhaps four or five. A tiny fraction when one considers that there are an estimated 130,000 land mines in south Lebanon.
At this isolated minefield beside Boundary Pillar 13 (BP13), southwest of Aitta Shaab, it has taken a week to destroy 250 land mines.
Colonel Oleg Khavroniuk, the Ukrainian battalion’s commanding officer, has said that under the present operational circumstances, it will take 37 years to remove the weapons from along the border alone.
The Ukrainians have been tasked with clearing a path through the minefield to the border opposite an “Israeli” outpost at BP13. The mines could have been planted more than 20 years ago. Winter rains and wind have exposed several of the deadly devices. Four of them lie in a row, 1 meter apart, their raised olive-green lids waiting patiently for an unsuspecting foot to set off the charge.
“This is a very dangerous place,” mutters Lieutenant Colonel Victor Baladynsky.
The 650-strong Ukrainian battalion is used to difficult mine-clearing operations. The battalion has gained the bulk of its experience removing huge quantities of mines left over from World War II in Ukraine. This enabled them to learn the unpredictable nature of land mines that have remained buried for decades. More recently, the battalion served in Afghanistan during the Soviet occupation in the 1980s.
A recently installed anti-personnel mine may require 8 to 10 kilograms of pressure to detonate. But a mine that has been buried for years is less predictable.
“With an old mine, it could take as little as 1 kilogram to explode. On the other hand, the fuse could have rusted and you could stamp on the mine without it detonating. It’s impossible to know for each one,” explains Khavroniuk. The Ukrainian colonel has personally destroyed more than 4,500 mines in his career, the highest number in the Ukrainian Army.
The bulk of the anti-personnel land mines the battalion unearths are “Israeli” No. 4 mines and French-designed 1951 mines which the “Israelis” manufacture.
“The 1951 contains about 90 grams of explosive. It’s very small but very dangerous. It can easily blow your foot off,” Khavroniuk says.
The 1951 mine is made almost entirely of plastic. Only the detonating pin is metallic. But because the pin is about the size of a pencil tip, the mine is almost invisible to a metal detector.
“Metal detectors aren’t much use in south Lebanon because of the amount of shrapnel in the soil,” Khavroniuk says. Even the high iron content of the sedimentary rock in south Lebanon can mislead metal detectors.
The battalion has encountered other mines, including a huge Claymore-style mine which is positioned above ground and detonated by a tripwire. When the mine explodes, it blasts hundreds of ball bearings in an arc toward whatever tripped the wire. Although that mine was “Israeli”, Hizbullah guerrillas used to manufacture their own version of the Claymore mine for roadside bombs, which were used with deadly effect against the occupation forces.
The mine was found at BP18, on the border south of Yaroun. The battalion cleared a path through the minefield to link a newly constructed Irish observation post with the rest of the UNIFIL area of operations.
“The soldiers were working in 50C heat. They could only remain on the field for 20 minutes at a time,” Khavroniuk says.
Each minefield is scrutinized before de-mining starts.
“We think of each minefield as a game of chess,” Khavroniuk says. “We give a lot of time to reconnaissance. We study not only the terrain but the description of the minefield. We analyze our previous experience and if necessary ask for helicopter recon. After all that, we begin the breach of the minefield.”
The human element of mine clearing takes a back seat initially. The Ukrainians have 20 different vehicles for de-mining. They include BAT 2, a multi-purpose vehicle protected even against radioactivity, and specially converted T-54 tanks equipped with rollers and flails.
The initial breach is made with a rocket trailing explosive rope fired over the minefield. The rope explodes, clearing a narrow path through the field which the sappers and other machines then expand.
“It’s equipment, equipment, equipment, until we’re sure that the worst danger is over, then we send in the soldiers,” Baladynsky says.
More than 100 mines were destroyed in the operation at BP18, the first for the battalion in Lebanon.
Land mines are also liable to move around in the soil over lengthy periods of time, which renders minefield maps useless.
Most minefields have a narrow “security strip” between the barbed wire fence and the first row of mines. Depending on the terrain, the security strip could be as little as 3 meters or as deep as 60 meters.
Khavroniuk says that when his battalion cleared a field at BP32, beside the “Israeli” settlement of Manara, they were informed that there were no mines planted in the “security strip.”
“But we found mines in the strip, up to 45 meters from where the minefield began. There’s no way of knowing if they were put there deliberately or not,” he says.
Isn’t 45 meters rather a long way for mines to travel by themselves? Khavroniuk just smiles.
Although the main task of the Ukrainian battalion is to remove mines along the border, they also regularly stumble across unexploded ordinance and even booby-trap devices.
The battalion sited one of its four positions on a minefield beside the Hamra crossing into the former occupation zone on the coast south of Tyre. The battalion cleared the minefield and destroyed 14 unexploded shells. They also discovered a mine with a delayed timer which had been washed up on the shore beside the new camp.
“The timer no longer worked but the explosives were still there,” Khavroniuk says.
The mine was blown up in the sea. Khavroniuk ordered a full search of the shore beside the camp for further mines or unexploded ordinance before allowing his soldiers to swim in the sea.
Booby traps provide an additional danger to the Ukrainian sappers. While inspecting an area near a new Fijian UNIFIL post on a hill overlooking Naqoura, a team of sappers found two unexploded “Israeli” 120mm tank shells hidden in a bush.
“The first shell was removed and there was no problem. The sapper thought the second shell would also be easy,” Khavroniuk says.
But as the sapper bent down to pick it up, he noticed a wire sticking out of one end of the shell. He also saw that the shell was resting on a stone wedge.
“It was a booby-trapped shell. The wire led into an explosive charge hidden in the ground beneath the shell. If the sapper had picked up the shell, the explosives would have detonated,” Khavroniuk says.
The bomb was blown up in a controlled explosion.
“The sapper can take nothing for granted,” says Khavroniuk. “He can’t make one mistake and can’t trust anything.”
