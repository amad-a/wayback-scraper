Leader receives Hamas delegation: solution to Palestine issue by Jihad…(1)

Leader-Imam Grand Ayatollah Sayyed Ali Khamenei reiterated Iran's condemnation of the crimes perpetrated by the Zionist occupation forces against the Palestinian people.

As he hosted a Hamas delegation, his Eminence Sayyed Khamenei said the heroic confrontations in which the Palestinian people is engaged are considered a great resurrection.

The recent developments have proven that the faith and awareness of the Palestinian youth besides the continuation of the confrontation and the intifada, cannot be stopped by bullets or threats, Sayyed Khamenei stressed, adding that solution of the Palestinian issue can be achieved through Jihad against the Zionist enemy.

In turn, Hamas Movement called on Arafat to step aside and not to abort the intifada.

Following the meeting with Moslems' Leader, Hamas' spokesman Ibrahim Ghoshe said, "We are witnessing a new intifada, we asked for the Islamic Republic's backing so that victory is achieved as it was achieved in Lebanon."

Hizbullah: We are ready to stand by Palestinians people…(3)

Hizbullah offered its services to Palestinians on Monday, saying the party was “devoted and ready to stand by its side.
“We’re confident that our liberation of Lebanese territories was the first step and the best example to expel “Israelis” from all occupied territories to complete the plan of liberation,” a statement from the group said.
“These clashes mean that the conflict with this enemy will not stop ... and indicate that the only style that will restore the stolen dignity and rights is that of the resistance and jihad,” it added.
The party condemned “the international community’s suspicious silence” toward what was happening and US attempts “to treat the criminal and the victim the same way.”

“Israeli” troops kill four “Israeli” Arabs as Intifada rage throughout occupied territories…(4)

 “ “Israeli” troops rolled out tanks in a show of force and fired rockets Monday to battle Palestinians wielding rocks and automatic rifles, as Palestinians raged through the West Bank and Gaza Strip, leaving scarcely a Palestinian town or village untouched.

The spiraling uprising, now in its fifth day, has killed at least 47 people and injured more than 1,000, nearly all the casualties Palestinian, and left hopes for a Mideast peace accord in tatters. Both sides acknowledged that talking peace was becoming untenable with a full-scale war being waged in the streets.

Even as pleas for restraint poured in from around the world, the climbing casualty count fueled Palestinian fury and spurred revenge attacks against “Israeli” settlers.

On the road to the Jewish settlement of Ariel in the West Bank, a 24-year-old “Israeli” was shot and killed - reportedly when he stopped to change a tire. A school bus on its way to the Jewish settlement of Shiloh came under fire, but no one was injured.

Fighting also boiled over into Arab towns in “Israel” proper, rattling the nerves of “Israelis” who have long feared an intefadeh, or uprising, by Arab citizens of the Jewish state. Five “Israeli” Arabs were killed in Monday's clashes alone.

Police and Palestinians fought a three-hour pitched battle in the alleyways of the “Israeli” town of Akko. Demonstrators trashed shops in the “Israeli” Arab town of Nazareth, Jesus' boyhood home. In the coastal city of Haifa - often cited as a model of harmonious coexistence of Jews and Muslims - “Israeli” Arabs staged a general strike in sympathy with Palestinian brethren.

The Palestinian lands, though, remained the epicenter of Intifada, with a shifting battlefront that hopscotched from north to south and back again. In the West Bank and Gaza, “Israeli” soldiers fought dozens of running battles - many with live ammunition - with Palestinian youths wielding stones and firebombs.

The detritus of conflict could be seen everywhere. Jagged rocks, spent shells, and broken glass littered roadways. Black smoke from piles of burning tires mixed with a white haze of acrid tear gas. The wail of Quranic verses from mosque loudspeakers mingled with the crackle of gunfire.

“Israeli” settlers were forbidden to travel the main thoroughfares in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. At the isolated Gaza settlement of Netzarim, the army sent a helicopter to retrieve “Israelis” who had been visiting for the Jewish New Year holiday. Army convoys escorted others out.

The growing use of heavy combat weapons by “Israel” contributed to the phenomenon of mass casualties from a single incident. Near Netzarim, the firing of one antitank missile into a building injured 35 Palestinians, hospital officials said.

In Nablus, a pair of young Palestinians - hands covered with the dust of stones they had hurled all day - said the risk of death or injury meant little to them.

``I don't have an alternative,'' said a high school student named Yousef, who refused to give his name for fear of arrest. ``I just have the rocks I can throw at soldiers.''

To these young demonstrators, symbolic victories are all-important: in the very gunsights of “Israeli” soldiers near Netzarim, Palestinian youths scaled an abandoned army tower to tear down the “Israeli” flag. At another clash, one young demonstrator flung himself flat on his belly as bullets peppered the street around him - but kept his red-green-and-black Palestinian flag aloft.

 

Berri urges Arabs to break ties with “Israel”…(5)

The spate of violence in “Israel” and the occupied territories prompted Speaker Nabih Berri on Monday to call on the Arab world to cut its official ties with the Jewish state and Premier Salim Hoss to urge for an Arab summit.
Berri denounced “Israel’s” killing of several dozen Palestinians and called on Arab Parliaments and Shura Councils to condemn “Israel’s” acts and stress Jerusalem’s Arab identity.
“I call on Arab governments to sever all diplomatic relations and forms of normalization with “Israel”,” Berri said in a statement on Monday.
He said “Israel” wanted to expel Quds’s Arab residents and do away with the city’s Muslim and Christian identities.
““Israel’s” crimes against Palestinian children, women and elderly civilians reveal once again the ugliest picture of state terrorism,” he said.
For his part, Hoss said that the television footage of 12-year-old Mohammed Aldura being killed in his father’s arms must be shown to the world. The international community must condemn “Israel” for its crimes against innocent children, women and elderly, he said.
“This is incontestable proof that “Israel” deliberately and intentionally killed Palestinians, and that it’s lying when it says that its soldiers were firing at armed Palestinians,” Hoss said.
“Two shameful things can’t be forgiven: the shame of killing women and that of killing children. “Israel” is drowning in both,” he added.
Hoss said the events required an Arab stand of solidarity, adding that it would be appropriate to call for an Arab summit.
The president of the Higher Shiite Council, Sheikh Mohammed Mehdi Shamseddine, called on Palestinians to continue their armed struggle against “Israel”, saying the struggle was one of the main ways for Arabs to retain their dignity.
Shamseddine appealed to all Arabs to help Palestinians protect Quds and restore their legitimate rights in their country.
He also urged the media to expose “Israeli” crimes.
Also condemning the violence were Grand Mufti Sheikh Mohammed Rashid Qabbani, Sayyed Mohammed Hussein Fadlallah, the Arab Journalists’ Union, the Baath and Syrian Social Nationalist parties, and other parties and organizations.

 

Arabs condemn “Israeli” use of force against Palestinians and west urges restraint…(6)

An outpour of anger has gripped the Arab world. Tens of thousands of people have taken to the streets of Amman, Sanaa and other major cities in support of Palestinians. Politicians across the Middle East and Western nations roundly condemned “Israel” for the intense clashes. Iran has called for a mass Islamic uprising against “Israel” while France denounced “Israeli” Likud leader Ariel Sharon for instigating the clashes. The Gulf Cooperation Council, which groups six Gulf Arab states, condemned the quote slaughter of Palestinians in the wake of Sharon's visit to Al-Aqsa. UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan appealed to leaders from both sides to end the worst fighting since 1996.

Pope John Paul II has called for peace in the Middle East and urged an end of warfare. France's Chirac warned “Israel” quote "one does not fight against popular emotions with tanks." The French president urged both “Israelis” and Palestinians to show restraint. Paris, which heads the rotating presidency of the European Union, accused Sharon of deliberately provoking the outbreak of violence. The European Union's special Mideast envoy Miguel Angel Moratinos also blamed Sharon for the fighting, adding he was "dismayed" to see peacemaking efforts wiped out by an act of provocation. Arab League Ministers held an emergency meeting in Cairo to discuss the surge of fighting in the West Bank and Gaza. The Palestinian Representative to the Arab League labeled the bloodshed as a massacre. The group demanded the UN Security Council conduct an investigation into the events and try the responsible “Israelis” in the International Criminal Court. The UN court due to be set up will deal with crimes against humanity and large-scale abuses of human rights. [Esmat Abdel-Meguid, Secretary General of the Arab League, "This is a terrible aggression and we are putting responsibility on the “Israeli” leader and the possibilities of that going to an international investigation have been discussed and a report will be put to the Security Council and to the European Union about the developments that we see as a clear aggression against the Palestinian people.] Abdel Meguid urged Arabs to support what he described as a new Palestinian uprising against “Israeli” occupation. Jordan opened up its hospitals to injured Palestinians and collected funds for them as King Abdullah, who blamed “Israel” for the clashes, met Arafat. Thousands of Palestinians and Jordanians took part in protests against “Israeli” brutality throughout the Kingdom. They burnt “Israeli” flags and urged Amman to expel the “Israeli” ambassador. Jordanian opposition groups called in the government to sever relations with “Israel”. Thousands of refugees in Syria also took to the streets in support for fellow Palestinians. In Sanaa, thousands of Yemenis marched in support of Palestinians. They chanted anti-”Israeli” slogans. Iraq called on the Palestinians to engage in holy war against “Israel” and to abandon negotiations.

Camps cry out over slaughter back home…(7)

Palestinians here continued to demonstrate on Monday in support of their compatriots battling “Israeli” security forces in “Israel” and the occupied territories.
In their fourth successive day of protests, school and shops closed in refugee camps, while Palestinians observed a day of mourning for those who have been killed.
Marches took place throughout the country ­ Beirut, the Ain al-Hilweh and Mieh Mieh camps in the South, and in Chouf, at Iqlim al-Kharroub.
As in the previous days, protesters held anti-”Israeli” banners and pictures of Al-Aqsa Mosque in Quds while chanting anti-”Israeli” slogans.
At Mieh Mieh, students huddled at the camp’s entrance, burning tires and “Israeli” flags.
Meanwhile, 200 Palestinians paraded in front of the UNIFIL headquarters in Naqoura with banners reading: “Quds is ours and we will not give it up.”
They also presented a UNIFIL officer with a letter addressed to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, denouncing the “violence that our people are being subjected to at the hands new of the Nazis.”
Meanwhile, doctors in Sidon held a sit-in in the Hamshari Hospital, while others demonstrated Bar Elias’ Naz-
areth Hospital.
Both groups issued statements condemning the Jewish state’s acts and called on Arabs “to stand by the Palestinians in the occupied territories.”
In Sidon’s Quds Square, hundreds of people gathered in front of a mosque to pray for the several dozen who have been killed.
Palestinians in Wadi Zeina carried giant pictures of Quds along with pictures of Mohammed Aldura, a 12-year-old Palestinian whose shooting death in the clashes on Sunday was captured by a television crew.
“From Rafah to Beirut, our people won’t die,” the group chanted. “You dirty Barak, our people are not cheap.”
The group then walked from Wadi Zeina to Sidon’s camps to link up with other protesters.
A mock funeral for the slain child took place in the Bourj al-Barajneh camp near Beirut, in a protest that was organized by local residents and saw participation by Hizbullah members.
Various Palestinian groups called for an emergency Arab summit. They called on the Organization of the Islamic Conference to act swiftly and defend Quds and asked the UN and the international community to stop “Israel’s” attacks.
 Meanwhile, Palestinians began a blood drive to help “ease the ordeal” for the growing number  of wounded Palestinians.
A statement signed by Palestinian refugees in 12 camps was sent to the International Committee of the Red Cross, asking the organization to take blood to the Palestinian territories.
A Palestinian official at the Mar Elias camp in Beirut said: “We’re planning to collect 3,000 units and we’re now in direct contact with the ICRC to see if they can help arrange the transportation of the blood.”
An ICRC official in Beirut responded: “As for us, it will be technically difficult to transfer those units across the border ... The Palestinian refugees should work with local NGOs or the Palestinian Red Crescent to see if they can help in this matter.”
Clinics in some camps are already accepting blood donations. ­

 

Hundreds join AUB’s Palestinians in protest against crackdown by “Israel”…(7)

Several hundred students met in front of the American University of Beirut’s West Hall on Monday to kick off the academic year with a demonstration protesting “Israeli” soldiers’ killings of Palestinian civilians.
Several student groups from AUB and Beirut Arab University planned a series of events beginning Monday that will culminate in a march to the United Nations House from the two universities at 2.30pm on Tuesday.
“The massacre that our people are experiencing in occupied Palestine concerns every one of us. With every drop of blood that drips there, drips part of our soul,” Tareq Musa, president of the AUB Palestinian club, told the participants following a moment of silence.
Clashes in “Israel” and the occupied territories over the last five days have left dozens of people dead and wounded hundreds. Right-wing “Israeli” opposition leader Ariel Sharon’s visit to Al-Aqsa Mosque compound in occupied East Quds Thursday instigated the confrontations.
While right-wing groups assert “Israel’s” sovereignty over Arab East Quds, UN resolutions confirm that it is occupied by “Israel” and demand the Jewish state’s complete withdrawal. The issue is one of the major sticking points of the stagnant final-status negotiations.
“We can’t carry stones or anything and support this new intifida, but we can at least support in spirit,” said a member of the Palestinian club who did not want to be identified.
““Israel” is starting a fire everywhere, and we hope we can put it out and realize our rights in Palestine, Lebanon and the Golan,” he added.
One student who plans on walking in the march Tuesday said he believed that Sharon knew what the bloody consequences of his visit would be.
“He knew that by going to such a holy site he would be greatly opposed,” said Walid Masroua, wearing a black ribbon handed out earlier that day to mourn the victims. “You could see it in the way he came with an army of bodyguards.
“Why as a Lebanese am I going? Because “Israel” is the enemy of the state. It withdrew from the South, but there are still detainees in “Israel”. And then there are a lot of refugees in Lebanon who are suffering.”
Others participated to express their disapproval of the what they saw as the Arab world’s weak response to the last few days’ events and to the peace negotiations.
“Quds is Arab. Arab nations’ solidarity on the issue is not proportional to what’s going on now,” said Said Sanadiki. “The events of the last few days just ignited all these issues.”

'We people are not afraid to die'…(8)

Apart from his name, there are two things that strike you about Nakad Nakad. The first is his warmth, and the second is the speed with which he acquaints you with his wound. The handshakes were barely over before he had hoisted his trouser leg to reveal a nasty-looking purple welt on his calf, the size of a tea crumpet.

"Police rubber bullet," he says. "We were doing nothing, just standing in the street, and suddenly – pow, pow, pow – the police went crazy."

Mr Nakad is an “Israeli” Arab, or as they prefer to be known, a Palestinian who holds “Israeli” citizenship. There are 1.1m of them in “Israel’s” 6.2m population, a minority not to be confused with the Palestinians of the West Bank and Gaza.

The former live inside “Israel”, passport holders with the right to vote; the latter are struggling to persuade the “Israelis” to return enough of their territory, occupied since 1967, for them to create a state of their own.

I had gone to see Nakad Nakad because he is active in Arab politics, the leader of the student branch of Habash party and he was involved in clashes with the police this summer. For days, reports had been circulating in the “Israeli” media that the “Israeli” Arab population was growing increasingly angry and militant. He warned trouble was looming, and it turns out – months later – he was spot on.

Um al-Fahm is a town of 35,000 Arabs just inside the pre-1967 “Israeli” border. Mohammed Ahmad Jabarin, a 24-year-old journalist, said: "The main problem is that we feel we have no status in the Jewish state as Arabs. They have confiscated our land, and demolished our houses which they say we build without permits. But we don't know where we stand under Jewish law." And yet they know their civil rights, political influence – they have 10 Knesset members – and welfare benefits could prove even less under the autocratic rule of Yasser Arafat.

But the issue is about identity and dignity, and ethnicity. Most “Israeli” Arabs are Muslims. When Ariel Sharon set foot on their holy sites in Quds last week, he triggered forces with roots deep in history and religion. Some have been radicalised by it.

"We people are not afraid to die," said Mohammed. "We are not afraid of the “Israelis”. If one of us dies we know it is for our holy sites, and our women will bring babies to replace us."

 

30 collaborators tried by military…(9)

The trial of 30 people on charges of collaborating with “Israel” during its 22-year occupation of the South opened at a military court on Monday afternoon.
The defendants, all of whom were in custody, were mostly members of “Israel’s” now-defunct proxy militia. Others were civilians who traded with “Israel” or traveled to the Jewish state.
The batch of suspects was the 29th since the trials of collaborators started on June 5, 12 days after “Israel” completed its pullout from the South.
Presided over by Brigadier Maher Safieddine, the court ­ which has ruled in more than 1,100 cases ­ has handed down prison terms of up to 15 years and is due to study around 1,400 other cases.
