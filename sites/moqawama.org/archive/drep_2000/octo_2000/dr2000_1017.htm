Introduction

Hizbullah’s Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah asserted that the “Israeli” colonel held by the Islamic Resistance (IR) was a spy captured in Lebanon which he entered under a false passport.

“When you have an officer of the rank of colonel who was worked for a long time with the Mossad and wanted to penetrate Hizbullah at a higher level and comes to Lebanon of his own free will… it is the legitimate right of the resistance to capture and detain him”, he said.

Nasrallah rejected reports the “Israeli” was snatched in Switzerland. “There was no capture in Switzerland or anywhere else. He arrived in Lebanon coming from Switzerland with a fake foreign passport, he added.

 

How the colonel walked into Hizbullah’s trap…(1)

Nasrallah: “Israeli” officer was captured in Lebanon not anywhere else

Hizbullah’s secretary-general  said Monday that the “Israeli” colonel it captured came to Lebanon of his own free will after being lured by a party member he was trying to recruit.
“The issue is not one of kidnapping, as “Israel” claimed, so it cannot label it as a terrorist act,” Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah told a news conference at the Imam Hassanein Mosque in Haret Hreik. “This is an “Israeli” colonel working for the Mossad who tried to infiltrate Hizbullah. We followed the issue up and he came to us on his own two feet.”
Tight security measures were in place at the conference, with Hizbullah security personnel not allowing any microphone or tape recorder near Nasrallah, and its members asking journalists not to enter the mosque with their mobile phones.
Nasrallah recounted the details of how the operation was conducted, beginning with how the “Israeli” colonel, identified as Elhanan Tennenbaum, first contacted a Hizbullah sympathizer abroad in a bid to gain information about the party.
Nasrallah offered no specific time frame as to when Tennenbaum first established contact with the resistance member, “who is close to a key Hizbullah figure,” saying only that it was “a long enough period of time.”
Tennenbaum, a member of a “foreign” intelligence agency, contacted the man in an attempt to recruit him. The Hizbullah member immediately informed the party’s security arm, which in turn began investigating Tennenbaum’s identity, even as it planned a way to lure him here.
“After following up the case, suspicion arose about the true identity of the agency this man claimed to belong to, particularly since the questions he was asking suggested that he was related to an “Israeli” security agency,” Nasrallah said.
Deciding to lead Tennenbaum on, Hizbullah chose to provide the “Israeli” with “significant information” that was nonetheless not harmful to the resistance.
“That was when the colonel began his attempt to meet our man in person, asking that they meet in a nearby European country,” Nasrallah said.
Hizbullah, however, rejected the idea of having one of its members travel to meet Tennenbaum and instead had its man threaten to cut communication with the “Israeli” colonel unless he agreed to meet in Beirut.
“He (Tennenbaum) was given more inside information about the party to build his trust further, until he was convinced to come to Lebanon,” Nasrallah said.
Because Tennenbaum held another passport in addition to his “Israeli” one, he could legally visit Lebanon. Nasrallah explained that although Tennenbaum had entered Brussels using his “Israeli” passport, he left for Beirut using a counterfeit foreign one.
“We were able to confirm that he was a Mossad official, and so we made the decision to capture him,” Nasrallah said.
The Hizbullah leader received confirmation of Tennenbaum’s identity, following a confession by the “Israeli” colonel, only a few minutes before arriving at Sunday’s urgent conference organized by the Arab and Islamic Nationalist Conferences.
“Because it was an occasion that joined Arab officials and activists, I decided to announce it then and there. Very few people knew about the operation itself, even within Hizbullah, so it was quite natural for everyone, including Prime Minister Salim Hoss, to be surprised by the news.”
Announcing the news on the eve of the Sharm el-Sheikh summit was “beneficial and gave the Palestinian people a good moral boost,” he added.
Nasrallah also said Hizbullah had every right to hold a Mossad colonel who was trying to spy on the party, particularly since he was on Lebanese territory.
“We’re still in a state of war with “Israel”,” he explained, “and the resistance has the right to capture any “Israeli” soldier whom we can use in a prisoner swap.”
Nasrallah said that Hizbullah’s capture of a fourth “Israeli” gave more weight to the party’s demand to release Lebanese detainees in “Israel”.
“I want to make this very clear: Hizbullah will not disclose any information without the release of detainees in return.”
Although Hizbullah does have other demands, these, Nasrallah said, will only be revealed through a negotiation process and not via the media.
However, he added that the three basic demands include the release of Lebanese and non-Lebanese detainees in “Israel”, the return of the bodies of fighters which “Israel” continues to hold, and the disclosure of the fate of those missing during operations against “Israel” and during the 1982 “Israeli” invasion.
Nasrallah denied “Israeli” claims that Tennenbaum was kidnapped in Switzerland and transported to Beirut in an Iranian plane, describing “Israeli” accusations of Iranian involvement as “a myth.”
“Hizbullah and Hizbullah alone bears full responsibility for capturing the “Israeli” colonel. We alone bear this responsibility and are ready to bear the consequences of our action.”
He also insisted that the group had no active cells outside of Lebanon.
Nasrallah would not say if Tennenbaum’s mission was to assassinate him, promising to reveal more information “later.”
Addressing the mothers of the three “Israeli” soldiers Hizbullah captured in the Shibaa Farms last week, Nasrallah said: “I want to tell you that your government has not worked seriously for the release of your sons. It is still maneuvering and betting to exert pressure on Hizbullah.
“I tell both the “Israeli” government and the mothers that this is a losing bet and a waste of time. We’re not in a hurry and are willing to wait five, 10, even 20 years and won’t give out information to any party without the release of detainees in return.”
Nasrallah said it was too soon to reveal whether Tennenbaum, who was labeled by “Israeli” officials as a businessman, had contacts or had conducted business with Lebanese businesspeople.
Although Nasrallah said UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan had not contacted him yet regarding Tennenbaum’s capture, he was confident that Annan would express interest in the case.
“This is the humiliating truth, that no one asks about the hundreds of detainees and the death of hundreds of civilians in Palestine. Yet the whole world reacts when three soldiers and a colonel are captured. Anyway, Annan will visit Beirut to inquire about their fate soon,” Nasrallah predicted.
The UN and Russia have been proposed as potential channels for Hizbullah’s negotiations. Nasrallah said he believed Germany has not contacted the resistance party yet because it was awaiting an “Israeli” request.
He said “Israel” knew better than to stage a wide-scale operation against Lebanon in response to the capture and repeated his warnings that any “Israeli” attack would meet a harsh response and that “the “Israelis” know that.”

“The families of some 19 Lebanese held hostage by “Israel” are hoping their loved ones will return home soon, after a possible “Israeli” prisoner swap with Hizbullah. They said the captivity of four “Israeli” soldiers is the only hope they have to see “Israel” release their relatives. We met the family of Mustafa Dirani, a leading resistance figure, who was kidnapped by “Israeli” commandos from his home in 1994. 

“ For the past six and a half years Zeinab Dirani has struggled to ensure her five children remember their father. Sara was only two years old when dozens of “Israeli” soldiers stormed their home in the cloak of the night. Zeinab encourages her children to work hard in school so their father will be proud of their grades. Collaborators ‘should face death’…(3)

Two military investigating magistrates on Monday asked that death sentence be handed down in absentia to 13 collaborators accused of killing and kidnapping civilians and entering enemy territory.
Rashid Mezher and Antoine Suleiman also issued arrest warrants against the 13 men, who remain at large.

Palestinian refugees call for armed struggle, not summit with “Israel”…(5)

“ Palestinian radical and Islamist groups held protests in refugee camps in south Lebanon against Yasser Arafat's participation in the Sharm el-Sheikh summit on Monday, calling instead for armed struggle against “Israel”.

Hundreds of Palestinians gathered outside the Ain Helweh camp near the town of Saida for a protest called by the Democratic and the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine (DFLP and PFLP), both of which oppose the peace process.

"We urge Arafat not to meet with the murderer (“Israel’s” Prime Minister Ehud) Barak, to declare a Palestinian state and to distribute arms to fight the Zionists," DFLP official Abu Ihab said in a speech.

An AFP correspondent said the protesters burnt US and “Israeli” flags, chanting that the summit in the Egyptian resort of Sharm el-Sheikh was orchestrated by the United States "to put down the new intifada", or anti-”Israeli” uprising.

The emergency summit was called after two weeks of “Israeli”-Palestinian clashes in which at least 107 people were martyred, all but seven of them Arabs.

Further south from Sidon, Palestinian Islamists burnt tyres outside the Rashidiyeh and Burj al-Shamali camps on the outskirts of the port city of Tyre and forced schools to stay shut.

 

Boy and policeman shot dead by “Israelis”…(6)

Two Palestinians, including a 15–year–old boy, have been martyred by “Israeli” gunfire yesterday.

The boy, Muayed Darwish, was shot in the head near an “Israeli” enclave in the West Bank town of Bethlehem.

He was in a coma for several hours, hooked up to a respirator, before being declared dead.

In the Gaza Strip, a Palestinian policeman was martyred after “Israelis” opened fire on Palestinians trying to cut holes in the border fence with Egypt.

Thirty–eight demonstrators were injured in the clashes. In the West Bank town of Nablus, 19 Palestinians were wounded, including two who were in serious condition.

The uprising cast a pall over US efforts to broker a truce between “Israel” and the Palestinians at an emergency Mideast summit in Sharm el–Sheik, Egypt.

The deaths brought to 99 the number of people martyred in 19 days of fighting, most of them Palestinians. More than 1,000 Palestinians have been injured.

 

Two “Israelis” wounded in Jordan shooting incident…(7)

Two “Israeli” soldiers were lightly wounded by gunfire Sunday from across the border with Jordan. The “Israeli” army said the soldiers, who were shot while on patrol in the Jordan Valley, returned fire. It was the first shooting incident on the Jordanian border since “Israeli”-Palestinian clashes raged more than two weeks ago. Amman confirmed that shots were fired at the “Israeli” patrol, saying unknown attackers fired near the Prince Mohammad Bridge, linking Jordan to “Israel”. Investigations are underway. Jordan and “Israel” signed a peace agreement in 1994. But there has been an outpour of anger against “Israel” due to its brutal crackdown on Palestinians. Most of Jordan's population is originally Palestinian.
