Nasrallah to religious and political leaders: ‘We are the cause of our own problems’…(1)

Hizbullah leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah urged the religious and political hierarchy to eradicate sectarianism and abandon the recent bickering that has helped fuel the country’s “most vicious disease.
“We have to have the courage to speak the truth and abandon Lebanese flowery speech.
“First, we have to get over the idea that we’re not the cause of our problems and that everything that happened in Lebanon was the result of outside forces,” Nasrallah said during a lunch at the Commodore Hotel to honor the media.
In an apparent response to the reactions for and against the statement by the Council of Maronite Bishops last week, Nasrallah said he simply wanted to present his point of view on the matter and does not want to impose it on anyone.
“We have a true Lebanese problem, which is sectarianism. Why do we avoid admitting it’s there? What happened in Lebanon was a sectarian war where each group allied itself with the brother, the friend or the enemy. It is we who opened this door and we’re responsible for our situation,” Nasrallah said.
The Hizbullah chief said sectarianism could only be removed by allowing officials in the country to sit down and seriously discuss their differences.
“Before the war, the Christians spoke in fear of the Muslims and the Muslims felt oppressed by the Christians. What do the Syrians have to do with this?” he asked, in response to Bkirki blaming Syria for the economic and political plight of the country.
“Escaping from the problem of sectarian realities by saying we have the Syrian problem is simply escaping from one difficulty to another.
“If we didn’t have sectarianism, we wouldn’t have had the Syrians,” he said, adding that everyone in the country still uses sectarianism as a tool to defend their interests.
“Just like we needed Syria to face “Israel”, we need it to face our internal problems. Syria is Lebanon’s security guarantee. Yes, we have an army, but it still needs some time to become stronger on
a national level because it’s
also built on sectarianism,”
he said.
Nasrallah began his speech by saying that the victory achieved following the liberation of the South in May was a victory for the whole country and not just Hizbullah.
Referring to the first speech the Hizbullah leader made in Bint Jbeil following the liberation, Nasrallah said Hizbullah has rejected the victory being attributed to the party alone.
“We knew the many dangers such a label would have on Lebanon. We didn’t want to use this victory to benefit our party because only Lebanon should benefit from this victory,” he said. Nasrallah said he had hoped that the liberation that defeated “Israel” would have constituted a basis for national unity.
“Those who sacrificed their lives, education and normal living conditions to achieve this liberation understand more than anyone else what it means to protect this victory. But those who read about this achievement like they read a story and discard it
will never understand that,” he said.
Nasrallah said it was a shame that the country did not have the chance to invest in the liberation as much as it deserved because parliamentary elections took the limelight so soon afterward.
“We fell from the heights of victory to the lowest level possible ­ sectarianism. Just like that, we went back 50 years and it is the responsibility of everyone to revitalize this victory,” he said.
Nasrallah also warned that “Israel”, through recent statements by “Israeli” officials, was preparing the international community to hold Hizbullah responsible for any military operation against the Jewish state.
Nasrallah, who said it was ridiculous to respond to comment made by “Israeli” officials, quoted Lieutenant General Shaul Mofaz, “Israel’s” chief of staff, who said Hizbullah has prepared an infrastructure in the South to launch attacks into “Israel”.
“Mofaz also said Hizbullah intends to target Jewish communities all over the world. The next thing he will say is that Hizbullah is following the “Israelis” to Mars.”
He said that it was natural for “Israel” to be worried because it continues to occupy Lebanese territory and holds Lebanese detainees in its prisons and is still a threat to Lebanon.
“Following the liberation, I warned that Lebanon was under “Israeli” threat. I said then that even if we didn’t do anything against it, the enemy could use any pretext to stir tension and hold Lebanon responsible for it,” the Hizbullah chief said.
He predicted that “Israel” would face a series of violent activities as a result of the many internal problems it was facing, warning that Lebanon could be held responsible for some of these acts.

Golden opportunity…(2)

Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah’s remarks on Friday that it is time to rid ourselves of the blight of sectarianism that has bedevilled this nation for decades and plunged it into a ruinous civil war that lies at the root of our present plight, is a signpost that we must all heed if we are to claw our way back into what Winston Churchill described in Britain’s darkest hour as the “sunlit uplands.” Nasrallah’s urgings are all the more forceful since they come from a man of great perception who led the successful resistance against “Israeli” occupation to eventual victory, a resounding defeat of an enemy long considered militarily invincible. That victory, which has impacted heavily on the stumbling peace process and stiffened the Palestinians in their struggle for their rights, was a victory for the nation as a whole ­ as Nasrallah has been at pains to impress on us all. It should not be squandered by our leaders, whatever their sect or religious affiliation, as they continue to bicker in petty squabbles over what is often little more than a camouflage for venal self-interest on their part.
Make no mistake about it; we are in the grip of an economic crisis that is not going to go away unless we all pull together as a nation. And as the economic misery deepens, political turmoil is sure to follow. This is a path we have been down before and we know the consequences ­ or we should by now.
As Nasrallah noted: “It is us who opened this door and we are responsible for our situation … Escaping from the problem of sectarian realities by saying we have a Syrian problem is simply escaping from one difficulty to another. If we didn’t have sectarianism, we wouldn’t have had the Syrians.” Ergo, the Syrians per se are not the problem; we are.
“We fell from the heights of victory to the lowest level possible ­ sectarianism. Just like that, we went back 50 years and it’s the responsibility of everyone to revitalize this victory.”
The time for action is now. Like Britain in 1940, we are facing a dire threat. We may never again have this chance to truly weld ourselves into a nation, united in the common goal of prosperity and dignity for all. The people spoke clearly and unequivocally in the parliamentary elections and the politicians are catching on ­ or should be. This is a chance for the president to assume the mantle of statesman and take the initiative to demonstrate that he is the nation’s leader by calling for a conference that embraces all sects and persuasions to formulate a clearly defined national strategy that will leave no one marginalized. A divided nation cannot ­ repeat cannot ­ overcome Lebanon’s myriad problems, and certainly not our economic crisis.
Nasrallah, rightly pointed out that many in “Israel” have not accepted defeat in the South well. The calm, beyond one or two isolated incidents, that has existed on the border since May 24 has taken them by surprise and left them uncertain and unsure. The impasse with the Palestinians and the Syrians has fueled this sense of unease. In the past, at least, this has usually boded ill for Lebanon, for so long the “Israelis”’ punching bag. Nasrallah’s comments, and Hizbullah’s discipline in the South, has answered only too well those in “Israel” who questioned what the resistance would do once the occupiers folded their tents and went home. Implicit in Nasrallah’s comments was that Hizbullah will not be the first to start trouble in the South. In turn, this should mean that peace negotiations with Syria and the Palestinians can resume, and eventually that the question of Syria’s relations with Lebanon can be resolved.
But first we, all of us, must set aside old animosities, and yes, even hatreds, and chart a course for ourselves to those elusive Churchillian “sunlit uplands” that beckon.

 

AFP protests against “Israeli” shooting at Lebanese Journalists

Boy shot in the head by “Israelis”…(3)

A 12-year-old boy was wounded on Thursday by “Israeli” fire while he was throwing stones at soldiers near the Fatima Gate.
Hussein Sirhan, from Kfar Kila, was shot in the head and was taken to a hospital in Nabatieh.
Meanwhile, the media watchdog Reporters Sans Frontieres protested on Thursday against what it said were incidents of “Israeli” soldiers shooting at Lebanese journalists on the border.
The Paris-based group said in a statement that it had sent a letter to “Israeli” Prime Minister Ehud Barak, who also holds the defense portfolio, calling on him to immediately open an inquiry into incidents that allegedly occurred earlier this month.
RSF quoted Taher Abu Hamdan, a journalist with Agence France Presse, as saying that “Israeli” soldiers fired without warning at journalists on Sept. 22.
“This act is very worrying because it’s the second time in a month that such an incident has occurred,” said Robert Menard, general secretary of RSF.
The statement said that “Israeli” soldiers also fired into the air on Sept. 12, “apparently to frighten three Lebanese journalists who were at the border to take pictures.”
The organization requested that those responsible for the incidents be punished.
No immediate comment was available from the “Israeli” Defense (War) Ministry.

Occupation violated Blue Line again…(4)

As part of the Zionist encroachments into Lebanese territories, an “Israeli” military seven-strong foot patrol on Wednesday advanced from the outpost of Tallet as-Sumaqa in the occupied al-Arqoub Hilltops.

The enemy patrol entered the Jal al-Ahmar area (outskirts of Kfarshouba) from which the occupation forces had retreated on May 24. They stayed there for thirty minutes before returning to their outpost in occupied Tallet al-Sumaqa.

A UN Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) Indian patrol arrived afterwards in the area but after it was too late.

 

Military court sentences 44 men for collaboration…(5)

A military court on Thursday sentenced 44 people to prison terms ranging from six months to seven years for collaborating with “Israel” during its 22-year occupation of the South.
Some of those sentenced were also fined between LL200, 000 and LL1 million and ordered to stay away from their villages for periods equal to their sentences once they finish serving their prison terms.
The court, presided over by Brigadier Maher Safieddine, also acquitted Najib Mustafa Ghazin and Qassem Mohammed Haidar and referred Mohammed Mahmoud Hariss to the Juveniles Court because he was under 18.
Most defendants were with the “Israeli”-proxy militia. Others, those receiving six-month sentences, were civilians who traded with “Israel” or traveled there.
The military court’s Thursday rulings brought the number of people tried to more than 1,100. The court has handed down prison terms of up to 15 years since the trials of collaborators began on June 5.
Also on Thursday, Assistant Military Investigating Magistrate Antoine Suleiman questioned 21 suspected collaborators. He ordered the trial of 18 and released the others.
After “Israel’s” May 24 withdrawal, some 2,200 “Israeli” agents surrendered or were captured. The rest, including some 300 militia officers, fled to “Israel” with their families.
Out of some 6,000 Lebanese who fled to “Israel”, about 200 have returned. Women and children went back to their villages, men surrendered to the military.
