Lebanese Detainee Denounces “Israel”

Obeid: We kicked “Israel” out by force

“ A spiritual leader of Lebanese guerrillas who was abducted by “Israeli” commandos 11 years Wednesday spoke out against his captors Wednesday and claimed credit for helping to drive “Israel” out of southern Lebanon.

Sheik Abdel Karim Obeid, who has been in prisons since 1989, showed that he has learned the language of his captors when he spoke to reporters in Hebrew before a hearing in “Israel’s” Supreme Court.

``There is no justice. In this whole country there is no justice,'' Obeid said as he and fellow detainee Mustafa Dirani, sat waiting to appeal their detention. The panel of five judges made no decision.

Obeid, wearing a white knit prayer cap and a dark blue prison uniform, spoke in simple but direct Hebrew about “Israel’s” May 24 withdrawal from southern Lebanon after 18 years.

``We kicked them “Israel” out by force,'' said Obedi, who was a local spiritual leader of the Iranian-backed Hizbullah when he was kidnapped by “Israeli” commandos in 1989.

Dirani, also had bitter words against “Israel”.

``If there was no “Israeli” oppression I'd be in Lebanon,'' Dirani said in Arabic. ``They occupy your land, arrest you in your own house, destroy your land, and kill your children.''

Obeid was interviewed on “Israel” television shortly after he was abducted. From then until a May 29 open court session, neither he nor Dirani was seen in public.

The two Lebanese have been held in “Israel” as bargaining chips in any negotiations on the release of missing airman Ron Arad, who was shot down over Lebanon in 1986 and captured alive.

They remain in custody despite a Supreme Court ruling in April that led to the freeing of 11 other Lebanese detainees, also held as bargaining chips. The ruling said “Israel” cannot hold foreigners unless they are a threat to state security.

The Tel Aviv District Court has ruled that the two still endanger national security and may be kept temporarily in administrative detention. They have never been charged or put on trial…(1)

On April 12, the Supreme Court ruled that the state could not hold a suspect in administrative detention unless he poses a security threat.
The state immediately asked Tel Aviv District Court to approve new administrative detention orders against Dirani and Obeid on the grounds that they threaten national security. Acting Tel Aviv District Court President Uri Goren approved the request on July 12. Zvi Rish, the attorney representing Obeid and Dirani, then appealed the ruling, claiming that it was illegal on procedural grounds as well as unfounded because Dirani and Obeid do not pose a security threat.
During yesterday's hearing, Rish claimed that Obeid's abduction had nothing to do with Arad. He had been kidnapped at the request of US authorities, who wanted to question him about the kidnapping of an American officer by a relative of his.
"It didn't sound good to admit that we kidnapped Obeid for the sake of Col. Higgins," Rish told the court. "It sounded better to say we did it for Ron Arad. It was a cynical exploitation of Arad's plight, because he had become a national myth by then."
"This is a very cynical accusation," Justice Tova Strasberg-Cohen told Rish. "You are saying the authorities have held Obeid for such a long time for no other reason than a manipulation."
Rish claimed that neither Obeid nor Dirani belong to Hizbullah and that Hizbullah is no longer a threat to “Israel” now that the “Israeli” Army has withdrawn from southern Lebanon. "There is no evidence to prove that if they are released, Obeid and Dirani will return to the cycle of violence," he said.
Gilad vehemently rejected Rish's claims. "We have all the proof we need of Hizbullah's plans," he said. "It regards “Israel” as Satan and is working in close cooperation with Iran."
According to Gilad, even though there are no hostilities on the northern border at the moment, "Hizbullah is building a very impressive infrastructure which has us very worried. It is building an artillery capacity that could cause serious damage to the North... they are making an enormous effort to carry out mass attacks in the heart of “Israel”.
According to Gilad, Obeid and Dirani will be welcomed back to Hizbullah and be given positions of command if released. Dirani was the head of a group which moved from Amal to Hizbullah. The organization, which is still intact, is awaiting Dirani's return, said Gilad. He described Obeid as a charismatic leader who can incite the masses and persuade followers to become suicide bombers…(2)

Kidnapping fears add to border tension…(3)

Growing tensions along Lebanon’s border with “Israel” and a feared impasse in the Palestinian track of the peace process are raising fears of potential violence along the frontier.
“Israel’s” chief of staff, Lieutenant General Shaul Mofaz, was reported Wednesday as saying that Hizbullah and Palestinian groups were preparing to kidnap “Israeli” soldiers or civilians.
Hizbullah in particular, Mofaz said, wants to capture “Israeli” soldiers to win the release of Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani, two resistance leaders held in “Israel” without trial since 1989 and 1994 respectively.
“The Iranians, who support Hizbullah, are building an infrastructure they feel will allow attacks inside “Israel”,” Mofaz said, adding that “both Hamas and the Islamic Jihad have placed abducting soldiers and perhaps civilians high on their list of priorities to negotiate a prisoner release.”
Hizbullah has deployed unarmed fighters in groups of four or five along the border from Ras Naqoura on the coast to Mount Hermon in the east. The squads monitor “Israeli” troop movements on the other side of the fence, creating fears among the “Israelis” that Hizbullah fighters are planning to snatch a soldier.
The kidnapping worries come as incidents of violence along the border are gradually spinning out of control. Although the bulk of stone-throwing has occurred at the Fatima Gate in Kfar Kila, the protests are beginning to spread elsewhere along the border fence.
The stone-throwing at Fatima Gate is irritating for the “Israelis” but generally harmless ­ the soldiers are posted in well-protected observation booths. Furthermore, the novelty has worn off and fewer demonstrators are visiting the site.
However, unrestricted access to the Sheikh Abbad hilltop has encouraged growing numbers of Lebanese to visit the tomb at the summit, which straddles the border, and taunt the “Israeli” soldiers standing just a few meters away.
UNIFIL sources said that on Tuesday evening, a Hizbullah supporter attempted to hoist a party flag from the UN flagpole on the summit. When he was obstructed by Ghanaian UNIFIL troops deployed on the hill, the man attempted to cross the UN-delineated Blue Line into “Israel”.
The “Israeli” soldiers stationed on the eastern side of the tomb intervened and snatched the flag from the Hizbullah supporter. He then left the scene.
At the same time, on the border a few hundred meters to the south, Finnish UNIFIL soldiers observed some 10 Lebanese hurling stones at a group of seven “Israeli” civilians.
The “Israelis” were throwing stones back. The fight continued for about 20 minutes until an “Israeli” Army patrol arrived and the “Israeli” civilians fled. The Lebanese then simply switched target and began stoning the “Israeli” soldiers.

20 more collaborators men in court…(4)

Twenty people, including former members of “Israel’s” now-defunct proxy militia, went on trial at a military court on Wednesday on charges of collaborating with “Israel”.
The court, presided over by Brigadier Maher Safieddine, originally started looking into the cases of 120 suspects but decided to postpone the trial of 100 suspects upon the request of their defense lawyers who said they needed more time to study the files.
The rulings were expected around midnight on Wednesday.
On Tuesday, the court sentenced 18 former militiamen to prison terms ranging from three months to three years for collaboration with “Israel”.
The convicts included Georgette Jirjis Tawili, a woman, to seven years hard labor in jail for torturing two inmates at the militia-run Khiam prison.
Tawili was also barred for 10 years from returning to her home area in the South on completion of her sentence. Other defendants were barred from returning to their home areas for two years. The 18 defendants were also fined up to LL1 million each.
The court acquitted four other defendants of collaboration charges.
The military court, whose verdicts cannot be appealed, has held 28 sittings and sentenced more than 1,000 people to prison terms of up to 15 years since the trials of collaborators began on June 5.
More than 1,500 defendants are awaiting trial, while 6,000 former militiamen and members of their families fled to “Israel” before the Jewish state completed its withdrawal on May 24.
