Hizbullah: position on coming cabinet depends on program…(1)

Hizbullah Shura Council member, Representative of Imam Khamenei in Lebanon, his Eminence Sheikh Mohammed Yazbek stressed that the party's position on the coming cabinet depends on its political program, especially regarding the resolution of the dispossession problem in Baalbek-Hermel, taking interest in the recently-liberated areas, following up the issue of the Resistance against the “Israeli” occupation which still occupies a dear part of our land, and tackling the issue of the detainees in the enemy's prisons.

Speaking at a celebration to mark the birthday of Lady Fatima al-Zahraa (A.S.) in Imam Khomenei's Husseineit in Baalbek, his Eminence warned those who label the Resistance as a "militia" against falling into the trap set by the Zionist enemy which demands that the Resistance be disarmed, "for the resistance's weapons keep the enemy in constant concern."

Lebanese civilian injured by “Israeli” landmine

A truck driver was wounded when a land mine, left behind by “Israeli” occupation forces, exploded, a security source said.
The mine detonated as Mohammed Hussein Ghibris, 39, was driving his vehicle near the former Swaida outpost near Nabatieh, the source said.
Ghibris was taken to the Popular Relief Hospital in Nabatieh for treatment. His doctors said he suffered moderate wounds.
“I was on my way to al-Wadi al-Akhdar between Kfar Roummane and Arab Salim to reopen my stone quarry when the explosion occurred,” Ghibris told reporters. “I took this road since government workers opened it with a bulldozer recently. No mines were found.”
According to police, nine people have died and 63 others have been wounded in land-mine and other ordinance explosions since “Israel” withdrew from the border area on May 24.
UN experts estimate that 130,000 mines and other explosive devices are scattered over the 850-square-kilometer area. The Ukrainian battalion of the UN Interim Force in Lebanon has started a large-scale process to clear landmines. But the battalion complains that “Israel” has refused to supply the mine sweepers with maps indicating the positions of the ordinance…(2)

 “Israel” aims to subvert relations between Syria, Lebanon: Radio Damascus…(4)

“ “Israel” is trying to take revenge for its May 24 troop withdrawal from southern Lebanon by driving a wedge between Damascus and Beirut, Syria's official radio said Thursday.

"“Israel” wants to avenge its defeat in south Lebanon and the victory which Lebanon won over “Israel”," the radio said, describing the “Israeli” pullback as the result of "the struggle of Lebanese resistance forces and Syrian support which consolidated security, stability and unity in Lebanon."

"Damaging Lebanon's unity, and Syria's support for the country is a strategic priority aim," the radio said.

"For “Israel” success in undermining unity and Syrian support for Lebanon is the first stage in moves aimed at destroying joint Arab action," it added.

The radio commentary came a day after Maronite Christian bishops in Lebanon raised a storm by issuing a statement calling for Syria to remove the 35,000 troops it keeps in the country, now that “Israel” has ended its 22-year occupation.

The statement was roundly attacked by Lebanon's top two leaders and by the president and members of the government, while many of the country's newspapers used it to call for serious dialogue.

Military Court sentenced 16 former militiamen…(5)

A military court sentenced 16 former militiamen on Thursday to prison terms for collaborating with “Israel” during its 22-year occupation of the South.
The defendants, all of whom were in custody, received sentences ranging from three weeks to five years. Two others, Ibrahim Sayyed and Butros Hakim, were sentenced in absentia to 15 years. Some of the defendants who were members of the “Israeli”-proxy militia were also fined LL400,000.
After “Israel’s” withdrawal, some 2,200 militiamen members surrendered to Lebanese authorities or were captured, while many others fled to “Israel” with their families. The military court has sentenced more than 1,000 people to jail with terms of up to 15 years since the trials began on June 5.
