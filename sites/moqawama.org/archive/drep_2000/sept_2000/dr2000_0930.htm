MP Qandil warns of attempts to strike Lebanese-Syrian alliance…(1)

Elected MP Nasser Qandil said formation of a national panel to eradicate sectarianism is a national task.

This committee is the only framework for national dialogue under the constitution's ceiling, Qandil said.

The Higher Lebanese-Syrian Council constitutes the constitutional road between Lebanon and Syria. It (the council) alone is the official institution entitled to activate the bilateral ties, he added.

Qandil said the southern front will see “Israeli” pressures as part of a plot to drag the state into a confrontation with the Resistance and the Palestinians' camps as prelude to settling Palestinians in Lebanon.

He warned against the seriousness of some Lebanese being induced into internationalization and engagement in attempts to find holes in Lebanese-Syrian ties.

The strength of the Lebanese-Syrian alliance is the only guarantee to prevent Lebanon from being encircled, he concluded.

 

Former detainees head to Italy to make their case…(2)

A delegation of former Khiam prisoners and children from Arab Salim will travel to Italy this week to lobby for the release of detainees in “Israeli” prisons and the liberation of the Shebaa Farms.
The Follow-Up Committee for Lebanese Detainees in “Israeli” Prisons and representatives from the Italian Embassy announced the group’s itinerary at the Press Federation on Monday.
“I’m happy I’m going. I want to tell them about how “Israel” treats us,” said 13-year-old Hussein Harb, who survived a bombardment of his school in Arab Salim on Dec. 16 last year.
“We weren’t doing anything to them. We were just sitting in school trying to study,” he said as he fidgeted in his seat.
The school, on the frontline of the former occupation zone, was hit by three mortar rounds fired from a nearby outpost manned by the now defunct “Israeli”-proxy militia.
Eighteen children were wounded in the bombardment, two of them seriously.
Hussein underwent two operations but still has shrapnel lodged in his back and suffers permanent nerve damage to his arm.
“The main purpose of the trip was to ease the pain of those who suffered horrible torture in Khiam detention center,” said Rabah Shahrour, who spoke on behalf of committee chief Mohammed Safa.
But the trip has a political angle and includes meetings with Pope John Paul II, Amnesty International and Italian non-governmental organizations.
Hussein and his parents will be joining 17 former Khiam detainees on a four-day trip to Italy courtesy of the Italian Embassy and Alitalia.
The group was selected based on gender, political affiliation and length of detention.
Suleiman Ramadan, whose stay in Khiam between 1985 and 2000 was one of the longest at the notorious center, is part of the group traveling to Italy.
“It’s a huge honor to host these guests in Italy because they suffered torture on our behalf and on behalf of all Lebanon,” said Italian Media Officer Shibli Issa Khoury, who introduced himself as half-Lebanese.
“I only regret that they won’t be joined by those who are detained in “Israel” and that the Shebaa Farms are still occupied.”
The Follow-Up Committee said on Friday that it considered the implementation of UN Resolution 425 to be incomplete because 19 Lebanese are still held in “Israel” and the Shebaa Farms are still occupied.
Shahrour said he hoped other countries would follow the Italians’ example and invite former detainees to visit them.
He argued that Lebanon exhibits a lack of concern for the plight of these “national heroes.”
“Italy is honoring our Lebanese detainees, but does Lebanon?” he asked.
“The government has only provided a few of our needs over the years and has yet to devise a plan for the care and reintegration of the former detainees.”
