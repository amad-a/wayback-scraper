Hizbullah: liberation hasn't ended issue with occupation…(1)

Hizbullah Deputy Secretary General his Eminence Sheikh Naem Qassem stressed that the "liberation of a large part of the south has not ended the issue with the “Israeli” enemy that has its own projects and plans and considers Lebanon a major arena for its activities."

The Islamic Resistance must maintain full readiness and precautions so that we will not be surprised by “Israeli” acts that influence the scene, Sheikh Qassem said.

39 more collaborators come home from “Israel”…(2)

Thirty-nine Lebanese, among them seven former “Israeli”-allied militiamen, who fled to “Israel” during its troop withdrawal in May returned home on Monday.
The 39 arrived by bus at the village of Rosh Hanikra in northwestern “Israel”. They were then picked up by two UN buses and driven across the border to Naqoura, where they were handed over to Lebanese intelligence officers.
Shahine is a 35-year-old woman with three children who refused to give her first name. “It was a miserable life over there ... Thank God I’m back,” she said as she collected the belongings Lebanese officers had just finished searching. She’d spent four months sharing two rooms with her family in northern “Israel”.
“There’s nothing like home,” she said. Her husband, a former member of the militia, opted to stay in “Israel” rather than face trial in Lebanon, she added.
About 6,000 Lebanese ­ mostly members of or linked to the now-defunct militia ­ and their families sought refuge in “Israel” during the withdrawal.
Jiryes Fardisi, a returning former militiaman, said coming back to his country was better ­ “regardless of what will happen to me.”
Among the seven ex-militiamen was the gardener of the top “Israeli” agent Antoine Lahd.
The women and children among Monday’s returnees ­ which brought to 889 the number of Lebanese refugees who have returned since the “Israeli” withdrawal ­ were free to go to their home villages.
The seven collaborators, including Fardisi, were taken away by army officers for interrogation.
The returnees included a batch of 16 children, aged between seven and 12, who met their grandparents in Naqoura.
“The parents of the children sent them home because they don’t want them to study in
an “Israeli” environment,” Therese Daoud said as she picked up her four grandchildren. “They want them to study in Lebanese schools.
“Their parents don’t want to return,” she explained. “They’re trying to leave “Israel” and travel to some other countries.”
On Aug. 3, some 1,300 Lebanese children of “Israeli” agents started school in “Israel”, where they were offered a “Lebanese program” of studies in Arabic, It also included seven hours of Hebrew each week to ease their move to the Jewish state.

Red Cross retrieves body of woman shot during pullout…(3)

The International Committee of the Red Cross on Monday repatriated the body of a Lebanese woman who died in “Israel”, where she had been receiving medical treatment after being shot by “Israeli” soldiers during their May withdrawal.
Raymonda Musa, 31, was hit in the back by a bullet May 24 when “Israeli” troops fired in the air to disperse a crowd of terrified Lebanese who were hoping to flee to “Israel” during the hurried withdrawal.
Musa’s parents, brother, sister and daughter, who had all fled to “Israel”, accompanied her body to Lebanon. Musa’s husband, however, remains in Sweden, where he is seeking political asylum for his family, the police said.
Her father, Raymond Musa, 48, said he planned to sue “Israel” for his daughter’s death.
“I had to dig up my daughter with no help from the “Israelis”. They didn’t even give me a coffin for her body,” he said.
“I’ve been pleading for six weeks to return but they (the “Israelis”) have banned me because I file a lawsuit against them and accused them of killing my daughter,” Musa said.
He added that the “Israelis” “even accused me of being a Hizbullah agent.”
“The situation of Lebanese there is a disaster. I used to meet with many of them. It’s better to be in jail than to live in conditions like these,” he said.
“We used to get exaggerated news that the people who returned to Lebanon are having their eyes gouged out and are being beaten and tortured. But all this is lies,” he said
“All Lebanese prefer to return and be jailed in their country but the “Israelis” are not allowing them. They’re not giving them exit permits,” he said.
Musa added that most Lebanese protested against sending their children to “Israeli” schools. “There are families who stage hunger strikes because “Israel” is lying to us.
“I call on all who stayed there to return quickly to Lebanon. I tell you from my home here that the treatment and reception I had were the most honorable,” Musa said.
Funeral services for Musa were held in her native town of Marjayoun.
