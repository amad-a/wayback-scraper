More “Israeli” air and ground violations…(1)

Zionist warplanes at noon Wednesday violated Lebanon's airspace and flew over the areas of Zahrani, Sidon (south) and the capital Beirut where they broke the sound barrier.

Earlier, “Israeli” espionage drones flew overnight over some southern areas.

On the other hand, the “Israeli” occupation army has been trying to establish its occupation of Lebanese territories belonging to the town of Kfarshouba at the Hassan gate through annexing these territories to the occupied Lebanese Shibaa Farms, although the international demarcation which has become to be known as the Blue Line or Larsen Line states that these territories are part of Lebanon.

Reports from the border zone unveiled that the international troops in the area not only ignore this aggression but are also speaking about an "error" that has considered these territories Lebanese.

Al-Manar Television's correspondent Ali Shaib and colleagues from other media who had gone to the area to examine the “Israeli” violation were provoked by the Zionist occupation troops who pointed their arms at them.

Meanwhile, the enemy troops at the Abbad Hilltop proceeded with harassing Lebanese visitors.

“Israeli” soldiers engaged in hand-to-hand fighting with Lebanese citizens who had been trying to near the tomb. UN Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) troops had to interfere in order to put an end to the fight while the occupation troops used a Merkava tank that was positioned at the barb-wired fence near the hilltop with its gun barrel in the direction of the visitors.

Army chief: liberation not accomplished yet…(2)

Army commander Gen Michel Suleiman said liberation of south Lebanon and the Western Beqaa has reunited parts of the country which had been dismembered by a 22-year occupation.

Liberation has not been accomplished yet, for the Shibaa farms and the areas on which the state has voiced reservations are not recovered, Suleiman said during an inspection tour that took him to military units' positions in the south and the Western Beqaa, Sidon's Eastern Suburbs, Jezzine, Mashghara and Jib Jineen..

Furthermore, “Israeli” aggression still threatens our land, nation and stability, he added.

The army chief said, "Whenever the enemy stumbles in its negotiations, we see atmospheres of sectarian projects brought to life in a bid to harm the achievement of liberation and distort the image of national unity.

Maronite bishops council calls for Syrian withdrawal from Lebanon serve “Israel”: Tlass…(3)

“Prime Minister Salim Hoss has expressed concern over rifts raised by the Maronite Bishops Council's statement that criticized the role of Syria in Lebanon and called for the withdrawal of Syrian troops from the country. Lebanese Army Commander General Michel Sleiman reaffirmed continued coordination with the Syrian army and recalled sacrifices made by Syria during the Lebanese civil war. General Sleiman stressed Damascus helped build a strong national army to preserve the state and its institutions. Meanwhile, Syrian Defense Minister Mustafa Tlass said the bishops' statement only served “Israeli” interests. 

“ Prime Minister Salim Hoss warned in an interview with the daily Safir newspaper against continued divisions over the Maronite Bishops' Councils' call for Syria to pullout its troops from Lebanon. Hoss said the debate is taking on a quote "dangerous path", which should be dealt with to preserve political stability in the country. Following a meeting of the highest Maronite Church body last week, headed by Patriarch Cardinal Nasrallah Boutros Sfeir, bishops called on Syria to withdraw troops from Lebanon and accused Damascus of depriving Lebanon of its sovereignty. The statement provoked a storm in Lebanon, with top officials and Muslim spiritual leaders roundly condemning it. Syria also condemned the statement. In an interview with the Liwa'a newspaper, Defense Minister Mustafa Tlas said the bishops' statement only benefits “Israel”, adding Damascus does not intend to interfere in Lebanese affairs despite strong bilateral ties. Tlass said Syria wants Lebanon as a strong and united country, and recalled Damascus's role in liberating southern Lebanon. The Syrian minister said it was the late President Hafez Assad who lobbied endlessly to legitimize resistance attacks against “Israeli” troops in south Lebanon following the Jewish state's 1996 aerial and ground offensive against Lebanon. Tlass also said the bishops should recall that Beirut asked Syria to send troops to Lebanon. Tlass said their aim was to protect the country's Christians. He also stressed, there is a number of security and strategic agreements between Lebanon and Syria, adding everything is open for negotiations through the appropriate channels. He also commented on reports that Sfeir is ready to meet President Bashar Assad when he visits Beirut, saying the Patriarch should meet his spiritual equivalent in Syria since he is not a politician. Sfeir was quoted by his visitors as saying he bears no hostility toward Syria, but wants Damascus and the international community to listen to Lebanese demands. Sfeir told his visitors he will not remain silent and will not be intimidated. The top Maronite cleric said “Israel’s” pullout from Lebanon now paves the way for discussing Syria's role in Lebanon. Syria had accused “Israel” of wanting to undermine relations between the two neighbors "to avenge its defeat in south Lebanon". 

Lebanese parties stress enmity to “Israel”, legitimacy of Syrian presence…(4)

Lebanese parties and forces have underlined adhering to the Lebanese invariable principles stated by the national accord document as for enmity to the Zionist entity and legitimacy of the Syrian presence in Lebanon.

In their meeting at the Baath Party HQ, the parties rejected any attempts to reconsider these invariable positions.

The continued mobilization against the Syrian presence in Lebanon is an attempt to topple the Taif Accord, the parties said in a communiqué, stressing the need to defend all the gains achieved thanks to steadfastness and Resistance.

More militiamen return ­ with bundles of cash…(5)

Another 33 Lebanese, carrying tens of thousands of dollars, returned from “Israel” to Lebanon on Wednesday, with security sources expecting a further 600 to arrive in the next two weeks.
Among the returnees were six former members of the “Israeli”-proxy militia, including a captain from the coastal village of Biyada, the highest ranking militiamen to have returned so far.
The Lebanese were driven from the border in UN buses to Naqoura where the collaborators were arrested by the Army while their families were permitted to return home.
Exactly 200 Lebanese have returned from “Israel” since last Friday. Some 6,000 Lebanese fled there during the “Israeli” troop withdrawal in May.
The exodus of the past week was prompted by the “Israeli” government granting cash payments to all former militiamen willing to return to Lebanon.
According to a security source who watched the Lebanese arrive in Naqoura, the former militia captain carried with him $122,000 in cash. The source said that $45,000 was the captain’s payoff from the “Israeli” government while the remaining $77,000 was to be split between four ex-militiamen who have already returned to Lebanon.
“They’re arriving with huge sums of cash in bundles of $10,000 each,” the source said. “The notes are brand new and so crisp you could shave with the edges.”
The “Israeli” agents handed the sums to family members before being detained by military intelligence officials. The security source said that another 600 Lebanese were expected to return before Oct. 12.
Among those not expected to return are former members of the militia’s security wing. Having imposed a reign of terror on inhabitants of the former occupation zone for over two decades, the former General Security Service (GSS) agents have little choice but to establish new lives elsewhere.
But it seems that some of them have decided to maintain links with Lebanon by resurrecting their old drug smuggling connections.
“Israeli” drug squad police arrested a former GSS agent on Monday along with a serving “Israeli” soldier and a civilian in Kiryat Shemona after finding 4 kilograms of heroin from Lebanon in an apartment, “Israel’s” Haaretz reported.
The GSS agent, now working as a farm laborer in Galilee, was spotted by the drug police receiving a package thrown over the border fence by a Lebanese civilian.
The police tracked the agent back to Kiryat Shemona. The arrests were made as the agent handed the package to the two “Israelis”.
Yitzhak Hai, the head of northern “Israel’s” drug squad, told “Israel” Radio that he expected drug smuggling between Lebanon and “Israel” to increase.
“In our opinion, from an assessment of the situation, as long as the border is quiet and life returns to normal, there will be an increase in the flow or attempts to smuggle drugs from Lebanon to “Israel”,” he said.
Several fortunes were made during the years of occupation by Lebanese and “Israelis” smuggling drugs.
The route extended from the Bekaa to south Lebanon then over the fence into “Israel”.
Ghajar, the isolated Alawite village now divided by the UN-delineated Blue Line, is reputed to have been a favorite meeting point to exchange packages of drugs for bundles of cash.
Drug profits are rumored to have fueled Ghajar’s transformation from a derelict and impoverished cluster of stone houses in 1978 to the pleasant collection of white-washed houses and bougainvillea-lined streets of 2000.
