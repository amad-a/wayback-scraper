New “Israeli” violation: occupation shoots at journalists, wounds civilian…(1)

A Lebanese civilian was slightly wounded in southeast Lebanon Friday when “Israeli” occupation troops fired at journalists across the border.

The incident occurred when three Lebanese journalists, including an Agence France Presse correspondent, drove from the frontier village of Kfarshouba up to the borders of the “Israeli”-occupied Shibaa farms.

As they arrived, three members of a group of some 18 “Israeli” soldiers in three vehicles immediately and without notice fired between their legs, AFP said.

The Lebanese civilian who was accompanying the journalists received light injuries in his leg from bullet splinters and was taken to a nearby clinic for treatment.

On September 12, “Israeli” soldiers fired shots in the air to scare off three Lebanese journalists in the same area adjacent to the Shibaa farms, occupied by "“Israel”" since 1967.

“Israeli” occupation forces retreated from southern Lebanon on May 24 following a 22-year occupation in which dozens of “Israeli” soldiers were killed by Resistance operations.

 

Detainees group says Beirut deployed too soon in South…(2)

“The government was premature in deploying the army into the South without Resolution 425 having been fully implemented by the “Israelis”, the Follow Up Committee for Lebanese Detainees in “Israeli” Prisons claimed on Friday.
“The resolution says there must be an end to the reasons for the conflict, and with 19 detainees still held, there’s still reason for conflict,” committee head Mohammed Safa told a news conference at the Press Syndicate.
Safa was speaking on the occasion of the committee’s eighth anniversary.
The committee is pressing ahead with its responsibilities despite last May’s closure of the Khiam detention center. Its members are disappointed with what they believe is the government’s neglect of their cause, especially following “Israel’s” withdrawal from the South.
“It’s a shame that we should have to organize sit-ins outside the Cabinet headquarters,” Safa said. “These detainees freed the country and Lebanon should treat them like soldiers.”
The committee is also lobbying for the allocation of social services to former detainees.
“My 16-year-old son was killed in 1982 while confronting “Israel”,” said Mariam Saeedeh. “There are 216 who disappeared fighting “Israel”. The government has declared them dead, but the least it could do is label them national martyrs.”
Beirut MP Beshara Merhej however commended the state for adopting the detainee issue as a national cause in 1992. “Before then it was considered an issue for disparate groups.”
Safa plans to focus on the international community and lobbying support in the European Parliament. With a film on the Khiam Prison to be released in January, he said he hoped that Khiam would become as “memorable part of world history” as Auschwitz Nazi concentration camp.

 

28 collaborators return from “Israel”…(3)

Their bags stuffed full of dollar bills courtesy of the “Israeli” government, 118 former “Israeli”-allied militiamen and their families returned to Lebanon on Friday, four months after they fled to “Israel” during the collapse of the occupation zone.
Further batches of ex-militiamen are expected in the coming days, with one security official suggesting that as many as 1,000 former militiamen may ultimately return.
The militia exodus from “Israel” appears to have been prompted by one-time payments of $17,000 by the “Israeli” government to every ex-militiamen willing to return to Lebanon.
Of the 118 returnees, 28 were members of the “Israeli”-proxy militia and two worked in “Israel”. The remainder were family members who were allowed by the Internal Security Forces to return to their homes. The militiamen ­ all of them low-ranking guards, cooks and mechanics ­ were arrested.
Before they took leave of their families, the former militiamen handed their wives bundles of cash for safekeeping.
The returnees arrived at the border at Ras Naqoura at 7am. They were brought by United Nations buses to Naqoura harbor, where some 300 relatives who remained in Lebanon during the “Israeli” withdrawal were waiting to greet them.
Rania Haddad, who was waiting for her uncle to arrive, said he would be better off “in prison in his country than in “Israel”.
“We have a just government and judiciary and I’m sure he’ll be treated right,” she said.
Soldiers and ISF officers allowed relatives 30 minutes to welcome the returnees and give them whatever they needed.
While the women and children were allowed to leave after giving a statement to the present security forces, the 28 collaborators remained behind.
One of the women sat under a tree with her crying child and fed him from a milk bottle inscribed with Hebrew letters.
“The bottle isn’t important,” the woman said, “what’s important is that we’re back and can say that we’ve woken up from a bad dream.”
At the northern corner of the port sat three children with their luggage and toys. The father of Nancy, Douaa and Ali was one of the 28 militiamen who returned.
Ali, 6, complained that “Israeli” children refused to play with him and he resorted to using the bathroom tub as a swimming pool to amuse himself.
Ahmad Abu Sari, 60, hugged his grandchildren and expressed happiness that they returned.
“Thank God the children and their mother (his daughter) came back before it was too late,” he said. “Let my son-in-law serve his time in prison. I don’t mind. What crime did the children commit that they had to be made to learn Hebrew in “Israeli” schools?”
The 28 men were later taken to the army barracks in Tyre while the women and children returned to their villages.

 

Sweden says it doesn’t want to take in collaborators…(4)

“Sweden is the latest in a growing list of countries to reject a request by “Israel” to take in dozens of its collaborators.
Swedish Ambassador Anne Dismorr confirmed on Friday that her government had rejected a request to accept “Israeli”-allied militiamen made by “Israeli” Cabinet Minister Haim Ramon during a visit to Stockholm.
“There’s no basis for asylum,” Dismorr said. “They don’t have any need for protection... they’re under no threat in “Israel”.”
Ramon had asked asylum for some 50-60 agents with relatives already living in Sweden.
Around 6,000 collaborators fled to “Israel” when the occupation zone collapsed in May. “Israel” has been attempting to resettle the its agents in third countries.
Most European countries have expressed reluctance to house the refugees, saying that “Israel” was the “country of first entry” for the refugees and, as a safe haven for the former militiamen, there is no need to seek their resettlement.
Germany is the only country so far to have accepted a batch of refugees, taking in 400 at the beginning of August. Australia has accepted 200 and Chile agreed to take in 20.
European nations are concerned that suspected war criminals could be included in any group of agents offered by “Israel”. The “Israeli” government has refused to provide background details of the militiamen awaiting emigration.
The Daily Star revealed last month that the Canadian government has compiled a list of former militiamen suspected of war crimes ­ all officials at the infamous Khiam detention center ­ and has circulated the list to friendly countries.
According to Swedish press reports, Ramon was unable to detail the refugees’ activities during their time with the militia but assured the Swedish government that none could be regarded as war criminals.
Sweden already hosts several ex-members of the militia, including Assad Sayed, a former interrogator at Khiam. Sayed, originally from Qlaya, achieved notoriety among the inmates of Khiam for his brutal methods during interrogations. He has lived in Sweden since 1992, when he quit the militia.
