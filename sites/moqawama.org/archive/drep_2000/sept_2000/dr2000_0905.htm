Introduction

Hizbullah’s Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah and a UN security envoy held talks on Monday over ways to secure the releae of Lebanese hostages held in “Israel” and retrieve the bodies of resistance martyrs.

UN, Nasrallah discuss detainees…(1)

”Hizbullah’s Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah and a UN security envoy held talks on Monday over ways to secure the release of Lebanese detainees held in “Israel” and retrieve the bodies of fallen fighters.
Benon Sevan, UN undersecretary-general and security coordinator, said the world body was working with all concerned to free the 16 remaining detainees.
“This is an issue we (want) to pursue,” said Sevan. “Therefore I have come here to resume our activities in this regard.”
Sevan, with UNIFIL press assistant Hassan Siqlawi, met Nasrallah at his offices in the southern suburbs of Beirut.
Sevan said UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan was expending great efforts to seek the release of detainees in “Israeli” prisons and the return of the bodies of resistance fighters.


“Israel” revisits claims of ‘super Katyusha’…(2)

“An “Israeli” general claimed on Monday that Hizbullah has long-range rockets capable of hitting the coastal city of Haifa and suggested that retaliation for any cross-border attack from Lebanon would extend to Syrian forces.
Fired from the border, rockets with a range of 100 kilometers could hit several main “Israeli” cities, including Haifa, Tiberias, Netanya and Herzliya, which is just north of Tel Aviv.
Even without launching a rocket attack, guerrillas could “throw hand grenades at the yellow buses” carrying “Israeli” schoolchildren or fire rifles at “Israeli” homes, he added.
They have the capability of starting an attack “in a matter of seconds,” Ashkenazi said.
The “Israeli” general warned that if the guerrillas attack, “Israel” would hit back not only “at those who pull the trigger.”
“We will deal also with those who allow it,” he said, a clear reference to Syria, which supported resistance efforts to oust “Israel” from its occupation zone in the South.
Since at least 1996, “Israel” has regularly claimed that Hizbullah is equipped with long-range rockets. Initially, the “Israeli” Army said that the resistance group possessed longer-range versions of the 122mm Katyusha rocket presently in Hizbullah’s armory. The larger version travels twice the distance of the standard rocket, the “Israeli” Army says.
Since the beginning of the year, “Israel” has been claiming that Hizbullah has acquired Iranian Fajar rockets with a range of at least 70 kilometers.
Ashkenazi complained that Hizbullah was behind the daily bouts of stone-throwing across the border, particularly at the Fatima Gate in Kfar Kila.
“They bring stones to the border in trucks and children to the border in buses” to throw the stones, he claimed.
He condemned UNIFIL for failing to stop the incidents of stone-throwing, arguing that the peacekeepers’ mandate requires the force to maintain calm along the frontier.
Though his soldiers are under orders to show restraint, he said, he sees this as a temporary situation, not to be tolerated for long.
Ashkenazi also speculated that Hizbullah might launch attacks across the border if a large-scale confrontation between “Israel” and the Palestinians broke out.
As head of “Israel’s” northern military command, Ashkenazi played an important role in the occupation of south Lebanon and was responsible for overseeing the troop withdrawal.
According to “Israeli” press reports, senior officers in the northern command and the “Israeli” Army’s general staff were furious at “Israeli” Prime Minister Ehud Barak’s decision to unilaterally withdraw from south Lebanon. ­


Arab League backs right to reparations…(3)

”The Arab League urged the international community on Monday to pressure “Israel” into making financial reparations for its 22-year occupation of Lebanon.
The Cairo-based organization issued its plea at the end of the two-day biannual meeting of foreign ministers. The meetings began on Sunday in the Egyptian capital.
“The League asserts Lebanon’s right to demand reparations for the vast human, financial and economic losses it sustained due to the repeated “Israeli” aggression on citizens and infrastructure,” the ministers said in a statement.
The ministers also warned that the continued presence of some 370,000 Palestinian refugees in Lebanon will remain a contentious internal issue which could impede a regional peace deal.
“If the refugee issue is not resolved it will shake up security and stability, as well as hurt chances of securing a just and comprehensive peace in the region,” the statement said.
The League said “all necessary measures” should be made to force “Israel” to free Lebanese hostages still in “Israeli” jails.
The ministers also underscored Lebanon’s claim to sovereignty over the disputed Shebaa Farms, as well as its right to liberate “every inch” of “Israeli”-occupied territory.
“We condemn “Israel” for its continued occupation of border posts, including the Shebaa Farms,” the statement said.
“Lebanon retains the right to use all legal means to liberate its land,” it added.
The League also called on Arab countries to fulfil financial aid pledges they made at the last Arab summit in 1996, and pledge cash to aid Lebanon’s reconstruction.
Foreign Ministry Director-General Zuheir Hamdan, who delivered Lebanon’s address to the assembly, said the government was adhering to three main issues: release of all “Israeli”-held detainees, the right of Palestinian refugees to return to their homeland, and the removal of three “Israeli” posts along the UN-demarcated Blue Line.


Jobran Tueini interviewed by the enemy radio?…(4)

The “Israeli” enemy's radio said it had interviewed the editor-in-chief of the Lebanese daily an-Nahar, Jobran Tueini, over the results of the general elections in Lebanon.

In the interview which was received in Lebanon, Tueini said Damascus was not very enthusiastic to interfere in these elections although its troops are on the ground and in spite of the fact that some of its apparatuses interfered.

Al-Manar television tried to telephone Tueini to ask him about the interview which is in contrast with Lebanese laws of boycotting the “Israeli” enemy, something that holds him accountable before Lebanese courts.  Tueini was not available, however, and the concerned in the daily failed to provide his mobile phone number.
