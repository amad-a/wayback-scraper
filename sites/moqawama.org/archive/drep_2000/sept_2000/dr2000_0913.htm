Hoss promises to air grievances before UN General Assembly…(1)

“Prime Minister Salim Hoss will tell the United Nations General Assembly on Thursday that Palestinians cannot stay in Lebanon and will also convey the country’s reservations over the UN’s Blue Line.
Hoss left the country on Tuesday for the United Nations in New York, where he will address the 55th session of the UN General Assembly.
In a statement made at the airport prior to his departure, Hoss reiterated that in his  address he would affirm Lebanon’s rejection to resettle Palestinian refugees on its territory and stress the need for them to return to their homeland.
He said he would also raise the country’s reservations over the Blue Line drawn by the UN to mark the country’s border with “Israel”.”
The marker demarcates the line behind which “Israeli” forces were required to withdraw to implement Security Council Resolution 425. Lebanon has argued that the UN is allowing “Israel” to remain on Lebanese territory in three areas: between Metulla and the Hasbani Bridge, Misgav Am near Adaisseh, and in a wadi south of Yaroun.
Other topics the premier will touch upon include Lebanon’s demand to restore its sovereignty over the Shebaa Farms, which “Israel” considers part of its territory. The UN has not recognized Lebanon’s right to the land.
He will also emphasize Lebanon’s need for international backing to carry out development and reconstruction projects, especially in the South.
Hoss said that while in New York he would use the opportunity to discuss a number of other international matters of interest to Lebanon with heads of participating delegations. He is also expected to meet UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan.
The premier reiterated that Lebanon was keen to hold a conference of donor countries in Beirut in the near future, possibly next month, to raise money for its development and reconstruction plans.

Occupation intransigent on Lebanese hostages, fears abduction for swap…(2)

The Zionist entity has once again expressed its fear of Hizbullah's abducting “Israeli” soldiers or colonizers in a bid to secure the release of Lebanese prisoners in “Israeli” detention centers, including Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid and Hajj Abu Ali Mustafa al-Dirani.

Shaul Mofaz, the enemy's chief of staff, said he insists that Obeid and Dirani must be kept in prison not only for the return of “Israeli” pilot Ron Arab, but also due to their contribution in "terrorist activities" besides underlining "“Israel’s”" insistence that all “Israeli” troops who had gone missing must be returned.

What Mofaz failed to say was that his entity has impossible conditions to release the 20 Lebanese hostages and that it has dismissed all international mediations to close this issue.

“Israeli” soldiers fire to scare off Lebanese journalists…(3)

“ “Israeli” soldiers fired shots in the air on Tuesday, apparently to scare off three Lebanese journalists including an AFP correspondent, who were taking pictures at the two countries' borders.

"They fired 17 shots in the air when we tried to take pictures of a team of experts carrying maps near Kfarshouba," said AFP correspondent Taher Abou Hamdan.

The other two correspondents were Dureid Kabboura from the al-Anwar newspaper and Hussein Hadifa from the al-Liwa'a daily, said Abou Hamdan.

"We were not scared. We remained standing and told them we were on Lebanese soil and that we did not have to leave," he said.

"A number of Hizbullah members came to see what had happened and then we saw the “Israeli” soldiers and the team of experts going back some 200 meters (yards) away from the border," he said.

The situation along the “Israeli”-Lebanese borders remains tense since the Jewish state's May 24 pullout from southern Lebanon after 22 years of occupation.

“Hizbullah, which had spearheaded the guerrilla war against the “Israeli” occupation troops, took up positions on the frontier afterwards.

But the Lebanese army has not deployed along the frontier, although it is present in the southern region along with contingents of United Nations peacekeepers.

Lebanese Woman Convicted of Torture…(4)

“ A military court Tuesday convicted and sentenced a woman to seven years in jail and hard labor for torturing two inmates at a notorious prison run by former “Israeli”-allied militiamen in southern Lebanon.

Georgette Jirjis al-Tawili was one of 18 former pro-”Israeli” militiamen who were sentenced by the court to prison terms of up to three years for collaborating with “Israel” during its occupation of southern Lebanon, the court said in a judgment posted on its bulletin board.

The court acquitted four other defendants of collaboration charges.

The defendants were all members of the “Israeli”-backed militia that collapsed as “Israel” withdrew its troops from south Lebanon on May 24, ending 22 years of occupation.

The prison in the southern Lebanese village of Khiam where Tawili worked gained notoriety for torture and for long detentions of opponents of “Israel’s” occupation. Human rights groups had long denounced conditions at Khiam.

Tawili was also barred from returning to her hometown in south Lebanon for 10 years after completion of her sentence, the court added. Such prohibitions are intended to protect the convicts from possible revenge attacks by anti-”Israeli” guerrillas.

The 18 defendants were also fined up to $666, the court statement said. They were the 27th batch of alleged collaborators to appear before the court since the “Israeli” withdrawal.

Lebanon, which is technically at war with “Israel”, prohibits any contacts with the Jewish state.

Some 2,200 members of the militia, which once numbered 2,500 fighters, surrendered to Lebanese authorities or were captured after “Israel” withdrew. The rest, mostly ranking militia officers, fled to “Israel” with their families.

The military court, whose verdicts cannot be appealed, has sentenced more than 1,000 people to jail terms of up to 15 years since the trials of collaborators began on June 5.

Two dozen more Lebanese refugees return home from “Israel”…(5)

“ Twenty-four Lebanese who had fled to “Israel” following its troop withdrawal from southern Lebanon in May returned home overnight, police said Tuesday.

Eight men, five women and 11 children were taken in buses of the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) into this town on the Mediterranean coast and turned over to Lebanese police, they said.

Among the men were three former members of the now-defunct “Israeli”-allied militia who were immediately detained, they said.

The other five men where kept for questioning while the women and children were allowed to return to their homes in the villages of Qlayaa and Rmeich, in the former “Israeli”-occupied border zone in south Lebanon.

Some 6,500 Lebanese followed the “Israeli” army as it withdrew from Lebanon in May.

According to police figures, about 600 of those who fled to “Israel” have returned to Lebanon.

More than 2,200 Lebanese are in the process of being tried before the military court in Beirut, or have already been convicted, for collaboration with “Israel” or simply having "contact with the “Israeli” enemy."

Anti-landmine conference opens in Geneva…(6)

The latest victim of “Israeli” landmines was a thirteen-year-old boy from the southern village of Kafra. He suffered severe wounds to his arm and head. There has been a steady stream of landmine casualties in the zone “Israel” occupied despite efforts to spread awareness about the dangers of explosives left behind by “Israel’s” occupation. An international conference taking place in Switzerland is trying to persuade governments that have not signed a treaty aimed at banning the use of mines to do so. Beirut had said it would only sign the treaty when “Israel” which planted at least a hundred thousand mines in Lebanon endorses the pact. 

“The campaign to rid the world of landmines is proving successful but pressure needs to be kept up on governments according to the pressure group wanting to ban them. Jody Williams who won the Nobel Prize as head of the Campaign to ban Landmines said more needs to be done about mine action and assistance to victims. (Williams saying: "It is pretty clear that the general trend continued to be positive in the global movement to get rid of landmines. Production is down from 55 countries to 16"). Signatory states to the 1997 Mine Ban Treaty have been meeting in Geneva to review progress and discuss the problems of implementation. So far, 138 governments have signed up to the Treaty and another 101 have ratified it. The treaty prohibits any use, production, trade or stockpiling of anti-personnel mines. A giant hour-glass placed outside the assembly room of the Palais de Nations is turning every 22 minutes- the time between landmine incidents- to remind meeting delegates of the urgency of the landmine crisis. The crisis has hit the region “Israel” occupied in south Lebanon for 22 years. Lebanese police say at least nine people have died and 63 other have been injured in explosions of “Israeli” landmines and unexploded shells since the “Israeli” withdrawal from the south Lebanon in May. UN officers estimate at least 50,000 mines were planted, most by “Israel” and its allied militia. Lebanese authorities are warning people of the danger of mines and unexploded shells in the former occupied zone. The first casualties started coming in just as “Israel” withdrew from the south and thousands of people flooded the region to celebrate. The Lebanese army and UN peacekeepers are clearing the mines in the area which amounts to more than 10 percent of Lebanese territory. The effort is hindered by the difficult terrain, lack of maps and limited funding. UN peacekeepers have accused “Israel” of providing inaccurate maps for landmines occupation troops planted in the south. Beirut estimated that some 8.6 million dollars are needed to clear southern Lebanon of mines.
