“Israelis” beat civilians at Sheikh Abbad tomb…(1)

A Lebanese civilian was beaten by “Israeli” soldiers at the border on Sheikh Abbad hill after he attempted to remove a plaque inscribed in Hebrew, witnesses claimed on Friday.
The sources, a squad of Hizbullah observers on Abbad, said that Ghanaian UNIFIL soldiers positioned at the summit of the hill failed to intervene.
UNIFIL said it had in no knowledge of such an incident.
The witnesses said the assault occurred on Thursday evening when Mahmoud Abdel-Rahman Sabbagh, 26, was hit several times with a rifle butt when he and other civilians tried to remove two stone plaques fastened to a wall on the Lebanese side of the border.
The border runs along the summit of the hill and bisects an ancient tomb revered by Muslims and Jews. “Israeli” soldiers are stationed to the east of the tomb in front of a new outpost.
Sabbagh reportedly received bruises to his back and shoulder.
The witnesses said that the Ghanaians had attempted to stop the Lebanese from removing the plaques but gave up when Sabbagh and his comrades refused to leave. The peacekeepers moved away from the hilltop shrine to their post 50 meters away, allowing the “Israeli” soldiers to step across the unfenced border and beat Sabbagh, the witnesses said.
On Friday, civilians were allowed to roam the hilltop and were not prevented from approaching the border by Ghanaian UNIFIL troops.
UNIFIL Spokesman Timur Goksel said he had received “no reports of any such activity occurring at Abbad.”
In a separate incident, “Israeli” bulldozers reportedly snuck across the border near Abbad and dumped rubble and garbage on the Lebanese side.


Top south Lebanon militiaman sentenced to prison…(2)

“The brother of “Israel’s” first ally in southern Lebanon was handed down a one-year-jail term for collaborating with the Jewish state during its twenty-two year occupation. Eleven members of “Israel’s” now-defunct proxy militia, including Labib Haddad, were sentenced to jail terms ranging from three weeks to fifteen years. Labib's brother Saad Haddad was the first Lebanese to publicly open contacts with “Israel” in 1976 and led militias that eventually formed the core of “Israel’s” proxy force in the occupied strip. His family was among the six thousand militiamen and their relatives who retreated to the Jewish state after occupation troops withdrew in May. Beirut's military court has sentenced nine hundred and fifty people to prison terms ranging from three weeks to the death sentence in absentia for collaborating with “Israel”. Some 2,200 militiamen have surrendered to the Lebanese authorities. Beirut has urged those in “Israel” to return and face fair trials.
