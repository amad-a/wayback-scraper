No Mideast peace unless Palestinians go home: Lebanese president…(1)

“ Lebanese President Emile Lahoud said Friday there will be no peace in the Middle East unless the Palestinian refugees return to their homeland, the official ANI news agency reported.

He made the remark in front of a British parliamentary delegation investigating the refugee situation in the Middle East.

"Lebanon is by no means concerned by current proposals on differentiating between the refugees of 1948 and those of 1967 or to allow a limited number of them to return for family unification or material lures to keep them in the host countries," he said.

The constitution of Lebanon says the refugees may not stay here permanently. The presence of 350,000 Palestinian refugees upsets this country's delicate ethnic and religious balance.

Earlier Friday, several dozen Palestinians gathered in front of the UN headquarters in Beirut and handed in a petition signed by 40,000 people and calling for secretary general Kofi Annan to proclaim their right to go home.

Fadlallah cautioned against yielding to “Israel”: blackmail…(2)

The leading Islamic cleris Sayyed Mohammad Hussein Fadlallah cautioned Palestinians, and Arabs and Muslims generally, against yielding to ““Israeli” blackmail” on the sovereignty of Jerusalem.
He said Jerusalem was “the holiest cause linked to religion, history and the future and its downfall is tantamount to the nation’s downfall.”
Referring to what he called repeated “Israeli” threats to commit a “large-scale aggression against Syria and Lebanon” if “Israel” were attacked, he said: “The “Israeli” enemy doesn’t have the initiative” to do so.
““Israel” knows that the threat to commit an armed aggression is double-edged,” the cleric warned, “and that the resistance movement possessed sophisticated, long-range weapons with which to retaliate. Destruction faces both sides.”
He also underlined the need for national unity and the importance of an “economic revolution” to ensure prosperity for the poor.

Zionists want to settle collaborators in occupied al-Quds…(3)

An “Israeli” daily affirmed the reports aired by the enemy radio that the “Israeli” entity is seeking to settle groups of the Lahdi collaborators in occupied al-Quds (Jerusalem).

The Jerusalem Post said al-Quds Mayor Ehud Olmert discussed the issue with the Maronite Metropolitan in occupied Palestine Paul Sayyah.

The case would allow around three thousand members of the militiamen or their families to reside in the holy city.

A final decision is due following the return of the enemy's prime minister Ehud Barak from the US.

The reason behind settling the collaborators and their families in al-Quds was clear from what Olmert said to Likud Knesset member Ayoub Qara.

There is no doubt that the Christian community in Jerusalem needs to be strengthened, Olmert said.

Al-Quds residents fear an “Israeli” unrest plot that aims to exploit these collaborators to provoke sectarian sentiments between Moslems and Christians after the militiamen failed in achieving their task in South Lebanon.

Palestinians deliver petition to UN over right of return…(4)

“ Several dozen Palestinians gathered in front of the UN headquarters in Beirut and handed in a petition signed by 40,000 people for the secretary general to proclaim their right to return to their country.

"The Palestinians of Lebanon are today more than ever attached to their sacred right of return to their country and refuse any concession on that front whatever the circumstances," said Ali Faisal, of the political office of the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine, which organised the rally.

He said the petition was signed by members of the Palestine Liberation Organisation's parliament, the Palestine National Council; leading academics, professionals; and others and calls for "the right to compensation, an inalienable right within the right of return."

The memorandum was addressed to Kofi Annan on the eve of the meeting of the PLO's Central Council, which acts on behalf of the National Council.

It reaffirms "the right of the Palestinian people to establish its independent state, to self-determination and to return," stressing that "no problem can be solved at the expense of another."

He also expressed the determination of the signatories to consider east Jerusalem as the capital of an independent Palestinian state.

There are some 350,000 Palestinian refugees in Lebanon, and the vast majority of them were originally from the Palestinian territories on which the state of “Israel” was set up in 1948.

Nearly half of them live in miserable conditions in a dozen camps spread around the different regions of Lebanon.

At Ain al-Hilweh, the largest camp in Lebanon in Sidon, 100 supporters of the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine staged a sit-in to call on the Central Council not to sell off their right of return.

They also demanded the immediate proclamation of an independent state with east Jerusalem as its capital.

The 129 council members are due to meet Saturday and Sunday to decide on whether to stick to the September 13 date for the declaration or to postpone it.
