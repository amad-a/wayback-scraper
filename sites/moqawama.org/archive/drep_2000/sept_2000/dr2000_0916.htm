Mad’ “Israelis”…(1)

“Israel” plans to instruct “mad” fighter pilots to bomb Al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem, a key Hizbullah official said Friday.
“The enemy wants to wipe out the mosque, then claim that the pilots who did it went mad,” Sheikh Mohammed Yazbek, Lebanon’s representative of Iran’s supreme spiritual leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, said during a sermon at the Imam Ali Mosque in Baalbek.
Yazbek said his allegations were based on “reports” he had received but did not elaborate on where they originated.
“Didn’t the enemy say that the “Israeli” who gunned down worshippers at the Ibrahimi Mosque went mad?” he asked, referring to the 1994 killing of 29 Muslims at a mosque in Hebron by an “Israeli” zealot who was beaten to death by other worshippers.
“Jews, suffering from megalomania, have no respect for the rights of others,” said Yazbek. “The proof is their usurpation of Palestine and the massacres they have carried out ever since.”

Contacts to secure release of Lebanese hostages from “Israeli” prisons…(2)

Reports in Beirut said contacts are being conducted to secure the release of Lebanese detainees in the “Israeli” occupation's prisons.

The contacts, which include the UN, the International Committee for the Red Cross (ICRC) and other channels, are taking place away from the media. They also include the dossiers of the prisoners and martyrs who have gone missing during the “Israeli” occupation of south Lebanon and the Western Beqaa.

“Israeli” occupation offers money to collaborators who go home…(3)

The “Israeli” occupation's government is offering its collaborators soldiers and their families, up to $20,000 if they leave "“Israel”" and return to Lebanon, a spokeswoman for the “Israeli” so-called rehabilitation administration confirmed Friday.

Some 4,700 former “Israeli”-allied militiamen, members of the defunct “Israeli”-backed civil administration there, and their relatives, are in occupied Palestine now. They left their homes in a hurry in May when the “Israeli” occupation forces retreated from southern
Lebanon ending a 22-year occupation.

The spokeswoman said dozens of families would like to return before the school year begins there in mid-October.

“Israel” is offering singles $12,000 to go home, a couple $15,000 and $1,000 more for each child up to a ceiling of $20,000 a family, the spokeswoman said.

Etienne Sakr, one of the chief collaborators who had fled to occupied Palestine and leader of the a militia called Guardians of the Cedars, said the offer was an attempt to encourage the Lebanese to leave.

The refugees were given four days to make up their minds though they could leave until October 22, he said.

Some wanted to return before the school year begins. However returnees are risking trials and jail sentences that could eat up those $20,000, he said.

"They are exposing them, which is very bad....This is not the way you deal with your ally that was faithful to you for 25 years," Sakr said. "They (the refugees) have been humiliated once (when they had to flee) and are humiliated again. They deserve dignity more than money."

The Rehabilitation Administration's spokeswoman denied "“Israel”" was encouraging the refugees to leave.

More than 650 refugees have returned to Lebanon and 400 have gone to Germany. "“Israel”" is funding their first year in Germany, the spokeswoman said.

Premier: powers are starting to hear us
Hoss reiterated Beirut’s reservations about UN-delineated blue line…(4)

Prime Minister Salim Hoss said Friday that world powers understood that Lebanon cannot bear a Middle East peace that leaves tens of thousands of Palestinian refugees on its soil.
“Lebanon has achieved some progress on the international scene regarding its rejection of the permanent settlement of Palestinian refugees on its territories,” he said. “There are signals from world powers that any peace can’t be achieved at Lebanon’s expense.”
Speaking to UN Radio in New York, Hoss, who is representing Lebanon at the UN General Assembly’s 55th session, reiterated Beirut’s reservations about the UN-delineated Blue Line used to verify “Israel’s” pullout.
“Lebanon insists that the Blue Line does not coincide with its international border,” he said, stressing that “so far, we can’t say that UN Security Council Resolution 425, which calls for “Israel’s” full withdrawal from Lebanon, has been implemented.”
Lebanon believes that the Blue Line deviates from the international border into its territories in three areas, the largest of which includes the western part of “Israel’s” Misgav Am border settlement.
Hoss said Lebanon also wants the Shebaa Farms returned.

“It’s almost certain that there will be no progress on the Lebanese and Syrian tracks of the peace talks, or the Palestinian track, before the US presidential election,” he added.
With respect to the conference of donor countries, called for by Lebanon to raise funds for a $1.2 billion government plan to re-develop the South, he said: “No final date has been set for the meeting, but we expect the meeting to be held on Oct. 9 and Oct. 10.”
Hoss later held separate talks with UN special envoy Terje-Roed Larsen, who said: “We discussed the probability of an upcoming donor conference. We’ve agreed to work on this issue and in particular to try to use a major conference of non-governmental organizations on Sept. 28 in Beirut to move this issue forward.
“The United Nations, the European Union and other donors and institutions ­ we’ll do our very best in order to alleviate the situation for the people in the South,” he promised.
Hoss also met with EU Middle East envoy Miguel-Angel Moratinos, who said: “We discussed the plight of the Lebanese in the South and the future of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon and how to secure peace and stability in the entire region.”

Italian MPs voice support for Palestinians…(5)

Italian parliamentarians voiced their support on Friday for the right of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon to return to their homes with East Jerusalem as the capital of their future state.
“Italy supports the Palestinian people in this respect,” said Guillielmo Lentio, head of the Italian Parliament’s Foreign Affairs Committee.
Lentio spoke during a news conference in Sidon’s Ain al-Hilweh refugee camp. He and more than 200 Italian human-rights activists, MPs and journalists are in Lebanon to take part in commemorative ceremonies for the 1982 massacre by “Israeli”-backed Christian militiamen of some 1,000 Palestinians and Lebanese at the refugee camps of Sabra and Shatila.
Accompanied by representatives of Italian non-governmental organizations and several journalists, the parliamentarians were received by the Alliance of Palestinian Forces and officials with the Popular Committees that run the camp. They toured schools and clinics run in the camp by the United Nations Relief and Works Agency, which provides social and health services to refugees.
The MPs met with Palestinians who explained the economic and social problems they face as a result of shrinking financial aid by donor countries to UNRWA.
Palestinian President’s Yasser Arafat’s Fatah Movement held a reception for the delegates, attended by some 5,000 refugees.
“Palestinians in Lebanon reject permanent settlement outside their homeland. They want to return to their homes and to determine their fate,” said Fathi Abul-Arwat, head of Fatah’s department for labor affairs.
Louisa Morgantini, a European Union parliamentarian and head of Italy’s International Peace Association, said: “We’re here to remember the 1982 massacre, which no one in the world should forget.”

Detainee’s mother urges action…(6)

Jamila Yassin, the mother of Anwar Yassin who has been held by “Israel” since 1987, said on Friday that she feared dying without seeing her son again.
“I’m not worried about my son being in prison, I know my son is a hero and will survive. I’m worried for myself because I’m old and sick and because I might not have the chance to see my son before I die,” she said.
Speaking on the 13th anniversary of her son’s detention in an “Israeli” prison, Yassin expressed disappointment with the “official” handling of the detainees’ file and urged Lebanese and international authorities to assume their responsibilities and put an end to the tragedy.
“Has the government forgotten the heroes responsible for the liberation of the land? They are human beings and are more important than land,” she said.
Anwar Yassin, who is now 34 years old, was wounded and captured during a confrontation with “Israeli” forces which lasted over 20 hours. In 1988, an “Israeli” court sentenced him to 30 years in prison.
Mohammed Safa, the head of the Follow Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in “Israeli” Prisons, called on the government to take more efficient action concerning the detainees’ release.
“The detainee issue should not be a bargaining card. We won’t accept those heroes becoming victims of countries’ regional and international interests,” Safa said. He added that the liberation could not be considered complete as long as the prisoners were still held by “Israel”.
“It’s no longer sufficient for the Lebanese government to ask for the release of the detainees in a memo or a speech,” Safa said.

They mourn the victims the world has forgotten

On a rubbish-strewn field the survivors remember as the people without a land visit the mass grave of hundreds of their relatives…(8)

By Robert Fisk in the Sabra and Chatila camps, Beirut

16 September 2000

They are bulldozing the garbage from the small plot of land behind the plane trees of Sabra and Chatila. Cheap concrete blocks are being laid to form a makeshift dais for speakers. A black flag has been draped by the wall to remind the Palestinians – as if they could forget – that here lies the mass grave of hundreds of their relatives. Up to 400 corpses lie beneath the soft red earth. Up to 2,000 died. But only now, 18 years after the majzara, the massacre, has the atrocity been commemorated.

Of course, if it had happened in a land to the south of here – a land called “Israel”, which the Palestinians of Sabra and Chatila still call Palestine – the world's press would gather each year at this time to honour the dead. Mercifully, “Israel” has never suffered such a slaughter. But if it had, the American president would surely make an annual pilgrimage to the site. Certainly, every US presidential hopeful would turn up. Relatives of the dead would be treated with reverence and generosity.

The mass grave of Sabra and Chatila, however, contains only Palestinians. Until last week it served as both football ground for youths and a garbage tip. Probably, in a few days' time, it will become a rubbish dump again. And the survivors of this most awful of weekends fester on in their hot concrete huts, unvisited by journalists or statesmen. Whenever I write about the massacre at this time, I always say that The Independent will be the only British newspaper to remember the anniversary. Sadly, so far, I have been right. A people without a country are a people without honour.

The facts of what happened between 16 and 18 September 1982 are not in doubt – save for the guilty and the insane. On 16 September, the “Israeli” army surrounded the adjoining Palestinian refugee camps of Sabra and Chatila and sent their Lebanese Christian Phalangist militia allies into them to hunt for "terrorists". There were none. Just civilians, whom the Phalangists murdered and raped in homes and backyards and streets, slaughtering up to 2,000 civilians, in some cases watched by “Israelis” from rooftops. Only on the 18th did “Israel” order its allies out of the camp. When I entered Sabra and Chatila early on the 18th – before the murderers had left – it was already a charnel house.

The Lebanese president-elect, Bashir Gemayel, had just been murdered. The Phalangists were in vengeful mood. Yet Menachem Begin, then “Israel’s” Prime Minister, was to claim he never imagined there would be a bloodbath. The “Israeli” Kahan commission report – which bleakly confirmed that “Israeli” troops saw the massacre taking place and did nothing – condemned Ariel Sharon, then Defence (War) Minister, for indirect responsibility. Mr Sharon is now leader of the right-wing opposition Likud party. He could yet become prime minister of “Israel”.

So this little patch of rotten earth, these few square miles of fetid huts and lanes, should attract at least a little attention at this time of year. The survivors are still here, many of them, still mourning their dead, still sweltering in their bullet-holed concrete slums. Mahmoud Ayoub is 73 now but remembers the day of the massacre as if it happened only a few hours ago.

"We knew the “Israelis” were circling the camp and I saw several men in Phalangist uniform wearing masks. Neighbours told me they were killing, but I didn't believe it because I heard no shooting. Then they said that the Phalangists were using knives and I believed them. My son Sobhi and I collected all the extended members of our family we could find – 65 in all – and we led them to the far side of the camp from their homes at night. We saved them all."

The subterranean cemetery is still there, a gruesome, dark place of faded "martyrs" pictures and cement grave markers, stinking of sewage.

Mr Ayoub married twice but his second wife, Hayat – 60 now, but only 42 at the time – lost at least 10 of her family in 1982. "My nephew, Mustapha Habrat, was hiding with the women and children in one of the air-raid bunkers we had built here," she says. "The Phalangists came and took all the men out to execute them. Before he was taken away, Mustapha took off his ring and gave it to his wife, Amal, saying, 'You will be able to sell this and get money for you and the children for a couple of days'. Then he was taken away and they shot all the men. But Mustapha, though he was hit many times, lay covered in blood and pretended to be dead."

The story – like all the stories in Sabra and Chatila – gets worse. "While he lay there, gypsies came to loot the bodies, to take the rings and the watches from the dead. When they tried to rob Mustapha, they found he was alive and managed to get him to the hospital."

Saved by those who would pillage his dead body, Mustapha searched in vain for his wife and three children. They had disappeared, were never seen again. Like many of the dead of Sabra and Chatila, they may lie under the golf course near Beirut airport, a mass grave that has never been – and never will be – opened.

The Ayoub family came from the Palestinian village of Majdeqrun in Gallilee. As one of the Arab fighters, Mr Ayoub was among the last to leave for exile in Lebanon in 1948. Some of his family stayed behind and are now “Israeli” Arabs; and old Mahmoud Ayoub was allowed by the “Israelis” to visit them in 1989. "I found our old home and wept," he said. "I went into the house – the “Israelis” had rented it to an Arab woman from another village – and I walked through my family's olive groves. I even went to Jerusalem and prayed at Al Aqsa. But I knew all the time that, in a month, I would have to leave."

Mrs Ayoub remains locked on to the moment of the massacre. "I was one of the first to come back and I found the streets heaped with bodies. I took pills to steady my nerves and went into each house. In some, the women were lying dead with their legs far apart. There was much rape. The men were lying on the floor in their blood. It was very hot. The bodies were rotting."

I don't tell Hayat Ayoub that I saw all this myself, the dead and the mutilated and the raped. They had even shot the horses in the camp.

And the lesson of all this? Mahmoud Ayoub does not believe there will ever be an independent sovereign state of Palestine. He doesn't trust Yasser Arafat and he doesn't believe he will ever return to live in his home. He blames the Americans and the Europeans and he is angered at the number of countries who refuse Palestinians the right to visit Europe.

"The Palestinians are like germs," he says. "No one wants us – because we don't have a country."
