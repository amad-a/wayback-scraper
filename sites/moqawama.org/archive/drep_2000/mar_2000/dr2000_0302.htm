Daily Report

March Thursday 02-2000:


Introduction

The "Israeli"-proxy militia suffered its highest fatality toll in a single incident for years on Wednesday when five militiamen were perished in an ambush near Ain Qinya position deep inside the occupied zone in addition to wounding three others, two seriously, in another attack staged by the Islamic Resistance (IR) men against Tair Harfa outpost. The news from inside the zone described the militia's reaction to the attacks made by IR as Choatic, adding fear has gripped the proxy-force.

In a clear violation, however, the "Israeli" occupying troops pounded several areas in South Lebanon wounding a child and damaging six houses.


IR kills 5 collaborators and wounds three others

Militia's worst death toll in resistance operations
In a new qualitative achievement,  in the framework of its chasing for the Zionist enemy and its collaborators - traitors of their country and people, and in spite of the complicated tactics and measures taken recently by the enemy, particularly in the eastern sector of the occupied zone, the IR team of martyrs Hussein Mazloum and Ali Medlej in the Special Forces Unit was able, yesterday, at 4:45, to penetrate these tactics and measures and to ambush a Lahdi force between the position and town of Ain Qinya. As the jeep car, carrying six "Israeli" agents, reached to the scene, the mujahedeen rushed upon it with their machine guns and hand grenades inflicting all of its occupants.

Meanwhile, the IR squad of martyrs Ahmad El-Ash'hab and Bilal El-Bazzal was falling upon Ain Qinya post barring it from reinforcing the attacked force, while at the same time, the IR group of martyr Ismail Younes was bombarding Abou Qamha position with the adequate arms paralyzing it completely.

Also, the IR unit of martyrs Hussein Kourani and Ali El-Shab blitzed, at 6:15, Baraasheit compound with the rocket-propelled grenades scoring accurate hits.

In the occupied Jerusalem, AFP reported that five "Israeli" collaborators were killed on Wednesday in an attack claimed by Hizbullah in "Israeli" occupied zone in southern Lebanon.

Furthermore, the IR team of martyrs Moussa Adib and Khodor Ghadboun targeted, at 9:10, the main fortification of Tair Harfa outpost as several "Israeli" agents were seen inside it. The mujahedeen, who used the rocket-propelled grenades, managed to strike it accurately.

The perished "Israeli" agents were identified as Mustafa Ismail Abou Hawileh, Shibaa, Monir, Monir Ali Daaibes, Showaya, Ramez Fayez Al-Choufi, Showaya, Afif Hamad Al-Choufi, Showaya, and Ghassan Fouad Alloud, Ain Qinya.

Later on, AFP mentioned that three more "Israeli" agents were wounded, two of them were seriously hurt(1)

For its part, the Lebanese newspaper The Daily Star reported that "So far this year 11 "Israeli"-proxy militia men have been killed and another 18 wounded.
Hizbullah said in a statement that a team from its special forces unit penetrated the zone and crept to within a few meters of a group of "Israeli"-allied militiamen. According to the statement, the militiamen had set up a temporary position along the road.
As the militiamen clambered aboard a military jeep, the Hizbullah fighters opened fire with machine guns and hurled hand grenades at the vehicle, killing all the occupants.
Hizbullah mortar teams shelled the outposts of Ain Qinya and Abu Qamha, allowing the ambush team to escape safely from the zone.
None of the enemy soldiers nor their collaborators dared to come close (to the ambush scene) for 40 minutes, the statement added.
"The SLA remained tight-lipped over the fatal attack on the militiamen. The "Israeli" Army, however, confirmed five fatalities but said initially that the militiamen were killed in a roadside bomb attack not a close-quarter ambush.
A subsequent investigation by the "Israelis" revealed that the jeep had been hit by a rocket-propelled grenade and small arms fire, confirming Hizbullahs account of the ambush.
It has become clear that the soldiers were killed by RPG and light weapon fire at their car  while they were carrying out a military operation. During the incident, an unknown type of bomb was also set off, an "Israeli" Army statement said.
Hizbullah conducts few close-quarter operations in the eastern sector. Even roadside bombs are rare compared to other parts of the zone.
This is due in part to Hizbullah having been unable to gain the same level of cooperation from the mainly Druze population as it has in the Shiite-dominated districts.
Tensions rose in Ain Qenya, however, with the discovery two weeks ago of a roadside bomb on the edge of the village. Two militiamen were lightly wounded when the bomb exploded"(2)

Egypt's Islamic Action Party meets Lebanon's Hizbullah(5)
A delegation from Egypt's Islamic Action party (IAP) held a meeting with Hizbullah officials at Hizbullah's Political Council HQ.

Discussions focused on Jihad affairs and means to resist the US-Zionist alliance.

IAP's delegation included the party's Secretary General Adel Hussein, Politburo member Wajdi Ahmed Hussein (also Al-Shaab Daily's Editor - in-chief) and Executive Committee member Dr. Najlaa al-Qiabbi.


"Israel's" Army readies Lebanon psychological-training plan(6)
" In the midst of public doubts over its continued presence in the security zone, the IDF* is drafting a program to mentally prepare soldiers for service in Lebanon.

The program, titled "Making Sense," will be implemented at the Guerrilla Warfare School in the IDF base at Elyakim. It will be integrated in the bush training combat soldiers go through there prior to their deployment in the security zone.

The program is being put together by the IDF's Behavioral Sciences Department and is mainly a framework for commanders to use to hold a discussion and deliver a message.

According to a senior officer familiar with the program, its aim is to help commanders relay to their soldiers an understanding of their mission's realities. As the officer explained, it is based on story telling, leading to a lesson or moral.

"Once the reason for being [in the security zone] was to defend the northern settlements; now they are also being told that their job is to allow the government the breathing space it needs to make decisions. Well, how do you convey that? It's difficult and this program is to help soldiers understand the realities of their mission," he said.

The program is in its final stages of preparation and is expected to be implemented shortly.

The senior officer also took issue with the name-calling of soldiers by senior commanders, who called soldiers "crybabies" who said they were afraid to serve in Lebanon and that the IDF had nothing to do there now that the government declared it was pulling out anyway in the summer.

"I'm against commanders calling soldiers names. They shouldn't pick on soldiers' weaknesses and fears. They need to consolidate them, create a framework to talk about it. You have to speak to a soldier, not call him names," said the officer.

Still, he said the media blew out of proportion the comments made by other commanders, whose style of motivating their troops was seen by some as offensive.

He was referring to Golani Brigade commander Col. Shmuel Zakai, who referred to the Four Mothers as "the Four Rags." He compared him to a coach trying to fire up his players before a big game.

"I very much understand Zakai and what he did. It is the style of some commanders, but he too shouldn't have used name-calling," the senior officer said.

The IDF has not released any studies regarding the impact the recent hostilities and public outcry has had on troops in the security zone. But the senior officer said he does not believe IDF soldiers in general suffered from a lack of motivation.

"It's very simple: "Israeli" youth today are very conformist. They have a very strong concept of security, they still believe there is an existential threat on the state and that war could break out one day," he said.

"But it also is based on individual motives for self-fulfillment. All this talk about collectivism being replaced by individualism is, excuse my French, bullshit. Men have always fought for reasons of individualism and self-glorification."
