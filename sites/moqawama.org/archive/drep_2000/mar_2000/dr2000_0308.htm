Daily Report

March Wednesday 08-2000:


Introduction
At least two members of the Israeli occupations proxy militia in the occupied southern Lebanon were killed in separate attacks by the Islamic Resistance (IR) fighters against the outposts of Houla and Hardoun.
Meanwhile, Hizbullahs Secretary-General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah stressed that IR will continue its armed struggle in South Lebanon until the last Israeli sokdier has left the region.
Also, Hizbullah issued a statement  in which it said that the Israeli cabinets decision to withdraw from occupied South Lebanon by the end of July 2000 was the clearest official a acknowledgement by the Zionist invaders of the failure of their occupation of Lebanon.

Two militiamen killed, one wounded in IR operations

At least two members of the Israeli occupation's proxy militia were killed and a third was seriously wounded in two Islamic Resistance (IR) operations against the occupation's outposts in Houla and al-Hardoun Tuesday morning.

Near the borders with occupied Palestine, IR's squad of Martyrs Ahmed Fouani and Jihad Hijazi at 9:00 a.m. Tuesday detonated a powerful roadside bomb against a Lahdi patrol that was moving along the Houla road. The militiamen in the patrol were killed or wounded, an IR communiqué read.
Earlier, IR's squad of Martyr Khalil Saad and Radwan Rasheed ambushed at 7:40 a.m. Tuesday another Lahdi patrol at the entrance of al-Hardoun outpost.

IR men peppered the militiamen with machine-gun fire, an IR communiqué said.

In the meantime, squad of Martyrs Ali Harmoush and Mohammed Faqih of IR's Artillery Reinforcement Unit (ARU) pounded the Hardoun outpost and barred it from backing the targeted patrol, while squad of Martyrs Moussa Salman and Hassan Salman paralyzed the al-Bayyada outpost and squad of Martyrs Wael Anani and Mahmoud Srour dealt with the Shamaa outpost scoring hits in its ramparts and fortifications. The confrontations went on for over two hours, the communiqué added.

Moreover, squad of Martyrs Ahmed Shamas and Ali Khalil shelled in a violent and concentrated manner a Lahdi force that tried to inspect the ambush site in al-Hardoun.
At dawn, squad of Martyrs Khodor al-Hajj Ali and Moussa Hamdan attacked the Beaufort outpost with rocket propelled grenades following IR's operations at night that had targeted the occupation's outposts in Baraasheet and Haddatha.

Security sources in the occupied zone affirmed that IR's operations killed three militiamen in Houla and al-Hardoun while the occupation's radio acknowledged that one agent was killed in Houla and another in al-Hardoun adding that a third was seriously wounded in Hardoun.(1)

For its part, Future TV reported that the two were to be reported in southern Lebanon since the Israeli cabinet decided to pullout occupation forces by July with or without a peace treaty. Thirteen militiamen have been killed so far this year. The fate of the militia, which is being trained, financed and controlled by Israel is up in the air. Israel's high court of justice late yesterday rejected a request by three militiamen for asylum. Israeli lawyers representing the men denounced the decision, saying their clients faced reprisals from the Lebanese authorities as well as Hizbollah resistance fighters in the event of an Israeli pullout. The Israeli cabinet has promised to guarantee the safety of the roughly 2,500 members of the militia but gave no details. Press reports have alleged for months that countries such as Australia, Canada and Turkey would grant political asylum to militiamen, particularly senior officers. But Hizbollah has urged these states not to take the Israeli-backed and financed troops, who are responsible for numerous massacres against Lebanese civilians. Hizbollah also declared that its offer to pardon repentant collaborators no longer stands after Israel's decision to pullout. Hezbollah had offered to pardon collaborators if they surrendered before the announcement of an Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon, in an attempt to encourage desertions among crumbling militia ranks. The militia has been deteriorating, with many defecting to government-held territories. Israeli allied militiamen collaborating with Israel face military court trials in Beirut. The head of the militia, Antoine Lahd, has been sentenced to death in absentia by a Beirut military court.(2)

Hezbollah struggle in south Lebanon continues: Nasrallah (3)
The Hezbollah fighters will continue their armed struggle in south Lebanon until the last Israeli soldier has left the region, the group's secretary general Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said in a French daily Wednesday.

"The only guarantee of a withdrawal (by Israeli forces) is to continue the resistance operations," he told Le Figaro.

"If two or three weeks pass without any Israeli soldiers killed or injured, Israel will forget about its occupation. Stopping the operations would help (Israeli Prime Minister Ehud) Barak to remain in Lebanon.

"We are helping Barak to keep his promises. We will continue to hit them until there is not one single Israeli soldier on our land," said the chief Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah.

He expressed doubts on the July date given for the unilateral withdrawal of Israeli forces from south Lebanon, which Barak's government announced on Sunday.

Israel army chief of staff Shaul Mofaz said Tuesday he expected the Hezbollah to continue their anti-Israeli attacks even after the army has withdrawn from south Lebanon by July.

Nasrallah said he doubted that the withdrawal would be completed by July, arguing that it is "highly probable that the Israeli government's decision is a political manouevre ahead of the Arab League foreign ministers' meeting in Beirut (on Sunday)".

Nasrallah said that decision on whether to continue the fight against Israel even if the withdrawal is completed was a decision that would be taken after the event.

Asked about recent comments by French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin who described the Hezbollah action as "terrorist" attacks, Nasrallah said "the Lebanese are fighting for the liberation of their territory".

"Therefore it's a resistance movement, not terrorism," he concluded, adding that Jospin had defended the occupying force. Paris was aligning itself with Washington, he said.

Hezbollah: Israeli cabinet's decision clearest acknowledgment of defeat   (4)
Hezbollah said that the Israeli cabinet's decision to withdraw from occupied south Lebanon by the end of July 2000 was "the clearest official acknowledgment by the Zionist invaders of the failure of their occupation of Lebanon and their defeat before the will and steadfastness of the Lebanese people and its heroic Islamic resistance."

In a statement, Hezbollah said that the "smallest Arab country" had forced the Israelis to retreat in "defeat and resignation."

"This decision turns (Israeli Premier Ehud) Barak's electoral campaign into a Zionist commitment for leaving Lebanon under the pressure of the continuous resistance operations, despite the fact that the enemy is trying to cover its historic and visible defeat through cowardly threats voiced by the Zionist entity's senior officials," the statement said, referring to recent threats by Israeli foreign minister David Levy to retaliate against cross-border attacks "child for child, soul for soul."

However, if "Israel" carried out attacks into Lebanon following a withdrawal, Hezbollah said that it retained a "legitimate right" to retaliate in kind.

"Barak knows very well that the Islamic resistance which forced him to retreat  will not stand idle in the face of Zionist attacks on Lebanese territories."

Hezbollah added that the Israeli decision had rendered it too late for any attempt by the occupation's proxy militia members to desert the militia and request amnesty from the Lebanese government.

"Faced with this historic defeat of the Zionist invaders, we reiterate our firm rejection to granting political asylum to any agent who served with the Zionist occupation. The agents must be subject to the law and serve the sentences against them without any forgiveness or leniency. By this, any repentance (by militia members) is rendered useless and meaningless with this Zionist decision having been taken."

Israel marks pockets of Lebanon as its own    (5)

Nicholas Blanford

If no agreement is reached with Syria by July, the Israeli government will order a unilateral withdrawal to the border but retain several key military outposts located just north of the border on land annexed by the Jewish state, a growing number of observers believe.
Israels deputy defense minister, Ephraim Sneh, has said that Israel will try to keep these pockets of Lebanese territory even if a deal is reached with Syria and Lebanon allowing for an organized withdrawal.
A senior Western military source described the proposed plan as preposterous, saying that it would bring the war to the Lebanese-Israeli border.
The so-called Purple Line encompasses several strategic parcels of land where the Israelis have pushed the border fence northward a few hundred meters, annexing the high ground with dominating views into Lebanon.
The four incursion zones ­ known as technical fences ­ along the border have been in place since the early 1980s. They include the border ridges near Yarin and Rmeish in the western sector, the hills between Adaisseh and Markaba in the central sector and the southern end of the Al-Marj valley in the eastern sector.
The Purple Line is also thought to encompass key posts such as the Abbad hilltop compound in the central sector, distinguished by its twin radio antennae and ever-present observation balloon.
The technical fences also are designed to provide protection for some Israeli settlements that abut the border, such as Misgav Am which lies behind the Adaisseh incursion. The incursions are known to the UN as Permanent Violations of the 1949 Armistice Agreement.
In December, Sneh said that the Israeli Army would pull back to behind the international border and the technical fences would be dismantled. His comments ­ which came after the resumption of peace talks between Israel and Syria ­ appeared to quash long-held speculation that the Jewish state might try to retain the strategic salients.
However, the stalled peace negotiations coupled with the Israeli governments policy of withdrawing the troops by July, appears to have helped resurrect the Purple Line option.
Sneh, who is a former Israeli Army commander of the occupation zone, said on Thursday that the option of returning to the Purple Line existed only in the event of a unilateral withdrawal. Yet on Sunday, within hours of the Israeli Cabinets unanimous approval for a July withdrawal, Sneh admitted that whatever the manner of the pullout, the technical fences would be kept.
The statement from the Israeli Cabinet inferred a degree of ambiguity, promising that troops would deploy on the border rather than a clear commitment to pull back to the internationally-recognized frontier.
The point was picked up by an unlikely collection of observers. The decision does not speak about withdrawal but refers to redeployment, wrote Mohammed Khair al-Wadi, the director-general of the Syrian government daily Tishreen. He accused Israel of wanting to keep strategic parts of south Lebanon.
Zeev Schiff, the respected military correspondent for the Israeli Haaretz daily, also described the Cabinet decision as amounting to a redeployment rather than a withdrawal.
He said that despite Barak approving the armys New Horizon withdrawal plan last December, not a single tender has been issued for the $240 million worth of defenses to be constructed along the border.
Nahum Barnea, the influential columnist in Yediot Ahronot, wrote that a withdrawal to the Purple Line was a predicament lying in wait for Israeli Premier Ehud Barak. If he leaves the fence where it is now, he will be providing Hizbullah with an argument to continue the fighting, Barnea wrote.
Nabatieh MP Mohammed Raad, who heads Hizbullahs politburo, agreed on Tuesday, declaring that resistance operations would continue if the Israelis occupy just one inch of Lebanese land.
Its only natural that we dont permit the Zionists to remain on one grain of Lebanese soil, Raad told The Daily Star. That means that the withdrawal must be all the way to the international border.
Barak has warned that cross-border attacks following a withdrawal will be met with severe reprisals. Yet if the Israeli Army deploys in the incursion zones ­ within 600 meters of the border ­ the likelihood of fighting spilling over the frontier into northern Galilee is considerably higher than at present, where the width of the buffer strip extends between 3 and 20 kilometers.
The Western military source, a long-time observer of the Israeli military, said he was baffled at how the Israelis expect to get away with it.
The source said it was perfectly clear that they have no idea what to do but believed a withdrawal to the Purple Line is under serious consideration by the Israelis. I see a very nasty situation down the road because the Israelis wont make a clean break  It will require a very forceful intervention from Washington otherwise it could become very nasty indeed, the source said.

BEIRUT LINKS SECURITY ALONG INTERNATIONAL BORDER TO GOLAN PULLOUT   (6)
Beirut has called on Israel to withdraw from occupied southern Lebanon as part of an agreement that includes an Israeli pullout from the Syrian Golan Heights. But Israel ruled out any return of the entire Syrian plateau. Prime Minister Ehud Barak, departing from past optimism on peace prospects, cast doubts on Israel's chances of overcoming differences with Syria and forging a US-mediated agreement over the Golan.

Beirut has warned Israel not to expect peace from a troop pullout from south Lebanon until it also withdrew from the Golan. Information Minister Anwar Khalil emphasized Beirut would give no guarantee of peace on Israel's last active battlefront until Syrian demands for the Golan are satisfied. Khalil said it is impossible to restrain resistance fighters while Arab land and rights are involved. Hizbollah, the main party resisting Israeli occupation in Lebanon, said an Israeli withdrawal constitutes a victory for the resistance. But Khalil warned that a pullout in the absence of a peace agreement could create a very dangerous situation for Lebanon. Khalil echoed remarks by top officials including President Emile Lahoud, who accused Israel of favoring a unilateral pullout in order to separate the Syrian and Lebanese tracks in peace-making. Israeli Foreign Minister David Levy claimed the intended Israeli withdrawal would leave Damascus without a powerful bargaining chip in peace talks over the Golan. The central leadership in Syria, the National Progressive Front, said after a meeting last night that Israel's decision to pullout from Lebanon is a repeat of similar moves Israel made whenever it was in a dilemma. The NPF, chaired by Vice President Zuheir Masharka, was briefed by Foreign Minister Farouk Sharaa. It welcomed a full and unconditional Israeli withdrawal form the south, in line with UN Security Council resolution 425. Damascus had warned that Israel seeks to drive a wedge between Lebanon and Syria. It also accused the Jewish state of wanting to maintain occupation of some strategic hills in the south following a promised withdrawal. Official press in Syria said Israeli leaders believe any retreat from Lebanon will put Damascus in a "trap", adding that has proved faulty time and again. Meanwhile, Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak has been under pressure by some members of his centrist One Israel alliance to narrow his peace program, abandoning the Syrian track in favor of the Palestinians. Israel has reiterated its rejection of a full withdrawal from the Golan, a key Syrian demand for the resumption of deadlocked peace negotiations

HARIRI SAYS RESISTANCE FORCED ISRAEL TO DECIDE ON WITHDRAWAL (7)
Former Prime Minister Deputy Rafik Hariri said Israel's decision to withdraw occupation forces from the south is not surprising. Hariri who was speaking to reporters in Dubai, said Lebanese resistance attacks forced Israel to take the decision.

Speaking at the Dubai Press Club, former premier and Beirut Deputy Rafik Hariri, who is in the UAE on the invitation of Defense Minister and Dubai's Crown Prince Sheikh Mohammed Rashid Al Maktoum, said Israel's decision to unilaterally withdraw from Lebanon should not be seen as a goodwill gesture. Hariri said while Israel was announcing its decision, its warjets were destroying civilian infrastructure and killing Lebanese children. Hariri stressed that it remains uncertain whether the pullout will be part of a peace agreement, adding quote there are report that talks on the Lebanese-Israeli and Syrian-Israeli tracks will resume within ten days. Hariri, who was accompanied to Dubai by former minister Fouad Sanyoura, MP Bassem Sabeh and top advisors, ruled out any possibility of a conflict between the Lebanese Army and Hizbollah resistance fighters following a possible Israeli withdrawal. Hariri does not believe the Israeli pullout will affect parliamentary elections in Lebanon scheduled to take place next summer. Hariri was also asked about the fate of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon. He said it will be great injustice to grant the refugees Lebanese citizenship since that will do away with their right to return to Palestine.

JOSPIN'S ISRAEL TRIP TURNED OFF FRENCH VOTERS (8)
French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin's popularity has slumped after his tumultuous trip to Israel and he would lose an election held now against President Jacques Chirac, an opinion poll showed on Tuesday. Voters clearly disliked Jospin's bid to play a more prominent role in foreign policy, which is normally the reserve of the conservative president he wants to challenge in 2002, the daily Liberation said in publishing the poll. Angry Palestinians stoned Jospin and he was condemned by protesters in several Arab countries last month after he called attacks by Hizbollah resistance fighters on Israeli occupation troops in Lebanon "terrorism". The poll showed Chirac would win a presidential race against Jospin by 52 to 48 percent if it were held now -- a score close to Chirac's victory over Jospin in the 1995 election. It was the Socialist Jospin's worst score since a slump in May 1999, since when he had consistently led the president as the voters' choice in the crucial second round of the next presidential election.

Fishermen in Tyre protest Israeli Navy harassment (9)
Some 200 fishermen from Tyre held a sit-in on Tuesday in front of the citys port to protest the Israelis human- rights violations.
We call on Lebanons senior politicians to lodge a complaint with the Monitoring Group over the Israeli Navys harassment and vandalism of our boats and fishing nets, said Khalil Taha, a spokesmen for the Fishermens Association in the city.
The five-nation Monitoring Group oversees the US-brokered 1996 April Understanding, which bans attacks on, or from, civilian areas.
The fishermens plaintive call comes on the heels of intensified Israeli Navy encroachments on the livelihood of fishermen.
The Israeli vessels often rend apart the fishermens nets, fire at their boats or detain them for up to six hours. Fishermen have even been issued warnings against angling deep into the Mediterranean, they said.
Ever since the 1996 Israeli blitz, we have been reeling under a stringent Israeli Navy curfew, which is nibbling at our meager source of income, Khalil said.
He called on the state-run High Relief Agency and the Council for the South to pay the besieged fishermen reparations and provide them with medical and social insurance to offset real or potential losses.
Around four days ago, Israeli sailors slashed my nets with machetes as I was fishing offshore, said fisherman Saeed Abu Jumaa, an Egyptian.
I told them I would lodge a complaint with the Egyptian Embassy in Beirut over this violation. One of them replied: We do not recognize any Arab country. All the land that lies between the Nile and Euphrates River is ours. We dont believe in peace.
There are reports of fishermen clandestinely angling at night, while their boats can be cloaked in darkness and there is plenty of catch. The secretive offshore fishing is done to avoid the menacing Israeli boats, which swarm the coast during the day.
Fishermen say that Israeli boats only allow Lebanese fishermen to fish in a 200-meter wide area located five meters off the coast, in waters where fish are scarce.
I call on UNIFIL and the International Committee of the Red Cross to come to our rescue, said fisherman Mohammad Taha as he repaired his torn nets.

Plea for release of Khiam seven.(10)
The Follow-up Committee for the Support of Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons on Tuesday sent an urgent plea to the international community to secure the release of seven Israeli-held Lebanese women.
We call on the United Nations follow-up committee and world human rights organizations to pressure Israel into releasing the women detainees and all other inmates in Khiam and other Israeli prisons, said a committee statement released on Tuesday, in observance of International Womens Day.
The committee, a non-governmental organization that serves the 144 Khiam inmates as well as former inmates, released a graphic statement recounting the daily human abuses that Khiams women inmates endure in the 16-year old facility.
The seven detainees are among the 500 women who were thrown into Khiam for alleged collaboration with the Hizbullah resistance movement, the Follow-up Committee, established in 1993, said.
The human rights group said women are poorly fed, kept in pigsties, hung on trees, flogged with plastic whips, and threatened to be burned to the stake. It also said the women are subjected to electric shocks, along with other flagrant human rights violations.
Between 1988 and 1989, 70 women were reported to have been held in the hilltop Khiam prison, the highest number held there in the detention camps history.
