Historic meeting vows support with one voice (1)

The Arab states brushed aside their internal disputes at the weekend to rally behind Lebanon, offering political and financial backing for the country that has taken the brunt of the 52-year-old conflict with Israel.
The day-long meeting of Arab League foreign ministers, which convened at the Grand Serail, produced a set of resolutions, which firmly condemned Israels repeated threats and assaults on Lebanon, upheld the right to resist occupation and called for a solution to the problem of Palestinian refugees residing on Lebanese soil.                                              
It also supported the unity of Syrian-Lebanese tracks in future negotiations and urged Arab states to reconsider normalizing relations with Israel and participating in multilateral regional cooperation conferences attended by the Jewish state.
The Arab League meeting, a bi-annual event that normally takes place in Cairo, was shifted to Beirut after the Feb. 8 Israeli air strikes on civilian targets and infrastructure. Its resolutions sent a powerful message to Israel that Lebanon was no longer an isolated country which it could strike at will.
 All 22 Arab League member states sent their foreign ministers to the meeting ­ a brisk turnout that underlined the mounting sympathy for Lebanons plight. But it was the powerful troika ­ Syria, Saudi Arabia and Egypt ­ that ultimately pushed the consensus through.
In opening remarks, Prime Minister Salim Hoss detailed Lebanons fears of a unilateral Israeli withdrawal that would undermine the chances of a comprehensive Arab-Israeli peace settlement, and push the region into further turmoil.
A pullout without an agreement for repatriating Palestinian refugees, Hoss argued, would leave a time-bomb that will threaten Lebanon and the entire region.
Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa, delivered statement endorsing the Lebanese stand. There is no doubt that the Israelis are now completely certain that they are not capable of singling out Lebanon under any conditions, he said. Today, the Lebanese resistance movement enjoys Arab respect, and south Lebanon has become the symbol of Arab steadfastness and dignity.
He extended President Hafez Assads support for Lebanon, its government, its people and the resistance.
Prince Saud al-Faisal, Saudi Arabias veteran foreign minister, praised the bravery of the resistance, a surprise remark coming from an ally of the US, which blames Hizbullah for the turbulence in the south.
Prince Saud questioned the real motives behind the Israeli governments recent decision to withdraw by July, with or without agreements with Syria and Lebanon. If the withdrawal will lead to holding Lebanon responsible for what is called the security of northern Israel and give Israel the pretext to demonstrate its grudge and enmity toward this unarmed country, it will be rejected altogether, he said.
Israel could not protect the areas under its own control. Therefore, guaranteeing Israels security can only be achieved by providing security to Lebanon, Syria and Palestine.
  He described the Lebanese as brothers who were forced to face challenges as a result of geographic and historical realities. The least we should do is to send Israel a clear and straightforward message that we all support Lebanon and are ready to give it the help it needs.
Egyptian Foreign Minister Amr Musa called for inter-Arab coordination to protect Arab rights. He said that as long as there is occupation there will be resistance and that the resistance is a result of the occupation, not a cause of it.
But he described the Israeli decision to withdraw from Lebanon as right, arguing that any Israeli pullback that liberates Arab land is welcome.
Egypt has been lukewarm to the concept of a joint Syrian-Lebanese peace track. But in his statement, Musa stressed the importance of such a tactic, warming up to the Syrians.
He said that Egyptian President Hosni Mubaraks visit to Beirut last month was a message to whom it may concern that the Arab land, including the Lebanese land, is not and will not be open for aggression.
We are here to start building up Arab solidarity  and rally behind our brethren in Syria, Lebanon and Palestine, so that they retrieve their rights and that comprehensive peace prevails in the region, Musa said.
As for the prospects for holding an Arab summit, this is the wish of all Arabs. We want the summit to come out with a vision for joint Arab interests on all economic, cultural and political levels, Musa said before discreetly slipping out of the conference room for a three-way meeting with Prince Saud and Sharaa.
The three spent nearly an hour ironing out disagreements on the phrasing of an 18-point Lebanese working paper, which the conference would later adopt as a pan-Arab stand.
Ministers also pledged to resume payment of $2 billion in aid promised in 1979 and reconfirmed at a Baghdad summit meeting in 1990, of which Lebanon has received a little over $420 million. One senior Gulf official said that the oil-rich states were ready to inject money into Lebanons empty coffers provided they are presented with a list of projects they would be financing. There would be no lump sum payments.

 

Calls for stronger Arab ties and support for resistance (2)
 The overwhelming message from the meeting of the foreign ministers was support for Lebanon in the face of Israeli aggression and occupation.
Omani Foreign Minister Youssef Bin Alawi Bin Abdullah, whose country headed the session, said that the Arab world would stand by Lebanon as a strong pillar during any circumstances it faces.
Responding to Israeli Foreign Minister David Levys threats last month to burn Lebanese soil and kill its children in the event of resistance attacks on Israeli civilians, Bin Alawi said: We want to reiterate that those who make such threats will not escape from the fire they light. Those who start a fire will be the first to burn, for Lebanon will always remain dear to the Arab nation.Bin Alawi added that Lebanons resistance to Israeli occupation was as legitimate as Europes resistance to Nazi occupation.
The only way to describe the Israeli presence in south Lebanon is that it is an occupation force and there is no way to justify the presence of the Israeli army in the Syrian Golan Heights and the Palestinian territory, he said.
Bin Alawi also called on nations that have privately expressed frustration with Israeli policies to air their complaints publicly.
He expressed his belief that the Arab states would meet at a summit organized by the Arab League to begin a new stage in strengthening Arab relations that would undoubtedly serve as a pillar in the formulation of a new Arab union.
Qatari Crown Prince Sheikh Jassem Bin Hamad Al-Thani followed his Omani counterpart and also stressed the importance of Arab unity.
Jassem stressed that the expressions of solidarity toward Lebanon should not be limited to official speeches, but should include financial aid. I would like to reiterate Qatars firm position that there is no solution to the struggle in the region except through a just and comprehensive peace based on UN decisions, the crown prince said.
Our Arab nation stands at a crossroads which demands that we forge a unified Arab strategy to deal with this situation, he concluded.
Yemeni Foreign Minister Abdel-Qader Bajamal, who also serves as the countrys deputy prime minister, described the Arab political, economic and cultural situation as one that brings depression and confusion and stands against all Arab national principles of unity and solidarity. Because of our insistence on achieving common action, we are presenting an initiative to the Arab League that calls for regular Arab summits, Bajamal said.
He ended his speech by warning that the Arab world was passing through dangerous and critical stages and that history will not have mercy on us but will judge us for our failure toward our peoples issues.
Algerian Foreign Minister Youssef el-Youssefi accused Israel of insincerely calling for peace while it continued to build settlements on Palestinian land.
Bahraini Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed Bin Mubarak Al-Khalifa said that the Israeli Cabinets decision to withdraw from the south in July signaled a victory for the Lebanese peoples resistance and perseverance. Mohammed said that he hoped that the meeting would constitute the first step toward strengthening Arab relations and organizing an Arab summit.
Tunisias Foreign Minister el-Habib Bin Yehia also called for an Arab summit that would further each countrys interests and strengthen inter-Arab relations.
The organization of regular high-level meetings has become an urgent necessity, particularly since other attempts at unification by different countries in the world have achieved significant results, Bin Yehia said.
He also called for the creation of an Arab free-trade zone as an appropriate first step to remove obstacles hindering economic cooperation.
Jordanian Foreign Minister Abdel-Ilah al-Khatib echoed the call for Arab unity, expressing his hope that Arab countries could confront regional and international challenges as a single entity.
We hope we are successful in sending a clear message to the international community so that it can guarantee an end to Israeli threats and military escalation, Khatib said.
Libyan Foreign Minister Abdel-Rahman Mohammed Shalqam repeated his Tunisian counterparts call for a free-trade zone not only to help foster unity but to achieve balanced international cooperation between Arabs and their neighbors.
Farouk Kaddoumi, chairman of the Palestine Liberation Organizations political department, also noted the need for Arab unity, saying: Geographical divisions and political independence should not lead to the accentuation of our differences and passing disputes, he said.

 

Support protects us from Israel (3)
President Emile Lahoud welcomed the results of the Arab foreign ministers conference, saying the resolutions adopted would deter Israel from launching further aggression.
Lahoud made the remarks during an official dinner for the visiting ministers at Baabda Palace on Saturday evening. Lebanon greatly values your solidarity in a move that expresses the aspirations of all Arabs in this sensitive juncture in the history of the Arab-Israeli conflict, he said.
For his part, Prime Minister Salim Hoss said he was pleased by the conference resolutions, saying they proved Lebanon will not be alone in the event of future Israeli aggression.
In a statement Sunday, the prime minister stressed the importance of two resolutions: the call for member states to reconsider normalization with Israel, and participation in multilateral peace talks should Israeli attacks against Lebanon continue. Hoss said the resolution gave credence to what we said during preparations for the conference... that its results would send a message to all concerned, and particularly Israel, that Lebanon will not be alone in facing Israeli aggression.
He cited support for Beiruts position on returning Palestinian refugees, the unity of the Lebanese and Syrian tracks, the need to respect the terms of the April Understanding, an unconditional Israeli withdrawal from the south and the freeing of Lebanese detainees.
Also speaking on Sunday, Hizbullah deputy Secretary-General Sheikh Naim Qassem described the results of the meeting as a sign of positive yet incomplete solidarity. Qassem said he would not focus on reservations the party had about the resolutions, because we only have one enemy, and we bless all those who stand behind the resistance and Lebanon in the face of the enemy. Qassem said Arab officials arrived in Beirut with their heads high as a result of the victories of the resistance.

 

Final communique offers 18 ways to say we support you (4)
The 18-point resolution adopted by foreign ministers during the Arab League Council of Ministers meeting in Beirut, March 11-12

The Arab League agrees to:
1-Strongly condemn Israel for its continuing occupation of parts of South Lebanon and the Western Bekaa, and denounce the continuing Israeli attacks against its land and people
2-Strongly condemn the recent Israeli attacks that targeted civilians, infrastructure and economic facilities in Lebanon, and the subsequent dangerous and rabid Israeli threats
3-Denounce the statements made by certain officials of friendly countries in the wake of the recent Israeli aggression, which equated Israel, the attacker, with Lebanon, which is being attacked; the League further expresses its astonishment and surprise at the issuing of such statements
4-Confirm that the Israeli attacks constitute a clear violation of the 1996 April Understanding, the United Nations Covenant, international legislation, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the most basic moral principles
5-Confirm the need for Israel to respect the April Understanding, and to reject its attempts to ignore or amend it, because this will lead to an escalation for whose adverse consequences Israel will bear full responsibility
6-Confirm its support for Lebanon in its permanent calls for the implementation of United Nations resolution 425 (1978); to call on the international community and the United Nations, particularly the Security Council, to urge Israel to implement this resolution unconditionally
7-Confirm its absolute support for Lebanon in its resistance against the Israeli occupation of South Lebanon and the Western Bekaa until the liberation of all Lebanese soil, based on the UN charter, as well as support its right to legitimately defend itself against occupation, since resistance is a result of occupation and not a cause of it
8-Call on Arab states with relations with Israel in the framework of the peace process to reconsider these relations following the recent Israeli aggression against Lebanon
9-Confirm that it stands by Lebanon firmly; to provide it with the necessary Arab support to strengthen its steadfastness, which expresses the cohesion and national dignity of the Arabs
10-Request Arab states to translate their resolutions of support for Lebanon, the steadfastness of its people, and its reconstruction (into reality) by living up to their commitments that were decided upon in Arab summits, especially the 1979 Tunis conference and 1990 Baghdad conference; to urge governments to begin living up to these commitments
11-Confirm the Arab states insistence on the Middle East peace process according to the principles of the Madrid Conference; to call for a speedy resumption of negotiations for a just and comprehensive settlement, which Israel has obstructed on all tracks
12-Call on Arab states participating in multilateral negotiations to reconsider their participation until fundamental and tangible progress is made on all tracks
13-Confirm the unity of the Lebanese and Syrian negotiating tracks, because this serves the interests of Lebanon and Syria, and the necessity of supporting the Arab position on all tracks; to emphasize that the primary foundation of a just and comprehensive peace lies in Israels full withdrawal from South Lebanon and the Western Bekaa to the internationally-recognized upon borders, from the Golan Heights to the June 4, 1967 line and from occupied Palestinian territories, including Jerusalem
14-Confirm the right of Palestinian refugees to return to their homes and reject their settlement outside Palestine; to warn against the absence of a solution to the issue of Palestinian refugees residing in Lebanon on the basis of their return to their homes will mean this issue will continue to resemble a time-bomb that threatens security and stability in Lebanon and hinders the attaining of a just and comprehensive peace settlement in the region
15-Condemn Israels inhuman and arbitrary practices against unarmed civilians in South Lebanon and the Western Bekaa and its arbitrary arrests, torture and expulsion; to demand the immediate release of all Lebanese detainees being held hostage in Khiam prison and in Israeli prisons and the disclosure of the fate of those missing, based on international custom and legislation and the Geneva Conventions
16-Support Lebanons efforts to call for the formation of a special international court to try Israeli war criminals who committed the Qana massacre in a UN base on April 18, 1996 and all other Israeli massacres that have taken place in Lebanon; compelling Israel to pay compensation to Lebanon for all human, material and economic losses resulting from its occupation and repeated attacks against Lebanese territory
17-Consider the Leagues council to be in open session and delegate the sessions chairman to reconvene it in the event that Israeli aggression against Lebanon is repeated
18-Charge the Arab Leagues secretary-general with implementing this resolution in coordination with the chairman of the session and Lebanons prime minister; to forward reports on this topic to the Leagues council

 

Angry Barak says resolution will not help bring peace (5)
Israeli officials on Sunday reacted angrily to resolutions endorsed during the weekend meeting of Arab League foreign ministers which called on Arab countries to reassess their ties with the Jewish state, and participation in the multilateral peace talks.
The negative reactions came as Syrias foreign minister, Farouk al-Sharaa, said that there were no indications that a resumption of Syria-Israeli peace talks was imminent.
The issue is absolutely not on the table, al-Sharaa told The Daily Star.
Summing up the foreign ministers conference, Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak told his cabinet that declarations of such kinds do not contribute to the atmosphere required to advance the peace process, and we do not need any approval from our neighbors.
Justice Minister Yossi Beilin, a leading advocate of a unilateral Israeli withdrawal from the south, called the position surreal.
In their final resolution, the ministers called on Arab states that have begun normalization with Israel to reconsider ties because of last months Israeli aggression against Lebanon, and to boycott multilateral talks with Israel until there was progress in peace negotiations.
This declaration is very grave. It reminds us of difficult periods in Israels relations with the Arab world, Danny Yatom, security adviser to Barak, told Israeli Army radio.
Foreign Minister David Levy said the double talk of war and peace casts a shadow on the real intentions of the participants in their relationship to the peace process. Israeli security would not rest on the mercy or the decisions of the Arab League, Levy said.
For his part, Amr Musa, the Egyptian foreign minister, called the Israeli criticisms groundless, adding: Let them say what they want. The ministers meeting came a week after the Israeli cabinet endorsed a withdrawal from Lebanon by July, whether or not a peace treaty is signed.
 

Ministers pressed to close Khiam jail (6)
The non-governmental Follow-up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in Israeli Prisons urged the Arab League and Arab Foreign ministers meeting on Saturday to press for the shutdown of Israels notorious Khiam detention center in occupied southern Lebanon.
The committee calls on the conferees at the Arab ministerial meeting in Beirut to encourage the UN and the international community to pressure Israel into closing down the Khiam detention center and releasing all Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons, said a committee statement released Saturday as the 22-member Arab League held its meeting in Beirut for the first time in three decades.
The follow-up committee put the total number of Lebanese detainees in Khiam and in Israeli prisons at around 174, including a number of women, juveniles and elderly. While we applaud your overture to hold the ministerial meeting in Beirut in a show of support against Israeli aggression, we wish to draw your attention to the plight of 174 detainees in Israeli prisons, 140 at Khiam, and 34 in other detention centers, said the statement.
The committee handed Arab League Secretary General Esmat Abdel Meguid and all 22 Arab foreign ministers a file listing the names of Lebanese detained in Israeli prisons.
An additional 34 detainees are being held by Israel as bargaining cards in any future prisoner swap deals between Lebanon and Israel, the statement noted. Although these detainees finished serving their jail terms more than 12 years ago, the Israeli government continues to renew their sentences in blatant violation of all international human rights conventions.
Israeli occupation troops released four detainees last week from the Khiam detention center near Marjayoun. All these inmates live in total isolation from the outside world, the statement said.
Meanwhile all freed detainees and civilians in the Israeli occupied zone who were expelled from their villages by Israeli occupation troops also called for public participation at a solidarity meeting organized by the follow-up committee to be held in Beirut on Monday.
We call on all freed detainees to come to the meeting in solidarity with those who continue to suffer appalling conditions inside Israeli prisons, said a statement released by the families of released detainees on Saturday.
Israel often forcibly deports residents of occupied villages to punish them for alleged collaboration with the resistance movement or refusal to join the ranks of the Israeli-allied militia.
Solidarity with the south is not a matter of grand speeches but can be demonstrated in giving deportees and families in occupied southern Lebanon who need financial support to fight off Israeli harassment adequate compensation, the statement added.

Morning Twilight could herald dawn of more fighting in south (7)
 The Israeli Army has prepared a plan for a unilateral pullout from Lebanon which states that key outposts north of the border are retained, a decision that almost guarantees continued fighting in the south.
Called Morning Twilight, the plan envisages boosting the fortifications of Jewish settlements near the border, retaining some outposts in Lebanon and preparing northern Israel for further bouts of fighting, according to the Haaretz daily. The Israeli Army has repeatedly declared its opposition to a unilateral withdrawal, fearing that without an agreement with Syria, Hizbullah or other armed groups will stage cross-border attacks into northern Israel.
In a meeting held on Friday with Premier Ehud Barak, Israeli Army staff reportedly refused to accept moral responsibility if a unilateral withdrawal leads to continued fighting.
According to Haaretz, Israeli Army staff are agreed that the first weeks, and perhaps months, following a unilateral withdrawal will witness sustained fighting. The conflict, they say, would dwindle after this period, Haaretz reported.
An Israeli Cabinet spokesman questioned by AFP on Sunday dismissed the Haaretz report, while an army spokesman refused any comment.
The proposals included in the Morning Twilight plan are the latest evidence to suggest that Israel has no immediate intention of staging a full withdrawal from Lebanon. Instead, the Israelis will withdraw to the so-called Purple Line, which incorporates some important military positions and a few ridges along the frontier where the border fence has been pushed northward.
Frederic Hof, a US-based analyst who is an acknowledged expert on the Lebanese and Syrian borders with Israel, said he was certain that the Israelis will pull back only to the Purple Line.
My assumption is that a withdrawal will most definitely be to the Purple Line in the event of a unilateral movement, Hof told The Daily Star. In the event of a treaty, a withdrawal might initially be to the Purple Line pending re-demarcation of the 1923/1949 line, he added referring to the original mandate boundary and the later Armistice Demarcation Line.
Haaretz said the decision to keep some occupation outposts in Israeli hands stemmed from the prohibitive cost of re-deploying the positions south of the border, a move which could cost millions of dollars.
This could be a very expensive and bloody decision, however. Nabatieh MP Mohammed Raad, who heads Hizbullahs parliamentary bloc, said that the resistance would continue operations if the Israelis occupy just one inch of Lebanese land.

 

Withdrawal won't be the final chapter (8)

By Arieh O'Sullivan

The IDF* does not expect the pullback from Lebanon to end the conflict in the North, even if it is accompanied by a peace treaty, and it is planning accordingly --

When it comes, the IDF withdrawal from south Lebanon will be no surprise. Even if it comes down to a unilateral pullout, it won't be in the dead of the night. It will be with a loud warning to everyone not to test the government by continuing the war in Israel itself.

And if the withdrawal does occur in the framework of an agreement, either with Lebanon alone or as part of an overall accord with Syria, the army is not deluding itself. It is operating on the assumption that no matter how it gets out of the security zone, it will not be in an atmosphere of guaranteed peace, and so it is preparing for the war to continue.

Sunday's remarkable decision by the government to quit southern Lebanon by July has major ramifications for the IDF and puts Chief of General Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaul Mofaz in an awkward situation of commanding troops who are sitting on their suitcases. With the July date dangling over his head like a sword of Damocles, Mofaz is aware of the apparent blow to IDF morale and motivation.

Soldiers and their parents are asking publicly why, if we're getting out anyway, should we wait until July? Why not get out tonight?

It's a no-win situation for the IDF. Many analysts believe that Lebanon is Israel's Achilles' heel; its deterrent effect fades the longer it stays there, particularly within the current restrictions on using force. If it pulls out, it will appear to be defeatable.

Hizbullah knows very well Israel's weaknesses - the saturated media coverage of the war in Lebanon, public opinion, and the sensitivity to IDF and civilian casualties.

Already this week, Hizbullah boasted that it "routed" the IDF out of Lebanon. "The decision taken by the enemy's cabinet to withdraw is the clearest official admission" of defeat, a Hizbullah statement said.

Mofaz has repeatedly said this week, first to high school students and then to the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee that the assessment of the IDF echelon is that Hizbullah will not cease its attacks even after Israel pulls out of Lebanon.

"It's possible we'll find ourselves redeployed along the international border, and the clashes will still continue in the North," Mofaz told students at a Jerusalem high school.

"Syria is in effect allowing Hizbullah to carry out attacks on IDF and SLA** soldiers in the security zone," Mofaz said. "[It could] wind down Hizbullah operations, but regrettably it supports these operations."

Nevertheless, and despite the rhetoric and dire predictions, the IDF and defense establishment have been preparing for a new reality in the North. They are calling it "New Horizon" and it entails not only a new and beefed-up set of outposts and electronic fences along the border, but a sophisticated hi-tech surveillance system that gives better control over the entire region.

The sophisticated system is already in place along parts of the border and will stretch from Rosh Hanikra along the sea to Mount Dov in the east if funds are forthcoming. So far the IDF has invested NIS 100 million in the development and deployment of the system.

It also includes advanced radar systems that will attempt to cover every physical approach to the border to spot terrorists attempting to infiltrate. The Home Front Command is paving new, less exposed approaches to the confrontation line settlements and are improving the shelters there.

ACCORDING to New Horizon, some 10 outposts in the security zone will be evacuated. Vital equipment, weapons, and sophisticated intelligence equipment will be brought back to Israel. At this stage, it is not clear whether the outposts themselves will be dismantled or left for SLA militiamen.

There are another eight outposts in the North which either straddle the international border or are a dozen, even a few hundred meters north of the border in Lebanese territory. Their placement was made for tactical reasons. They include outposts such as Barkan, Livneh and Tzivoni in the west and Tziporen near Kibbutz Menara.

The army is reportedly to set up temporary outposts to replace the 10 evacuated in the security zone until the permanent ones are ready. These temporary outposts can be set up quickly, with soldiers being housed in trailers or tents.

While senior IDF** commanders said that in the long run the redeployment would reduce the number of soldiers deployed in the North, the number of troops is likely to increase during the initial stages. They will act as a sort of human barrier to ensure that no terrorists are able to infiltrate and attack civilian targets.

The active defense was to have included the Nautilus Katyusha rocket laser gun, but that has recently developed problems and the joint US-Israeli program reportedly won't be deployed until next year at the earliest.

Deputy Defense Minister Ephraim Sneh, who has long opposed a unilateral withdrawal, echoes the assessment of the IDF that getting out of Lebanon will not end the conflict there. Commenting on the cabinet's unanimous decision, he said the public's weariness, combined with a low threshold for casualties, is what brought about the change now.

"It is easy for people to be lured into delusions that certain situations will be easier. The government has to consider this too," Sneh said in an interview with Army Radio.
