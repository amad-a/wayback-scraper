Introduction

Jospin pledged, in this letter, support for Lebanon and voiced sympathy for its continuing plight under "Israeli" occupation.

In a new violation of the April Accord, however, the "Israeli" warplanes staged a number of sorties into South Lebanon destroying two houses at Jibaa town.

In the occupied Palestine, "Israel's" Army is expected to issue tenders for dismantling most of the "Israeli" occupation outposts in the occupied area, moving others and building new ones in the framework of a new plan of the withdrawal dubbed New Horizon.


Hoss exposes two faces of French PM
Jospin letter pledged to safeguard Lebanons interests on visit to "Israel"(1)
"Beirut on Tuesday released the text of a recent letter from French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin in which he pledged support for Lebanon in the peace process and voiced sympathy for its continuing plight under "Israeli" occupation.
The letter, dated Feb. 15, contrasted sharply with Jospins controversial remarks on the Lebanese resistance which he voiced in "Israel" last week and which enraged the Lebanese government, French President Jacques Chirac and Arabs at large.
It was released by Prime Minister Salim Hoss as Jospin appeared in the French Parliament to defend himself against an avalanche of criticism from fellow politicians.
As it did in the past, France is by Lebanons side and will remain determined to work on achieving a just and comprehensive peace in the region that safeguards Lebanons legitimate interests, Jospin wrote to Hoss.
I would like to assure you that I will adhere to this policy during my visit to "Israel" and the Palestinian territories, the French premier added.
Ten days later in Jerusalem, Jospin tagged Hizbullahs operations as terrorist acts, enraging Lebanon and other Arab countries. Hoss office did not comment on Tuesday on the content of Jospins message.
However, Jospin defended his contentious remarks about Hizbullah on Tuesday, telling the National Assembly, the French Parliament, that his statements were aimed at condemning the escalation of violence in the Middle East.
Leaving aside the use of one controversial word, what I wanted to do was denounce the deliberate escalation of violence aimed at hampering a fragile peace process, Jospin said.
The letter the French premier sent two weeks ago was in response to a complaint Hoss issued after "Israel" bombed three Lebanese power stations on Feb. 8.
In the current difficult and painful situation faced by Lebanon, Frances support for your country is natural and assumed, Jospin said in the message. The renewed violence that Lebanon came under in recent days has concerned my fellow ministers and I. Once more, the Lebanese people were hit hard by a sudden increase of tension.
The message went on to say that the situation has not stabilized yet and the Monitoring Group in the south is having a hard time trying to meet over the recent developments. I hope the deadlock will not persist.
With respect to the 1996 April Understanding that bans attacks by "Israel" or the resistance from or at civilian areas, Jospin wrote: The understanding and the group monitoring it are the best available means for containing outbreaks of violence that threaten civilians.
France is serious in safeguarding this understanding that has proven several times to be beneficial, Jospin added, pledging that France and the United States, which co-chair the Monitoring Group, will continue to contact concerned governments to secure a meeting for the group as soon as possible.
On Feb. 11, "Israel's" delegates walked out of a Monitoring Group meeting to protest Hizbullahs killing of an "Israeli" soldier. The group has not met since.
Meanwhile, French President Jacques Chirac said on Tuesday that his countrys Middle East policy was unchanged, despite the controversy raised by Jospins comments. He has summoned his premier for a meeting on Wednesday.
In the Middle East, Frances policy is balanced and impartial. That is the policy that contributes most to peace, and its unchanged, Chirac said at a press conference in The Hague, where he is on a state visit.
My ambition is to work with the government to ensure that Frances voice in Europe and in the world is strong, clear and respected, the president added.
French Foreign Minister Hubert Vedrine insisted in various television and radio interviews that Frances impartiality in the Middle East was unchanged.
Before the National Assemblys meeting, Patrick Devedjian, spokesman for Chiracs right-wing Rally for the Republic, said on French Television: When you make a mistake, you shouldnt be arrogant about it.
Rally MP Pierre Lellouche told French Radio that Jospin, who heads Frances Socialist Party, had confused his role as premier and his likely role as presidential candidate in the 2002 presidential elections. You cant try and score political points on such a sensitive subject and get away with it.
He added that while Jospin was entitled to his opinion, he would have been well advised to discuss the matter with Chirac, who usually sets the countrys foreign policy.


Victims families hold peaceful protest(2)
"Relatives of victims of "Israeli" attacks held a peaceful demonstration outside the French ambassadors residence on Mathaf on Tuesday to protest against Premier Jospins comments on Hizbullah.
He had no right to say Hizbullahs actions were terrorist because without them more precious lives would be lost, said Mohammed Yateem, whose son Basil was killed while on duty as a firefighter during the June bombing of the Jamhour power station.

Were not here to make problems. We want to protest what has been said about Hizbullah, Yateem said as he clutched a photograph of his son. There has to be an end to the pain and suffering of the families of those who died and those who are living but still sacrificing their lives in "Israeli" prisons.
My son sacrificed his life while serving his country but he wasnt carrying arms and never killed anyone in his life. Does that make him a terrorist too? he asked, taking out a blown-up picture of charred human remains with a blackened skull in the foreground: Thats my son ­ like charcoal in the ground. Thats the last time I saw him, a few hours after the fire was put out.
The protest, which lasted an hour, ended without any trouble with police at the heavily guarded embassy.
Two men carried banners with the words Jospin is Zionist and not French. A picture of a dead girl spilling out of a car window with the words "Israel's" gift to the children of Lebanon was held up by a young boy, while tearful mothers of detainees in "Israeli" prisons held up pictures of their loved ones. A memorandum denouncing and condemning Jospins remarks was presented to Antoine Sivan, political adviser at the French Embassy.
We are expressing our surprise and pain over the position of Jospin because it was contradictory to the French policy that we are used to, the memorandum said. Hasnt Jospin asked himself where the "Israeli" soldiers are being killed? They are being killed on the land that they have occupied, it continued. Our resistance fighters are not terrorists; they are expressing their faith in the sacredness of our land and we are all willing to be martyrs for the sake of our country. Isnt that what you French did when the Nazis invaded French territories?
How dare Jospin say that Hizbullah are terrorists when theyre the only ones who have protected our country, said Hanieh Fawaz Bdeir, whose son Mohammed Ali Bdeir has been detained in "Israeli" prisons for nine years.
I want my son back, said Damiyah Amar, whose son Ahmad has been detained for the past 14 years. Jospin had no right to say anything against Hizbullah because theyre our only hope of seeing our children again.
The "Israelis" were denying the existence of detainees in their prisons, said Ahmads brother Khaled Amar, without Hizbullah, no one would have believed us.


Khatami: Muslims unite(3)
" Iranian President Mohammad Khatami on Tuesday called on all Muslim countries to back Lebanons fight against "Israel's" occupation.
The Lebanese cause should be the cause of every Muslim country and every Muslim human being, he said after a meeting with Speaker Nabih Berri. That is how we feel about Lebanon in Iran.
Berri, who was in Tehran to head the first meeting of Muslim parliamentarians, also met Khatamis predecessor, Ali Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani.
After holding talks with Iranian counterpart Ali Akbar Nateq Nouri, Berri said that he expressed to the Iranian officials his fears that "Israel's" attacks were meant to provoke tension between Lebanon and Syria.
Berri, who held talks with Iranian Foreign Minister Kamal Kharrazi on his arrival in Tehran on Monday, is due to return to Beirut on Wednesday.
The conference endorsed a document prepared by the Lebanese Parliament calling for the implementation of UN Security Council Resolution 425, which calls for "Israel's" unconditional withdrawal from Lebanon, and created a Union of Muslim Parliamentarians.


Arab Lawyers throw weight behind Lebanon(4)
"The Union of Arab Lawyers has decided to hold its annual conference in Lebanon in a show of support for the countrys steadfastness against "Israeli" aggression, the groups secretary-general said on Tuesday.
The union led a demonstration in Cairo last week to support Lebanons right to defend itself and its rights against Zionists, Farouk Abu Issa said.
Were men of law and know that Lebanon has the right to continue its resistance against the occupation, side by side with Syria, which protects Lebanon as much as Lebanon protects (Syria), he added outside Premier Salim Hoss office after holding talks with him.
To show its support for Lebanon, the union will hold its annual conference in Beirut in October. Many Arab and international rights groups will take part in the meeting, said Abu Issa, who also spoke to President Emile Lahoud and Deputy Speaker Elie Ferzli.
"He was accompanied by a number of other legal officials".


Tenders for Lebanon pullout said imminent(5)
"After more than a month of delays, the Defense Ministry expects to get the go ahead this week to issue tenders for moving and building new positions and other preparations for an IDF* pullback from Lebanon.

"Defense sources said that Chief of General Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaul Mofaz is currently studying the new plan, dubbed New Horizon, and is expected to approve it in the coming days.

Once the approval is given, the Logistics and Technology Branch adds its recommendations for military installations and the Home Front Command adds its for the civilian settlements. It is then passed on to the Defense Ministry, which is responsible for issuing the tenders.

"[The approval] is supposed to happen very soon, in the next couple of days," said one Defense Ministry source. "We expect work to begin within a week of issuing the tenders."

The tenders will be to dismantle most of the IDF outposts in the security zone, moving others, and building new ones. Roads and other infrastructure are to be built to support the new deployment. A new electronic border fence is also supposed to be erected along the international line.

Tenders will also be issued to complete the "protection package" for border settlements and others in the region. This will entail erecting security fences, lighting, electric gates, and guard posts. Some will have new approach roads paved to them so they are not exposed to the border, which in some cases is a few dozen meters away.

Sources said the list of contractors has already been drawn up and priority will be given to those from the confrontation-line area.

Some NIS 300 million has already been allocated to the Defense Ministry for the initial stages of the project, defense sources said. Senior IDF officials estimate the total cost of the redeployment to be about NIS 1 billion.

The tenders are already over a month behind schedule. Senior defense officials said late last year that, according to the original schedule, tenders should have been issued in January and work begun in February. But military sources said that the IDF was prepared to channel added manpower to the front if it looked like work would not be ready by the declared pullback date.

Over the years the border fence has been moved, sometimes into Lebanon and sometimes into "Israel" along more defensible venues. According to the plan, the new fence will demarcate the exact border. Bypass roads will be abandoned.

The IDF outposts in the security zone will be dismantled and some straddling the international border will have to be moved.

But according to Channel 1, if a unilateral withdrawal is implemented, the IDF will not pull back completely along the international border, but maintain a presence in outposts just over the border.

The reasoning behind this would be to preserve some bargaining chips, but it would not eliminate the motive behind the Hizbullah guerrilla war to oust "Israel" completely from Lebanon, Channel 1 said.

A very senior IDF commander said that the IDF has submitted to the government requests for some changes along what the army calls the "purple line," or international border. These include the line around outposts located at key geographical locations.

Mofaz has said that he still holds fast to his position against a unilateral withdrawal. But because Prime Minister Ehud Barak has already and repeatedly declared that the troops will be out by July 7, Mofaz could find himself being asked not if he agrees with the decision to get out, but if he can carry it out".


"Israel" firm on July withdrawal(6)
"Israel" will withdraw its troops from south Lebanon in July even without a Syrian promise to prevent cross-border attacks on its north from Lebanon, a senior "Israeli" Cabinet minister said on Tuesday.
I think that the judgment has been delivered and the army will leave Lebanon at the beginning of July with or without an agreement, Finance Minister Avraham Shohat said during a visit to "Israel's" early-warning station in Mount Hermon, a Syrian mountain occupied by the Jewish state.
Prime Minister Ehud Barak has promised to get his army out of Lebanon, where it has been suffering casualties at the hands of resistance fighters, by July, but has added that this will be by agreement.
Barak, who claims that Syria is able to prevent resistance attacks, said this week that if no agreement were achieved by July "Israel" will know what to do, but did not elaborate.

Military Operations(8)

The Islamic Resistance's (IR) squad of Martyrs Mahmoud Sha'hade and Mohammad Haidar on Tuesday attacked enemy movements in the "Israeli" occupation's outpost in Kassaret al-Oroush in a violent and concentrated manner inflicting definite casualties in the ranks of the enemy.

In the meantime, IR's squad of Martyrs Ahmed Khawaja and Hassan Wehbi targeted a Zionist infantry force in the Kassaret al-Oroush outpost while squad of Martyrs Saed Darweesh and Hussein Moghnieh attacked the Dhour al-Kassara outpost with machine guns and rocket propelled grenades scoring definite hits.

Also, the IR team of martyrs Wafic Zahwi Zehri and Abdel-Rassoul Rodwan Baydoun detonated, at 20:00, a powerful roadside bomb as a Lahdi motorized patrol was traveling on the road linking between the positions of Qabaa and Mashaaroun striking one military vehicle and inflicting all of its occupants.
