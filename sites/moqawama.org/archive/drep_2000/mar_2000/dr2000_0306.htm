Daily Report

March Monday 06-2000:


Introduction

Twenty two years after the "Israeli" invasion of South Lebanon and less than a year after the "Israeli" enemy Prime Minister Ehud Barak first promised, as part of his election campaigning, to "bring the boys home", the "Israeli" government voted yesterday to Barak's proposal to withdraw the troops by July.

However, hundreds of Lebanese and Jordanians held a candle-lit rally outside the offices of the Jordanian prime minister calling for an "Israeli" military pullout from South Lebanon.


"Israeli" Cabinet backs plan for July troop withdrawal
Increasing pressure is being applied on all sides for a negotiated withdrawal
"An end to "Israel's" 22-year occupation of the south drew a step closer on Sunday with the "Israeli" Cabinet voting unanimously in favor of Premier Ehud Baraks proposal to withdraw the troops by July.
Prime Minister Salim Hoss welcomed "Israel's" decision but said he would prefer to see a pullout accompanied by a comprehensive peace settlement.
The "Israeli" Army will deploy on the border with Lebanon by July 2000 and from there will ensure the safety of the northern towns and villages, "Israeli" Cabinet secretary Yitzhak Herzog said after the meeting. The government will act to ensure that this deployment will be carried out in the framework of an agreement.
If no agreement was forthcoming by July, Herzog said the Cabinet would meet again to weigh its options, suggesting the July deadline would still stand.
In the event that conditions arent conducive to an Israeli redeployment in the framework of an agreement, the government will convene at an appropriate time to discuss the method of implementing Cabinet decisions, the statement said.
The announcement came at the end of a two-session debate on consecutive Sundays in which "Israeli" ministers discussed the occupation of the south.
Hoss welcomed the "Israeli" Cabinet vote but voiced suspicion over "Israel's" intentions if a unilateral withdrawal was carried out.
Whether or not the "Israeli" Cabinets withdrawal decision is a mere ploy, we still welcome an "Israeli" pullout from Lebanon that takes place in accordance with UN Resolution 425, at any time, Hoss said.
We would have preferred that such a withdrawal be implemented within a peace agreement. But we do not trust "Israel's" intentions if a unilateral pullout does take place.
In any case we welcome any withdrawal that liberates our land and reinstates the lifeline with our brethren in southern Lebanon. We have demanded a pullout as long as 22 years ago, when UN Security Council Resolution 425 called on "Israel" to withdraw unconditionally from Lebanon to the internationally-recognized border.
Although "Israeli" Cabinet approval for Baraks July deadline was expected, the "Israeli" governments decision is the most significant step toward ending the occupation since the administration of Benjamin Netanyahu formally recognized Resolution 425 in April 1998.
Its the end of the tragedy. Its the boys return home and the end of bleeding in Lebanon, Barak said on "Israeli" television.
Asked if there was a possibility "Israeli" soldiers would remain in Lebanon past July if no agreement was reached with Damascus, Barak told "Israel's" Channel Two television: By July well be deployed along the border.
""Israeli" Minister Haim Ramon said that the Gordian knot which attached "Israel" to Lebanon had been cut.
The cost of staying in Lebanon outweighs the reasons for being there, Ramon said on "Israeli" public television. There was no more political or military logic for staying. Anything we can do from south Lebanon, we can also do from the international border.
Ramon said that if, after two or three months, it became clear that a July agreement with Syria would be impossible, "Israel" would likely withdraw from Lebanon unilaterally.
The "Israeli" army has consistently warned against a unilateral withdrawal, arguing that Hizbullah
and other groups would continue attacking "Israel". On Sunday, "Israel's" chief of staff, General Shaul Mofaz, reiterated the militarys view of the dangers inherent in a unilateral withdrawal.
It could be that we will find ourselves deployed along the international border, and there will still be fighting in the north, he warned.
Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa said last week in an Al-Hayat interview that a unilateral withdrawal would be suicide for "Israel". But "Israel's" deputy defense minister Ephraim Sneh has reawakened the prospect of a unilateral troop withdrawal to the so-called
Purple Line, which follows the international boundary except for a handful of strategic places where the border fence has been pushed a few hundred meters into Lebanon.
Sneh said last Thursday that "Israel" would return to the international frontier if an agreement was reached with Syria and Lebanon. If no agreement was reached, then the troops would keep the incursion zones on the border.
If there is an agreement, its based on the international border, Sneh said. If there is no agreement, we have no such commitment.
Known as technical fences, the incursion zones allow the "Israelis" to dominate high ground with views deep into Lebanon. The UN describes them as permanent violations of the 1949 Armistice Agreement.
Snehs comments contradict assertions in December that "Israeli" troops will retire to the international border and that technical fences would be dismantled.
A withdrawal to the Purple Line would not halt resistance activity. Hizbullah has repeatedly stated it intends to carry on attacking the occupation forces until a full withdrawal to the international boundary is carried out(1           )

"Acting Kiryat Shmona Mayor Sami Malul said it would be a grave mistake for "Israel" to leave Lebanon without an agreement. "This will return life in the North to intolerable. This could lead to another war in Lebanon." The municipality is to discuss the cabinet decision and pass its own resolution to the government.

There were mixed reactions to the decision among residents of the confrontation-line communities. "There is fear of the unknown," said a resident of Kiryat Shmona.

There was similar trepidation in Moshav Margaliyot. "We don't see anyone securing our safety," said moshav council head Eitan Dodi.

Members of Kibbutz Misgav Am expressed hopes the Israeli Army will move the border fence so that it does not abut their land after the redeployment.

Justice Minister Yossi Beilin, one of the first prominent politicians to call for a withdrawal from Lebanon, was visibly moved by the decision. "This is a victory for all those who are not willing to dance to the tune of Hizbullah," he said. "And this is a victory for all those who believed straight thinking would eventually win out over the consensus"(2)


Hundreds hold candle-lit anti-"Israeli" rally outside PM's office(3)
" Hundreds of Lebanese and Jordanians on Sunday held a candle-lit rally outside the offices of the Jordanian prime minister calling for an "Israeli" military pullout from south Lebanon.

""Israel" wants to burn our land and kill our children, Lebanon works for peace and implementation of 425," said a banner in reference to the UN Security Council Resolution calling for unconditional "Israeli" pullout.

The protest came as the "Israeli" cabinet gave its unanimous support Sunday to Prime Minister Ehud Barak's plan to end "Israel's" 22-year occupation of Lebanon by July.

Lebanon's ambassador to Jordan Adib Alamuddin said he hoped the announcement was "sincere and not a ploy", echoing a similar reaction in Beirut by Lebanese Prime Minister Salim al-Hoss, as around 500 protesters holding up Lebanese flags broke into nationalistic songs.

Alamuddin, who led the rally along with his Egyptian and Bahraini counterparts and the Syrian charge d'affaires in Amman, also voiced hope  that Arab popular support for Lebanon following "Israeli" attacks last month that knocked out power stations, will "influence political decision-making".

Jordanian Prime Minister Ayman Majali made a brief appearance at the rally after receiving Alamuddin in his office and reiterated Amman's full support for Lebanon and a comprehensive peace in the Middle East".

"Israel's" "security zone" in South Lebanon(6)
" "Israel's" self-declared "security zone" in South Lebanon, which the cabinet agreed Sunday to quit by July, covers 850 square kilometres (340 square miles), nearly a tenth of the country.

It is a legacy of occupation that stretches back nearly 22 years and has cost "Israel", its allied militia and its guerrilla opponents in the Lebanese resistance dearly in dead and wounded.

"Israel" first invaded Lebanon on March 14, 1978 advancing as far as Beirut and forcing PLO leader Yasser Arafat to flee the city.

"Israel" ignored a UN Security Council resolution passed in the wake of the invasion calling for it to pull out of Lebanon "forthwith", but in 1985 retreated to the positions it currently occupies, arguing that it needed a buffer zone to protect its northern territories from cross-border attack.

Normally "Israel" maintains an estimated 1,000 soldiers, security force members and intelligence staff in the territory, with access to the rest of Lebanon via five strictly controlled crossing points.

But estimates by the UN Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) put the current figure at twice that number.

Reinforcing the "Israeli" presence is the South Lebanon Army (SLA), the pro-"Israeli" militia that is nearly 3,000 strong.

The SLA defends 18 positions in the zone, against seven held by the "Israelis". Their unwanted nickname, the "sandbags" reflects the fact that it is often their soldiers who have borne the brunt of Hizbullah guerilla attacks.

So far this year alone, 11 SLA soldiers have died to seven losses within the "Israeli" forces, and some members are losing heart and deserting.

Ranged against "Israel" and its allies is Hizbullah, which has pledged itself to fight the Jewish state's occupation of Lebanon. The organisation is backed by Syria, Iran and Lebanon.

Caught in the middle are about 4,500 UNIFIL troops, originally sent in by the Security Council in 1978.

They are made up of soldiers from Fiji, Finland, France, Ghana, India, Ireland, Italy, Nepal and Poland. All except the Indian troops are stationed outside the occupied zone.

UNIFIL's original brief, to verify an "Israeli" military withdrawal, restore peace and help the Lebanese government extend its authority over the area, quickly became academic in the face of "Israel's" refusal to respect UN calls to withdraw.

UNIFIL has instead had to provide bomb disposal, first aid and other humanitarian services for villagers in the area caught up in the fighting.

Since 1978, 240 UNIFIL soldiers have been killed -- by deliberate acts of violence or by accident.

Lebanese estimates suggest that of 400,000 civilians living in the region before the occupation, there are now fewer than 125,000 in 167 towns and villages, mainly Shiite but also Christian, Druze and Sunnis.

But despite the violence that has left entire districts deserted, many of those remaining are worried about the financial implications of an "Israeli" withdrawal.

They do not pay tax to the Lebanese government even though since 1995 in symbolic activities the government has maintained roads and electrical and water supplies -- and pays the salaries of civil servants.

About 2,500 southern Lebanese go to work in "Israel", earning twice as much as they would in Lebanon for comparable work.

Many local people also benefit from the money that the UN presence injects into the local economy.

The SLA militia members and 250 others working for the area's civilian administration under "Israeli" control face a particularly uncertain future if "Israel" pulls out.

"SLA commander General Antoine Lahd was condemned to death in his absence by a military court in Beirut for collaborating with "Israel"".
