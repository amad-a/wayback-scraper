Israeli border will get no protection
President issues stern demand that Palestinians plight must be addressed(1)
In the toughest warning to Israel following its decision to withdraw from the south, President Emile Lahoud declared on Wednesday that Lebanon would not protect the Jewish state from possible Palestinian guerrilla attacks in the event of a unilateral pullout.
The president affirmed that unless the plight of Palestinian refugees was settled, Israels security along the northern border would remain precarious.
Israeli hints for security guarantees after (a unilateral) withdrawal and its threats if these guarantees are not secured are totally rejected, the president said in a statement. Accepting them would mean that we have accepted the settlement of Palestinians on our land.
Lahoud said that an Israeli troop withdrawal would only solve part of the problem.
However, another major part will remain, which is the problem of the Palestinian refugees, he said.
How can we guarantee these borders when there are in the Palestinian camps tens of thousands of armed refugees asking for the right to return, he asked. Solving the Palestinian problem, Lahoud said, is above all an Israeli responsibility, since they were evicted by the Israelis in the first place.
The president explained that giving security guarantees to Israel outside a peace settlement will encourage the Jewish state to continue ignoring the Palestinian question.
What will force Israel to find a solution to the Palestinian problem if we were to preserve security on the border after a withdrawal that would take place outside a comprehensive and just peace? he asked.
Lebanon is not ready to return to the atmosphere of the 1970s, or to play the role that Israel has failed to play since its 1982 invasion to end Palestinian guerrilla attacks on Israel.
Lahoud also noted that any Israeli withdrawal from the south and Western Bekaa under the impact of the resistance attacks and the Lebanese and Syrian firm stance is considered a victory for Lebanon and Syria, and a great military and political defeat for Israel.
In Parliament, Speaker Nabih Berri echoed Lahouds comments, asserting that Lebanon will in no event be the guardian of Israels border. Addressing Arab women parliamentarians at Nijmeh Square, the speaker said that international peace and security cannot be guaranteed through a military retreat or through other military moves.
Only once the Israeli occupation of the Arab territory is ended and the Security Councils resolutions implemented would it be possible to achieve peace and security, the speaker noted.
Berri added that the impression which Israel was trying to spread, that the Lebanese state was opposed to a unilateral withdrawal, was completely erroneous. What Lebanon fears, said Berri, is Israels real intentions, on the basis of our experience with Israel over more than a quarter of a century.
He pointed out that any Israeli withdrawal which does not come as part of the implementation of UN Resolution 425, constitutes a tactical step backward, as it does not guarantee that Israel will not undertake future attacks against Lebanon.
It is Lebanon that should be asking for the protection of its border and not Israel, the speaker noted.
Berris visitors quoted him as saying that peace negotiations will be in serious danger if no improvement is registered by the end of April. This does not mean that there is no solution, Berri reportedly said. But the process will become more difficult because the US presidential elections will be getting closer, he noted.
In Damascus, Abdullah al-Ahmar, the No.2 in the Baath Party, blamed Israel for the impasse in the peace talks and criticized Israeli attacks in Lebanon.
Israeli leaders statements on peace are intended to deceive and conceal their true aggressive and racist positions, he said in a speech in Damascus marking the 37th anniversary of the ruling Baath Partys rise to power.
Israel is responsible for the blockage in the peace process because it is sneaking out of its peace obligations, especially the retreat from the Golan and the drawing of the border at the June 4, 1967, line, he said.
He characterized Israels decision to pull its forces out of south Lebanon by July, with or without an agreement with Syria, as obscure, and another Israeli maneuver.
The statements by Lahoud and Berri came after the two men met Wednesday morning in Baabda.

 

Three wounded as Israeli agents shell Lebanese Army post
Violence flared anew in the south on Wednesday, with an Israeli allied tank artillery round wounding three people and an Israeli helicopter gunship destroying a deserted house in Majdal Zoun.
Abbas Najdi, a 19-year-old army conscript, and Ismail Zein, a civilian, were wounded when a tank shell exploded beside the Mansouri checkpoint on the edge of the occupation zones western sector. Four cars were set alight in the explosion and completely destroyed, security sources said.
A total of six tank rounds were fired into the area, one lightly wounding a Fijian UNIFIL peacekeeper manning a checkpoint near Biyout Siyyad in the UN-patrolled no-mans land before the first Israeli proxy checkpoint at the Hamra crossing. Security sources said the Fijians checkpoint also came under heavy machine gun fire.
Security sources said the shelling came from the Israeli agents-manned Biyada outpost on the hills overlooking the Hamra crossing and Mansouri village. The militiamen used an old T-55 tank parked beside the compound to shell the area.
 At the same time as the tank shelling, two Apache helicopters hovering above the Teir Harfa outpost fired three missiles at a house in Majdal Zoun, security sources said. Only one of the missiles struck the house, causing extensive damage. The other two exploded harmlessly.
The concentration of Israeli and collaborator posts on the western tip of the zone has seen the vicinity around Majdal Zoun and Mansouri being among the most dangerous areas on the frontline.
Over the past year, one resident of Mansouri has been killed in the fighting and 12 others wounded.
Wednesdays attack on the Lebanese Army checkpoint was identical to a bombardment on Aug. 3 last year, in which a soldier lost his leg and a civilian was wounded. The helicopter attack also was not the first of its kind on Majdal Zoun.
Last Aug. 10, a pair of Apache helicopters destroyed an abandoned house on the edge of the village, overlooking the occupation zone.
In recent months, the Israelis have used their impressive airborne arsenal to destroy buildings along the frontline. on Jan. 30, Israeli warplanes flattened a half-built house in Kafra. Recently several buildings have been targeted by warplanes and helicopters in Ain Qana, Jarjoueh, Jbaa and Ain Buswar in Iqlim al-Touffah. ..(2)

Arab League expects unified stance at Beirut meeting ..(5)
The Cairo-based Arab League's ministerial council will meet Saturday and Sunday in Beirut in a show of solidarity with Beirut and against Israeli attacks on Lebanon, League officials said.

Foreign ministers from the League's 21 country members and the Palestinian Authority will focus on the situation in Lebanon "to adopt a unified Arab position," League spokesman Talaat Hamed told AFP Wednesday.

However, a foreign ministry source in Beirut said that only 15 countries' foreign ministers will attend, but that every League member except Somalia will be represented at the meeting, as will the Organization of African Unity.

Among the issues to be discussed is a proposal to hold regular Arab summits.

The League decided last month to take the unusual step of holding the ministerial meeting in Beirut after Israeli air raids knocked out power to much of Lebanon and wounded 22 civilians on February 8.

The Israelis had been retaliating for attacks by Lebanese Hezbollah guerrillas who had killed five Israeli soldiers in southern Lebanon.

Since then, two more Israeli troops have been killed in their country's "buffer zone" in southern Lebanon.

The ministerial council plans to adopt "measures of material and political support for Lebanon," said the League's deputy secretary general in charge of Arab Affairs, Ahmed Ben Helli.

"So far, Egypt, Saudi Arabia and Kuwait have given aid for reconstruction in Lebanon, but we are going to submit a joint project at the meeting that would coordinate Arab efforts on this," Ben Helli told AFP.

"It is essential "to show Lebanese citizens that brother states support them."

"Arab solidarity got a boost after the Israeli attack on Lebanon," he said, giving as an example "the warm welcome for the proposal on holding periodic summits," item two on the meeting's agenda.

Yemen proposed the inclusion in the League's charter of a mechanism for holding periodic summits. The charter does not address such summits and says that the ministerial council is the League's highest body.

"That the meeting is being held in Beirut shows that Arab states are fed up. It signals to the world that the situation in Lebanon can also create a positive dynamic," Egyptian political commentator Mohammed Sid-Ahmed said.

"The issue of Arab summits is an additional symbol of the desire for Arab unity," he added.

Sid-Ahmed stressed that the decision to hold the meeting in Beirut flowed from Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak's February 19 solidarity visit to Beirut.

Before Mubarak's surprise trip to Beirut, "the Arab position was characterized by weakness. By going to Lebanon, Mubarak embarrassed certain parties," he added.

 

Lebanese Hezbollah seeks permission to broadcast by satellite ..(6)

Lebanese Hezbollah, which carries out most of the anti-Israeli attacks in south Lebanon, officially requested permission Wednesday to broadcast its television station by satellite.

The Lebanese Information Group, which manages Hezbollah's Al-Manar television station, said in a news release that Communications Minister Issam Naaman promised a Hezbollah delegation to arrange the permit as soon as possible.

Hezbollah member of parliament Mohammed Raad, who led the delegation, told reporters they wanted "to spread resistance news in the Arab world and the whole world."

Hezbollah satellite television would initially broadcast to Arab countries and Europe and later expand into the Americas and Australia, Lebanese Information Group chairman Nayef Krayyem said.

Al-Manar broadcasts 12 hours a day and 30 percent of its programs are news.

The television station produces 75 percent of its programs.

Its statement of purpose is "to safeguard Islam's values and strengthen the civilizing role of the Arab and Islamic nation while focusing on the conflict with the Zionist enemy."

 

LEBANESE WOMEN DETAINED BY ISRAEL RECALL THEIR PLIGHT ..(7)
The Follow-up Committee for the Support of Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons have issued an urgent plea to the international community to secure the release of seven Lebanese women in the Khyam detention center. Two former prisoners recall their plight in the notorious detention center.

Farida Raslan recalls the evening when Israel's proxy militia in occupied south Lebanon stormed her home and kidnapped her. She was taken to the Kyiam detention center which is jointly operated by Israel and the militia. Farida is from the occupied village of Murkaba, and following a resistance operation against Israel, which has occupied Lebanon for 22 years, the Jewish state's proxy militia kidnapped villagers for interrogation. Farida recalls during her six years in the detention center, she was subjected to torture, such as standing for hours while guards threw water at her. Militiamen on several occasions threatened to rape her. She is now an active member in an organization campaigning to have detainees released and is married to a former Khiam prisoner and lives in Beirut. Zeinab al-Hajj was kidnapped in 1988 from her village Aaramta in Jezzine. She was initially released after nine months of detention. Four days later, the militia abducted her for another four years. Zaynab, who had a small clothing store in Aarmta, now lives in Beirut and misses the days when she made her own living. A total of 500 women have been thrown into Khiam since its establishment in 1985. Today seven women remain in the detention center. The Follow-up Committee for the Support of Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons released an urgent plea to the international community to secure their release. Human rights organizations, such as the London-based Amnesty International, have documented cases of torture and abuse in the detention center. There are presently a total of 144 detainees in Khiam.

 

LEBANESE WOMEN CALL ON UN TO HELP RELEASE DETAINEES FROM ISRAELI DETENTION CAMPS ..(8)
First Lady Andree Lahoud earlier launched the Lebanese campaign to promote women's rights. Participants at the event which took place at the press syndicate in Beirut, signed a memorandum condemning Israeli attacks on Lebanon. First Lady Lahoud praised Lebanese women who are resisting Israeli occupation in the south and who remain steadfast in front-line towns and villages. Activists also signed a letter addressed to the United Nations, in which they called for the closure of all Israeli detention centers in occupied southern Lebanon, and the release of Lebanese detainees.

Reports: Israel is vacating Aishyeh post..(9)
Press reports in Beirut quoted sources in southern Lebanon as saying Israel is vacating the Aishyeh post, south of the Jezzine enclave, as part of it's so-called New Horizon plan to withdraw from the region. Sources said Israel instructed its allied militia to start redeploying to areas about five kilometers away from the internationally-recognized border. The report has yet to be confirmed.
