In visit without secrets Abdallah praises resistance(1)
"Saudi Arabias Crown Prince Abdallah bin Abdel-Aziz ended a triumphant 24-hour visit on Friday by underlining his countrys support for Lebanons resistance against "Israeli" occupation.
But perhaps more importantly, the visit signaled a nascent rapprochement between Hizbullah and the Saudi kingdom.
Paying tribute to the resistance, Abdallah asserted that it demonstrated the Arabs ability to regain their rights and pride.
President Emile Lahoud said the visit reinforced Lebanons immunity against "Israel's" destructiveness.
"Lahoud said the visit consolidated Arab solidarity by supporting the unity of the Lebanese and Syrian stands regarding achievement of a just and comprehensive peace; regaining all (Arab) rights through an "Israeli" withdrawal from the south, the Western Beqaa and the Golan; and guaranteeing the right of Palestinian refugees to return to their country.
The president said he valued Saudi Arabias support.
In a statement issued on Friday, Abdallah praised the strong people of Lebanon, who hold the resistance flag in one hand and the tools of reconstruction in the other: A hand that protects and another that builds, a reference to the slogan of the Lebanese Army.
I have full confidence that they will regain their rights, liberate their territory, and reconstruct their country, the prince added. He also asserted that Saudi Arabia was, and still is, with you for better or for worse.
Stressing the support of Arab countries for the Lebanese, Abdallah said Lebanon will not be weak and alone. On the contrary, it is strong through the support of its brethren, from the Atlantic Ocean to the Gulf.
Abdallah met Hoss before ending a four-day tour that also took him to Egypt and Syria.
A senior political source described Abdallahs stop in Beirut as a visit without secrets, suggesting that there was no hidden agenda, but rather an openly announced goal: providing financial and moral support and paying direct and impassioned tribute to the resistance.
This was a significant move through which political observers observed the rise of a new relationship between Hizbullah and Saudi Arabia.
Following the 1979 Iranian revolution, relations between Riyadh and Tehran, which provides important backing for Hizbullah, were strained. But ties have steadily improved since President Mohammad Khatamis election in 1997.
The degree of direct support for the resistance was noticeable in the princes statement, the source commented.
Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak took a similar stand on the resistance during his surprise visit here last month.
According to the source, the Lahoud administrations support for the resistance and adoption of its slogans has helped build Arab support for the resistance.
Abdallahs visit included an unprecedented meeting on Thursday between the crown prince and a delegation of Hizbullah MPs.
Baalback-Hermel MP Ibrahim Amin Sayyed, who heads the partys parliamentary bloc, praised Abdallahs support and said the move was the core of the confrontation with the Zionist enemy.
Another sign of the growing rapprochement was the banners of welcome and praise for the future king of Saudi Arabia in Beiruts southern suburbs.
In an interview with CNN, Saudi Arabias veteran foreign minister, Prince Saudi al-Faisal, said the impression that one should get from this visit is  that everybody should come to the aid of this country.
The series of visits to Lebanon by high-ranking Arab officials has helped to create a strong impression that this solidarity has been revived.
After Mubaraks short stay and the high-profile visits of the Kuwaiti foreign minister and the Saudi delegation, the next 10 days will see the arrival on Monday of Jordanian Foreign Minister Abdel Ilah al-Khatib followed by a visit by Iranian deputy Foreign Minister Mohammed al-Sadr, and the Arab foreign ministers meeting in Beirut on March 11-12".

More "Israelis" back unilateral Lebanon withdrawal: poll(3)
""Israeli" public support for a unilateral troop withdrawal from south Lebanon continues to grow, according to an opinion poll published Friday.

Almost two-thirds of the population, or 61 percent, support a pullout without an agreement with Lebanon or Syria, according to a Gallup poll published in the Maariv newspaper, up from 56 percent previously.

Thirty-one percent are opposed, down from 34 percent previously, according to the survey of 670 people.

Prime Minister Ehud Barak has pledged to bring the troops home by July, even without a peace deal with Syria, but has warned that a unilateral withdrawal could be more bloody.

Calls for a pullout have mounted following the death of seven "Israeli" soldiers this year in guerrilla attacks led by the Hizbullah.

The poll, conducted on Wednesday, the very day Barak lost a crucial first round parliamentary vote on his peace plans with Syria.

But the poll showed "Israelis" split 50-50 on whether they would support a peace agreement in a promised referendum on a return of the occupied Golan Heights to Syria.

And 69 percent said they did not believe there was a chance of the Syrians agreeing to a peace deal that does not include a withdrawal from the Golan to the lines in place before the June 1967 war when "Israel" captured the plateau.

Barak's coalition government suffered a blow on Wednesday when parliament supported a bill that would make it harder to win public support for a Golan handover, by requiring a majority of all registered voters rather than just those who vote.

The Gallup poll carried a margin of error of 4.5 percent".


"Israeli" navy detains Lebanese fishermen(4)

" Some 30 Lebanese fishermen were detained and interrogated by the "Israeli" navy Friday for four hours off the southern port city of Tyre, Lebanese police said.

The fishermen had gone at dawn in six boats to the help of another crew in danger of sinking when they were surrounded by two "Israeli" warships and three gunboats.

They were questioned for four hours before being allowed to return to Tyre, the police said.

The "Israeli" navy bans Lebanese fishermen from venturing more than two kilometres (1.5 miles) offshore on the pretext of preventing the infiltration of anti-"Israeli" guerrillas into northern "Israel" or "Israeli"-occupied southern Lebanon.

"Friday's incident took place less than one kilometre from the shore", the police said.

"Israel's" human sandbags see the writing on the wall(8)
Nicholas Blanford concludes his report on the crumbling morale of Antoine Lahds renegade force as the military withdrawal pledged by Ehud Barak draws ever nearer

"The fate of the militiamen serving with the SLA* could differ drastically depending on whether "Israel" withdraws unilaterally or as part of an agreement with Lebanon and Syria.
Despite assurances from the "Israelis" that the SLA will not be abandoned in the event of a unilateral troop pullout, the question of what to do with the estimated 1,500 militiamen is posing a considerable dilemma for the "Israeli" government, military sources say.
On the other hand, a coordinated withdrawal should lead to an organized dismantling of the SLA and the deployment of peacekeeping forces into the former occupation zone. The militiamen will be given the option of leaving the country or remaining behind, probably to face minimal terms of imprisonment.
In the event of a unilateral withdrawal, the 700 to 1,000 "Israeli" troops remaining in the zone could probably pull out of Lebanon in the space of two or three days at most, security sources say. The retreating soldiers would likely receive constant air support as well as a blanket artillery bombardment north of the zone to provide covering fire against any last offensive by the resistance.
The "Israeli" Army would then take up positions along the frontier while Barak repeats his warning that any cross-border attacks will meet with severe reprisals.
The spanner in the works of this otherwise relatively straightforward scheme is the fate of the SLA.
It would be politically unacceptable for "Israel" to simply abandon their allies after more than two decades of cooperation and inter-dependence. Many "Israelis", including members of the government such as Deputy Defense Minister Ephraim Sneh, insist that the Jewish state owes the SLA some degree of protection.
If it hadnt been for the SLA, the "Israelis" would have left a long time ago, said a teacher from the Maronite village of Rmeish in the western sector. The "Israelis" have a guilt complex about how to deal with them after a withdrawal. Every time Barak says hes going to leave, the "Israelis" meet the SLA to calm them down.
The SLA mans the frontline posts on the edge of the zone while most "Israeli" positions are to the rear along the border. As such the SLA have been described as "Israel's" sandbags, the first layer of defense against the resistance. If the order for a unilateral withdrawal is given, then the logistics of disbanding this first layer of defense while still under fire from the resistance would be formidable.
The "Israelis" would have to arrange the removal from Lebanon not only of those militiamen who wish to leave the country but their families as well. Furthermore, assuming that the Lebanese Army and UNIFIL are not deployed in the areas unilaterally vacated by the "Israelis", the resulting security vacuum could prompt many militiamen, who would have stayed in the event of a coordinated withdrawal, to abandon their homes as well.
A Western military source said the fate of the SLA in the event of a unilateral withdrawal is one of the key unanswered questions.
The "Israelis" keep telling the SLA not to worry, but I dont think they know what to do with them, the source said.
To defer this hazardous and huge logistical undertaking, it has been speculated that the "Israelis" will create a temporary solution for the militia by deploying them in a buffer strip along the frontier.
Before he was assassinated in January, Aql Hachem, the imposing SLA commander of the western sector, was touted as a possible leader for the post-withdrawal buffer force.
Antoine Lahd, the former army general who commands the SLA, has to all intents and purposes quit the militia. Although no formal announcement has been made ­ nor will be made given the likelihood that the militia will be disbanded in the near future ­ Lahd is a leader in name only, spending more and more time with his family in France.
With or without Lahd, the prospect of a continued SLA presence is welcomed by some of the more resolute supporters of the militia. Of course, this is the best solution, said Elias, a resident of Qlayaa who left the militia three months ago after 15 years service but still retains close links.
We have the will to continue but it would be difficult unless the "Israelis" supplied us with weapons.
Even with the support of the "Israelis", however, an SLA buffer force is an impractical solution. Seventy percent of SLA personnel are Shiite and Druze and most would probably abandon the militia the moment the "Israelis" retreat to the border.
Even Lahd has said that if theres a unilateral withdrawal then the Shiites in the militia will drop their arms because they dont really feel theyre members of the SLA, said the teacher from Rmeish. Without an enormous influx of new recruits, the SLA would have considerable difficulties manning a zone which ­ to accommodate "Israel's" security needs ­ would have to stretch from Naqoura on the coast all the way to the tri-border area with Syria and "Israel", a distance of 73 kilometers.
The demography of the border area also would hamper an efficient defense of the zone. Much of the strip is composed of pockets of Christian villages ­ such as Rmeish and Ain Ibl in the western sector and Marjeyoun and Qlayaa in the eastern sector ­ surrounded by Shiite villages.
Even in the unlikely event that all the Christians opted to remain in the militia, their strongholds would be surrounded by an unsympathetic and potentially hostile population of Shiites.
It doesnt make sense to leave the SLA behind as a fighting force, said the Western military source. There are more than 1,000 Shiites in the militia. Theyll all resign when the "Israelis" leave. This scheme of a buffer zone is just wishful thinking from some of the old (Saad) Haddad crowd.
Another suggestion being aired is the establishment of a cordon sanitaire along the border manned by UNIFIL peacekeepers and possibly French troops as an interim measure until peace is concluded.
I think the "Israelis" want this buffer zone. It would be to protect the residents in the south as much as the settlements in "Israel", said Michel, a militiaman from Rmeish.
Ali, a Shiite former militiaman from Aita Shaab, also believed that a multinational force would have to be deployed in the south if the "Israelis" withdraw unilaterally like Kosovo or East Timor.
However, UNIFIL, and any other foreign force, can only deploy in the vacated area at the request of the government. Most observers believe that if "Israel" withdraws unilaterally, UNIFIL and the Lebanese Army will remain in their present positions north of the occupation zone. The zone will remain a security vacuum until a comprehensive peace deal is concluded.
UNIFIL spokesman Timur Goksel admitted that the role of the peacekeepers following a unilateral withdrawal remained unclear.
This is a pertinent question that many people would like answered, but no-one seems to know. Nobody is talking about it, he said.
If Ehud Barak achieves his goal of securing a troop pullout within the framework of an agreement with Lebanon and Syria, the prospects for the former occupation zone are considerably brighter. With the goodwill of all parties, it should not be difficult to arrange the replacement of "Israeli" troops with UNIFIL peacekeepers, French troops and the Lebanese Army.
The presence of foreign peacekeeping forces may encourage many junior ranking members of the disbanded SLA to stay in Lebanon. Although the government refuses to offer any guarantees of immunity from prosecution for SLA members, it is likely that most militiamen ­ those who were pressganged or joined to earn a living ­ will receive a token punishment.
This could fall along similar lines to the prison sentences handed down to the scores of SLA men who turned themselves in to the authorities following the militias retreat from the Jezzine enclave in June.
"The hard-core militiamen ­ long-serving officers or those with blood on their hands who have received death sentences in absentia ­ will almost certainly depart Lebanon with the "Israeli" Army".


"Israel's" proxy militia: Who they are and where theyre at(9)

History

The Army of Free Lebanon militia was founded in 1976 by Saad Haddad, a former major in the Lebanese Army. Antoine Lahd, a former general in the Lebanese Army, replaced Haddad following the latters death in 1985. The "Israeli"-allied militia was renamed the South Lebanon Army.
The militia originally numbered around 3,000 men. Today the figure is closer to 1,600 distributed in 48 positions and five garrisons.

Mission

Since "Israeli" troops withdrew from the rest of Lebanon in 1985, the mission of the militia has been to man frontline positions under direct "Israeli" control to prevent the resistance from infiltrating the occupation zone. The "Israeli"-proxy militia does not patrol the whole zone as the "Israelis" control the hilltop positions near the international border and other key positions.
Regular militiamen are paid $500 a month, junior officers $700 a month and senior officers around $900 a month. Some 2,500-3,000 relatives of the militiamen have jobs in "Israel".

Composition

The militia is composed of two brigades: the eastern brigade, headed by Nabih Rifai and the Western Brigade, commanded by Fares Kassis, who replaced Aql Hachem. Other than the brigades, the "Israeli"-allied militia also includes the independent Squad 20. The official deputy commander of the militia is Karamallah Said.

Deployment

The area of operations of the Western Brigade stretches from the coast to the border with "Israel" between Blida and Odaisseh in the central sector.
Its headquarters are located in the former Lebanese Army barracks in Bint Jbeil. The brigade is composed of three battalions: the 70th, the 80th and the 81st. The brigade includes around 700 men distributed in 22 positions and one headquarters.
The brigade controls the crossing points of Hamra on the coast and Beit Yahoun.

The Eastern Brigade operates north of the Litani River to Aramta and eastward from Kfar Kila to Shibaa. The headquarters is in Marjeyoun. The brigade is composed of three battalions: the 90th, the 10th and the 30th.
The brigade has around 750 men distributed in about 26 positions and four garrisons. In addition to the three battalions, the brigade is composed of a command group of 150 men and a reserve force which has 45 men.
The crossing points of Kfar Tibnite, Rihan and Zimraya fall under its authority.

The Independent 20th Battalion, headquartered in Hasbaya, consists of around 170 men in five companies.
