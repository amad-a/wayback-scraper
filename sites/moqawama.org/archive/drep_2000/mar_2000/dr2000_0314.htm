Two dead as Israeli agents attack checkpoint
Indiscriminate fire was militias deliberate attempt to harm civilians  (1)
A soldier and a civilian were killed, and two other people were wounded after the Israeli-allied militiamen fired tank rounds at an Army checkpoint near Mansouri on Monday.
The shelling was described as a deliberate attempt to cause civilian casualties.
The militias deadly attack came just five days after an identical bombardment at the same checkpoint wounded a soldier and a civilian.
Mondays shelling was seen as a second attempt by the militia to incur civilian casualties in a bid to provoke a cross-border rocket attack into northern Galilee in the hope of scuttling the Israeli governments intention to withdraw by July.
The Israeli Army, apparently fearing a response from Hizbullah, immediately launched warplanes to patrol the skies above the south

The Army said that a soldier, Abdel-Salam Hanqir, was killed and another soldier, Ali Abbas, was wounded. The civilian fatality was named as Hassan Zeeni. His father, Ali Zeeni, was wounded. The Zeeni family own a gas station close to the checkpoint. So far this year, two civilians have been killed and another 43 wounded in the fighting.
Sources said that UNIFIL received no customary shell warning from the Israeli Army before 11 105mm tank shells and 120mm mortar rounds began exploding on and near the checkpoint. The checkpoint was hit by three rounds fired from the militia-operated T54 tank located near the Biyada position on the ridge overlooking Mansouri, security sources said.
The Army said that it retaliated by shelling the Hardoun post with tank fire.
A senior security source described the Lahdi bombardment as a beautiful trap, designed to get the Lebanese engaged.
The source said that the Israeli Army had stepped up its pre-emptive artillery bombardments in recent days, with some 400 shells fired between Thursday and Monday. Clearly the Israeli Armys trigger finger has been allowed more freedom, the source said.
The source said the Israeli proxy militia has also been given a free hand to fire its antiquated Soviet-era tanks, most of which are no longer mobile and serve as artillery platforms beside the collaborator positions. The source said that Mondays fatal barrage on the Army checkpoint was bound to happen.
The militia has several times in the past sparked serious escalations in the south when indiscriminate artillery fire has caused civilian casualties and led to retaliatory cross-border Katyusha attacks by Hizbullah.
The source said that Mondays incident was clearly a deliberate attempt to incur casualties as the Israeli agents had not attempted to hit the source of Amal mortar fire from the attack on Hardoun. With the militia facing an uncertain future, the source said that it could serve the militias interest to stir up an escalation at this time.
The shelling came hours after Israeli warplanes staged precision air strikes against two positions manned by the Palestinian Fatah Intifadah group in the Bekaa, one near Raite and the other near Yanta.
An artillery cannon, and a truck were reported damaged when nine missiles struck Wadi Aswad, 2 kilometers north of Yanta. The floor of the wadi was littered with shrapnel from the air strikes as a handful of armed Palestinian fighters patrolled the scene. They said there were no casualties from the raids, although there were bloodstains on a track leading into the wadi.
The raids were seen as a response to a statement made by President Emile Lahoud last week in which he warned that Lebanon would not stop Palestinian fighters from attacking Israel unless refugees were allowed to return to their former homes. In May, 1998, 10 members of Fatah Intifada were killed and 27 wounded when Israeli warplanes bombed a base belonging to the group.
The group, lead by Colonel Abu Musa, split from Yasser Arafats mainstream Fatah movement in 1983 and opposes the peace process.

Israels anger confirms conference was success(2)
Arab League secretary-general Esmat Abdel-Meguid on Monday said Israels anger over Arab foreign ministers meeting was a sign of the conferences success.
Israels angry reaction proves that our meeting in Beirut has achieved its goals, he said after meeting with Speaker Nabih Berri. The meeting was historic, gave Lebanon real support and shows that the Arabs have a better approach to their problems.
Abdel-Meguid, who left for his office in Cairo in the afternoon, sponsored the meeting, which grouped 22 Arab foreign ministers in Beirut on Saturday as a show of solidarity for Lebanon after the recent Israeli attacks and threats.
Israeli officials chided the meeting because in one of their 18-point resolutions, the Arab League ministers called on countries that have relations with Israel to reconsider associating with the Jewish state.
Israel will pull out from southern Lebanon and the Golan, Abdel-Meguid said, responding to a question over Israels decision to pull out from the south by July 7.
He denied that the Arab ministers kept quiet on the Israeli decision to withdraw because Syria and Egypt had opposing views on the pullout.
Berri also received a delegation from the Federation of Arab Labor Unions, led by the groups secretary-general Hassan Jammam, who said that the visit was an expression of solidarity with Lebanon.
Accompanied with labor leaders from Lebanon, Egypt, Kuwait, Tunisia and Syria, Jammam said: Arab laborers fully support the Lebanese resistance movement.
Tyre MP Ali Khalil, chairman of Parliaments Foreign Affairs Committee, echoed Abdel-Meguids sentiment about Israeli anger over the Arab meeting.
The enemy is usually angered by Arab solidarity. Its anger means that the meeting succeeded in giving Lebanon and Syria support for their struggle to liberate their territories from Israeli occupation, he said after meeting Prime Minister Salim Hoss.
In addition, Baalbek-Hermel MP Ammar Musawi, a member of Hizbullahs parliamentary bloc, said: We are satisfied with the Arab foreign ministers decisions that made it clear to the enemy that Lebanon and its resistance are not alone. The decisions need follow-up.
Transport Minister Najib Mikati, during a lecture at the Louaizeh University in Kesroun, said that the coming days will be full of challenges that require a unified national stance.
He described the Israeli decision to withdraw from Lebanon as a new Israeli maneuver that seeks to single out and weaken Lebanon so that it can break the simultaneity of the Lebanese and the Syrian peace tracks.
Asked about the future of Hizbullah if there is a peace accord, Mikati said: The resistance is a result of the occupation. Once the occupation is over, Hizbullah will continue to play its political role. The group has taken part in all elections held since the end of the war.
He also said that Israels pullout from Lebanon is not linked to Syrias military presence here. A pullout from Lebanon is a Syrian strategic goal.

Dirani accuses Israeli jailers of rape and torture  (3)
Imprisoned Lebanese Hezbollah militant Mustafa Dirani filed a 1.5 million dollar lawsuit against Israel on Monday, claiming he has been systematically beaten and raped by his jailers.

And the group he once led issued a statement from Beirut promising that Israel would pay "one way or another."

In Israel, lawyer Tzvi Rish, representing the plaintiff, said: "Mustafa Dirani has been harshly tortured and kept completely naked for a month by Shin Beth investigators, and an Israeli soldier was even allowed to sodomise him several times."

Dirani is one of several leading Hezbollah figures being held by Israel as bargaining chips to secure the return of missing Israeli servicemen, including air force navigator Ron Arad who was captured in Lebanon in 1986.

He was abducted from his Lebanese home by armed Israeli commandos in May 1994 and has since been held under "administrative detention" without charge or trial, a system denounced by human rights groups as a violation of international law.

Rish, who has long campaigned for the release of the prisoners, said the suit was filed against the state of Israel in a Tel Aviv court Monday, but added: "Nothing can compensate my client for the very serious attacks on his honour."

He said Dirani had also endured beatings, sleep deprivation and been forced to squat for hours with his hands tied behind his back.

"Mustapha Dirani was also savagely raped with the aid of a club, and he was refused all medical assistance," he added.

Israeli officials have yet to respond to the accusations.

The group that Dirani led, "Faithful Amal" issued a statement Monday that said: "The Israeli enemy will pay sooner or later for these acts."

The group called on the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) to intervene to ensure Dirani's safety and asked Lebanese President Emile Lahoud to alert the international community to the case.

The group described the "shameful" methods used by the Israelis as "Nazi."

According to the Israeli human rights group B'Tselem, around 125 Lebanese are still held in the notorious Khiam jail in the Israeli occupied zone of south Lebanon and another 14 in jails in Israel.

A vengeful Israeli militia places Ain Tinta under siege  (4))
Around 100 residents of the tiny hamlet of Ain Tinta in the western Bekaa have been besieged in their homes for almost two weeks after the Israeli allied militia imposed a crackdown on the remote frontline district.
The two narrow lanes leading to the hilltop community from the villages of Mimes and Khalawat are regularly targeted by heavy machine-gun fire from the militia-manned outposts in Ain Qenya and Shwaya, 2 kilometers away on the other side of a steep brush-covered wadi.
Any villagers trying to leave Ain Tinta face Lahdi gunners. The wooded slopes of the wadi between Mimes and Ain Tinta are pounded daily by tank and artillery fire. Flares and tracer bullets light up the night sky.
We cant sleep with all the shelling, said Salman Maadah, a resident of Mimes. We cant even walk 100 meters from our houses because they start shooting at us. Its a nightmare.
The ordeal began two weeks ago after five militiamen were killed on the outskirts of Ain Qenya by Hizbullah fighters, taking advantage of ample ground cover and rugged terrain to stage a rare infiltration of the usually-peaceful eastern sector.
The ambush stunned the militia and they have since turned the craggy landscape of pine forests and olive groves just north of the frontline into a free-fire zone to prevent a repetition of the fatal Hizbullah attack.
The collaborators issued threats on Voice of the South. Weve been living in fear, said Nayef Abul-Izz, also from Mimes, a mixed Druze-Christian village.
The electricity line which passes through occupied Hasbaya before reaching Mimes and Ain Tinta was cut by the Israeli agents and the residents were forced to rely on generators.
The only outside help for Ain Tinta has come from the International Committee for the Red Cross which sent a team into the village last week.
The curfew means that we cant bring in doctors in an emergency, said an Ain Tinta resident staying in Khalawat. The ICRC were shot at by the collaborators when they drove into the village. Since then no one has entered and no one has left. He said that while there was ample food in the village, heating oil was running low.
The crackdown has seen at least one casualty. Kamal Abu Ibrahim was struck by bullet shards and stones when he came under heavy machine gun fire last Thursday while tending his fields near Ain Tinta.
Abu Ibrahim said that he had been working in front of the militia for about half and hour when they began shooting.
There were bullets flying all around me, then I was hit in the leg. I fell to the ground and lay there for 15 minutes as they kept shooting, he said.
Abu Ibrahim said that he had managed to creep away through the undergrowth and return to his home in Khalawat.
Why did they want to kill me? Did they see any Hizbullah people where I was farming? They could see me with the naked eye and could tell I was not with the resistance, he said, indicating his black shirwal, shirt and white skullcap.
Abu Ibrahim said that many people in Khalawat were related through marriage to militiaman in Ain Qenya. Two of the five militiamen killed in the ambush were distant relatives of mine, he said.
He rejected any loyalty for his militia kin in their outposts on the other side of the wadi. If the situation stays like this, well all join Hizbullah and start shooting back at them, he said.
