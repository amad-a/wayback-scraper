Tardy israeli allied defectors will be cut no slack  (1)
With pullout confirmed, Hizbullah MP Raad sees no need for sympathy

The israeli proxy militia`s last chance for clemency from the government vanished when Israel confirmed as policy a July withdrawal from the south, Hizbullah MP Mohammed Raad said on Friday.
From now on all militiamen who defect from the militia face the full punishment of the law, he said.
For over two years we have been telling them to defect. If they decide to defect only from now, then they never took us seriously, Raad told The Daily Star.
Raad, an MP for Nabatieh, said that most militia defectors have received prison sentences of about six months to a year, reduced from the statutory three to 10 years.
In 1997, Hizbullah introduced a bill in Parliament that would pardon or considerably reduce all sentences on any militiamen who deserted the militia within four months of the date the legislation became law. However, the bill was never passed.
None of the parliamentary blocs approved of the bill, Raad said.
The MP said militiamen who had failed to defect earlier had now left it too late.
If a person wanted to repent, he should have done so before the date for the end of the occupation was announced, he said.
Hizbullahs public profile is riding high at present. Israels decision to turn Premier Ehud Baraks promise of a July withdrawal into policy, with or without an agreement with Syria, has been translated by many as an implicit declaration of military defeat by Hizbullah.
The party also benefited from the response of the Arab world, which rallied to Lebanons support in the wake of Israels devastating air strikes against power installations last month and the subsequent unsympathetic remarks from French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin and the US State Department.
That outpouring of support was manifested by the Arab Leagues choice of Beirut as the venue for its two-day conference, beginning Saturday, and the visit to Lebanon of President Hosni Mubarak, the first Egyptian head of state to visit Beirut in more than 40 years.
Also, Saudi Crown Prince Abdullah bin Abdel-Aziz recently showered the resistance with praise following an unprecedented meeting with Raad and other Hizbullah MPs.
The overwhelming support of the Arab people such as in Jordan, Palestine and Egypt has pressured governments to come out in support of Lebanon, especially those leaders who are viewed as representatives of the Arabs, Raad said. This also explains why Emir Abdullah met with us. Since most Arab countries look at Saudi Arabia as a big brother, it was in their interest to show support.
Raad argued that the popular acclaim Hizbullah has received from the Arab world has alarmed the US. He suggested that Washington had encouraged the United Arab Emirates to declare it would stay away from the Arab League meeting if Iran attended.
 The United States would have been bothered by Iran attending the conference because Iran would have influenced the Arabs to pay less attention to the peace process, Raad asserted.
The Hizbullah MP insisted that, despite Israels decision to withdraw from Lebanon by July, the resistance would continue attacking israeli troops until the occupation ended.
Israel hopes to strike a deal with Syria before July in which Damascus would bring about a ceasefire in the south, letting the israeli Army withdraw quietly.
But Raad said that no such ceasefire would be demanded by Syria, nor granted by Hizbullah.
Syria never tells the resistance what to do, he said. Syria will not be disturbed if we continue attacking the israelis as they retreat ... and the israelis will cry.

All for one is the battlecry (2)
On the eve of a milestone Arab League foreign ministers meeting, diplomatic sources said on Friday that Lebanons positions on key issues looked likely to be adopted by the conference.
Some fine-tuning of the final communique ­ dealing with regional peace, support for the resistance, the return of Palestinian refugees, reviewing normalization with Israel and financial assistance ­ took place during talks between Prime Minister Salim Hoss and Esmat Abdel-Meguid, the Arab Leagues secretary-general, the sources said.

Meanwhile, Arab foreign ministers arriving on Friday voiced unanimous support for firm and decisive resolutions reflecting the extent of Arab solidarity for Lebanon and during regional peace negotiations.
The flurry of sometimes-chaotic arrivals at Beirut International Airport, where visiting officials addressed dozens of reporters packing the VIP lounge, began at 2pm.
Egyptian Foreign Minister Amr Musa, who arrived on a private plane at 4:10pm, said the meeting represented a strong message of the Arab Leagues support for member countries as well as regional peace.
The meeting also will show that the Arabs want dialogue and want to look out for each others interests, he said.
Musa, who was greeted by Prime Minister Salim Hoss representative, Post and Telecommunications Minister Issam Naaman, said he hoped that discussions would highlight the countrys collective desire to engage in fruitful dialogue about Arab issues.
Musa gave an upbeat assessment of the meeting, which he said was needed to halt israeli aggression against Lebanon.
It will also herald the start of a constructive Arab dialogue, he said. The goal is to give out a positive Arab stance regarding a number of issues .
Its very important for us to put an end to the events that led to the destruction of Lebanons vital infrastructure, he said.
The Egyptian minister also dismissed previous negative reports about the state of Arab disarray and lack of solidarity.
The Arab stance was never as bad as it was portrayed. There has always been a lot of understanding and consultation among Arab states, he said.
Musa stressed that he backed President Emile Lahouds position regarding a unilateral israeli withdrawal from the south.
The Egyptians will support anything the Lebanese decide on, he said.
 Bahrain`s Forign Minister Sheikh Mohamad Bin Mubarak Al-Khalifa said he hoped the meeting would result in resolutions that would aid Lebanon on various levels, including financially.
I think each Arab state will help Lebanon in any way it can, be it politically or financially, he said.
Any attack on Lebanons territory is an attack against all Arabs. The League will take firm and fruitful resolutions. The whole world can see for itself how Israel is breaching all charters and United Nations resolutions, he added.
He also voiced support for the convening of an Arab summit meeting in support of Lebanon, an idea that has been brought up in recent weeks but brushed aside due to the wide-ranging inter-Arab disputes that have prevented a plenary summit since the 1990 Iraqi invasion of Kuwait.
Yemens Foreign Minister Minister, Abdel-Qader Bajamal, told reporters at the airport that the meeting would not merely be an exercise in verbal support for Lebanon.
I dont believe we came all the way here just to say a few words, he said.
Bajamal added that it was only natural for Lebanon to receive financial assistance for its struggle against Israel. He said he hoped the meeting could help map out a common Arab strategy for the future.
Kuwaits Foreign Minister, Sheikh Sabah al-Ahmad al-Sabah, who arrived on a private plane and was greeted by both Shalaq and Information Minister Anwar Khalil, said he welcomed any event that would bring Arabs together.
 Sheikh Sabah also praised last weeks visit to Beirut by Saudi Crown Prince Abdullah bin Abdel-Aziz, saying it reflected the Arab worlds desire not to leave Lebanon isolated in the conflict with Israel.
Rashed Abdullah al-Nouaimi, the foreign minister of the United Arab Emirates, who arrived on a private plane at 4:30pm, praised the resistances steadfastness against the occupation, describing it as truly honorable. He, too, backed the idea of an Arab summit meeting.
Hoss and Minister of Economy and Industry Nasser Saidi were on hand at 5:10pm to officially welcome Qatars Crown Prince, Sheikh Jassem Bin Hamad Al-Thani, and Foreign Minister Sheikh Hamad Bin Jassem Bin Jabr.
 Tunisian Foreign Minister Al-Habib bin Yehia, who landed on a commercial plane at 5:15pm, was received by Naaman and Saidi.
We are here to express our support for Lebanon regarding Israels attacks that violated international laws and Lebanons sovereignty, he told reporters, adding: I hope that the meeting will pave the way for an Arab summit.
Omans foreign minister, Youssef bin Alawi, was received by Saidi at 6pm. Asked about provision of financial aid to Lebanon, he said: Oman has always supported Lebanon and will continue to do its duty toward this brotherly country.

 

Sharaa: Israel will never dictate conditions to us (3)
Israel will never shake the Arabs resolve to regain their territorial rights, no matter how much pressure it applies to Lebanon and Syria or maneuvers to split Arab ranks.
This was Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaas message to Israel as he arrived in Beirut on Friday for the Solidarity and Support for Lebanon Arab conference, convening on Saturday at the Grand Serail.
The veteran Syrian diplomat was afforded an official reception at the Masnaa border post as he drove from Damascus. Public Works Minister Najib Mikati was on hand to meet him.

As a sisterly country, we fully support the solidarity with Lebanon that were experiencing. Were certain that the Arab states would not waver in their support for Lebanon, and we in Syria, who have offered great sacrifices on behalf of our brethren in Lebanon, will continue to do so, because no matter what Israel does it will never dictate its conditions to Lebanon or Syria, Sharaa declared.
As for Israels argument that Damascus was concerned about an israeli withdrawal from south Lebanon because it could lose an important bargaining chip in future peace talks, Sharaa said: We are for an unconditional israeli withdrawal from any occupied Arab territory. We want a comprehensive and just peace, based on international resolutions calling for a land-for-peace deal and a solution to the problem of the Palestinian refugees.
Sharaas remark reflected Syrias backing for Beiruts quest to move the issue of refugees into future bilateral negotiations with Israel, rather than delay the issue until a later stage of the process.
Later in Beirut, Sharaa conferred with Prime Minister Salim Hoss for an hour. He later stressed the importance of the Arab Leagues decision to shift the meeting from Cairo to Beirut following the Feb. 8 israeli attacks on Lebanons civilian infrastructure.
Its an expression of Arab solidarity with Lebanon and support for the resistance against Israel, Sharaa said. israeli aggression on Lebanon must cease.
Returning to Israels misconceptions about Syrias position on Baraks threat to withdraw from the south by July 7, Sharaa said: We welcome an unconditional withdrawal within the framework of Resolution 425.
Suggestions that Syria is annoyed or perplexed, or that it does not wish to see Israel pull out to the internationally recognized border are wrong and baseless.
This only confirms Israels bad intentions toward Lebanon and the Arabs, Sharaa said, hinting that the Jewish state may be plotting a new conflict after its retreat and blame it on Syria.
In Damascus, a government newspaper dismissed israeli efforts to revive peace talks with the Palestinians as a move to overshadow the Beirut conference.
Israel tries before the meeting starts to gesture one way or another that its involved in working for peace in the region, Al-Baath said. Its not the first time it maneuvers to defeat the results of an Arab meeting viewed as strengthening the Arab position against Israel.

 

Abdel-Meguid hails bravery of resistance (4)
Arab League Secretary-General Esmat Abdel-Meguid hailed the bravery of the resistance movement against Israels occupation on Friday as well as Lebanese-Syrian coordination in the Middle East peace talks.
The meeting of Arab foreign ministers in Beirut on Saturday and Sunday will be a clear and significant message to the whole world that Arab countries support Lebanon, he said after talks with President Emile Lahoud. Lebanons strong, courageous and wise resistance shows that Arab rights will never be lost. I say this being an Arab before being the secretary-general of the Arab League.
Asked if the meeting would confirm Lebanons coordination with Syria in the peace talks, Abdel-Meguid said: The relations between the two countries are natural. The Lebanese-Syrian coordination, interaction and integration are basic to both of their interests.
Abdel-Meguid, who arrived here on Thursday night from London, will preside over the Arab League-sponsored meeting of Arab foreign ministers.
The meeting was moved to Beirut from the leagues Cairo headquarters as a show of support for Lebanon, which has been building up since Israels air raids that leveled three power stations in Lebanon on Feb. 8.
Lahoud, who awarded Abdel-Meguid the highest rank of the Order the Cedars, said: The meeting is an expression of Arab support for the Lebanese-Syrian steadfastness against Israels attacks and for the countries efforts to retrieve their rights.
Earlier, Abdel-Meguid met Prime Minister Salim Hoss to put the final touches to preparations for the ministerial meeting. I salute the Lebanese and resistance for their admirable bravery in the face of repeated israeli aggression, Abdel-Meguid said afterwards.
We intend in these two days to bring the spirit of the Arab League to Beirut. I shall call this landmark gathering the meeting of solidarity and support for Lebanon, he added.
 Late on Friday, Abdel-Meguid met the nine members of Parliaments Loyalty to the Resistance bloc at the Palm Beach Hotel where he is staying.
Hoss, who doubles as foreign minister, conferred separately with Iraqi Foreign Minister Mohammed Saeed Sahhaf, whose country chairs the current Arab League session.
I discussed with Prime Minister Hoss the resolutions that may come out of the Arab ministerial meeting, particularly those pertaining to practical and financial support to Lebanon, Sahhaf said.
Hoss also received the foreign ministers of Oman and Yemen, Youssef bin Alawi bin Abdullah and Abdel-Qader Bajamal.
Former Foreign Minister Fares Boueiz received Egyptian Foreign Minister Amr Musa, who said: The Arab League cannot be held responsible for old promises to give aid to Lebanon. We must look into means to help Lebanon, regardless of past pledges.
 

Israel against taking in proxy militia after promised Lebanon pullout  (5)
Israel is strongly opposed to granting asylum to members of its proxy militia and their families following the pullout from south Lebanon which it has pledged to conduct by July, Deputy Defence Minister Efraim Sneh said Friday.

"Bringing the members of the israeli-proxy militia and their families to Israel and transforming them into refugees is no solution either for those who come here or for those who stay in Lebanon," said Sneh, who is a key confidant of Prime Minister Ehud Barak.

"We are not talking about a handful of collaborators here but a force of 3,000 men who is drawn from a community of 150,000 Christians, Shiites and Druzes in south Lebanon which has been allied with Israel for more than 20 years," he told public radio.

Sneh warned that Israel "will react if the militia members are the victims of revenge attacks by Hezbollah," without elaborating on the nature of the reprisals he was threatening.

The israeli high court of justice Monday rejected a request for asylum from three militiamen.

The israeli lawyer representing the three, Zvi Rish, said he was "disappointed" by the court's decision and called on the israeli authorities to "grant asylum to the members of the militia."

Rish argued that his clients faced reprisals from the Lebanese authorities as well as Hezbollah guerrillas in the event of the withdrawal from south Lebanon which the israeli cabinet agreed early last week to carry out by July with or without a peace deal with Beirut and Damascus.

The Beirut government insists that all israeli`s allied militiamen and other residents of the border zone accused of "collaboration with the enemy" be prosecuted.

The israeli cabinet has promised to guarantee the safety of the roughly 2,500 members of the militia but given no details.

israeli Transport Minister Yitzkak Mordechai called on fellow ministers to "meet urgently to agree contingency plans for granting asylum to our allies in the militia."

Press reports have alleged for months that countries such as Australia, Canada, France or Turkey would grant political asylum to militiamen, particularly senior officers, if they request it.

Two collaborators were killed in separate attacks by Hezbollah guerrillas on Tuesday, bringing the militia's losses so far this year to 13, according to an AFP tally.
