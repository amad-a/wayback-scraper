Hezbollah vows to teach Israel "lessons" for deadly attacks (1)
The Hezbollah group vowed on Tuesday to teach Israel "lessons" for carrying out attacks that killed two people and wounded four in southern Lebanon Monday.

"The latest Zionist aggression against Lebanon is an expression of frustration and bitterness at Arab support" for Beirut, a Hezbollah statement quoted its Deputy Secretary General Sheikh Naim Kassem as saying.

The Arab League held an unprecedented ministerial meeting in Beirut on Saturday which expressed strong solidarity with Lebanon and its armed resistance against Israeli occupation troops in southern Lebanon.

"This aggression against the Palestinians, the Lebanese and the Lebanese army was meant to tell the Palestinians that 'I will chase you in Lebanon and I will fight you before you fight me,'" Kassem said.

"It wants to tell the Lebanese army that 'you will be targetted by our strikes so don't side with the resistance' and wants to tell the people that 'we will strike you to punish you for being with the resistance,'" he said.

"We tell the Israelis that these messages will backfire on you because the Islamic Resistance will continue and will teach you additional lessons you will never forget," Kassem said.

Kassem said the Israeli raids laid bare the true nature of the plans of Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak "which call for a withdrawal while concealing the desire to reap the benefits of this withdrawal."

Barak's government decided on March 5 to withdraw from southern Lebanon by July 7 with or without an agreement with Beirut and Damascus, both of whom considered the move as a "trap" or a "maneuver" to break their unified position at the peace process.

Kassem was speaking to the Cairo-based Nile satellite channel, a Hezbollah statement distributed in Beirut said.

A subsequent Hezbollah statement accused Israel of violating the terms of the April 1996 truce agreement which bans combattants in southern Lebanon from harming civilians.

"The Israeli enemy will bear responsibility for the consequences of these violations," said the statement.

"We are forced to adopt deterrent measures in the field which will increase the crisis facing the enemy and put an end to its continued violations against civilians," it warned without elaborating.

Earlier Tuesday, the Arab League condemned the Israeli raids in south Lebanon, saying Israel should face the consequences.

"This new attack, which amounts to a serious blow to the will for peace, will not escape the force of the Lebanese resistance," the league said in a statement.

It condemned Israeli policy "which aims to provoke Arab public opinion.

 

Hezbollah puts torturing Dirani before world's conscience (2)
Hezbollah issued a statement on torturing prisoner Hajj Mustafa Dirani by the Israeli occupation forces.

"We read in the media yesterday about the brutal psychological torture that brother prisoner Hajj Abu Ali - Mustafa al-Dirani had been subjected to at the hands of the Zionist grudge and dirtiness that have reached a lowest level," a Hezbollah communiqué read.

"As we consider this hideous act as one of the worst in the Zionists' black history, and as we condemn what has been perpetrated by these low criminal hands, we put this horrible crime before the world's human rights groups, the international groups for prisoners and detainees, and the whole international community which was shocked by the disgraceful news."

We urge them all to take prompt action to secure the release of Freedom Fighter Prisoner al-Dirani and all the prisoners and detainees in the darkness of the Israeli prisons, the communiqué added.

"The Islamic Resistance, which has shouldered the liberation of its soil and people from the claws of the Israeli usurping enemy, regards the issue of all prisoners as its first and major cause."

Liberating the prisoners is one of IR's first duties which it has always worked to achieve. It will keep on working hard as much as it can to have this goal materialize, the communiqué concluded.

 

Lahoud talks tough in bid to clear the air (3)
 President Emile Lahouds insistence that Israeli promises of withdrawal from the south did not comply with UN resolution 425 marked the end of a Lebanese diplomacy which has often relied on tears and complaints, and only proven to be useless, a senior political source said on Tuesday.
The president, choosing to clarify the Lebanese stand as many times as he feels is necessary, replied on Monday to local and foreign criticism of Beiruts official position on key issues: a unilateral Israeli withdrawal and the Palestinian issue.
Diplomatic sources said that one of the most eminent critics was United States Secretary of State Madeleine Albright.
The sources said that during a meeting she held in Washington on Monday with Arab diplomats, in the presence of US Ambassador David Satterfield, Albright expressed surprise as to what she perceived as Lebanons refusal to let Israel implement Resolution 425.
Lahouds statement came after the Washington meeting, and appeared as a reply to the various remarks made by Albright and other foreign politicians, but also expressed in Lebanese political circles.
A week ago, the president issued a statement in which he said that Lebanon would not protect the Israeli border in the event of a unilateral withdrawal. Lahoud hit out on another front on Monday: he said that although a pullout would mean a victory for Lebanon against Israel, a unilateral withdrawal should not be interpreted as an implementation of Resolution 425.
It is clear that the Israeli proposal to withdraw is the outcome of Israeli losses and is not in harmony with UN Resolution 425, which Israel has been refusing to implement since 1978 ­ the year of the resolutions endorsement.
Lahoud reaffirmed Beiruts official position concerning the possibility of a withdrawal: We have already said that we dont only welcome an Israeli withdrawal but also call for celebrating our victory over Israel should a pullout occur.
The political source said that even the Israelis did not present the proposed unilateral withdrawal as an implementation of Resolution 425. This was also stressed by the president, who rejected the skewed interpretation of facts by those who volunteered to justify an Israeli withdrawal, describing it as an implementation of Resolution 425, whereas even Israel did not pretend that it was.
The source said that an Israeli withdrawal in conformity with the UN resolution would constitute a step toward peace, whereas the Israeli proposal sought only to limit Israeli losses. As such, it does not reflect peaceful intentions and cannot be viewed as the implementation of Resolution 425, the source explained.
The president is convinced that a withdrawal is a necessary move but not a sufficient one, the source added.
In his statement, Lahoud said the pullout is not a gift granted to us from anybody. We have paid its price in advance, at a time when many states were watching us, leaving Israel free to invade Lebanon, demolish it, and kill and displace its people.
Lahoud was addressing the US, but was also replying to the remarks of Egyptian Foreign Minister Amr Musa, who welcomed the announcement of Israeli withdrawal and said it should be looked at positively.
Lahoud also stressed on Monday the issue of some 360,000 Palestinian refugees who reside in Lebanon, after warning a week ago that Beirut could not give security guarantees to the Jewish state in the event of a withdrawal, because this would imply that the refugee issue had been settled.
The president said: These states which advise us to be grateful to Israel for its good deeds, mainly the withdrawal, are intentionally forgetting the facts  the Palestinian problem in Lebanon has a different aspect than in other Arab countries: its not a displacement problem but a security and military problem that has been exacerbated over the years.
Therefore, we have the right to confront the Israeli occupier with this issue, which will be the first problem to surface after the withdrawal.

 

Hoss: bloody message will not terrorize us (4)
Victims of latest Israeli assaults laid to rest amid defiant mood
Mondays deadly flare-up in the south will not scare Lebanon into changing its stance on the occupation and the peace process, said Prime Minister Salim Hoss on Tuesday, as Hizbullah vowed to teach Israel a lesson for shelling that left two dead near Tyre.
Israels frequent bloody messages will not terrorize Lebanon The martyrs sacrifices will continue to guide us in making the right decisions, Hoss said.
The resistance will continue as long as the occupation persists, and the unity of the Lebanese and the Syrian tracks will be maintained  Also, Lebanon will not waver in its call to safeguard the right of Palestinian refugees to return home, he said in a statement.
A solider, Abdel-Salam Hanqir, and a civilian, Hassan Zeeni, died and four others, including two soldiers, were wounded by the Israeli-allied South Lebanon Army militias shelling in Mansouri as Israeli warplanes raided suspected guerrilla strongholds belonging to both Lebanese and Palestinian organizations.
Military police carried Hanqirs coffin to Sidons new cemetery on Tuesday and a representative of Army Commander General Michel Suleiman read a eulogy over the grave.
Zeeni was laid to rest in Qlayli in a separate ceremony.
Hoss said: Israel, which could not contain its anger over the unanimous solidarity that Lebanon received from Arab foreign ministers, unleashed its war machine against Lebanon, its army and people, and its Palestinian guests.
He was referring to the meeting of Arab foreign ministers in Beirut on Saturday, which supported Lebanon. Israeli officials accused the ministers of taking extremist positions.
The Arab League also condemned Israels attacks on Tuesday, saying Israel should face the consequences.
It said: This new attack which amounts to a serious blow to the will for peace, will not escape the force of the Lebanese resistance.
Hoss also called on the United States and France, co-chairs of the April Understanding monitoring committee, to activate their efforts to reconvene the panel, which has not met since Israel walked out on Feb. 11.
He said Israels attack on Mansouri violated the 1996 understanding, which bans the launching of attacks at or from civilian areas.
Also on Tuesday, Parliaments Foreign Affairs Committee described the Israeli attack as a challenge for Lebanon, Syria and the whole Arab World, as well as for the decisions reached by the foreign ministers.
Committee chairman Tyre MP Ali Khalil hailed the president, the government, Lebanese diplomacy, the resistance movement and the Lebanese people for managing to convene the meeting.

 

Israel condemned for abusing Dirani (5)
Premier Salim Hoss on Tuesday condemned Israels alleged torture of imprisoned resistance leader Mustafa Dirani and urged the UN to stop Israels brutal and terrorist actions.
We demand an end to such Nazi, terrorist, brutal and inhumane acts, Hoss said in a letter addressed to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan.
The premier sent similar letters to the International Committee for the Red Cross and the UN Human Rights Commission. He met the ICRCs Lebanon chief, Henri Fournier, and requested that the Red Cross attempt to meet Dirani and investigate alleged acts of torture.
Dirani, leader of the Believers Resistance Group, has filed a $1.5 million lawsuit against Israel, claiming that his jailors raped and tortured him.
Dirani has been held in administrative custody since May 1994, although no official charges have been filed.
He was abducted from his Bekaa home by Israeli commandos attempting to secure information about Israeli Air Force navigator Ron Arad, shot down over Lebanon in 1986 and missing ever since.
The Israelis have consistently refused to grant the ICRC access to Dirani since his 1994 kidnapping, Fournier said.
Fournier told The Daily Star that the ICRC was doing its utmost to persuade the Israeli authorities to allow the organization to see Dirani.
Tyre MP Mohammed Abdel-Hamid Beydoun, who heads Amals politburo, also condemned the alleged atrocities.
We call on the international community to condemn Israel and expose its crimes, he said, urging the government to raise the detainees issue in international fora. The Believers Resistance Group warned on Tuesday that Israel would pay for these shameful acts.
Hizbullah called the alleged abuse one of the worst crimes in the Zionists tarnished record.

Palestinian presence in Lebanon a military problem: Lahoud (6)
Lebanese President Emile Lahoud repeated a warning to Israel over its planned pullout from south Lebanon, in a statement issued Tuesday to mark the 22nd anniversary of the first Israeli invasion of his country.

Lahoud said there was no question of Lebanon offering any security guarantees to Israel as long as the question of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon remained unresolved, the official ANI agency reported.

"There will be no understanding over guarantees after the retreat as long as the problem exists," he said.

As long as Israel did what suited it and dumped its problems on Lebanon there could be no progress, he added.

"When we raise this question, they tell us that our position suits Syria. In reality, this position serves Lebanon above all. If it suits Syria too, we don't regard that as inconvenient," he continued.

Lahoud also criticised countries that "pretend to have forgotten that the nature of the Palestinian problem in Lebanon is just a question of refugees, as is the case in most Arab countries."

The United States, as mediators in the peace process, supports the Israeli view that the issue of Palestinian refugees in Arab countries since the Jewish state was founded in 1948 forms part of the "final status" talks Israel is holding with Yasser Arafat's Palestian Liberation Organisation.

Lahoud was repeating a warning he issued last Wednesday in the wake of Israel's March 5 announcement that by July it would pull its troops out of southern Lebanon, which it first occupied on March 14, 1978, with or without accords with Syria and Lebanon.

Lahoud warned then that the fate of the 367,000 Palestinian refugees residing in Lebanon -- half of them in 12 refugee camps there -- had to be resolved in order for him to consider security guarantees for Israel.

Lebanon has always refused to give permamnent shelter to the Palestinians, fearing they will upset the country's delicate political and religious balance.

In his latest statement, Lahoud said Israel was preparing to pull out because of the losses its troops had suffered in Lebanon, not out of respect for the 1978 United Nations Resolution 425 that called for their withdrawal.

Lahoud also denounced Monday's shelling by Israel of a Lebanese army checkpoint near the coastal city of Tyre -- which killed two people -- and air attacks earlier in the day against targets near the Syrian border.

These raids targetted camps of two Palestinian groups in a move seen as a warning to them not to complicate the Israeli pullout from Lebanon.

Also Tuesday, Lebanese army commander General Michel Suleiman met with representatives of eight Palestinian opposition groups at his office in Beirut's northeastern suburbs of Yarze, officials said.

They included officials from the Saiqa, the Islamic Jihad in Palestine, Fatah-Intifada, the Popular Struggle Front, Hamas, the Palestine Liberation Front, the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine-General Command and the Palestinian Communist Party, they said.

During the meeting, Suleiman asserted Lebanon's backing for the Palestinians' right to return home, they added.

 

Two Lebanese women in Israeli jail "taken to hospital after torture" (7)
Two Lebanese women detained without charges or trial in a jail in Israeli-occupied southern Lebanon were taken to hospital after being tortured, a Lebanese human rights group said Tuesday.

Freelance journalist Cosette Ibrahim and Najwa Samhat were taken by Israeli forces and their allied South Lebanon Army militiamen to hospital in the town of Marjayoun in the central sector of the border area, said the group.

"They were taken to hospital after their health conditions deteriorated because of torture at the detention center," said a statement by the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of the Lebanese detainees in the Israeli Prisons.

"Samhat suffered severe haemorrhage after having a miscarriage at the detention center," it said.

Ibrahim, 25, was taken for treatment in hospital because "she suffers from a severe ulcer and she is constantly hooked to serum in her cell because she vomits everything she eats," it added.

The committee did not give details on the kind of torture to which the two women were subjected.

Ibrahim was snatched by Israeli security agents from her home in the border enclave on September 2 and detained in the Khiam jail, run by the SLA militia under Israeli supervision.

Israel detains 174 Lebanese, of whom 140 are held in the Khiam jail and the remaining 34 in prisons inside Israel.

Among the Lebanese detainees are five young women and a number of teenagers and elderly men, according to the follow-up committee which claims that 16 detainees died under torture in Khiam.

It also claims that all prisoners in Israel were still being detained even though they have completed their sentences.

Human rights groups, including the London-based Amnesty International, have repeatedly denounced cases of torture and ill-treatment of Lebanese detainees in Israeli jails.

 UN Resolution 425 comes to classroom (8)
Children are being urged to discuss the occupation in the south to build national unity
 To mark the 22nd anniversary of the Israeli invasion of the south, Education Minister Mohammed Youssef Beydoun urged intermediate and secondary school officials across the country on Tuesday to dedicate two hours to discussing United Nations Resolution 425.
According to Albert Chamoun, an official at the Education Ministry, this annual practice is aimed at raising awareness among future generations of Lebanons cause against Israel, and efforts made by the government and the resistance to recover occupied land.
The public needs to be aware of the governments policies and obligations, and that process of educating them should begin at school, he said.
Chamoun said that children needed to learn about what led to the creation of Resolution 425, about its content, and about what it means to Lebanon.
A part of our territory is occupied and there is international recognition that Israel is an aggressor and that it should withdraw its troops from Lebanon unconditionally, he said.
Children have the right to know and to ask questions and we have the obligation to answer these questions, he added.
Aida Khatib, head of the primary and intermediate public school teachers union, said she considered the ministers decision a positive step.
She said that depending on their capacities, schools would either gather their students in the schoolyard or auditorium to sing national songs and listen to stories about the resistance and Israeli aggressions, or participate in class activities including drawing, singing or discussions on the issue.
According to Khatib, growing a strong national affiliation is a basic component of education.
It is the role of the teacher to encourage children to love their country, to learn about its history and to be willing to serve it when duty calls, she said.
According to Khatib, the fact that Israel is aggressive and carries out inhuman acts on a daily basis has made it easier for teachers to portray Israel as the enemy.
Our children hear about other children being killed in their schools in the south, and they know about the atrocities that the enemy is committing on our people and to our villages, she said. Its always beneficial to have them air out these issues.
Khatib said that such discussions should not be limited to official ceremonies and should occur year round or whenever appropriate.
Rana Qatrib, an elementary school teacher at the Zahrat al-Ihsan School, said that her nine-year-old students were very interested in the discussion they had and raised many good questions.
Qatrib, who in addition to discussing UN Resolution 425 with her students, raised the subject of the Qana massacre, said she was impressed with the quality of the questions.
We mostly spoke about unity and support to our brothers and sisters in the south, she said.
These kids amazed me with their questions. They wanted to know more about the history of the situation, and how Israel came to be the enemy, she said.
Among the questions raised by students were why the Arabs hated the Israelis and loved Lebanon, and why there was only one day dedicated to supporting the south. Others wanted to know about the Golan Heights, and about who would guarantee that if Israeli abides by Resolution 425, they wont return later.
