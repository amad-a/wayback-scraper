Hezbollah: The country that gives collaborators asylum protects criminals and killers (1)
There is no justification whatsoever for any country to give political asylum to the officers and members of the Israeli occupation's proxy militia other than protecting criminal killers who have perpetrated massacres against our people, Hezbollah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said.

His Eminence told the French dailies Liberation and Le Figaro, "Throughout the past years, the collaborators in Lahd's militia have been given several chances to repent."

"Had they repented in the past years or even months, they would have had a definite opportunity to get extenuating circumstances. But after the recent decision  taken by the Israeli government, repentance has become meaningless, for they have served the Israeli occupation till the very end," Sayyed Nasrallah said.

"These are traitors, collaborators, and killers that must be tried, just as in any country whose soil is liberated."

"We consider that any country that grants them political asylum will be, in reality, protecting killers and criminals, I mean by this all the countries that are reported to be about to give political asylum to these people," he added.

We understand it when this right is given to those who politically disagree with the regimes in their countries, but (for this right) to be given to the killers and criminals who have perpetrated massacres, this has no justification other than protecting criminals, the secretary general said.

The natural solution for these (collaborators) is to give themselves in to the Lebanese judiciary and be tried before Lebanese courts in line with the observed laws.

 

Arabs flock to Beirut to show solidarity
Hoss: Resistance will not cease as long as a single inch of Lebanese territory is occupied  (2)
The Arab League chief and regional foreign ministers began thronging to Beirut on Thursday for a weekend conference and landmark show of solidarity with the only nation still actively fighting Israel and footing the bill for five decades of conflict.
Esmat Abdel-Meguids arrival coincided with mounting Lebanese demands for active support of its casuse: the liberation of its territory from occupation within the framework of a comprehensive Arab-Israeli peace, and a solution to the presence of Palestinian refugees on its soil.
Abdel-Meguid said that the Saturday-Sunday meetings at the Grand Serail would be a historic conference because it will confirm Arab solidarity with Lebanon.
On arrival at the airport, he said: We look forward to resolutions that will be implemented, a clear reference to previous pledges of support that remained ink on paper.
Perhaps the conference will have an impact even before its convening, he said.
Welcoming Abdel-Meguid at the airport was Prime Minister Salim Hoss, who played down the possibility of financial assistance to Lebanon. This is not the reason why the meeting was called for. We respect the conditions of Arab countries, and do not want to embarrass anyone, although we can say frankly that we are in need of material support, he said.
To avoid overshadowing the main goal of the conference, officials focused on behind-the-scenes contacts to ensure that the agenda would not be cluttered with too many issues unrelated to the Arab-Israeli conflict and Lebanons plight.
Only 10 issues would be tackled instead of the original 30 proposed by the various member states, diplomatic sources said.
A day after President Emile Lahoud had stressed the importance of the Palestinian presence here, Hoss hammered home the theme on Thursday, insisting that no settlement could become a peace without two things taking place: the return of Palestinian refugees to their homes and a solution for the issue of Jerusalem.
A Western diplomatic source said that Lebanon was suggesting that the Palestinian presence issue be included not only on the Arab League agenda but also in future bilateral negotiations with Israel.
The source said the United States viewed this as an additional hurdle to its efforts to kickstart peace talks between Syria and Israel, which Lebanon would join later.
Washington prefers that the issue be addressed in the multi-lateral process, in which a committee chaired by Canada was created in 1991, but got bogged down when the bilateral peace talks began faltering in 1996.
Hoss said that in reviewing the duties assigned to this (multilateral) committee, we found that there was no mention whatsoever of the right of refugees to return to their homes. Its duties are restricted to improving their living conditions in Lebanon and other Arab countries.
He rejected the view that the issue should be resolved in final- status negotiations between Israel and the Palestinian Authority, since Lebanon was directly concerned with the future of 360,000 refugees on its soil.
The premier cited assurances from French Foreign Minister Hubert Vedrine and a letter from Washingtons ambassador to the United Nations, Thomas Pickering, which asserted that the ordeal of Palestinians uprooted from their homes in the 1948 and 1967 wars would not be solved at Lebanons expense.
In a separate statement released on Thursday, Hoss said he expected the foreign ministers to adopt the cause of liberating Lebanese territory from Israeli occupation, support for the policy of jihad and noble resistance to combat Israeli occupation of Lebanese land, and the demand for a just and comprehensive peace in the framework of the unity of the Lebanese and Syrian negotiation tracks.
The premier added that resolutions that would be adopted by the ministers would confirm that Lebanon will no longer be alone in any new Nazi-Israeli aggression; rather the entire Arab nation will be by its side.
Summing up Beiruts hopes for the conference, Hoss said: success is a certainty and failure is impermissible.
He said he rejected giving Israel free notice about Beiruts intentions in the event of a unilateral withdrawal, but when pressed, said: The resistance will not cease as long as a single inch of Lebanese territory is occupied.
Foreign Ministry sources expected that the meeting would result in a pledge of financial aid for Lebanon, but the exact amount has yet to be decided.
Lebanon is planning to ask for the remainder of $2 billion in aid promised by Arab states in 1979. Only $400 million has been paid so far.
According to a document prepared by the Foreign Ministry, Lebanon will insist that Israel should not carry out a withdrawal that would keep the international border unstable.
It also seeks support for Lebanons right to pursue resistance against Israeli occupation and backing for the right of Palestinian refugees to return home.

ISRAEL EXPECTED TO MAKE FINAL DECISION ON APRIL TRUCE MEETINGS ON THURSDAY (3)
Security sources in southern Lebanon expect Israel to make its final decision on whether to take part in cease-fire meetings on Thursday. French and US representatives to the five-nation truce panel had held a series of talks with top Lebanese, Syrian and Israeli officials, in a bid to reconvene panel meetings.

Israel has boycotted panel meetings since its war jets raided Lebanese civilian targets last month, destroying three power plants, and injuring 22 civilians. The panel which is made up of delegates from Lebanon, Israel, Syria, the US and France is expected to rule that Israel's recent raids violated the 1996 truce accord, which bans attacks on and from civilian targets but permits Lebanese resistance operations against Israeli occupation forces in the south. Following the massive air offensive, Israeli officials said they want to change the rules of engagement in southern Lebanon, by adding a clause to the April Understanding banning all resistance attacks in the zone Israel occupies. Lebanon and Syria categorically rejected the Israeli attempt. Paris and Washington have been holding intense contacts to reactivate the panel's meetings. Panel meetings are seen as a mechanism to de-escalate violence in the south. France's representative to monitoring group, Laurent Rapin, held talks with UN peacekeeping officers in the border town of Naqoura, where delegates discuss Lebanese and Israeli complaints over alleged truce violations. But Israeli attacks continue unabated. A Lebanese soldier and a civilian were wounded in an Israeli bombardment of an army checkpoint late yesterday. Observers insist that the Lebanese army's is really targeted by Israeli fire whenever the Jewish state wants to send "messages" to the Lebanese government. A Fijian soldier of the UN force in the area was also wounded in Israeli shelling. Lebanon and Syria want Israel to withdraw from the south and the Golan as part of a negotiated settlement based on UN resolutions. Israel's cabinet last Sunday endorsed Prime Minister Ehud Barak's plans to end the 22-year occupation of southern Lebanon by July. Officials in both countries have cautioned against a unilateral Israeli withdrawal from south Lebanon, since Israel did not indicate it will fully pullout from occupied territories. The volatile issue of some 360,000 Palestinian refugees in Lebanon will remain unresolved. Beirut made clear it will not protect Israel from any attacks by Palestinian militants based on some 12 highly-armed refugee camps.

 

ARMY COMMANDER CALLS FOR FULL AND UNCONDITIONAL ISRAELI WITHDRAWAL FROM LEBANON (4)
Lebanese Army commander General Michel Sleiman said any Israeli pullout from southern Lebanon should be unconditional and include all occupied territories. The Army commander inspected front-line military posts in the south, including the Mansoureh check point which came under Israeli attack yesterday. One soldier was injured. General Sleiman rejected Israeli threats. He also warned that the Army will not stand guard to Israeli borders. The Army commander stressed, a unilateral Israeli pullout from Lebanon will not affect the steadfastness of Beirut and Damascus.

 

Support our women in south, First Lady tells Arab parliamentarians (5)
Arab women parliamentarians in Lebanon to demonstrate their support of Lebanese women resisting Israeli occupation visited Qana on Thursday.
They laid a wreath on the graves of the 107 victims of the April 1996 massacre when Israel shelled a UNIFIL post where people had sought refuge.
Earlier in the day, they had paid a visit on Andree Lahoud, wife of President Emile Lahoud, at the Presidential Palace in Baabda.
Lahoud reiterated that the role of Arab women to liberate their land was a decisive factor in letting women out of their social prison bars.
Women now express their demands for human rights and have moved forward to claim advanced positions in society, Lahoud said. She added that the initiative made by Arab parliamentarians to visit Beirut to show their support to Lebanese women was evidence of their faith.
It also demonstrates that this is the cause of every Arab woman who stands in support of women detainees in Israeli prisons, Lahoud said.
On their return trip from Qana, the women parliamentarians visited Randa Berri, wife of speaker Nabih Berri, in Mseileh, who commented that their show of support reflected the significance of the resistance.
The women you are visiting in the south today suffer from Israeli aggression on a daily basis but they believe that Arabs dont have to fear anything as long as there is a resistance, Berri told the delegation. She urged her visitors to show strong united support behind women suffering from the occupation.
I also want you to know that women can still influence decisions even though they do not hold decision-making positions, she said.
The Arab parliamentarians had dinner on Wednesday evening at the home of former premier Rafik Hariri and his wife Nazek. Mrs. Hariri urged Arab women to unite to face the common difficulties they suffer.
During the year of Arab women, we are required to make a move, initiate and cooperate for better women in a better society who is complemented by better men they love, and not fear, she said.

 

Boueiz casts doubt on Israels July withdrawal (6)..(I.A.)

Former Foreign Minister Fares Boueiz cast doubts on Thursday on the Israeli governments ability to meet its July 7 deadline for withdrawing from occupied south Lebanon.
Its not clear whether Israel will implement UN Resolution 425 by pulling back to the international border and resolving the fate of its allied militia Boueiz said after conferring with Cardinal Nasrallah Butros Sfeir at the Maronite Patriarchs seat at Bkirki.
He also cautioned against Israeli schemes of staging a troop redeployment instead of a complete withdrawal to the internationally-recognized

Border.
The Israeli government may wish to maintain Israeli proxy militiamen as a powderkeg it could detonate at will to escalate the conflict, he said.
Boueiz said he also was wary of Israeli hints of readiness to pull out from the Golan Heights, Syrias precondition to restarting peace talks.
How can the Israeli government put a peace treaty with Syria to a referendum when it cannot even get more than 51 percent of the Knessets votes? Boueiz said.
He said that the two-day Arab League foreign ministers meeting, which will be held in Beirut this weekend, should have been held immediately after Israels Feb. 8 air blitz, and not more than a month later.
Nevertheless, this ministerial meeting will help bolster Arab unity and will bring together ministers whose countries do not enjoy good relations, Boueiz said. Some of the ministers will be meeting for the first time.

A Lebanese farmer was shot in the legs by Israel's proxy militia Thursday as he cultivated a field outside the Israeli-occupied border strip, police said.

Kamal Fayez Bu Brahim, 52, was hit by fire from a 12.7 mm gun from the Israeli proxy militia position at Shuwaya, on the eastern edge of the Israeli-occupied zone.

Since five militiamen were killed March 1 by Hezbollah guerillas, the militia has carried out a series of punitive measures against residents of villages across from the scene of slayings.

Red Cross sends rescuers into Lebanese village under fire for nine days (9)
The Red Cross sent a team of rescue workers Thursday to a southern Lebanese village that has been under constant fire for nine days from the adjoining Israeli-occupied zone, police said.

The International Committee of the Red Cross sent food and medicine to Ain Santa, which has been paralyzed by fire coming from the the Israeli-held zone since March 1 when five members of pro-Israeli South Lebanon Army militia ally were killed by Hezbollah guerrillas.

The Red Cross received the militia permission to enter Ain Santa, two kilometers (more than a mile) from the eastern edge of the zone, and sent a doctor and a nurse who have examined 18 patients, police said.

The terrified residents are unable to put their cattle out to pasture because of the shooting, police said.

The constant attacks also prevent 15 children from Ain Santa from attending school in neighboring villages.

The Red Cross confirmed to AFP that it sent a team to the village and that it will continue to help and enable the children to go back to school as soon as possible in safety.

Hezbollah's deadly assault on the Israeli militia was followed by attacks in the vicinity of six villages across from where the killing took place. Twenty homes were damaged in the pounding.

The militia destroyed an electrical post, knocking out power to 10,000 people in the area. The militiamen also refused to let state electrical workers repair the damage, and the Red Cross is negotiating security guarantees for them.

 

Israeli proxy militia releases four inmates from Khiam (10)
Israels proxy militia on Thursday released four Lebanese inmates from the notorious Khiam prison, including two women who were detained on allegations of collaborating with the resistance movement.
Three of the four prisoners, who are all natives of the Israeli-occupied enclave, were allowed to return to their hometowns of Dbeen, Aita Shaab and Qlaia.
The fourth, a woman from Arnoun, was driven by the International Committee of the Red Cross to the Nabatieh Serail, where she was welcomed by officials and relatives.
Shoumalqan Assaf, 23, was scheduled to meet her mother on Thursday in an ICRC-arranged family visit to the detention center in the occupied Israeli strip. Her mother was transported to Khiam prison, along with 20 other relatives of nine Khiam prisoners, only to discover that her daughter was being released and that she would find her in Nabatieh.
Assaf recounted to reporters her arduous stay in Khiam prison, where she shared a cell with four other women, including journalist Cosette Ibrahim, who was arrested by Israeli troops last August while visiting her parents in the occupied zone.
The inmates are suffering from severe neurological and psychological disorders and they have delegated me to urge senior politicians to seek their release, Assaf said.
She was detained three-and-a half months ago by the Israeli agents for allegedly collaborating with Hizbullah, spying on Israeli troop movements and relaying information to the Lebanese intelligence service.
I was admitted twice to Marjayoun hospital during my detention because I suffered a low count of red blood cells for some time, she said.
The prisoner release reduced the number of Lebanese detainees in Khaim to 140, five of them women.

 

Commando's family asks High Court to fire Navon(11)
The family of 3PO Guy Golan, a naval commando killed during a botched raid in southern Lebanon, yesterday asked the High Court of Justice to order the firing OC Chaplaincy Corps Maj.-Gen. Gad Navon because of his responsibility for the misidentification of body parts belonging to Golan and another soldier and their consequent burial in the wrong graves.

The burial mix-up was the last in a series of tragic events that began with the deaths of 11 commandos and an Israeli Army doctor on September 4, 1997.

A commission of inquiry led by retired judge Eliahu Vinograd found that Navon, 79, who has served as chief chaplain for the past 23 years, was responsible for a foul-up in which additional body parts, retrieved after Golan and 3PO Raz Tebi had already been buried, were mistakenly deposited in the other's grave.

The Vinograd Commission ruled that the errors were due to mistaken identification by an anthropologist acting at Navon's insistence. The anthropological examination was conducted by Prof. Baruch Erensburg at Sheba Hospital at Tel Hashomer after Navon decided to bypass the L. Greenberg Institute for Forensic Medicine in Abu Kabir because he thought its procedures were unnecessarily long.

Representing the Golan family, attorney Elad Shraga wrote that according to the committee findings, "the responsibility for the horrible mistake in the identification of the body parts and their burial in the wrong graves fell squarely on Navon. Furthermore, the committee reached grave conclusions about the way Navon conducted himself throughout the affair and, among other things, ruled that his actions were determined by extraneous considerations."

The petitioners asked the court to order the government to show cause why it should not fire Navon, and a temporary injunction preventing him from officiating at Israeli Army ceremonies and participating in General Staff meetings until the main petition is heard.

Following the Vinograd Commission findings, Navon announced he would retire on Independence Day.
