Hizbullah: Assad's death will change nothing; no gains for "Israel"(1)

Hizbullah's Deputy Secretary General his Eminence Sheikh Nahim Qassem said the late Syrian President Hafez al-Assad "was a great and strong man who had served Lebanon and its Resistance besides contributing in the liberation process and having great roles."

Speaking at a commemoration rally in the town of Aramta, Qassem said, "We tell those who are betting on changes following President Assad's death: all the things are moving in the direction of a quiet shift of authority without any major changes neither in Syria nor in Lebanon. ""Israel"" will not be getting any gains at this historic stage."

Sheikh Qassem reiterated Hizbullah's position on the collaborators, demanding strictness in their trials in order to fortify the country and the citizens and return things to normal course.

The Lebanese law punishes theft with detention in public right affairs up to one year. Is it then acceptable that those who have betrayed their country have some people seeking excuses for them to show that they were not traitors for 22 years but poor oppressed people, and during their fear they had been forced to kill and destroy", he said.

This logic is neither acceptable nor credible; who says these collaborators are affiliated with one sect or group? He asked.

Strict trials must be adopted to prevent others from daring to collaborate (with the enemy) in the future and in order to not to read in the newspapers that some wish they were collaborators as they see the lenient trials, Sheikh Qassem concluded.


Step by step: "Israeli" frontier takes shape(2)

Just meters away from the border with "Israel", two generals, one Lebanese, the other Irish, squint in the bright midday sun at an array of hand-held electronic devices as an aide reads out a list of coordinates.
Are we okay? asks Brigadier General Jim Sreenan, the Irish officer who serves as UNIFILs deputy commander.
Yes, were in agreement, replies General Amin Hoteit.
Smiles break out among the multinational group of soldiers clustered around the generals. Mabrouk, someone calls out.
The location of Boundary Pillar 26 on the border between Lebanon and "Israel" has just been formally approved and verified by the Lebanese Army and the United Nations.
The process of confirming "Israel's" withdrawal from Lebanon began last Thursday when two teams on either side of the border set out from Ras Naqoura on the coast. The separate Lebanese and "Israeli" teams were accompanied by UN officers belonging to both UNIFIL and the Observer Group Lebanon, part of the UN Truce Supervision Organization.
Ahead of them lay a little more than 100 kilometers of international border, ending near Shibaa Village, none of which has been verified independently since the early 1950s.
Sreenan said that UNIFILs task and the work of the accompanying Lebanese officers differed slightly. We have two different purposes here really, he said. Were here to confirm the withdrawal of "Israeli" forces from Lebanon. The Lebanese are checking the precise demarcation of the border.
The Irish general explained that UNIFILs task was relatively straightforward. We can see easily whether there is an "Israeli" position on Lebanese soil, he said. Precisely measuring the delineation of the border was a more painstaking exercise.
So the process takes a little longer, he said.
Time, however, is in short supply, at least as far as the UN is concerned. UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan has been badgering the government to speed up the process of verifying the border, thus enabling plans for UNIFILs expansion and new deployment to proceed.
But Lebanon has aired some objections to the line drawn up by a UN team of cartographers, headed by Annans special envoy Terje Roed-Larsen, behind which the "Israelis" are supposed to withdraw.
Annan decreed last month that the withdrawal line should follow the 1923 Anglo-French boundary ­ ratified by the 1949 Armistice Agreement.
Larsen, however, appears to have acquiesced, in part, to "Israeli" security demands and has deviated his line from the 1923 border in a few places. The three areas of contention cited by Lebanon are near Rmeish, where "Israel" reportedly has yet to fully dismantle an incursion zone, at the kibbutz of Misgav Am above Odaisseh and at Ghajar, village on the Lebanese-Syrian border.
Consequently, progress has been slower than Annan would have preferred.
Everyone is talking about time except for us, said a Lebanese Army officer on the team. We have to check our borders properly.
The team began its task on Monday with an inspection of Boundary Pillar 19 near Yaroun in Bint Jbeil. However, the threat of landmines in the area forced the team to postpone checking the precise location of No. 19 until the area had been cleared by Lebanese Army engineers.
The presence of minefields along the border has added to the delay in verifying the "Israeli" withdrawal. Army engineers have been accompanying the verification team since the operation began last week. An ambulance is also included in the 11-vehicle convoy.
By early afternoon, the team had reached a border crossing between Aitaroun and Blida beside Boundary Pillar 26.
A group of armed and uniformed Hizbullah fighters stood guard beside the shattered remains of an "Israeli" position which was dynamited by the resistance last week. The fighters were keeping an eye on a newly constructed "Israeli" outpost ­ its observation towers strewn with camouflage nets ­ just 200 meters away on the other side of the border.
Out came the GPS (global positioning system) handsets, a navigation tool that uses satellites to pinpoint a location anywhere on Earth. These would ascertain the coordinates of boundary pillars that could be viewed, but not reached on foot.
Calculations also had to be made to correlate the two different systems used by the United States and France, upon which the accuracy of UN maps and Lebanese Army maps, respectively, are based.
The teams verification efforts soon attracted a swarm of local reporters.
One of the team suggested discussing a query over the location of Boundary Pillar 19 near Yaroun.
Lets talk about 19 later, away from the journalists, Sreenan said through gritted teeth as the reporters continued to film, photograph, and eavesdrop on the team.
Sometimes we have minor disagreements over the measurements, admitted the Lebanese Army officer. But if there is a disagreement, we continue to discuss it until were all agreed. Its generally just a matter of a few meters.
But there are no disagreements over Boundary Pillar 26 and the team swiftly moves on to No. 27.
Thence it follows a straight line to cairn 27, situated 700 meters west northwest of the village of Kades, near the large tree of Khorbet Menafir, reads the quaint 77-year old description which accompanied the map charting the 1923 Anglo-French border.
The large tree no longer exists. Instead, the team was afforded a stunning view of woods, olive groves and wheatfields in Galilee and the sepia-colored slopes of the Golan Heights rising gently through the heat haze to the east.
Boundary Pillar 27 was out of sight from where the dusty track terminated in a small wadi. The marker was located above the wadi. The UN delegation had already satisfied itself that there were no "Israeli" troops on the Lebanese side of the border at this point and were ready to move on.
But Hoteit wanted to see the border marker and suggested marching up the side of the wadi.
Ask him if he knows the land-mine situation here, Sreenan said.
Theres an old incursion and a former outpost up there, replied a Danish OGL officer, gazing up at the bleak, grassy slope. Im not going up there, he added emphatically.
Neither am I, muttered an OGL officer from New Zealand.
The mine-clearing squad was called in to clear a path up the side of the wadi as the team departed to Boundary Pillar 28, planning to return to 27 later.
I think this operation will take a few days yet before its over, Sreenan said as he stared into the distance.
Hoteit grinned broadly when asked how much longer he believed the task of verifying the border would take.
It will take, he said, as long as it takes.


Civilian injured by mine in southern Lebanon(3)

" A teenage youth had to have his leg amputated Monday after being injured by a mine in the former "Israeli"-occupied zone of southern Lebanon, Lebanese police said.

The incident brought the toll of civilian casualties from mine explosions to three dead and 11 injured since the "Israeli" pullback on May 24.

Walid Awaid al-Mohammed, 17, was injured in the village of Ain Arab, near the border with "Israel".

No mine-clearing operations had been carried out before hundreds of thousands of civilians poured into the area, which had been barred to them for 22 years.

UN experts arrived in Lebanon on May 31 to draw up a mine clearance plan with the Lebanese Army.

The "Israeli" army handed over to the UN Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) a map showing the mines it had laid in southern Lebanon in several sectors of the occupied zone.


Occupation's air violations and soil theft continue(4)

The Zionist warplanes violated the Lebanese airspace for the third time since the retreat of the "Israeli" occupation forces from Lebanon on May 24.

Three enemy warplanes on Monday flew over the western range of Lebanon's Mountains in Beqaa breaking the sound barrier.

Meanwhile, the occupation's bulldozers proceeded with stealing soil from Lebanese territories.

Al-Manar Television's cameras on Monday monitored the theft from the Kfar Kila outskirts that are still under "Israeli" occupation.

The enemy's trucks moved the stolen soil to the Mtulla colony where it will be used in fields near the border.


Grave of fighters believed killed by "Israeli" forces found in southern Lebanon(5)

"A grave containing the bones of around 15 youths believed killed in fighting when "Israeli" forces invaded Lebanon in 1978 was discovered near this village in the former "Israeli"-occupied zone, police said.

But one woman, who said she lived in the area at the time, said it was commonly believed then that 28 people were actually killed.

The 35-year-old woman said most of those killed were taking cover in a house that "Israeli" tanks shelled over the course of several hours, ultimately destroying it.

Police said a pathologist who examined the bones said they undoubtedly belonged to young people and that many of the bones had been broken.

The discovery was made during work to widen a road in the area.

Residents of nearby villages said those killed belonged to the Lebanese Communist Action Organization or to the Fatah student battalion of Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat, most of them Lebanese university students.
