Lebanon leaders look to resist settlement of Palestinians…(1)

Lebanon's higher defence council, the country's top political body, met Wednesday for the first time since 1993, sending a message to the Palestinian Authority that it will stand against any compromises on Palestinian refugees' right of return with “Israel”.

Convened by Lebanese President Emile Lahoud, the council debated ways to foil “Israel’s” feared bid to trade sovereignty over East Jerusalem's holy sites for the right of return of 3.7 million Palestinian refugees.

Lebanon counts 367,000 refugees from land lost to “Israel” since 1948 within its borders, and refuses to grant them citizenship.

“Israeli” Absorption Minister Yuli Tamir, speaking on “Israeli” public radio Tuesday, suggested “Israel” would trade sovereignty over the al-Aqsa mosque compound for concessions on the refugee issue.

"What is vital for us is to obtain the most important thing, the renunciation by the Palestinians of the right of return of refugees to territory under “Israeli” sovereignty," Tamir said.

On Sunday, Lahoud insisted that Lebanon's refugees "must someday leave", warning strongly against any concessions.

The defense council, created in 1984, also examined an incident Monday where “Israeli” military fired upon Lebanese territory from Shibaa Farms.

 

Ex-prisoners released by Syria admit to having worked for “Israeli” secret service…(2)

Investigators say that a pair of detainees whom Syrian authorities recently handed over had been acting as undercover agents for “Israel’s” Mossad.
Judicial sources said that Jihad Jammal and Khaled Yassin, a Palestinian, had committed “Israeli”-ordered terrorist acts in several Arab countries, including Lebanon, Syria, Libya and Egypt.
State Prosecutor Adnan Addoum referred the two men’s cases to the Military Tribunal on Wednesday.
Yassin, a former member of the Democratic Front for Liberation of Palestine, admitted to blowing up a mosque in West Beirut in 1994.
The sources said the bombing, ordered by Mossad, was aimed at diverting the Lebanese authorities’ attention from an earlier Mossad-instigated church bombing on the eastern side of the capital. The former blast killed and wounded several people.
Yassin, who was arrested by the Syrian authorities in 1997, said Mossad ordered both operations in order to sow division and stoke sectarian tension.
He confessed to being trained by Mossad officers who ordered him to provide them with information on Palestinian factions in Lebanon.
Yassin said he was ordered to create political conflict between Libya and Egypt by leaking false news of Libyan nationals residing in Cairo who were supposedly planning to commit terrorist acts in the Egyptian capital.
Mossad officers also asked him to spread rumors of terrorist acts targeting several prominent Lebanese politicians.
Jammal confessed that he, too, carried out several operations for the Mossad until his arrest in 1994. Among them were the capture of Lebanese and Palestinian resistance fighters, gathering information on the Syrian army in Lebanon, and planting explosives near Syrian army positions.
Jammal said he contacted the Mossad through his relative, Farouk Jammal.

 

LAU Human Rights Club brings Al-Aqsa intifada to Beirut…(3)

The image of a threatening “Israeli” soldier stamping temporary passports greets onlookers as pro-Palestinian anthems by songwriter Ahmad Kaabour echo in the background.
These are scenes, not from the news coverage of the war in Palestine, but from Intifadat al-Quds; a three-day exhibit by the Lebanese American University’s Human Rights Club.
“We want visitors to know what it’s like to have to get a special passport and be bullied by soldiers in order to enter your own land,” said LAU student Dima Tahtah, who was dressed in army clothes while impersonating a merciless “Israeli” soldier.
The exhibit, or rather show, is an artistic re-enactment of the Palestinian intifada, which is concluding on Thursday at the Beirut campus’ Sheikh Zayed Hall.
According to Abir Abadi, the coordinator for the Human Rights Club and the initiator of the project, the exhibit is primarily a tribute to suffering and injustice. “Every day human-rights are being brutally violated in Palestine,” said Abadi angrily.
“As a human-rights club, we cannot sit around and do nothing. This is our contribution.”
At the center of the room is a dome-shaped stack of rocks, symbolizing the struggle for the liberation of Al-Aqsa Mosque, the stones used to fight for Palestinian freedom, and the tombstones representing the dead.
“We placed candles on it to show that it’s also a symbol for the graves of the martyrs,” Abadi said.
Surrounding the dome is a giant “Israeli” flag placed on the floor and covered with virulent slogans such as “Resistance is existence,” and “Stop the massacre of children.”
According to Abadi, placing an internationally recognized flag on the floor went against human-rights ethics, “but we did it anyway, as a sign of protest,” she said, adding that the organizing committee for the exhibit had wanted to place the flag all over the floor for people to step on. “But people in the administration (at LAU) objected,” Abadi said.
One of the walls of the exhibition room is decorated with a four-meter-long poster portraying the Palestinian uprising and bloodshed, and the wall opposite it is covered with intifada caricatures from the Arab press.
A third wall is strewn with painful images of dead and wounded children, and directly underneath hang drawings of fear and violence painted by Palestinian children from the Bourj al-Barajneh refugee camp.
“It’s powerful and it’s touching, simply because it’s the truth,” said graphic design student Riad Hawwa, who was moved by the drawings.
“This exhibit should be preserved and turned into a mini Palestinian museum,” he added.
Another visitor, Dima Issa, deplored the fact that images like those on display were not shown often enough in the media, “especially the Western media.”
Most of the photographs were donated by various student parties affiliated with Hizbullah, Amal,  and the Islamic Youth League.
The highlight of the exhibit is perhaps a two-meter-long wooden slingshot, standing at the corner of the room.
“This remains the greatest symbol of the Palestinian struggle,” said Mohammad Abbas, another organizing member.

 

3 Palestinian martyrs and tens of wounded in Rafah confrontations…(4)

 ”Israeli” troops killed three Palestinians in separate incidents in Gaza on Wednesday, one of them a 14-year-old boy caught in a fierce five-hour gun battle near the Egyptian border, hospital officials said.

Palestinian hospital officials and witnesses said Hani Youssef al-Sufi, 14, was killed during a heavy exchange of fire involving “Israeli” troops and Palestinian gunmen in Gaza's Rafah district near the Egyptian frontier.

They said 25-year-old Awad Zaloum was also killed and at least 40 other Palestinians were injured in the confrontation.

In the second incident, civil emergency worker Rifaat Faisal Abu Marzouk, 28, was shot in the head by “Israeli” soldiers guarding the Jewish settlement of Netzarim, the officials said.

The deaths brought the toll to at least 333 Palestinians, killed in “Israeli”-Palestinian clashes that erupted in late September.

Palestinian witnesses said “Israeli” troops in Rafah battling Palestinian gunmen had fired assault rifles and  anti-tank rockets from several armored personnel carriers and two watchtowers overlooking Palestinian residential areas.

 Two soldiers were lightly wounded.

The Voice of Palestine radio said that three sisters returning home from school and a one-year-old girl at home were wounded by shrapnel from “Israeli” gunfire.

A Palestinian military official said preliminary reports indicated the battle began when “Israeli” bulldozers sought to erect a new strong point near Palestinian homes.

The head of Palestinian public security in Gaza said that in the incident near Netzarim, “Israeli” troops opened fire, unprovoked, at a truck carrying civil emergency workers traveling on a road near al-Shuhada intersection.

``We in the Palestinian General Security Force consider this incident another of the deliberate attacks '“Israel”' is carrying out every day to kill as many unarmed Palestinian civilians as possible,'' a police statement said.

``There was no justification for this incident except hatred and “Israeli” arrogance...We hold the “Israeli” army fully responsible for this incident,'' the statement added.

Moreover, a Jewish settler was stabbed by a Palestinian youth in al-Quds (Jeruslem). His injuries were reported to be serious. 

 

Iran launches major mobilization campaign for Jerusalem day…(5)

Iran has launched an unprecedented campaign to mobilize nationwide rallies on Friday to mark the annual Jerusalem Day in support of the Palestinian struggle against “Israel”. Authorities of all political stripes in the Islamic republic, which does not recognize “Israel”, have repeatedly urged a massive turnout to protest against the hated "Zionist regime."   Some 230 MPs signed a petition Wednesday underlining their support for the Palestinian cause and denouncing the "silent complicity of the West in the massacre of Palestinian children."  Jerusalem Day was first conceived after the 1979 Islamic revolution by the late founder of Islamic Iran, Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, and is observed each year on the last Friday of the holy Muslim month of Ramadan. This year it comes after nearly 12 weeks of bloodshed in “Israel” and the Palestinian territories. State radio and television have repeatedly broadcast Tuesday's message from Iran's supreme leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei to Muslims throughout the region hailing the latest intifada.   Khamenei, who last week called the Jewish state a "cancerous tumour (which) must be ripped out of the region," said in his message that jihad or holy war was the only way forward for the Palestinian struggle. "After the Zionist regime's failure in Lebanon, the Palestinians understood that their enemy is not invincible and that is why they have decided to rise up," he said.   Khamenei once again called on Islamic nations to break all ties with the Jewish state and said the latest intifada will "free Palestine and Al-Quds from the clutches of the occupiers."   Other leaders as well as university and political groups have also backed calls for a strong turnout in the streets Friday in support of the Palestinians. An enormous demonstration is planned in the capital of Tehran, where organizers have mapped out seven marching routes which all lead to the campus of Tehran university, where weekly Friday prayers are held. Meanwhile state-run Khabar television has launched a competition, offering prizes for viewers and internet users who can identify important dates and events in the "history of the occupation of Palestine."   Iran said Tuesday that renewed US-hosted peace talks with “Israel” and the Palestinians were aimed at extinguishing the intifada. "The resumption of negotiations can only be seen as having short-term aims and will not assure the long-term rights of the Palestinian people," foreign ministry spokesman Hamid-Reza Asefi said, cited by the official IRNA news agency. "As the Zionist regime's policy of terror, repression and murder against the defenseless Palestinian people continues, such talks represent an effort to divert the aims of the intifada," Asefi said.   "They are a means of extinguishing the resistance of the Palestinian people," he said, reiterating Tehran's call for Palestinians to have the right to return to their homeland -- a demand rejected by “Israel”.
