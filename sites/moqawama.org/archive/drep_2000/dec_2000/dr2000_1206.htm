Red Cross denied access to “Israeli” captives…(1)

A Red Cross envoy said Tuesday that Hizbullah rejected his request to visit four “Israelis” it captured in October.
Angelo Gnaedinger, who heads the International Committee of the Red Cross’ operations in the Middle East and Europe, met with Hizbullah’s deputy secretary-general, Sheikh Naim Qassem, at the party’s headquarters in Haret Hreik.
Emerging from an hour-long meeting, Gnaedinger said he had discussed the fate of Lebanese detainees in “Israel” as well of that of four “Israelis” in Lebanon. The “Israelis” include three soldiers and Elhanan Tennenbaum, who Hizbullah says is a Mossad agent. “Israel” maintains he is a reserve army colonel.
Gnaedinger said Qassem rejected his request to meet the “Israeli” captives.
Hizbullah captured the soldiers in an ambush on Oct. 7. A week later, word of Tennebaum’s abduction came out with Hizbullah saying they caught him in Lebanon, while “Israel” claimed that he was kidnapped in Europe.
Hizbullah, which did not comment on the meeting, has repeatedly refused to divulge any information about the four “Israelis” before 19 Lebanese detainees in “Israel” are freed.
“We’re fulfilling our role as facilitators of negotiations that we hope will lead to the release and repatriation of Lebanese in “Israel”,” said Gnaedinger.
Having visited Gaza, the West Bank and “Israel”, Gnaedinger said he was on the last leg of a mission aimed to update the ICRC on recent conflicts and tensions in the Middle East.
Gnaedinger called for the immediate release of Lebanese detainees in “Israeli” prisons, whom he said he had also been denied permission to visit.

 

“Israelis” wound young hunter along border…(2)

“Israeli” forces shot and wounded a 16-year-old boy on the “Israel” borders, United Nations sources said Tuesday.
Habib Srur was shot in the right leg while on a hunting trip in Aita Shaab village, a few meters from the border.
The area faces the “Israeli” settlement of Zarit. Two Palestinian protesters were shot and killed in the same area by “Israeli” soldiers while demonstrating with a group in October.
Srur was found by a Nepalese UNIFIL patrol, who took him by ambulance to a UN field hospital in Naqoura and then to Sidon.
The UN said it opened an investigation into the incident, the circumstances of which remain unknown.
In a separate incident, a Syrian man who wandered into “Israel” Tuesday told “Israeli” police he entered by mistake after failing to find work in Lebanon.
Rifai Salem Odah, 31, was caught by the police and said he had mistakenly crossed the border during the night while trying to return home from a failed job search in Lebanon.
The police said they detained Odah after he crossed “Israel’s” northern border with Lebanon at the Shibaa region.
An “Israeli” woman working in the fields of the Dan communal farm some 10 kilometers south of the border, alerted the “Israeli” police after she found Odah wandering near the border town of Ghajar.
“Israeli” security forces were investigating how someone could unknowingly cross that far into the country. ­

 

Murr moves against any normalization with “Israel”…(3)

In a move to combat normalization with the Jewish state, the Ministry of Interior has stressed that municipal councils and unions must gain ministry approval before signing any twin-cities  agreements or sending delegations to conferences and assemblies abroad.
Interior Minister Elias Murr said Tuesday that the reason for the decision was related to the country’s foreign policy.
Murr issued a statement which detailed conferences such as the Euro-Med gathering in Marseille held earlier this month, in which “Israeli” officials were present.
He said that after coordination with the Foreign Ministry, Lebanese mayors who had been invited to the gathering were informed “a few days ahead of time” that “Israeli” delegations would be present.
Contacts with “Israelis” are prohibited by law.
Members of Progressive Socialist Party leader Walid Jumblatt’s Democratic Gathering Parliamentary Bloc had questioned the government concerning the issue on Monday after receiving complaints from a number of displeased mayors.
But Beirut Mayor Abdel-Monem Aris called the new move “a positive step,” saying that “it is the responsibility of the interior minister to supervise and to agree on the performance of the municipalities, and it is the responsibility of the mayors to keep the minister updated about their projects.”

 

Seventeen Lebanese sentenced for entering “Israel”…(4)

“ The Beirut military court has sentenced 17 Lebanese, including two women, for entering the enemy state of “Israel”, judicial sources said.

The two women were fined 333 dollars each by the court at a session late Monday, the sources said.

Two men were sentenced in absentia to two years in jail while the remaining 13 others were given prison sentences of three weeks and were ordered to pay 666 dollars, they said.

They were all charged with entering enemy territory without permission when they lived in “Israeli”-occupied southern Lebanon, the sources said.

In total, 1,664 sentences have been ordered since the June 5 start of the trial of some 2,200 Lebanese accused of collaborating with “Israel” before its May 24 withdrawal from Lebanon after 22 years of occupation.

Charges have included collaboration with “Israel”, entering “Israel” or enrolment in the now-disbanded “Israeli”-allied militia.

After sitting just once a week during the court holiday season, the military court has returned to holding three marathon sessions per week to wrap up the matter.

Judicial sources said Tuesday a third former militiaman identified as Abdel Monem Salim Qarout, died in prison from cancer 15 days ago.

 

Students in Sidon march for Intifadah…(5)

In support of the Palestinian uprising, Lebanese youth organizations and parties, and the student committee of the Lebanese University in Sidon organized a massive march in the city.

Hundreds of students demonstrated in Sidon's main roads, raising the Palestinian and Lebanese flags and banners calling for an escalation of armed operations to kick out the “Israeli” occupation.

They also called for the boycott of U.S. products in response for its support of “Israeli” aggressions.

Second “Israeli” diplomat is shot in Jordan…(6)

An “Israeli” embassy employee in Jordan was shot and wounded in the foot yesterday, in a sign that tensions ignited by a 10-week Palestinian uprising may be widening.

The “Israeli”, named by the “Israeli” foreign ministry as Shlomo Ratzbi, was leaving a food store in an upmarket area of the capital, Amman.

The attack bore a remarkable similarity to an attack in Amman on 19 November when an “Israeli” diplomat, Yoram Havivian, was slightly wounded in the arm and leg by someone who fired on his vehicle.

The incidents appear to be linked to the mayhem in “Israel” and its occupied territories, where more than 300 people – nine out of 10 of them Arabs – have died since the start of the al-Aqsa intifada in September.

Jordan signed a peace treaty with “Israel” in 1994, but popular opposition to “Israel” has continued, driven by the Palestinians who form about 60 per cent of the population.

The attacks will deepen the feeling among “Israelis” that they are surrounded by enemies, and will add weight to the warnings that the conflict could easily expand into a regional war.

Their Prime Minister, Ehud Barak, is said to be reconsidering a decision to hold early elections, partly because he is aware that he has only a remote chance of securing an outline peace deal with the Palestinians, which he needs for re-election. Meanwhile, the “Israelis” are waiting to see if Benjamin Netanyahu will launch a political comeback by declaring his candidacy for Prime Minister.

A week ago, Mr Barak agreed to elections, anticipating the will of a hostile Knesset which later the same day voted in favour of doing so.

 

The intifadah, matyrs, funerals goes on…(7)

Two Palestinians were martyred Tuesday when “Israeli” troops opened fire at them in the West Bank.

One man was shot during stone-thowing at “Israeli” soldiers near Deheisheh refugee camp outside Beit Lahm. The second Palestinian was killed during protests in the town of Ramallah.

Meanwhile Palestinians in Gaza buried a senior military leader of the Izzeddine al-Qassam Brigade, Hamas' military arm. The remains of Awad Selmi were found blown up on Monday near Mintar (Karni) crossing point.

Mohammed al-Zahhar, a Hamas leader, held “Israel” responsible for Selmi's death, asserting that military leader will be revenged.

On the other hand, an “Israeli” military source admitted that a Zionist settler was shot and injured near Ariel Settlement in the West Bank when a Palestinian opened fire at him.However,the source later on said that the settler  was injured by mistake by a bullet from an “Israeli” soldier at a checkpoint.

On Monday evening, “Israeli” occupation forces unleashed heavy machine-gun fire on the West Bank Palestinian town of Beit Jala from its positions in Gilo Settlement. Several Palestinians were wounded.

 

Priests not to pray on collaborators…(8)

"Because they betrayed their people, and are collaborating with the occupation army, praying for them is prohibited may it be in the church or mosque."

This was stated by Christian priests joining Muslim clergymen in their decision not to pray for the dead Muslims and Christians who are killed while serving the “Israeli” army.

The Spokesman of the Orthodox church archimandrite Hanna Atallah welcomed the decision of Muslim Clergymen, saying that many Christian priests support the decision.

“Israeli” daily Yediot Ahronot who reported the news said that due to the boycott the Arab community in the Zionist Entity imposes on the Arabs serving the “Israeli” army a lot of Arab youth refrained from entering the “Israeli” army and withdrew their applications.
