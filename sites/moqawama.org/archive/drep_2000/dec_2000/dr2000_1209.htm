December Saturday 09-2000

 Thurs2ay 29-2000

Introduction

“Israeli” threats to retaliate against any operation by the Islamic Resistance (IR) men prove that “Israel” has “a desire to spread violence’, Head of the Loyalty to Resistance Bloc MP Mohammad Raad said. “Hizbullah is “ready to respond to any Zionist action”, he added.

In the meantime, President Emile Lahoud reiterated that deploying the Lebanese Army in the South was “out of the question” as long as the country “is in a state of war with “Israel””.

Elsewhere, a 14-year-old boy is another victim of a land mine explosion in southern Lebanon region formely under “Israeli” occupation.

Barak threats prove desire to spread violence: Hizbullah…(1)

“ “Israeli” threats to retaliate against any operation by Hizbullah prove that “Israel” has "a desire to spread violence", Hizbullah MP Mohammad Raad was quoted in the Lebanese daily As-Safir Friday as saying. “Israeli” Prime Minister Ehud Barak's saying that Hizbullah-led operations in the Shibaa Farms "area are an attack on Zionist sovereignty reveals a desire to spread violence to Lebanon and the region", Raad said. Barak said Thursday that “Israel” will act assertively to defend its citizens and soldiers and will not tolerate any Hizbullah attack on the Shibaa Farms, an area occupied by “Israel” since 1967. Hizbullah is "ready to respond to any Zionist action", Raad said. On December 1, “Israel” had threatened Syrian troops in Lebanon with reprisal if Hizbullah continued its attacks on “Israeli” positions in the Shibaa Farms area. The resistance group killed an “Israeli” soldier and wounded two others in an attack on November 26, triggering “Israeli” air strikes against southern Lebanon for the first time since its withdrawal from the area in May. In October, Hizbullah raided an “Israeli” army post in the same occupied Shibaa Farms area and captured three soldiers. 

President stresses ‘priorities’…(2)

President Emile Lahoud said Friday that some parties were raising the issue of Syria’s presence here while forgetting the “negative aspects” of the Palestinian presence and their possible resettlement here.
Lahoud, who met with Khaled al-Marzooq, the managing editor of the Kuwaiti daily al-Anba, accused the unnamed parties of causing “dissension.”
Lahoud also reiterated that deploying the army in the South was “out of the question” as long as the country “is in a state of war with “Israel”, which is
still continuing its aggressions and violations.”
The president said he recently conveyed to the United Nations’ special envoy to the Middle East, Miguel Angel Moratinos, that no country subject to another country’s aggressions would put its army on the border zone.
He said that such deployment would subject Lebanon to “constant aggression.”
Lahoud rejected any comparison of “Israel’s” occupation to the Syrian presence, stressing that Lebanon would “help Syrians obtain their rights,” referring to the “Israeli” occupation of the Golan Heights.
Lahoud received a letter from the head of the Arab League, Esmat Abdel-Meguid, who said the league would continue to carry out Arab resolutions.
Abdel-Meguid was referring to aid promised by Arab countries to Lebanon for reconstruction, especially in the liberated areas.

Sayyed Fadlallah: "The enemy only understands the language of resistance"…(3)

Ayatollah Sayyed Mohammed Hussein Fadlallah said that mounting talks in American and “Israeli” press about the dangers of a regional war are part of a preventive war aimed at pressuring Lebanon and Syria, in addition to pushing Arab leaders to apply pressure on the Resistance in Lebanon.

During his Friday speech in Al-Hassanain Mosque, Sayyed Fadlallah called for putting more pressure on the “Israeli” enemy so that it withdraws from the remaining occupied Lebanese territories because "the enemy only understnads the language of reistance."

Moreover, Fadlallah asserted the necessity of settling the internal differences and producing a united political stand to confront the dangers the “Israeli” enemy poses.

Ayatollah Fadlallah praised the Arab and Islamic peoples for boycotting American products.

PM Hariri: "Shibaa Farms are Lebanese, and war is not for now"…(4)

Lebanese Prime Minister Rafic Hariri said Friday that despite “serious” “Israeli” threats against his country, there would be no “armed struggle” in the region.

"We cannot say that the “Israeli” threats are not serious,” Hariri told a delegation of the Lebanese Press Syndicate. “They are possible, but I consider the breakout of an armed struggle unlikely."

He said, however, “The armed struggle is ruled out, but sometimes people act contrary to logic."

Hariri’s comments came after “Israeli” Prime Minister Ehud Barak said Thursday that “Israel” would retaliate against attacks by Hizbullah  against “Israeli” targets.

The Lebanese Prime Minister reiterated that the occupied Shibaa Farms are part of Lebanese territory and U.N. resolution 425 covers this region as well.

Shepherd hurt after stepping on land mine…(5)

A 16-year-old shepherd was wounded in the South on Friday when a land mine left behind by “Israeli” forces exploded.
Ali Zouhour was tending his herd in eastern Yohmor, in an area close to Beaufort Castle in the former occupied zone, when he accidentally stepped on the explosive device.
Zouhour was treated at Nabatieh Hospital, where he is reportedly in a stable condition.
Nine civilians have been killed and 61 others wounded by mines since “Israel’s” troop withdrawal in May. A dozen military personnel have also been hurt during mine-clearing operations.
The United Nations estimates that there are more than 130,000 mines and unexploded shells in the formerly occupied area.

6,000 march in Lebanon, urging escalation of intifada…(6)

“ Nearly 6,000 Lebanese and Palestinians marched throught the streets of this southern Lebanese town Friday, shouting for the Palestinians of the intifada, or uprising, to take up arms against “Israelis”.

"Throw away the olive branch, and carry arms," marchers shouted while unfurling Lebanese and Palestinian national flags.

Lebanese and Palestinian sports, workers and cultural associations organised the event to honour the thirteenth anniversary of the start of the Palestinian territories' first intifada (1987-1993).

Ali Khalil and Ali Khrayss, Lebanese parliamentary members from Tyre, led the marchers through the streets. Many protestors came from the three Palestinian refugee camps around Tyre.

The protestors carried banners which read: "The intifada is the way to recover stolen Palestine"; "It must not be at any price to recognise the state of “Israel”..."

The 11-week old intifada has claimed more than 300 lives, mainly Palestinian.

On the "Day of Rage" 7 Palestinian martyrs, tens of wounded and 3 “Israelis” killed…(7)

Palestinian Intifadah fighters carried on their operations against the “Israeli” occupation. Friday morning, Palestinians attacked a vehicle carrying “Israeli” settlers at the entrance of Kiryat Arba Settlement in the West Bank. As a result, two settlers were killed and a third was injured. The Palestinian fighters were able to leave the site of operation.

Thursday evening another “Israeli” settler was killed and one injured in a similar attack near Ariha.

Later on Friday, five Palestinians were martyred, four of which are policemen, when “Israeli” tanks shelled the West Bank town of Jenine.

Meanwhile, clashes between the intifadah youths and occupation forces erupted in various locations on the "Days of Rage" (Friday and Saturday) designated by the Palestinians to commemorate the 13th anniversary of the first Palestinian intifadah in 1987.

A 17-year-old Palestinian from Beit Laham was martyred when he was shot during a clash between “Israeli” soldiers and hundreds of Palestinians as they tried to cross an “Israeli” check point to reach mosques in al-Quds (Jerusalem). 10 other Palestinians were also wounded.

In the West Bank town of Ramallah 20 Palestinians were injured, of which five seriously when the “Israeli” army used heavy machine guns and bombs to attack them.

In al-Khalil (Hebron) town, 7 Palestinians were wounded in stone-throwing clashes. 14-year-old Ahmad al-Kawwasi was shot in the head and his condition is critical, said hospital sources.

Palestinian sources also said that three Palestinian children between 2 and 5 years old were injured in al-Khalil after armed Jewish settlers opened fire at a Palestinian car.

At  Mintar (Karni) Gaza crossing point “Israeli” soldiers opened fire at a car carrying civilian Palestinians. The outcome: three injured including a 4-year-old child.

Confrontations also broke out after the Friday prayers at the Al-Aqsa Mosque in holy Quds when young Muslim worshippers hurled stones at heavy “Israeli” security. Several people were injured and ambulance crews say they treated at least five Palestinians who were wounded by “Israeli” bullets.

Waving fists and shouting, 'God is great', thousands of Palestinians had poured out of the Shrine. They set fire to an “Israeli” police station near the Gate, by the walls of the Old City.
