Hizbullah denies reports on prisoner swap…(1)

Information attributed to “security sources” and published in Al-Mustaqbal newspaper about developments in a German-mediated prisoner swap operation are simply “assumptions,” a Hizbullah source said Monday.
“Hizbullah’s position is clear: any information about the swap operation will be exclusively disclosed by Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah,” a Hizbullah press officer said.
Al-Mustaqbal, which is owned by Premier Rafik Hariri, has published several reports  about information on the swap operation mediated by Germany.
Its latest report, published Monday, said Hizbullah rejected an “Israeli” offer to trade the remains of fighters for information on the four “Israelis”. ““Israel” has proposed to Hizbullah to swap the remains of its martyrs for information on the “Israeli” captives and Hizbullah rejected that,” the newspaper said.
However, the official Hizbullah response to Al-Mustaqbal’s report was that “we have no information whatsoever.”
A Hizbullah source said that since Nasrallah announced that German mediation was under way, activity had been taking place. “But no one except the secretary-general can give any information about the swap,” the source said.
A political observer said that Al-Mustaqbal’s reports about inside information on the swap were not necessarily given directly by Hariri. “The Lebanese government is aware of all the developments related to the swap operation, but the prime minister has also adopted the policy of keeping the information away from media consumption.”
However, according to the observer, the daily could be getting analyses from an aide to the prime minister.
Germany is “definitely” not the source of information, as its Foreign Ministry has adopted a policy of not revealing any information about the swap and “it has proven this credibility during the last swap operation it mediated between Hizbullah and “Israel” (in 1999).”
Nasrallah said last week that he would return the “Israelis” only after “Israel” freed all its Arab detainees and returned the remains of Lebanese and Palestinian fighters killed during its invasions.
He also said “Israel” must provide maps of minefields it planted during its occupation of the South and information on the fate of Lebanese and Palestinians who went missing during that period.
On Sunday, Al-Mustaqbal said “Israel” would provide the maps, but that it appeared to have rejected the other demands.

 

Beirut sounds alarm on settlement of refugees
Lahoud orders diplomatic offensive to warn that Palestinians can’t stay…(2)

Top leaders spoke out strongly against settling Palestinians here Monday as diplomats mounted a campaign to alert the international community that Lebanon “will not budge” on the issue.
President Emile Lahoud issued instructions to intensify the diplomatic offensive.
Rising concern at the prospect of Palestinian settlement comes as “Israeli” and Palestinian negotiators get set to resume talks in Washington on Tuesday. Palestinian President Yasser Arafat indicated Sunday that under discussion is a proposal to have the Palestinians give up their demand for refugees to return in exchange for sovereignty over East Jerusalem.
Premier Rafik Hariri rejected any “Israeli”-Palestinian agreement that fails to take into consideration the issue of Palestinian refugees here.
“Lebanon will not approve, whether directly or indirectly” such an agreement, Hariri told MPs in his parliamentary bloc at a meeting in Koraytem.
Calling settlement “unacceptable,” Hariri said the country wanted the Palestinian people to regain all of their rights, including their homeland, with Jerusalem as its capital.
Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud summoned ambassadors of the five permanent members of the UN Security Council as well as other envoys, to convey Beirut’s stand.
He told reporters that the position was “one of the principles to which we strongly adhere and from which we will not budge under any circumstances,” adding that such ideas on settlement “neither serve the cause of peace … nor Palestinian rights, and contradict Arab and Islamic summit resolutions.”
He said Lebanon felt it was necessary, on the eve of the expected resumption of Palestinian-”Israeli” talks, “to underline the importance of the issue and not to bypass the principle of land for peace and a just and comprehensive peace.”
The US charge d’affaires, David Hale, who represented US Ambassador David Satterfield, declined to comment after his meeting with Hammoud, while the newly arrived British ambassador, Richard Kinchen, said he would relay Lebanon’s request to his government.
Chinese Ambassador Lie Zhentang told reporters that “China’s stand is very clear. We always support international legitimacy and back the Arab countries, including Lebanon.
“We call for a just and legitimate solution to the question of Palestinian refugees and I will … convey Lebanon’s stand to my government,” he added.
French Ambassador Philippe Le Courtier affirmed that the refugee issue “must not be solved at Lebanon’s expense,” adding that “we consider this question to be fundamental for the whole international community.”
Russian Ambassador Boris Polotin said Moscow felt that any solution to the refugee question “should take into consideration Lebanon’s interests.”
The dean of the Arab diplomatic corps, Qatari Ambassador Mohammed Ali Nueimi, said his country supported Beirut.
Swedish Ambassador Anne Elizabeth Dismor said she had reiterated her country’s support for the refugees’ right to return. She also reminded Hammoud that her country would, as of Jan. 1, assume the EU presidency.
Haig Sarafian, the Canadian ambassador, said his country’s position was well known. “We understand the Lebanese stand, and Canadian backing does not come through the ambassador, but through the authorities.”
Separately, Fatah’s commander in Lebanon, Sultan Abul-Ainayn, told a news conference at the Rashidieh refugee camp that Palestinians rejected the notion of settlement.
“Palestinians,” Abul-Ainayn declared, “are very attached to the right of return home according to UN resolutions.”

 

“Israeli” army fires three shells on southern Lebanon…(3)

“ The “Israeli” army Monday morning fired three shells on southern Lebanon from the Shibaa farms area, Lebanese police said.

The “Israeli” shells were fired from an observation post on the western side of Mount Hermon and exploded in an uninhabited mountainous region two kilometres (1.3 miles) from the Lebanese border.

“Israel” warned Lebanon and Syria of firm retaliation if Hizbullah led one more attack on its troops in the Shibaa farms, an area “Israel” claimed from Syria in 1967 and on which Beirut recently laid claim.

Since the May 24 withdrawal of “Israeli” troops from southern Lebanon after 22 years of occupation, Hizbullah has been leading military operations against the Shibaa farms.

On November 26, an “Israeli” soldier died in a bomb attack led by Hizbullah, and six weeks earlier, three more were captured in the Shibaa farms.

The “Israeli” air force flew over the area Monday, Lebanese police added.

“Israeli” fighter-bombers have frequently violated Lebanese air space since the May 24 pull-out.

 

Lebanon files complaint to UN over “Israeli” flights…(4)

“ Lebanon has again complained to the United Nations over continued “Israeli” air force violations of Lebanese airspace, the national news agency ANI said Monday.

"Lebanon has filed a complaint against “Israel” to the United Nations, after repeated “Israeli” flights over Lebanese skies and repeated violations by breaking the sound barrier," ANI said.

“Israeli” warplanes last broke the sound barrier over Beirut and cities in southern Lebanon and the eastern Beqaa Valley on Saturday.

“Israel” violated Lebanese airspace 123 times in the one month after the soldiers were snatched, according to UN figures.
