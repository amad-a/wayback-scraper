“Israelis” acknowledge talks on possible swap
Sneh: ‘No one expects to get our boys back without something in return’…(1)

“Israel” said Tuesday that it was holding German-brokered negotiations with Hizbullah to swap two resistance leaders with four “Israelis” but said it did not expect a deal soon because Hizbullah offered no information on their condition.
“In the last few days there was the first contact under German mediation with Hizbullah people,” said the “Israeli” military’s chief of staff, Lieutenant General Shaul Mofaz. “This was just the start of a process that I believe will take time.”
Hizbullah seized three “Israeli” soldiers and one reservist colonel in October to win the release of Hizbullah’s Abdel-Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani of the Belivers Resistance. The party has demanded the release of all Lebanese detainees in “Israel”.
Obeid and Dirani were seized by “Israeli” commandos in 1989 and 1994, respectively, to be used as bargaining chips for missing “Israeli” airman Ron Arad, whose plane was shot down over the South in 1986.
Obeid and Dirani made a rare public appearance in “Israel’s” Supreme Court on Tuesday as it rejected their appeal against a lower court ruling that upheld “Israel’s” right to keep them in custody. The court rejected the appeal, accepting the state’s argument that the two still pose a danger to “Israel’s” security.
But Mofaz said that reports that both sides were close to an agreement were overly optimistic. “We still have not received information on the condition of the captives,” he told “Israel” Radio.
He said that Hizbullah’s refusal to offer information on the three soldiers, a key “Israeli” condition for progress in the talks, was hampering the talks.
“That is just the start of a step that in my estimation will take time,” Mofaz said. “Unfortunately the media reports were too optimistic in terms of the process of the negotiations.”
“Israel’s” deputy defense (war) minister, Ephraim Sneh, said “Israel” would be willing to trade Obeid and Dirani for the captive “Israelis”, arguing that it was clear the price of any exchange would be the release of the two resistance leaders.
“We will have to pay,” Sneh told “Israel” Radio. “No one expects to get our boys back without something in return. We are not naive.”
Sneh did not elaborate on whether “Israel” would demand Arad and other soldiers missing in Syria in any deal.
“These people (Dirani and Obeid), one of them is personally involved in the capture of Ron Arad, the other is a senior member of Hizbullah ­ they will be part of the price,” he said.
Sneh said the two leaders were “prominent components” of any payment by “Israel”. He also said “Israel” was willing to do “many, many things” to bring “Israeli” soldiers home but insisted there was no breakthrough.
“The government of “Israel” is working in several channels to make contact” he said.
Sneh would not comment on reports that “Israeli” Foreign Minister Shlomo Ben-Ami was meeting with his German counterpart, Joschka Fischer, about the contacts.

“Israel” supreme court rejects appeal on detained Lebanese militants…(2)

“ “Israel’s” supreme court on Tuesday rejected an appeal against the continued detention of two Lebanese Muslim leaders, held for years without charge or trial.

Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid and Mustapha Dirani were abducted by “Israeli” commandos during the Jewish state's occupation of Lebanon as bargaining chips in any negotiations on the release of missing “Israeli” servicemen.

The five-member court rejected the appeal and will give its reasons in the next few days, a court spokesman told AFP.

The two men are being held in administrative detention, a system condemned by human rights groups under which “Israel” holds detainees without charge or trial for renewable six-month periods.

It is one of the lingering points of hostility between “Israel” and Lebanon since the Jewish state's ended its 22-year occupation of its northern neighbour in May.

Sheikh Obeid, a local spiritual leader of Hizbullah, was kidnapped by “Israeli” commandoes in 1989 while Dirani was seized in 1994.

The two remain in custody along with another 17 militants despite a supreme court ruling in April ordering the release of another 13 Lebanese prisoners held as bargaining chips.

In particular, “Israel” is seeking information about the fate of Ron Arad, an “Israeli” airman who was shot down over Lebanon in 1986 and captured alive.

Hizbullah, which spearheaded the armed campaign against “Israel’s” occupation, captured three “Israeli” soldiers in early October and later that month said it had captured an “Israeli” colonel working for “Israeli” intelligence who had entered Lebanon on a false passport.

A Lebanese newspaper said Monday that German mediation has made progress in securing a swap of Lebanese detained in “Israel” against the captured soldiers, with a deal due "within 10 days."

Reporters take swipe at Goksel…(3)

Local reporters in the South  criticized UNIFIL spokesman Timur Goksel on Tuesday for “undermining their credibility,” following comments by the UN official a day earlier.
“We denounce Goksel for undermining our credibility and dignity, we who have been working for so long. We wonder, is the reporting of people’s concerns and suffering considered ignorance, fabrication, and nonsense?” asked a statement issued by the reporters.
The reporters said they refused to “respond in the same way as the …official, who issued an irresponsible statement.”
They also demanded that the Press Federation and the Photographers’ Union take a “firm position in response to this insult to our dignity.”
On Sunday Goksel accused an “irresponsible” reporter of creating news about a UN modification of the Blue Line, which was carried by “a list of newspapers, television, and radio stations.”
In its Tuesday edition, The Daily Star ran a headline that indicated Goksel singled out Hizbullah’s al-Manar TV station for irresponsible reporting. Goksel criticized many media outlets and did not name al-Manar. The Daily Star regrets the error.

40 former collaborators imprisoned…(4)

A military court late Monday sentenced 40 people to prison terms ranging from three weeks to three years for contact or collaboration with “Israel”.
Two men received three years imprisonment for belonging to the former “Israeli”-proxy militia; 11 defendants received jail terms of three weeks plus fines of LL1 million for entering “Israel”.
The defendants were the 58th batch of collaborators to stand trial since “Israel’s” May withdrawal. The vast majority of the  2,500-militiamen surrendered to the Lebanese authorities after the “Israeli” pullback.

“Israeli” commando assasinates a Fatah member…(5)

For the second time in 48 hours an “Israeli” commando unit assassinates an intifadah fighter in a barbaric manner.

Fatah Movement, Arafat's PLO faction, announced that “Israeli” soldiers opened fire using heavy artillery on Youssef Ahmad Abou Soy outside his residence. Seventeen bullets pierced his head, neck, chest, and legs.

On the other hand, three “Israeli” soldiers were wounded during exchanges of fire Monday evening with Palestinian gunmen in the Palestinian town of Khan Younis in Gaza. The “Israeli” troops shelled Deir al-Balah Palestinian refugee camp, in the town.

Shooting also erupted between “Israeli” troops and Palestinian policemen in the village of Sereen in Ramallah,in the West Bank. Three Palestinian policemen were wounded.

In addition, intifadah fighters opened fire on an “Israeli” occupation outpost near the Egyptian border and on the “Israeli” Kfardaron Settlement, southern of Gaza Strip.

Concerning the awaited mission of U.N. observers to the Palestinian territories faced by an “Israeli” objection, the U.N. Security Council under U.S. pressure postponed a vote on a draft resolution on the observers mission.

The Security Council said the postponement of such a vote was due to ongoing diplomatic efforts which aims at halting confrontations in Palestinian territories.

Arabs pledge support for Palestinians…(6)

An Arab ministerial committee said it was in favor of granting 100 million dollars in urgent aid to the Palestinians. The head of the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) political department Faruq Qaddumi said the matter was received "very positively." Qaddumi told reporters during a joint news conference with Arab League Secretary General Esmat Abdel Meguid that the Palestinian delegation to the summit follow-up committee in Damascus asked for an urgent advance for the Palestinians, who are continuing their uprising. Meguid said they expect the funds to reach the Palestinians as soon as possible. Arab officials said the Palestinians were asking for 100 million dollars from Arab support funds for the Palestinian established at the October Arab summit in Cairo, when Arab leaders promised a total of one billion dollars for the Palestinians. The money was divided into two funds: one dedicated to preserving the Arab character of occupied Arab easy Al-Quds and the other set up to assist families of the nearly 300 Palestinians killed by “Israeli” soldiers since late September. The financial aid was the only recommendation announced by the committee but the official Syrian SANA news agency published the text of recommendations. They included a plan to study the reactivation of the Office of the Boycott of “Israel” (OBI), whose job was to release every six months a blacklist of companies maintaining relations with “Israel”. The meeting also recommended the Arab Parliamentary Union approach the European Union to "carry out its decision to import Palestinian products and a ban on imports from the (Jewish) settlements." The meeting included foreign ministers from Egypt, Lebanon, Bahrain, Jordan, Tunisia and Morocco. 

UN accuses “Israel” of impeding relief supplies into Gaza strip…(7)  

The United Nations refugee relief agency has accused “Israel” of impeding two and half months of humanitarian supplies from entering the Gaza Strip by imposing unreasonable security restrictions and charging thousands of dollars in port fees.   "Humanitarian supplies should be facilitated and not obstructed. We are not getting the trust and support that we deserve from “Israel”," Peter Hansen, commissioner-general for the UN Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East (UNRWA) told AFP in Gaza City.   "We are facing a very, very serious situation of deprivation in everyday supplies such as nourishment, medicine, building materials to repair refugee shelters destroyed by the shelling, and artificial limbs," said Hansen, adding that food stocks were all but depleted.   “Israel” slapped a tight blockade on the Palestinian territories when fighting broke out in late September, dealing a crippling blow to the economy of the Gaza Strip and West Bank.    Lengthy security checks of UN containers at “Israel’s” Ashdod port and the Karni crossing into the Gaza Strip, along with steep financial charges that the UN agency has refused to pay have prevented all relief aid from being delivered, according to UN officials and a report shown to AFP.   Hansen said the agency was "anticipating a crisis" unless the restrictions were lifted immediately.    The agency -- which serves a 3.7 million Palestinian refugee population in the region -- has raised the issue with “Israeli” authorities, who say "they are looking at it," and at the UN general assembly, Hansen said.   "We will continue to remind “Israel” of its humanitarian and legal obligations," said Hansen, whose staff has complained to the “Israeli” Army and the foreign ministry, to no avail.   “Israel” is asking the UN agency for some 120,000 dollars in storage and handling fees for two months worth of supplies, mainly food and medicine, curently at the port.   Authorities are also demanding another 40,000 dollars in fees at Karni crossing, Lionel Brisson, the agency's director of operations in Gaza said.   Storage and handling fees imposed on an additional 25,000 tonnes of emergency relief expected for delivery, along with future charges if restrictions are not eased, could cost the UN agency "hundreds of thousands of dollars," Brisson said.   "We consider that “Israeli” authorities are responsible for these charges because they are holding up the delivery of supplies," he said.   The charges are ontop of some 3.8 million dollars in customs duties that the UN has been charged by “Israel” since 1993, after the signing of the Oslo peace accord.   Before 1993, humanitarian aid was exempted from duty.   Since the Palestinian uprising against “Israel” erupted, some 21,000 families or 100,000 people, considered "worst cases," have received UN food rations.  But most food stocks have dried up, and another 100,000 families or 600,000 people are hungry, the agency said. At a refugee camp in Rafah, where Hansen paid a visit on Monday, angry women shouted at UN workers for not giving them food rations. "Our families are hungry," they yelled, tugging the arms of aid workers and journalists. "Unfortunately they are not considered the worst cases," said a UN staffer, apologetically. Last week, the UN Middle East coordinator said “Israel’s” closure of the Palestinian territories was "counter-productive" and had wiped out years of progress.  Terje Roed-Larsen said in a bleak report that the Palestinian economy had lost some 500 million dollars during the first 60 days of the violence and that unemployment was now running at 40 percent.   The blockade, which prevents Palestinians from working in the Jewish state, had thrown 260,000 people out of work while a total of one million  -- about one third of the population -- had suffered a "serious income loss," he said.   On Monday, Palestinian health minister Ridayh Zahnoun said “Israel” was blocking medical supplies from entering the territories.  The minister also said he had raised the issue with a US-led fact-finding committee which arrived Monday to investigate the root of “Israeli”-Palestinian violence, which has left 320 mostly Palestinians killed in 11 weeks.   "“Israel’s” occupation is still preventing international and Arab aid to the Palestinian territories," Zahnoun told a press conference.   He said “Israeli” authorities were barring the delivery of 20 ambulances from Eygpt, another 18 from Amman, and a total of 70 trucks full of medical supplies from Iraq.
