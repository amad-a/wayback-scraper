Hizbullah stays silent on captives…(1)

Nasrallah: Enemyh will become weaker and more humiliated by Intifada and Resistance

Hizbullah’s Secretary-General Hassan Nasrallah said on Friday that the party would continue to remain secretive about the fate of four “Israelis” it holds captive and urged Palestinians to continue fighting “Israel”.
Nasrallah was addressing a Palestinian crowd by phone at a Hamas rally on the occasion of the movement’s anniversary in Nablus.
“We insist on our position regarding the “Israeli” soldiers and detainees we hold in our hands,” he said. “In the end, it is the “Israelis” who will bow.”
Nasrallah claimed that “Israel”, “backed by the United States,” was incapable of acquiring any information on the fate of three soldiers and the one other “Israeli” it captured in October. The party plans on swapping the four “Israelis” for Lebanese and Arab prisoners held by “Israel”.
“With patience and perseverance, we will be able to liberate our prisoners and detainees (in “Israel”) along with yours, and fulfill theirs and their families’ dearest hopes,” he told the group.
Nasrallah urged Palestinians to mount their fight against “Israel” arguing that they were fighting the “holy battle” of all Arabs and Muslims.
“The responsibility bestowed on you at this stage is great, dangerous, historical, and exceptional,” Nasrallah said. “You must maintain great confidence in your ability to win over “Israel” as long as you continue to believe in God.”
He called on all Palestinians to be confident that they could not only liberate the Arab lands “Israel” illegally occupied in 1967, but could also “obliterate this rapist entity and this cancerous tumor.”
He said the notion that “Israel” could be removed from existence must be promoted because it had “extremely great possibilities” of being realized.
“Your enemy,” he said, “will become weaker and more humiliated and divided every new day of your intifada and resistance in occupied Palestine.”

 

Hizbullah visits detainees families on Eid al-Fitr…(2)

A delegation of Hizbullah social workers and released detainees visited the families of Lebanese detainees in “Israeli” prisons.

The delegation first visited the family of Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid.

Families of Mohammed Bdeir, Ali Balhass, Jawad Kasfi, and Yusuf Wazni also received the delegation who wished them a happy Eid al-Fitr.

The family of the Lebanese detainees Mustafa Hammoud was visited in the mosque of Markaba village after the Zionist enemy destroyed their house.

The delegation transmitted the greetings of the Secretary-General of Hizbullah Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah.

 

“Israeli” occupation wounds five Lebanese civilians at border…(4)

“Israeli” troops continued its aggressions against Lebanon Thursday by firing at Lebanese civilians across the border, wounding five including an eight-year-old boy.

The “Israeli” soldiers manning the al-Abbad hill opened fire while the victims were trying to prevent the soldiers from picturing them.

An “Israeli” army source, trying to justify the shooting, claimed that soldiers fired at the legs of two armed Lebanese civilians who pointed guns at them during stone-throwing clashes near a tomb on the border.

However, the source did not speak about the Lebanese child, who was directly targeted by an “Israeli” soldier as pictures taken by Al-Manar T.V. depicted.

At least 12 demonstrators have been killed, both Lebanese and other Arabs, and more than 40 have been injured by “Israeli” fire at the border over the past seven months.

Moreover, “Israeli” fighter-bombers broke the sound barrier for the third consecutive day over various Lebanese cities in the south and over Beirut.

The “Israeli” violation of Lebanese airspace coincide with the Eid al-Fitr which marks the end of Ramadan fasting month.

The continuous “Israeli” infringements prompted Lebanese President Emile Lahoud to ask the Foreign Ministry to complain to the United Nations about the airspace violations.

 

“Israel’s” Lebanese collaborators go back on the air…(5)

“For more than 20 years “Israel’s” allies in Lebanon helped boost the Jewish state's occupation with regular broadcasts over the radio. Today the “Israelis” are gone, but the "Voice of the South" is back with its decidedly “Israeli” view of the region.

The new station began broadcasting for the Christian and Muslim holidays and is called "Mediterranean Basin Experimental Radio." But, broadcast like the Voice of the South on 756 kilohertz medium wave, few Lebanese believe the name-change is more than cosmetic.

"The presenters are all the same. We all recognized their voices instantly, with no doubt whatsover," said a resident in the zone “Israel” occupied for 22 years.

Voice of the South was run by the South Lebanon Army, “Israel’s” proxy militia during the “Israeli” occupation. The station headquarters, just into Lebanon from the “Israeli” border, was hastily dismantled on May 24, hours before the last “Israeli” troops left.

Mediterranean Basin Experimental Radio comes through with great reception in the former occupied zone, and the station can be heard from 50 kilometers (30 miles) away. The radio is most popular in Christian villages, the key centers of support for “Israel” during the occupation.

With tensions still high with “Israel”, the station's rebirth has predictably infuriated officials in Lebanon and in Syria, the major powerbroker in Beirut.

Qassem Hashem, a member of Lebanon's parliament who is part of Baath party, told AFP that Mediterranean Basin Experimental Radio was "a flagrant interference in Lebanese political life and reveals “Israel’s” intentions to continue its aggression against Lebanon."

And the station hardly shies away from the issues that enrage “Israel’s” foes. The radio's political program kicks off with Lebanese songs of nostalgia and then usually dwells on the need for Syria to withdraw its 35,000 troops from Lebanon.

Other favorite topics on the radio are the internal situation in Syria, particularly rifts within President Bashar al-Assad's family and the ruling clique, along with "the danger of Iran, whose sole goal is to export Islamic revolution to Lebanon".

But the biggest draw to the radio station is not politics, but family. Some 4,000 Lebanese fled to “Israel” following the May withdrawal, likely fearing retribution from Beirut authorities or Islamic militants, and many of the refugees left behind relatives.

Each day dozens of people, mostly women and children, send messages to their loves ones left behind in Lebanon. The broadcasters identify their first names and their home villages, but never their surnames.

"Hello, my name is Karin. I am from Rmeish and I am 11 years old. For the holidays I want to send my greetings to my friends and my instructor Siham and I hope to come back to school as soon as possible," begins one message.

"I give kisses to my grandmother, my grandfather and my aunt. We are in good health; don't worry about us," says Lara, from Qlaiaa.

Camille, apparently a former pro-”Israeli” militiaman, is slightly blunter: "I greet my loved ones in Ain Ebel. My wife and my children are doing fine and send you kisses for the holidays. We hope the situation will turn around soon and we will see each other soon in the homeland we miss."

The station broadcasts in the morning and the evening with a slogan: "We are with you to reduce distances and heal the wounds."

 

Vigil remembers victims of intifada…(6)

A candle-light vigil took place on Monday evening in support of the intifada and an expression of solidarity with the hundreds who lost their lives in the Palestinian-”Israeli” struggle.
Organized by the Arab National Forum and the Coordination Forum of Palestinian non-governmental organizations, the vigil was held at the Palestinian Martyrs’ Graveyard on Shatila Roundabout and drew more than 200 Palestinians and Lebanese.
The graveyard, where Palestinians, Lebanese and even Japanese who were killed fighting “Israel” are buried, was decorated with hundreds of candles, as were the surrounding walls.
A concentration of candles bearing the names of the people killed in the current intifada were placed on a memorial statue for the victims of the 1976 Tel al-Zaatar massacre. A candle with the name of Mohammed al-Durra, whose brutal shooting was aired on television around the world, was raised above the rest. Holding candles, people walked through the graveyard toward the statue to pay homage to the victims.
“Whatever we do to show our solidarity here is not enough compared to the sacrifices our people are making in Palestine,” said Kalthoum Ghandour, a dentist with the Palestinian Red Crescent, guarding her candle from the breeze.
“The intifada needs support from all Arabs and people who love peace,” intoned Qassem Ayna of Beit Atfal as-Soumoud, an organization that cares for orphaned Palestinian children in Lebanon.
Ayna added that “Israel’s” violation of the rights of children, women and the elderly “is clear,” but he said that many of the victims are high-school and university students.
At a time when “Israeli” and US proposals threaten to exclude the right of Palestinian refugees to return to Palestine, the solemn yet hopeful, vigil also served to restate this right. It ran simultaneously with a number of similar gatherings around the world, including the United States, Australia, Palestine and even Haifa, annexed by “Israel” in 1948.
Rabih Bashour, who helped coordinate the vigils, said he was pleased with the unified show of solidarity.
With Haifa’s participation, Bashour said, “this is a rare link between Palestinians inside and outside occupied Palestine.”

 

A Palestinian martyr and 15  injured in new clashes…(7)

A Palestinian policeman was killed in a battle involving “Israeli” tank fire near the Gaza  Erez crossing point Friday, underscoring the fragility of a U.S. bid to mediate a Middle East peace accord in the next three weeks.

Palestinians said the policeman was killed by an “Israeli” tank shell fired in a gun battle that broke out after “Israeli” soldiers bulldozed trees near a Palestinian military post.

During the battle, one of the fiercest in more than a week, Palestinian police and snipers exchanged fire with “Israeli” soldiers near the Erez crossing in Gaza..

The death brought the toll to at least 305 Palestinians killed in a three-month-old Palestinian Intifadah (uprising).

Palestinian hospital sources said 15 Palestinians at least were shot and wounded by “Israeli” soldiers' bullets at the northern entrance of the West Bank town of Ramallah during stone-throwing clashes.

After Thursday's bombing, the “Israeli” occupation tightened its closure of the West Bank and Gaza, barring Palestinians from moving between West Bank cities. Palestinians call the edicts, which kept several thousand people from their jobs, collective punishment.

The closure also prevented thousands of Palestinians from participating in the Friday prayers at Al-Haram al-Sharif in holy Quds.

The security measures come a day after the Islamic Jihad Movement killed an “Israeli” explosives expert and a soldier in a roadside bombing on the border between Gaza and "“Israel”".

The explosives expert was dismantling a false bomb when a second device hidden nearby exploded after soldiers had rushed to the scene.  Earlier, two pipe bombs went of in a Tel Abib bus, wounding 14 “Israelis”, one critically.

Analysts said the Gaza and Tel Abib operations succeeded along with the ongoing intifadah in causing the failure of Sharm al-Sheikh Summit between Mubarak, Barak, and Arafat on Friday under U.S. auspices. Analysts consider, as a result, that the “Israeli”-Palestinian negotiations enters into a very fragile phase, with chances to reach an agreement quite low unless unawaited surprises occur.
