December Wednesday 20-2000

 Thurs2ay 29-2000

 

More confrontations in occupied Palestine…(1)

Palestinian territories witnessed another day of clashes between the “Israeli” occupation and Palestinians. Tuesday morning, clashes broke out between Jewish settlers and Palestinians at Beit Anoun crossroad in the west Bank town of al-Khalil (Hebron). Trouble began when a group of settlers blocked the road around Halhoul, stopping the traffic.

In al-Khalil town, as well, students in the Abdel Hayy Shaheen Palestinian school were saved from a massacre. A time bomb was found in the school and Palestinian bomb experts hurried take control of the situation.

In Rafah, in the Gaza Strip, “Israeli” bulldozers under artillery shell protection, razed a vast area of land belonging to Palestinians.

Following fierce fighting between “Israeli”s and Palestinians in the Jewish settlement of Psagot near the West Bank town of Ramallah, a number of settlers for the first time fled their homes for several hours fearing the gunfire.

 

Demonstrations against Palestinian-”Israeli” negotiations…(2)

In protest against resuming negotiations between “Israeli”s and the Palestinian Authority, various cities in the West Bank closed businesses.

Massive marches also took place in several Palestinian cities against the “Israeli” occupation. Marchers demanded for a halt in negotiations with the occupier and for the continuation of the intifadah.

During the funeral in Gaza of a Palestinian police officer, Abdel Muti Sabaawi, Palestinians also called for a stop of negotiations. The police officer was killed due to an “Israeli” bomb explosion,which he was trying to dismantle in Gaza strip.
Meanwhile, Palestinian Authority President Yasser Arafat traveled to Cairo where he is to hold talks with Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak concerning the ongoing communications aimed at reviving the settlement process. A Palestinian source said Arafat will also discuss with Mubarak the “Israeli” aggressions against the Palestinian people.

 

Khatami warns U.S. of boycott dangers…(3)

Iranian President Mohammed Khatami said in remarks published on Tuesday that Arabs must carefully weigh any decision to apply an oil boycott or economic sanctions against countries backing "Israel".

``It is the Arabs' and Muslims' right to take decisions which protect and maintain their interests,'' Khatami told Qatar's Arabic-language al-Sharq newspaper.

``But we must study and understand the negatives and positives of using the oil weapon and economic sanctions, especially with America.''

He was answering a question on the possibility of an oil embargo and economic sanctions against supporters of "Israel", particularly the United States.

``America supports 'Israel' so blindly that we have started to think that the U.S. capital isn't in Washington but in Tel Aviv,'' Khatami said.

``America must take a just view of our issues so that we can break the walls of mistrust so that we are not forced to use weapons whose effectiveness it (the United States) knows well,'' he added.

At least 330  Palestinians, have been killed since “Israeli”-Palestinian clashes erupted in September.

There have been some calls in Arab and Muslim countries for an oil embargo and a boycott of U.S. and “Israeli” products.

Gulf states alone, led by Saudi Arabia, sit on some 65 percent of the world's proven oil reserves and some Arab states in North Africa are also key oil exporters.

Iran, a major oil producer, does not recognize "Israel". The Islamic republic opposes the Middle East "peace" process and has repeatedly called for stronger support from Muslim and Arab countries to back the Palestinians against the Zionist state.
