Hizbullah-”Israel” prisoners swap deal within 10 days: paper…(1)

“ German mediation has made progress in securing a swap of Lebanese detained in “Israel” against “Israeli” soldiers captured by Hizbullah, with a deal due "within 10 days", a Beirut daily said Monday.

"The contacts Germany has been undertaking between “Israel” and Hizbullah have gone a long way toward reaching an equitable exchange operation in which the party Hizbullah will realize its demands," said As Safir.

"An agreement is due to be reached on all details within 10 days," it said.

The paper said a German delegation held talks last week in Beirut with security officials from Hizbullah over the prisoners' swap.

It said "“Israel” had put forward many conditions which Hizbullah rejected, based on the principle of the equity formula agreed upon by the German government as a basis for the exchange operation."

Last Tuesday Angelo Gnaedinger, the delegate for Europe and the Middle East of the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC), said he passed on to Hizbullah information from “Israel” about Lebanese prisoners there.

He was speaking after meeting here with Sheikh Naim Qassem, the Hizbullah number two.

Hizbullah has been calling for the release of all Arab prisoners in “Israel”, particularly Lebanese militants captured during “Israel’s” 22-year occupation of south Lebanon.

The ICRC has asked in vain to see “Israeli” prisoners held in Lebanon, but faced a rejection by Hizbullah because “Israel” denies access to one of the 19 Lebanese it is detaining, leader Hajj Mustafa Dirani.

Hizbullah captured three “Israeli” soldiers in early October in Shibaa Farms, claimed by Lebanon but occupied by “Israel”. The three taken were Sergeant Majors Omar Suad and Avraham Binyamin, and Sergeant Adi Abitan.

On October 18, Hizbullah said it had captured an “Israeli” colonel working for “Israeli” intelligence who had entered Lebanon on a false passport.

In “Israel”, he was identified as Elhanan Tannenbaum, a reserve colonel specialising in electronics.

 

“Israeli” supreme court to study appeal by Lebanese detainees…(2)

“ “Israel’s” supreme court will hear an appeal on Tuesday against the continued detention of two Lebanese leaders who were kidnapped and held without trial for several years, their lawyer said.

Sheikh Abdel Karim Obei and Mustapha Dirani were captured in 1989 and 1994 respectively by the “Israeli” army during its occupation of Lebanon to use as a bargaining chip for informaion on “Israeli” soldiers missing in Lebanon.

Since then, they have been placed in administrative detention without charge or trial for renewable six-month periods, a practice condemned by human rights organisations.

"It is a supplementary hearing based on our July appeal over the previous renewal of adminstrative custody, given that we have so far not received a decision," Tzvi Rich told AFP Monday.

Rich said he was not sure if the court, which released 13 other Lebanese detainees in April, would make a decision on Tuesday.

 

Lahoud urges UN to step up mine-clearing…(3)

President Emile Lahoud said Monday that he hoped UNIFIL would continue to maintain peace in the South and remove life-threatening land mines.
Lahoud, who met with the personal representative of the UN secretary-general for southern Lebanon, Rolf Knutsson, urged the organization to “intensify” its search for mines.
Now finishing his term
in Lebanon, Knutsson bade Lahoud farewell and said that he was “honored and privileged to have had an opportunity over the past four months to work in Lebanon at a time when the country stands reunited after decades of occupation.”
He added that this achievement should be accompanied “as quickly as possible by a comprehensive, just and lasting peace in the Middle East.”
Knutsson said that “it is of vital importance to maintain peace and tranquillity along the Blue Line as an essential contribution to such a regional peace.”
Knutsson will be succeeded by Staffan de Mitsura, who is due to arrive in January.

 

Lebanese shepherd injured by “Israeli” shots in southern Lebanon…(4)

“ A Lebanese goat shepherd was lightly wounded Monday when “Israelis” shot on hills near Shibaa Farms sector, Lebanese police said.

Sixty-year-old Mohammad Zahra had his leg injured by shrapnel. An “Israeli” tank fired six shells and a burst of machine-gun fire in the late afternoon "very abruptly and despite no incident having been reported in the area," police said.

Twenty goats were killed by the shooting.

The Shibaa Farms area was captured by “Israel” from Syria in 1967 but Beirut recently laid claim to the area, a position backed by Damascus.

On November 26, Hizbullah led a bomb attack on the sector, killing an “Israeli” soldier.

“Israel” responded by saying it would launch heavy strikes on Lebanon if the attacks were not stopped.

In October, Hizbullah kidnapped three “Israeli” soldiers in the Shibaa Farms area in a bid to secure a prisoner swap with “Israel”.

 

South Lebanon slowly starts to lose its mines– but too late for some…(5)

Mohamed Sowaid is nine and has multiple shrapnel wounds. His brother Ali – equally mutilated – is only five. Issam Missilmani is a 33-year-old fisherman who had his right foot and left hand blown off.

Alaa Hussein is 17 and Mohamed Hijazi is 22; both were blown up, Mohamed losing his left foot. And they are just the mine casualties of southern Lebanon in one day this month.

In all, 11 civilians have been killed and at least 56 seriously wounded in the fields around their homes since the “Israeli” army retreated out of Lebanon in May, leaving behind 70,000 mines.

The figure is official. The four volumes of documents and minefield maps, which the withdrawing “Israeli” army handed to the United Nations after they ended their 22-year occupation of 10 per cent of Lebanon seven months ago are a history of warfare, of No 4 “Israeli” antipersonnel mines and No 10 plastic mines, of booby-traps and minefields atop minefields.

In all, the UN believes there are 130,000 mines and booby-traps and unexploded bombs scattered across the harsh, brown hills of southern Lebanon, some of them dating back to the French mandate and the Vichy French-Allied battles of the Second World War.

Fresh from 10 months in Kosovo, Don Macdonald, 46, from Forres in Grampian – after 22 years as an RAF bomb disposal officer – sits in his office at Naqqoura on the Lebanese-”Israeli” border with computer banks that show him the location of the tiniest bits of ordnance in the wadis and thickets of this dangerous countryside. The “Israelis” handed over a list of 288 booby-traps. At Kfar Roumane was "a [booby] trapped vest [flak jacket] and side charge", near Yohmor was "a trapped LAW [Light AntiArmour Weapon]". Both were left during the occupation for Hizbollah guerrillas to pick up.

Intriguingly, their location proves that the “Israelis” sent patrols right through the UN peace-keeping lines to leave these booby-traps close to the Litani river.

"So far, the UN's Ukrainian battalion has destroyed 2,135 mines to clear land used by UN troops. The Lebanese government has to de-mine most of southern Lebanon; and, so far, the authorities have scarcely started to fence off the minefields. One of the little boys so badly wounded had seen a television programme about mines and tragically decided to try his own hand at clearance.

There are Russian PMD-6 mines – left by the “Israelis” or the Palestinians – and French mines from 1947, left along the coast in the very last days of the mandate. In some places, “Israeli” minefields are planted on top of Second World War minefields, layers of Lebanon's terrifying history waiting to explode and re-explode under future generations. A three-man UN mine action coordination cell operates with the UN Interim Force in Lebanon, its New Zealand boss, Greg Lindstrom, paying tribute to the detail of the maps the “Israelis” handed over, which included suspected minefields left by earlier occupiers.

"There's a peace dividend to all this," he says. "Clearing minefields means that people can come back to their lands. I saw this in Mozambique. It's a great feeling to see these people return to farm their land." But the task that faces Lebanon, let alone the UN, is awesome. Of the “Israeli” mines, 50,644 lie along the UN's "blue line" – roughly corresponding to the international frontier – while another 48 clusters of minefields, containing 7,529 devices, are placed elsewhere.

Around every “Israeli” artillery position and outpost of “Israel’s” now defunct South Lebanon Army militia lie acres of ordnance waiting to explode. Children regularly play in the old redoubts; at least 45 of these have minefields round them. More than 288 “Israeli” booby-traps – including bombs disguised as rocks – lie undefused among the rocks and vegetation.

Are they inherently evil things, these lethal little creatures amid the dark, beautiful hills? "All weapons are," Mr Lindstrom says. Mr Macdonald is more philosophical. "In the hands of someone in the military, used in a military sense, mapped and taken away, that's one thing. It's the mines that are unmapped and left to kill others. It's not the mine that's the problem. It's the people who use them."

 

“Israeli” occupation assassinates an Islamic Jihad military leader…(6)

"Al-Quds Brigade," the military arm of the Islamic Jihad Movement in Palestine, announced in a statement on Monday the martyrdom of one of its military leaders, Anwar Hamran.

The statement said that "Hamran (from the village of Arrabah in Jenine) was assassinated Monday by a Zionist commando unit in the town of Nablus (West Bank) when the “Israelis” opened fire at him. Nineteen bullets were found in his body."

"The heroic martyr, accused by the Zionist authorities of being responsible for the bold operation in Yehuda and al-Khudaira, is one of the heroic military leaders of the Islamic Jihad. He participated and designed many courageous operations - martyrdom operations, gun fire attacks, ambuscades - which targeted the occupation soldiers and settlers in various locations in the Zionist depths and in the West Bank, killing and injuring several persons in the ranks of the enemy," announced the Islamic Jihad which maintained that it will retaliate very soon for the assassination.

"The response will be quick and painful so that the occupation shall know that the blood of martyrs is the flame of revolution, the fuel of the intifadah and the price of victory," asserted the statement.

In addition, the Palestinian police reported that 14-year-old Ahmad Qawasemah died from earlier wounds. “Israeli” soldiers had shot him Friday in the West Bank town of al-Khalil.

Meanwhile, military operations against the “Israeli” occupation continued. Intifadah fighters opened fire on  “Israeli” military outposts in al-Khalil (Hebron) and on a convoy of “Israeli” vehicles on the Khan Younis-Gaza road.

Zionist Settlements of Kfar darom, Nevi Dekalin, and Gosh Katif in the Gaza strip were all attacked by Palestinian resistance fighters.

On the Rasheed road east of al-Quds (Al-Quds), an “Israeli” patrol was attacked by molotov bottles.

On the other hand, the “Israeli” Radio said that eight persons, including two “Israeli” policemen were wounded in a premeditated fire in a house in Tel Aviv.

 

U.S. led fact-finding committee starts with a bias for “Israel”…(7)

The fact-finding commission led by former American Senator George Mitchell began investigations concerning the confrontations in occupied Palestine on Monday. Members of the commission met the "“Israeli”" Prime Minister Ehud Barak,and they will later meet the Palestinian Authority President Yasser Arafat.

The general-director of the “Israeli” Foreign Ministry said that unofficial talks  held with the commission members reduced “Israeli” fears about their mission.

The “Israeli” officials said that the “Israelis” and the Palestinians will submit a written handout of their stands about causes of the confrontations, believing that the commission will rely on written documents rather than investigating independently.

The Palestinian Center for Human Rights Monday called on Palestinian civilian and political organizations not to meet the five-member fact-finding committee.

“The Palestinian Center for Human Rights is calling upon all organizations in the Palestinian community to boycott the fact-finding committee that was formed by United States of America,” a statement from the group said.

The PCHR statement said the committee formed by United States “is missing the minimum limit for any neutral and objective investigative committee.” It said it did not believe the committee would be objective in investigating the “crimes and the severe violations that the “Israeli” occupation forces have been carrying since September 29.”

“Forming the committee by the United States means that the aim is to neglect the implementation of the Security Council Resolution 1322 that had clearly said to form quick and objective investigative mechanism,” the statement said.

After the Sharm El Sheikh, Egypt, summit in October, it was decided that both President Clinton and U.N. Secretary-General Kofi Annan would form an inquiry committee to investigate the “Israeli”-Palestinian conflict. The committee that was formed comprises several international figures and is headed by Mitchell.

 

Letter of Palestinian detainee Ahmad al-Dakamisa to his people…(8)

Ahmad Al-Dakamisa, a Palestinian Mujahed (struggler) detained in Jordan, sent a message to the heroic Palestinian people.

He said, "This is a message from a prisoner, written by my heart's ink. In my dark cell I smell the odor of your pure souls. I send kisses to your feet. I hear the gushing of your blood, the sound of stones hurled and your heart beat, like muezzins calling for freedom and swearing that Palestine will be finally for the Arabs."

Al-Dakamissa ended his message saying: "Oh! children of this land free us, for you are Arabs, your voice is Arab, your wounds are that of Arabs."
