Nasrallah: Berlin is in on swap negotiations…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general has confirmed reports of German mediation for a swap involving the three “Israeli” soldiers the party captured in October.
“There is German mediation which has already started and Hizbullah has officially informed German mediators about its demands and vision to conduct a full exchange operation,” Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah told Reuters Saturday.
Nasrallah spoke after “Israeli” President Moshe Katzav told Germany’s Bild am Sonntag newspaper Saturday that Germany was mediating to try to help secure the release of the three “Israeli” soldiers.
“The federal (German) government undertook a new initiative this week and passed on the message that we are ready to make agreements to ensure the welfare of the three soldiers,” Katzav said.
Nasrallah said Hizbullah was waiting to hear “Israel’s” response to demands that could lead to the exchange of the soldiers for the 19 prisoners it wants released.
“The matter is now pending “Israel’s” response to these demands,” he said.
A government spokesman in Berlin noted Chancellor Gerhard Schroeder had promised “Israeli” Prime Minister Ehud Barak that he would do what he could on the matter when the two met during Schroeder’s tour of the Middle East in October.
Schroeder pledged at the time, that Germany ­ which last year mediated the release of five Lebanese fighters detained without trial in “Israel” ­ would move “discreetly and emphatically.”
But he also warned that Berlin’s influence should not be over-estimated.
The spokesman said he had no information on the progress of the mediation effort.
Hizbullah captured the three soldiers in an ambush after “Israel’s” 22-year occupation of the South in May. A few weeks later, Hizbullah captured an “Israeli” intelligence officer after luring him to Lebanon.

Lahoud and Hariri discuss Barak’s move…(2)

Hizbullah: Resistance & Intifada stand behind “Israeli” government collapse

President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Rafik Hariri met on Sunday to discuss the latest local and regional developments, particularly in light of “Israeli” Premier Ehud Barak’s surprise resignation.
The two agreed on “following closely” the latest developments in “Israel”.
Speaking after his meeting with the president, Hariri linked Barak’s move to the situation in the Middle East.
“What happened in Lebanon and especially in the South, where the resistance achieved victory over the occupation, has been clearly reflected by what is happening in the Palestinian lands, and it has put “Israel” and its leaders in a difficult position,” he said.
The prime minister made the comments during an iftar at Koraytem which was attended by several Beirut MPs, ambassadors, army and security forces personnel and orphans.
““Israel’s” critical position is caused by its obstinacy and the Arab steadfastness,” he said. “It has but one logical, reasonable and legal solution for meeting international resolutions, which is to move forward in the peace process, to achieve a just and comprehensive peace.”
Barak unexpectedly submitted his resignation Sunday, paving the way for national elections in 60 days.
Meanwhile, Hizbullah on Sunday claimed partial credit for Barak’s resignation.
The party said its two-decade-long fight to end “Israeli” occupation of the South and a 10-week old Palestinian intifada were the key factors in Barak’s downfall.
“The Islamic Resistance and the intifada in Palestine were able to lay the foundation for the government collapse in “Israel”,” said Nabil Qaouk, Hizbullah’s south Lebanon commander.

 

Collaborators complain from “Israel’s” maltreatment…(3)

After the defunct collaborators militia, so-called Southern Lebanese Army, bound their fate to that of “Israel”, the latter denies the collaborators services and rejects their presence.

About 500 collaborators with their families, who fled Lebanon along with the “Israeli” troops, plead before the “Israeli” Supreme Court to oblige the “Israeli” government to grant them the “Israeli” nationality in addition to the privileges enjoyed by the regular “Israeli” army. They also asked to be compensated for the losses they bore since the “Israeli” withdrawal from Lebanon.

The collaborators complained that the “Israeli” authorities not only ignore and ridicule them but also deal with them as inferiors.

They stated that “Israel” is neither providing them with suitable living conditions nor schools for their children.

It was also mentioned in the plea that the collaborators (with the former “Israeli” occupation in South Lebanon) fear returning to their homes because their lives are endangered, as they said.

However, “Israel” is proposing money for the collaborators for them to leave, which clearly shows the “Israeli” policy, stated the plea.

 

Goksel: "We are not border guards"…(4)

Timor Goksel, the political counselor of the UNIFIL forces in southern Lebanon, asserted that some other intentions stand behind the “Israeli” accusations that the U.N. forces in south Lebanon helped Hizbullah to capture the 3 “Israeli” soldiers in the occupied Lebanese Shibaa Farms.

In an interview with the Manar Channel correspondent in Tyre, Goksel denied that the UNIFIL has played any such role.

He furthermore affirmed that the UNIFIL is not a border guard in response to another accusation saying that the UNIFIL allowed a Syrian worker to infiltrate into the occupied zone a few days ago.

 

“Israeli” army strengthens its presence along border…(5)

Fortifying procedures along the Lebanese border continue in full swing around the clock to build outposts and military strongholds for the “Israeli” occupation forces.

Fortification works are centered especially in the Shibaa Farms and occupied Kfar Shouba Heights.

Along the blue-line drawn by the United Nations hundreds of vehicles are clearly seen at work to wrap up the electronic fence and to support major outposts.

 

Demonstrators call for solidarity in face of “Israeli” atrocities…(6)

Several hundred people marked this year’s anniversary of the UN Declaration of Human Rights at UN House on Sunday by condemning “Israeli” aggression against Palestinian stone-throwers.
Hafez Toutan, a former mayor of Nablus who is in the country for medical treatment, said “these demonstrations of solidarity are priceless in raising our spirits. They are appreciated on the inside more than you
can imagine.”
Toutan said he was heartened to see so much support in Lebanon for the intifada during the two weeks he has been here and hopes governments all over the Arab world will start listening to the “Arab street.”
“We also learned a great lesson from Hizbullah. They showed us what
a small resistance group can do in
the face of the strongest military power in the world. What was taken by force can only be returned by force,” Toutan said.
Minister of State Beshara Merhej, who was there in a personal capacity, said: “We want the Palestinians to feel that while their men are under fire, we are siding with them and in solidarity with them.”
He believed that Lebanon
was “fulfiling its obligation” toward the Palestinian people by having resisted “Israeli” occupation in the South. Merhej also supported strengthening the “Palestinian quest” to curtail “Israeli” aggression in the region and allow peace to prevail.
“We want to send the message that we will never surrender. They made us wait 50 years and could make us wait 100 more, but we’re used to long-term struggle and will overcome,” the minister said.
Merhej dismissed calls on President Emile Lahoud to open a new front in the South. “It’s just the talk of eager young men,” he said.
A children’s march to coincide with the event, planned in refugee camps in Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and Palestine Saturday, was postponed until Monday because of attacks on the West Bank’s Dheisheh camp.
Organizers of Black Ribbon Day, as the day is called, also coordinated picture exhibitions of the intifada around the country to help raise awareness.
A group of Ain al-Hilweh refugees said the many demonstrations and marches support the “cause” not only by raising morale in occupied Palestine but by also giving frustrated Palestinian refugees a “role in the struggle.”
“They’re throwing stones against assault rifles, and here, we fight with our voices,” said 19-year old Faten Khatib. “We wish they would open up the borders so that we can help.”
The atmosphere in Ain al-Hilweh is downcast. People refrain from wearing colorful clothing or make-up and traditionally joyous weddings have turned into small family affairs. Residents are already planning to forego the three-day Eid celebrations following the end of Ramadan.
For many it was particularly important to show those in occupied Palestine that “Beirut is with them” because of their common experience in resisting “Israeli” aggression.
“Beirut means so much for Palestine,” said organizer Rabia Bashour. “It’s the only other capital that the “Israelis” reached that was shelled by them. There’s a blood tie between Lebanon and Palestine because of that.”

 

Fierce confrontations in occupied Palestine, settlers attack openly…(7)

The Palestinian people responded to the calls of 13 Palestinian factions by going on a 3-hour-strike and participating in massive marches in the West Bank on the second day of "Palestinian Rage".

Fatah, Arafat's PLO faction, Hamas, and the Islamic Jihad Movements urged Palestinians to join in marches in solidarity with the seven "intifadah martyrs" who were killed by the “Israeli” occupation Friday, in one of the bloodiest days of the ten week revolt.

Some 50,000 mourners came to Jenin, in the West Bank, for the funeral of five Palestinians, including four policemen, killed by “Israeli” tank fire.

Palestinians expressed outrage at the killing, chanting "Revenge, revenge".

The Ramallah funeral in the West Bank turned into a huge demonstration against the occupier.

"We demand to sweep away Zionism and occupation," shouted mourners at the funeral of 16-year-old Amman Samu al-Mashni, shot dead in al-Quds (Al-Quds).

Witnesses in the West Bank town of al-Khalil (Hebron) said that a 13-year-old Palestinian, Mansour Naji Jaber, was seriously wounded when “Israeli” settlers attacked his home in the town.

The settlers, who probably came from Kiryat Arbaa Settlement, occupied the boy's house after scuffling with his family, said eye witnesses. The Zionist settlers were also reported to have taken over another Palestinian house in al-Khalil belonging to the Jaber family.

Meanwhile, intifadah fighters carried out more attacks against “Israeli” troops in the Gaza strip and West Bank, which are under a severe “Israeli” closure.

Three “Israeli” settlers had been killed and one injured near al-Khalil town and in Ariha on Friday.
