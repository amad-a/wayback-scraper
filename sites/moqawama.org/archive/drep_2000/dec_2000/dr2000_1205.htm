December Tuesday 05-2000

 Thurs2ay 29-2000

 

“Israeli” airforce flies over southern Lebanon…(1)

“ The “Israeli” airforce flew over southern Lebanon Monday and followed the Anti-Lebanon mountain range up to Baalbek, 100 kilometers (60 miles) east of Beirut, Lebanese police said.

“Israeli” warplanes flew over the zone formerly occupied by the Jewish state as well as the Iqlim at-Tuffah region.

The “Israeli” fighters broke the sound barrier above the city of Baalbek, provoking a loud boom.

“Israel” has intensified its military flights over southern Lebanon.

“Israeli” fighter-bombers and helicopters fired missiles at targets in southern Lebanon on November 26.

According to the United Nations, “Israel” violated Lebanon's airspace 123 times between October 8 and November 7.

Lebanon has filed several complaints to the UN against “Israeli” flights over its territory, saying they were a violation of the Security Council Resolution 425 stipulating “Israel’s” complete withdrawal from southern Lebanon.

 

Syria warns “Israel” of a response to threats…(2)

In response to the recent “Israeli” and U.S. threats, Syria asserted via western diplomatic channels that any attack on Syria will be followed by a Syrian attack on “Israel”.

The Syrian response came with movements of the Syrian troops deployed in Lebanon.

 

UN troops may be deployed to Shibaa Farms…(3)

The United Nations and the United States are considering proposals to deploy troops from UNIFIL or peacekeepers based in Syria to the Shibaa Farms in an attempt to defuse tensions in the area.
A senior UN source said the proposal was part of a “multi-channel approach” to pressure Lebanon and Syria to halt Hizbullah attacks in the Shibaa Farms and send Lebanese troops to the border with “Israel”.
The source said that the proposal to deploy peacekeepers in the Farms originated from “Israel” and is being considered by the United Nations.
According to the source, “Israel” suggested deploying UNIFIL troops or soldiers belonging to the UN Disengagement Observer Force (UNDOF), which has monitored the cease-fire on the Golan Heights since May 1974.
The Shibaa Farms lies outside both the UNIFIL and UNDOF areas of operation. UNDOF soldiers, however, are permitted by the “Israelis” to patrol the Farms occasionally.
The United States has “taken an interest” in the idea, the source added.
A Western diplomat located in Beirut dismissed the proposal as unrealistic.
“It’s as if by sending a couple of UN soldiers to the Shibaa Farms, a regional war will be averted,” the diplomat said.
Deploying UNIFIL troops to the Shibaa Farms would be a tacit recognition of Lebanon’s sovereignty over the area, the diplomat added.
The Shibaa Farms were excluded from UNIFIL’s mandate as defined by Resolution 425. The UN has said that as the Shibaa Farms is patrolled by UNDOF, the fate of the area must be decided in peace talks between Syria and “Israel”.
The proposal is not expected to become reality, however. The deployment of UNIFIL and UNDOF forces is subject to authorization by the Lebanese and Syrian governments, respectively. Neither government is likely to sanction the deployment of UN peacekeepers into the Farms.
Nonetheless, the proposal coincides with a concerted effort by the international community to pressure Lebanon and Syria into preventing Hizbullah from attacking “Israeli” forces in the Shibaa Farms.
“Israel” warned Friday that Syrian military posts might be targeted if Hizbullah continues  attacks against “Israeli” forces.

 

Lahoud urges UNIFIL to intensify de-mining work…(4)

A spate of recent land mine accidents spurred President Emile Lahoud on Monday to demand that UNIFIL be informed of the necessity of intensifying mine clearance in the South.
Lahoud also said he considered the land-mine victims as casualties of war, describing their condition as indicative of the continuing “Israeli” occupation and its negative effect on the life of southerners.
Meanwhile, the minister of energy and water urged Prime Minister Rafik Hariri to request logistical support from Europe in clearing mines.
“The issue of de-mining has to become the issue of the international community and assistance encouraged,” Mohammed Abdel-Hamid Beydoun said, commenting on the six people wounded in mine-related incidents in the last three days.
“This problem must not expand. We should not see innocent victims fall as a result of the incapacity of Lebanese institutions or establishments to face the problem,” Beydoun said.
He suggested Lebanon submit a file to the United Nations demanding that “Israel” be held accountable for laying the mines and seeking compensation.
“Lebanon must call for a conference similar to the donors’ conference to mobilize the international community on the issue of mines,” he said.
The issue was also raised by Tyre MP Abdullah Qasir and Marjayoun-Hasbaya MP Qassem Hashem, who demanded immediate help for the completion of the de-mining process.
Qasir demanded that the government consider the issue one of its priorities and pressed for more intensive awareness campaigns. He also asked the government to contact friendly states to find a quick solution to the problem of mines.

 

Lahoud: "Intifadah  a normal consequence"…(5)

President of Lebanon Emile Lahoud said, "The Intifadah (uprising) of the Palestinian people is a normal reaction to the stake on individual solutions set up by Oslo agreements, which are in contradiction to the joint Arab efforts recquired to realize a just, comprehensive, and lasting peace."

Lahoud told a delegation of Fatah Intifadah Movement that the Palestinian uprising reflects a strong popular will to confront the “Israeli” obstinacy.

Lahoud maintained that the Lebanese liberation experience of the south is the best illustration that rights can only be regained through resistance.

 

Jewish settlers attack injures thirty Palestinians…(6)

 The Ibrahimi Sanctuary massacre was about to be repeated Sunday night in the village of Husan, near the West Bank town of Beit Lahm, when armed Jewish settlers opened fire at  Palestinians who were going into a mosque to pray.

A report by Palestinian news agency Wafa quoted Palestinian eyewitnesses saying 30 residents were shot and injured, while they were going into the mosque. Ten were seriously hurt, the witnesses said.

The witnesses said a group of armed Jewish settlers in a GMC van parked near the main mosque of the village. The armed settlers-wearing civilian clothes and under the “Israeli” army cover - opened fire at the Palestinians and wounded 30 people.

Residents of the village said the “Israeli” army isolated their village from other West Bank territories and that the “Israeli” soldiers delayed medical workers and ambulances for a half-hour.

Witnesses also said that the GMC van seen at the shooting was the main van that ran over a 12-year-old boy several days ago. The boy was killed immediately and the van fled.

Meanwhile, intifadah fighters carried out more attacks against “Israeli” military vehicles and Jewish settlers. The latest operation took place Monday morning when an “Israeli” patrol was attacked near West-Bank town of Jenin. Palestinian Mujahedeen also launched attacks against “Israeli” positions near Rachel's Tomb near Beit Lahm.

Fire was exchanged between Palestinian gunmen in al-Jelmi village of “Israelis” in the settlement of Kadim and Ghanim close to the town of Jennine.

 

The Ocupation tries to impose its control on Rafah crossing point…(7)

The “Israeli” army expressed its intention to reopen Monday Rafah crossing point connecting Egypt with the Gaza strip for several hours daily after more than 3 weeks of its closure, a source in the Palestinian Authority said.

This decision was broken during a meeting held Sunday on the crossing point, the source stated.

The Palestinian side rejected conditions set by the “Israelis” saying it was incongruent with the agreement concerning crossing points.

The Palestinian source made clear that “Israel” wants the Palestinian workers to be checked in addition to decreasing their number and this was what the Palestinian side didn't agree upon. The source pointed that the “Israeli” side reported that the crossing point will be reopened without reaching an agreement. Still the crossing point wasn't opened by midday.

 

A Hamas military leader martyred in a suspected assassination…(8)

An active military leader of Hamas' armed branch, Izzeddine al-Qassam Brigade, was martyred in Gaza strip.

The martyr Awad al-Salami Abu Mujahed, 32-years-old, had been spent 3 years in the prisons of the Palestinian authority.

Palestinian sources said the body of Salami was found at the rear of cement factory in the region of al-Mintar (Karni), without giving additional information.

Salami is married and has five children. He is one of Izzeddine al-Qassan's prominent leaders and was chased for long by the “Israeli” occupation which accused him of being responsible for the assassination of an “Israeli” secret colonel in 1993.

 

Band countries to hold urgent meeting in Cairo…(9)

The Arab band countries (Egypt-Jordan-Syria and Lebanon) representatives will hold an urgent meeting Tuesday in the Arab league headquarters to consolidate a decisive Arab stand with respect to the “Israeli” military escalation against the Palestinian people, a source in the Arab League said.

"The meeting will be considered an implementation of what was agreed on in yesterday's meeting of the Arab league follow-up commission," the league's spokesman, Talaat Hamed, said.

"The meeting is included in the framework of the political plan recommended by the commission to support the Palestinian people's Intifadah. It is as well a preliminary step to the next meeting for the follow-up commission held by the 9 countries' foreign ministers in Damascus next Sunday," said Hamed.

The follow-up commission of the Arab league dedicated to support the Palestinians held an urgent representatives meeting presided by the Egyptian Prime Minister Amro Moussa yesterday.

Moussa said, "Economic resolutions in addition to an international mobilization plan were agreed upon to support the intifadah," without giving additional details.
