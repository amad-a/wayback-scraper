“Israel” trains commandos for kidnapping Lebanese: newspaper report…(1)

“ “Israel” is carrying out intensive training of commandos to carry out kidnappings in Lebanon using helicopters, the al-Kifah al-Arabi daily said Thursday, quoting security sources.

"“Israeli” armed forces have carried out intensive training for operations they planned to carry out in Lebanon, including attempted kidnappings of leading Lebanese figures, involving helicopters," according to the daily.

"The fly-overs of “Israeli” helicopers and planes which have been happening recently are part of the preparations for such operations," the paper continued.

According to the United Nations, “Israeli” planes violated Lebanese air space 123 times during the period from October 8 to November 7.

 

Call goes out for help to remove mines…(2)

Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud said Thursday that intensive contacts were underway with friendly states, specialized agencies, and the UN to help rid Lebanon of over 130,000 mines planted in the South by “Israel” prior to its withdrawal last May.
Speaking to reporters, Hammoud said the contacts, undertaken via ambassadors accredited in Beirut or Lebanese embassies abroad, focused on providing assistance to detonate the mines. He added that assistance could be provided in the form of equipment, training, and experts.
“These mines are intolerable, in view of the casualties that they have caused,” he said.
Hammoud also suggested that the Cabinet would soon be involved, indicating that it would consider a Defense Ministry plan to de-mine. During Wednesday’s Cabinet session, Energy and Water Resources Minister Mohammed Abdel-Hamid Beydoun called for a “comprehensive plan” to remove the mines.

 

Ain al-Hilweh remembers first Palestinian intifada …(3)

Some 200 Palestinians marked the 13th anniversary of the first intifada at Ain al-Hilweh refugee camp in Sidon on Friday.
Demonstrators showed their solidarity with the Palestinian people by carrying flags and maps, and waving banners urging Palestinians to unite and “destroy “Israel”.”
Four Palestinian children spent 90 minutes constructing a cardboard mock up of the Jewish settlement of Gilo, complete with “Israeli” flags.
A demonstrator then doused the structure with kerosene and set it ablaze, destroying it in less than 30 seconds.
Protesters also submitted a letter to United Nations Secretary-General Kofi Annan, urging the Palestinian Authority to stop “meeting with the “Israeli” government,” and to close all “secret channels of contact.”
The letter was also addressed to the head of the Arab League, Esmat Abdel-Megid, and Palestinian President Yasser Arafat.
It asked the two leaders to support the Palestinian people financially, and demanded that Arab countries sever all political and economic ties with the Jewish state.
In Hermel, about 1,000 people demonstrated in support of the ongoing intifada, the Lebanese resistance to “Israel”, and Syria’s presence in Lebanon.
The mayors of Hermel, Ibrahim Shahin, and al-Qaa, Eid Matar, joined the crowd, which expressed its support for the Palestinian people and the strategy of resistance in order to liberate occupied land.

 

More attacks against “Israeli” occupation…(4)

In the West Bank, a Palestinian gunman shot at “Israelis” near the Jewish settlement of Shavei Shomron, near Nablus. Two were seriously wounded and one lightly.

The injured were rushed to Tal Ashomer hospital, near Tel Aviv.

The “Israeli” army said the Palestinian was driving in a car when he shot at the “Israelis”.

Moreover, intifadah fighters opened fire at the “Israeli” settlement of Nevi Decalim southern of Gaza strip. They also shot at “Israeli” soldiers at the Mintar (Karni) crossing point using machine guns.

The “Israeli” police said that a Palestinian opened fire on an “Israeli” vehicle, north of al-Quds (Al-Quds), Thursday morning.

On the other hand, a spokeswoman for the “Israeli” police said the "return of calm" may contribute to the reconsideration on Friday of measures denying Palestinians from the West Bank and Gaza strip access to the holy Aqsa Mosque in al-Quds for Friday prayers.

Meanwhile, thirteen Palestinian organizations issued a statement announcing two days of Palestinian rage starting from Friday, on the occasion of the anniversary of the first intifadah in 1987.

 

Increase of “Israeli” troops for settlements protection…(5)

Following the “Israeli” army failure to protect the many Jewish settlements, implanted in the West Bank and Gaza strip, from the attacks of Palestinian intifadah fighters, the occupation army decided to increase the number of soldiers guarding these settlements.

The “Israeli” Radio reported that these measurement are to be carried out after the army reevaluated the security situation of these settlements which are in general probable targets of Palestinians.

As a result, the number of soldiers deployed in these settlements will be multiplied by three, and the military presence will be beefed up by the introduction of military units expert in fighting.

“Israeli” daily  Ha`aretz said that about five hundred of the soldiers now guarding 150 settlements posses little fighting experience.However, the army decided to soon deploy 1300 soldiers, most of which have combat experience.

 

Hamas military leader fled prison…(6)

While news spread out about the escape of Mohammed Dayf, the leader of Izzeddine al-Qassam Brigade, Hamas' armed branch, from the Palestinian Authority prison, “Israeli” authorities warned that it will consider the Palestinian leadership responsible for consequences of Dayf's actions.

The Palestinian newspaper Al-Ayyam reported that Hamas Movement will start its communications with Palestinian security agencies to know the fate of Izzeddine al-Qassam's leader, especially that his life is endangered since he is wanted by the “Israeli” authorities.

Earlier, Hamas sources revealed that Mohammed Dayf was able to flee five days ago a prison of the Palestinian preventive security in Gaza, where he was in detention for more than seven months.

 

'Collaborator' gets death sentence in Palestinian court…(7)

A Palestinian court in Nablus sentenced a Palestinian man to death yesterday for helping “Israel’s” secret service assassinate a military commander of Hamas, the militant Islamic group.

The judge found Allan Bani Oudeh guilty of spying for “Israel” and taking part in the murder on 23 November of his distant relative, Ibrahim Bani Oudeh, a local leader of the armed wing of Hamas.

Palestinians attending the open hearing, most of whom appeared to be Hamas members, clapped and shouted "long live justice" when the sentence was announced.

The Hamas leader was killed when his booby-trapped car exploded. Allan Bani Oudeh then fled to “Israel” but was captured by Palestinian security forces when he returned to the West Bank.

The court ruled that Bani Oudeh had worked for “Israel” as a collaborator. He said the “Israelis” had blackmailed him into helping, but he denied planting explosives in the car.

It was the first time a Palestinian court has sentenced a convicted collaborator to death.

Before, collaborators have been summarily executed by Palestinian activists without trial. The “Israeli” authorities declined to comment on the verdict and death sentence.

There are no appeals against state security court verdicts but the Palestinian President, Yasser Arafat, must approve an execution before it is carried out.

Hamas opposes Palestinian negotiations with “Israel” and has killed numerous “Israelis” in suicide bombings. The military wing of Hamas, the Izz el-Deen al-Qassam Brigades, claimed responsibility for the car bombing on 22 November that killed two “Israelis” and wounded 60 others in the northern “Israeli” town of Hadera.
