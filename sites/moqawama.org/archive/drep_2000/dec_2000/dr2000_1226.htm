December Tuesday 26-2000

 Thurs2ay 29-2000

 

Serious efforts are made to release detainees…(1)

Hizbullah's south Lebanon official, Sheikh Nabil Qawook, asserted that the “Israeli” leadership is seeking the help of all international mediations for the release of the 3 “Israeli” soldiers and one colonel captured by Hizbullah. Qawook considered this situation  another victory for the Resistance.

"The equation is now for the Resistance's advantage and serious efforts are being made to complete the victory and bring back those detained (in “Israeli” prisons) to their families and countries," said Qawook during an iftar to honor the liberated detainees.

 

Two “Israelis” wounded in intifadah operations…(2)

An “Israeli” truck driver was wounded when his truck was targeted by gunfire near the Jewish Settlement of Itamar in the West Bank. The driver was transported to the hospital. “Israeli” forces combed the area following the attack.

“Israeli” sources said that an “Israeli” military outpost was also attacked by gunfire coming from Salem village.

Near the Lebanese-Palestinian border, an “Israeli” general was critically wounded and 3 other soldiers were also injured due to the explosion of a hand grenade. The “Israeli” army described the incident as accidental.

The explosion occurred inside a tank positioned near Meskaf Am settlement, according to a military source which said that the “Israeli” northern commandership is investigating in the incident.

Sunday evening, armed confrontations erupted between “Israeli” soldiers and Palestinian gunmen in the Gaza strip and West Bank.

A source in the “Israeli” occupation army said that a Jewish woman settler was injured by stones hurled at her car near the West Bank town of Beit Lahm.

In the Gaza Strip, intifadah fighters exploded a roadside bomb when an army-protected settler vehicle passed near Netzarim settlement. Following the explosion, fierce gunfire fighting broke out between “Israelis” and Palestinians.

The “Israeli” forces in retaliation bombed using rockets Palestinian  houses near the settlement.

Moreover, “Israeli” bulldozers uprooted large fields of trees to pave a new road leading to the Gosh Katif Settlement in Gaza Strip.

On the other hand, “Israeli” troops carried out a campaign in which fourteen young Palestinian men from the West Bank village of Northern Aseerah near Nablus were arrested. Most of those arrested are university students whom the “Israelis” accused of belonging to Hamas movement.

Helicopter gunships and trucks carrying a big number of soldiers participated in the military operation. The village residents asserted that “Israeli” security agents had exact information on the location of those to be arrested, which means that collaborators played a message role in this operation.

 

Ramadan to end on quiet note in Middle East amid Palestinian uprising…(3)

 Muslims across the Middle East will mark the end of the holy month of Ramadan this week with Eid al-Fitr, but celebrations will be reduced to a minimum as “Israelis” keep up their killing campaign against Palestinians in the occupied territories. Muslims will likely celebrate Fitr on Tuesday or Wednesday, with Muslim countries' religious authorities meeting Monday night to decide when the holy month of Ramadan has ended according to the Islamic lunar calendar. No celebrations have been scheduled for Fitr in the Palestinian territories, where more than 350 Palestinians have been killed since the uprising erupted in late September.   In the West Bank and Gaza Strip, 13 Palestinian organisations, including the Islamist movement Hamas and Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat's Fatah, recently called for Fitr to be made a day of mutual aid and solidarity with the families of intifada victims.   And in Jordan, where more than half of the five million population is of Palestinian origin, King Abdullah II announced he will not meet and greet his subjects for the annual Muslim feast of Fitr. The Muslim Brotherhood also asked the celebrations be kept to a minimum "out of respect for the victims of the intifada."  Fitr will fall this year at roughly the same time as Christmas, and Arafat marked the occasion with a trip to Jesus Christ's traditional birthplace of Bethlehem, in the Palestinian leader's first trip to the West Bank since the intifada broke out. In the heart of Beirut, a big Christmas tree stands within a few yards only of an artificial fountain decorated with a moon crescent, the symbol of Islam. Prime Minister Rafik Hariri said Friday that "the fact that Muslim and Christian religious celebrations coincided reaffirmed the Lebanese people's desire to consolidate coexistence between the various communities in the country."   Some Muslims have even adopted symbols of Christian folklore. The Lebanese resistance movement Hizbollah organized an event on Friday with Christmas presents distributed to children by Santa Claus.   Christmas trees decorate many Muslim homes in Lebanon. The country's Christian community also participates in celebrations to mark the end of Ramadan.   In Iran, the world's largest Shiite Muslim country, the population is expected to celebrate the end of Ramadan on Wednesday. The supreme guide, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, will lead a collective prayer for the occasion.   In Egypt, preparations have been under way for two weeks. The shops are packed with a full array of goods and the public gardens, which are traditionally invaded by thousands of visitors for Fitr, have been cleaned up.   The transport infrastructures are on alert to face the expected flow of Egyptian expatriates coming back for the celebrations.   In Iraq, the population is preparing for another Fitr under UN embargo. But Baghdad shops are better stocked this year, and Iraqis can find more traditional products for their celebrations, although prices remain too high for most of them.   In the Gulf's oil kingdoms, rulers usually mark Fitr by issuing amnesties for many prisoners.
