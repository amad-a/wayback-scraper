"Deal" over Palestinian refugees concerns Lebanon: Iran…(1)

“Iran's ambassador to Lebanon Mohamed Ali Sobhani said a "specific deal" over Palestinian refugees is being finalized between “Israeli” and Palestinian negotiators. The Iranian envoy who held talks with President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Rafik Hariri said the deal affects the Palestinians and their "neighbors especially Lebanon". Sobhani reiterated Tehran's rejection of any resettlement of Palestinians outside their home towns and villages, from which they were expelled when “Israel” was created in 1948. Lebanon earlier warned “Israel” and the Palestinian Authority of the "harm" from any separate deal leading to the permanent settlement of Palestinian refugees abroad. "This is a basic Lebanese national issue on which we cannot make any compromise," said Information Minister Ghazi Aridi. "We refuse any implantation and we assert the right to return home for our Palestinian brothers. From there, we warn against any such agreements or separate deals, which have done harm before and that would undoubtedly do even more harm if (such deals) are brokered in the next phase," he said after a cabinet meeting. Lebanon hosts 367,000 Palestinians, who were expelled from their homes when “Israel” was created in 1948, and refuses to grant them citizenship. Aridi said "the unity of the Lebanese is necessary, more than ever before, to confront the continuous attempts to force ... (Lebanon) to accept what it has refused in the past and what it will continue to refuse, mainly through separate deals which have already weakened the Arab position and have brought nothing to those who made them." The cabinet session came immediately after the meeting, for the first time in seven years, of Lebanon's Higher Defense Council, the country's top political body. The meetings were mainly meant to send stern messages to the Palestinian Authority, currently engaged in peace talks in Washington with “Israel”, that it will stand against any compromises on Palestinian refugees' right of return. Aridi said the government "looked into" matters discussed by the Higher Defense Council, whose decisions were not disclosed. The council, created in 1984, grouped Lahoud, Prime Minister Rafik Hariri and the ministers of defense, interior, foreign affairs, finance and economy, as well as the army commander, the head of security agencies and the general prosecutor. In its morning session, it debated ways to foil “Israel’s” feared bid to trade sovereignty over occupied East Al-Quds's holy sites for the right of return of 3.7 million Palestinian refugees. “Israeli” Absorption Minister Yuli Tamir suggested Tuesday that “Israel” would be prepared to trade sovereignty over the al-Aqsa mosque compound in occupied east Al-Quds for concessions on the refugee issue. "What is vital for us is to obtain the most important thing, the renunciation by the Palestinians of the right of return of refugees to territory under “Israeli” sovereignty," Tamir said. The Palestinian Authority and a number of Palestinian opposition groups reiterated this week their continued rejection of any permanent settlement of Palestinian refugees in their respective asylum countries. On Monday, Lahoud announced an international campaign against the permanent settlement of Palestinian refugees in the country. The same day, Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud summoned each of the ambassadors accredited in Lebanon to relay Beirut's position on the matter. The council also examined an incident Monday in which the “Israeli” military fired on Lebanese territory from Shibaa Farms, a mountainous Lebanese region still under “Israeli” occupation. The Shibaa Farms have remained a main point of contention since the “Israeli” troop pullout from southern Lebanon in May. 

‘30 countries’ back no-settlement policy…(2)

Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud said Thursday that 30 Arab, Western, and African states have so far voiced support for Lebanon’s refusal to settle Palestinian refugees.
A statement from President Emile Lahoud’s office said Speaker Nabih Berri praised Lahoud’s recent rejection of any prospects of settlement and the necessity of guaranteeing the right of return for Palestinians.
Berri informed Lahoud of his position during talks Thursday with the president at Baabda Palace, during which both men agreed on the necessity of rejecting any solution for Palestinian refugees that may be reached at Lebanon’s expense. The issue also topped discussions between Lahoud and Iran’s ambassador, Mohammad Ali Sobhani.
“Certain signals show that a deal is being cooked up for Palestinian refugees,” Sobhani said. “This issue doesn’t only concern the Palestinians. It concerns several neighboring states, such as Lebanon.”
Foreign Ministry sources reiterated that the Palestinians’ right of return was absolute. The sources said Palestinians must be granted both the right to compensation and the right of return, unlike what is stipulated in the UN General Assembly’s Resolution 194, which entitles the refugees to only one of the two. “The Palestinians’ right to compensation must not be subjected to their right of return and must not cancel or abolish this right in any way,” one ministry source said.
The source said the recent diplomatic campaign launched by Lahoud stemmed from the president’s concern that solutions to the refugees issue may come at Lebanon’s expense.
“The status of a refugee is a temporary one which urges a just political solution by the international community,” the source said. “The right of return must be at the core of this solution.”
They said that no country or “group of states” must be forced to bear the responsibility. Only the country that caused this exodus must be made to bear this responsibility, along with the international community.
They said Lahoud launched his recent diplomatic campaign and convened the Higher Defense Council within 72 hours after receiving information that President Clinton would attempt to reach the framework for a solution to the refugees before the end of his term.
The campaign comes in the wake of information that one of the current American proposals to end the Palestinian-”Israeli” conflict calls for “Israel’s” relinquishing of parts of East Al-Quds to Palestinian sovereignty, provided that Palestinian refugees give up their right of return to their homes.

 

Arrest warrants issued for four of 54 ex-detainees…(3)

Military investigating magistrates issued arrest warrants on Thursday against four men handed over by the Syrian authorities last week as part of a group of 54 detainees.
Two warrants were issued against Hassan Mehdi and Ali Zeidan, who are both in custody, on charges of setting up gangs to execute terrorist acts, threatening the government’s security, and spying for “Israel”.
Chief Military Investigating Magistrate Riad Talih requested that an unidentified former “Israeli”-allied militiaman, who was one of those handed over by Syria, be sentenced to life in prison with hard labor. Talih accused the man of contacting the enemy, entering its territory, and executing terrorist acts.
Investigating Magistrate Abdullah Hajj requested that Hikmat Beydoun be sentenced to death for spying for “Israel” and providing it with information.
All suspects have been referred to the Military Tribunal.
l The Military Tribunal handed down sentences to 24 people, mostly former militiamen, late Wednesday after convicting them of collaborating with “Israel”.
The prison terms ranged from three weeks to 15 years.  The defendants were also fined between LL400,000 and LL5 million. The court acquitted two defendants of collaboration charges and dropped charges against five others. Three of the 24 defendants were sentenced in absentia to 15 years in jail for collaborating with “Israel” and joining the militia.

Two Palestinian martyrs, four “Israelis” wounded in an intifadah attack…(4)

Intifadah strugglers went on with their operations against the “Israeli” occupation.

Thursday morning, a Palestinian motorist ran his car into four “Israeli” soldiers at Al-Ram checkpoint in the West Bank, north of al-Quds (Al-Quds).

The four soldiers were wounded, two critically.

“Israeli” troops shot dead a Palestinian, Ahmad Meraash, at al-Mintar (Karni) crossing point. Meraash was shot in his chest.

Another Palestinian, Rashed Barhoum, died today of wounds sustained in Wednesday's Rafah clashes, in the Gaza Strip, mounting the number of Palestinian martyrs to five during two days.

Moreover, confrontations erupted Thursday between Palestinians and “Israeli” soldiers in Khan Younis, in the Gaza Strip.

 

“Israeli” army admits plans to assassinate Palestinian leaders…(5)

The “Israeli” Radio reported a senior “Israeli” army General to have said that the “Israeli” forces track Palestinians, accused of leading intifadah field activities, in the aim of eliminating them. The general labeled this new tactic as successful, saying that it aborts Palestinian operations and reduces the level of fighting. He also revealed that the assassination of intifadah leaders are mainly executed by sniper fire.

On the other hand, “Israeli” daily Yediot Ahronot reported that the first Lieutenant Ghal Levi (23 years) is the one that led the group of “Israeli” soldiers who killed the son of Hizbullah Secretary-General, Hadi Nasrallah and his two colleagues in the Resistance, Haytham Mughniyyeh and Ali Kawtharani, during fighting in formerly occupied south Lebanon, in 1997.

The newspaper said that Levi died in a car accident in Boghotta with a friend of his. Levi had  received a military medallion for killing Hadi Nasrallah and his friends in south Lebanon.

 

Iran to receive more wounded Palestinians…(6)

The Islamic Republic of Iran will receive a new group of Palestinians wounded by “Israeli” bullets in occupied Palestine.

Ramadan Sharif, the spokesman of an Iranian officials body responsible for collecting donations and receiving the injured, said 12 to 14 wounded Palestinians will reach Tehran via Jordan.

Moreover, Sharif said Iran will send more humanitarian aids, food and clothes, to the Palestinians.

 

“Israel” dumps toxic waste in Golan…(7)

“Israel” has dumped toxic waste in the occupied Golan Heights. Syrian Golan students said during a protest in Damascus they had sent a message to U.N. Secretary-General Kofi Annan accusing “Israel” of dumping toxic waste inside towns on the Heights and distributing poisonous paint to Syrian citizens in the area. Medhat Saleh, who said he represented Golan citizens, said barrels of paint, which included poisonous substances that could cause cancer and gene deficiencies, had been discovered near five Syrian villages there. Asked about the accusations, the “Israeli” Environment Ministry said in a statement: "“Israel” does not transfer any type of waste to the Golan Heights." But it said that about a month ago, a Golan settler, in a private business deal, stored "a certain quantity of paint, whose sell-by date had expired, in a warehouse in the Druze Arab village of Majdal Shams". The students, studying at Syrian universities, staged a sit-in in Damascus in front of the offices of the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC) and the United Nations. “Israel” occupied the Golan Heights from Syria in the 1967 Middle East war. Peace talks with Syria faltered a year ago over the future of the strategic plateau, which Damascus wants returned in its entirety.
