Hamas, Hezbollah urge escalation of Intifada(1)

The Lebanese Hezbollah and Palestinian Hamas Islamic groups called Friday for an escalation of the Intifada, or Palestinian uprising.

Hezbollah Deputy Secretary General Sheikh Naim Kassem  blasted "Barak's proposal which is a declaration of defeat before the Intifada."

"It proves the ""israeli"" view which is based on postponing all solutions which would lead immediately to an explosion," he said.

Kassem called on the "Arab and Islamic peoples never to forget that the al-Aqsa Intifada will continue."

He also urged "a progressive development of the resistance because ""Israeli"" only understands the language of weapons and is only defeated when its nationals fall under the direct strikes of our fighters."

"Although it is still in its early stages, the Intifada has made great achievements until now. It has succeeded in destabilizing the ""israeli"" regime and Barak is today mourning his regime and is getting ready for early elections."

"No ""israeli"" official can stand before the Palestinian people...and no regime in the""israeli"" entity will ever enjoy stability after today because our people will continue their Jihad and struggle," he said.

"The Intifada should be escalated, using all available means. It is normal for the Intifada to be developed," Khaled Meshaal, head of the Hamas political bureau, told thousands at a Hezbollah rally in Beirut's southern suburbs.

"The popular resistance and the armed resistance are heading together toward victory because the objective of our people is to expell the occupier and regain Al Quds, sacred sites and every inch of Palestinian land," he said.

Meshaal said "our people in Palestine who have offered more than 270 martyrs, 10,000 wounded and 6,000 prisoners will continue the Intifada, are working to escalate it and will never back down."

"The Intifada is a message for abolishing the choice of negotiations and strengthening the choice of Jihad (holy war) and resistance," said the official from Hamas which is violently opposed to peace talks with ""Israeli"".

Meshaal rejected a recent proposal by ""israeli"" Prime Minister Ehud Barak -- trailing opposition rivals in opinion polls ahead of elections and fighting off challenges in his own party -- for an interim peace accord with the Palestinians.

"The projects that Barak is proposing are mere electoral outbidding tactics," he said.

 ""Israeli"" threatens Syrians with air bombardment

Armed forces warn that further Hizbollah attacks on ""israeli"" soldiers or civilians will lead to retaliation against troops in Lebanon(2)

""Israeli"" is threatening Syria with air strikes against its military bases in Lebanon if there are further border attacks on ""Israeli"" soldiers or civilians by Hizbollah guerrillas.

Such warnings were common during ""Israel's"" occupation of south Lebanon but they have resumed following fresh Hizbollah attacks. They raise anew the alarming spectre of a wider Middle East conflict.

The threat against Syria's 20,000 troops in Lebanon was outlined yesterday by a senior ""israeli"" security source. He said Syria possessed the power to rein in Hizbollah, but instead Bashar al-Assad, its president, encouraged it. The Syrians did not understand the consequences of their "dangerous policies", said the source.

Hizbollah recently renewed attacks on ""Israeli"", capturing four soldiers in the last two months and killing one with a roadside bomb. It is acting on the pretext that ""Israeli""is still occupying the Shebaa Farms, a small pocket of what it claims was occupied by ""Israeli"" as part of the Golan Heights. The United Nations has ruled it to be on the ""israeli"" side of the "blue line" that marks out the ""israeli""-occupied Golan from Lebanon.

""Israeli" 's" annoyance with Syria's new young president has been heightened by disappointment that he has not proved any more likely to succumb to ""israeli"" demands than his father, Hafez al-Assad, who died in June. The bizarre, but widely circulated notion that "Dr Bashar's" British training as an ophthalmologist and his interest in computers would soften his stance on Syrian interests – making him, in western eyes, a relative "moderate" – has predictably proved to be no more than diplomatic guff.

The ""israeli"" military is alarmed by Bashar al-Assad's eagerness to forge closer relations with Iraq in contrast to his father, whose hostility towards Saddam Hussein reached a peak in 1991 when he supported Operation Desert Storm. Last month Izzat Ibrahim, a member of the Revolutionary Command Council, was the most senior Iraqi official for two decades to visit Damascus. Reports abound that Iraq is piping oil to Syria in defiance of sanctions.

Bashar al-Assad "was expected to be more moderate due to his western education," said the "israeli" security source, who requested anonymity. "But he is behaving just the opposite way. He is very extreme. He has turned very hard in the direction of the Iraqis." If "Israeli" gets embroiled in a conflict with Damascus, the source warned, then Iraq would be "delighted" to join in.

This week, "Israeli" bombarded a Lebanese town after a Hizbollah bomb killed one of its soldiers. But Ehud Barak, the "israeli" prime minister, has held back from retaliation over the captured men – three soldiers and a reserve colonel – because he did not want to open another front while battling the Palestinian intifada.

"israeli" troops shot dead two more Palestinians yesterday – one, a 12-year-old boy – after unrest erupted following the first Friday prayers of Ramadan.

Although the "israeli" Army principally blame the Syrian president for their embarrassing setbacks at the hands of Hizbollah, it is also pointing a finger at the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon. It claims that the guerrillas who seized the three soldiers were disguised as UN peacekeepers, using equipment acquired from an Indian battalion. A UN spokesman has called the report incorrect. The story does, however, smack of a larger "israeli" publicity campaign – an effort to undermine Palestinian efforts to persuade the United Nations to send an observer force to the occupied territories.

 

Lebanese shepherd looses foot after stepping on landmine(3)

An 18-year-old Lebanese shepherd had to have his foot amputated Friday after he stepped on a landmine in southern Lebanon, police said.

Abbas Ali Fakih was injured when the mine exploded near the town of Bent Jbeil in the former "israeli"-occupied border zone in southern Lebanon.

Nine civilians have been killed and 55 others injured by mines since the May 24 "israeli" withdrawal from southern Lebanon. A dozen Lebanese troops were also injured during mine clearing operations.

The UN estimates that there are at least 130,000 "israeli" mines and unexploded shells remaining in southern Lebanon.

On November 14, the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon announced that its mine clearing units had removed 1,520 explosive devices.

 

EU envoy repeats call for army to head for border
Angry Hariri rules out deployment, demands clarification(4)

European Union Middle East envoy Miguel Angel Moratinos  urged the government on Friday to deploy the Lebanese Army on the "israeli" border to avoid a security setback.
“The UN forces are doing a great job and enjoy the support of the European Union. But we also wish that the Lebanese Army be deployed as soon as possible on all Lebanese territory,” Moratinos said following talks with Prime Minister Rafik Hariri.
Moratinos, who arrived in Lebanon from Syria, said he received assurances from the premier that a “stable situation will be maintained in the region.”
But an informed source said that Hariri was “displeased” with Moratinos’ claim that he had received assurances from the premier.
The source said Hariri told Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud to “indirectly” ask Moratinos about his statement.
The source said Moratinos agreed to clarify his comments to reporters after Hammoud asked him to do so during their meeting. Moratinos then read a statement to reporters in which he said “there will not be peace and stability in the region if the situation in the South remains fragile, and unless there is no comprehensive peace.”
“The situation is still very tense … But it is the time for peace,” Moratinos said. “We need to exert serious work that will lead to the resumption of the peace talks on all channels.”
He told reporters that his talks with Hariri touched on the Lebanese Army’s deployment.
Moratinos dismissed suggestions that UNIFIL would be reduced to 3,000 men and women when the UN Security Council meets in January to renew the peacekeeping force’s mandate.
A statement issued from Hariri’s office after the talks said the situation in the South was still “fragile because of "Israeli"’s continued occupation of the Shebaa Farms and its daily provocations.
“The only guarantee for stability in south Lebanon is through reaching a peace agreement involving Lebanon, Syria and "Israeli", and not through steps that comfort "Israeli" while the region remains in constant tension,” the statement said.
Moratinos expressed the EU’s support for the government’s economic drive, as well as the EU’s desire to achieve progress toward the achievement of a partnership deal.
Moratinos said he told President Emile Lahoud that “we must take advantage of the current opportunity we have for peace from now and until May, to reach a final agreement on all levels, including the Palestinian, Syrian and Lebanese.”
Lahoud told Moratinos that the only way for just and comprehensive peace and stability in the Middle East was through "Israeli"’s withdrawal from all occupied Arab territory and its agreeing to the legitimate rights of Palestinians.
The president reiterated “Lebanon’s views regarding the situation in the South from all its aspects,” a statement from the Presidential Palace said
Lahoud told Moratinos it was necessary for the EU to increase the assistance it had decided to give Lebanon for reconstruction projects in the South and other regions.
Moratinos said Lahoud also asked for EU support for Lebanon’s economic drive and the need to implement all bilateral deals signed between both sides.
Sources close to the meeting said that Lahoud “understood” from his conversation with Moratinos that no conditions would be placed on Lebanon in exchange for EU financial assistance.
Speaker Nabih Berri, who also met with Moratinos, sought a larger and firmer European role in the peace process.
“The European Union pledges to exert the efforts to ease the situation. It’s time for the EU to fulfill its responsibilities,” Moratinos said after his talks with
the speaker. ­

 

Bail for old or sick "israeli" agents(5)

Elderly or sick collaborators could be released from prison on bail pending their court hearings, Chief Military Prosecutor Nasri Lahoud said Friday.
 Lahoud said that the military tribunal has decided to study the medical and criminal files of each collaborator currently under arrest.
“If it turns out that his medical condition is very critical and his crime is minor, we will release him on bail,” Lahoud said, “provided he appears in court when we summon him.”
He said a “minor crime” would be someone who had entered enemy territory ­ in this case, "Israeli".
“If, for example, they arrest a 68 year-old who needs angioplasty, it is better to release him than keep him in jail while he suffers,” Lahoud explained.
But he also made it clear that the court would not release those accused of major crimes, regardless of their medical condition.
The military prosecution usually keeps a medical file of each defendant under arrest, and would only grant bail after consulting a doctor.
Former the collaborators who were previously sentenced could be released if their medical conditions are considered critical ­ provided their sentences do not exceed six months.

Television news is secret weapon of the intifada(6)

To the casual visitor, Manar Television is a run-of-the-mill professional television station, its newsroom humming with reporters, its studios hushed for hourly news reports, its state-of-the-art editing suites flanked by batteries of satellite screens, the tape library computercatalogued on dozens of shelves.

But you need only read the posters on the newsroom wall to realise this is no ordinary station. "We are with you – we will not forget you," they say, a photograph of a Palestinian boy with a slingshot superimposed on the bearded face of the chairman of the Lebanese Hizbollah, Sayed Hassan Nasrallah.

For Manar (Lighthouse in Arabic) belongs to Lebanon's Hizbollah guerrilla army. It is the secret weapon of the Palestinian intifada against "israeli" occupation, the loyal supporter of armed resistance, devoting at least half its 24-hour-a-day satellite broadcasting to the battle between Palestinians and Israelis in the West Bank and Gaza.

Live news reports, video-clips and "resistance" songs, 50 specially composed for the intifada, are slotted between quiz shows, documentaries and calls to prayer for the millions of Palestinians who tune in to Manar's satellite channel.

Nayef Krayim, the chairman of the multimillion-dollar operation in Beirut's southern suburbs, is a former guerrilla who started at Manar as a presenter seven years ago.

"Our message is very simple," he says. "Hizbollah has a lesson to teach about how land should be liberated: the only way to achieve your goal is by armed resistance, not just by throwing stones."

Manar regularly shows combat footage of its battle with "israeli" troops, a struggle that led to "Israel's" retreat from almost all of southern Lebanon in May. Mr Krayim hopes his Palestinian viewers will get the message.

Indeed, the Hizbollah casts a critical eye on the Palestinian-"israeli" mini-war, noting with scorn what it regards as the unsoldierly use of Palestinian weapons. "We think it is very bad when the Palestinians shoot their guns at funerals," Mr Krayim says. "Random firing is very unprofessional. We prefer they use their bullets somewhere else."

Enemy of "Israeli" though Manar is, the station plans to start a Hebrew language service at the end of the Holy fasting month of Ramadan, for "israeli" viewers. "We have graduates from "israeli" prisons who speak fluent Hebrew," Mr Krayim says with a grim smile.

"We already have a programme about the "israeli" media presented by an ex-prisoner called Adel Termos who translates from Hebrew. He does live translations from Hebrew into Arabic when [the "israeli" Prime Minister] Ehud Barak gives a press conference."

When I dropped by, Ghada Assaf, whose modest hejab did not obscure her obvious beauty, was reading an objective account of the "israeli" election call, much of it culled from Western news agencies.

Most of the male reporting staff – there are 40 journalists on Hizbollah's payroll – have been guerrillas. "It's harder to run a news service than a military operation," one said. "Here you've got to be responsible for political content as well as split-second timing. There's a lot of tension in running a newsroom."

Ali Haj Youssef from Baalbek is the duty editor and I cannot resist asking him if he does not wonder whether a large "israeli" aircraft will not one day come and drop a very big bomb on his station. "I'm not worried," he says. "If I become a martyr for our message, I don't have a problem with that." Which is not exactly the reaction you would get on The Independent's newsdesk. Then again, Manar's message is highly exclusive.

"Some Palestinians did ask us to produce a programme that teaches teenagers how to make explosive devices," Mr Krayim says. "We said that wouldn't be very appropriate on television." When I suggested one reason for his refusal could be the excuse it might give the Israelis to bomb the place, he gives me the long, penetrating Hizbollah look that usually means yes.

When I ask where the money comes from to run this operation – I suspect that Iran provides much of the cash – Mr Krayim raises his hands as if invoking the generosity of God.

In the days of its all-out battle with the "israeli" army in southern Lebanon, 40 per cent of Manar's production was coverage of the local war, including fearful clips of Hizbollah attacks on "israeli" artillery positions, the video-crews running in flak jackets alongside the guerrillas to get their footage.

On the wall of an editing room are photographs of Bakr Haider Ahmed and Behjet Dakroub, two Manar reporters "martyred" on consecutive days during "Israel's" 1993 bombardment of southern Lebanon.

The station's marble floors, oak security doors and staff canteen – firmly locked during Ramadan – speak of a conglomerate that intends to go on broadcasting long after Middle East wars have ended.

"But the war is not going to end before four million Palestinians have returned to their homes," Mr Krayim says. "Only then can we talk about the end of the war. We have a message and it is about Islam, not about war. This is a modern and civilised message.

"After 18 years of resistance, we finally reached the "israeli" border at Khiam," Mr Krayim adds. "But after the liberation of southern Lebanon, nothing is impossible. We are not going to need 18 more years. That's a conviction."

 

Palestinian refugees condemn America's blind bias (7)

About 4,000 Palestinians demonstrated at the Nahr al-Bared refugee camp north of Tripoli on Friday to mark International Solidarity Day with the Palestinian people.
Youths and students condemned America’s “blind bias” for "Israeli" and accused the White House of having always supported injustice and aggression.
The protesters also condemned  the UN’s silence on "Israeli"’s “crimes” and its violation of several international resolutions.
Abu Louay, a commander in the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine, said the intifada would not stop “despite all American pressure and the continuous "israeli" savagery.”
He urged the UN to play its role and stop the "israeli" aggression and occupation of Palestinian and Arab lands, and stressed the Palestinian people’s refusal of any settlement or expulsion projects.

  Arab foreign ministers to meet in Damascus in December(8)

A number of Arab foreign ministers will hold talks next month in Syria in their second meeting to follow-up on a recent Arab summit of solidarity with the Palestinians against "Israeli", Lebanese Foreign Minister Mahmud Hammud said here Thursday.

Foreign ministers of Lebanon, Syria, Egypt, Tunisia, Jordan, Saudi Arabia and the Palestinian Authority as well as Ismat Abdel Meguid, the head of the the Cairo-based Arab League, will meet December 10-12 in Damascus, he said.

"Discussions will focus on ways to back the Intifada (Palestinian uprising), the al-Aqsa fund, the necessary assistance and about the situation in the Palestinian territories in general," he told reporters.

The foreign ministers held their first meeting on the sidelines of a summit of the Organization of the Islamic Conference (OIC) in Qatar earlier this month. After Damascus, they were expected to convene in Tunisia.

The ministers form a "follow-up committee" created at the October 21-22 Arab summit in Egypt which backed the Palestinian uprising and established funds for the victims of clashes with "israeli" forces and to ease their economic dependence on "Israeli".

But they stopped short of scrapping Arab ties with the Jewish state, although a number of Arab states have since closed down liason offices with "Israeli".

The next Arab summit is to be held in Amman in March 2001.
