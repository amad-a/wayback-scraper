Daily Report

February Thursday 17-2000:


Introduction

Hizbullah's Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah vowed that the resistance will continue killing the "Israeli" occupation soldiers and their agents regardless of the consequences, adding, "Barak knows well that the Katyusha rockets are still in the equation".

In a new intimidation signal against Lebanon and its people, however, the "Israeli" enemy government has sanctioned the creation of a three-man committee that will be empowered to order immediate aggressions into Lebanon without heaving to seek prior approval of "Israel's" security cabinet which consists of eleven ministers.

Meanwhile, hundreds of American University of Beirut students pledged continued allegiance to the resistance men struggling to liberate the "Israeli" occupied areas in South Lebanon and Western Beqaa.


Nasrallah: IR is committed to kill more "Israeli" soldiers(1)
"Hizbullahs secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, told a gathering of some 3,000 supporters in Nabi Sheet in the Beqaa that the resistance is committed to killing more of the enemys soldiers and its militiamen.
Let them do whatever they want, he said. Whatever the price is and the sacrifices are, we will destroy any formula in our bid to liberate our land.
Nasrallah was speaking at a commemoration for his predecessor, Sayyed Abbas Mussawi, who was martyred eight years ago, along with his wife and child, in an ambush by an "Israeli" helicopter gunship.
Further attacks on Lebanons infrastructure were unlikely, Nasrallah said. They cannot continue to terrorize us because when they sent their aircraft to strike, they had to order 300,000 settlers to the shelters. He ("Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak) is unable to do that every day.
The Hizbullah leader also reiterated that the resistance reserved the right to fire Katyusha rockets at northern "Israeli" settlements in retaliation for "Israeli" attacks on Lebanese civilians.
Barak knows well that the Katyushas are still in the equation. The "Israeli" settlers whom he ordered into shelters for three days rotted because they knew there was a possibility the Katyushas could fall at any moment, Nasrallah said.
When the Katyushas become predictable they lose their importance as a weapon, he said. It would be a mistake if our enemy finds out when we are going to use the Katyushas and when we are not.
Nasrallah also described the recent "Israeli" claim that Hizbullah was violating the terms of the April Understanding by staging operations from civilian areas as lies.
Let them tell me from which village the Blat operation was staged or Aql Hachems killing or the Izzieh operation, he declared. The resistance is committed to the terms of the understanding because it protects our people.
Barak, Nasrallah said, was facing a crisis. Neither the peace talks are moving forward, nor the security arrangements are achieving their goals.
Whatever Barak does, he will be defeated and has absolutely no choice but to surrender and withdraw quickly, he said.

Barak granted power to launch more aggressions into Lebanon(2)
Barak appeared to be in a more combative mood on Wednesday. The 11-man "Israeli" security cabinet appointed him to head a committee of three ministers with the power to authorize immediate reprisal operations following Hizbullah assaults against "Israeli" troops in the occupation zone.
The other committee members are Levy and Transport Minister Yitzhak Mordechai.
The plan was opposed by three ministers ­ Education Minister Yossi Sarid, Justice Minister Yossi Beilin, and Public Security Minister Shlomo Ben Ami.
We cannot get into a situation in which military actions are authorized automatically, Ben-Ami told "Israel" television.
Observers suggest that the creation of the committee has less to do with facilitating reprisal operations and more to do with appeasing Baraks domestic audience, which has grown angry at the governments inability to thwart the recent spate of troop casualties in the south.
The idea behind forming the committee, apparently, is to speed up the process of retaliation. Yet any attacks on Lebanese infrastructure would still have to be preceded by the lengthy process of ordering the 300,000 civilians of northern "Israel" into bomb shelters in case Hizbullah responded with a cross-border Katyusha barrage.
The "Israeli" Army already retaliates against Hizbullah attacks with air raids and artillery shelling. If the "Israelis" wish to escalate the level of retaliation and target Lebanese infrastructure again, it makes little difference whether the order is given by an 11-man committee or a three-man committee, observers say.


Into the streets we go: AUB students condemn US support for "Israeli" strikes(3)
Hundreds of students demonstrated at the American University of Beirut on Wednesday, lashing out against US foreign policy and "Israeli" aggression as they took their rally into the streets of the capital.
The protest began at noon outside West Hall as about 500 students gathered, many carrying enlarged photographs of the damaged power plants and banners calling for the expulsion of US Ambassador David Satterfield. Other signs read: I resist, therefore I exist; Stop US aid to "Israel", and Resolution 425 is the only way we accept.
Students were objecting to US government support for attacks by "Israel" last week which ruined three power plants, wounded 18 people and plunged Lebanon into darkness once again.
As one student explained, although it has been a week since "Israeli" warplanes blasted Lebanon, the students only demonstrated now because there were end-of-semester examinations last week.
Kamal Sanjakdar, one of the students involved in organizing the protests, told those in the crowd that war is no longer fought simply with arms but also with pens and minds.
Were all students and capable of thought, he cried out, using a bullhorn so that everyone could hear.
Were the youth. Were the agents of change. We stand for absolute freedom, 100 percent freedom, he shouted, cheered on by the eager crowd.
Sanjakdar reminded his fellow students that the war in the south continues daily, and that the south is paying the price for the entire country, and for the region.
What kind of peace do we have when, every day, Lebanese in the south are killed? he asked. We will not back down on Resolution 425 because its the only solution.
Lashing out against the American governments support of "Israeli" aggressions, Sanjakdar called for banning Satterfield from AUB.
The ambassador is no longer welcome on campus, he shouted.
The students agreed, responding to his words with thunderous cheers of support.
When one student asked why there was no similar lashing out against AUB president John Waterbury and provost Peter Heath, the response which came back was unwavering: We are against American foreign policy, not Americans.
As the students marched toward College Hall, the momentum rose with each step. They left from the main gate, making their way along Bliss Street and then up Jeanne dArc Street to Sidani Street.
With a downpour temporarily cooperating for the one-hour march, storekeepers and passers-by came out, stood along the sidewalks and cheered the students on. Many even marched with them temporarily.
The students then made their way up to Hamra Street, down Abdul Aziz and back to the campus, incessantly chanting anti-US slogans and proudly singing the Lebanese national anthem. Throughout their march, the police rushed to block traffic and ensure that the march remained peaceful. They were clearly hoping to avoid a repeat of Tuesday nights events, when students had gathered at the Assembly Hall where Satterfield was attending a concert by the Apple Hill Chamber Players, the first embassy-sponsored event in 19 years.
According to an engineering student who preferred to remain anonymous, the protest began small, with only 20-30 students holding up banners, lighting candles, and shouting anti-US slogans such as Shame on you, Shame on America.
Fortunately, the numbers increased and we were 200 to 300 students chanting and voicing our frustration at the situation, he said.
According to the student, protesters blocked entrances to the Assembly Hall so that Satterfield would acknowledge their presence ­ maybe even talk to them ­ on his way out. But there werent enough of us and he managed to slip out a side door and into his car, he said.
The student said that while security officials tried to clear the way and even shoved a student or two, nobody was hurt.
We even tried to chase his convoy but they were driving quite fast across campus, he said. I even had to jump out of the way, as did other students.
Although there was no official statement from AUBs administration, Dean Kevlin, AUBs recently appointed dean of student affairs, was trying to get a handle on the situation Wednesday morning.
That the incident ended there was fortunate, Kevlin said. I certainly see the students point, he added, but this wasnt the most effective way to make it.
Kevlin said the students should have considered inviting the ambassador to an open debate where they could voice their concerns and frustrations.
But according to one student who was standing close by, this approach was tried last year, but the ambassador would not answer any of our questions.
Kevlin said that Tuesday nights protest, which could have turned ugly, proved that certain guidelines have to be discussed and agreed upon with the students.
But of course Im sympathetic and the university fully supports freedom of speech and the right to protest, said Kevlin, who, to demonstrate his support, actually stood alongside students during Wednesdays demonstration.
But, for now, the only feeling that AUB students appear to be experiencing is a need to accelerate their actions. They have organized another demonstration on Thursday which will begin on campus and end at the US Embassy in Awkar".


World sides with us over "Israeli" attacks(4)
Hundreds of people across the world are protesting against "Israel's" bombing of Lebanon this week, and more demonstrations are planned, to allow Arab voices to be heard above the din of pro-"Israeli" media.
The average American is ignorant of whats going on, said Nabil Mohammed, organizing director at the Arab-American Anti-Discrimination Committee during a phone interview from Washington. They only get one side of the story.
The latest aggression in Lebanon has once again been portrayed as a defensive act, he explained. People here keep hearing about "Israeli" civilians and the peace process. But they have to understand that the first step in the peace process is an "Israeli" pullout.
Using civil protest as its medium, the ADC made the other sides voice heard by organizing a candlelight vigil in front of the "Israeli" Embassy in Washington on Tuesday. We tried to explain to everyone that the occupation of south Lebanon is in fact illegal, and that resisting it is a human right.
More than 200 demonstrators carried signs and handed out flyers. We were hoping to raise awareness by targeting the rush-hour commuters heading home and gaining media coverage, he said. We handed out more than 400 fact sheets to passers-by as well as the employees leaving the embassy at the end of the day. Many people stopped and asked questions and a lot honked in support of our chants ­ End the occupation. Stop killing our children. Although the Washington Post didnt come out, both the Associated Press and LBC did.
Demonstrators carried candles for two hours in solidarity with the millions sitting in darkness in Lebanon, Mohammed said. Encouraged by the demonstrations success, many are returning to the embassy this week to repeat the effort.
Another ADC chapter organized a similar movement on Americas West Coast. Over the weekend, more than 300 people picketed in front of the "Israeli" Consulate in Los Angeles to put pressure on the Jewish state to implement UN Resolution 425, which calls for "Israel" to unconditionally withdraw from Lebanon. Were planning a much larger demonstration next week that should bring together people from all over the US, said Mohammed. Well be picketing in front of the White House to focus this time on changing government policy.
Smaller demonstrations took place in many other states such as Texas, where many Arabs and Arab-Americans reside.
The Association for Peace in Lebanon, an umbrella group for many Arab student organizations in Boston universities, will hold a rally in front of the John F. Kennedy Federal Building on Thursday.
The association is inviting anyone concerned about this gross violation of human rights to join in voicing their concerns of the American governments policy in condoning and funding "Israeli" actions.
One of the groups main aims is to inform average American citizens about their role in helping to fund "Israeli" military activities. As American citizens, we have not only a right but a responsibility to speak out, the statement said.
Hundreds marched through the streets of Paris on Sunday, while 18 deputies in Great Britains House of Commons signed a petition condemning the "Israeli" attack as a violation of human rights and of the Fourth Geneva Convention, which prohibits targeting civilians and infrastructure. The MPs wrote that the aggression does not serve the peace process in the region, but obstructs it and called for the respect of the April Understanding and UN Resolution 425.
"Israel's" BTselem human-rights group reminded its government last week that under the understanding, "Israel" and those cooperating with it will not fire any kind of weapon at civilians or civilian targets in Lebanon. It said the attacks on the power stations and other infrastructure contravene international humanitarian law and are prohibited.
BTselem released a report in January that discussed the wide range of human-rights violations in the context of "Israel's" occupation of south Lebanon.


Berri calls for spotlight on air strikes(5)
Speaker Nabih Berri asked civil and governmental organizations on Wednesday to contribute to an awareness campaign against "Israeli" violence.
This, the speaker said, would ultimately force the Jewish state to stop its terrorist activities against Lebanon.
Berri was speaking in Parliament at a meeting of the National Committee for the Commemoration of the "Israeli" invasion of Lebanon on March 14, 1978, and of the Qana Massacre on April 18, 1996.
I asked for a rapid meeting of the committee because it has proven to be extremely efficient in past years, said Berri. Lebanons officials and prominent members of society, as well as its friends, are being asked to launch an international campaign to face the nightmare of the "Israeli" threat against civil life in Lebanon.
He said it was important to form national sensitivity to the reality of the "Israeli" occupation and attacks, especially at a time when the "Israelis" have threatened to hit the countrys vital utilities and infrastructure again.
He said that the reason for the escalation of tension and violence is mainly due to the continuous "Israeli" occupation of large areas of Lebanon, and to "Israel's" refusal to implement UN Resolution 425.
Berri further pointed to "Israeli" attempts to distract attention from the real reason that led to the halt in Syrian-"Israeli" peace negotiations.
The cause of the halt, he said, is "Israel's" refusal to withdraw from the Golan Heights.


Sharaa blames "Israel" for its aggression on Lebanon(6)
Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa responded furiously to a series of colorful threats delivered against Lebanon last week by his "Israeli" counterpart, David Levy.
The foreign minister has illogically and irrationally placed the blame on others for "Israels" blatant aggression on Lebanon, Sharaa declared in a letter to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan.

It is crystal clear that "Israel" has not only stalled the peace-making process, but it has also rendered it meaningless, he added. It only wants to use its military might, rather than resort to diplomacy to resolve the Middle East conflict.
Sharaa stressed that international norms and peace pacts recognized the legitimacy of resistance movements, such as Hizbullah, to combat an occupying force. "Israel" is insolent enough to depict itself as the victim rather than the aggressor, he said.


Mofaz gets mixed reviews at home(7)
Although the "Israeli" security Cabinet granted three ministers the power to order a rapid response to resistance attacks, there was no word on the fate of a proposal by General Shaul Mofaz, the chief of staff, to shell Lebanese villages if Hizbullah stages attacks from them.
After the meeting, Mofaz said it was inconceivable for Hizbullah alone to have total freedom of action to attack our soldiers from villages.
Mofaz aired his views in public on Tuesday, a move that prompted equal amounts of support and criticism.
Soldiers have the right to open fire on those who attack them, dovish Education Minister Yossi Sarid told "Israel's" Army Radio, but there is a difference between firing blindly and putting civilians in danger and the right to defend yourself.
Sarid accused Mofaz of trying to manipulate the Cabinet by speaking to the media about his demands before presenting them to the ministers.
But deputy defense minister Ephraim Sneh agreed with Mofaz. "Israeli" soldiers have the right to fire toward the area which was the source of the shots, he said. Its the basic military doctrine.
Seven "Israeli" soldiers have been killed by Hizbullah fighters in the past three weeks, five of them by TOW anti-tank missiles. The other two were killed by a Sagger anti-tank missile and a combined mortar and Katyusha bombardment. The better accuracy and range of the TOW over the Sagger has allowed Hizbullah to penetrate the "Israeli" Armys heavily fortified outposts in the occupation zone.
The "Israeli" Army is examining how to protect the occupants of these posts from further missile attacks. Among the reported recommendations are new procedures for soldiers manning observation posts.


Golani chief scolded for calling reporters 'rags'(8)
"Chief of General Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaul Mofaz yesterday stepped into a verbal battle between the media and the Golani Brigade commander Col. Shmuel Zakai, who reportedly derided "Israel" Radio talk show hostess Shelly Yachimowitz and military reporter Carmela Menashe, as well as members of the Four Mothers group, as "rags."

Mofaz said he asked OC Northern Command Maj.-Gen. Gaby Ashkenazy to look into the reports to see if Zakai really made the comments, which he was quoted as making in a closed-door motivational talk with soldiers at a training base, where Golani soldiers prepare for combat in Lebanon.

"I am waiting for the results of the examination and if he really made these statements it is grave and I condemn them," Mofaz told Army Radio. "No IDF officer should make these sort of comments."

Last night, the IDF* Spokesman confirmed that Zakai had mentioned the Four Mothers, an organization lobbying for a withdrawal from Lebanon. According to the spokesman, Zakai called the group a "legitimate organization in a democratic country."

Ashkenazy reprimanded Zakai for "his choice of words."
