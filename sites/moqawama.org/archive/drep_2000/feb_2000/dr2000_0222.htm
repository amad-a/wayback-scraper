Daily Report

February Tuesday 22-2000:

 

Introduction

Hizbullah's Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said that the most important results of the latest "Israeli" failed operations was bringing down the image of the "Israeli" enemy premier Ehud Barak as a man of peace.

Meanwhile, Hizbullah reported, in statement, that the sudden visit paid by the Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak to Lebanon "has proven that Arab unity is a very important factor in confronting the "Israeli"-American arrogance besides being an auxiliary way to recover rights and liberate land".


Hizbullah: Resistance operations not subject to calculations, aggression brought down Barak's peaceful image(1)

"The most important results of the latest "Israeli" failed operations was bringing down the image of ("Israeli" occupation's prime minister Ehud) Barak as a man of peace, Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said.

The Secretary-general told al-Hayyat newspaper he expects ""Israel"" to resort to achieving a certain military achievement to recover its positions in order to return to the April Understanding Monitoring Group.

, Sayyed Nasrallah said we do not deny the charge of driving ""Israel"" through the operations to a unilateral unconditional withdrawal."

The fact that firing Katyusha rockets was subject to calculations does not mean that the operations (against the "Israeli" occupation forces) are likewise, for these are open, Sayyed Nasrallah said.

The secretary-general commended the Lebanese official and popular unity to support the Resistance, besides the high performance of president Gen. Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Dr. Salim al-Hoss adding that he believes President Lahoud, in his warning to ""Israel"" that "our blows will be more painful" did "not mean that the state will strike; he meant that the resistance will."

Commenting on Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak's visit, His Eminence hoped "the Egyptian attitude will become as embracing and protective as the Syrian and Iranian stands."


Hizbullah pays homage to Egyptian president(2)

" Lebanese Hizbullah praised Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak Sunday for his lightning visit to Lebanon to condemn "Israeli" air attacks on civilian targets.

Hizbullah, which carries out most of the anti-"Israeli" attacks in "Israeli"-occupied southern Lebanon, said in a statement it also "paid homage" to Mubarak for "his support for the resistance."

"It's been proven that Arab solidarity can counter "Israeli"-US arrogance and help free the occupied territories," Hizbullah said.

"The United States should not be trusted because it sponsors "Israeli" aggression," Hizbullah said, and praised "the Egyptian people who rose up against the Zionist enemy and usurper."

After Mubarak met Lebanese President Emile Lahoud Saturday, they issued a statement upholding the "right" of Lebanese guerrillas to continue attacks on "Israeli" troops in southern Lebanon.

"The latest "Israeli" aggression on civilian infrastructure in Lebanon is a blatant violation of the April (1996) understanding," which bans combatants from targeting civilians, the joint statement said.

"Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levy deplored Sunday Egypt's support for Hizbullah.

"We are saddened by the support for Hizbullah expressed by Egypt, which we regard nevertheless with the greatest respect," Levy said on public radio.

"Israel's" February 8 air raids knocked out power too much of Lebanon and wounded 22 civilians. The raids were in retaliation for Hizbullah attacks that killed five "Israeli" soldiers and the deputy commander of "Israel's" South Lebanon Army ally".


Mubarak's weekend's visit sparks more positive comments

Hoss said on Monday that Egypts support for Lebanon culminated in the surprise, landmark visit.
With "Israel" increasingly isolated and pressure on its government mounting to defuse the latest tensions, Hoss said he hoped that the Egyptian presidents initiative would help relaunch the five-nation Monitoring Groups activities.
The visit is the result of intensive Egyptian diplomatic activity to support Lebanon and prevent "Israel" from repeating its attacks against civilian infrastructure, Hoss said.
After the "Israeli" attacks earlier this month, the April Understanding committee halted its meetings as "Israel" refused to attend.
Meanwhile, the weekend visit continued to spark positive comments from politicians who, like Hoss, considered the Egyptian presidents move as a clear sign of solidarity with Lebanon.
Tyre MP Mohammed Abdel-Hamid Beydoun said that one of the visits most important aspects was Mubaraks emphasis on Lebanons right to resist "Israeli" occupation.
The MP was referring to the joint statement issued by the two presidents summing up their positions. The statement had stressed the right of resisting "Israeli" occupation until liberation is achieved.
The MP said: This assertion is a rejection of the repeated excuses used by "Israel" to justify its calls for halting the resistance. Beydoun added that the visit constituted a message to "Israeli" public opinion that, if Ehud Barak does not reach a settlement with Syria, he will jeopardize peace with Egypt.
Political observers said that when Lahoud warned "Israel" on Friday against painful blows that would follow any "Israeli" violation of the April Understanding, he meant that, in such an eventuality, Arab states that have normalized relations with "Israel" could reconsider these relations. This is why, observers said, the president linked the painful blows to the diversity of "Israel's" interests.
Political sources said Lahoud received positive remarks from Mubarak, sparking hope that Egypt would consider imperiling its relations with "Israel" if a new attack were launched against Lebanon.
The tough tone of the joint statement reflected the extent of Egypts support for Syria, with the two presidents having emphasized the need to support the simultaneity of the two peace tracks. One MP said that even Lebanon and Syria could not have produced a more uncompromising statement.
Observers also pointed to other possible dimensions of Mubaraks unexpected visit.
The Egyptian president might have seen an opportunity to put behind him an incident which took place this month in Egypt. Barak, speaking at a joint news conference with Mubarak in Cairo, said "Israel" would retaliate heavily for Hizbullahs killing of a senior Israeli-allied militia official, Aql Hachem. Egypts ambassador to Lebanon, Adel Khodari, explained that Mubarak was unaware of what Barak was saying, as he was speaking in Hebrew"(3)

  

"Jordan's King Abdullah had reiterated Amman's condemnation of "Israel's" February 8 air strikes. In an interview with the Kuwaiti al-Rai al-Am newspaper, King Abdullah said the strikes on civilian installations had "no justification". The Jordanian monarch said the attacks violated quote all international laws. Abdullah, whose Kingdom signed a peace treaty with "Israel" in 1994, also called for the resumption of Middle East peace talks"(4)


Arab officials expected to support Lebanon(5)

"Following Egypts lead, Arab envoys are due in Beirut this week to express their solidarity in the face of "Israeli" attacks and threats.
Kuwaiti Foreign Minister Sheikh Sabah al-Ahmad al-Sabah will visit Beirut on Tuesday and is expected to meet President Emile Lahoud and Premier Salim Hoss.
On Sunday, the Kuwaiti minister met with his Egyptian counterpart, Amr Moussa, in Cairo for a briefing on President Mubaraks visit to Lebanon.
The US ambassador to Kuwait was summoned to the emirates Foreign Ministry on Thursday, and told that Washington must intervene to halt "Israeli" aggression against Lebanon.
Farouk Kaddoumi, head of the PLO politburo, is expected to arrive in Lebanon on Tuesday and meet Hoss on Wednesday.
Kaddoumi met Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa in Damascus on Monday. We discussed with Sharaa the right of the Lebanese resistance to carry out operations until "Israel" withdraws from Lebanese territory, Kaddoumi told reporters.
The two called for a political solution to the Arab-"Israeli" conflict. We agreed that peace must be founded on complete "Israeli" withdrawal from occupied territories in line with the Madrid Conference principles, Kaddoumi said.
Syria supports Palestinian and Lebanese aims for a complete ("Israeli") withdrawal from their occupied territories and wants to eliminate inter-Arab disagreement and strengthen solidarity, Kaddoumi said.

Algerian President Abdel-Aziz Bouteflika said a peace treaty with "Israel" was possible only if it admitted to the right of Palestinians to establish their own state with Jerusalem as its capital and unconditionally withdrew from the Golan Heights and south Lebanon. Bouteflika also praised Lebanons resistance forces.
Meanwhile, Zouheir Hamdan, the Foreign Ministrys director general, met Lebanons ambassador to Amman, Adib Alameddine, who briefed him on a campaign by Lebanese expatriates in Amman to collect donations to help rebuild the three power stations destroyed in the "Israeli" raids.


SYRIA EXPECTS RESUMPTION OF APRIL TRUCE MEETINGS(6)

"Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk Sharaa, expected in a newspaper interview, the five-nation truce panel in southern Lebanon to convene a meeting in the next few days. "Israel" has so far been reluctant to attend a meeting of the cease-fire monitoring group.

"In an interview with Beirut's daily Safir newspaper, Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk Sharaa said the five delegates to the 1996 April truce panel could meet in a few days. There have been mounting calls from Arab states and the international community for a convention of the panel. Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak and his Lebanese counterpart Emile Lahoud over the weekend warned "Israel" against ignoring the accord on the rules of engagement in southern Lebanon. Both "Israel" and Lebanese resistance fighters are barred from targeting civilians by the 1996 "April Understanding". "Israel" walked out of a February 11 meeting of the group monitoring the agreement, saying it would no longer abide by it. But on Saturday the "Israeli" army said it submitted six complaints to the five-nation committee since then, a move Prime Minister Selim Hoss described as positive. Hoss said if "Israel" did not want a meeting of the panel, which includes delegates from the US, France, "Israel", Syria, and Lebanon, it would not have filed any complaints. Hoss expressed optimism over the prospects of holding a truce meeting soon. Beirut has been holding diplomatic contacts with Paris and Washington to hasten a meeting. But the Secretary-General of Hizbullah Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah warned in an interview published today that "Israel" may launch a military assault against Lebanon before attending truce meetings. Nasrallah said "Israel's" attacks on Lebanon this month were quote a failure, adding the Jewish state will have to achieve some kind of military victory before going back to the rules of the accord. The United States and France have been holding intense contacts to persuade "Israel" to return to the truce panel, which was created following "Israel's" wide-scale attack on Lebanon in 1996".


Lebanese-Americans continue to protest the "Israeli" aggression.
Long after the bombs, voices are still heard(7)

"Never shy when it comes to supporting their fellow countrymen back home, the Lebanese community in Texas, USA held rallies in the city of Austin at the local university and the Capitol Building yesterday.
"The rallies were co-ordinated by student and community groups that live in and around Austin with some help from the American Arab Anti Discrimination Committee. The protesters wanted to make the citizens of Austin more aware of the recent "Israeli" aggression against Lebanese civilian installations and of Lebanons plight to end more than 20 years of occupation in the South. The turnout was high considering the relatively small Lebanese community in Austin and the event received local media coverage.

A protest of their own(10)
 With an IDF* withdrawal from Lebanon edging closer, the Four Mothers protest group has come a long way.
 "I'm a proud rag," Ami Katz declares defiantly. "This setting in which you've found me isn't strange at all; the setting in which my son is serving, in Lebanon, is what's strange."
Despite the disclaimer, it is indeed surprising to come across Katz, a stout ultra-Orthodox man draped in unkempt Hasidic clothing, standing atop the "mothers outpost" that Orna Shimoni and the Four Mothers group have set up near the prime minister's house in Jerusalem to protest the IDF's continued presence in South Lebanon. "You know what's not right here?" Katz asks. "It's wrong that my son is sitting up there, not that I'm sitting here."
Katz, 42, a settler in the Gush Etzion bloc and the father of nine children, is accustomed to being unusual. He even seems to enjoy flouting expectations. At the age of 15, as a secular Tel Aviv youth, he met Rabbi Haim Drukman, who was delivering a lecture to the socialist Hashomer Hatzair youth group to which Katz belonged. The teenager was mesmerized by the religious message propagated by the charismatic lecturer. Soon thereafter, he left his secular city high school and took up religious study at the Ponevezh yeshiva; and he traded away a left-wing political outlook, moving to the right. His flirtation with right-wing politics, however, lasted just a few years, and today Katz belongs to Meretz. He continues to live within the Haredi world while marching to the beat of his own drum.
"I don't represent the typical Haredi, and in the eyes of the ultra-Orthodox, I'm not a desirable Haredi," Katz reflects, sitting on a stool next to a railing that separates the compound from the street, on which some vehicles passing by toot their horns in support of the compound's pro-withdrawal message. "I don't want to be a stereotypical Haredi because I can't stand them, ideologically. The Hasidic dress I wear is in solidarity with a rich family heritage; it isn't a political statement."
Katz's son Menahem has followed a route opposite of his father's, leaving the religious world at the age of 15. Today the young man serves as a paratrooper. His estrangement from Judaism is acute, as exemplified by requests he has relayed to his father about his military service - for one thing, he has asked to receive neither a religious nor a military burial should he die in the IDF. "He doesn't think that a 21-gun salute will take him to where the dead go," the father says, explicating Menahem's sentiments.
Referring to the dilemmas his son faces while serving today for the second time in the security zone, Ami Katz says, "In the meantime, he's there, and he's not refusing orders. I salute him for that, because I don't know what I'd do in his place."
Katz and a few other men are demonstrating at the Four Mothers compound, and yet this pro-withdrawal protest remains primarily a women's story. Now claiming a central role in a searing political debate, the Four Mothers group has come a long way, overcoming qualms felt by women's movements about the moral and practical proprieties of using gender, and the womb, for protest purposes. Such scruples persisted, even though men in politics have not hesitated to use body symbols - war scars, for instance - for protest. For years, women's movements debated whether they should act in their own separate voice.

A protest of their own

Today the answer to this debate might seem self-evident, as women's demonstrations have become an inseparable element in the country's protest landscape. Yet if they have become common, women's protests continue to bear distinctive characteristics, in terms of their persistence and the spirit that animates their activities, and also with respect to their perspicacious ability to identify borders demarcating the public's endurance levels - to distinguish between pain that must be tolerated and suffering that is entirely unnecessary - before other sectors in the society are fully aware of such threshold levels and breaking points.
This foresight was evident when women organized after the opening of the Western Wall tunnels in Jerusalem and the ensuing bloodshed, and it has manifested itself in the case of Lebanon as well.
With the passage of time, messages propounded in women's protests have changed. In the beginning, somewhat hesitant activities stressed themes such as the sanctity of life and the foolishness of waging wars when other options were available; and Orna Shimoni, the mother of Ayal, a soldier killed in Lebanon, limited her campaigns to the demand that the Lebanon issue become a high priority on the national agenda. Today, the women call in a cogent, unfaltering voice, demanding a withdrawal from Lebanon.
The current round of protests in front of Ehud Barak's house started exactly a week ago, and it's not clear how long it will last. The establishment of the protest tent was preceded by four letters sent to Barak by Orna Shimoni. None of these was answered; the evasive recalcitrance hurt her. She thought that her letters deserved, at the least, a reply of the "I received your letter" variety.
"The impact of these changing circumstances is impossible to miss. Even if one comes without public opinion polls in hand, in a visit to the protest compound it is impossible not to discern the profound change in the attitude taken by "Israeli" society toward the women's protest movement and the situation in Lebanon. Times and attitudes have indeed changed since Deputy Knesset Speaker and Likud MK Meir Cohen-Avidav termed the Mothers Against Silence group "fat women" - the derogatory term was condoned by many for some time, but the recent across-the-board uproar denouncing the "four rags" sneer made by an IDF officer portended a societal change.
Similarly, attitudes concerning "Israel's" presence in Lebanon have changed significantly. About a year ago, Shimoni lingered for a month in front of the President's Residence in Jerusalem, demanding that the mothers group be allowed to meet with the security cabinet. Responses at the time ranged from public apathy to overt displays of a lack of sympathy shown by pedestrian passers-by. Now times have changed. Groups of supporters flock to the protest tent from all parts of the country; drivers passing by roll down their windows to utter sympathetic words. Seen no longer as a group of hysterical women, the mothers now express a mounting public mood.
While I was speaking with Shimoni, a religious woman wearing an elegant hat approached her, explaining that she had been invited to protest against the withdrawal from the Golan Heights. "But," the woman said, "it's more important to be here. When are we going to get out of Lebanon already?" she asked Shimoni, who has come to be regarded as an authority on this question. "When 100,000 people come here," Shimoni replied.

Wanted: 100,000 protesters

One hundred thousand protesters are not to be found in the compound, but public support for the cause is surging. On Sunday, a group of merchants from the Karmel Casbah visited the mothers tent, bringing crates of fruit and vegetables. Residents from buildings nearby furnish food and drink as well; the owner of a Jerusalem hotel has made a room available to Shimoni.
The protest compound is reviving scenes known at this site only once before in the past, during the stormy protests opposing the Lebanon War that were staged in front of the house when Menachem Begin lived in it. After almost 20 years, the circle has closed. Nadia Klein, a left-wing English teacher from Jerusalem, took part in the previous demonstrations. In 1982, her son was two years old; today, Eran Klein serves in the Nahal infantry corps, and his unit is scheduled for Lebanon duty in another month.
"It would have been unfathomable at the time to imagine that I would come here again when Eran was in the army," she says, "but here I am, not only because our sons are falling as casualties, but because I think it's immoral that we're there."
Klein's personal story encapsulates not only the continuing trauma in Lebanon, but also the complexity of the protest that the mothers group promotes. While Klein protests in favor of withdrawal from the security zone, her son feels guilt-stricken due to the fact that he won't go up to Lebanon with his unit mates because of his participation in an officers' training course. "There is a phenomenon among our children - some cut themselves off from our activity," Klein says, as other mothers in the protest compound nod in wan assent. "They don't speak with us about our activity, even if they support it. My son only asked that I don't embarrass him," she says.
The women's group copes with delicate family wrinkles of this sort and other poignant dilemmas. In general, it's hard to protest in a small country, where everyone seems to be connected with one another, either by social, familial or other bonds.
The notorious "rags" remark made by Brigade Commander Shmuel Zakai has already been forgiven; yet comments made by Deputy Defense Minister Ephraim Sneh that "the Four Mothers don't tell the whole truth" (in other words, that they lie) will be harder to forget. "I'm very angry with him," says Bruria Sharon, a member of the group.
Sharon comes across as a walking multimedia show in the protest tent. She's hooked up to radio and telephones and covered with slogans and stickers. "If we're not telling the whole truth, let's talk about what he [Sneh] is concealing, the fact that Hizbullah hasn't once tried to infiltrate "Israeli" territory, and the fact that nothing catastrophic happened after the withdrawal from Jezzine. The truths he isn't telling are surely more important than ours," she exclaims in a defiant tone.
Orna Shimoni is also furious about Sneh's comment, though she isn't angry with him personally. Sneh was a close friend of her late husband, a Labor Party stalwart. "Ephraim gave me a lot of support during the period of mourning," she relates. "In fact, during the Shiva [mourning period] for Ayal, we gave him a platform to persuade those present that his death wasn't in vain, that the children are really being killed in defense of the settlements in the north. But now I can't forgive him. If the brigade commander's remark was a stupid slip of the tongue, Ephraim's comment was well-considered, and it was a blow below the belt."
Relating tales of the past, criticizing rough responses to still ruder realities, receiving well-wishers and curious passers-by and responding to world media that have come to document their activities, the mothers spend their days in the protest compound. Sometimes politicians stop by - Yael Dayan came on Sunday, and Minister Binyamin Ben-Elizer came accompanied by journalists ("He wanted to sound off and not listen," the protesters complain). From the protesters' point of view, more moving was the visit made by a cab driver, who brought a pizza.
