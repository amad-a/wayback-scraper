Daily Report

February Wednesday 09-2000:


Introduction

Hizbullah said that the "Israeli" aggression against Lebanon's infrastructure is a "flagrant breach of the April Understanding" adding that "we realize that the settlers in the northern settlements are hiding in their lairs while they should pay the price for their Premier's criminal acts that they back". Hizbullah stressed that "we reserve the right of retaliation in the appropriate time that may be very soon".

In this regard, the Islamic Resistance (IR) men blitzed the "Israeli"-manned outpost of Dabsheh on the edge of the central sector of the occupied zone killing, at least, one "Israeli" soldier and wounding another. Also, an "Israeli" agent was perished of wounds received by pinpoint mortar fired by the IR militants also at Bir Kallab outpost.

Meanwhile, Lebanon's President Gen. Emile Lahoud stressed that ""Israel" wants us to protect its soldiers who occupy Lebanese areas, but Lebanon will never protect the "Israeli" occupying troops", adding "the Resistance is the right of any people which suffers from occupation".

However, the "Israeli" settlers in the northern colonies are complaining that they are unable to remain underground indefinitely waiting for the next Hizbullah strike. Analysts have pointed out that Hizbullah's waiting game has taken its toll on the nerves of northern settlers and has scored a significant success.

Also, the IR squad of martyrs Ali Hussein Ismail and Mustafa Masshoud Chokair targeted, at 16:00, the western fortification of Bir Kallab where enemy movements were spotted. Using the rocket-propelled grenades, the mujahedeen managed to destroy if completely and inflicting its occupants.

Earlier, the IR group of martyrs Imad Ahmad El-Mawla and Ali Hassan Farhat attacked, at 12:30, Bir Kallab outpost with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades scoring direct and certain hits.

Moreover, the IR unit of martyrs Ali Ahmad Dagher and Abdel-Amir Sadeq Ansari targeted, at 14:45, enemy movements inside Bir Kallab outpost also. The militants, who used the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades were able to chalk up direct and definite goals.

Gunships rocket south after Hizbullah strikes(3)
""Israel" struck for a second night running on Tuesday with helicopter gunships rocketing a building in Tyre, hours after defiant resistance fighters killed an "Israeli" soldier in an ambush.
The 11pm helicopter attack came after "Israelis" confirmed the latest fatality and declared a state of emergency in northern "Israel" for an initial period of 48 hours.
Two men were wounded when three missiles were fired into the top floor of an 11-story building located beside the sea front on the southern end of the Tyre peninsula.

Helicopter gunships also raided four locations in Iqleem al-Toffah between the villages of Arab Salim and Jibaa.
The renewed air attacks came a day after a series of raids by "Israeli" warplanes plunged Beirut into darkness, wounded 16 civilians and dealt a new blow to the already stumbling peace process.
"Israeli" public television reported that the state of emergency grants the military broad powers in anticipation of possible new air raids over Lebanon. Apparently the message Prime Minister Ehud Barak wanted to get across to Hizbullah and Syria by ordering raids against civilian targets in Lebanon  was not received, the televisions military commentator said.
Fears that violence in the south could spiral out of control were fueled by reports that "Israel" has decided to abandon the April Understanding.
We are not willing to abide by the rules of the game, as they were until today, said Haim Ramon, a Cabinet minister and close confidant of "Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak.
It has to be clear to all the players in Lebanon that the era of the Grapes of Wrath understandings is over, said Ramon.
Barak, however, has yet to comment on whether the understanding would be officially scrapped by "Israel".
Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa said that "Israel" had shaken the foundations of the peace process with its blitz and must take full responsibility for the consequences.
The Syrian minister spoke on the same issue with his Iranian counterpart, Kamal Kharrazi. According to the Syrian National News Agency, the ministers said: "Israel" was responsible for the military escalation and the violation of the April agreement. Kharrazi also discussed the escalation with Prime Minister Salim Hoss.
SANA quoted Sharaa as saying that both the Lebanese and Syrian leaderships are committed to sparing civilians on both sides of the border physical harm and material losses.
SANA added that France and the US had been asked by Sharaa to notify "Israel" that it was imperative for it to uphold the terms of the April Understanding to avoid further escalation and salvage the peace process.
The comments were seen as an indication that Syria is unwilling to sanction cross-border reprisals by Hizbullah, risking a complete collapse of peace hopes.
Commenting on Hizbullahs restraint in the wake of the "Israeli" air raids, a senior Lebanese government source said that contacts had been made with the resistance. He said that Hizbullah
has been acting with extreme wisdom, as it always does.
Hizbullah said the air raids against power stations in Jamhour near Beirut, Deir Nbouh in the north and in Baalback would not curb resistance attacks against "Israeli" troops in the occupation zone.
The party warned that we reserve the right to respond at the right time, which could be very soon. Hizbullah was swift to honor its pledge. At 2.50pm a resistance squad fired rockets at the Dabsheh outpost, killing one soldier and wounding another.

Cabinet stresses: Lebanon won't give up resistance(4)
The Council of Ministers held an extraordinary session to discuss that "Israeli" enemy's aggressions that targeted civilian facilities.

The session was held at 11:00 a.m. under President Gen Emile Lahoud who held ""Israel"" responsible for the deterioration and its consequences.

This "Israeli" aggressive method is not new for Lebanon. ""Israel"" has deliberately obstructed the recent negotiations to pressure Lebanon and Syria, the president said.

In the last few weeks, ""Israel"" violated the April Understanding on a daily basis by targeting civilians in south Lebanon although Lebanon and its Resistance have observed the understanding. These aggressions culminated in the barbarian aggression on civilian infrastructures, he added.

Lahoud stressed that ""Israel"" alone is to be blamed for the consequences that will result from the collapse of the April Understanding if it ("Israel") continues to act as though the understanding did not exist."

These aggressions will not create a new field equation in the interest of ""Israel"", especially regarding (the Resistance's) carrying out daily operations against the enemy in the occupied zone until the occupation is removed, the president said.

On the other hand, Lahoud said Lebanon adheres to the April Understanding provided that ""Israel"" stops attempting to create new field equations outside the understanding's framework.

The president briefed the cabinet on the phone call he received from Col. Dr. Bashar al-Asad who informed the president that Syria is ready to assist Lebanon in repairing the damage caused by the "Israeli" bombardment.

Lahoud gave instructions to all ministries and administrations to take care of the wounded and treat him at the expense of the Health Ministry besides starting immediately to repair the damage inflicted on the infrastructures.

The cabinet's communiqué read, ""Israel" wants us to protect its soldiers on the Lebanese territories. If the crimes perpetrated by "Israel" last night against civilian infrastructures are in retaliation for the operations carried out by the Resistance recently, it must be known that the Resistance has not violated the April Understanding, for its operations were restricted to military targets within Lebanon's borders."

"On the other hand, "Israel" tore the understanding by striking civilian targets in Lebanese territories."

"Let it be known that Lebanon will never protect the "Israeli" occupation of its territories, for Resistance is a legitimate right for any people whose land is under occupation, the cabinet's communiqué concluded.

Tensions are running high among the "Israeli" settlers(9)
"Military commanders admit it is becoming increasingly difficult to keep northern area residents in bomb shelters, a move that while providing them with maximum protection upsets the daily routines and lives of all involved.

Many Kiryat Shmona residents have abandoned their homes and their city, opting to spend time with family or friends in other areas of the country or checking into hotels and guesthouses. Kiryat Shmona residents are complaining that they are unable to remain underground indefinitely, waiting for the next Hizbullah strike.

Following the prime ministers declaration of a state of emergency in the north, residents are now compelled to comply with orders to enter into bomb shelters and failure to do so may be met with punishment under the law. The state of emergency encompasses areas from Rosh HaNikra to Metulla.

Meanwhile, IDF* supply personnel are distributing basic supplies and food to area residents inside the bomb shelters, including fresh bread and other foods, diapers and formulas for infants and more. Officials admit that tensions are running high in the northern border city, with many residents abandoning bomb shelters, willing to face the wrath of local security and defense commanders rather than remain in the shelters.

Analysts have pointed out that Hizbullahs waiting game has taken its toll on the nerves of northern residents and has scored a significant success, with the lives of residents and business communities in shambles, waiting for what is believed to be an imminent attack.

Tuesday morning in Kiryat Shmona and Nahariya brings another day of waiting. Factory owners and proprietors of small businesses insist they are unable to absorb additional losses of businesses, adding that government promises for compensatory payments are soon forgotten when the emergency situation comes to an end. Some stores such as groceries and supermarkets are opening, with owners explaining they feel a responsibility to the community, to supply families and children with the basic essentials during these most difficult times. On Tuesday, several falafel stands and kiosks also opened their doors for business but this was prior to the declaration of the state of emergency.

Sentries are posted at the periphery of the areas included in the state of emergency, barring visitors and those persons not having a valid reason to enter. One may not travel freely in the areas included in the emergency district, with military commanders warning the tense quiet could vanish instantaneously with the Hizbullah launching of a single Katyusha rocket.

The Prime Minister/Defense Minister has made two visits to Kiryat Shmona over recent days in a show of solidarity with area leaders, adding that the coming days will be difficult ones for area residents. On Wednesday morning, Mr. Barak will once again convene the Security Cabinet and on Wednesday afternoon, Foreign Minister David Levy will convene a meeting in Jerusalem of all foreign diplomats stationed in "Israel", to provide an official explanation of the situation in the north and security zone.


Uncertainty, gripes, and anger in a Kiryat Shmona shelter(10)
" Kiryat Shmona was cloaked in uncertainty last night, but it was an uncertainty different from that which gripped the nation south of Yesud Hama'ala, and therefore out of Katyusha range.

In bomb shelters in Kiryat Shmona, like public shelter Gimmel 312 on Rehov Eilat, the uncertainty was not whether the IAF was right in bombing Lebanese infrastructure, but rather whether it was wise to leave the shelter and go home to use the bathroom, or whether it was prudent to step out for a quick smoke.

While the rest of the country was preoccupied with the big questions posed by the bombing raids, whether they will have an impact and whether the Syrians really care if the lights go out in Beirut, in this particular shelter the concerns were much more immediate - where to shower, what to eat, how to keep the kids from climbing the walls.

"I have a three-and-a-half year-old girl who I can't bring down here because she has asthma and the dust would kill her," said Rahel Fahima, 29, a mother of three.

While Fahima is in the shelter with her elderly mother, her husband is in the family apartment with the children.

"I know it's dangerous, but I can't bring the kids here, they would go crazy," she said.

Public shelter Gimmel 312 is not the orderly, bright bomb shelter the public sees on television when one of the country's leaders comes to pay a visit and show support. Far from it.

Public shelter Gimmel 312 is small and malodorous. The 20 or so people here sit on hard cots and gripe. They gripe about the noise and the boredom and the fact that the councilors the mayor boasted to "Israel" Radio were visiting all the shelters in Kiryat Shmona had, for some reason, not visited theirs. They complained that they do not have transportation to the only supermarket open in the city.

And, objectively, they have what to gripe about. The toilet is a hole in the ground, the beds are hard, there's no television outlet, and the radio reception is bad.

"These are people who have lived here their whole lives, who have gone through this before, but are still scared and angry," said Chana Manne, duty psychologist for Kiryat Shmona's emergency medical services. "They feel like sitting ducks."

What was most interesting about the anger of those in this shelter was that it was anger about the conditions, anger at not having the means - like some of their neighbors - to go south for a couple of days.

It was anger at the government for not paying them enough attention and anger at the rest of the country for going on with life as usual.


Northern shutdown is costly to country(11)
" The forced shutdown in the north is taking a heavy economic toll on the country, with the economic damage caused to businesses and the service industry estimated at NIS 10 million a day. The direct damage to industrial works in the Upper Galilee as a result of the shutdown is estimated at NIS 3 million to NIS 5 million a day. Some 60 industrial and agricultural plants have been forced to shut down in the Upper Galilee. The damage to the industrial works is particularly serious, since the shutdown comes immediately following the ports strike.
According to figures published yesterday by the Chamber of Commerce, the losses for about 3,500 commercial enterprises in the north are estimated at NIS 8 million in daily receipts and another NIS 2 million at service establishments, such as restaurants and hotels. Chamber of Commerce Deputy Director Yossi Shustak appealed yesterday to the Income Tax Commission to amend legislation on indemnities for businesses forced to shut down because of the security situation and to adapt the legislation without discriminating between market sectors.
Shustak explained that the law grants compensation to businesses for wages paid for canceled shifts, but that no arrangement has yet been made for direct losses to a business's income arising out of a loss of sales. He added that the current law provides compensation for the business and service industries only after a minimum of at least seven consecutive days of hostilities. Most shutdowns in the Upper Galilee last fewer than seven days. Industries, on the other hand, he explained, are eligible for indemnity from the first day of security-related shutdowns.
In a related matter, "Israel" Electric Corp. CEO Rafi Peled said yesterday that during times of emergency, "Israel" Electric could encounter a shortage of workers to run the company's power stations because company employees in certain professional and specially skilled jobs are recruited into the army. "Without the proper skilled staff, the electric company cannot run its power stations properly during times of emergency," said Peled at a meeting of the authority on power during times of emergency.

Destruction rained down from the night sky as warplanes pounded power stations leaving smoldering evidence of enemy aggression
Another "Israeli" blitz leaves chain of raging infernos from north to south(13)
By mid-morning the fire had been doused and the smoke smothered, but earlier on Tuesday the interior of the Jamhour power station was an inferno.
Streams of burning oil jetted out of banks of electrical machinery that were on fire, as dense black smoke blotted out the stars above. The air reeked of burning rubber and plastic. The flames reached as high as a five-storey building and bathed the surrounding area in an eerie orange glow.
Firefighters fought a losing battle to contain the blaze, directing jets of water and foam at the inferno. Burning oil poured down a conduit, the flames threatening to trap firemen and reporters. A hose was hurriedly directed toward the drain as choking reporters scurried through the thick black smoke.
One civilian was wounded when the "Israeli" warplanes bombed the plant in the first of two sorties.
A Syrian worker who lived in a small building beside the power station said he heard the jets flying low over Jamhour.
I went outside to listen to them and as I went back into the house, the bombs hit the plant, he said.
Three employees of the Melec engineering company remained in the main building, ignoring the inferno raging around them.
I told them to leave and come to my house nearby but they refused to leave their machines, an engineer with Melec said. He added that the employees had not been injured in the raid.
One of the bombs had blasted a 2-meter deep hole in the slip road leading to the power station. Army bulldozers clanked back and forth, hurriedly filling the hole with rubble and soil to allow the firefighting vehicles through.
Other troops monitored cellular phones and walkie-talkies. Anxious faces constantly turned skywards. Everyone remembered the tragic consequences of the last time Jamhour was bombed in June 1999. Several fireman had been killed when "Israeli" warplanes returned for another sortie just after a firefighting team had entered the burning plant.
After touring the area on Tuesday morning, George Mouawad, director-general of Electricite du Liban, told reporters that 90 percent of the facility had been destroyed.
Mouawad was accompanied by Syrian Electricity Minister Munib Saem Dahr, who inspected the charred site filled with twisted metal. Shattered glass crunched beneath his feet.
A somber Dahr, who stopped to gaze at the ruin before him, pledged that Syrian and Lebanese solidarity would not be broken by this act of barbarism.
Security sources said the plant was hit by at least three bombs ­ one directly striking a row of transfer stations, destroying 11 of the 12.
Another bomb struck the main building, destroying the control center, ripping into concrete and shattering windows.

                                                   Baalback
Try as they might, Tuesday morning is one morning that Caroline, Katrine, Fadia and Karine Jaafar are unlikely to forget.
Fast asleep in their home, some 500 meters across the road from Baalbacks power station, the girls awoke to a deafening roar and a rain of glass and pieces of bedroom furniture as their room was torn apart by the blasts leveling the power station.
We were asleep and suddenly we couldnt see or hear anything, said their mother, Katia. The electricity was gone and it was so dark we couldnt even see each other.
Too frightened to leave the house, Katia grabbed her screaming daughters, aged 10, eight and six, and her son, and made a blind dash for the bathroom at the back of the house where they sat huddled under the staircase. She tried to comfort her children as best she could but the three girls were bleeding from wounds caused by the shower of glass.
It was so dark and we could not get out, she said. The children were screaming.
Katia and her family waited until the roaring outside had died down and then ran for shelter. They headed straight for the house of Katias brother, Ali Jaafar, 1.5 kilometers up the road toward Baalback. Katia remembers nothing of the journey.
When they arrived, they found that their sanctuary too had been damaged. Showing reporters around the remains of his plush carpeted bedroom, strewn with broken glass from the shattered windows, Ali Jaafar, 56, explained what he had seen the night before.
The first rocket didnt break them, he said, pointing to huge cracks running vertically and horizontally across the back and side walls of the room. The second rocket broke the windows.
His sisters home, though structurally intact, suffered far more damage and the interior was a total wreck. Glass, broken furniture and slates from the roof carpeted the floor.
The fields outside the home revealed more savagery of the blast with strewn bits of twisted metal beams, rocket casing and chunks of concrete ripped loose and blasted in all directions.
Compared to this, Ali Jaafar escaped lightly. None of his family were physically injured in the blast and his home will be more easily repaired. He is simultaneously thankful and defiant: We dont need bridges or water or electricity and we want nothing from "Israel", he said with the righteous determination that belongs to the unfairly punished. Weve been at war with "Israel" for many years, were not sorry and we want the resistance to see it to the end.
Lying huddled together under a blanket, the children, though clearly traumatized, appear to remember few specifics.
Theyre still very scared, Katia said pointing toward her eight-year-old lying prone with a bandaged forehead. And Katrine is running a fever.
Under the watchful gaze of her grandmothers, and after some gentle encouragement from her father Hussein, Karine raised her right hand and made the victory sign. Smiling shyly, she ventured the only comment any of the sisters make on the previous nights events: I dont like "Israel".

                                                 Deir Nbouh
 Much of north Lebanon was plunged into total darkness early on Tuesday, after "Israeli" jets pounded the Deir Nbouh power station near Zghorta.
The attack destroyed two transformers, one which transferred electricity from Syria to regions in Akkar, Dinnieh and Zghorta, and another which transferred electricity from the Baddawi plant to Beirut. However, most of Tripolis power supplies survived the blow, as the citys grid is linked to the Deir Ammar and Horeisha plants which were not targeted.
George Mouawad, the director general of Electricite du Liban, indicated that the two small transformers that survived the attack could help provide some electricity to the northern villages, although the rationing would affect Tripoli.
EDL would link a high voltage cable from the Deir Nbouh station to the Deir Ammar station which will distribute electricity to the northern regions.
However, he noted that the Deir Ammar station would not be able to supply electricity to the entire region. He said that maintenance teams had begun repairing the damage, adding that army engineers would clear the rubble to allow for more extensive repair work.
Were studying other alternatives so that well be able to supply all regions with six to eight hours of electricity per day inside and outside Beirut, Mouawad said.
