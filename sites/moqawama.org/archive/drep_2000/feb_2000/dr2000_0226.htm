Daily Report

February Saturday 26-2000:


Introduction

French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin's racist comments took Lebanon and Islamic countries by surprise. Hizbullah blasted Jospin as a racist who harms French interests in the Middle East, adding that "the Lebanese and the arabs, peoples and government, were surprised by the racist position when he legalized the neo-Nazi Zionist terrorism killing children, destroying civilians infrastructure".

Lebanon's Premier Salim Al-Hoss summoned France's ambassador to Beirut to express the country's discontent after Jospin branded Hizbullah resistance attacks in occupied South Lebanon as "terrorism".

In the occupied West Bank, the angry university students threw stones at Jospin on Saturday in protest at his description of the Resistance attacks on "Israeli" troops occupying South Lebanon as "terrorist acts".


Jospin a racist harming French regional interests: Hizbullah(1)

"The Lebanese Hizbullah blasted on Friday French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin as a "racist" who harms French interests in the Middle East.

"The Lebanese and the Arabs, peoples and governments, were surprised by the racist position when he legalized the neo-Nazi Zionist terrorism killing children, destroying civilian infrastructure," said a Hizbullah statement.

"The position of Jospin puts France in contradiction with its interests in Lebanon and the Arab world, and in contradiction with its attempts to build an Arab policy which takes into consideration a French role independent from the American policy," the group said.

Jospin said on Thursday in Jerusalem that "France condemns Hizbullah's attacks, and all types of terrorist attacks which may be carried out against soldiers or possibly "Israel's" civilian population."

"Jospin's position forces France, which is a partner of the April accord at the insistence of Lebanon and Syria, to cover the violation of the agreement contrary to its promises," Hizbullah said.

The April 1996 agreement bans combatants across the Lebanese-"Israeli" borders from harming civilians or launching attacks from civilian areas.

The committee monitoring the agreement is made up of representatives from Lebanon, "Israel", Syria, France and the United States.

"This immoral declaration destroys the bases of the French policy and ... the prime minister has committed an enormous crime to reveal his Jewish relations even if this greatly harms Arab-French relations in a way that can only be erased if France acts promptly to erase this shame," Hizbullah said.

It added said Jospin had expressed "an aggressive position no less cruel than the Zionist crimes or their American cover."

"Jospin's position deeply harms all moral values and international norms, as well as the charter of human rights which makes the glory of France, in order to satisfy his Zionist tendencies... this is a shameful spot in the history of France," it said.


Jospin terror jibe triggers fury in Beirut
Hoss: Dangerous and hard to believe(2)
Franco-Lebanese relations, which had flourished under President Jacques Chirac, plummeted to their lowest ebb on Friday following the French premiers shocking denunciation of Hizbullahs terrorist acts.
Prime Minister Salim Hoss blasted his French counterpart, Lionel Jospin, and summoned Ambassador Philippe Lecourtier, demanding an official explanation. Political sources said Lecourtier would travel to Paris on Saturday for consultations.
Jospin, in an attempt to defuse Lebanons fury, made new remarks to reporters in Jerusalem on Friday in which he sought to separate Frances solid backing of Lebanon and his stance on Hizbullah.
At a news conference in Jerusalem on Thursday night, Jospin said: France condemns Hizbullahs attacks, and all types of terrorist attacks which may be carried out against soldiers or possibly "Israel's" civilian population.
He urged "Israel" to show restraint and avoid harming civilians in retaliation, but further incensed Lebanon by saying he could understand why "Israel" felt the need to respond to attacks he said could not be viewed as acts of resistance.
On Friday, Jospin returned to the subject but, in a clear effort to calm troubled waters, spoke of acts of war in Lebanon without any mention of terrorism.
At a time when negotiations between Syria and "Israel" are getting going with difficulty, I dont think its the right moment for acts of war, Jospin said in his modified stand.
When reminded of his earlier reference to terrorist acts, Jospin said: It doesnt seem to me that those were exactly the words I used.
In any case  France is the friend of Lebanon, is attached to the sovereignty of Lebanon and the integrity of Lebanon. We have always condemned, me first of all, the "Israeli" occupation of south Lebanon. We therefore hope "Israel" will withdraw.
In Paris, Elysee spokeswoman Catherine Colonna said that the French president has ordered Jospin to report to him as soon has he returns from the Middle East. She declined to comment on his remarks in "Israel".
Hoss described Jospins remarks as dangerous and hard to believe.
Was the French land that was occupied by Nazi Germany a security zone and was the French resistance a terrorist act? the premier asked. Since the peoples right to resist occupation is safeguarded by international accords and laws, how can such a heroic act be branded terrorist? If "Israel's" soldiers are to be safe, then the "Israeli" Army must leave Lebanon.
Hoss accused "Israel" of terrorism, saying that it occupies our land, launches daily attacks against villages, destroys the infrastructure, detains many prisoners and threatens to burn Lebanons soil.
The premier was referring to recent warnings by "Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levy that if Hizbullah continued to attack "Israeli" troops, the Jewish state would make Lebanons soil burn.
Hoss said that Lebanon expects France, a friendly country with historic ties to Lebanon, to renew its support for the Lebanese cause and back our right to liberate our land.
Jospins comments are astonishing because they came
while efforts were being made to reconvene the April Understanding Monitoring Group, Hoss added.
Hoss argued that recent resistance attacks did not violate the 1996 understanding, which bans resistance fighters and "Israeli" troops from launching attacks from or at civilian areas. Hizbullah lambasted Jospin as a racist.
Jospins position endangers French interests in Lebanon and the Arab world, and it contradicts its attempts to build an Arab policy independent from American policy, the group said in a statement. This immoral declaration destroys the basis of the French policy.
Hizbullah added that Jospin had expressed an aggressive position no less cruel than the Zionist crimes which America sanctions. Jospins position harms all moral values and international norms, as well as the Charter of Human Rights in order to satisfy his Zionist tendencies. This is a shameful blot on the history of France.
A Hizbullah source questioned the apparent lack of coordination between Jospin and his Foreign Ministry, noting that although Lecourtier, who has been here for only a few days, has not visited Hizbullah, his predecessor, Daniel Jouanneau, enjoyed good relations with Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, the partys secretary-general.
Ambassador Jouanneau held regular meetings with Sayyed Hassan, the source said.
Former Prime Minister Rafik Hariri, a personal friend of Chirac, telephoned the French president to protest about Jospins remarks.
Sheikh Mohammed Mehdi Shamseddine, head of the Higher Shiite Council, said this issue does not deserve anything less than the resignation of the French government. The Lebanese government cannot deal with this prime minister. He is no different from an "Israeli" minister.
He added in his Friday sermon that this is a big issue and the French state and leadership should correct it immediately. This man has incited the "Israelis" to kill us and now hes asking us not to target "Israeli" soldiers. The objective, the honor and the dignity of the resistance is to kill the "Israeli" soldiers.
The Progressive Socialist Party contacted Socialist International, a world grouping of socialist movements, including the PSP and Jospins French Socialist Party, to ask the French prime minister about the motive behind his comments.
The PSP, led by Chouf MP Walid Jumblatt, also asked the group to take a stance regarding "Israel's" aggression against Lebanon, even though such a stance will be a little bit late.
Lecturing in Sidon later on Friday, Jumblatt said that Jospins presidential aspirations for the 2002 elections were behind his hostile remarks.
In Cairo, Arab League Secretary-General Esmat Abdel-Meguid said he was surprised by Jospins comments.
The Lebanese resistance, which defends Lebanons legitimate national rights, is fighting courageously against "Israeli" occupation. That is its legitimate right guaranteed by international law, Abdel-Meguid said.
He likened Hizbullah to the French resistance against the Nazi occupation during World War II.


Students Fire Stones at French Prime Minister(3)
" Angry university students threw stones at French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin on Saturday in protest at his description of guerrilla attacks on "Israeli" troops occupying south Lebanon as ``terrorist acts.''

A news photographer was injured in the protest by several hundred students during Jospin's visit to Bir Zeit University in an area of Ramallah where Palestinians have civil control.

The students called "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak a ''terrorist'' and Jospin his accomplice. It was not immediately clear whether Jospin would later in the day tour the Khan Yunis refugee camp in the Gaza Strip as scheduled.

Jospin, on a visit to "Israel" and the Palestinian-ruled areas, in Jerusalem Thursday night appeared to depart from traditional French thinking by characterizing Hizbullah attacks on "Israeli" soldiers in the south Lebanon occupation zone as ''terrorist acts.''

France has long called for "Israel" to quit the ``security zone'' that it set up in south Lebanon in 1985 with the declared aim of preventing attacks by guerrillas on northern "Israel".


Arab League head "surprised" by French PM's remarks about Hizbullah(4)
" Arab League chief Esmat Abdel Meguid said Friday he was "surprised" that French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin called Lebanese Hizbullah attacks on "Israelis" "terrorist."

"I'm surprised by the French prime minister's comments calling the Lebanese resistance terrorist," Abdel Meguid told reporters.

But Abdel Meguid said "the Lebanese resistance, which defends Lebanon's legitimate national rights, is fighting courageously against "Israeli" occupation. That's its legitimate right guaranteed by international law."

He likened Hizbullah, which carries out most of the attacks on "Israeli" troops occupying a "security zone" in southern Lebanon to "the French resistance against the Nazi occupation" during World War II.

The French resistance, led by Charles de Gaulle, "allowed France to win back its independence and freedom," Abdel Meguid said.

France and the United States chair the international panel monitoring the rules of engagement for "Israel" and Lebanese guerrillas. The rules were decided on in an oral accord by "Israel" and the guerrillas in April 1996.

Abdel Meguid said that the April 1996 understanding "recognizes the legitimacy of the Lebanese resistance."

Egyptian film director Yussef Shaheen also stressed his suppport Hizbullah, saying he was surprised that Arab armies did not help the guerrillas fight "Israeli" troops in south Lebanon, the Arab newspaper Al-Hayat reported.

"How is it that Arab military forces were not made to go to south Lebanon?" he asked.

Hizbullah's "fighters attack "Israeli" occupation forces in Lebanon, and I support them from Cairo with all my might and all my life," he said at a Cairo University conference.


Gulf newspapers take up Hitler theme in attack on "Israel's" Levy(5)
"Gulf newspapers Friday took up the Hitler theme in strong attacks on "Israel's" Foreign Minister David Levy, comparing him to the Nazi dictator for his threats to set Lebanon ablaze in case of guerrilla attacks.

Levy has caused fury in the Arab world for his threats, particularly a warning that if Kiryat Shmona, the main town of northern "Israel", were hit by Hizbullah guerrilla rockets from across the border, "Lebanon will burn, blood for blood, child for child."

One United Arab Emirates daily said there was "no difference between those who burnt people in the gas chambers and who burn children, women and the old with napalm bombing and murderous shelling".

Al-Ittihad added that Levy's statements "proved that Zionism was the descendant of Nazism".

Describing Levy as a "pocket Hitler", another Emirati daily, al-Khaleej also attacked "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehbud Barak for defending his foreign minister as a "moderate voice".

"What is extremism on the "Israeli" scale if burning Lebanon is put forward as moderation? Is it to exterminate three million Lebanese, raze their houses with bulldozers or erase Lebanon from the world map?" asked the paper.

"Levy has embodied Hitler in threatening ... to burn Lebanon if the town of Kyriat Shmona in the north of "Israel" is bombed by Hizbullah," Saudi paper al-Madina said.

"Levy's threats demonstrate that the man has not yet got rid of his ghetto complex and appears to be experiencing nostalgia for the false sense of security that the isolation of ghetto provided," it said of the Moroccan-born "Israeli".

Levy repeated his threat of bloody reprisals against Lebanon on Thursday, despite rising outcry within "Israel" and in Arab countries to the similar comments he made a few days before.

"If their actions continue they will come to harm," he said, referring to Hizbullah. "("Israel's") riposte will be very strong and virulent if by misfortune our citizens are attacked."

Damascus radio Thursday also compared Levy to Hitler, whose regime murdered million of Jews during the Holocaust of the 1940s.


"Israeli" paper says "Israeli"-proxy militiaman discussing relocation to Cyprus(6)

" "Israel's" proxy South Lebanon Army (SLA) is hoping for a possible relocation to Cyprus, if the "Israeli" army withdraws from its security zone in south Lebanon, the "Israeli" daily Yediot Aharanot said Thursday.

According to the paper, the militiamen, fearing Hizbullah reprisals, want to relocate their families into the border zone close to the northern Turkish sector of Cyprus.

Cyprus has been divided since 1974 when Ankara invaded the northern third following a Greek-Cypriot coup in Nicosia seeking union with Athens.

A senior collaborator officer told the paper that his men were awaiting the return from France of their commander, Antoine Lahd "to know what will become of us".

"They prefer to be transferred to a country close to Lebanon, and Cyprus is the favored option for most of the rank and file soldiers," he said, adding under cover of anonymity, that the officers wanted to move to Sweden or Denmark.

"Israeli" officials responsible for organizing a future evacuation of the militiamen denied to the paper that a Cyprus relocation plan existed.


Schoolchildren denounce Levys threats(7)
Schoolchildren and their families from throughout Lebanon protested in front of the UN House on Friday against the fiery comment of "Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levy. Earlier this week Levy threatened to punish Lebanon if Hizbullah attacked northern "Israel".
In his comments on Wednesday, Levy vowed that If Kiryat Shmona burns, Lebanon will burn, blood for blood, soul for soul, child for child.
The schoolchildren held up posters supporting UN Resolution 425, which "Israel" has defied since its occupation of southern Lebanon began in 1978.
We reject Baraks threats to burn Lebanon, said sixth grader Ali Jammoul, 11.
Hazem Beblawi, executive secretary of ESCWA, made an appearance at the sit-in and addressed the children. The UN was founded to defend peace and human rights, he said, especially those of children, the hope of the future.
Miss Lebanon, Norma Naoum, was also at the protest for what she said was a show of support for the children of south Lebanon who live under almost daily "Israeli" bombardments.


Sudan condemns "Israeli" threats against Lebanon(8)

"Sudanese Foreign Minister Mustafa Osman Ismail on Friday condemned "Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levy for making "irresponsible threats" against Lebanon. Ismail told reporters on arrival in Cairo that a decision to move the venue of a March 11 Arab foreign ministers' meeting to Beirut from Cairo reflected Arab support for Lebanon. He said the six-monthly Arab League ministerial meeting would help "direct international attention towards the repeated "Israeli" attacks on (Lebanon's) infrastructure, especially after the irresponsible threats spoken by Levy...which were unfortunately backed by his prime minister (Ehud Barak)". Levy on Wednesday told "Israel's" parliament the soil of Lebanon would "burn" if Hizbullah fired rockets at Kiryat Shmona and other northern "Israeli" settlements. "Israeli" aircraft bombed three Lebanese power stations, wounding 22 civilians, this month in retaliation for Hizbullah resistance attacks that have killed seven "Israeli" occupation soldiers in south Lebanon this year".
