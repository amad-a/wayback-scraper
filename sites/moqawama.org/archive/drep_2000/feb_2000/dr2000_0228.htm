February Monday 28-2000:


Introduction

Hizbullah's Deputy Secretary General H.E. Sheikh Nahim Qassem warned French Premier Jospin not to think of coming to Lebanon without apologizing for the affront he caused to the Lebanese resistance and people.

The Saturday's stone-throwing protesters had shown a "blessed sign of solidarity with the Lebanese people and Hizbullah from young people who refuse to capitulate to the Zionist enemy", he said.

French President Jacque Chirac has reprimanded his prime minister for branding Lebanese resistance operations as "terrorist acts" as relations between the two leaders plunged to a new low.

Premier Lionel Jospin's statements have also provoked fury across the Arab world.

However, an "Israeli" soldier has been sentenced to 28 days in jail for refusing to serve in Lebanon after he reportedly said he was afraid of serving there.


Hizbullah demands apologies from Jospin for "terrorist" remark(1)
" Hizbullah warned French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin not to even contemplate visiting Lebanon before apologizing for his remarks branding its attacks on "Israel" "terrorist".

"Jospin is not wanted in Lebanon and I don't want him to think of visiting this country before apologizing for the affront he caused to the Lebanese resistance and people", Hizbullah deputy leader Sheikh Nahim Qassem said in a statement.

Jospin's remarks at a Jerusalem press conference Thursday caused a storm in the Arab world and led to the French prime minister being stoned Saturday by students at Bir Zeit university in the West Bank.

Qassem said Jospin had hurt the feelings of women, children, the wounded "and all those who suffer from "Israel's" occupation of southern Lebanon.

He hailed the action of the students as "a blessed sign of solidarity with the Lebanese people and Hizbullah from young people who refuse to capitulate to the Zionist enemy."

The general outcry in the region against Jospin proved that the resistance to "Israel's" occupation had solid support, he added.

Lebanese papers devoted most of their front pages Sunday to reports and photographs to the attack on Jospin, which wounded him slightly and seriously damaged his car.

An AFP photographer also had an ankle broken when he was knocked down by one of the vehicles in the prime minister's convoy.

The press highlighted a statement by French President Jacques Chirac's office saying that Jospin's statements were not binding on France, whose policy in the Middle East remained unchanged.

One daily, Al-Diyar, published a commentary by British regional expert Patrick Seal, saying Jospin had been manipulated by "Israel" into making a serious error which proved he knew nothing of the local situation.

The editorialist of al-Anwar, Rafic Khoury, said Jospin's remarks were dangerous in that they went along with those of US officials who wanted to alter the situation in southern Lebanon by halting Hizbullah's actions before peace talks began.

Jospin said Thursday in Jerusalem, "France condemns Hizbullah's attacks, and all types of terrorist attacks which may be carried out against soldiers or possibly "Israel's" civilian population."

Hizbullah, backed by Lebanon, Syria and Iran, leads the fight against "Israel's" 22-year-old occupation of southern Lebanon.

Its killing of five "Israeli" soldiers in the occupied zone prompted "Israeli" air raids earlier this month which knocked out much of Lebanon's power supply and wounded 22 civilians.


Chirac chastises Jospin for insult against Hizbullah
French policy unchanged despite premiers faux pas(2)
"French President Jacques Chirac has reprimanded his prime minister for branding Lebanese resistance operations terrorist acts as relations between the two leaders plunged to a new low.
Premier Lionel Jospins statements, made while visiting "Israel" and the Palestinian territories, have also provoked fury across the Arab world, leading to a confrontation on Saturday with protestors at Bir Zeit.
A statement issued late Saturday from the Elysee Palace indicated that Chirac had telephoned the prime minister to remind him of Frances delicate equilibrium in its support for "Israel" and the Arabs. Putting this impartiality in question would strike a blow to the credibility of our foreign policy and Frances capacity to work toward peace, the statement said.
Chirac reaffirmed the constancy of French foreign policy and the equilibrium that drives our interaction in the Middle East, an interaction that remains unchanged, it said.
The episode constitutes the greatest clash on foreign policy between the two leaders since they began sharing power three years ago.
Jospin has indicated that he would not see Chirac in person until just before a Wednesdays Cabinet meeting, even though the president had summoned him to a meeting on his return Saturday from "Israel".
He cut short his Middle East visit on Saturday after students at the West Banks Bir Zeit University pelted him with stones and eggs.
The French premier was slightly injured and had to retreat to his car as Palestinian students hurled stones following his speech at the university.
He later said that the incident would not affect French-Palestinian relations..
 
In Paris, President Chirac said in a statement that he was shocked by the attack on Jospin and called the incident regrettable.
In Beirut, Speaker Nabih Berri said on Sunday that Jospins comments infringed on President Chiracs prerogatives. Speaking at the airport before leaving for Tehran to preside over the first conference of Islamic parliamentarians, he added: Its a pity that the French premier, obviously to serve trivial personal political interests, has forgotten that Lebanons territory is occupied.
Berri welcomed the protest of Palestinian students on Saturday.
The protest is yet another expression of the growing Arab support for Lebanon, he added. Popular Arab support is needed to back up the efforts made by Arab leaders to curb "Israel's" aggression. But we hope the Arab leaders will not restrict themselves to theoretical support.
For his part, Prime Minister Salim Hoss said on Saturday that any people under occupation have the right to launch resistance attacks.
I was surprised when I heard Mr. Jospins comments, he added, especially given Frances spectacular experience in resisting Nazi occupation.
The Lebanese resistance movement is abiding by the April Understanding, while "Israel" violated it when it attacked three power stations earlier this month, he said. It is not acceptable to describe the Lebanese resistance movement as terrorist, he said, speaking to Egypts Voice of the Arabs radio.
Hoss reacted to Jospins comments on Thursday by summoning French Ambassador Philippe Lecourtier and asking him to convey to his government Lebanons condemnation of Jospins statement.
The Foreign Ministry received assurances from President Chirac that the French foreign policy would not change as a result of Jospins comments, on Saturday, a source at the ministry said. On Sunday, Hizbullahs deputy secretary-general, Sheikh Nahim Qassem, warned Jospin against coming to Lebanon without apologizing first for his comments.
Jospin is not wanted in Lebanon and I hope that he wont think of visiting this country before apologizing
for the affront  to the Lebanese resistance and people, especially the children, the women and everyone who suffered from "Israeli" attacks, he said.
 Former Prime Minister Rafik Hariri, a personal friend of Chiracs, telephoned the French president twice on the weekend to denounce Jospins comments.
A source close to Hariri said that Chirac confirmed that the balanced French policy in the Middle East  will be maintained. Chirac also said that France is keen to maintain its credibility among the regions countries in order to support the efforts to achieve peace.
Meanwhile, in Sidon, Gerard Larcher, the vice president of the French Senate, handed over to the non-governmental Vocational Care Association a mobile library ­ a bus with modern reading facilities that will tour rural areas.
Larcher refused to comment on Jospins statements, saying: It is not proper to comment on the matter now. Ill meet President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss tomorrow (Monday) to convey to them the views of President Chirac and the French people.
He later said that France will remain on Lebanons side, as it did when General Charles de Gaulle was president.
During the event, Social Affairs and Labor Minister Michel Musa did not comment on the matter either, but said that as long as "Israel" is terrorizing our people and destroying our infrastructure, we will continue to follow in the footsteps of Frances resistance movement.
Ambassador Lecourtier also attended the event and told Musa in the presence of reporters that he was sorry for recent developments, including Jospins comments.
In the West Bank town of Nablus on Sunday, Palestinian students set fire to portraits of Jospin and "Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levy at a rally to protest their inflammatory remarks over Lebanon.
About 200 students at the Najah University joined the demonstration. Jospins remarks are racist, said one banner. All voices that attack the resistance should shut up, said another.
Jospins comments followed Levys fiery speech in which he warned that "Israel" would retaliate blood for blood, child for child if Hizbullah attacked "Israel's" northern territory.
Egyptian Foreign Minister Amr Moussa said on Saturday that Egypt appreciates French diplomacy and hopes that (Jospins) statements are not a sign of a change in French policy toward the Middle East.
Jospins remarks are in contradiction with the policy of France in the region, Moussa said, in the first Egyptian reaction to the controversy.
In Syria, the official Al-Baath newspaper blasted Jospins two enormous mistakes, the first in offending the Lebanese resistance and the second in ignoring the racism and terrorism of his "Israeli" hosts.
In Iran, the Sobh-e Emruz daily said that Jospin may have caused irreparable harm to France. It accused the prime minister of pandering to Frances Jewish community in preparation for running in the next French presidential elections against Chirac.


Thousands protest in Lebanon as French PM again criticised in "terrorist" row(3)
"On Sunday thousands of Lebanese and Palestinians, most from the Ain Helweh Palestinian refugee camp, protested in the southern city of Sidon, chanting anti-Jospin slogans and brandishing portraits of Jospin labeled: "Zionist."

"From Bir Zeit to Beirut," they shouted, "the people are not dead."

In a speech in Beirut later Qassem called on the French people to "punish" Jospin, saying he had slandered the whole of the Islamic and Arab world.

Around 200 Palestinian students protested in the West Bank city of Nablus, setting fire to posters of Jospin and "Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levy, who has warned "Israel" will retaliate "child for child" if attacked directly by Hizbullah.

The press throughout the Middle East also kept up attacks on Jospin, whose 72-hour tour of "Israel" and the Palestinian territories which ended Saturday became a diplomatic embarrassment for Paris.

In Syria the official Al-Baath paper blasted Jospin's "two enormous mistakes, the first in offending the Lebanese resistance and the second in ignoring the racism and terrorism of his "Israeli" hosts."

The daily, mouthpiece of the ruling Baath party, said Jospin "should have had the courage to denounce the Nazi declarations of Levy, who threatened to burn the children of Lebanon."

Syrian state radio took a slightly more conciliatory tone, saying that despite the Jospin debacle Damascus "should not overlook France's longtime efforts in the Arab-"Israeli" conflict."

It called on Paris to "take a more active political and diplomatic role to restrain "Israeli" aggression" but added Jospin had "complicated the situation and harmed efforts to bring the "Israelis" back to reason."

Lebanon's ambassador to Algiers, Ahmad Abdallah, said in a newspaper interview that Beirut "firmly believes that these remarks are more campaigning than anything else."

He told the El-Amel paper in Algeria that the minister "greatly fears the Jewish lobby in France."

In Iran, which along with Syria backs the Hizbullah in their campaign to end the nearly 22-year "Israeli" occupation of south Lebanon, the Sobh-e Emruz daily said Jospin may have caused "irreparable" harm to France.

Like other critics it accused Jospin of playing to France's Jewish community with an aim at running in the next French presidential elections against conservative incumbent Jacques Chirac.

The official Al-Ittihad daily in the United Arab Emirates said Jospin had committed "an enormous error," adding it hoped that Chirac would be able to "rectify the matter."

Saudi Arabia's Al-Bilad paper said France would have to "distance itself from Jospin's remarks in order to maintain its good relations with the Arab world."

While many papers said it was clear that Jospin's comments did not reflect official thinking in Paris, the Al-Raya paper in Qatar asked if the French minister was not indicating "the beginning of a change in French policy."

Much of Egypt's press on Sunday cited an interview with a German magazine in which President Hosni Mubarak expressed Cairo's support for Hizbullah militants.

In Jordan the opposition Islamic Action Front issued a statement calling on Paris to "take up a just position which accords with the right of the Palestinian and Lebanese people to fight the ("Israeli") occupation."


Palestinian Students Protest Against Jospin(4)
" Thousands of angry Palestinian students emptied West Bank classrooms Sunday to burn French flags and photographs of French Prime Minister Lionel Jospin in protest at his remarks about Lebanon.

``Jospin, take off the mask and show that you're a Jew-man,'' 3,000 university students in the Palestinian self-rule city of Hebron chanted.

During a visit to Jerusalem Thursday Jospin described Hizbullah guerrilla attacks on "Israeli" soldiers in the south Lebanon occupation zone as ``terrorist acts.''

Waving fake guns, masked members of the Islamic militant group Hamas carried a coffin through the protesters as a symbol of martyrdom in the armed struggle against "Israel".

In the West Bank town of Nablus, hundreds of students shouted ``Down with France, America and "Israel".''

``We reject Jospin's statement about the Lebanese resistance movement and we demand the release of the students detained yesterday and the re-opening of Bir Zeit University,'' student council member Nader Swafeh told Reuters.

Saturday, a mob of students stoned Jospin and his car at Bir Zeit University near Palestinian-ruled Ramallah after he tried to explain his position on Lebanon. More than a dozen students were arrested and the school closed until Tuesday.

Jospin seemed to depart from traditional French policy when he characterized the guerrilla attacks on "Israeli" soldiers in "Israel's" self-declared security zone as acts of terror.

In an attempt to appease the infuriated Arab world, he spoke Friday of ``acts of war'' in Lebanon and omitted any reference to terrorism .

"Israel" jails soldier too scared to go to Lebanon(6)
" An "Israeli" soldier who said he was too scared to return to Lebanon has been jailed for 28 days in an unprecedented ruling by the military, the army said Sunday.

Chief army spokesman Oded Ben-Ami said in a statement announcing the sentence against Aharon Ben Menacheh that the army treated "disobedience very seriously."

But Ben Menacheh's brother criticised the penalty, saying, "Army officials refused to listen to my brother's problems before punishing him."

The morale of the "Israeli" army has taken a severe blow with the death of seven soldiers in Hizbullah guerrilla attacks so far this year in south Lebanon, the last Arab-"Israel" battlefront.

Several men serving across the border have recently defied their superiors to speak out publicly to the media and appeal for them to be brought home now, a plea that has the support of the public according to recent opinion polls.

But Prime Minister Ehud Barak has stuck by his oft-repeated pledge to withdraw the troops who occupy a self-declared "security zone" in south Lebanon by July".


A letter from Beirut(7)

By Ghina

The following was received this week by Ha'aretz:
The following is a forwarded e-mail I received from a friend. It is a letter from a Lebanese citizen who is suffering in the current conflict. I think it provides insight and exemplifies how the Lebanese feel.
Sincerely,
Waseem Bhatti
Jersey City, New Jersey
February 9, 2000
Eighteen years ago, I found myself standing in front of an "Israeli" soldier who was searching my home for weapons. Imagine my poor mom, a terrorist!
Eighteen years ago, I screamed loud and in his face, begging him to leave us alone. I am 26 years old and I feel like screaming in the face of this damned world. Justice, fairness, human rights ... these are all garbage.
The electricity has just been temporarily restored for one hour, so I'm taking this opportunity to write. I don't know when I'll be able to write again, but I want to say that we are not silent, we were never silent; we are trying to defend our country.
People were trying to protect their children when suddenly, in the middle of the night, an angel visited us, using his planes to strike at innocent people. I felt the smell of death today, the smell of war. I spent today covering the windows of my home with sheets and blankets so that we would not feel the cold air coming into our bones. The low-altitude air raids pressured most of the glass of the buildings in the area, and a window fell on my brother, badly injuring him. I gave him two units of blood - is that why I feel that all the letters in the alphabet are the same? Is that the reason why the United States ambassador to Lebanon asked the Lebanese to show restraint?
My brother is in the hospital tonight. Mom is with him and within an hour I will be with him too, on this dark, cold night in Beirut, this Arab city that resisted the "Israeli" army in the invasion of 1982.
I just wanted to send you a salute from a broken human being. It is not that I feel weak. Rather, I am angry, I am furious. Now I know that humans can be treated like garbage when big heads decide so.
This is retaliation? For God's sake, we are defending our home. Isn't that one of the rights that people have?
Beirut was very noisy today. The shops are trying to compensate for their sure losses this month, and their generators make me nervous. I felt that I might end this day in one of the mental hospitals. I am still awake from last night; after two more hours, it will be 24 hours that I have gone without sleep.
We are not silent. We want to protest, but in front of what? The damned United Nations or American embassy? We are the terrorists! That's all people know! This damned world is blind.
Mom was cleaning up all the damned broken glass in the house, while I was trying to buy bags of candles with valentines on them. How thoughtful of this angel Barak, who disguised himself once in the 1980s as a woman and killed a Palestinian leader in the area of Beirut.
In Beirut, the capital of my dark country, there has been no power supply for seven months. The city has suffered damage amounting to millions of dollars.
They told me that I should have some juice after giving my brother some blood, but I cannot imagine having any kind of food. I do not want to eat, for God's sake. I want justice. Is that too much to ask? Please, anyone who is out there - are you listening?
Is that the smell of death or am I feeling cold?
I will spend this night alone and the cold air will be celebrating my birthday with me. I am angry and I feel that I can scream so loud. This damned cold wind is getting stronger, but I have no glass shield to keep it from coming inside. No one is thinking of replacing glass because who knows when "Israel" will want to defend "its right to occupy us"!!
I have to send this to you now, for I will lose power in minutes, both from the power supply and the power of my fingers. I am cold ... maybe if I felt that you were reading me, reading this, then I would feel warm.
This is your correspondent from Beirut and Lebanon! Ghina.


SLA* still in denial on IDF leaving southern Lebanon ..(8)
 More import to them - Hizbullah will lead a hostile attitude to the SLA and will strike against those cooperating with "Israel".
 The tone of SLA members is becoming sharper as the phrase "withdrawal following an agreement" is being uttered with increasing lack of conviction by Barak and his cabinet.
But despite the worries, the IDF** believes that the residents have not reached a "decision-making crossroads," regarding their next move. As long as they see a chance that the IDF withdrawal will follow an agreement, many continue to view cooperation with "Israel" as advantageous.
In view of this, they are in no hurry to abandon their homes. This is a fateful decision, and it is still early. For the time being, it is difficult to give up possessions and income in either the security zone or in "Israel", at a time when Hizbullah can only offer immunity (temporary and uncertain) following the withdrawal.
A man with 20 years of SLA service will not rush to risk his benefits and pension. Paradoxically, many SLA men for a breakthrough in talks with Syria. For years they opposed an "Israeli" withdrawal, but in their current view a withdrawal following an agreement is the best of several worse alternatives.
For its part, the Beirut government has intensified its pressure on the residents of southern Lebanon and intelligence officers are busy trying to recruit among them.
 The killing of SLA deputy commander Akl Hashem a month ago was considered a turning point. Senior members of the SLA are seriously concerned for their personal safety. They move about in as much secrecy as possible, using diversionary routes and telling few of their plans.
"If they got to Akl, who had protection, they can get to us," they tell their IDF counterparts. The limited "Israeli" response to Hashem's killing (a failed attempt on the life of a senior Hizbullah member, Ibrahim Akil, in early February), did not impress them.
""Israel" does not know how to avenge and does not understand the Middle East," SLA officers said again as they did after a Hizbullah bomb killed Brigadier General Erez Gerstein two years ago.
What did please the SLA troops were the threats leveled by Foreign Minister David Levy last week that pledged to burn Lebanon in retaliation for attacks on "Israel". "I am willing to kiss David Levy," said a senior SLA man at his battalion HQ at Hasbaya last week. In southern Lebanon, Levy's words are regarded as an expression of reliability and force, regardless of what lies behind them.
Unlike the IDF, which is making intensive preparations for the withdrawal, the SLA is making none. But its men have many requests. Foremost among these is the cancellation of long prison sentences, passed against them in absentia by the authorities in Beirut.
More than 10,000 such sentences have been handed out by Lebanese courts.

The great majority want to stay in the country. "We did not sacrifice so much here for so many years to run away to Canada or Switzerland," they say. Apart from some among the senior officers of the SLA and the security services, few will even consider leaving.
Economics (foreign bank accounts) and personal safety (liable to become targets for revenge attacks) are the main incentives for leaving. There is no substantive movement to set up villages of refuge inside "Israel". Open discussion of compensation is not heard yet, although all are expecting "retirement" packages.
Matching Barak's hints, the key date for southern Lebanese is the beginning of May. When it becomes absolutely clear that "Israel" is indeed pulling out, each will worry about his own. Some will flee to the north or abroad, others will try to purge themselves in the eyes of Hizbullah.
Of course, there are those who will continue to cooperate with "Israel" until the very last minute. The risks for IDF soldiers will increase accordingly. In the public debate on the issue of a unilateral "Israeli" withdrawal from southern Lebanon, one crucial unknown is not being mentioned: How will the South Lebanon Army (SLA) conduct itself in the coming months?
The importance of this in determining the future situation in southern Lebanon cannot be overstated. The abandoning of outposts en masse - or worse, the turning of some SLA men against "Israel" - will require a sharp change in IDF's strategy in southern Lebanon. Currently this is based on "freezing" the situation and holding out against Hizbullah attacks until decisions are reached at the political level. "A dramatic change in the way the SLA conducts itself will also force the IDF to reconsider the SLA's bitter opposition to a unilateral withdrawal," says a senior officer at the General Staff.
"The SLA withdrawal from Sujud earlier this month was, for the present, an unusual event. It was done, under instructions from above, to avoid the disorderly withdrawal of SLA men under heavy Hizbullah shelling (and especially while Hizbullah cameramen were filming).
However, during the last few weeks signs of concern and uncertainty are increasing in south Lebanon.
"Residents in the security zone, not directly linked with us, are beginning to distance themselves from the IDF and the SLA," says an "Israeli" officer with many years of service in southern Lebanon. "In the south people are beginning to grasp that Prime Minister Ehud Barak means what he says and that it looks like the IDF is pulling out in July, even without an agreement."
Since last December's renewal of negotiations between "Israel" and Syria, the withdrawal scenario has become a reality for residents of southern Lebanon. In talks with IDF officers, both civilians and SLA members express concern and bitterness. "Who will take care of us, who will protect us? What will happen to the families of dead or injured SLA soldiers who were hurt because of you? We did not fight on your side for 23 years for this." "Israelis" are having a difficult time convincing their allies there is no cause for concern.
In southern Lebanon, a unilateral IDF withdrawal will be portrayed as a resounding victory for Hizbullah.
