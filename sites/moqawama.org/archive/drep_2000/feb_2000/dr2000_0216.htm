Daily Report

February Wednesday 16-2000:


Introduction

As a reaction against USA's support positions for "Israel" in its aggression against Lebanon last week, student protestors, chanting anti-US slogans, gathered at the Lebanese University - Saida branch, calling for the expulsion of US Ambassador Satterfield from Lebanon.

Meanwhile, the "Israeli" occupying troops renewed their aggressions on the Lebanese areas as the enemy raided and bombarded Western Beqaa and Iqleem al-Toffah.

However, the Week of the Islamic Resistance (IR) will start today with a series of activities, conferences and symposiums that will go on for a complete week.


Students protest US support from "Israel"(1)
"Responding to MPs calls for reactions from the countrys youth, Lebanese University students in Sidon demonstrated on Tuesday to protest the US governments sanctioning of the latest "Israeli" raids in Lebanon.
Representing parties from across the entire political spectrum, the students waved Arabic and English banners calling for the ousting of US Ambassador David Satterfield.
Kick out Satterfield and close down "Israel's" den of spies, they shouted, referring to the US Embassy.
Demonstrators then gathered on campus to raise a life-size statue of US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright with a sign above her head that read: The United States is the mother of world terrorism. On Monday, a paper effigy of Albright, blood dripping from her mouth and her features contorted, was erected in Sidons Nijmeh Square.
Albright had ruffled Beiruts feathers by accusing Hizbullah of provoking "Israel's" military onslaught, which wrecked three power plants and wounded more than 20 people.
The protesters called on the government of Prime Minister Salim Hoss to deliver a harshly worded letter to the United States to spell out Lebanons outrage over "Israel's" latest military action in Lebanon.
The students also called on Lebanons audiovisual and print media to boycott the coverage of US-sponsored events.
Meanwhile, the General Labor Confederation held an urgent meeting Tuesday to discuss actions to protest the "Israeli" attacks. In a statement issued after the meeting, the GLC announced that it was organizing a large-scale demonstration at 2pm on Friday at the Barbir Square. Protesters will march toward UN House in Riad Solh.
Students at the American University of Beirut will hold a demonstration Wednesday at noon in front of West Hall.


Shammas takes stand for Hizbullah(2)
"Maverick Beirut MP Jamil Shammas called for the creation of a Holy Trinity party on Tuesday to mobilize Christian resistance fighters in support of Hizbullah. I call in my loudest voice for the creation of such a party so that we can fight together as Muslims and Christians to oust the enemy, he said.
"Shammas also demanded the creation of an Arab lobby to boycott US-made products and companies.
In a fiery statement, Shammas slammed "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak, describing him as the Fuhrer of neo-Nazism who died in Germany and reincarnated in "Israel".


IR Week in Lebanon: activities and symposiums(3)
"The Week of the Islamic Resistance starts tomorrow (Wednesday) with a series of activities, conferences and symposiums that go on for a complete week.

Minister of Education Mohammed Youssef Baydoun called on the Lebanese universities and schools to dedicate a whole class on Thursday to discuss the resistance, its role and heroic acts.

"As the heroic Resistance proceeds with performing the duty of liberating our precious soil; due to its pioneer role, the large sacrifices it is offering for the sake of the homeland, the martyrs it is presenting, and its huge offering that enhances the steadfastness of the Lebanese people; and out of the need for supporting it with all available means, we (the Education Minister) ask all educational officials to give the topic of supporting the Resistance, talking about Resistance, and explaining its role and actions a whole school class on Thursday, February 17, 2000," a circular issued by Minister Baydoun read.


Katyushas have a course of their own(4)
"We parked the Land Rover in the upper-class Christian Ashrafiya neighborhood and wondered in our hearts about how they would receive us. Suddenly an old man came out of one of the stores and shouted to us, 'Hizbullah, Hizbullah.' We were sure that we would immediately be told to leave. But the man smiled, and right away he was joined by other people, Christians, who shouted cries of encouragement and support to us. We knew then that we'd won.".This story was related by a Hizbullah fighter to journalist Hala Jaber, who wrote a book about Hizbullah. Perhaps it characterizes the great change that has swept across the Lebanese population and affected Hizbullah's status.
The incident dates from the days of Operation Grapes of Wrath, in April 1996, before the landing of the shell that killed approximately 100 people seeking shelter in a Unifil camp in Kafr Kana. Beirut was flooded with some 500,000 Lebanese refugees who were fleeing "Israeli" attacks; southern villages were evacuated, as was the city of Tyre, for the first time.
The "Israeli" strategy, which had already been used in Operation Accountability, relied on the outdated view that the expulsion of villagers to the capital city would put pressure on the Lebanese government, which would in turn pressure Syria to calm Hizbullah. The IDF* assured the public that this time it intended to get rid of the threat of katyushas once and for all. The "Israeli" government at the time had a grander ambition, to use the military operation to obtain better understandings than those obtained after Operation Accountability in 1993, but primarily, to use it as political lever that would enable it to withdraw from Lebanon.
Messages the "Israelis" sent via the Americans to Lebanon and Syria specifically stated that if after the operation, quiet prevails for nine months, it will then be possible to discuss an IDF withdrawal from Lebanon.
However, things developed differently. This brutal operation, with 650 sorties and around 25,000 mortar shells fired during its first week, created a Lebanon different from what the prime minister's advisers recognized. Hizbullah's information department and press office worked around the clock, operated radio and television stations and broadcast live the horrors of the war. At the same time, the Lebanese newspapers published announcements calling on citizens to contribute to and volunteer for the organization. "You can contribute toward the purchase of a bullet, Katyusha or missile. You can volunteer for us or purchase uniforms for fighters, you can donate money to the Hizbullah fund or funds supporting it," the ads and radio announcements said, making it sound like the Shirutrom telethon to raise funds for the "Israeli" army. One Lebanese woman donated tens of thousands of dollars, "but only for the purchase of katyushas that should be fired in my name against "Israel"." Another citizen wanted to donate $25,000 to buy katyushas, but imposed conditions that Hizbullah could not accept. "He wanted us to fire the katyushas every day in volleys of five," a Hizbullah commander told the Lebanese journalist. "We couldn't accept this condition because according to our rules, the enemy can't know how many rockets will be fired at him and what the scale of an attack will be."
But thanks to Operation Grapes of Wrath, Hizbullah was able to capture more than just the hearts of the Lebanese public in all its variety. The political echelon, and especially the prime minister at the time, Rafik Hariri, who prior to the operation referred to the organization as "a problem" and explained that Hizbullah might become a burden, changed their colors and adopted Hizbullah as a legitimate national resistance organization. Syria's policy also changed. In general Syria is, and was, in the habit of telling Hizbullah what it could not do, but did not get involved in the details of the organization's operations or activities, beyond the red lines it had established.
Around the time of the operation, Shimon Peres announced his decision to move up the elections from September to May, and Damascus saw that as an "Israeli" attempt to ruin the negotiations with it, because moving up elections, after all, means there is no negotiation partner until the election results are known.
Hizbullah's attacks in March 1996 could therefore, from Syria's perspective, serve perhaps as a necessary catalyst for negotiations with "Israel" because Syria then faced a complicated situation. Jordan and Egypt had already signed peace agreements with "Israel"; Turkey, two months prior to the operation, had signed with "Israel" what was to Syria a threatening military cooperation agreement, the negotiations with the Palestinians, although frozen due to the series of terrorist attacks, had not been canceled and representatives of the Gulf states were at the time in the midst of a modest flirtation with "Israel".
Syria was isolated and the strength of Operation Grapes of Wrath made it suspect that Lebanon might collapse under the pressure and undermine Syria's control there. Assad could not know that by the end of that week, he would be the real winner.
From "Israel's" perspective, the operation was not only an attempt to realize its hope for a new military order in Lebanon, but also a political necessity in order to restore the popularity of Shimon Peres, which had dissipated due to Hamas attacks. The "Israeli" public demanded a response to both the terrorist bombings and Hizbullah attacks. On March 4, 1996, Hizbullah killed five soldiers in the security zone. "Israel" did not respond because at the time, the Sharm el-Sheikh anti-terrorism conference was in progress. On March 20, Hizbullah killed another "Israeli" soldier. On March 30, Hizbullah fired katyushas on Kiryat Shmona after "Israel" attacked a Shi'ite village. After a Lebanese boy was killed in Baraasheit by an "Israeli" mine, Hizbullah fired katyushas at Kiryat Shmona, injuring six residents. The government could no longer withstand the public's calls and on April 11, it launched the operation that finally ended the understandings reached after Operation Accountability.
"Israel" was certain it was going to obtain a new set of understandings. U.S. President Bill Clinton, who was visiting Tokyo and Moscow at the time, urgently dispatched Secretary of State Warren Christopher and Dennis Ross. Peres announced that it was "still too early to conduct negotiations." After a few days, the U.S. relayed to Syria a message with "Israel's" conditions for negotiations. These included the disarming of Hizbullah, no attacks against IDF forces in the security zone, Syrian guarantees of quiet in the area, and if it lasted nine months, "Israel" would discuss a comprehensive withdrawal from Lebanon.
Syria rejected the conditions outright and was especially angry at Washington for not including UN Resolution 425, which called for an unconditional "Israeli" withdrawal from Lebanon.
At the same time, Syria and Lebanon received encouragement from France. Jacques Chirac, the first head of state to visit Lebanon since the end of the civil war, compared Hizbullah to the World War II French Resistance. His foreign minister, Herver? de Charrette, tried his hand at independent mediation and failed. "Israel" was in the midst of the euphoria of political and military victory and awaited a Lebanese and Syrian capitulation.
All along, Hizbullah did not stop firing katyushas at "Israel". Then on April 18, one "Israeli" artillery shell altered the military and political front. The Kana shell and some 100 casualties quickly became part of the Lebanese ethos. The United States became a target of Arab criticism; Egypt, Jordan and the Gulf states denounced its support of "Israel", and pro-American feeling from the Gulf War rapidly disappeared. "Israel" also sought a quick escape. The American pressure prevailed and the promise that this battle would be precise and sophisticated crumbled in Kana. "Israel" was transformed into the party responsible for a human tragedy.
Peres, who had refused to conduct early negotiations, announced after Kana, that "it is possible to conduct negotiations also not under fire, we are not obligated to fire." "Israel's" ambitions were shattered one by one. The demand to disarm Hizbullah was no longer mentioned, Syria became a protector and respected mediator, and not a responsible party, and instead of the written commitment "Israel" obtained from Hizbullah in Operation Accountability, there were only unsigned understandings that would be overseen by an impotent committee.
Hizbullah became a full partner in the political process and now had support and complete legitimacy throughout Lebanon; and the northern communities realized within two weeks that the threat of katyushas had not passed.
"The Grapes of Wrath understandings, at best, remain a yardstick for periodically checking the level of escalation. They have not managed to protect the lives of IDF soldiers or of Lebanese citizens and since then, the shelters in northern communities have been used frequently."


"Israeli" Aggressions(5)

In its statement, the IR mentioned the following "Israeli" aggressions:

Between 14:45 and 14:55, the perimeters of Majdal Zoun village came under fourteen 155mm missiles.

Between 15:45 and 18:40, Slouqi Valley was bombarded by about one hundred and three 155mm missiles.

19:10, three 155mm missiles hit the peripheries of Jibaa town.

22:00, four 160mm missiles struck north of Zillaya village.

Between 23:15 and 23:40, Habboush River watercourse was pounded by fifteen 120mm and 155mm missiles and a hail of 12.7mm heavy machine gun bullets.

23:35, the perimeters of Kfar Romman town came under seven 81mm rounds of a volley of 12.7mm heavy machine gun bullets.


Military Operations(6)

The IR team of martyrs Hassan Abdel-Aala Qassir and Assi Mohammad Zeineddine attacked, at 11:25, Bir Kallab outpost with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades destroying the northern rampart and inflicting a number of casualties.

Fifteen minutes later, the IR squad of martyr Hassan Trabolssi reiterated targeting Bir Kallab outpost destroying a 12.7mm heavy machine gun as it was firing at Loweizeh village.

Also, the IR group of martyrs Talal Abdel-Rassoul Nasser and Hussein Suleiman Chaitou attacked fiercely, at 14:35, an enemy force as it was beefing up the "Israeli" post of Radar. Using the proper weapons, the mujahedeen managed to inflict several casualties, whereas the smoke columns were seen emanating out of it.
