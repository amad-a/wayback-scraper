Daily Report

February Monday 14-2000:

 

Mother of latest "Israeli" soldier to be killed in south begs for Barak to be the last casualty
Hoss: Resistance is a legitimate right(1)
"Marking the end of the most violent week in eight months in south Lebanon, "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak gave himself until April on Sunday to make a decision on whether to withdraw from the region.
Barak was addressing his Cabinet during its weekly meeting as the mother of a soldier who was killed on Friday begged him to make her sons death the last "Israeli" casualty in Lebanon.
Barak reportedly said that although he understood the fears of "Israeli" soldiers who wanted to come home safely, it would take two or three months to explore every chance of achieving a deal with Damascus.
Benjamin Ben-Eliezer, "Israel's" communications minister and an aide to Barak, said on Sunday that if it proved impossible to secure an agreement with Syria, "Israel" would withdraw without one, perhaps by as early as April.
If the political echelon decides on a unilateral withdrawal in April, the army will be ready in May to defend the northern border, he said.
Barak has promised to withdraw by July, preferring to do so through an agreement with Syria but not ruling out a unilateral pullout.
Opinion polls in "Israel" in recent days have shown growing popular support to act ahead of Baraks July 7 target date for withdrawing the soldiers stationed in the south.
Meanwhile, Prime Minister Salim Hoss said on Sunday that had he asked US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright to rein in "Israel's" attacks against Lebanon. Albright has blamed Hizbullah for the flare-up.
My latest action was to send a letter to Albright after the threats by Barak against Lebanon, Hoss said in a radio interview, during which he listed his latest diplomatic efforts.
The premier reiterated the resistances right to launch attacks against "Israeli" military targets, adding that it did not violate the 1996 April Understanding. Under the unwritten 1996 Grapes of Wrath accord, both sides agreed to refrain from launching attacks from civilian areas or from targeting civilians.
The resistance to the occupation is a legitimate right for any people whose land falls under occupation, he said.
He said that Lebanon abided by the April Understanding and rejected attempts to violate it.
Hoss noted that his recent meetings with US, French and other diplomats as part of Beiruts drive to seek international support for pressuring "Israel".
On Saturday, Hoss contacted Syrian Foreign minister Farouk al-Sharaa, with whom he discussed the "Israeli" delegations walkout from the Monitoring Group meeting in Naqoura on Friday.
Both men reportedly expressed dismay over comments and reports by US officials and journalists which had portrayed the resistance as the aggressors.
Hoss also contacted the secretary-general of the Arab League, Esmat Abdel-Meguid, who was quoted as telling him that whatever affects Lebanon affects all Arabs.
President Emile Lahoud received a telephone call from Abdel-Meguid on Sunday, in which the Egyptian reiterated his full support.
"Meanwhile, a senior US official admitted on Sunday that last weeks "Israeli" air raids on power facilities had violated the April Understanding.
US Assistant Secretary of State Edward Walker, in an interview with Qatars satellite TV channel Al-Jazeera, said that this amounts without a doubt to a violation of the April (1996) arrangements.

Barak: "Israel" would not stage a pullout "with our tail between out legs"
"Israeli" killed soldier's father: "Israelis" are just cannon fodder(2)

" Amid growing ministerial calls for an immediate Lebanon withdrawal, Prime Minister Ehud Barak yesterday admonished cabinet members for not backing the IDF* - and in a message to Hizbullah, said "Israel" would not stage a pullout "with our tail between our legs."

At the weekly cabinet meeting, Barak reportedly warned of a heavy price still to pay both in southern Lebanon and possibly at home over the next few months.

In Yavne yesterday, hundreds of people attended the funeral of Sgt. Tzahi Itah, of the Givati Brigade's engineering unit, who was killed in a Hizbullah missile attack on the IDF's Beaufort Castle outpost in the eastern sector of the security zone on Friday.

Itah's father, Lt.-Col. (res.) Arye Itah, who helped to resurrect the Givati Brigade in which Tzahi served, eulogized his son. He laid his own medal for bravery on the coffin, saying he wished he had been able to give it to his son personally; instead it would remain with him forever.

Prior to the funeral, at the military section of the Yavne Cemetery, Itah made an impassioned plea to Barak to pull IDF troops out of the security zone as soon as possible.

"Ehud Barak, of whose military capabilities as chief of General Staff, a commander, and as a great strategist I am well aware, has laid down a timetable. But I'm also aware that he won't be able to comply with that timetable and that he has to shorten it, and I call on him, before I bury my son, to pull the others out of there.

"There is no reason why any other boys should fall there. They are just cannon fodder... I call and plead with him in an educated manner, despite the grief and pain I feel now, to pull the troops out of there."

But Barak made it absolutely clear he wants "Israel" to call the shots and that is the reason he is insisting on an agreement on the Lebanon front no later than July. "Israel" has been stuck in a vicious cycle over the years when it was attacked in southern Lebanon or in the northern settlements, Barak reportedly told the cabinet".


In east Jerusalem, Hizbullah's lethal attacks evoke admiration(3)
"It is an adage that may be as old as politics itself: Where you sit determines where you stand.

As members of a nation with first-hand experience of "Israeli" occupation and as spectators to a peace process that seems to be going nowhere, east Jerusalem residents yesterday voiced admiration for Hizbullah's recent spate of lethal attacks against the IDF in Lebanon.

In west Jerusalem, media descriptions often refer to Hizbullah's fighters as terrorists. But in Abu Rajih's coffee house, near Salah a-Din Street, patrons tend to view them as warriors against "Israeli" occupation in a battle of right versus wrong.

Between nargila puffs of Ajami tobacco grown near Nablus, the middle-aged customers stressed that during the latest round of violence, Hizbullah has struck only at military targets.

"The Hizbullah fighter is fighting for his freedom," said a man who owns a farm in the West Bank.

The man added that he is pleased when he hears reports of IDF soldiers being killed in Hizbullah attacks. "I'm happy because the Lebanese are occupied just like I am. Jerusalem is occupied. If I wasn't occupied, I wouldn't be happy about it. We don't want human beings to be killed. We want peace and pray for peace but the peace is as far away as the moon. "Israel" understands only weapons and war," he said.

Another man, visiting from the United States where he now resides, added: "It's not a question of being happy. It is the right of every citizen whose land is occupied to resist that occupation. The success of Hizbullah is the success of this right."

The same man discounted the condemnation of Hizbullah attacks put forward by US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright, who stressed in remarks over the weekend that "Israel" has made clear it intends to pull out of southern Lebanon. "When? When will "Israel" withdraw?" the man asked. "It's all talk. We don't trust anybody. They should move out this minute."

A photographer, aged 50, added: "We are opposing war. I'm not happy when I hear that soldiers die. But I don't know what "Israel" is doing there. In the south of Lebanon, there are boys and girls who get killed by "Israeli" weapons. So what should Hizbullah do?"

Jerusalem Media and Communications Center director Ghassan Khatib said satisfaction over Hizbullah attacks against the IDF is widespread among the Palestinian public.

"This attitude is a result of the very negative feeling towards "Israel" among the public, along with growing frustration and humiliation that "Israel" is not giving any regard to agreements and to [the Palestinian] leadership. So when someone hurts the "Israelis", people feel this is language the "Israelis" can understand," Khatib said.

Hamas leader Mahmoud Zahar said yesterday that Hizbullah attacks against the IDF underscore his organization's point that armed resistance is the way to deal with "Israel". "We are looking for the liberation of Lebanon, and that will be a good success," Zahar said. "It will help the others to believe that without resistance we are wasting our time."
