Daily Report

February Thursday 10-2000:


Introduction

Hizbullah announced that after the "Israeli" enemy's failure in targeting one of the Islamic Resistance's (IR) cadres and the "Israeli" heavy losses among its occupation troops, the IR's heroes have foiled the attempt of protecting the "Israeli" soldiers and their agents in its cradle as they were able on Tuesday to kill two Zionist soldiers and wounding others in an attack on one of the fortified ramparts of Al-Dabsheh outpost.

Meanwhile, foreign diplomats in Beirut suggested that Hizbullah had gained an enhanced reputation from the latest confrontation with "Israel" by refraining from launching Katyusha rockets across the border. "Hizbullah had been very wise to confine its operations to South", the diplomats added.

However, Lebanon had called for an urgent meeting of the Five-Nation Monitoring Group to discuss the "Israeli" air aggressions on civilian targets.

Laurent Rapin, France's delegate to the Monitoring Group, said "Israel" could not unilaterally change the April Understanding. The panel, he said, functions on the basis of decisions which must be made unanimously".


We reserve the right to retaliate, Hizbullah(1)
"Following the tough and successive blows that were inflicted on the Zionist enemy and its collaborators last week; after the enemy's failure in targeting one of the Islamic Resistance's (IR) cadres and violating the April Understanding in this attack; and following the scandalous footage which showed the Zionist troops' low morale and poor level of performance against IR's attack on the occupation troops in Blat, Ehud Barak and the Zionist security cabinet decided to escape the quagmire by launching a new aggression that targeted power plants in Jamhour, Baalback, and Tripoli," a Hizbullah communiqué read.

The enemy's air aggression aimed to achieve one declared objective: protecting the Zionist troops and their collaborators, as was declared by Barak himself who thought that he would be able to subdue the Lebanese and hold them responsible for protecting his occupation besides working on establishing a new equation based on not attacking the occupation troops and its collaborators in exchange for not bombarding civilian infrastructures, Hizbullah said.

"IR's heroes, who know the Zionist enemy very well, have foiled the attempt in its cradle as they were able today (Tuesday) to kill two Zionist soldiers and wounding others in an attack on one of the fortified ramparts of al-Dabsheh outpost besides targeting Zionist forces in the Bir Kallab outpost and inflicting definite casualties."

IR reaffirms its insistence and determination on confronting and pursuing the Zionists and their agents along with continuing the operations against them until they leave our soil defeated and without any conditions or gains, Hizbullah said.

"IR considers the "Israelis"' targeting of power plants in Lebanese territories was a blatant violation of the April Understanding for which IR still reserves the full right to retaliate at the proper timing and in the form IR chooses."

The Zionist enemy has to submit to this understanding and avoid civilians and civilian infrastructures. It must also not forget that IR's hand is over the hands of its troops and collaborators, the Hizbullah communiqué concluded.


Hizbullah gains moral high ground(2)
"Amid indications that tension in the south is easing after some of the worst violence in months, foreign diplomats in Beirut suggested on Wednesday that Hizbullah has gained an enhanced reputation from the latest confrontation with "Israel" by refraining from launching Katyusha rockets across the border.
My impression is that "Israel" has been acting outside the April Understanding and Hizbullah has been acting within the understanding, said a European diplomat. Hizbullah has gained the moral high ground by acting with restraint. I think someone needs to stand up and say this on the record.
Another diplomat said: All foreign diplomats I have spoken to are in agreement that Hizbullah has been very wise to confine its operations to the south.
"Israel's" devastating aerial assault on Lebanons power grid backfired, diplomats say, while Hizbullah gained the upper hand.
Hizbullahs threat to retaliate was alone sufficient to achieve complete paralysis in northern "Israel" as the entire population of 300,000 civilians was ordered to flee or sit tight in bomb shelters. Around 80 percent of Kiryat Shmonas population was reported to have left by Wednesday, with economic losses estimated at $2.4 million per day. All this without a single Katyusha rocket crossing the border.
Hizbullah did a better job by not firing the Katyushas and ironically I think the "Israelis" are very upset by this unexpected restraint, said a Western military source.
"Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levys bloodcurdling threat on Wednesday that if Katyushas cross the border Lebanons vital interests will go up in flames sounded particularly hollow. It has become evident now that Hizbullah has no intention of granting "Israel" justification for further air assaults and, instead, is content to retaliate by killing "Israeli" soldiers in the occupation zone.
Some form of "Israeli" military response to the surge of casualties in the south was anticipated, but few expected "Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak to target Lebanons infrastructure. With three power stations lying in ruins and the "Israeli" war machine poised to strike again, Hizbullahs decision ­ presumably encouraged by Syria ­ to refrain from a Katyusha barrage was well-judged.
There was, in fact, only one appropriate and viable response that could restore the upper hand to Hizbullah. It came at 2.50pm on Tuesday, 13 hours after the air raids began, when an anti-tank missile fired by Hizbullah fighters struck an observation turret in the Dabsheh outpost above Nabatieh, killing an "Israel" soldier.
Barak was left in an awkward position. He had justified the air strikes on Lebanons power plants by claiming that Hizbullah has continually broken the understanding by launching attacks from populated areas.
Although both sides routinely flout the understanding, Hizbullahs four attacks since Jan. 25, in which six soldiers were killed, provoking "Israel's" retaliation, were all conducted within the rules.
Hitting Lebanese infrastructure was a decision the "Israeli" premier might have regretted on Tuesday afternoon when he was informed that another soldier had been killed in south Lebanon. Barak appeared to have exhausted his political currency with Mondays air raids against the power plants. Battering Lebanons infrastructure for a second night in a row risked angering the international community, including the US, particularly as Hizbullahs deadly attack on Dabsheh had once again remained within the rules.
Instead, Barak pursued the softer option of retaliating with a helicopter gunship strike against a Hizbullah communications center in Tyre.
By Wednesday morning, "Israeli" warplanes were back to hitting regular, everyday targets in frontline areas.
The conclusions to be drawn from the escalation ­ if it really is over ­ are three-fold.
First, it is evident that it will take some major civilian casualties in Lebanon for Hizbullah to seriously consider firing Katyushas across the border. Clearly, Syria is coordinating closely with Hizbullah on this issue and remains unwilling to risk a complete collapse of peace prospects with "Israel".
The second point is that Syria will not interfere with Hizbullahs campaign against the occupation forces ­ so long as the fighting is contained within Lebanon. Growing calls in "Israel" for Barak to demand a ceasefire in the south as a condition for resuming peace talks are likely to be ignored by Syria.
Thirdly, Hizbullah is taking ­ and will continue to take ­ full advantage of this leeway. The fighters have received TOW anti-tank missiles, with which they have successfully exploited a chink in the armor of "Israel's" heavily-fortified compounds in the south. TOWs were used in both Tuesdays attack on Dabsheh and the Jan 31. attack on the Izzieh position in which three soldiers were killed. The US-made missile has a range of 3,750 meters, more than 1 kilometer further than the Soviet Sagger rocket traditionally employed by Hizbullah. The TOWs improved range and superior accuracy over the Sagger has allowed the resistance fighters to guide the missiles directly into the slit windows of observation posts in "Israeli" positions, killing and wounding anyone inside.
Hizbullahs adoption and skilled use of the TOW has had, and will continue to have, serious ramifications for the "Israeli" Army during its final months in the occupation zone. This suggests that the south is set for further violence in the coming weeks as the pattern that defines the conflict continues its cyclical nature of escalation and lull.


"Israel" goes back on its threat to annul truce panel; truce group's co-chairman tells FTV "Israel" breached accord by attacking civilian targets(3)
""Israel" has lodged a complaint with the international panel that monitors a four-year-old truce in south Lebanon, despite earlier suggestions by a cabinet minister that it no longer accepted the accord. "Israel" accused the resistance of attacking occupation posts from populated areas in Nabatieh. Lebanon had called for an urgent meeting of the panel to discuss "Israeli" raids on civilian targets. The co-chairman of the five-nation panel, France's Laurent Rapin told Future Television the group is expected to meet tomorrow. Rapin said "Israel" breached the 1996 accord by deliberately attacking civilian targets.

" The French Ambassador to the five nation cease-fire panel in southern Lebanon, Laurent Rapin, said in an exclusive interview with Future Television that "Israel" is expected to take part in a cease-fire meeting tomorrow. "Israeli" Cabinet minister Haim Ramon who yesterday announced "Israel" would no longer respect the 1996 truce agreement, later said the government would observe the accord's conditions once the border situation returned to normal. "Israel" radio quoting Barak's office, indicated that the prime minister himself regarded the agreement as "still in force". But Communications Minister Binyamin Ben-Eliezer today reaffirmed "Israeli" intentions to modify the April agreement, saying "Israel" wants to adjust the four-year-old truce so it will not quote work for the advantage of Hizbullah. But the co-chairman of the panel, Rapin, stressed, "Israel" has no right to unilaterally modify the agreement, since there are five parties to it. They include the US, France, "Israel", Syria and Lebanon. Rapin also accused "Israel" of deliberately breaching the agreement by attacking Lebanese power plants and civilian targets. The agreement forbids attacks from and on civilian targets, but permits resistance operations against "Israeli" occupation troops in southern Lebanon. France, Lebanon, and Syria have called for an urgent meeting. Official Syrian press reported that Foreign Minister Farouk Sharaa and his US counterpart Madeleine Albright agree that the panel should meet "within 48 hours". The US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright accused Hizbullah of instigating the "Israeli" raids. She justified the attacks on Lebanon, saying "Israel" sent a very strong signal that it quote does not want this escalation. Albright claimed the "Israeli" raids did not target civilians and did violate the April truce agreement. She said the solution to the crisis could be found by the five-nation monitoring group. Barak claimed Hizbullah violated the agreement. Lebanon warned that "Israel" would pay dearly if it decides to annul the agreement. Walid Shoucair, a leading Beirut analyst, told Future Television, highly successful Hizbullah attacks did not violate the April accord, since the attacks took place on occupied Lebanese territory. Shoucair said the attacks frustrated Barak who is trying to minimize troop losses in Lebanon before an expected July withdrawal. The "Israeli" premier wants to lower domestic calls for an "Israeli" pullout from Lebanon while Mideast peace talks are being conducted. He failed to do that and has now failed to change the formula of the April accord. Lebanon has yet to relaunch peace talks with "Israel". Syrian-"Israeli" negotiations which only started in December have been frozen following "Israel's" refusal to commit to a full withdrawal from the Syrian Golan Heights. Albright was expressed optimism about Mideast peace talks, saying Washington is used to "ups and downs" in peace moves".


"Israeli" troops appeal for Lebanon pullout(4)
""Israeli" soldiers on active service in Lebanon issued a personal appeal for an immediate withdrawal from the south. Soldiers told "Israel's" public radio the army has its hands tied for political reasons and cannot act as it should. The radio said it was the first time "Israeli" soldiers had spoken out publicly and directly from their posts in occupied south Lebanon. Soldiers told radio commentators, they are suffering too many losses and should leave as soon as possible. One soldier said "Israel" can never win the war against Hizbullah, which leads the resistance campaign to end "Israel's" 22-year occupation of Lebanese territory. The army command came down harshly against the interviews".


Victims and damage in "Israeli" aggressions overnight and in the morning(5)

The enemy aircraft at 7:00 a.m. Wednesday raided the hilltops overlooking the Zimraya crossing (western Beqaa) firing 4 air-to-ground missiles in two sorties.

Several raids targeted the Daidaba Hilltops.

The occupation's warplanes then targeted the Zawtar Highway hitting the car of Rajaa Shaitani. The car flipped over and Shaitani sustained light injuries.

Another raid targeted the outskirts of Maifadoun. The missile explosions' pressure damaged three houses.

Tuesday evening, the occupation's helicopters raided the town of Tyre targeting an apartment building with four missiles. One of the missiles hit an apartment used as a religious school wounding three people.

Moreover, the occupation's aircraft staged five raids on the town of Ain Bousoir damaging several houses and shops besides the town's electricity grid and water pipes.

One of the missiles also hit the town's highway creating a large hole that closed the road. The Ministry of Public Works repaired the road this morning.

The enemy's warplanes staged several mock air raids over the coastal road between Beirut and Sidon. The enemy aircraft also flew over the capital Beirut.

"Israeli" warplanes at 19:00 Wednesday raided the Wadi al-Qaissieh area.

"As the raids were being staged, the villages adjacent to the front lines were bombarded by the enemy's artillery".
