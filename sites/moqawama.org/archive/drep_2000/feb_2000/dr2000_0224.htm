Daily Report

February Thursday 24-2000:


Introduction

The Arab foreign ministers will held a two-day semi-annual meeting in Beirut next month rather than in Cairo to show solidarity with Lebanon in the wake of the "Israeli" aggression on Lebanon's civilian targets.

The Arab League said the "Israeli" threats against Lebanon and financial aid to help Beirut reconstruct the power stations will top the agenda of discussions in Beirut.

Meanwhile, the "Israeli" threats against Lebanon continued as the "Israeli" enemy foreign minister David Levy was quoted as saying: "Lebanon will burn if Hizbullah hits Kiryat Shmona with Katyushas" adding that "Blood for blood. Soul for soul. Child for child".


Arab ministers to meet in Lebanon
States set aside differences to show solidarity over "Israeli" aggression(1)
Brushing aside schisms that have plagued their ranks, Arab states will dispatch their foreign ministers to a gathering in Beirut next month in a rare show of solidarity with Lebanon and stark defiance of "Israeli" and US anger over mounting support for the resistance.
The bi-annual meeting of the Arab Leagues ministerial council ­ the first to be held in postwar Lebanon ­ is scheduled for March 12-13, offering an opportunity to close ranks after a decade-long split, largely triggered by Iraqs invasion of Kuwait.
The decision to shift the venue from the Arab League headquarters in Cairo to Beirut was announced by the organizations secretary-general, Ismat Abdel-Meguid. He told reporters in Cairo that the participants will focus on political and financial support for Lebanon.
Beirut was quick to welcome the decision, which officials here interpreted as another warning signal to "Israel" that Arab tolerance of its bellicose policies was rapidly fading and that Lebanon was no longer on its own in the confrontation.
Prime Minister Salim Hoss told the weekly Cabinet session that the Lebanese diplomatic offensive had paid off. After so many Arab setbacks and for the first time in very many years, the Arab public opinion has been mobilized, he said, referring to the wide-spread protests in support of Lebanon. The Arab media was also mobilized to serve the Lebanese cause.
Hoss paid tribute to Egyptian President Hosni Mubaraks landmark visit on Saturday, which included a stern warning that "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Baraks policies had jeopardized the chances of a regional settlement and put at risk "Israel's" relations with peace partners such as Egypt and Jordan.
But in sharp contrast to the Arab backing, the United States has come out strongly in support of "Israel", placing the blame for the Feb. 8 air strikes that targeted Lebanons electricity grid on Hizbullah attacks on occupation forces in the south.
State Department spokesman James Rubin on Tuesday criticized Arab support for the resistance. He said: We dont think its wise to be supportive of the enemies of peace.
Speaking in Washington, Rubin said: Hizbullah, by action and word, is trying to kill the peace process and we dont think they should be supported.
 Berri said he believed that Arab countries, which have for long reneged on promises of financial to Lebanon, were more serious in their support this time.
Independent analysts also saw a shift in regional politics, which began with the initiative taken by Mubarak, the first Egyptian president to make a state visit to Lebanon since the overthrow of the monarch in 1953.
Mubaraks visit was highly important, remarked Patrick Seale, Syrian president Hafez Assads biographer and long-time Middle East specialist. In a sense, it was a warning to "Israel" that it cannot behave completely at will in Lebanon.
Speaking to The Daily Star on a private visit to Beirut, Seale said that he believed the unity behind Lebanon could trigger the closing of Arab ranks, and underlined the importance of remarks made in Beirut by Kuwaits foreign minister on Tuesday.
Sheikh Sabah al-Ahmad al-Sabah had publicly stated that the emirate supported convening a pan-Arab meeting, even if Iraq were to take part.
I think the support Lebanon got from Kuwait is very important, Seale said.
Sheikh Sabahs remarks during a six-hour visit to Beirut demonstrated how far Kuwait would go in backing Lebanon, even to the extent of sitting face-to-face with its No. 1 foe of nearly 10 years.
A senior Arab diplomatic source disclosed that during Mubaraks historic visit last weekend, Egyptian officials
suggested to Hoss that the Arab foreign ministers meet in Beirut. Hoss was unenthusiastic, concerned that inter-Arab disputes, especially the Kuwait-Iraq conflict, would overshadow the gathering and ultimately embarrass his government.
But Sheikh Sabahs reassurance encouraged him to step up diplomatic activity in favor of hosting the gathering, which the source said would take place at the Grand Serail.
Another indication of warming Arab relations came from Palestinians. Farouk Kaddoumi, head of the Palestine Liberation Organizations political department, told Hoss on Wednesday that Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat was eager to mend fences with Beirut and Damascus and wanted to visit both countries. It was not clear what Hoss response was.
The premier, speaking at his office at the Grand Serail after conferreing by telephone with the Arab League secretary-general, said the meeting in Beirut will be of great support to Lebanon against "Israeli" threats.
The Arab League secretary-general told me in a phone call that the Lebanese issue will be on top of the meetings agenda, Hoss said. He reiterated his satisfaction that "Israel" had filed a complaint on Saturday to the international truce-monitoring committee, saying the move shows that "Israel" still recognizes the committee and the truce.
Our diplomatic campaign was fruitful and will be maintained to avoid further "Israeli" attacks on civilian targets and to revive the truce-monitoring committee, he said.
Hoss added: Lebanon prefers a pullout with an agreement, but it also will welcome a unilateral withdrawal because that was our demand from the beginning.


Syria praises Arab solidarity and support for Lebanon(2)
"Official press in Damascus praised Arab solidarity in the face of "Israeli" attacks on Lebanon. Government-run newspapers stressed "Israel's" latest air blitz drew strong support for Lebanese resistance activities from throughout the Arab world. Meanwhile, Damascus accused "Israel" of preventing any progress in Middle East peace negotiations.

" Syria hailed Europe's role in the Middle East peace process. Official press in Damascus accused Washington of quote covering up for "Israel" following its raids on Lebanese civilian installations. But Italian Prime Minister Massimo D'Alema said following talks with Syrian President Hafez Assad in Damascus late yesterday, Syria still accepts Washington's role as broker of frozen peace talks with "Israel" despite criticism of US defense of this month's "Israeli" air blitz on Lebanon. D'Alema said President Assad is very concerned about peace-making. Official media in Damascus had questioned Washington's mediating role in the peace process after US officials justified air strikes on Lebanon. The Italian premier was upbeat, expressing confidence peace talks will resume. He stressed Syria wants serious negotiations with "Israel". Peace talks, which only resumed late last year after a nearly four-year break, swiftly ran into trouble over "Israel's" rejection of Syrian demands for a commitment to withdraw from the entire occupied Golan Heights. "Israel" wants negotiations to first focus on security and normalization of ties. D'Alema blamed "leaks" for the breakdown of US-brokered talks and called for any resumed negotiations to be kept firmly under wraps. "Israeli" newspapers published the US working document agreed at the last round of negotiations infuriating Washington and Damascus. Syrian officials were quoted as saying President Assad told D'Alema he wanted a greater European Union role in reviving the stalled talks".


FRANCE REAFFIRMS SUPPORT TO BEIRUT(3)

"France's Ambassador to Lebanon Phillipe Lecourtier delivered a letter to Prime Minister Salim Hoss from his French counterpart Lionel Jospin, in which he reiterated Paris's support for Beirut. Jospin expressed concern over the upsurge of violence in the region. He called for the resumption of Middle East peace talks. The French Ambassador said Paris and Washington are working hard to convene a meeting of the April cease-fire group in southern Lebanon".


Levy: We'll repay Hizbullah attacks 'child for child'(4)
" Lebanon will "burn" if Hizbullah hits Kiryat Shmona with Katyushas, Foreign Minister David Levy warned again yesterday. He further threatened that the revenge would be commensurate, including the harming of Lebanese children.

"Blood for blood. Soul for soul. Child for child," Levy declared in the Knesset during a prolonged tirade, which sparked a fierce row with Arab MKs, several of whom were removed.

Levy's comments came in response to a motion to the agenda on Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak's recent statements in support of Hizbullah and incitement in the Arab world against "Israel".

"I want the things to be clear, not as a threat, but a warning before calamity," Levy said.

According to Levy, this approach prevented an escalation during the recent hostilities in Lebanon, and "the message was understood."

Issam Mahoul (Hadash), who was removed for shouting during the session, said: "Child for child is a primitive, fundamentalist, and dangerous expression that puts us back to the days of an eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth. It is not suited to governments."

Ahmed Tibi (Arab Movement for Renewal) said that for the first time in the state's history the government intends, under certain conditions, to murder Lebanese children. "This was an immoral statement," he said.

Azmi Bishara (Balad) said it had appeared that Levy had "lost his mind while trying to defend his statement that Lebanon will burn and expressed vengeance instead of exercising the cautious diplomacy expected from the foreign minister."

Education Minister Yossi Sarid said such types of statements are superfluous and are "not suited to the character of the government, its policies, or aims."

Meretz whip Zehava Gal-On called for Levy's removal from the security cabinet. Someone with an "eye-for-an-eye" security and political policy is not fit to serve in the security cabinet, she said.

During the exchange with the Arab MKs, Levy also slammed them for what he said was their support of Hizbullah and failure to support the state of "Israel".

Bishara said during the debate: "Hizbullah is fighting against the "Israeli" occupation."

Levy shot back. "Who are you trying to look good for? Hizbullah? Who are you defending? You are defending Hizbullah."
