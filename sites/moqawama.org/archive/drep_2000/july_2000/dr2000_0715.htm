Hizbullah: We don't forget our prisoners, will work to free them by all means(1)

Hizbullah has underlined that it does not forget its prisoners and detainees whom the party will seek to release "with all means".

We will never rest until we see them free; we will work with all means to secure the release of Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, Hajj Mustafa Dirani and all the hostages that are detained in Israeli prisons, read a Hizbullah communiqué on Friday.

The communiqué came in response to a ruling issued by an Israeli occupation court to extend the "administrative detention" of Sheikh Obeid and Hajj Dirani.

Hizbullah put the ruling, with all the facts it implies, "before the international forums, the UN, the Security Council, the International Committee of the Red Cross, and the human rights groups in particular" stressing that the Israeli occupation's entity is a terrorist one that does not heed the international conventions and laws and is an outlaw entity par excellence.

"The extension, just as the abduction, is an aggression against the sovereignty of the Lebanese state, an excessive violation of human rights and a flagrant scandal against the international community which sees these violations and remains silent," Hizbullah said.


Messages of hope rise up for remaining detainees in Israel(2)
Balloons float unhindered across the border at Fatima Gate to mark first Lebanese Hostage Day

The wind blew in the right direction at Fatima Gate on Friday, taking messages of freedom and hope to the 19 Lebanese who remain in Israeli prisons.
Balloons carrying the names of Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons and words like freedom and love blew over to Israel in a gesture that some 200 Lebanese made to show their support for the prisoners.
The supporters gathered at the gate and visited the former Khiam prison to mark the first Lebanese Hostage Day, organized by the Follow-Up Committee For The Support of Lebanese Detainees in Israeli Prisons. The event drew former prisoners, detainees friends and family members and members of non-governmental organizations, as well as visitors to the border.
Once again, relatives of the 19 detainees urged the government and the international community to double their efforts to bring home their sons and brothers.
The government is so busy negotiating violations over a few meters of land. I want to ask them: What is the land worth without its heroes? said Mohammed Safa, the committees secretary-general. He asked that the United Nations keep its promise to seek the liberation of all the detainees.
Safa also said that the government should start leading this battle with more vigor and stop being shy in demanding the liberation of its citizens.
We cant accept Israeli propositions to exchange detainees with the Israeli-allied militiamen or with criminals such as (missing Israeli airman) Ron Arad. Our detainees are heroes and will not be traded for criminals, he said.
Help our sons come back to see the land they were imprisoned to liberate, shouted Umm Anwar Yassin.
Before arriving at the gate, the group visited Ansar, the site of the first Israeli prison in Lebanon, now just a patch of land with a cafeteria. The prison operated between 1982 and 1985. More than 15,000 people were detained and about 20 died during the ordeal.
Hussein Qassir, who was among those held at in Ansar, said he was proud of the strength of the resistance since 1982, but added that he had grievances with the government.
There is still much that needs to be done, mostly in terms of the rehabilitation and the social and economic support for all the released detainees, he said.
According to Imad Hamdan, head of the Islamic Scouts Association, the Ansar site will become a national museum where artillery left by the Israelis after the pullout will be on exhibition. The project also includes displaying a plan of the former prison and an exhibition wing for handicrafts made by detainees.
After Ansar and the Fatima Gate, the group concluded the tour with the now customary trip to Khiam.
Once again, former detainees recounted and enacted stories about atrocities in the prison. They showed the crowds the investigation corridor, and the 70-by-70 centimeter chicken coop where detainees were left for long hours before they broke down.
The prisons guards used to watch the boxing matches on the Israeli proxy militias Middle East TV every Saturday and come to experiment on us, said Ali Raad.
He described the physical abuse, such as pouring hot and cold water over the detainees heads, as crowds gathered at the spot were 14 people were tortured to death.
This is where I grew up, said Afif Hammoud, 26, who spent 10 years in Khiam. Many issues remain unresolved for those who were held at the prison. I really cant believe that at a time that when there are about 2,000 released detainees who are either homeless or cant find a job, there are countries proposing to take in the collaborators, he said.
Were calling for fair trials for all the unfortunate people who had to deal with Israel. But that doesnt mean that the government should let the older people who served Israel for more than 25 years go just because theyre old, he added.
When someone shouted that the prisons guards should be brought back to Khiam, Hammoud replied: This place has become the place of our freedom. It is now sacred with the blood of all those who were killed in it and should never be desecrated by those filthy beings.
One of the female detainees, who declined to give her name, reported that after her liberation, she suffered from bleeding for three years and was never able to sustain a pregnancy because she was sodomized and subjected to electric shock torture.
According to Bassam Qantar, 22, whose brother Samir was seized in 1979 and faces five life sentences in Israel, Fridays event was very important, but still lacked a national scope. He said that despite the growing interest in the issues of the detainees and the missing, the only truly involved citizens were friends or family members.
We have to keep on organizing these events because we need to keep people interested, otherwise they will forget, he said. We must keep working hard to turn these individual tragedies into a national problem.


Israel repeats commitment to correcting troop encroachments in  Lebanon

UN special envoy gives himself a pat on the back(3)

Terje Roed-Larsen said on Friday that most of the nine remaining Israeli border violations have been rectified and that he expected an end to the problem by the beginning of next week.
The UN special envoy to the Middle East said that once the violations have ended, UNIFIL peacekeepers will be able to deploy up to the border in accordance with Resolution 425.
Larsen, speaking after a meeting with President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss at Baabda Palace, said the remaining encroachments should be resolved soon.
I brought good news to the president and prime minister today, Larsen told reporters.
The government of Israel has committed itself formally to  rapidly rectifying all violations, he said.
He added that there had already been significant improvement along the line and most of the violations which we have observed have already been rectified.
UN inspectors and a team from the Lebanese Army will inspect the line on Monday to check if Israel has complied with the UN demand to end all violations, Larsen said.
Most of the encroachments involve Israeli patrol roads and small observation posts containing one or two soldiers north of the line of withdrawal, the so-called Blue Line. Although the roads themselves are not considered violations, Israeli soldiers still patrol them on a daily basis.
According to security sources, eight violations appear to have been rectified by the Israelis.
A senior political source said that Larsen was informed during the meeting that new violations were occurring, such as the stealing of soil near Odaisseh. Larsen admitted knowledge of this, the source said.
Lebanon also has complaints over the differences between a list of map coordinates presented by the UN in June and the coordinates for the Blue Line in the Odaisseh area, the source said.
Lebanon rejected the possibility of establishing a mechanism to monitor violations of the Blue Line, as has been reportedly proposed by Israel.
Larsen was told that Lebanon would not participate in a joint committee with Israel. Instead, UNIFIL should be charged with monitoring the Blue Line. Any discrepancies spotted by Lebanon would be reported to UNIFIL, the source said.
UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan is reportedly pleased with Larsens efforts to end Israels violations, according to a report sent from New York to the Foreign Ministry on Friday.
The real test of Israels intentions will be answered in the next 72 hours, a Western diplomat in Beirut said.
The UN report added that Lebanons request to renew UNIFILs mandate was concise and rested on two points: renewing the mandate as of Aug. 1 and calling for the release of all detainees in Israeli prisons.
Annan is expected to conclude his report on UNIFIL by Thursday. It will be immediately distributed to the members of the Security Council for their perusal prior to a July 28 meeting to extend UNIFILs mandate.
A statement from Baabda said that Lebanon renewed its objections to three violations of the Blue Line and said that a withdrawal would not be considered complete until all Israels violations have ended.
Larsen leaves Lebanon on Sunday for Israel.
Larsen said that he had raised the plight of Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons with Israeli Premier Ehud Barak and that he would follow up the issue.
Meanwhile, an official in Beirut said that the government may ask countries with good ties to Israel to help secure the release of Lebanese detainees. Germany has been cited as a possible interlocutor due to its role in prisoner swaps between Lebanon and Israel in the past.
Hizbullah renewed its pledge on Friday to liberate resistance leaders Sheikh Mustafa Dirani, Abdel-Karim Obeid and other detainees held by Israel.
We will not rest until we see them free. We will use all means to liberate them and other detainees held in Israeli jails, the group said in a statement.
An Israeli judge decided on Wednesday to keep the two leaders in custody for at least another three months, ruling that they remained a threat to security.
Obeid, who was a senior Hizbullah official, was kidnapped by Israeli commandos from his home in the South in 1989.
Dirani, who was a resistance leader, was seized by Israeli commandos from his home in the Beqaa in 1994.


Israel hauls Lebanese topsoil into Galilee(4)

Israeli trucks have been cited transporting fertile topsoil from southern Lebanon into the Jewish state, in clear violation of the country's sovereignty. UN spokesman Timur Goksel said peacekeepers will verify the border region to examine any transfer of soil from fields by Odaisseh village. Goksel stressed, there have been Israeli encroachments in the area. 

 Agence France Presse said more than one hundred Israeli trucks were seen crossing Lebanon's border, unloading sand and rocks on a dirt road close to the international frontier. It quoted sources as saying an Israeli bulldozer has been paving the dirt road. The London-based daily al-Hayat said the trucks have also been transporting fertile Lebanese topsoil into Israeli farms near Metulla. The paper added, many old trees inside Lebanon have been razed by Israeli troops. Before withdrawing occupation troops from Lebanon in May, Israel admitted to stealing hundreds of tons of top soil from areas around the southern village of Odaisseh for agricultural terraces inside the Galilee. The military then accused Israeli civilian contractors of carrying out the illegal soil transportation. So far, Israeli officials have not commented on the latest cross-border earth hauling. Lebanese have for decades been accusing Israel of stealing fertile soil and water from the south, especially from the Wazzani river basin. People from the town said Israel has for years been channeling Lebanese water into its northern settlements. The pipes can clearly be seen running from the river, across the border fence to a small pumping station guarded by Israeli soldiers. Locals say water tankers arrived every few days to take the water away. Beirut had vowed to file an official complaint to the UN. Water Resources Minister Suleyman Traboulsi recently said Beirut has scientific proof that Israel is stealing water from the Hasbani and Wazzani tributaries. 


Lebanese civilian injured after driving car over landmine: police(5)

 A Lebanese civilian was injured Friday when a landmine exploded as the man drove through an area formerly controlled by the Israeli army, police said.

Hussein Wahbi, 40, was injured and his car dammaged in the incident, which occurred in the village of M'likh. He was taken to hospital.

Since Israel withdrew from southern Lebanon on May 24, six civlians have been killed and 19 injured by exploding mines.

The United Nations estimates that there are some 130,000 mines planted in the formerly occupied zone.


Israel considers relaunching propaganda radio station in Lebanon(6)

 The Israeli army is thinking of bringing back the propaganda radio station it ran during its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon, an army official said Friday.

"The station will broadcast news for the residents of south Lebanon and warnings to Hizbullah," the militant group that spearheaded the armed opposition to Israeli occupation, and be resumed in the next few weeks, said the official, who requested anonymity.

The plan has met opposition, however, from other army officials, who told Israeli public radio that they feared restarting the station would give the impression that Israel wanted to continue meddling in Lebanese affairs.

"Voice of the South" was operated by Israels proxy militia in the area, and most of its employees fled south when Israel ended its occupation May 24.


Wives, children of former Israeli-allied militiamen flee to Israel from Lebanon: official(7)

 Two women, accompanied by their seven children, fled through barbed wire into Israel from Lebanon Friday in a bid to join their husbands, former members of the Israeli-backed militia in southern Lebanon, the official ANI news agency reported.

Fleeing the country were Samira Ghasham, wife of Camille Harfouche, and her five children, as well as Sihem Nassif, wife of Sherif Shain, and her two children, ANI said.

The two men are among 6,000 former militiamen and family members who crossed over into Israel after that country's army ended a 22-year occupation of south Lebanon on May 24.

The Hizbullah, which spearheaded the guerrilla campaign to oust Israel from Lebanon, has recently set up observation posts along the border aimed at intercepting Lebanese fleeing to Israel.


80 collaborators tried(8)

Approximately 80 Israeli-proxy militiamen stood trial on Friday for collaborating with Israel and were expected to be indicted later in the evening, judicial sources said.
The trial was the 17th for the collaborators. Military Judge Maher Safieddine has presided over the hearings, which so far have sentenced more than 750 militiamen to prison terms ranging from a few months to up to 15 years.
Most of the men on trial on Friday confessed to enlisting in the militia for fear of being expelled from their villages, but they denied killing civilians or conducting military operations.
