Israel promises UN it will fix new violations(1)

Israel promised Wednesday to immediately rectify a series of new border violations, which, if fulfilled, will allow UNIFIL peacekeepers to deploy into the liberated areas Thursday.
A UN source said that UN Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen was told by General Gabi Ashkenazi, the head of the Israeli Armys northern command, that the violations of the so-called Blue Line at Abbad hill and Manara would be cleared up overnight.
The Israeli promise came as the deployment of UNIFIL peacekeepers to the border remained on hold Wednesday while Larsen inspected the latest Israeli violations for himself.
Larsen is scheduled to hold a meeting with President Emile Lahoud at Beiteddine, the summer residence of the head of state, at 11 am Thursday morning. The UN envoy is expected to tell Lahoud the results of his talks with Ashkenazi.
A UNIFIL officer said that hundreds of peacekeepers were ready to move into the former occupation zone and were merely awaiting the green light.
Information Minister Anwar Khalil did not appear confident of an immediate end to the violations, saying that there were lingering sticking points and that the issue has not been resolved completely.
Mr. Larsen has promised the president and the prime minister to resolve for good these new violations, Khalil said after the weekly Cabinet meeting.
Larsen, accompanied by Brigadier General Jim Sreenan, UNIFILs deputy commander, was ferried by helicopter from Naqoura to an Irish battalion post in Shaqra. From Shaqra, the UN envoy traveled to Abbad hill on the border opposite Houla, where Israeli forces were paving a road that encroaches onto Lebanese soil. Larsen watched the road works through his binoculars as Sreenan explained the nature of the new violation at the position.
From Houla, Larsen moved to the second spot of Israeli encroachment near the Israeli settlement of Manara, where he was joined by the Lebanese inspection team headed by Brigadier General Amin Hoteit.
At Manara, Hoteit led the UN and Lebanese teams to the site where the Israelis had encroached on some 4 hectares of Lebanese land along the barbed wire fence surrounding the settlement.
Hoteit warned reporters against speaking to any Israeli journalists or soldiers who were heavily deployed near the common border at Manara.
We found Israeli correspondents covering todays events. Because they are here, you should be here. No one is better than anybody else and thats why I summoned you here. But I want you to practice self-restraint, Hoteit told the Lebanese media team after they were kept waiting at a distance from the border.
I will consider any sort of conversation with the Israeli journalists a great violation. You are also barred from talking to any Israeli, be it a civilian or a soldier, said Hoteit.
Hoteit cautioned the journalists to refrain from trespassing the blue line that separates the Lebanese road from the Israeli asphalt.
The Israelis have bitterly resented the path of the Blue Line at Manara. The line runs beside a civilian road, leaving no room for the Israeli Army to erect a border fence and construct a military patrol road.
Several Israeli soldiers mingled with Israeli journalists, who tried in vain to elicit comments from their Lebanese counterparts.
The Israeli newsmen greeted the Lebanese media with Shalom, without drawing a response from the Lebanese side. Also, busloads of Israeli tourists waved to the Lebanese and hollered greetings in Hebrew before moving on.
Atop the hill in Manara, Larsen, Hoteit and the two teams began to demarcate the site of the Israeli violation.
Larsen refused to comment to reporters on the violations, only conceding that UNIFIL had reported new violations along the Blue Line which are being worked out.
For his part, Hoteit said that the Lebanese inspection team is willing to cooperate fully with UN to resolve the new violations and retrieve all Israeli annexed areas.
We affirm our legal right to retrieve each grain of sand, following the strict instructions of President Emile Lahoud. We will not give up a grain of soil. Lebanon has retrieved its territory and its capable of building a whole new town on the reclaimed land, Hoteit said.
Larsen then departed, leaving Hoteit and his team to put markings on the 2.8 kilometer stretch regained from Israel.
An hour later, Hoteit and Larsen met near the ancient tomb on the summit of Abbad hill. Hoteit told Larsen that Lebanon required the entire summit of the hill because the present division grants Israel a more strategic position to threaten Lebanon.


Leader's envoy congratulates Lebanese, tours south(2)

Foreign Affairs Adviser to the Leader of the Islamic Ummah Grand Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, Dr Ali Akbar Velayati, held a series of meetings on the situation in Lebanon and the region with senior Lebanese officials, including President Gen Emile Lahoud, Speaker Nabih Berri, and Prime Minister Dr Salim al-Hoss.

Velayati, who arrived in Beirut on Tuesday, relayed a message to President Lahoud from Leader Khamenei who congratulated the Lebanese on the victory and liberation of the land from the Israeli occupation underlining the strong ties between Lebanon and the Islamic Republic of Iran and Tehran's readiness to assist Lebanon more to overcome its economic and living crisis.

The envoy also met other political and religious leaders before heading to the south in the afternoon where he toured the liberated areas.


Eleven Lebanese return from Israel(3)

 Eleven Lebanese who fled to Israel during Israeli's troop pullout from southern Lebanon in May returned Wednesday and surrendered to Lebanese authorities, police said.

Khalil Elias Hashem, his wife, two young children, his father and mother as well as Michael Assaad Hajj, his wife, their son, daughter-in-law and a grandchild crossed back from this coastal border post, they said.

The two families, carrying their personal belongings, were driven by two vehicles of the United Nations Interim Forces in southern Lebanon (UNIFIL) to posts of the Lebanese General Security and Internal Security Forces.

Some 6,000 Lebanese and former members of the now defunct militia fled to Israel during the Jewish state's May 24 pullout from south Lebanon.

Lebanese military courts have so far issued sentences against 900 people among than 2,200 Lebanese suspected of having collaborated with Israel.

Hajj told AFP that most of the Lebanese who fled to Israel wanted to return home, adding that some 150 people were planning to cross into Lebanon on Sunday.

"Every day there are street protests in the 25 places where the Lebanese were housed in Israel. They want permission to return. It took 15 days for the Israelis to allow us to return," said Hajj.

"The Lebanese there even fight with Israeli soldiers. They throw stones at them, they insult them and accuse the Israelis of letting them down after years of cooperation," he said.


Trials of collaborators continue(4)

A military court in Beirut proceeded with the trials of the 19th batch of the Lebanese who had collaborated with the Israeli occupation forces.

Elsewhere, a spokesman for the German government affirmed that four hundred Lahdi officers and militiamen arrived in Germany following an earlier agreement in this regard between German Foreign Minister Joshca Fischer and the enemy's prime minister Ehud Barak that was reached during Fischer's visit to the occupied territories last June.

The spokesman said his government will take the necessary security measures to protect the collaborators whose arrival schedule, movements and residences will be shrouded with strict secrecy.

The agents are expected to be distributed to various German provinces in Rhine, Bavaria, Berlin and Baden.
