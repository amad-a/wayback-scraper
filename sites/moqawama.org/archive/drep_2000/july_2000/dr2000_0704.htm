PFLP leader praises Hizbullah successes(1)

Habash: Lebanons resistance model can be repoeated in Palestine

George Habash, leader of the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine, said on Monday that the principle of the resistance that won back South Lebanon could also be applied in Palestine.
Lebanons resistance model can be repeated 100 percent, meaning that we consider resistance the only means to achieve our aims, Habash said following a meeting with Hizbullah Deputy Secretary-General Sheikh Naim Qassem.
Habash ­ who was accompanied by Beirut MP Najah Wakim, former Hizbullah MP Ali Ammar and Hizbullah politburo member Hassan Hodroj ­ said however that the type of resistance would be different.
We have to take into consideration the specific nature of the West Bank and Gaza but in the general framework we have to prove that resistance is the only option, said Habash, who met Qassem at Hizbullahs headquarters in Haret Hreik.
The Palestinian officials visit was to congratulate Hizbullah officials on the victory following the liberation of the South.
We consider the Israeli withdrawal a unique victory.
What I have in my mind is the 1973 victory and the steadfastness of the Palestinian resistance during the 1982 invasion, and the intifada, he said.
Habash, who earlier visited President Emile Lahoud to congratulate him on the liberation, added that Lebanons resistance was unique in its coordination with the political system.
Im both gratified and content to hear from Hizbullahs leadership that it is necessary to take the position of the political system into consideration.
There is also the issue of the national and popular unity around the resistance. What is also comforting is Hizbullahs consideration of other political parties, he said.
Habash said he and Qassem discussed the challenges and dangers facing the Palestinians and the Palestinian cause, commenting on the intention of the Palestinian Authority to announce the creation of a Palestinian State on Sept. 13.
The Zionist-American plan is to give Abu Ammar (Palestinian Authority President and PLO chairman Yasser Arafat) a state in return for the concession of Jerusalem, the 4 million Palestinian refugees, borders, water and some settlements, Habash added.


Hizbullah: sturdy Lebanon imposes its conditions and refuses to give up a particle of its soil(2)

Hizbullah's South official Sheikh Nabil Qawouq affirmed that Lebanon, which is in a strong position today, shall not cede one inch of its land including Shebaa Farms, and other strategic sites which the Israeli army decided to withhold under security pretexts.

Sheikh Qawouq, who spoke during the commemoration of martyr Ali al-Zein and the annual remembrance of Shhour village's martyrs said, "In the past the enemy had taken advantage from times of weakness, so it occupied big parts of our land. Nevertheless, the status quo today is different; the rules of the game have changed; and sturdy Lebanon is now the one to impose its conditions. And we shall not cede an inch of our land."

Sheikh Nabil Qawouq pointed out that the Israeli enemy is now trying to push the UN forces in Lebanon to serve their project, and charge them to end the border drawing mission quickly at the expense of Lebanon. Qawouq confirmed that Lebanon is not unable to liberate the rest of its occupied soil. Moreover, Hizbullah's official for the south threatened "Israel" of hindering the liberation of Lebanese detainees in its prisons and use them as a means of pressure on Lebanon.

Qawooq held "Israel" responsible for the outcomes of its "terrorist positions" as far as Lebanon is concerned and the detained in "Israel".

Israel violates line of withdrawal in Lebanon - UN report says(3)

 A UN report delivered to the Security Council Monday outlines 12 violations by Israel of Lebanese territory, including technical fences or equipment that lie on the Lebanese side of the line of withdrawal.

The United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) report dated Sunday said five of the violations involve fences stretched a few meters (feet) into Lebanese territory, and the remaining seven consist of patrol tracks or the position of Israelis next to the border line.

The report was given to all 15 council members, but there was no debate on its findings, diplomats said.

The United Nations drew the "blue line" to verify that Israeli forces had completely withdrawn from Lebanese territory on May 24.

The Security Council certified the Israeli retreat on June 23, but UNIFIL has accused Israel of a number of violations on Lebanese territory.

The Lebanese government asked the United Nations to put an end to these violations and opposes the deployment of UN troops in the territory from which Israel withdrew until the situation is rectified.

Also on Monday, the UN Security Council met with its chief cartographer to discuss the "blue line" drawn between Israel and Lebanon.

The meeting was designed for the cartographer, Miklos Pinther, to tell the council members behind closed doors how the United Nations drew the line contested by both countries.

Pinther is due to meet in Beirut at the end of the week with Terje Roed-Larsen, the UN special envoy to the Middle East.


Larsen due to return on Friday to address border feud(4)

Prime Minister Salim Hoss confirmed on Monday that United Nations Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen would visit Beirut on Friday to investigate the complaints of Israeli violations along the border.
Larsen, who will be accompanied by chief UN cartographer Miklos Pinther, is expected to visit Israel on Wednesday, followed by Syria, then Lebanon.
The UN team will tour the border and then meet with President Emile Lahoud and Hoss at the presidential palace.
Hoss said he had not received any official response to the invitations he sent to foreign ministers of potential donor countries for a conference to gather funds for rebuilding the South.
Meanwhile, the UN withdrawal verification team toured the border from inside Israel on Monday to inspect the locations of nine Israeli violations of the line of withdrawal, the so-called Blue Line.
According to a UN source, despite intensive activity along the border to build new fences, the violations remained in place.
Among the violations being checked on Monday were two machine-gun posts set up on Lebanese territory at Boundary Pillar 33 and BP38.
BP33 was the location of the former Abbad compound in the central sector beside Houla.
The Blue Line ­ which at this point follows the international border ­ sliced the Abbad post in half, requiring the Israelis to dismantle their fortifications inside Lebanon. Although much of the post has been demolished, the Israelis still maintain a machine-gun post on the Lebanese side.
Similarly, a lone soldier armed with a light machine gun and hidden beneath camouflage net has been deployed on observation duties just north of the Blue Line at BP38, east of Metulla.
The source said that the results of the inspection would be sent to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, who has taken a personal interest in developments along the Blue Line.
The UN team is apparently losing patience with Ehud Baraks procrastination over ending the trivial violations.
Its like two elephants making love, said the source. Lots of dust and noise, but it takes two years to deliver.


Beirut hot on Berlin for accepting collaborators(5)

The government has filed an official complaint with Germany for hosting former Israeli-proxy militiamen and their families who fled Lebanon during the Israeli withdrawal from the South.
According to a diplomatic source, after being tipped about their arrival by the Lebanese Embassy in Berlin, officials in Beirut filed the complaint.
When Beirut informed Berlin that the people it took in were Lebanese and not Israeli, German officials said that they could stay in Germany for a year and that it did not consider them political refugees, the source said.
But the source added that this period could well be extended, and noted that Israel was paying the expenses of the militiamen.
The source claimed that Germanys decision to host the men was a departure from Berlins earlier statements that it would deport collaborators who found their way into the country.
Lebanons ambassador to Germany, Melhem Misto, asked the German government to avoid splitting up families if it decided to deport the collaborators.
Meanwhile at the Military Tribunal, the trial of 120 alleged collaborators ­ the twelfth batch of suspects to appear before the court so far ­ opened on Monday.
The court, presided over by Brigadier Maher Safieddine, listened to the closing arguments of military prosecutors and defense lawyers in the afternoon.


Israeli bungled plan to spy on Mossad suspect(6)

An Israeli secret agent pleaded guilty in a Swiss court yesterday to charges relating to a spying plot that created a diplomatic incident between the two countries. It is believed to be the first time a confirmed member of Mossad, the Israeli secret service, has gone on trial outside Israel.

Known only by the cover name of Isaac Bental, the man was brought into the federal court in Lausanne by a back door to avoid photographers, and testified with his Swiss lawyers and Israeli embassy representatives at his side.

He refused to give his real name, telling the court through an interpreter: "It would affect my own personal security if I gave my true identity." In a concession to Israeli sensitivities the presiding judge did not insist that the man should divulge his true identity.

Bental, who admitted the main charges, of installing an illegal listening device and carrying false identity papers, said he had joined Mossad to help his country and had never carried out acts which went against his conscience.

The spy's lawyers presented documents from the Israeli government saying he would never return to Switzerland on duty for Mossad.

Bental was caught with four other Mossad agents trying to install a telephone bug in an apartment block in the Swiss capital, Berne, in February, 1998. Right up until the first day of the trial it remained unclear whether Israel would return Bental, who had been released on £1.2m bail after two months in prison, to Switzerland.

Bental maintains that the owner of the apartment, a Swiss of Lebanese origin, was a suspected member of the Islamic organisation, Hizbullah. He said his mission had been to save lives and prevent terrorist attacks. However, the bungled operation  one of a series involving Mossad agents  caused deep embarrassment in Israel and the head of the secret service resigned.

It also strained diplomatic relations, already overshadowed by Switzerland's treatment of the assets of Jews who died in the Holocaust. Switzerland demanded and received an apology from Israel, but the Swiss police also blundered. They released the four other agents, not appreciating the significance of the affair. A sixth man, believed to be the leader of the operation, was never caught.

The hearing is expected to last another four days.
