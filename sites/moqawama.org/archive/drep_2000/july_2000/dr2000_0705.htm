Introduction

Hizbullah Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah paid a visit to the Islamic Republic of Iran to thank Tehran for its longstanding support and denounce Israels continued aggressions on Lebanon. Our struggle and our resistance will continue so long as there is one inch of occupied Lebanese territory, he said upon arrival where he was given a reception usually for heads of state.

However, Beirut military court handed down sentences to 57 Lebanese accused of collaboration with Israel, including ten years to a double agent who turned in his sister to the Israelis.


Hizbullah's Secretary-General carries with him to Tehran the joy of liberation and victory

A Hizbullah delegation headed by the secretary-general Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah began on Tuesday his first visit to Iran since the "Israeli" defeat and withdrawal from south Lebanon on May 24. The delegation was given a distinctive reception at Tehran Airport.

They were received by the director of the office of the Islamic Nation leader Hujjat al-Islam Mohammed Kalbaykani, president of the Lebanese Iranian joint committee housing minister Abd-al-Ali Zahda, the foreign minister assistant for middle east affairs Mohammed Al-Sadr, the representative of the head of parliament Ayatollah Karubi, the Lebanese ambassador in Tehran Adnan Mansour, the Iranian ambassador in Syria Hussein Al-Islam and the Iranian ambassador in Beirut Mohammed Ali Subhani.

"Sayyed Nasrallah arrived to a reception generally accorded to heads of state", reported France Press Agency.

Hizbullah's general- secretary said upon his arrival that he is visiting Iran to offer his gratitude to the Leader of the Islamic Revolution Imam Ali Khamenei and all the officials in the Islamic Republic for their supporting and upholding the Lebanese people in resisting the Israeli aggressions since 1982.

Sayyed Nasrallah remarked that Iran is a real partner in Lebanon's victory against the Israeli occupation forces(1)

Nasrallah vowed on Tuesday to press for Israels full withdrawal from the South.
Although Israel has evacuated large parts of Lebanon, it still controls certain regions, Nasrallah told reporters after arriving in Tehran.
For us a handful of our soil is of the same value as several kilometers. We will continue to fight until the occupation is completely over. The full eviction of the Zionist regime is our main demand, he said.
Everyone knows Iran suffered for its support of Hizbullah and it shares in the victory of the people of Lebanon, he said. I would like to congratulate Iranians on this great victory.
Iran has always, without compromise, been on our side, he said, adding that Israel was the origin of all the problems in the region.
Apart from Khamenei, the Hizbullah leader will also meet Iranian President Mohammed Khatami during his visit.
Also on Tuesday, Khatami reiterated Irans support of Hizbullah, calling for the Zionist regimes complete withdrawal from the South.
The Lebanese peoples victory is the fruit of their resistance, he said.
Iran shared in Lebanons victory when Israel left the South. Foreign Minister Kamal Kharrazi quickly flew to Lebanon to visit the former occupation zone(2)

The visit is the Hizbullah leader's first to Iran since Israel withdrew from southern Lebanon on May 24, ending 22 years of occupation. The Lebanese government insists Israel still violates the border at certain points, a view partially upheld by the United Nations.

"This visit has great importance," said Adnan Mansur, Lebanon's ambasador in Tehran.

"Sayyed Nasrallah's visit was necessary to thank the Iranian government for its support of the resistance and to contribute to the strengthening of relations between the two peoples," the ambassador said.

Iran showed jubilation when Israel left southern Lebanon, with Foreign Minister Kamal Kharazi quickly travelling to Lebanon to walk in the former Israeli zone alongside the Lebanese.

UN Secretary General Kofi Annan recognized Iran's role in the region last month by visiting Tehran at the start of a Middle East tour devoted to Lebanon.

During his visit, Nasrallah will also meet with Iranian groups that have supported Hizbullah or assisted families of soldiers killed in combat, including the Foundation of Martyrs and the Committee for the Aide of the Imam(3)


Court gives double agent 10-year term(4)

A military court handed down sentences to 57 suspects accused of collaboration with Israel on Tuesday, including one of 10 years to a double agent who turned over his own sister to the Israelis.
Ibrahim Beydoun, a former member of Israels now-defunct proxy militia said he went to an Israeli officer after his sister Sonia proposed that he work as an agent for the resistance.
He testified that the officer gave him a small recording device that taped everything his sister said, although Beydoun said he eventually destroyed the recorder and confessed.
Sonia was detained for a few months in the militia controlled Khiam detention center before being expelled from the occupied zone. He himself was also held at Khiam for four years.
The military court also forbade Beydoun from entering the former occupied zone for 10 years after he finishes his prison term.
The other defendants received the usual prison terms of between a few months and a few years, in addition to orders prohibiting them from returning to their home areas for several years after they finish serving their sentences.
The court postponed for two weeks consideration of the cases of 63 other alleged collaborators after their lawyers requested time to study their clients files. The trials of all suspects opened on Monday.
The latest sentences, handed down overnight, bring to 662 the number of verdicts reached since the court began holding three weekly sessions on June 5 to try some 2,200 Lebanese accused of collaborating with Israel.


Here comes Hizbullah(5)

Get ready, Lebanon: the party that brought you the liberation of the South has moved beyond the prolonged distraction of fighting foreign occupation and set its sights on the decrepit political establishment whose indolent ways and crooked practices have waged their own internal war on this countrys future. This rebellion will not be led by guns and rockets, but those entrenched political forces who stand between Hizbullah and its goals have only two choices: get on the bandwagon or get out of the way.
After 18 years of doing battle with one of the worlds most efficient and ruthless military machines, Hizbullah has finally prevailed. Its fighters are justifiably proud of their achievement, but its leaders now have more time to spend on purely domestic issues. The parliamentarians who previously concentrated on supporting the resistance movement are now branching out into areas of public policy long neglected by their counterparts in traditional-style parties, and the guerrillas who spent their time driving the enemy off our land stand ready to be the foot soldiers of a grassroots political movement with no precedent in Lebanese history.
This is no phony party built around the memory of a long-dead family patriarch, the financial resources of a billionaire, the ancient reputation of a tired academic, or the connections of a former warlord. Hizbullahs primary identification may be with under-privileged sections of the Shiite community, but its program and priorities have more in common with everyday Lebanese of all sects than is the case with any other leading political movement in this country.
The partys leading lights have not invented a super-secret socio-politico-economic theory that will magically cure all of Lebanons ills. They have simply taken the obvious realization that the system requires urgent repairs and tethered it to a political machine that is young and fresh, uncorrupted by the corridors of power, and ­ most importantly ­ propelled by adherents who are used to doing whatever it takes to get what they want.
The partys roots are in armed resistance, but its future lies in principled opposition to those who would perpetuate a system whose utter failure has bled this country of its economic energy, social cohesion and political stability. Its tenets may be based in religious belief, but its policies are firmly grounded in temporal goals such as the defeat of corruption and cronyism, the
creation of hope and opportunity, and the triumph of merit and accountability.
Hizbullah understands what most individuals ­ and a very few politicians ­ all over this country do, namely that the status quo, like the occupation before it, is unacceptable.
And herein lies the challenge for those groups and individuals who have grown accustomed to moving in the highest strata of national power: people driven by a genuine desire to bring about far-reaching change in this country have heretofore operated singly or in twos and threes, but now they are being presented with an opportunity to ally themselves with a powerful movement whose aims largely coincide with theirs, whose supporters are numerous and enthusiastic, and whose respect abroad grows with each passing day.
Since no one doubts Hizbullahs commitment to achieving its goals, and no serious-minded person would argue that Lebanons political system needs anything short of a drastic overhaul, those who have looted our foundering ship of state have been served notice that they will no longer be allowed to do so with impunity. The determined people who dared to take on Merkava tanks will not be intimidated into silence by a few detestable power-brokers whose bad suits, fat cigars, and loathsome amorality would have made them fit in nicely with the mobsters of 1950s Havana.
Nor will Hizbullah and its newly empowered allies have much patience for the allegedly well-meaning but obviously cowardly lot who have politely stood aside as the aforementioned parasites raped this country and its people. They too will have to change their ways or be swept aside.
An unabashedly selfish and shortsighted elite has had its way with this republic since its birth, banishing the middle class into exile or poverty and grinding the poor into ever-more appaling conditions. But now the real Lebanese, people of all faiths who do not have fancy yachts moored off our coast or gaudy villas perched on Frances, are gaining a voice of their own. They will be heard now, and one way or the other, those who ignore them will be turned out like yesterdays trash.
There is no need for things to get ugly, no need for people to take to the streets. That will only happen if the powers that be refuse to heed this warning and those that are sure to follow from Hizbullah and its growing team of allies. The days of running away from reform, and of having some so-called leaders rob us blind while others hide their heads in the sand, are over.


Finns buy Israeli missile tested on Lebanese civilians(6)

Finland has agreed to purchase an Israeli anti-tank missile that members of UNIFILs Finnish battalion saw being test-fired against civilian targets in south Lebanon over a 16-month period.
One Lebanese civilian was killed and at least four others were wounded before the Israeli Army declassified the weapon and placed it on the international arms market.
The Spike anti-tank missile will equip the Finnish Armys high-readiness brigades once Helsinki has concluded the terms of the $40 million deal with the German-Israeli EuroSpike consortium.
The Finnish Defense Ministry told The Daily Star it was unaware that civilians had been killed and wounded by the Spike but said it was unlikely that the decision to purchase the missile would be reversed.
The Spike is one of three advanced anti-tank weapons developed by Israels state-owned Rafael armaments company. The other two weapons are the Gil, a shoulder-fired missile with a range of 2.5 kilometers, and the NT-Dandy ­ also known as Long Spike ­ a long-range missile capable of being fired from ground platforms or helicopter gunships.
The Spike is the medium-range version of the NT-Dandy.
In August 1998, The Daily Star revealed that the Israeli Army was testing a new, top secret anti-tank missile in the South, and identified the weapon as probably the NT-Dandy. In response to the story, Rafael said that the only anti-tank missile developed by the company was the Gil.
The Spike was declassified by the Israeli Army a year later and the NT-Dandy joined its two smaller versions on the global arms market last November.
In a bid to increase international sales of the weapons system, Rafael formed the EuroSpike consortium with the German companies, STN Atlas, Diehl and Rheinmetal.
According to Janes Defense Weekly, the hi-tech Spike can be used in a fire-and-forget mode in which the gunner locks the warheads seeker onto the target and the missile finds its own way there. Alternatively, a fire-observe-and-update mode allows the missile to switch targets during flight via a fiber-optic wire.
The missile, which was sold to the Singaporean Army more than a year ago, is considered one of the leading weapons of its kind in the world, in direct competition with the US Javelin and two systems developed by Euromissile: the TRIGAT medium-range missile and the Milan 3.
But the Spike and the NT-Dandy have a less auspicious reputation among the Lebanese, where the battlefields of the South allowed the Israeli Army to test the missiles with impunity.
The first recorded sighting of the Spike/NT-Dandy in south Lebanon was on Feb. 25, 1998, when a missile was fired from the Israeli outpost in Taibeh at a civilian car in Majdal Silm, 7.5 kilometers away. The occupants of the car escaped unhurt.
Three months later, two identical missiles were fired from Taibeh at a squad of Amal fighters on the edge of Majdal Silm.
The Taibeh outpost was in the center of the area manned by the Finnish UNIFIL battalion. Finnish soldiers reported that the missiles were fired 2 minutes apart and made a noise like a jet plane. The first missile veered off course and exploded harmlessly. The second, the soldiers recorded, appeared to be searching for its target as it approached the guerrillas before plunging to the ground and exploding. Three fighters were wounded in the blast, one seriously.
Just 12 days later, another missile sought out and killed Mohammed Mouqalled, 17, in a wadi beside Arab Salim. Mohammeds brother Hussein, 20, suffered a burns to his back in the explosion.
On July 6, 1998, Norwegian UNIFIL soldiers witnessed the missile flying over their sector before crashing into a hill. The Israeli Army instructed UNIFIL not to approach the crash site.
On Nov. 13, 1998, three children were targeted by the missile in the same place where Mouqalled had been killed six months earlier.
Rami Abu Zeid, 13, was struck by metal fragments in the shoulder, chest and stomach when the missile exploded 3 meters away from him. His friends, Ali Darwish, 14, and his brother Jaafar, 10, escaped unhurt.
The last recorded firing of the missile was on June 20, 1999. At least two civilians, including a 3-year-old child, were wounded when one of three missiles slammed into an abandoned house in the center of Qabrikha, 30 meters from a Ghanaian UNIFIL position.
The Ghanaian peacekeepers logged the source of the attack as coming from an Israeli outpost on the border beside Margaliot, 8 kilometers southeast of Qabrikha and out of direct line of sight to the targeted building.
Witnesses from the village and circumstantial evidence, however, indicated that the missiles were fired from Taibeh, 5.5 kilometers to the northeast.
The five attacks against civilian or guerrilla targets over the 16-month period were all linked by the lengthy distances flown by the missiles, the weaving trajectories and identical fragments collected by UNIFIL, the Lebanese Army and The Daily Star from the blast sites.
Arto Koski, spokesman at the Finnish Defense Ministry, said the Finnish government had no knowledge of the attacks.
We didnt have any kind of information on these unfortunate incidents, he said.
Although Koski said he would inform his superiors in the ministry, he thought it would have little effect.
I dont think that the procurement program, which has been confirmed by the ministry, would change, he said. We based our decision on technical and commercial evaluations.
The UK and Germany have both chosen Euromissiles TRIGAT for their armies. But the UK has indicated it is seeking a lighter anti-tank weapon for its rapid-reaction forces. Germany is also considering purchasing a lighter missile. According to Janes Defense Weekly, the only real competitor in this category to the US Javelin is the Spike.
