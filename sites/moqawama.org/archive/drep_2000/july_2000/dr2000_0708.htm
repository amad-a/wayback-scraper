Hizbullah chief warns against "dangerous" Israeli presence in region (1)

Hizbullahs Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, warned Friday that Israel posed a dangerous presence in the region.

Nasrallah, who arrived in Iran on Tuesday, was welcomed as a guest of honor at Tehran University, where he spoke on the occasion of Friday prayers.

Referring to the withdrawal of Israeli forces from south Lebanon on May 24 after a 22-year occupation, Nasrallah, speaking in perfect Farsi, expressed hope that the Palestinian people will "succeed in achieving a great victory, just like the one in Lebanon" and warned against "the great regional danger, Israel."

He said Hizbullah will "attain a final victory" through a jihad, or Islamic holy war.

Nasrallah's words were warmly welcomed by a crowd that chanted: "Hizbullah will die (rather) than accept any compromise," and "Death to America, Death to the Zionist regime, Death to Israel!"

Throughout his visit, his first to Iran since the Israeli withdrawal, Nasrallah met with various high-ranking Iranian officials, including Iran's supreme leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei.

Founded in the mid-1980's with Iran's support, Hizbullah spearheaded the armed opposition to Israeli occupation.

The Lebanese government insists Israel still violates the border at certain points, a view partially upheld by the United Nations.

 

Round 1 to Beirut in talks with UN envoy (2)
Larsen talks to Lebanese about Israeli border violations

Terje Roed-Larsen said on Friday that a major donor effort to attract funds for the reconstruction of the South would be launched at the end of July with a preliminary meeting in Beirut.
In a bid to allay a series of misunderstandings, the UN special Middle East envoy, accompanied by his team, gave an accurate and detailed explanation of the circumstances surrounding the creation and delineation of the UNs line of withdrawal, the so-called Blue Line.
Larsen sough to quash speculation that he had brought with him to Beirut a new Blue Line that favored Israels security demands, declaring that the line has remained the same since first being unveiled early in June.
Let me be crystal clear on this, he said. There have been no changes to the line since the 6th of June, when we finished working on the line.
He said that he had given the Lebanese and Israeli governments a new map that showed the delineation of the Blue Line in greater detail.
A statement from President Emile Lahouds office said that Lebanon continues to object to three points along the Blue Line ­ Misgav Am, Metulla and Rmaish ­ which it says are inconsistent with the internationally recognized border.
It said that Beirut reiterates its position on the necessity of amendments so that the Blue Line is in conformity with the international border.
But the statement said the two parties agreed that Lebanons list of Israeli violations was in conformity with that of the UN, which is working to eliminate them, adding that it would resume talks with Roed-Larsen next Thursday.
A senior political source said that although Larsen insisted that the map he presented was no different than the one drawn by UN cartographers last month, the Lebanese team found differences in several areas. Larsen tried to explain that these were necessary technicalities, but the Lebanese remained unconvinced.
Beirut insisted that the map be adjusted to eliminate the discrepancies. At that point, Larsen and his team left the meeting room for a 30-minute private discussion. When they returned to the plenary meeting, Larsen suggested that this issue be postponed until his next trip on Thursday.
We won the first round, the source commented when asked to evaluate the days events.
The source said Lebanon welcomed Larsens efforts to promote a conference of donor nations, and denied there was any discussion of an imminent deployment of UNIFIL or Lebanese government forces in the South before Israels border violations end.
Larsen crossed over from Israel in the morning and held a brief meeting in Naqoura with UNIFILs commander, Major General Kofi Seth Obeng, and Timur Goksel, the senior adviser.
The UN envoy, accompanied by Miklos Pinther, the UNs chief cartographer, arrived in Beirut by UNIFIL helicopter and met Speaker Nabih Berri at Ain al-Tineh.
In the afternoon, Larsen held a three-hour clarifying session on the Blue Line with President Emile Lahoud, Prime Minister Salim Hoss and the director-general of Surete Generale, Major General Jamil Sayyed, at Baabda Palace. Israels continued violations along the Blue Line and the upcoming donors conference were also discussed. What was not discussed was the anticipated deployment of UNIFIL troops in areas vacated by Israeli forces. Lebanon insists that it will not authorize the deployment of UNIFIL troops to the border until Israels violations cease.
Flanked by members of his team, Larsen later told reporters at UN House that there was a three-step approach to the proposed donor conference.
We will have a planning meeting in July followed by the consultations, then a major donor conference, he said.
Larsen added that he hoped the conference would provide funds for several years of reconstruction and rehabilitation work in the South.
But Larsen clearly wanted to lay to rest what he called misunderstandings over the delineation of the Blue Line.
The first misunderstanding, he argued, was the belief that there is an established border universally agreed that is waiting to be dug up.
Larsen said the UN cartographic team had to rely on often very contradictory sources to complete what was an exceedingly complicated task in drawing up a line of withdrawal.
The second misunderstanding was that the Blue Line differs from the international border.
To the best of our knowledge, the Blue Line is where the international boundary goes, he said.
The other misunderstandings cited by Larsen are the alleged alterations to the Blue Line since it was first unveiled, political bias in defining the line, and the belief that UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan had announced that Israels withdrawal was complete based on Larsens advice.
The secretary-general was informed by UNIFIL that for the first time in 22 years no Israeli soldiers were on Lebanese territory. But they were subsequently found on Lebanese territory.
One of Israels complaints over the delineation of the Blue Line is the fate of Ghajar village, which has been cut in half by the path traced by the line.
At present, Israeli soldiers still send patrols through what is now the Lebanese half of the village. No Lebanese have attempted to enter Ghajar as yet.
Larsen said that Annan had sent letters to both Lahoud and Israeli Premier Ehud Barak urging both countries to act with restraint and caution.
The issue of this village is high on the agenda, he said.

 

Larsen: no disagreement between us on violations (3)

Lebanon and the United Nations are in complete agreement over every aspect of the violations of the so-called Blue Line committed by Israel, Terje Roed-Larsen said on Friday.
There is no disagreement between the UN and Lebanon on the number, where and what the violations are, Larsen told The Daily Star in an interview.
The UN Middle East envoy said there were about 10 violations of the Blue Line depending on how you count them.
Israel has admitted there are violations on some of the points but they dont recognize all of them, he said.
Larsen said that he had spent five hours with the Israelis on Thursday attempting to end the cross-border transgressions and that he would be raising the issue once again on Monday when he returns to Israel.
There is a will by the government of Israel to rectify the violations. It remains to be seen if deeds follow words, he said.
Larsen added that the Israelis were unhappy with delineation of the Blue Line and had disputed some stretches.
They say they have security arguments; we say we have cartographic arguments. I told them that its not they who define where the line goes; its we who say where the line goes, he said.
In many cases, the violations consist of patrol roads that stray north of the Blue Line in some places. The roads themselves are,t violations. When they fall north of the line they become Lebanese. The problem is when the Israelis use the roads, Larsen explained.
He added that UNIFIL has noted vehicles, bulldozers, and soldiers north of the line which are clear violations.
The Blue Line provides an unambiguous reference point, he asserted.
Either theyre in or theyre out, the envoy said. Theres no in-between.
One violation involves the theft of top soil from the Lebanese side of the Blue Line, 1.5 kilometers southeast of Metulla.
Hizbullah fighters stationed along the border have reportedly threatened to open fire on the bulldozers unless the machinery is moved and the violation ends.
On the proposed international conference to generate reconstruction funds for the South, Larsen said that it had required a strong effort to keep donor countries interested in the project once Lebanon began slipping out of the headlines after the pullout.
Although a preliminary meeting is scheduled for the end of July in Beirut probably mainly at ambassadorial level, Larsen expected the final donor conference to materialize after about two months of consultations.
The most important thing is to institutionalize the donor effort, to create a mechanism to generate funds over a number of years, he said.
Criticism over the delineation of the Blue Line has plagued Larsen and his team since it was first unveiled at the beginning of June. Although the UN envoy faced criticism from Beirut over the path of the line, the objections of the Israelis were considerably more heated.
Emotions have been running high on the Israeli side but not on the Lebanese side, he said.
The Blue Line is a boundary identified on professional merit and nothing else, Larsen said.
To help explain the line to skeptics in Lebanon and Israel, Larsen was accompanied by Miklos Pinther, the UNs chief cartographer, who carried with him a detailed map.
The map, a 1:50,000 satellite photograph, traces the line through and past recognizable landmarks such as Lebanese villages, Israeli settlements, ridges, hills and wadis.
Larsen also said that the differences between Lebanons version of the international border and the UNs Blue Line were considerably smaller than was generally believed.
There is maybe 98 percent agreement, he said. Both the differences voiced by Lebanon and Israel are very small.

 

Lebanon sentences 20 people for collaborating with Israel  (4)

The Beirut military court Friday sentenced 20 people to terms of one to four years in prison for collaborating with Israel during its occupation of the southern part of the country.

In addition, two of the 20 have been banned from residing in the south for periods equal to their prison terms which are, respectively, one and four years.

The latest sentences bring to 691 the number of verdicts since the court began its work on June 5, tryingsome 2,200 Lebanese accused of collaborating with Israel during its 22-year occupation.

 

Families of the Resistance's martyrs toured the liberated areas (5)

Mothers and fathers of martyrs toured the liberated villages of south Lebanon on Friday.

400 persons participated in the tour, organized by the "Foundation of the Martyr" and the Hizbullah's Central Media Office. They first stopped at the previous Israeli detention camp of Khiam where they listened to the explanations of liberated detainees, who spoke of their sufferings during their days of imprisonment in the detention camp.

The parents of martyrs then visited Fatima Gate crossing in Kfar-Kila. Upon seeing Israeli soldiers, they started chanting, "Death to Israel, death to America", and called for the liberation of Jerusalem. One of the group, 63 year- old Amin Abbas spoke in Hebrew through a megaphone, challenging the soldiers on the other side of the border.

"Israelis, hear me well," he said. "My son died in combat to liberate our land from your occupation. We are not afraid of you, and we will go on to Jerusalem, Haifa, Jaffa and Tel Aviv."

After touring the many spots where martyrdom operations had been carried out, a celebration was held in the town of Bint-Jbeil, during which speeches was made praising the Resistance and the martyrs.

 

MOSSAD AGENT SENTENCED TO ONE-YEAR SUSPENDED PRISON TERM IN BERN (6)

Switzerland's highest court sentenced a Mossad agent to a one -year-suspended prison term for a bungled wire-tap job in 1998. The Israeli agent, known as Isaac Bental, admitted to three charges against him, including political espionage, acting illegally for a foreign country and using fake papers. The Federal Court also barred the man from entering Switzerland. Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak voiced satisfaction with the verdict, which allows the Mossad agent to return to Israel immediately. In 1998 Bental and four other Mossad agents were caught installing a listening device in an apartment of a Lebanese-Swiss man. Bental claimed Abdallah al-Zein coordinated Hizbollah activities in Switzerland. Bental's defense lawyer said in his closing statement to the court that his client was quote "obliged" to collect information on al-Zein, even if it violated Swiss law. Al-Zeinn denied any links to Hizbollah, which spearheaded a resistance campaign to oust Israel from southern Lebanon. Bental is believed to be the first Mossad agent to be tried outside the Jewish state.
