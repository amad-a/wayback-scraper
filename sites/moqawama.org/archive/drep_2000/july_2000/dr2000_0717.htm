Beirut to verify end of violations(1)

A team of UN personnel and Lebanese Army officers are scheduled to verify whether Israel has ended its violations of the line of withdrawal on Monday, leading to the deployment of UNIFIL peacekeepers up to the border.
There are nine existing violations, according to the UN, most of them consisting of areas where sections of the old border fence and patrol roads stray onto Lebanese territory and small observation posts located north of the so-called Blue Line.
Israel promised last week to end the violations. Security sources believe that most ­ if not all ­ were amended by the onset of the weekend.
A Western diplomat in Beirut on Sunday described July 17 as a critical date for ending the violations.
Fulfilling the remaining components of Resolution 425 cannot occur until the violations are concluded. The government has said it will not approve the permanent deployment of UNIFIL into the area vacated by Israeli troops until Israeli forces remain behind the Blue Line.
The diplomat said that the international community was ready to give loans and grants to Lebanon to help rebuild not just the South but the rest of the country. However, this will only happen once peace and stability is restored to the South, the source said.
The results of the border inspection will be reported by General Amin Hoteit, the head of the Lebanese team, to the government, while UNIFIL will contact UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan on their findings.
Annan has taken a personal interest in following developments in south Lebanon since he announced on June 24 that Israel had fully departed Lebanon. The subsequent Israeli violations were seen as serving to undermine his personal credibility, as well as that of the UN.
UN Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen is in Israel to monitor and deal with any Israeli failure to fulfill its pledges to end the border encroachments.
The diplomat said that Larsen had proposed a mechanism to monitor border problems which involves establishing two committees: one Lebanese and one Israeli. Both will include international delegates. Lebanon and Israel have reportedly agreed to the proposal.


Army grants honors to 24 soldiers killed by Haddads militia in 1976(2)

The army buried on Sunday the remains of 24 soldiers who were killed inside the Marjeyoun barracks by members of Saad Haddads Israeli-allied militia in 1976.
The procession set off from Sidon and passed through Nabatieh and neighboring towns before arriving in Qlaiaa, where the families of the dead removed the coffins from the Red Cross ambulances and bore them shoulder-high to Marjeyoun.
Draped in Lebanese flags, the coffins were showered with rose petals and rice by mourners and onlookers until the procession reached the barracks.
The soldiers, who came from six different southern towns, were buried with military honors.
The bodies were exhumed from a mass grave discovered near Marjeyoun after the May 24 Israeli withdrawal.
