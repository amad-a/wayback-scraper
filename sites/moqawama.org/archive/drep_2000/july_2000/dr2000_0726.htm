Leader's Representative offers congratulations in Beirut

The Foreign Affairs Adviser of the Islamic Ummah's Leader Grand Ayatollah Sayyed Ali Khamenei arrived in Beirut Tuesday to meet Lebanese President Gen Emile Lahoud, other senior officials and political groups' heads.

Former foreign minister Dr Ali Akbar Velayati was met at the Masnaa crossing point at the Lebanese-Syrian border by Zuheir Hamdan, director general of the Lebanese foreign ministry (representing Prime Minister Dr Salim al-Hoss), MP Hussein al-Hajj Hassan, Iran's ambassador to Lebanon Mohammed Ali Sobhani, and delegations from Hizbullah and Amal.

Velayati told reporters that he was carrying an oral message from Sayyed Khamenei to Gen Lahoud(1)

The message ... is that Syria, Lebanon and Iran should take a unified position and coordinate their moves in order to have a common standpoint on all issues, Velayati said after talks at Baabda Palace. This message includes an affirmation from the spiritual leader that all three states must strengthen their ties, especially in the wake of the events that have taken place in the region, he said(2)
The envoy, who was also due to meet with Parliament Speaker Nabih Berri and Prime Minister al-Hoss, is expected to tour southern Lebanon, which was occupied by "Israel" until it withdrew its troops on May 24.

"I want to convey Leader Khamenei's congratulations to Lebanon's government, people and Resistance on the great victory which has been achieved in South Lebanon (the liberation of South Lebanon from Israeli occupation) which was not a victory for the Lebanese people alone but a victory for all the Islamic peoples," Velayati said(3)

Velayati had previously been in Damascus, where he met President Bashar al-Assad, Foreign Minister Faruq al-Shara and Damascus-based radical Palestinian groups opposed to Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat and his talks with Israel.

Velayati also met in Syria three visiting leaders of the Palestinian Islamist movement Hamas, to whom he "expressed Iran's complete support for the Palestinian resistance and emphasized the need to liberate all of Palestine and not sell off Jerusalem."

Syria has close relations with Iran, its main ally in the region, and leaders of the two countries consult each other frequently.

Both countries back the Lebanese Hizbullah movement which spearheaded the guerrilla war in southern Lebanon until the end of the Israeli occupation(4)


Israeli violations keep UNIFIL men away from border
UN envoy expresses deep disappointment(5)

UNIFILs long-awaited deployment throughout the former occupation zone was postponed on Tuesday after four new Israeli violations of the so-called Blue Line of withdrawal were discovered by the Lebanese Army.
The emergence of the new violations came hours after the United Nations declared that the Israeli transgressions of the Blue Line had been cleared up, a move that was to allow hundreds of UNIFIL peacekeepers to take up new positions on Wednesday morning in towns and villages throughout the liberated areas.
Prime Minister Salim Hoss confirmed the existence of new Israeli violations and said that he had informed UN Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen.
We asked him to do everything he can as quickly as possible, Hoss told The Daily Star. We were promised quick action (by Larsen) to end these new violations.
According to a statement, Larsen expressed his deep disappointment at this Israeli behavior and stressed that these repeated Israeli violations were unnecessary, counter-productive and totally unacceptable.
In the statement, Larsen also conveyed to Israeli officials in strong terms that this pattern of behavior had to be changed immediately. The UNs Middle East envoy has canceled his program in Beirut and together with Brigadier General Jim Sreenan, UNIFILs deputy commander, will personally go to inspect the border, it said.
The latest setback to deploying peacekeepers up to the border came three days before a conference of ambassadors and funding organizations to discuss means of helping finance the rehabilitation of the South.
Hoss dismissed the possibility that the continued delay in deploying UNIFIL would backfire on the planned meeting.
Were not to blame for what is happening, Hoss said. Were not to be penalized for something that Israel is responsible for. The fault lies with Israel.
The violations were spotted by General Amin Hoteit, head of the Lebanese delegation to the border verification team, who flew the length of the Blue Line Tuesday on board a UNIFIL helicopter.
Hoteit said that four violations were recorded: at Boundary Pillar 4 at Labboune, 4.5 kilometers from the coast; near Yaroun in the western sector; between BP32 at the Israeli settlement of Manara and BP33 on Abbad hill.
A UNIFIL officer said that the violation at Labboune consisted of a cement block which had been pushed by the Israelis 2 meters across the Blue Line. There was no apparent explanation why this had occurred.
The violation between Abbad hill and Manara consists of a road running alongside a border fence some 7 meters into Lebanon. Hoteit said that the road had not been there last week when the area was last inspected by the joint Lebanese-UN border-inspection team.
Another violation at Abbad ­ an observation post that strayed 80 centimeters across the Blue Line ­ was dismantled and reassembled inside Israeli territory last weekend.
A meeting in Naqoura between Lebanese Army officers, headed by Hoteit, and UNIFIL personnel was canceled.
The six existing UNIFIL battalions had made preparations during the day to begin the task of deploying troops on a permanent basis into the former occupation zone. The battalions presently patrol the area in armored cars.
The Irish battalions commandant, Joe Donagh, said that some 40 soldiers had been awaiting final orders to deploy to Aitaroun and Ain Ibl at 6am Wednesday. We were just waiting for the final word, he said. He said that a platoon of around 30 Irish peacekeepers were ready to deploy in Aitaroun and 10 more would move into Ain Ibl.
Initially the peacekeepers in Aitaroun would live in an empty house while the soldiers in Ain Ibl will stay in tents until permanent buildings have been constructed, Donagh said. Mine clearers will go in first to check the area but the rest of the troops will be ordinary infantrymen and usual logistics personnel.
UNIFILs Indian battalion was also ready to take up new positions along the border south of its present sector.
Major Rajinda Bansiwal said that more than 100 soldiers were preparing to deploy in Marjeyoun, Qlayaa, Khiam and Mari.
The unexpected delay throws into doubt not only the success of Thursdays initial meeting of potential donor countries but also the renewal of UNIFILs mandate. The present mandate expires on July 31.
UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan approved a six-month extension of the mandate on the condition that UNIFIL is allowed to deploy to the border and that Lebanon will strengthen its own presence in the area.
The European Union has made it known to the government that it is unwilling to channel funds to Lebanon unless the state is seen actively reasserting its presence in the border area.
UNIFILs postponed deployment has not affected a planned tour of the South organized by the UN Development Program and the Ministry of Economy and Trade on Wednesday by 40 ambassadors along with representatives of funding groups.


Hizbullah: Islamic Resistance to maintain preparedness(6)

Hizbullah Secretary General's Political Assistant Hajj Hussein al-Khalil stressed that the Islamic Resistance will maintain its preparedness to be ready for any scenario in which the Zionist enemy would attempt to launch aggressions against Lebanon.

Speaking at a political symposium held by the "Moslem Engineers' Union" in Tyre (South), al-Khalil said, "The Resistance's retaliation will be severe, to go with the enemy's aggressions."

We are certain that the enemy has nothing but evil intentions when it comes to Lebanon and Hizbullah which constitutes a deterrent force, he said.

Therefore, the Jihad thought will always be present and a major rule in our movement, he concluded.
