July Friday 07-2000

June Thursday 29-2000


Larsen coming with a new Blue Line map(1)

Terje Roed-Larsen arrives in Beirut on Friday to present a new map to Lebanese officials in an attempt to clear up Israels continuing violations of the Blue Line and allow for a deployment of UN peacekeeping troops to the border.
The UN Middle East envoy, accompanied by Miklos Pinther, the UNs chief cartographer, is scheduled to arrive in Naqoura from Israel at 9am. There he will hold a brief meeting with UNIFILs commander, Major General Kofi Seth Obeng, then fly to Beirut by helicopter.
Larsen will meet Speaker Nabih Berri at 11am, and President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss at 2pm. He will spend Friday night in Beirut and possibly remain for further consultations on Saturday.
Larsen discussed the continued violations of the Blue Line with Israeli officials on Thursday. Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak has given assurances that the border violations will end.
UNIFIL reported 12 violations of the Blue Line to the UN Security Council on Monday. The violations are consisting of security fences and patrol roads straying north of the Blue Line. At least four of the violations reportedly consist of observation posts north of the line. These are at Boundary Pillar 38, 1.5 kilometers southeast of Metulla; beside the Manara settlement; at the former crossing into Israel south of Rmeish; at the former Abbad outpost overlooking Houla.
A 13th violation was reported Wednesday in which Boundary Pillar 4, consisting of two concrete blocks, was moved deeper into Lebanon by the Israelis.
UNIFIL is reportedly sending patrols along the border every night to ensure there are no further violations involving the boundary markers or movement across the Blue Line.
Larsens latest visit comes amid mounting speculation that the Blue Line has been amended, particularly as Pinther is coming with a new map.
The UN insists that the line remains the same. UN sources said the new map carried by Pinther shows the Blue Line more clearly than before, in a bid to clear lingering confusion over the delineation of the line.
While Lebanon has objected to the lines differing from the international border, Israels protests stem from the belief that the path traced by the line undermines the security needs of the Jewish state.
In particular, Israel has objected to the UN decision to abide by the international border at Ghajar, 4 kilometers east of Metulla. The decision places two-thirds of the village inside Lebanon while the other third remains within Israeli-occupied Syria ­ creating a security nightmare for the Israeli Army.
But the delineation of the Blue Line was deemed final and non-negotiable by Larsen.
A new line would only prompt a fresh wave of complaints from whichever side feels that it has lost out in the amendments. Lebanons vociferous campaign against Larsens Line has waned as the UN line of withdrawal became a fait accompli. Instead, attention shifted to Israels objections to the Blue Line and the continued failure of the Israeli Army to fully withdraw from Lebanon.
The idea of an independent line as an arbiter of the withdrawal was preferable to the far thornier problem of having the UN attempt to define an international border between Lebanon and Israel. The UN has taken great pains to emphasize that the Blue Line is simply a line of withdrawal and it is up to Lebanon and Israel to delineate their mutual boundary.
But there has been no convincing explanation from the UN as to why the cartographic team did not base the delineation of the line on the 1949 Armistice Demarcation Line. The ADL exactly follows the 1923 boundary except for the last 6 kilometers in the east. The protests from both sides surrounding the Blue Line would have been undermined if the UN had stuck to a line it had endorsed 51 years ago and was still regarded as the legitimate international border.
The UN has admitted that key documents outlining the border were destroyed in a flood. Furthermore, the UNs map of the 1949 ADL has gone missing ­ possibly also a victim of the flood. Requests have been made of the UN from journalists and academics in Lebanon and the US for copies of the ADL map, to no avail.
The report of the Israel-Lebanon Mixed Armistice Commission on the redemarcation of the ADL between 1949 and 1951 would also be an invaluable source of information in helping define the delineation of the Blue Line.
Israel reportedly has a four-volume file of information on its northern border with Lebanon. This was delivered to the UN before the Blue Line was drawn up. Sources in New York and Washington said that the Israelis mounted an effective lobbying campaign to sow as much confusion over the delineation of the border as possible.
Lebanon inadvertently added to this process, the sources said, by waiting until the last minute before sending information, much of which was insufficient.
The outcome has been a line which pleases no one and has caused Israel to stall over full withdrawal, which in turn has delayed UNIFIL deployment.


Israel increases its border violations and its theft of Lebanese soil before Larsen's arrival at Beirut(2)

Ahead of the U.N. envoy Terje Roed Larsen's visit to Beirut on Friday, Israel has multiplied its border violations.  Late on Wednesday, the Israeli occupier innovated a military outpost,  west of the site of Ruwaisah Al-Alam on the occupied hills of Kfar-Shouba.

Moreover, the Israeli army intensified its patrolling along the Lebanese Palestinian border, through the villages of Ghajar, Abassieh, Nuhaila and reaching the Shebaa position.

Meanwhile, Israeli forces utilized bulldozers to shovel Lebanese soil then carried by trucks to the Israeli settlement of Mutella. The soil has been  stolen from an area labeled by the UN verification team as Lebanese property.


Israeli  soldiers injure two Lebanese near Fatima gate(3)

 Israeli soldiers have injured a Lebanese man and a five-year-old child along the international frontier. Mohamed al-Khatib and a young child were hit when the soldiers threw stones from their observation point at Fatima Gate beside the Lebanese village of Kfar Kila. Witnesses also reported property damage and said loud speakers at the Israeli position began to broadcast obscene insults in Arabic. Tensions have been mounting along the border. An Israeli military spokesman claimed a soldier was injured by stones thrown from the Lebanese side yesterday. Israel said it was the second incident in two days in which an Israeli soldier has been injured in stone throwing. In a message to UN peacekeepers in southern Lebanon, the Israeli army accused Beirut of being behind the quote border troubles. Israel filed 80 complaints over border incidents to the UN in one week. Israeli troops stationed along the frontier with Lebanon have been opening fire on demonstrators and civilians visiting the border fence. Thirteen civilians have been wounded by Israeli fire since occupation troops withdrew from the south in May.  

 

The Islamic Conference for foreign ministers condemned the Israeli border violations(4)

The Islamic Conference for foreign ministers denounced the continuous Israeli occupation of Lebanese territory,and affirmed Lebanon's rightfulness in recovering every inch of its land, as well as, the Lebanese people's right to resist the Israeli occupation with all available means until the liberation of all Lebanese land.

It also announced its support in granting Lebanon financial and humanitarian aids to face its many economical needs, especially in the recently liberated zone.
