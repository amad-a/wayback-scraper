UNIFIL goes to  the border today
Construction of illegal Israeli road holding up full deployment(1)

The first permanent deployment of UNIFIL peacekeepers into the former occupation zone is to begin at dawn Friday despite the presence of an unresolved violation of the so-called Blue Line at the Israeli settlement of Manara.
An Irish unit of around 20 soldiers has been instructed to deploy in Yaroun by 6am while a similar number of Ghanaian troops will set up camp opposite Manara, a UNIFIL source told The Daily Star.
The order to deploy a token peacekeeping force into the liberated areas came despite the continued construction of a road beside Manara which strays onto Lebanese territory.
The Irish soldiers will live in tents in a field just south of Yaroun near the border where there is another suspected Israeli violation in the form of a military outpost on Lebanese soil.
The UNIFIL source said that the Ghanaian soldiers will monitor further Israeli violations at Manara. The choice of Yaroun for the other deployment was to allow Irish soldiers to begin clearing landmines from near an old Israeli outpost, the source said.
The failure to rectify the Israeli violation at Manara has delayed the full deployment of some 500 UNIFIL peacekeepers into the liberated areas.
General Amin Hoteit, who heads the Lebanese delegation to the border verification team, inspected Abbad hill and Manara ­ the scene of two violations ­ during the afternoon. While the Abbad violation had been rectified to Hoteits satisfaction, Manara remained unresolved.
There is a road in our territory 200 meters long and 20 meters wide, he told The Daily Star. Hoteit said that the Israelis had stopped building the road temporarily two days ago, allowing the UN to announce that all violations had been cleared up.
They recommenced construction on the road after the announcement and they have not stopped, he said.
Hoteit claimed that the Israelis also violated the Blue Line by occupying a position on Lebanese soil at Boundary Pillar 18 on the border at Yaroun. But Hoteit noted that the Israelis had retreated five meters from the summit of Abbad hill, just north of Manara, in compliance with the Blue Line. The move grants Lebanon access to a tomb disputed by Muslims and Jews.
Israel says the grave is that of Rabbi Ashi, a Jewish sage who lived in the fifth century. Lebanon says a Muslim holy man Sheikh Abbad, who lived about 500 years ago, is buried at the site.
According to tradition, Sheikh Abbad lived as a hermit in the area about 500 years ago with religious followers who made mattresses which they sold near the Sea of Galilee.
Rabbi Ashi lived about 1,600
years ago and assembled the Babylonian Talmud, an interpretation of Jewish oral law.
Hoteit said that half the holy site was owned by Lebanon and the other half by Israel. The staircase leading to the site, however, will be shared by both sides, he said.
Earlier in the day, UN Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen traveled to Beiteddine, the summer residence of the head of state, to brief President Emile Lahoud on his meeting Wednesday with senior Israeli Army officers. Larsen returned to Beirut and met Prime Minister Salim Hoss at the Grand Serail.
Afterwards, Hoss told reporters that three of the four new Israeli violations of the so-called Blue Line had been amended and the last was in the process of being rectified.
We expect the last violation to be resolved very soon and consequently we hope that UNIFIL will be able to fan out in its zone of operations very soon, he said.
Hoss confirmed that a 1,000-strong force comprising 500 Lebanese soldiers and 500 members of the Internal Security Forces would also deploy in the liberated areas.
It is unclear when UNIFIL will be permitted to deploy into the border area in view of the continued violation at Manara. For the time being, the Israelis appear to have no intention of rectifying the violation. The path of the Blue Line at Manara was the source of heated arguments between Israel and the UN. The line runs alongside a civilian road on the edge of the hillside settlement, providing no space for the Israeli Army to construct a patrol track  and border fence.
Meanwhile, the UN Security Council unanimously approved on Thursday a six-month renewal until Jan. 31, 2001, of UNIFIL's mandate.

The resolution called on the parties to exercise utmost restraint and cooperate fully with the UN and UNIFIL.
It also called on the government of Lebanon to ensure the return of its effective authority and presence in the South and in particular to proceed with a significant deployment of the Lebanese Army as soon as possible. The resolution welcomed the establishment by the government of checkpoints in the vacated area and encouraged it to ensure a calm environment throughout the south, including through the control of all checkpoints.
The council would review the situation by early November and consider any steps it deems appropriate regarding UNIFIL.

Lebanon adheres to President's memo to Annan: land and hostages(2)

Lebanese high-ranking political sources underlined Lebanon's attachment to its right to recover all its soil, regardless of the pressures the the Israeli enemy tried and would try to exercise.

The official Lebanese National News Agency (NNA) quoted the sources as saying, "Lebanon will never backtrack on all that was stated in the presidential memorandum addressed to UN Secretary General Kofi Annan."

"Lebanon adheres to recovering every grain of its sand that is under occupation besides the liberation of all the Lebanese hostages that are held in the enemy's prisons. Lebanon will make every possible effort to achieve this through all the means granted by the international legitimacy until the achievement of a just comprehensive peace, which also requires complete withdrawal from the Golan Heights and giving Palestinian refugees the right to return to their land."

NNA quoted the same sources as saying that the achievements made by Lebanon through its negotiations with the UN, thanks to the firmness of the Lebanese standing, made the whole world realize that "Israel" has not implemented resolution 425.

Collaborators men face death for murders(3)

Six former Israeli-proxy militiamen who gunned down five Hizbullah fighters should receive the death penalty if found guilty, Chief Military Examining Magistrate Riad Talih said on Thursday.
Five of the militiamen confessed before the court that sometime between 1990-1993 they rounded up five Hizbullah members and shot them point blank in a militia position near Shumraieh on the orders of Aql Hashem, the commander of the occupied zones western sector who was assassinated by Hizbullah in an ambush in January.
The fatal shooting of the Hizbullah members ignited heavy fighting between the collaborators and the resistance that left one militiamen dead and another, who is now on trial, wounded.
The five detainees are Ibrahim Sayyed, Khodr Bedair, Ahmad Faqih, Hassan Qlait, Hussein Yehia and Hassan Mousa, who is still at large.
The six joined the militia in 1978. The five at the trial proceedings admitted to divulging classified information about the resistance to the Israeli secret service until they handed themselves in to Lebanese authorities after the May Israeli withdrawal.
The six militiamen, who were operating under the command of Hassan Musa, the head of the militias security in Kfar Kila, had bombed the prayer hall in Bzoun and killed its sheikh.
Talih also demanded that another two collaborators involved in the killing be sentenced to up to 15 years with hard labor.


Eight more Lebanese return from Israel(4)

Eight more Lebanese who fled to Israel during its troop pullout from southern Lebanon in May returned Thursday and surrendered to authorities, police said.

They were driven by two vehicles of the United Nations Interim Forces in southern Lebanon (UNIFIL) to posts of the Lebanese General Security and Internal Security Forces.

Eleven Lebanese had similarly returned from Israel to Lebanon on Wednesday.

Some 6,000 Lebanese and former members of the now defunct militia fled to Israel during the Jewish state's May 24 pullout from south Lebanon.

Lebanese military courts have so far issued sentences against 900 people among the than 2,200 Lebanese suspected of having collaborated with Israel.

But returnees have said that most of the Lebanese who fled to Israel wanted to return home.
