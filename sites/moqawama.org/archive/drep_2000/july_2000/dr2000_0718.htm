Hizbullah: our victory over occupation one of civilization and existence(1)

The victory achieved by the Islamic Resistance over the Israeli enemy was not a military victory in a contingent battle in which a contingent enemy was defeated, President of Hizbullah's Political Council MP Mohammed Raad said.

It was a victory of civilization and existence in a decisive battle at an important historic moment. It (the victory) hit the bases of the Zionist project which wanted to control our region, human beings and resources, added Raad who was speaking at a rally organized by the Martyr's Foundation to honor orphans' sponsors.

"We did not defeat the Zionist military machinery; those who evaluate the victory on these grounds are wrong. We have defeated the Zionist racist mentality which has paved the way for all aggressions and conspiracies that tore our Ummah and divided it into entities."

Raad unveiled that the enemy left in the battlefield documents that revealed a lot of information about penetrations in our society, calling for maintaining the victory through fortifying the society and returning the people of the liberated towns to their homes to live there instead of being displaced people in Beirut's Misery Belt.

"The bickering, rounds and bargains though which the UN envoy (Terje Roed-Larsen) wants to impose certain things on Lebanon will be in vain.  The positions of the state and the Resistance are clear."

We are waiting to see the efforts of the international envoy bear fruit and the pressures on "Israel" succeed in forcing it to end its encroachments, he said.

We are also observing the silence of the international community and the UN secretary general pertaining to the Israeli aggression of extending the detention of Lebanese detainees, he added.

However, they need to maintain this silence when the Zionists start crying out at the hands of the Resistance men who are determined to free all detainees and prisoners.  We said that we will be patient for some - not long - time, and now we say this waiting period is almost over, he concluded.


Hizbullah increases presence at Lebanon-Israel border(2)

 Hizbullah, which spearheaded armed resistance against Israeli occupation of southern Lebanon, bolstered four of its positions Monday along the border with Israel, an AFP correspondent observed.

Using tractors, the militant group put up soil embankments five to six meters (16 to 20 feet) wide and two meters (six and a half feet) high, along with blocks of reinforced concrete and battered containers at four positions along 15 kilometers (nine miles) of the eastern border section.

Hizbullah fighters have also stepped up motorcycle patrols of the border zone.

Hizbullah, which takes credit for forcing Israel to end its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon in May, put up 20 posts last week from the Mediterranean coast to Mount Hermon near Syria.

The moves come as UN troops wait to deploy into the area until Israel rectifies alleged violations of the border. Lebanon has also refused UN calls to send its army into parts of the southern zone where Hizbullah has set up.

Hizbullah, founded in the 1980s, enjoys wide support in Beirut and in Syria.


Lebanon, UN start verifying end of Israel: border breaches(3)

United Nations personnel and Lebanese Army officers began what was hoped would be the last formal inspection of the border on Monday to ensure that Israel has removed all violations along the so-called Blue Line.
Progress was slow, however, and members of the joint team estimated it would take two to three days to complete the task.
During the years of occupation, Israel annexed a 5-kilometer swathe of Lebanese territory along the border from Labboune, 4.5 kilometers from the coast eastward to Duhayrah, pushing the border fence northward into Lebanon for an average of 500 meters to capture the high ground.
The reason why they felt it necessary to move the fence became abundantly clear on Monday when Lebanese Army officers stood on the edge of an escarpment at Labboune and looked down on the neat rows of houses in the Israeli settlement of Shelomi 200 meters directly below. From Labboune the border drops vertically down the escarpment, granting Lebanon unobstructed views over western Galilee, including the border settlement of Hanita, beside Shelomi, and the coastal town of Nahariya.
Who needs mortars and rockets? A snipers rifle would be enough here, a member of the UN team noted.
Down at the foot of the escarpment, clanking machinery and the rumble of trucks could be heard as Israeli engineers feverishly worked to complete the new fence.
Four kilometers to the east, the team paused to inspect a section of the Blue Line at BP8, the location of an Israeli outpost. On the other side of the fence, the sleek profile of a Merkava tank peeked above the outposts ramparts. Israeli soldiers squatted beneath camouflage nets along the edge of the position and watched the border inspectors do their work.
Brigadier General Jim Sreenan, UNIFILs deputy commander and head of the UN team, said that the inspection was going smoothly but would take another day or two to complete.
It depends where General Hoteit wants to go along the border, he said.
A UN member of the team said that seven of the nine violations recognized by the UN had been cleared up. The remaining two are at the former Israeli outpost at Abbad, opposite Houla, where a machine-gun post is still located on the Lebanese side of the Blue Line and a small excavation site nearby.
I expect well find those two violations corrected when we get there, he said.
At a remote Israeli hilltop outpost south of Aita Shaab, a water tank had been placed just 2 meters over the Blue Line.
Standing before the outpost beneath the blistering afternoon sun, Sreenan pointed out that the errant water tank was now inside Israel. But Hoteit was not immediately convinced and noted that a stone rampart had been built on the Lebanese side.
The inspection team wound up its work in the late afternoon at the former border crossing south of Rmaish, which is now a Hizbullah position. Another patrol track had dipped inside Lebanon at this point for a few meters.
As the team inspected a new fence that cut off the old patrol track, they were watched by a group of armed Hizbullah fighters on one side and several Israeli soldiers on the other.


Islamic Resistance announces martyrdom of two of its men in a regrettable incident in Markaba(4)

The Islamic Resistance announced the death of the two dear martyrs Ali Theeb Haidar and Qassem Ali Ahmed Ammar who have ascended to the Supreme Kingdom after years of Jihad and patience.  They were martyred in a regrettable and painful incident as they were trying to settle a dispute which occurred in the liberated town of Markaba, to prevent its escalation and to obstruct attempts to undermine the great unity and noble moral positions that came with and after the victory in Lebanon after the Israeli occupation forces' pullout.

"We consider that what happened was a limited incident, even if painful," an IR communiqué read.

Hizbullah said it was "working with Lebanese security services which took the initiative to settle the issue with various parties."

"We consider that all these parties' compliance and their positive behavior is another proof that they have chosen unity to overcome all the Israeli plots," it added.

Martyr Ali Theeb Haidar (Falah), 34, from Bleeda, married, joined IR in 1989, took part in high military and educational courses, participated in several qualitative operations against the Israeli occupation forces, was martyred on July 15, 2000.

Martyr Qassem Ali Ahmed Ammar, 24, from Meis el-Jabal, joined IR in 1993, took part in several military and educational courses, participated in several Jihad operations, was martyred on July 15, 2000.

IR held funerals on Sunday for Martyrs Haidar and Ammar. They were buried in their respective villages of Bleeda and the nearby Mais el-Jabal in the western sector of the former occupied zone.


Occupation violates Lebanese airspace while teams were verifying encroachment removal(5)

The Israeli occupation's warplanes Monday morning violated the Lebanese airspace at a high altitude as a joint Lebanese-UN team was verifying whether "Israel" has ended the remaining encroachments on southern Lebanese territory.

A convoy of vehicles carrying Lebanese and UN officers started the verification operation from the Tallet al-Labbouneh hill, near the coastal border town of Naqoura.

The convoy was due to continue the operation by driving along the Lebanese-Palestinian international border, stepping down at the points of contention and verifying that encroachments were corrected.

At Tallet al-Labbouneh, the border fence seemed to have been pushed back a few yards toward "Israel", an AFP correspondent said.

Certification that "Israel" has stopped all encroachments into Lebanese territory could take hours or days, Lebanese officials said.


Civilians increasingly in danger from landmines in south Lebanon(6)

 In a corner of his garden in this south Lebanese town, Hilal Dia points to two olive trees, unpruned for 20 years, their trunks almost hidden by now dead grass.

Dia hasn't gone near those trees in all that time, he says, because three or four landmines lay buried around their bases.

"We bought some barbed wire six months ago, but we haven't had time to lay it out," he says.

For his neighbor, Colette, familiarity with something so dangerous and so close cost her a leg.

While she was picking nuts from her walnut tree she slipped on a rock, detonating a mine underneath that she had assiduously avoided for years.

Dia has been lucky so far, and Colette still has her life, but the number of accidents involving landmines in southern Lebanon has been alarmingly on the increase since Israeli troops withdrew from the region on May 24 after a 22-year occupation.

Since then, six civilians have been killed and 50 injured by exploding mines, with an official at one non-governmental organization dedicated to demining saying people out of unawareness or even a grain of fatalism about things risk "there being many victims."

The Israeli withdrawal brought with it the virtual overnight disintegration of Israels client militia which had helped Israel control the 900 square kilometer (360 square mile) zone that ran along the 125-kilometer (78-mile) border with Israel.

Timour Goksel is spokesman for the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL), which is soon expected to move into the formerly occupied zone as a peacekeeping force and one of whose most pressing jobs will be the demining of the area.

Goksel said Israel left behind 130,000 mines sown in southern Lebanese soil and the job of finding and removing them is a "dangerous mission" that will be a "long and exacting task."

There are 650 Ukrainian army engineers and Swedish specialists waiting to take on the task, which Goksel said will have a "military and a humanitarian aspect."

The military component of the job, viewed as top priority, will consist of "opening up roads" leading to UNIFIL positions the length of the border, where Israel has more often indicated the presence of minefields.

Israel has supplied the UN with maps showing the location of minefields it left behind, but which do not specify how many mines there are in each field.

The job of the UN sappers will be to neutralize the terrain surrounding hundreds of former Israeli army and its militia installations in the zone.

UNIFIL suffered its first loss to landmines in 1978, when a Swedish soldier was killed.

In the 22 years that have followed, UNIFIL has destroyed or neutralized 30,000 mines.

The work is complicated because the Israeli army and its militia were combating a guerrilla force and not conventional forces. That calls for defense geared more toward the use of anti-personnel mines -- which are sensitive to the slightest pressure -- than anti-vehicular mines that require greater weight to detonate.

After clearing up the border area, there is still the problem of clearing mines further north, particularly along the line that separated southern Lebanon from the rest of the country.

In these areas, which were battlegrounds for the resistance and the Israeli army, the mines have been left in a more anarchic way, with minefields not systematically mapped out, according to a UN officer.

With the return of peace, civilians have been going into the areas to reclaim their fields or to picnic on riverbanks.

Land movement represents another danger. "A peasant who had been working his land for four years was killed by the explosion of a mine that probably was not there in previous years," said Nasser Abu Lteif, the president of a Lebanese non-governmental organisation (ngo), Vision Association.


Collaborators: We have been thrown to dogs(7)

 According to a senior military source, the country is spending NIS 1 million daily to cater to the needs of the Israeli-proxy refugees living in Israel. The senior official explained that the absorption process is not proceeding well and as a result, the refugees continue to dwell in hotels and guesthouses at a cost of NIS hundreds of thousands daily, while apartments prepared for them stand vacant.

In a related matter, hundreds of the refugees and their family members on Friday demonstrated in Ashkelon and Netanya against what they called the poor treatment they are receiving from the country, adding, We have been thrown to the dogs!

Demonstrators, members of Israels former allied force in southern Lebanon, exclaimed that they have been forgotten in small stuffy rooms while given NIS 2000 monthly for a family of six persons.

We have become refugees in a foreign land, explained one resident of Ashkelon, who added that conditions for him and his family are unacceptable.

Another militiamen soldier expressed his absolute disgust, adding that he served as Israels ally in the militia for 15 years, and is now a refugee with no hope for a brighter tomorrow.
