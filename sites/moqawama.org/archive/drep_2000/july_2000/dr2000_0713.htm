Israeli court extends detention of top Lebanese prisoners(1)

 A Tel Aviv court Wednesday extended by three months the administrative detention of two Lebanese leaders, Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid and Mustapha Dirani, who were abducted from Lebanon by Israeli commandos.

Sheikh Obeid was seized 11 years ago, and Dirani six years ago.

Until now the court has always extended their periods of detention by six months at a time.

At the end of April the Supreme Court ordered the release of 13 other Lebanese prisoners it was holding without trial or beyond the expiry of their sentences, on the grounds that their detention was a violation of human rights.

The men were being held as bargaining chips in an attempt to obtain information about the fate of Ron Arad, an Israeli airman captured during fighting in Lebanon.

Sheikh Obeid and Dirani were kept in prison, although they had been seized for the same reason. The prosecutor justified their continuing detention on the grounds that they were a "danger to Israels security."

The Tel Aviv court accepted the validity of the argument.

However, the lawyer for the two men, Zvi Rish, criticised the decision, saying his clients had said they would no longer call for anti-Israeli attacks since Israel withdrew its forces from southern Lebanon in May.

"In fact, the danger to the state lies in keeping them in detention," he told Israeli radio.


Lebanon denies what the Israelis are spreading: an undeclared  agreement on Ghajar village(2)

Lebanon denied reports that said Lebanon  and "Israel" in an undeclared agreement accepted to share sovereignty over Ghajar village. Official sources quoted the Lebanese president Emile Lahoud as saying "there is no agreement declared or non- declared between Lebanon and "Israel" on the future status of Ghajar village".

Such rumors are being circulated on the eve of UN special envoy Terje Roed Larsen's return from "Israel" to Beirut.

An Israeli official on Wednesday told France Press Agency, "Israel" and Lebanon have reached an undeclared agreement through UN's mediation on the future status of Ghajar village.

This village has been divided into two parts, one part in Lebanon and the other in "Israel", after the UN demarcation of the blue-line, which divided the village.

The Israeli official added, "based on the non official agreement, "Israel" agreed that Lebanon controls the northern part of the village, and decided to stop, lateron Israeli soldiers from patrolling the village.


Larsen gets deal to end violations(3)

Israel agreed on Wednesday to rectify nine border violations by the end of the month, clearing the way for UN peacekeeping troops to be deployed along the Lebanese border.
United Nations special envoy Terje Roed-Larsen announced the agreement after meeting Israeli Foreign Minister David Levy. Larsen described a list of nine violations agreed on by Israel and Lebanon as minor.
Levy affirmed that Israel was working in full cooperation with the UN and would comply with the proposed alterations. A full Israeli pullout would allow UN Interim Forces peacekeepers in Lebanon to deploy in the former occupation zone.
Larsen said that most of the violations dealt with infrastructure, referring to military equipment and installations remaining on the Lebanese side of the border.
In other cases, Israeli fences were found on Lebanese territory, most of them no more than 20 meters beyond the border.
The most difficult issue to resolve will be the status of Ghajar, where residents are resisting UN efforts to run the border through the village.
The UN has determined that the village be split between Lebanon and the Golan Heights, an area occupied by Israel since 1967. The villagers oppose dividing their village and are pressing the UN to declare the area as entirely within either Lebanon, Israel or Syria.
Larsen would not update reporters on current negotiations regarding the villages fate but said negotiators were looking for a permanent solution which focuses on the best interests of the villagers.
In a statement issued after the meeting, Levy said that Lebanons failure to deploy its own troops along the border violated the same UN resolutions that had mandated Israels withdrawal.
The presence of UN troops in the region doesnt come in place of the Lebanese governments obligation to deploy its troops in the area, he said.
Larsen is due to arrive in Beirut on Thursday afternoon. He is scheduled to meet President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss on Friday morning.
In Beirut, the government officially asked the UN Security Council to renew UNIFILs mandate for six months, Lebanese diplomatic sources said.
In a message to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, the government also requested that the forces mandate be renewed under the same conditions like every time, one source said.
This means that the force would still be expected to implement UN Security Resolution 425 by verifying Israels withdrawal, deploying in the vacated area and help the Lebanese government spread its sovereignty there, he added.
UNIFILs term expires on July 31. Since the forces creation after Israels 1978 invasion, its term has been renewed automatically every six months pending the Israeli withdrawal.
Foreign diplomatic sources said that Annans report to the Security Council regarding Lebanons request was expected to include a note welcoming Israels pullout in accordance with UN Resolution 425.
Basically, he will welcome the fact that the withdrawal took place according to the three conditions he set in May for the pullout to be acceptable by the United Nations, one source said.
The conditions included the dismantling of Israels proxy militia, the removal of the militias heavy arms and Israels support for the peacekeepers efforts to verify the withdrawal, he added.
The secretary-general, according to another source, would also refer to the border violations and the efforts made by Larsen to put an end to these violations.


Another 46 collaborators sentenced(5)

A military court sentenced another 46 people on Wednesday to prison terms ranging from three weeks to 15 years for collaborating with Israel during its occupation of the South.
Of the sentences issued, 13 were in absentia. Ten of those sentenced were forbidden by the court from entering their home regions for periods ranging between one year and five years after finishing their prison terms.
The 46 were accused of joining the now-defunct Israeli-allied South Lebanon Army militia, entering the enemy state of Israel and collaborating with the Jewish occupiers.
The latest verdicts bring the number of Lebanese sentenced since June 5 to 796. At that time the court began holding marathon sessions three times a week to wade through the 2,200 people charged with collaborating during Israels 22-year occupation, which ended May 24.


U.S. to participate in the conference of donor countries(6)

The American ambassador in Beirut David Satterfield declared on Tuesday that his country accepted Lebanon's invitation to participate in the conference of donor countries for the reconstruction of the liberated areas in South Lebanon.

Satterfield explained that he informed  Lebanese PM Salim Al-Hoss of Washington's decision.

He said, "we want to cooperate with Lebanon, the donor countries, the world Bank and the United Nations in order to layout a program to aid Lebanon in the reconstruction of its south".

Satterfield affirmed that U.S. also wishes to specify the urgent needs of South Lebanon and use the available money to answer these needs.


44 Swedish mine experts join the UNIFIL(7)

44 Swedish soldiers, experts in the removal of mines, joined on Tuesday evening the United Nation forces in Lebanon.

UNIFIL sources made clear that the soldiers arrived in Beirut on Monday evening and were installed at Borj Kalawai, the general headquarters of the finnish UN battalion.

UN general-secretary Kofi Annan had announced, during his visit of the UNIFIL's general -headquarters in Naqoura on June 19, that around a thousand UN soldiers will be sent to Lebanon starting from July to reinforce the UNIFIL forces there.

There are at present 4500 UN Soldiers in south Lebanon coming from 9 countries: Finland France, Ghana, India, Ireland, Italy, Nepal, Poland, and Fiji.

ISRAEL TO END LEBANON BORDER VIOLATIONS SOON  UN(4)  

 A United Nations official said on Wednesday Israel had agreed to end its violations along the redrawn border with Lebanon by the end of July and he hoped peacekeepers could deploy soon after that. U.N. Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen said it was hard to say exactly when peacekeeping troops would deploy in south Lebanon "because we are waiting for all Israeli violations to come to an end".     "We, that is the U.N., have reached an agreement with the government of Israel how to handle that, and we have taken note that the government of Israel has committed itself to rectify everything by the end of the month, and I do hope that that immediately will trigger UNIFIL to deploy throughout the south." UNIFIL, the U.N. Interim Force in Lebanon, is a 4,600-strong multinational force which is to be increased by 1,000 men in order to deploy to the frontier. Israel withdrew its troops from southern Lebanon in May, ending a 22-year occupation of parts of the south. U.N. cartographers then demarcated a new frontier based on the 1923 border. An end to Israeli encroachments on the line is a key condition of the Beirut government to approve the deployment of UNIFIL peacekeepers along the border. The United Nations had hoped to deploy up to the border by July. Larsen told reporters before talks with Israeli Foreign Minister David Levy that there were nine Israeli border violations with some "infrastructure elements" in them. "There is full agreement between the government of Israel and UNIFIL," he added. "I have also taken note that the Lebanese government also agrees on it that these are the final list, as we see it, of infrastructure violations."    
 "I hope that the list we received from the U.N. is the final one and that more will not be found," Levy said. "It is a way...to delay the part that the Lebanese are obligated to...such as moving the Lebanese army down south."  Larsen could not confirm a report in the Israeli newspaper Haaretz on Tuesday that Israel and Lebanon had reached an "unwritten agreement" on the divided border village of Ghajar. The new border runs through Ghajar, leaving part of it in Lebanon and part in the Israeli-occupied Syrian Golan Heights. Residents passed a letter to Annan during his trip to Israel in June protesting any division. Haaretz said Israel had agreed not to encroach on the Lebanese part of the village and its troops were now patrolling only the southern part taken from Syria. Israels Defence ministry has declined to confirm the existence of an unwritten agreement. "What I can confirm is that the secretary general both in writing and orally has urged all parties concerned to show restraint, and so far for all social dimensions, the village remains unified," Larsen said. "This is very high on our agenda and we are continuously focusing on this issue, hoping to find a permanent solution which is in the best interests of the villagers."
