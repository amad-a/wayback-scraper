Hizbullah: some of the conditions made by donor states are hostile(1)

The victory which has been achieved (against the Israeli occupation forces) extended beyond a military one and was an ethical and principle victory, Hizbullah Deputy Secretary General his Eminence Sheikh Nahim Qassem said.

Some are upset by the ethical victory more than they are by the military one, he added at a rally to honor sponsors of martyrs' orphans.

"Those who think our performance has stopped here are ones who don't read the situation and ideas well," Qassem said underlining that Hizbullah has never worked for a partial project.

He said Hizbullah will respond to some voices and actions only in a friendly manner in order to foil any plans prepared secretly to shake or affect this victory.

His Eminence reiterated rejecting the carrot-and-stick policy which some of the countries that will take part in the donor states' conference are trying to impose on Lebanon.

If one probes some of these conditions, one finds out that they are hostile and based on stopping the resistance activity and accomplishing the policy which they had failed to market, he said.

Calling for patience and endurance, Qassem stressed that had the compensations which must be paid by the Israeli occupation for the aggressions launched against Lebanon been demanded, there would have been enough money to reconstruct Lebanon and pay back its debts.


PM rejects settling Palestinians, Hizbullah's arms pose no problem(2)

Prime Minister Dr Salim al-Hoss said Hizbullah's arms do not pose a problem "for the party has a sage leadership that is aware of the national interest."

We guarantee that Hizbullah will not do anything to block the mandate of the UN Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) in the south, al-Hoss told the French daily Liberation.

The premier reiterated Lebanon's position on settling Palestinians in Lebanon.

We reject the idea of settling Palestinians permanently in Lebanon.  We support the Palestinian cause whose essence is the principle of the right of return to Palestine, he said.

Consequently, we call for the implementation of UN resolution 194 which states their right to return to their homeland, he added.

The Lebanese constitution bars settling the Palestinians in Lebanon according to the amendments introduced in the Taif Accord, al-Hoss concluded.

 

Inspectors tie up loose ends on Blue Line(3)

The joint United Nations-Lebanese Army border verification team completed its inspection of the frontier on Thursday as the last of 650 Ukrainian soldiers arrived at Beirut Port with demining equipment.
The verification team, which has spent the past five days checking for Israeli violations of the so-called Blue Line of withdrawal, wound up the tour at Ghajar, east of Khiam.
The team spent the morning flying along the border from Naqoura to Ghajar to inspect sections of the frontier not clearly visible from the ground.
Work had been expected to be over by Wednesday, but difficult terrain and land mines have delayed the process.
But the team leaders were upbeat about the progress so far. Its going very well, UNIFILs deputy commander, Irish Brigadier General Jim Sreenan.
Everything, said Lebanons Brigadier General Amin Hoteit, is going smoothly.
The team later set out by four-wheel-drive toward the village of Ghajar, a large part of which was awarded to Lebanon under the blue line demarcation. The villages residents have been protesting the division of their community.
The team stopped outside but did not enter the village, where hundreds of men, women and children gathered on the fence shouting across a valley.
The officers then moved to an area across from the Israeli settlement of Metulla, where another Israeli violation appeared to have been rectified. The Israelis had cut down scores of trees before moving back the fence about 100 meters.
At one point in the Metulla area, about a dozen former Israeli-allied militiamen stood at the fence, watching the team work.
Only one suspected violation has been discovered. An observation post on Abbad hill ­ former site of an Israeli outpost ­ will be inspected by UN personnel on the Israeli side of the border on Friday. The Lebanese team is planning to clear a path though a 300-meter deep minefield before the border on Friday, allowing officials to examine a section of the Blue Line adjacent to the Manara settlement.


Lebanon sentences 31 more Israeli collaborators(4)

 A Beirut military court sentenced another 31 Lebanese on Thursday to sentences of up to 15 years in prison for "collaborating" with Israel during its 22-year occupation of the southern part of the country.

The verdicts bring to 900 the number of Lebanese sentenced since the court began mass trials of 2,200 people June 5.

Eight Lebanese were sentenced in absentia Thursday to 15 years in prison each, while the 23 others received sentences ranging from several weeks to three years behind bars.

The court, which had been holding three marathon sessions a week, plans to hold sessions on Wednesdays only until September 30 as other Lebanese courts observe a summer recess.

Israel pulled the last of its troops out of south Lebanon on May 24.
