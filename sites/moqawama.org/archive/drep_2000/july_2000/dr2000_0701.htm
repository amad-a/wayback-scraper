UN trips over withdrawal line it drew in the South(1)

The United Nations appears to have breached its own Armistice Agreement of 1949 by creating a withdrawal line between Lebanon and Israel which deviates from the Armistice Demarcation Line (ADL), according to Frederic Hof, a Washington-based consultant and expert on the borders of the Near East.
Hof told The Daily Star that the delineation of the Blue Line, created by the UN to measure Israels withdrawal from Lebanon, appeared to contradict the terms of the 1949 agreement by following a different path to the (ADL).
The ADL matches the Anglo-French frontier of 1923, except for the last 5 kilometers in the east, and is considered by the UN as the internationally-recognized boundary between Lebanon and Israel referred to in Resolution 425.
The Daily Star revealed on Friday that the Blue Line differs from the ADL in three places: south of Rmeish; at Jisr Hasbani in the east; and between Odaisseh and Misgav Am.
The latter deviation places around 20,000 square meters of Lebanese territory and several homes in Odaisseh technically on the Israeli side of the line. The anomaly arose when the settlers of Misgav Am built homes on Lebanese soil during the years of occupation after the military had pushed the border fence deeper into Lebanon.
The awkward situation in which Misgav Am found itself was not unique. The village of Ghajar in the east also faced the prospect of being split in two if the Blue Line was to follow the international boundary. In the case of Ghajar, the UN was firm and the line duly passes through the village, placing around two-thirds officially inside Lebanon, much to the anger of the residents and the Israeli Army.
But Misgav Am, apparently, was spared due to fierce lobbying of the UN and the US State Department by Israel.
Thus, the UN decided that the Blue Line would pass 200 meters west of the ADL, brushing past the edge of the kibbutz. The move allows Israeli settlers to keep their homes constructed on Lebanese soil.
If the Blue Line authorizes an Israeli military or civilian presence on the Lebanese side of the ADL, such an authorization would appear to violate Article IV of an armistice still recognized by the UN as binding on both parties, Hof said.
Article IV states that the ADLs purpose is to delineate the line beyond which the armed forces of the respective parties shall not move. The same article prohibits civilians from crossing the line.
Hof, the author of a forthcoming monograph entitled Beyond the Boundary: Lebanon, Israel and the Challenge of Change, said that the UN has consistently upheld the validity of the agreement.
A paragraph in a UN document entitled A/6701/ states that while the parties can revise or suspend the agreements, there is no provision in them for unilateral termination of their application.
As such, although Israel unilaterally rejected the agreement in the wake of the 1967 war, the move was never acknowledged by the UN. Lebanons stance is that the Agreement still stands.
The UN is right to insist that border demarcation is the job of Lebanon and Israel, not the UN, Hof said. Yet absent the agreed termination of the armistice by both parties, the UN simply cannot knowingly authorize the breaching of the ADL by either party, if that indeed is what has happened.


Lebanon denies that UN forces in the south was supplied by Israel with maps showing the locations of mines planted by the occupation(2)

The Lebanese army denied on Friday what France Press Agency quoted from Lieutenant colonel colonel Amine Hoteit saying that UNIFIL (the united Nations Interim Force in Lebanon) was told by Israel of the presence of 15,000 mines, and that it supplied maps showing the whereaborts of only 3,000 mines.

A communiqué from the army high command stressed that Hoteit never made such remarks, and called the media not to spread such reports without verifying that it is correct.


Mrs. Lahoud invites Khiam women to discuss problems(3)

Andree Lahoud, the presidents wife, visited Israels former detention center at Khiam on Friday, where freed inmates greeted her with lists of grievances.
Accompanied by Marjeyoun-Hasbaya MP Ali Hassan Khalil and Um Mehdi Sbaiti, head of Hizbullahs women groups in the South, Lahoud inspected several cells inside the complex, now an historic monument and tourist attraction.
A dozen women former detainees met Mrs. Lahoud and urged her to lobby the government for aid to those Lebanese who had been incarcerated in Israeli and militia jails.
The presidents wife was clearly moved by the plea of Kifah Afifi, who spent six years in Khiam. Im married to a former detainee, who spent eight years here. We have two children now. Neither he nor I have work, she said.
My husband and I suffered. But our children have the right to schooling, she said. We dont want charity. We want jobs.
Aida Jumaa, who spent eight months in the prison, said: The government should give our children scholarships.
Rana Awada said she spent a year in the prison with her father Zaki. My father underwent open-heart surgery after his release. But, despite our pleas, the government has not given us aid, she said.
She lashed out at the Council for the South.
Its always an ordeal to go to the council and ask for help. There are always excuses for telling us to go and come back later, she said.
The presidents wife said she would invite all liberated women detainees to meet with her to discuss means for sorting out their current social problems.
Women of the South are very special. She added. They suffered for years in support of the cause of liberation.
Lahoud later inspected the Health Ministrys clinic in Khiam, the Marjeyoun public hospital and Marjeyouns Orthodox Archbishopric, where she met Archbishop Elias Kfoury, Marjeyoun Melchite Archbishop Antoine Hayek and Nabatieh Governor Mahmoud Mawla.


Tribunal hears 25 collaboration cases(4)

The trial of 25 people on charges of collaborating with Israel opened at a military court on Friday, the ninth one-day trial since June 5.
The court, presided over by Brigadier Maher Safieddine, questioned the suspects, most of whom were former members of Israels now-defunct proxy militia. The others are accused of traveling to Israel for work. The verdicts and sentences were due to be handed down around midnight.
The tribunal, meeting three times a week, has so far sentenced 533 people. The harshest sentence was 22-and-a-half years in prison given in absentia to a collaborator, but generally, sentences range between a few months and a few years.
