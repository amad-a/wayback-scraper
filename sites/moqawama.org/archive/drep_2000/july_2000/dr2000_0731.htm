Unifil begins deployment in South Lebanon
Israeli violations down to one suspect outpost(1)

The full deployment of some 600 UNIFIL troops will be discussed Monday morning between Lebanese Army officers and key UNIFIL personnel at the Ministry of Defense, a day after the UN force strengthened its presence along the border.
Around 120 peacekeepers deployed to four points along the border early Sunday after UNIFIL received authorization from President Emile Lahoud.
The approval came following a meeting on Saturday in Naqoura between Brigadier General Maher Toufeili, the governments coordinator to UNIFIL, and the peacekeepers commander, Major General Seth Kofi Obeng.
Acting UNIFIL spokesman Daljeet Bagga said that the peacekeeping force was ready to deploy troops to 20 positions in the former occupation zone.
Everything depends on the Lebanese government. They have to give us the green light and then we can deploy, he said.
The number of Israeli violations of the so-called Blue Line of withdrawal has gradually decreased from around 13 two weeks ago to one last suspect position. The Lebanese border verification team want to inspect an Israeli outpost on the frontier south of Yaroun. The Lebanese suspect that part of the position might still remain on Lebanese soil.
A platoon of Irish peacekeepers, which set up position beside the Israeli outpost on Friday, has taken measurements at the site and reportedly believes that there is no violation.
However, the Lebanese team, which flew over the outpost on Friday, wants to inspect the site on the ground.
Ukrainian engineers were busy over the weekend clearing a path through the minefields running along the border to enable the verification team to access the suspect site. The Ukrainians are working hard, Bagga said. The moment that everything is cleared then we can take in the Lebanese.
The deployment of an additional four UNIFIL platoons to the border Sunday morning reportedly ran into difficulties when some 35 Nepalese soldiers were allegedly barred access by a squad of Hizbullah fighters to the frontier south of Rmaish. The Nepalese unit waited for two hours while Toufeili negotiated with the fighter having arrived at Rmaish by helicopter with the newly-promoted deputy commander of UNIFIL, Major General Jim Sreenan.
The leader of the Hizbullah unit ­ which has manned the former crossing at Rmaish since the Israeli withdrawal in May ­ reportedly said he had not been informed that UNIFIL was to deploy.
Both UNIFIL and Hizbullah played down the incident. In a statement, Hizbullah described as unfounded reports that its fighters had blocked the UNIFIL soldiers from deploying. UNIFIL said that the deployment went smoothly and no problems were encountered, contrary to initial press reports.
By late afternoon, the Nepalese were erecting tents and leveling the ground with earth-moving equipment prior to building more durable accommodation.
The other three deployments proceeded without difficulty. Around 30 Fijian troops traveling in armoured personnel carriers deployed at Labboune, 4.5 kilometers from the coast. A platoon of Swedish mine clearing experts and Finnish soldiers were dispatched to Jabal Hammamis, east of Metulla.
The arrival of four more platoons to the border raises to six the total number of UNIFIL positions along the frontier. Irish and Ghanaian troops deployed south of Yaroun and on the edge of the Israeli settlement of Manara respectively on Friday.
The six locations lack any physical barrier along the border and were the sites of previous Israeli violations of the so-called Blue Line of withdrawal.
The government has asked that permanent obstacles, either a fence or concrete blocks, are put in place to prevent Israel from repeating the violations. The presence of UNIFIL troops at these locations is expected to prevent the Israelis from transgressing the Blue Line.
Many of the Israeli violations have been of a petty nature, consisting of cement blocks pushed across the line or lone soldiers manning observation posts on Lebanese soil.
It remains unclear why these violations have been permitted, particularly as the Israelis have been repeatedly calling on the Lebanese government to send the army to the south.
Some observers suspect that the violations are not supported by the Israeli government but have been quietly approved by the militarys Northern Command. The hawks among the Northern Command apparently have little respect for Premier Ehud Barak and have taken advantage of the Israeli leaders considerable domestic difficulties to flex a little muscle.


Hizbullah threaten US embassy move(2)

Hizbullah leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah threatened to destroy the American embassy in Israel and kill its diplomats if the mission was moved to Jerusalem from Tel Aviv.
You can move your embassy to Jerusalem and send your diplomats there. But honest people can turn your embassy to rubble and send your diplomats back in coffins, Nasrallah told a rally in the village of Jibsheet.
This is the only logic that can be understood by the United States and Israel. Acting according to the advice of friends can only bring further humiliation and weakness, he added.
Last week, President Bill Clinton announced that he was considering the prospects for moving his countrys embassy in Israel from Tel Aviv to Jerusalem.
A US embassy in Jerusalem, similar to the usurping Zionist entity, has no future in the region as long as there are people willing to launch a war of jihad (holy struggle) and be martyred in such a war, Nasrallah said.


Presidents Lahoud and Assad discuss Israeli border violations(3)

Syrian President Dr Bashar al-Assad called President Gen Emile Lahoud on the telephone on Saturday.

The presidents who discussed the developments in the region agreed that "Israel impedes the peace process, along with the deployment of UNIFIL by continuing its violations" of the Lebanese-Palestinian border.
