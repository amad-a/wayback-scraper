Hizbullah: four steps to fortify victory over occupation(1)

The Islamic Resistance has marked the passage of a week since the martyrdom of Ali Haidar and Qassem Ammar with a massive rally between the towns of Mais el-Jabal and Bleeda, the two martyrs' hometowns.

Tens of thousands of people attended the event which was also attended by Hizbullah Deputy Secretary general his Eminence Sheikh Nahim Qassem along with MPs, politicians, military figures and Ulama (religious scholars).

The Markaba incident which led to the martyrdom of brothers Ammar and Qassem was a criminal act. Those who had directly perpetrated it are to be held responsible for that, Qassem said, demanding that the judiciary severely punish the perpetrators, "for they are murderers."

These criminals' political affiliation has nothing to do with the affair, he said.

We know that Amal Movement has not taken a decision to launch a war against Hizbullah.  Hizbullah also has not taken a decision to create an internal problem and unrest.  Even if this act was done by Amal members, the official political decision has nothing to do with what these individuals have done, Qassem said.

The punishment must be on the grounds of the individual crime that was perpetrated, he added.

The deputy secretary-general pointed out that Hizbullah has defined four steps to fortify the victory (against the Israeli occupation forces) which has been achieved in South Lebanon.

"First, to stay away from being dragged into small affairs into which those who have been harmed by the victory are trying to drag us,

"Second, calling on the state to assume its role and shoulder its responsibilities of preserving security besides taking interest in the fields of reconstruction and development in the liberated areas,

"Third, giving the liberation experience message and faith dimensions,

"Fourth, calming down the internal arena, on top of these issues is the electoral alliance with Amal, he concluded.


Annan: Now we can go in side by side
Larsen reports most positive meeting with Lahoud(2)

UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan confirmed on Monday that all violations along the Israeli-Lebanese border had been cleared up and hoped UN and Lebanese troops would be able to deploy along the frontier within the next few days.
Beiruts next move, according to political sources, will see Lebanese officials begin the process of carefully studying a map outlining the proposed deployment of UNIFIL troops in the former occupied zone.
Annan said that since Israels withdrawal in May, UN troops had been patrolling the vacated area but had not actually been deployed to the border.
I hope to see that done in the next few days, he said.
What had held us up was we were hoping to clean up all the violations on the (UN demarcation) Blue Line before we did that, Annan said.
The violations have all now been cleared. Ill be reporting to the Security Council today with the latest developments on the ground. I also expect to speak to President Lahoud of Lebanon.
And the UN troops would deploy to the border and the Lebanese would also deploy their own troops alongside the UN troops, he added.
Annan said UN and Lebanese officers were now working on this, so I hope in the next few days you will see our troops on the border, the secretary-general said.
The announcement came after UN Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen said Israel and Lebanon had stopped violating each others territories, paving the way for the deployment of UNIFIL.
UNIFIL is now able to report to the secretary-general that we have hit a point of zero violations of the line, Larsen told reporters after a meeting with Lahoud at Baabda Palace, during which the final Israeli encroachment was being dismantled.
After UN and Lebanese officers toured the border by helicopter on Monday afternoon to verify the situation, the UN turned over a map of UNIFILs proposed deployment to General Amin Hoteit, the head of the Lebanese verification team.
Political sources said the Lebanese team would study the map intensively before offering their comments on the new arrangements. The verification of the last point of dispute involved a 75-meter incursion over the Lebanese border into the village of Yarin, Tyre.
Larsen said he had his most positive meeting with Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss at Baabda Palace.
The secretary-general and President Lahoud agreed that when we hit this point of zero violations, UNIFIL should be redeployed in the South, Larsen said.
Political sources said the meeting between Lahoud, Hoss and Larsen also resulted in an agreement regarding the disputed village of Ghajar.
The agreement was that although the UN consented that two-thirds of the village was Lebanese, neither UNIFIL, nor Lebanese or Israeli armies would be allowed into it. The only force that would take control of security on two-thirds of the village would be the UN Disengagement and Observation Force (UNDOF), which is based on Syrias Golan Heights.
A statement from Baabda Palace said that the Lebanese side forcefully raised the issue of Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons. Larsen, sources said, promised to discuss the issue at a meeting with Ehud Barak when the Israeli Prime Minister returns from the Camp David meeting.
Speaking after meeting Speaker Nabih Berri, Larsen said the speaker showed him documents related to the issue of the Shebaa Farms, which are claimed by Lebanon. The UN official did not elaborate or take a position on the issue.
Larsen also assured that UNIFIL would continue to monitor the Blue Line on a daily basis to check for any possible Israeli violations, adding that the UN will act on the basis of information from the Lebanese authorities. He said any violations would be reported to Annan, who will then relay them to the Security Council.
Larsen added that the UN will continue to support the government through UN peace-keeping operations and will continue our strong support at the political level. We will also carry on and spearhead the development effort in the South together with the international donor community, UNDP, the UN system and the World Bank.
Preparatory measures will be taken both by the Lebanese army and UNIFIL Tuesday and hopefully shortly after that the (UN troops) redeployment will take place, Larsen said.
The scope of the armys deployment in the South was not decided, he added.
