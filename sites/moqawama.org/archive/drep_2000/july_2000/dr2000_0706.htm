Leader of the Islamic Nation: Hizbullah should continue its struggle until triumph over the Zionist regime(1)

Leader of the Islamic Nation Ayatollah Al-Ozma Al-Sayyed Ali Khamenei called on Hizbullah "to continue its struggle until reaching triumph over the Zionist regime."

Ayatollah Khamenei said during his meeting with Hizbullah's Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah visiting Iran since Tuesday, "The recent triumph of the resistance represents the first phase in the battle against the enemy, and Hizbullah must continue on this road with wisdom, planning and vigilance up until the final victory."

"The liberation of the occupied territories is the main axis in the war against the Zionist regime," Ayatollah Khamenei stressed.

"The recent victories in South Lebanon have greatly encouraged Palestinian youth in the war against the Zionist regime," Sayyed Khamenei told Hizbullah leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah.

He also assured the Hizbullah leadership of Iran's "moral support," saying that "the movement is well established and from now on, must make its decisions in complete independence, coupled with wisdom."

Sayyed Nasrallah, on his part, congratulated the leader of the Islamic Nation for the "recent triumph over the Zionist enemy". He affirmed, "Unity and Solidarity between the government, people, and resistance are major factors that led to victory."

Hizbullah's chief said, "the holy war against the Zionist regime will continue until the complete liberation of occupied Lebanese territories as well as the release of all detainees."


South conflict goes on, says Fadlallah(2)

Senior cleric Sayyed Mohammed Hussein Fadlallah said on Wednesday that Israels war against Lebanon was not over yet and encouraged Palestinians to take advantage of a fractured Israeli government.
We believe Israels war against Lebanon is not over on all levels, Fadlallah said during his weekly conference.
Fadlallah argued that Israeli Premier Ehud Barak was hoping to muster more support by withdrawing from the South. He said Barak wanted the pullout to help pull together his fragile government and force the Palestinians to accept his terms in the Israeli-Palestinian negotiations.
But the tactics failed, he said, as Baraks coalition has crumbled, while the Palestinians have been unwilling to give in.


Liberation not the end, says Franjieh(3)

Agriculture Minister Suleiman Franjieh said on Wednesday that even though the occupation was over, the war with Israel was still continuing.
Were still in a state of hostility with Israel. Israel is raising its tone once again on the issue of resettling Palestinians, Franjieh said in comments during a ceremony honoring Lebanese students and public servants at UNESCO Palace.
In addition to Franjieh, the event was attended by several other ministers and senior officials, including Education Minister Mohammed Youssef Beydoun, who represented President Emile Lahoud at the ceremony.
The liberation is not the end of the road or a substitute for a just and comprehensive solution that guarantees real peace for the region, Franjieh said.
He maintained that although all the peoples of the region had to enjoy peace, it must not be at the expense of Lebanon.
He also defended the Syrian military presence here, arguing that it was a requisite for civil peace. The brotherly Syrian presence was and remains a guarantee for civil peace and a support for Lebanons stand in face of Israels conspiracies, he said.
He added that even if Israels occupation was over, its maneuvers and threats to strike Lebanon again are not. Franjieh called for more rationalism to deal with the transition, describing the current situation as extremely sensitive.


Lebanon warns against a new Blue Line
UN envoy will need to produce positive results(4)

Israel and the United Nations are seeking to play down Israeli violations of  Lebanese territory on the eve of a new trip by Terje Roed-Larsen, the special UN envoy whose two-fold mission aims to defuse the border tension and press for a deployment of peacekeepers in the area.
Larsen flies into Israel on Thursday and arrives in Beirut the next day. His itinerary will include on-site inspections of the so-called Blue Line, the boundary the UN is using to monitor Israels withdrawal, on both sides of the fence.
The visit comes amid mounting dismay in Beirut at suggestions that potential amendments to the Blue Line would favor Israel and move the boundary even further from the internationally recognized 1923 border.
The adjustments have been made by senior UN cartographer Miklos Pinther less than a month after the establishment of the Blue Line following Israels May 24 withdrawal.
Pinther had helped map out the initial withdrawal line, and the reported proposals for its amendment come as a surprise to the Lebanese government.
While Lebanon remains committed to the internationally recognized border, the UN has said that Israels withdrawal would be considered complete when the Blue Line was respected.
According to UNIFIL reports, Israel is still violating the Blue Line in at least 12 locations.
It is believed that Pinthers new line aims to give Israel the right to remain in these areas.
According to diplomatic sources, Larsen will also ask the Lebanese government for a green light to deploy UNIFIL troops all the way to the border before July 31, when UNIFILs mandate expires. Beirut has adamantly rejected any change to the status quo until violations of the Blue Line cease.
However, Lebanon remains open to close cooperation with the UN, provided Larsen demonstrates a serious intention to deal impartially with the pending problems.
Meanwhile, a senior Lebanese official revealed that a few days ago, the UN sent a list of coordinates to Lebanese officials to help to mark out the original Blue Line on a map.
The list of coordinates as sent by the UN  differs from the initial Blue Line in eight places, the source said, rejecting any attempts to impose a new withdrawal line on Beirut.
As a result of this new complication, Lebanon sent a memorandum to Annan, asking him to send experts to Lebanon to verify that the two lines did not match. The experts are expected to arrive with Larsen on Friday.
However, this new obstacle was at the center of a meeting on Tuesday between Lebanese and UN officials.
Asked about his view on the matter, Irish General Jim Sreenan of UNIFIL told Lebanese officials that as an executive official rather than a technical expert, he could not personally assess the issue. This question, he said, should be examined by Larsen and the experts accompanying him.
Also during the meeting, UN and Lebanese officials reviewed Israels 12 violations of the initial withdrawal line.
Following these discussions, the political source said it was clear that Annan supported Lebanons position.
The unexpected development concerning the Blue Line will undoubtedly contribute to creating an atmosphere of unrest which could affect the new series of consultations between Larsen and Lebanese officials.
The source also said Lebanon was aware of UN efforts to put in the groundwork for a positive meeting, through the team of UN cartographers in Lebanon.
The UN team is trying hard to demonstrate its goodwill and desire to work constructively with Lebanon, the source said. This has been made clear by the UN officials to local media.
Lebanese officials welcomed these encouraging efforts and would continue to cooperate with the UN, according to the source. However, the UNs positive attitude will have to be translated into action.
Larsen will be asked to rectify infractions of the Blue Line and to acknowledge Lebanons internationally recognized border. ­


UNIFIL: Israeli border violations stops our deployment...(5)

UNIFIL spokesman Timor Goksel said that the Israeli violations of the "blue-line", drawn by the UN, keeps the UN forces from deploying on the Lebanese border with Palestine.

Goksel, in an interview with the Lebanese daily al-Liwaa published Wednesday, said the peace- keepers could only deploy throughout the former- Israeli occupied zone when the border violations are ended.

"As a peacekeeping force we have to deploy up to the border, but we can only do it with the agreement of the Lebanese government. But the Lebanese government will not give its agreement as long as the violation persists and Lebanon considers -- rightly -- that it is the injured party."


The Union of parliaments of the member countries in the Islamic Organization Conference praises  Lebanon's victory(6)

The Parliamentary Union of the member countries in the Islamic Organization Conference praised the great victory accomplished by the Lebanese people after a long route of struggle against the Israeli occupier. The Union stressed that UN resolution 425 should be applied and the "Israeli" occupier should withdraw from all Lebanese territory, including Shebaa farms.

The Union of parliaments also called for the release of all Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons, and stressed on the importance of supporting Lebanon financially to rebuild the liberated villages and towns.


13th batch of collaborators men sentenced(7)

The Military Tribunal passed sentence on former Israeli-allied militiamen on Wednesday, the 13th group to stand trial since the liberation of the South.
Five men were given sentences ranging from three weeks to two years, while several other trials were adjourned until a later date of the request of defense attorneys.
Investigating Magistrate Riad Talih recommended that five other collaborators trial receive terms from three to 15 years. The court issued warrants for eight more men charged with serving in the militia.
