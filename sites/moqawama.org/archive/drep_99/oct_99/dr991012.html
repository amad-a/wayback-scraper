October Tuesday 12-1999:

Introduction
An Israeli allied militiaman was wounded yesterday in an Islamic Resistance (IR) attack on Bayada outpost in Western Sector of the Israeli-occupied zone.

Meanwhile, a new Israeli violation of the April Understanding made by the Israeli occupation troops when they bombarded the residential neighborhoods of Mansouri village with several 155mm missiles.

One collaborator wounded by IR attack(1)
The Islamic Resistance (IR) team of martyrs Imad Ali Balhas and Hassan Youssef Balhas attacked, at 11:00, Hardoun outpost with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades. Accurate hits were scored, the mujahedeen told.

In the meantime, the IR unit of martyrs Mohammad Hussein Barad and Ali Mohammad Serhal fell upon Bayada position with the suitable weapons chalking up accurate goals.

For its part, Israel has admitted that one Israeli collaborator was suffered injuries in this attack.

New Israeli violation of April Understanding(2)
A fresh Israeli violation of the April Understanding, which bars targeting civilians, was recorded today (yesterday) as enemy 155mm rounds landed in the neighborhoods of al-Mansouri.

More than 15 rounds hit the town damaging the its main water reservoir besides the houses of Jamil Zeid, Riad Zabad, Ali Hassan Khashab, and the head of the police station in the town Hassan Ali Khashab.

The new breach of the understanding was the most recent in a long list of Israeli violations that went on throughout last week.

The outskirts of Baraasheit, Aita El-Jabal, Haddatha, Haris, Touline, Qabrikha, Wadi Slouqi, Wadi al-Hojeir, the Zahrani River gorge, and the Habboush Bridge surroundings were subject to sporadic shelling all day long Sunday, while the occupation's warplanes staged a number of mock raids over the targeted areas.

The April Understanding Monitoring Group is supposed to discuss the Israeli occupation's violations of the understanding which included wounding four Lebanese civilians, and damaging houses, properties, and facilities in five Lebanese villages in the enemy's air raids and bombardment..

For its part, the Islamic Resistance (IR) issued a communiqué in which it reported the following Israeli aggressions (3):
Between 11:05 and 11:25, the residential neighborhoods of Mansouri village came under fifteen tank rounds, three 155mm missiles and 12.7mm heavy machine gun fire causing damage to the houses of Jamil Zeid, Riad Zabad, Ali Hassan Khashab and Hassan Ali Khashab.
11:20, Jib Soweid area was shelled by three 155mm missiles.
11:50, two 155mm missiles were fired at the south of Jibal El-Botom.

ISRAEL DRAFTS LEBANON PULLOUT PLANS(4)
Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak's pledge to withdraw his forces from Lebanon by next July has been put in motion. Israels army is reportedly drafting a number of pullout plans and scenarios.

 Top Israeli army officials met for the first time over the weekend to review strategies for a possible troop withdrawal from occupied southern Lebanon. The meeting, chaired by chief of staff Shaul Mofaz debated plans for both a unilateral pullout and a withdrawal within the framework of peace agreements with Lebanon and Syria. Sources quoted in Israel said Prime Minister Ehud Barak prefers the pullout to be part of an agreement, but added a withdrawal would go ahead anyway in the absence of such a deal. But the Israeli military has for long opposed a unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon. Israels so-called northern command has proposed installing equipment on the internationally-recognized border that would make the area inaccessible. The families of some 2,000 Israeli allied militiamen in the south will reportedly be relocated to Israel in the event occupation forces withdraw from here. Barak's advisor Danny Yatom has warned Lebanon of a quote hot winter ahead, reiterating Barak's pledge to withdraw from the south with or without a peace agreement with Beirut and Damascus. Former army chief and now minister, Amnon Shahak, who once said occupation forces will remain in Lebanon for another thousand years if no security arrangements are reached, stressed if cross-border clashes ensue the withdrawal, Israel will strike Lebanon. But Hizbullah's deputy secretary-general Sheikh Nahim Qassem, whose group is the main resistance force battling Israeli occupation in Lebanon, brushed aside Barak's promises to withdraw from the country, saying the Israeli premier initially made the pledge hoping a peace agreement would be forged. Sheikh Qassem also stressed no one will stop a unilateral Israeli withdrawal from the south. Effective resistance attacks and mounting Israeli casualties in the south have created an outcry in Israel for ending 21-years of occupation in Lebanon. Thirteen Israeli soldiers have been killed in resistance operations in the south since the beginning of 1999.

Sealed-off Ainata: another couple expelled(5)
Reports from the zone occupied by the Israeli forces in south Lebanon revealed that the occupation's collaborators last weekend expelled Naef Halaweh, 62, and his wife Zainab Melhem Jomaa, 58, from their hometown of Kfar Killa.

The couple were on their way back to the town from the liberated areas when one collaborator militiaman called Khodor Hammoud Joumaa tore their passage permit and told them to go back to the liberated areas and never return to the occupied zone, or they will be arrested and imprisoned in al-Khiam detention center.

The blockade imposed by the occupation on the town of Ainata has entered its second week.

Those hailing from the town staged a sit-in Sunday afternoon in front of the Ainata Cultural and Social Center in Beirut. The protest was attended by MPs, social figures and hundreds of people.

The protesters urged the Lebanese government and the International groups to help break the town's siege and release its nine residents who have been detained at al-Khiam detention center.

Panel sends warning to IDF* chief rabbi in body parts mix-up(6)
IDF Chief Rabbi Major General Gad Navon is among three people who received warning letters yesterday from the Winograd Committee investigating the affair in which body parts of naval commando soldiers killed in Lebanon were mixed up. The three were warned that they might be hurt by the committee's findings.

The committee, headed by retired judge Eliahu Winograd, was appointed by Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz to examine how the body parts of Staff Sergeant Raz Tebi and of Staff Sergeant Guy Golan, who were killed in South Lebanon in September of 1997, were mixed up in the soldiers' burial.

The Winograd Committee is the second committee investigating the affair. It was preceded by a committee headed by retired Judge Tsevi Tal, which examined why some of the body parts were buried 10 months after the incident, near the graves, without the bereaved families being notified. The Tal Committee did not publicize any personal conclusions in the affair.

Over the past several weeks, the Winograd Committee heard testimony from most of the people involved in the affair, including members of the Personnel Division, the Medical Corps and the military rabbinate. Yesterday it sent a letter, according to Article 539 of the Court-Martial Law, to Navon, to the head of the Chaplaincy Corps's burial division, Lieutenant Colonel Itamar Guthartz, and anthropologist Baruch Ernsburg.

The article states that warning letters are to be sent out when the investigation committee believes the reputation of an officer or soldier might be hurt by its findings. The letters allow the warned to consult lawyers and to ask the committee if they may view the evidence, present their own witnesses and give their version of events. The warning letters do not indicate that those warned will necessarily be placed on trial.

It is not the committee's role to seek guilty parties, but rather to clarify the circumstances of the mix-up and to reach the necessary conclusions. When it completes its work, the committee will deliver its conclusions to the IDF attorney general, who will decide whether to instruct the military police to launch a criminal investigation into the affair.

The estimation in the Chaplaincy Corps is that the warning letter will make it impossible for the chief rabbi to postpone his retirement. Navon has already announced that he plans to retire in January, following hints by Mofaz that he should soon be replaced.

Navon and Guthartz do not appear to have contacted lawyers yet. Guthartz is expected to turn to the military defense within a few days. Navon's associates have said, on the other hand, that the chief rabbi might prefer not to consult with a lawyer at all. Navon believes that at his advanced age, 81, the military prosecutor will not choose to place him on trial, even if the committee's conclusions against him are serious.

A senior IDF rabbinate official told Ha'aretz yesterday that the atmosphere at the rabbinate following the warning letters was grave. "It's not that it was unexpected, in light of the questions asked by the committee, but there still is a fear that Guthartz will be saddled with the blame," he said.
