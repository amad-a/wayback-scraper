HIZBULLAH VOWS TO CONTINUE RESISTANCE EVEN IF PEACE TALKS RESUME(1)
" The Lebanese resistance movement Hizbullah has warned there will be no let-up in its armed campaign to oust "Israel" from Lebanese territory even if there is a revival of long-stalled peace talks.

"Resistance operations will continue until there is a full "Israeli" withdrawal from south Lebanon," said the Hizbullah official for south Lebanon, Nabil Qauq. "There will be no guarantees (of a halt in operations) during negotiations," he said in an interview with Magazine, a French-language weekly due to appear on Friday. Qauq said there were US efforts to relaunch peace talks between Lebanon and "Israel" that have been frozen since 1994. "But no matter what the result of these efforts, that does not change the reality of the resistance. Hizbullah will force "Israel" to pull out in disarray, under fire, to ensure it does not think of carrying out other attack," he said. Quaq said he was "skeptical" about the intentions of incoming "Israeli" prime minister Ehud Barak, suggesting his current stance was different from the one he adopted during the campaign for the May elections. Barak, a former army chief of staff, has pledged to withdraw "Israeli" troops from Lebanon within a year and resume negotiations with Syria. But Qauq said he believed there would not be a full "Israeli" withdrawal "but a redeployment with the aim of reducing losses." Hizbullah leads the armed campaign to oust "Israeli" troops from Lebanon".

FOUR LEBANESE RELEASED FROM KHIAM JAIL IN SOUTHERN Lebanon(2)
" Four Lebanese were released Thursday from Khiam jail in the "Israeli"-occupied zone of southern Lebanon, where they had been detained for several months, security services outside the zone said.

The Red Cross escorted Hassan Mohammad Shuayb, 72, and Farah Kesrawani, 35, out of the occupation zone, and handed them over to the Lebanese authorities. Shuayb had been held for 10 months, and Kesrawani for five. Kesrawani's wife, who was arrested at the same time, remained in detention. "Israel" also released Majida Suweydan and Haidar Issa. They returned to their homes inside the occupation zone. The Khiam prison is run by the "Israel" and its local allied. About 130 people are still detained there. Several of them have been imprisoned for a number of years without trial. Human rights organizations say they are subjected to inhumane treatment. Meanwhile, the "Israeli" allied militia expelled a Lebanese man in his 70s from the occupied zone Thursday, witnesses said. Khalil Dib Saab, 74, of Shebaa village in the western part of the zone, said he had been accused of "collaboration with the anti-"Israeli" resistance." He said militiamen had beaten him with rifle butts. Witnesses confirmed that his arms and neck bore traces of blows. Several dozen inhabitants of Shebaa have been expelled since the beginning of the year".

Report: IDF*, SLA** soldiers smuggling drugs and arms(5)
"Soldiers in the IDF's Lebanon Liaison Unit are collaborating with militiamen of the South Lebanon Army to smuggle drugs, arms and other items into "Israel", according to the IDF's judge advocate-general, Uri Shoham".

"In his annual report, recently distributed to the General Staff, Brig. Gen. Shoham states that the smuggling phenomenon also exists in units which are not part of Northern Command but sometimes do operational activity in South Lebanon. But he notes that because Northern Command bears overall territorial responsibility for the sector, "the major part of the smuggling episodes in the IDF are related to soldiers of Northern Command." In 1998, the year to which the report refers, Northern Command was led by Maj. Gen. Amiram Levin, deputy head of the Mossad and then by Maj. Gen. Gaby Ashkenazi.

"The report also notes "an increase in the number of drug episodes" in Central Command. Shoham finds "a worrying trend of the use of dangerous drugs by soldiers, as individuals and in groups, in army units in general and combat units in particular." Singled out in this connection are the Paratroop Brigade and the Military Police.

"The report notes an increase in indictments for sexual harassment within the IDF - 25 such indictments in 1998, as compared with 16 the year before.
