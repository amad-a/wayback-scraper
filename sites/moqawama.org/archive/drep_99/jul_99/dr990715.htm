Israeli theft of Lebanese soil continues
The Lebanese Foreign Ministry today (Wednesday) requested its permanent delegate to the United Nations to call for an investigation regarding the theft of Lebanese soil from the occupied zone by Israel.

The Israeli occupation force is transporting agricultural soil from the arable lands of al-Dardara and al-Khiam in the Marjeyoun district to a new agronomic settlement near the al-Mtilleh settlement.

A similar theft and violation was halted last year when the Lebanese government lodged a complaint to the UN Secretary General Cofi Anan.

Israeli troops storm the occupied town of Aita al-Shaaeb
Israeli troops today (Wednesday) stormed the occupied town of Aita al-Shaaeb in south Lebanon abducting Mohammed Moussa Rida, 23, a resident of the town who was then led to the Saf al-Hawa interrogation center.

NTERNATIONAL PANEL CONDEMNS ISRAELI RAIDS ON LEBANON
BEIRUT, LEBANON, July 14 (FUTURE NEWS) - The international panel monitoring a cease-fire in southern Lebanon has condemned last month's Israeli bombardment of Lebanon which killed eight people, injured 64, and destroyed power stations and bridges. The group also called on Israeli occupation forces and Lebanese resistance fighters to maintain calm ahead of an expected push for peace. The group met yesterday for the first time since Israeli Prime Minister Ehud Barak ended a boycott ordered by the previous Israeli cabinet. The panel said its delegates from the US, France, Syria, Lebanon, and Israel, have reaffirmed their commitment to the truce understanding. The committee accused Israel of "deliberately conducting air strikes against infrastructure targets" in Lebanon on June 24. Hizbollah was held responsible for firing Katyusha rockets on northern Israel that left two Israelis dead. The group permits resistance attacks on occupation forces in southern Lebanon, but bans attacks on and from civilian targets on both sides of the border.

LEBANON COMMEMORATES DAY OF DETAINEES IN ISRAELI DETENTION CENTERS
BEIRUT, LEBANON, July 14 (FUTURE NEWS) - The follow-up committee for the release of Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons today appealed for pressure on Israel by UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan and international organizations to release some 170 Lebanese detained in Israeli-run detention centers. Friends and families of detainees held a sit-in near parliament building to commemorate the national day of Lebanese detained in Israeli jails. Another rally was held infront of UN House in downtown Beirut. UN representatives were handed a letter addressed to Kofi Annan, calling on him and on the international community to demand the shut down of the Israeli-run Khyam detention center in occupied southern Lebanon. A number of human rights agencies, including Amnesty International, have confirmed that torture and maltreatment are systematic in khyam. Detainees are held without trial or charge. Israel has been detaining some 180 men, women and children in the Khyam detention center in occupied southern Lebanon and inside Israel. It accuses them of 'collaborating' with Lebanese resistance fighters struggling to liberate southern Lebanon. Most of the detainees are held without trial or charge, and some are even officially held as hostages.

Report: Hundreds of Lebanese have undergone torture in this notorious detention center in occupied southern Lebanon. Many have died in the Israeli-run Khyam prison. Some 150 Lebanese are believed to be held here. Their ages range between 12 and 85, and are accused of quote collaborating with Lebanese resistance fighters who are battling Israeli occupation forces. In clear breach of international law, the detainees have not been charged or put on trial. In 1995, 103 detainees were released. They recounted the torture they had undergone at the hands of occupation forces. In June last year, Israel released 60 detainees and the bodies of 40 resistance fighters in return for the remains of an Israeli soldier killed in September last year during a botched raid on the village of Insariyeh. Detainees too described their plight. A number of international human rights organizations, including the London-based Amnesty International, have condemned the detention circumstances at Khyam. Nine boys are detained there. They reportedly suffer from severe malnutrition and other ailments. Israel is a signatory to the Convention on the Rights of the Child, which prevents child torture and abuse. Reports issued by Amnesty International say that detainees in Israeli prisons are subject to flogging, electric shocks and hot and cold water thrown at them simultaneously. Other forms of torture include blindfolding detainees and locking them in a room with hungry dogs. In an unprecedented move, Israel's Supreme Court authorized the Israeli government to hold ten Lebanese detainees as hostages to secure the release of Israeli servicemen missing in action. Amnesty International condemned the ruling saying that explicitly legitimizes hostage-taking. The Israeli court acknowledged that the practice infringed on human rights but said Israel's quote "vital interests" took precedence.

LEBANESE MOTHERS WEEP FOR SONS HELD BY ISRAEL
BEIRUT, LEBANON, July 14 (FUTURE NEWS) - (Reuters) - After years of campaigning to free their sons from Israeli jails, Lebanese mothers said on Wednesday they feared that hundreds of captives would be forgotten in the efforts to resume Middle East peacemaking. "I want my son back.

We would have all turned into refugees if he and his friends had not defended our land. I hope the peace brokers will remember them first," cried Jamila Yassin on Wednesday, the national day commemorating Lebanese citizens held by Israel. Jamila's son Anwar has been held for more than 13 years in the notorious Khiam prison, run by Israel's militia proxies in occupied south Lebanon. He is one of more than 340 Lebanese and Palestinian residents of Lebanon detained by Israeli forces, some as long ago as 1978. Many were civilians kidnapped from Lebanon in contravention of international law, human rights groups say. Others were captured in the war of attrition waged by Lebanese fighters to end Israel's military occupation in the south. The prisoners include Abdeh Melkani, 70, the only woman still held hostage. Another captive, Suleiman Ramadan, refuses his mother's visits because he does not want her to see what is left of one leg, amputated while in prison in 1985. Many are maimed and severely depressed, according to the Beirut-based Follow up Committee for the prisoners. "Their trials, if they were ever held, were formalities," said Mohammed Safa, the committee president. "Holding Lebanese hostages and torturing our prisoners is allowed under Israeli law. It is a most outrageous violation of international law," Safa said. The detainees went on hunger strike on Wednesday to press for their release and commemorate their day while their families held strikes in front of the premises of international organizations all over Lebanon. "We plead to the international community to pressure Israel to release our sons," said Bassam Kintar, a protestor. His brother Samir, 37, was arrested by Israel 21 years ago . Fares Bouez, the former foreign minister who campaigned during his term in office on behalf of the prisoners called upon Israel's new prime Minister Ehud Barak to secure their release before resuming the stalled peace talks in the region. "Ending wars starts by the release of prisoners. It would be a positive signal by Barak," said Bouez. Barak said he wanted to withdraw from south Lebanon in one year and resume peace talks with Lebanon and Syria. Talks between Israel and Syria and Lebanon stopped in 1996. But Damascus and Tel Aviv exchanged positive signals on their resumption after the election of Labour leader Barak in May.

Family and friends mark yet another Detainee Day
Ranwa Yehia
Daily Star staff

Relatives and friends of those being held in Israeli prisons marked Lebanese Detainees Day on Wednesday with sit-ins around the country and calls for the closure of the Khiam detention center.

In commemoration of the occasion, President Emile Lahoud reiterated that the return of detainees was a government priority, adding that the country would not rest until every detainee was released.

Speaking to his visitors at the Presidential Palace, Lahoud stressed that the international community was aware of the human-rights violations being committed against children, women, men and elderly people.

At least 100 people joined a sit-in in front of UN House in Riad Solh Square called by the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in Israeli Prisons.

Hassan Saeed, his mouth covered with a piece of black cloth, stood carrying a picture of his brother Ghassan, who was detained by South Lebanon Army militiamen last year.

This is how the SLA are forbidding detainees in Khiam from speaking, Saeed said in a muffled voice as he pointed to the black cloth.

Jamila Yassin, 67, stood under the scorching sun listening to Issam Naaman, the minister of post and telecommunications, make a speech.

She gazed at a photograph of her son pasted onto one of the banners carried by demonstrators. As Naaman condemned continued Israeli human-rights violations and a 1998 Israeli Supreme Court ruling that legalized the use of detainees as bargaining chips, Yassin wept.

Her son Anwar was detained in 1986 after participating in an attack on an Israeli patrol in the occupation zone. He celebrated his 19th birthday a month after his detention.

The last I heard of Anwar was a year ago. I havent received any letters from him since, although I keep asking the Red Cross about him, Yassin said.

She believes the SLA is withholding her sons letters because she has given numerous interviews to the media about the conditions under which he is being kept.

Although other families are allowed to see their detained relatives every three months through the International Committee for the Red Cross, Yassin has not been allowed to visit.

Im not frustrated because my son sacrificed his life for his country. If I had the choice I would let him do it again, but what kills me is the flagrant violation of rights. How can a country that occupies another give a 30-year-sentence to someone who is fighting to liberate his country? Yassin asked as she covered her face with both hands.

Muntaha Haidar of Baalbek carried a white balloon with her sons name written on it.

Her son, Samir Mohammed Kharfan, was serving at an army post in Sidon when he disappeared in 1983.

I kept searching for him and asking around. Five years later someone sent me a letter saying that my son was in Khiam but the ICRC still cant find his name, Haidar said.

As she spoke, former detainee Afif Hammoud approached. Hearing part of her story, Hammoud old
Haidar that he never heard her sons name during the 10 years he spent in Khiam, but knew another man with the same family name.

Yes, Ahmad is a distant relative. He told me that when the SLA held him in detention, the first thing they did was ask him what he knew about my son. He was too scared and denied he was related to him, she said in reply before walking away.

Hammoud did not see much reason to celebrate the first anniversary of his release from Khiam. What for? Ive received no official recognition for sacrificing 10 years of my life for my country and very little government support. Not only that, every time I apply for a job and they find out I was a former detainee, they tell me that my services are not needed, Hammoud said.

He was among 55 detainees released in July 1998 as part of a swap agreement between Lebanon and Israel.

Mohammed Safa, the secretary-general of the committee, handed a UN official a letter of appeal addressed to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan and Arab League Secretary-General Esmat Abdel-Meguid.

As a committee member read out the appeal, which calls on the international community to exert pressure on Israel for the release of detainees, several people chained their hands together and covered their eyes and mouths with black cloths.

Bassam Qintar, whose brother Samir was arrested 21 years ago, shouted anti-Israeli slogans as he held up his chained hands.

In Tripoli, the mother of Yehia Skaff, who was detained in 1978, pleaded for her sons release. Similar demands were made at sit-ins in Baalbek and Nabatieh.

After 21 years, a father still waits
Mohammed Zaatari
Daily Star correspondent

Abu Alaa Mansour is still waiting for his son Ahmad to come home, 21 years after he disappeared following his participation in an operation against Israel in Bint Jbeil.

I dont know if hes dead or alive and so I keep hoping that I will see him one day. He would be 7
now, sighed Mansour.

Speaking from his home in Sidon, Mansour described the suffering he has gone through since his sons disappearance.

I developed a heart condition and underwent open-heart surgery three years ago. I was so afraid of dying. The only thing I could think of was that my son would return one day and not find anyone, Mansour said. Ahmads mother died six years ago.

I knocked on every door and talked to every politician, but it was no use, he said.

Many MPs who promised Mansour that they would raise the issue didnt do anything, he complained.

Every time a detainee is released from an Israeli prison, Mansour pays them a visit to ask about his son.

I almost gave up hope until one day in 1996 a former detainee told me he had heard my sons name in one of the Israeli prisons. But he said he never saw him, Mansour added.

Mansour has asked officials to pressure Israel to find out about his sons whereabouts.

Mohammed Safa of the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in Israeli Prisons, has tried to locate Ahmad but to no avail.
There are many cases like Ahmads and were committed to all of them, he said.

Safa said that the latest study by the committee indicated that there were at least 165 missing Lebanese thought to be detained in Israeli prisons.

Israel continues to deny this, but I doubt their claims. Israel admitted only two weeks ago that it had been holding a female detainee we did not know about for nine years and this is enough evidence for us to suspect that there might be more, Safa said.

Mansour said that families of detainees in Khiam and other Israeli prisons get to see their relatives every three months.

While they interact with their children and exchange letters, I sit here feeling like Im living in hell.
