Israeli occupation punishes Aita al- Shaeb
Going in and out from the town of Aita al Shaeb in the occupied zone in South Lebanon is still restricted by the Israeli occupation forces who also cut off the supply of water and power to the town.

The occupation's blockade against Aita al-Shaeb, which started ten days ago, is beginning to affect the steadfast residents, especially the sick and the elderly who are in dire need of medication and food, a resident said by phone from inside the sieged town.

Most bothering are the storming and arrest acts who included ten youth, the older one is aged at less than 19, some of then came to spend the summer vacation in their hometown, the resident added.

Last night, a joint Israeli army and intelligence force stormed the occupied town and arrested Mohammed Nehme Rida, 16, and Hassan Moussa Rida, 15, after releasing Zahra Hassan Rida, 17, a school girl arrested yesterday morning (Wednesday).

The blockade was imposed directly after the Islamic Resistance's (IR) roadside bomb attack against collaborator Ahmed Shebli Saleh who was seriously injured.

But when the Israelis failed to trace the IR Mujahedeen who staged the attack and returned to their base safely, the enemy forces imposed the blockade and started the arrests of innocent youth who not related to the attack at all.

LEBANON STARTS TRIALS OF PRO-ISRAELI MILITIAMEN
Five members of Israel's proxy militia in south Lebanon went on trial Wednesday, the first of 278 charged following the militia's withdrawal from a strategic area of Lebanon last month. Lebanon's military court is due to hold trials every week for militiamen who surrendered to the Lebanese army after the June 3 pullout from Jezzine, an area just to the north of the south Lebanon zone occupied by Israeli troops. One of the five whose case was heard Wednesday amid tight security, Toni Assad Nasr, was sentenced to 10 years in jail in absentia for "collaboration with the enemy Israel," legal sources said. A second defendant was sentenced to one year in prison and a third received six months on the same charges. Of the 278 facing trial, 60 are still on the run. About 200 militiamen have already been tried in recent years, and most received short jail sentences, while some were also been granted amnesty by former president Elias Hrawi. Militia commander Antoine Lahad has been sentenced to death in absentia for treason. The Jezzine withdrawal was widely seen as a practice run for the full Israeli withdrawal from south Lebanon pledged by Prime Minister Ehud Barak within a year. Earlier this month, the Israeli army planned series of measures to prevent the collapse of the militia and boost morale, which has been hammered by guerrillas attacks and by the pullback from Jezzine.

Detainees parents visit sons in Khiam prison
Following a two-week interruption due to the Intifada of the detainees, the Israeli occupation reversed the decision concerning the banning of parent's visits to the Khiam prison and allowed the International Red Cross to organize the weekly visits again.

Therefore, an IRC vehicle today (Thursday) transported eight detainees' parents to the Khiam prison to meet six of their imprisoned sons who are: Karam Mustafa, Rabah Abu Faour, Hussein Ayyoub, Ali Ghanwi, Majeed Turmos and Hassan Mouqashar.

Despite the bad conditions inside the prison, the parents were relieved to see their sons. They called on the Lebanese government and International organizations to work for the immediate release that Hezbollah initiated with the cooperation of the IRC to close the file of Lebanese detainees in Israeli prisons who are imprisoned with trials.
