Daily Report
 
 
July Tuesday 6-1999:

Satterfield "promoting" pressure on Lebanon(1) 
"US ambassador to Lebanon David Satterfield is once again "promoting" US pressure on Lebanon, as the restart of negotiations is approaching.

"Following a meeting with House Speaker Mr. Nabih Berri, Satterfield said that all parties should fundamentally back down from what he called as "violent acts in south Lebanon", if they want peace.

"Despite Satterfield's call to "strict implementation of the April Understanding" he avoided commenting on the partial "Israeli" pull outs".

"On the other hand, it is well known that Hizbullah will proceed with the military operations until the "Israeli" occupation army is driven out of south Lebanon and Western Beqaa, with consideration to avoiding civilians from the acts of vengeance sought by the "Israeli" army and the collaborators each time the Islamic Resistance succeeds in hitting or achieving a victory against them".

"Definitely, this will pressure the enemy to withdraw from occupied Lebanese villages and cities".

"Israeli" aggressions again and again
"Tyre fishermen are still being aggressed and harassed by the "Israeli" occupation for the third week.
This morning (Monday) the "Israeli" gunboats pursued the fishermen to the south of Tyre near Qlaileh where they forced them to leave their fishing nets behind, after they fired heavy machine guns at them.

On the ground, the "Israeli" artillery bombarded orchards, fields and woods in the vicinities of Haddatha, Aita al-Jabal, Sraira and al-Qatrani, while intensive warplanes' hovering accompanied the shelling"(2)

Between 10:35 and 15:55, six 155mm missiles were fired at Haddatha woodland.
18:35, the perimeters of Baraasheit town exposed to a barrage of 12.7mm heavy machine gun bullets.
Between 18:40 and 21:55, Jib Soweid area was bombarded by thirteen 155mm missiles.
21:45, several tank rounds struck Mount Al-Rafih.
Between 22:05 and 22:30, ten 155mm missiles slammed into Mount Al-Zaatar.
22:40, Qaissieh Valley was shelled by five rounds.
 

Hizbullah escorted Martyr Tfaili(4)
"Hizbullah today (Monday) escorted Martyr Abbass Mohammed Tfaili to his final resting place in a crowded convoy, attended by party officials, his family and a huge crowd of Baalback residents".

"Martyr Tfaili was hit during the "Israeli" air raids against the town of Baalback ten days ago.

"He was born in Baalback in 1970, joined Hizbullah's general recruitment in 1988, underwent several military and educational courses, participated in many confrontations against the "Israeli" occupation and was wounded in one of them in 1995".

Syrian assistance for Lebanon(5)
"Technicians at the Electricité du Liban (EDL) had began installing three power transmitters out of ten truck loads of power accessories that arrived in Lebanon Saturday coming from Syria as a gift.

"When these transmitters are installed the power feed for the Capital (Beirut) and the suburbs will increase to 18 hours each 24."

"On the other hand, Prime Minister Dr. Salim al-Hoss received a phone call from his Syrian counterpart Mahmoud al-Zouabi, informing the PM about President (Hafez) al-Assad decision to supply Lebanon with 60,000 tons of cement as a gift from Syria to Lebanon to help it rebuild the destruction caused by the daily "Israeli" aggressions against Lebanon".

Hoss: Support detainees(6)
"Prime Minister Salim Hoss announced Monday that a diplomatic campaign is underway to publicize the cause of Lebanese detainees in the run-up to the annual commemoration of their plight July 14.

He urged the nation to observe the occasion and called on all media to highlight this national cause and explain the suffering of detainees.

The Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in "Israeli" Prisons is highlighting July 14 as part of its general campaign to release our dear citizens, he said, adding that public and private media organizations should turn the date into a national occasion so that everyone shares in saluting the detainees and living the pain and suffering they do.

Hoss said that he had asked the Lebanese delegation at the United Nations and the Human Rights Commission in Geneva to stress the significance of the occasion and urge the international community to help return the detainees.

We also discussed the issue with the ambassadors of the five permanent member countries of the UN Security Council and asked all our diplomatic missions to mobilize efforts toward that end, the prime minister said.

He described the detainees as living martyrs who are resisting the occupation through their refusal to submit to the enemy.

We have an obligation toward them, to support, help and work continuously until they are free to come home to their families, the premier said. We hope that this happy day is not far off.

Enemy army complaining from soldiers' mothers(7)
"Outgoing "Israeli" war minister Moshe Arens admitted the failure and defeat experienced these days by the "Israeli" occupation army in south Lebanon".

"The worst problem ""Israeli"" is facing are the military operations staged by the Islamic Resistance against the "Israeli" army, Arens said.

"On the other hand, after one week of admitting the failure of the "Israeli" army, "Israeli" chief of staff Shaool Mofaz is talking now about difficulties the occupation is facing, but this time with the "Israeli" community.

"He said the influence and domination of the "Four Mothers Movement" is constantly increasing and interfering with army details through their links with the Press and the "Israeli" Judiciary.

"Mofaz complained that the mothers are using cellular phones (mobiles) to talk with their sons in occupied south Lebanon, supplying them with information about the training and movements of the "Israel" army".

Collaborators involved in drug and arms smuggling to "Israel"(8)
"Soldiers in "Israel's" Lebanon Liaison Unit in the occupation zone are collaborating with Israels militiamen to smuggle drugs, arms and other items into "Israel", according to the "Israeli" Armys judge advocate-general, Uri Shoham".

"In his annual report, he stated that "Israel's" northern command, which has responsibility for the occupation zone, is involved in the major part of the smuggling episodes in the "Israeli" Army".

"Last August, a sergeant from the Rihan post in the northern sector and a militiaman were arrested for smuggling 10 M-16 assault rifles and five handguns into "Israel", and selling them for $1,000 each. The weapons were supplied by the militiaman. The "Israeli" soldier also smuggled crack cocaine into "Israel".

Drug smuggling, which has reportedly been occurring since the 1978 invasion, has become a major headache for the "Israeli" Army, which has introduced extensive searches of all vehicles and soldiers departing the zone".

"The drugs allegedly enter the zone via the western Beqaa and are sold directly to "Israeli" soldiers. In some cases, soldiers hurl bags of hashish across the border fence to accomplices on the "Israeli" side of the border".

"Shohams report said soldiers in the elite Parachute Brigade, which serves in Lebanon, are particularly prone to drug-taking".

Givati: Not ready for combat?(9)
"In a few weeks the Tzabar regiment of the Givati Brigade will complete its operational service on the northern border. Within a short period, four of its soldiers were killed. Following a series of mishaps, the regiment commander was apparently forced to resign. Now it appears that even before beginning their operational duties, officers in the northern command had warned the regiment was not ready for service in southern Lebanon".

"On March 16, the regiment completed preparatory training for service (for various reasons doing two instead of three weeks) in Lebanon at the Elyakim training base. The training course was summed up as a disappointment. The regiment commander reported a series of failures in the training exercise.

"The auxiliary regiment commander had even more serious things to report. "I do not feel prepared for the missions I will have to fulfill," he said. The base commander listed the various mishaps in the training course. "The regiment is not qualified at this point to enter into operational service," he summed up. In conversation with officers of the northern command, the battalion commander also warned that his soldiers were not ready for the mission.

"Despite this, it was decided in the northern command to send the regiment for operational service. But the material they missed in their training course would be made up before they were actually sent up to serve. The first tragedy, however, occurred just a short time after the soldiers arrived in the north. After a fight between two soldiers, one shot and killed the other and then killed himself.

"A few weeks later another soldier was killed, this time by a roadside bomb placed by the Hizbullah near the Ras Bayada outpost in Lebanon. The investigation did not reveal any faulty action by the soldiers. In yet a third case, a soldier was killed and two soldiers injured when their jeep overturned.

"An investigation showed that the soldier had taken the vehicle without permission and had driven wildly "to test its performance." Following the wave of tragedies, Lieutenant Colonel Manny decided to resign".

"IDF* spokesman here admitted there were faults in the brigade's preparation. But the IDF said, "The preparation of the brigade before it went to the front was as planned. The tough events of murder and suicide and the jeep accident were not related to the brigade's preparation for operational activity."

"The IDF spokesman is right. It would appear there is no direct link between lack of preparation and the undisciplined atmosphere current in the regiments. However, the official response notwithstanding, serious discipline problems were clearly apparent even before the training course in Elyakim. These included a mutiny in the auxiliary regiment. Strangely enough, unlike the IDF spokesman, Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz often draws a direct line between discipline and operational functioning of the soldiers".
