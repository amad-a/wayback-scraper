Daily Report  
 
February Wednesday 24-1999:

Introduction
The heroes of the Islamic Resistance (IR) made an outstanding victory yesterday as they ambushed an "Israeli" elite paratrooper commando unit which was in its way to penetrate the liberated areas at Berkat Jabbour area in Western Beqaa.

The three-hour fierce clashes resulted in killing three "Israeli" officers and wounding seven others. The ambush was the worst "Israeli" Army loss since twelve "Israeli" navy commandos were killed at the southern village of Insarieh in September 1997.

In Jerusalem, the "Israeli" enemy Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu described the attack as "a very, very bitter blow" and a "very big toll for "Israel"".

IR engages in heroic saga in western Beqaa, deals painful blow to "Israeli" "elite" forces… (1)
"The Islamic Resistance (IR) issued the following communiqué about the western Beqaa confrontations which took the lives of 3 "Israeli" elite officers and wounded seven others:

"A fresh victory has been made in the western Beqaa by the heroes of the Islamic Resistance who affirmed to the enemy that all its desperate attempts to recover the initiative are doomed to fail.

"The Mujahedeen taught the "Israeli" enemy a tough lesson and dealt a painful blow to its elite forces when the Mujahedeen engaged in a heroic battle against an "Israeli" force which tried to penetrate the liberated areas. IR men killed or injured the force troops and returned safely to bases, after taking enemy weapons.

"IR special forces' squad at 00:30 (Tuesday) ambushed a Zionist commando unit which tried to infiltrate the liberated areas near the Jabbour Hilltops and Maidoun (western Beqaa).

"When the enemy force reached the ambush spot, it was blitzed by the Mujahedeen who used their machine guns and hand grenades.

"The fierce clashes lasted for three hours. Fifteen casualties were inflicted on the enemy troops who were in a state of high chaos and confusion.

"The aggressor force was not able to retreat as the Mujahedeen completely controlled the battlefield. The enemy had to bring in its helicopters which used their heavy machine guns' fire in an attempt to break the siege off the targeted force and pull back the casualties.

"The enemy's attempts failed as IR's Artillery Reinforcement Unit (ARU) targeted the enemy's retreat lines and helicopter landing sites.

"At 3:30 a.m., the enemy introduced ground forces backed by vehicles and helicopters to back the targeted force.

"The back-up forces were intercepted by the Mujahedeen's fire which scored direct hits.

"The Mujahedeen returned safely to their bases after bringing with them enemy weapons."

In other operations against "Israeli" occupation forces, IR's squad of Martyrs Ali Oullaiq and Habib Tarhini at 6:00 a.m. (Tuesday) blitzed the "Israeli" occupation's outpost in al-Qalaa.

The Mujahedeen were able to destroy the outpost's western fortification.

In the meantime, IR's ARU was pounding the same outpost with the appropriate weapons, while squad of Martyrs Hassan Tarhini and Moussa Fahs staged a similar attack on the occupation's outpost in al-Dabsheh scoring direct hits.

At 9:40 a.m, IR's squad of Martyrs Hassan Krayyem and Hamad Qatbai targeted enemy movements in the Blatt outpost, while squad of Martyrs Wissam Fsaie and Fadi Abboud attacked the Radar-Bayyada outpost and destroyed its western fortification's radar.

Moreover, IR's squad of Martyrs Zahi Haidar Ahmad and Ghassan Za'atar at 13:30 attacked the occupation's outpost in Beir Kallab and destroyed the outpost's main fortification and radar.

In the meantime, squad of Martyrs Mohammed Mazloum and Ahmed Shahrour attacked the Sojod outpost, while squad of Martyrs Ali Khalil and Ahmed Hariri targeted the enemy's outpost in Kassaret al-Oroush.

The Mujahedeen used machine guns, mortars and rocket propelled grenades in the three attacks, scoring direct hits in the targeted outposts.

3 "Israelis" killed in Hizbullah ambush . This year’s first fatalities intensify pressure to withdraw from south… (2)
"In their bloodiest ambush in 17 months, Hizbullah fighters Tuesday killed three "Israeli" officers and wounded five other soldiers, rekindling the debate in "Israel" over its occupation policy with calls for a punitive military offensive competing with demands for a withdrawal".

With the "Israeli" army reeling over the loss of officers from an elite commando unit, an embattled Premier Benjamin Netanyahu demanded that Syria rein in Hizbullah guerrillas.

The fatal ambush was “a very, very bitter blow” and a “very big toll for "Israel". It’s a hard day,” he told reporters in Jerusalem.

"The battle shattered the recent lull in the volatile period before "Israeli" elections in May.

"Describing the pitched battle as a “heroic epic,” Hizbullah said a “special forces” team had lain in wait for the "Israeli" patrol. “The resistance surprised the "Israelis" when they were four meters away and fired at them with machine guns and hand grenades,” a statement from the group said.

The "Israeli" commanding officer, Major Eitan Balahsan, 30, was killed almost instantly along with
two lieutenants when the guerrillas opened fire. The wounded included another officer. One man was in serious condition, three in moderate condition and one slightly hurt.

The guerrillas were able to withdraw under covering mortar fire from their colleagues leaving the "Israeli" troops “in a state of extreme loss and confusion,” the Hizbullah statement said.

"Israeli" warplanes fired several missiles into the area while helicopter gunships combed the surrounding wadis and artillery units fired around 200 shells in a bid to hit the retreating fighters.

Other helicopters attempted to land to evacuate the casualties and withdraw the troops but came under heavy ground fire from Hizbullah mortar teams.

The fighting continued for three hours with warplanes returning at dawn to launch more missiles into the area.

"The clash resulted in the first "Israeli" fatalities this year and the highest casualties in south Lebanon since September 1997, when 12 soldiers were killed in a bungled commando raid on Insariyeh.

Last year, 24 "Israeli" soldiers were killed in the south and scores more were wounded.

Security sources remarked that the clash had occurred in a usually quiet sector of the zone and suggested that the "Israeli" commandos were after a specific target rather than on a routine search-and-destroy mission".

"Observers noted that given the general lack of hostilities in the area, Hizbullah’s ambush was unlikely to have been staged by chance. Instead the guerrillas appeared to have had advance information of an "Israeli" unit heading north out of the zone".

"The ambush was well-planned. The ambush squad would have remained in contact with the mortar teams to the rear to relay the map coordinates pinpointing the approaching patrol, allowing the mortars to be calibrated. It was a testimony to the accuracy of the Hizbullah mortar operators who were dropping rounds very close to their comrades once the shooting began".

"Later Tuesday, at a mountain cave less than 5 kilometers from the scene of the battle, the five guerrillas who staged the ambush displayed a blood-stained shirt and M16 rifle they said belonged to one of the dead "Israeli" officers".

“We expect "Israel" to retaliate and we’re ready for them. It’s business as usual,” said one of the unidentified fighters, clutching a balaclava in his hand.

In "Israel", Avigdor Kahalani, "Israel's" Internal Security minister, repeated his view that Lebanon’s infrastructure should be targeted every time an "Israeli" soldier is killed in the south.

Yossi Beilin, a Labor party MP and leading advocate of a unilateral withdrawal, wrote to Defense Minister Moshe Arens and urged him to pull the troops out of Lebanon.

“I call on you to end the foolish "Israeli" march and give life to soldiers who may otherwise have to continue to sacrifice their lives on Lebanon’s moloch (child-devouring) altar,” he wrote.

Qassem: the "Israeli" unit was trying to make a security achievement… (3)
"The "Israelis" were infiltrating to do something which can be recorded as an achievement, but the resistance men were waiting for them", Hezbollah Deputy Secretary-General his Eminence Sheikh Naem Qassem said.

"Asked whether "Israel" will use this operation to wage an aggression on Lebanon, he said, ""Israel" is always the aggressor. It does not need pretexts or justifications. The mere occupation is an aggression which is inflicting harm on our civilians, but we will always be ready to confront these aggressions."

"Regarding Netanyahu's proposal for withdrawal from south Lebanon, Sheikh Qassem said "The "Israeli" position is clear: they want security arrangements in exchange for withdrawal, but they talk about this position in different ways, once they talk about withdrawal in exchange for Lebanese army deployment, on other occasions they speak about a withdrawal for indirect agreement or arrangements to insure that settlements will not be in danger."

"We got used to these statements being reiterated. They carry nothing as far as we are concerned, because they are reiterations of the "Israeli" position which means "conditional withdrawal", and we reject any conditional withdrawal for it is impossible for us to reward the occupation, he said.

If they want to pull out, the road is there; they must withdraw from our land as they entered", he added.

The operation is a hard response to barb-wiring Arnoun, says al-Khalil… (4)
"As the unjust enemy leadership proceeds with staging more aggressions by setting artificial barb wires in the dear town of Arnoun and tries to grab more of our soil in front of the international community, the hard response came today by the arms of the Resistance men, Hezbollah Secretary general's political Aide Hajj Hussein Al-Khalil said today (yesterday)".

"Commenting on IR's qualitative operation in which 3 occupation officers were killed, al-Khalil said "the Mujahedeen's response came to eradicate all the enemy's absurd illusions and to insure that the occupation's officers and soldiers will never be safe, for all the measures adopted by the enemy command will never protect them from the resistance's fist, but will increase the strength of our resistance men and their resolve to continue the Jihad and liberation, and boost our people's unity and persistence to cling to our pure land."

"I don't think today's operation is the first shock to the "Israeli" military establishment, nor will it be the last, for the enemy command knows very well that the initiative is in the Islamic Resistance's hand, he said.

"However, this command tries in vain from time to time to fabricate false achievements," he stressed, adding that "What happened today is practical and substantial evidence that annexing villages will place the Zionist society and its leadership in one quagmire after another."

The enemy has to choose: either to withdraw from our land or remain under the hammer of the Resistance men, al-Khalil concluded.

Jerusalem post: 3 Paratroop officers killed in Lebanon
"In a painful blow to the IDF*, the army today buries Maj. Eitan Balahsan, the commander of its elite Paratrooper Reconnaissance Unit, who was killed early yesterday morning along with two other officers in a clash with Hizbullah in south Lebanon".

"Five paratroopers were wounded - four moderately and one lightly - in the firefight north of the security zone. The Hizbullah guerrillas apparently escaped unharmed and later showed off weapons and bloodied booty captured from the stricken IDF squad"

"Balahsan, 30, of Moshav Ramot Naftali, is to be buried on his moshav this afternoon.

Lt. Liraz Tito, 22, of Petah Tikva, was buried in his hometown yesterday, and Lt. David Granit, 22, of Ofra, was laid to rest yesterday on Jerusalem's Mt. Herzl".

"The costly clash was the first time soldiers were killed in a direct firefight with Hizbullah guerrillas in more than a year. The three officers killed were the first fatalities in Lebanon this year.

Senior commanders insist that, despite the loss, the IDF will continue with its present tactics in south Lebanon. Still, Defense Minister Moshe Arens said he plans to hold consultations with IDF brass today to discuss the situation in Lebanon".

"Prime Minister Binyamin Netanyahu demanded yesterday that Syria put an end to attacks by Hizbullah guerrillas on "Israeli" troops in south Lebanon."

"It's clear that if Syria wants to stop these attacks it has the power to do so. We expect them to curtail aggression against "Israel"," Net-anyahu told reporters after visiting the wounded at Haifa's Rambam Hospital.

Labor Party opposition leader Ehud Barak, in turn, said a political solution is the only answer to the Lebanon quagmire.

The IDF will not be able to leave Lebanon without an agreement between Syria and "Israel", President Ezer Weizman earlier told reporters at the hospital.

But, he said, it would meanwhile be necessary for the army to look into the possibility of revising some of its tactics in the field.

"Some of the tactics and methods will have to be reviewed," Weizman said. The president stressed that it is necessary to sit down and talk with the Syrians.

"In every negotiation, there is give and take," Weizman said. "We have to sit down and see what can be given and what can be received in return. Without dialogue, there will never be a solution."

It makes no difference who will be chosen prime minister in the forthcoming elections, Weizman said. "Whoever it is will have to push forward the peace process, first on the stalemated Palestinian track and then with the Syrians."

"The shootout took place around 12:30 Monday night, when a small Hizbullah squad opened fire on a relatively large team from the elite unit. Balahsan and Tito were killed immediately. Granit was killed during the subsequent fighting at close quarters".

"OC Northern Command Maj.-Gen. Gaby Ashkenazy said the unit was on a mission to intercept Hizbullah guerrillas. The clash occurred on a slope called Jabal Kalat Jabour, about a kilometer north of the zone and about 22 kilometers north of Metulla."

"Despite the losses, Ashkenazy praised the paratroopers' performance, saying that combat was difficult in the thick bush of south Lebanon".

"After the clash, Hizbullah fired mortars at the paratroopers and the IDF retaliated with artillery. Helicopter gunships and later jets strafed the nearby hills to prevent the guerrillas from escaping and provide cover for helicopters evacuating the wounded and dead."

"In Beirut, Hizbullah issued a statement saying its fighters had intercepted a unit of "Israeli" commandos on the edge of the security zone, as they were trying sneak into "unoccupied Lebanon."

It boasted that its fighters killed or injured 15 soldiers. Reporters later were taken to a mountain cave where a Hizbullah commander showed off a captured assault rifle, a radio, a black face mask, and a blood-soaked IDF shirt that had been torn by bullets".

"The commander, in his mid-20s, had a blood-stained bandage on his left cheek, covering a wound apparently suffered in the fighting. He and other guerrillas covered their faces to conceal their identities".

"He used a map to show how his fighters ambushed the "Israelis", but declined to answer questions on whether Hizbullah had advance knowledge of the paratroopers' movements"… (5)

"Ashkenazi said the grim results of the encounter were an inevitable "part of fighting at very close range. The battle took place on overgrown, rocky ground, in which it wasn't possible to deploy our forces properly. Most of the wounded were from the first round of firing, in which Hezbollah enjoyed the advantage of surprise." This, he said, is what decided the course of the battle".

"Ashkenazi said the incident will require the IDF to consider revising some of its tactics. Several senior officers added that the episode indicates a problem with the way IDF forces are operating. It is impossible to attribute such losses merely to bad luck", they said… (6)

On border, slain officer's dad urges: 'Pull out now'… (7)
"The father of the paratrooper commander killed in Lebanon Monday night has urged that the IDF abandon the security zone".

"I don't know what the IDF can do in Lebanon, or what our soldiers are looking for - especially outside the security zone," said Claude Balhasan, father of Maj. Eitan Balhasan.

"Today I lost a son. The people of "Israel" lost an outstanding commander. Tomorrow, it could be someone else. It's a waste of time. We must get out of Lebanon."

Balhasan, who lives on Mosahv Ramot Naftali on the northern border, has four sons serving in the paratroopers.

"An enormous crowd, including numerous politicians, is expected to attend Balhasan's funeral, scheduled for 2 P.M. today at the moshav cemetery. Lt. Liraz Tito, 22, of Petah Tikvah was buried yesterday in Segula, and Lt. David Granit, 22, of the settlement of Ofra, was buried in Jerusalem's Mount Herzl military cemetery".

"It is unbelievable," said Granit's sister, Hadass. "On Saturday night we parted from him, I gave him a kiss, I never thought it would be the last time."

Granit was due to leave the army in four months. His twin brother, Yishai, had already been demobilized.

"Granit's father, Menahem, said David had decided to become an officer after his former commander in the unit, Capt. Eran Shamir, was killed in action a year ago".

"Granit is survived by his parents and four brothers and sisters. His parents, Menahem and Na'ama, were among the founders of Ofra".

"Several of the wounded soldiers' parents, meanwhile, said yesterday that they had not even known their sons were in Lebanon".

"Yisrael Shahar, father of Kobi Shahar, 21, of Arad, said he knew his son was in a combat unit, but never knew the unit was in Lebanon. "When did they go to Lebanon? When did they leave Lebanon? That he would never tell us until everything was over," Shahar said. "He's like all the others in the unit: They don't talk, they don't tell."

"Lior Ophir, 20, of Jerusalem, another of the wounded soldiers, confirmed that most members of his unit do not tell their parents they are serving in Lebanon. "It was a real bombshell to wake my parents up at 3 A.M. and tell them I was wounded in Lebanon," he said. "When my mother came to [Rambam] hospital, she said immediately that she had had a mother's intuition that I was there."

"Lior's father, Moshe Ophir, said the lack of knowledge had never stopped them from worrying, however. "We certainly feared that something like this could happen," he said.

"On the political front, the deaths immediately revived the long-standing argument over a unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon. MK Yossi Beilin (Labor), who heads the movement for a unilateral withdrawal, called on Defense Minister Moshe Arens to pull the IDF out of Lebanon immediately.

Former IDF chief of staff and Center Party founder Amnon Lipkin-Shahak responded that withdrawal would only be possible following an agreement with Syria".

Worse than bad luck… (8)
"During the past year, whenever IDF soldiers were wounded by mortars or landmines, IDF generals, led by the chief of staff, would say: "That's the way the Hezbollah operates because of its inferiority in face-to-face combat, but when it comes to a real encounter, force against force, the IDF has the upper hand at the critical moment."

"Two nights ago, more than two dozen soldiers from the Paratroopers Reconnaissance Unit deep inside Lebanon encountered a three-man Hezbollah guerrilla force."

"The result: Three dead officers, including the commander of the unit, and five wounded soldiers. All three guerrillas apparently got away safely. The force was surprised, at very short range. The commander and another officer were killed in the first burst of fire. The guerrillas opened fire from harsh, rocky terrain, where movement is difficult. The paratroopers suffered from bad luck - and anyone who hikes at night on those difficult paths knows that he's depending on luck".

"Maj. Gen. Gabi Ashkenazi, who heads the Northern Command, and other top officers gave full backing yesterday to the commanders in the field. That's important and worthy, especially since there's another funeral today and debriefings are still going on. But it's also important to point out that the unit encountered precisely the kind of scenario for which they are trained - a group of soldiers in the field ambushed by a small unit of guerrillas who know the terrain well. "The entire training system is based on the scenario that happened last night," said one senior officer, "and if this is the result, something is definitely wrong ... You can't blame everything on the tip of the boot ... There are two possibilities. Either the IDF identified the problem, but it failed to provide the proper training. Or, that at the critical moment, the force didn't do what it was trained to do, and something went wrong".

Arnoun issue to be taken to UN… (9)
"Lebanon decided Tuesday to take the issue of Arnoun straight to the UN Security Council, despite the threat of a veto from the United States, after the Monitoring Group failed after two days of discussion to reach a consensus on what action to take".

"President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss took the decision during an evening meeting at Baabda. Mr. Hoss then called Speaker Nabih Berri to explain the situation"

"The complaint, the first in almost three years, will seek a meeting of the Security Council. While Washington is certain to use its veto power to prevent a condemnation of "Israel", Lebanon hopes that its diplomatic offensive would muster enough political pressure from other countries to force "Israel" to reverse the annexation of the village last week".

"At the end of a 32-hour session Tuesday, the Monitoring Group issued a statement saying that its representatives had shown “serious concerns surrounding this incident and its potential for raising tensions.”

"But it added that they had failed to agree on whether the laying of mines and razor wire fencing in Arnoun constituted a violation of the April Understanding not to target civilians".

“The Lebanese delegate stated that the only acceptable outcome of the meeting would be an "Israeli"
commitment to return Arnoun to how it was before. As a result there was no agreement on a common course of action,” the statement said.

Mr. Hoss rejected "Israel's" previous claims that the Monitoring Group had no mandate to discuss the Arnoun problem.

“This issue is surely part of the group’s mandate. Civilians are paying the price of Arnoun’s annexation. The group is supposed to protect the interests of the civilians and the residents of Arnoun have been displaced from their village,” he said.

"President Lahoud in turn insisted that if the Monitoring Group did not take the necessary measures, Lebanon would contact the United Nations. The president had remained in direct contact with Colonel Maher Toufeili, the Lebanese representative to the monitoring body, and when it became clear that the deadlock was intractable, he and Mr. Hoss changed course".

"Mr. Berri, who had also discussed the issue with Mr. Satterfield, deplored Israel treatment of civilians in the south. “The farmers can’t work in their fields and the children can’t go to school. We want the "Israelis" to evacuate the village,” he said, adding that the credibility of the Monitoring Group was at stake over the matter".

“I think the government should file a complaint to the Security Council even if the U.S. is likely to veto any resolution condemning "Israeli" aggression,” the speaker said.

“If the council fails to condemn "Israel", nobody should be surprised to see vast numbers of Lebanese marching on Arnoun,” he added.

"Mr. Berri also warned "Israel" against any attempt to use a pullout from Arnoun as a bargaining chip for reducing the resistance in the south".

Protesters at sit-in demand bigger support for Arnoun… (10)
"Relatives and friends of Arnoun’s stranded inhabitants gathered at a sit-in in the southern suburbs Tuesday to urge increased protest action against "Israel's" latest expansion of the occupation zone which isolated the village last week".

"At the Dar al-Aloum school in Hay al-Sullom, the protesters, many of whom are former residents, drew up a list of actions to be taken to make Arnoun a “model for other occupied villages.”

""Israeli" troops spent last Wednesday night rigging up barbed wire around the village and planting land mines along the length of the new border."

The unanimous response of the 32 remaining families in the village was not to abandon their homes. “We’re no longer able to live off some of our land because we can’t reach it. "Israel" has made us prisoners in our own homes,” Bahij Ajami, 70, one of the trapped residents, told The Daily Star in a telephone interview Tuesday".

"On the night of the annexation, bulldozers used earth to build barricades across the entrance, cutting off the road that leads to Nabatieh".

 "In a statement released by the SLA** immediately after the annexation, the "Israeli" Army promised residents they would be allowed “unlimited free movement.”

They can leave the zone, but only via the SLA checkpoint at Kfar Tibnit, a walk of almost 4 kilometers along a dirt track.

“Snipers often target this road because "Israeli" troops commute along it,” Mr. Ajami said. “Also, my daily expenses will increase since the cost of bringing water into the village will more than double.

“We will not accept any solution other than opening the main road,” said Mr. Ajami.

His cellular phone is now his only means of communication with the outside world.

Young men from Arnoun rarely visit their families in the village, which "Israelis" and SLA militiamen constantly watch from their stronghold in Beaufort Castle.

But Hassan Marouni, who lives in Beirut, was determined last week to visit his sister Fatima who now lives alone at the family house.

Another sister, Raqi, said Tuesday: “Hassan shouted out to my sister from behind the barbed wire that separated them. It’s torture for us and even more for my sick mother to know that this is the only way we can see and talk to her.”

"Almost 5,000 people have been displaced from Arnoun following repeated assaults by "Israeli" soldiers and the SLA".

"Its annexation brings to 104 the number of villages in the south and western Bekaa whose residents are living under occupation, many of them for the past 17 years".

"At the sit-in, Imad Ajami, a journalist and former resident of Arnoun, called on relatives and friends of his village to display their support by fasting and organizing weekly demonstrations outside the village.

The first march will take place Thursday at 11 A.M. and is due to be attended by Arab ambassadors and officials".

"The International Committee for the Red Cross Tuesday delivered food rations to almost 100 trapped residents".
