Israeli military warns of war with Syria after south pullout(1)
Israeli Army intelligence appears to have strengthened its opposition to a unilateral withdrawal by warning Premier Ehud Barak on Tuesday that pulling the troops from the south would increase the chances of direct conflict with Syria.

The assessment came as the European Unions Middle East envoy, Miguel Angel Moratinos, on a visit to Beirut, said that Baraks priority was to order a withdrawal within the framework of a peace accord with Lebanon and Syria.

Israels Maariv newspaper quoted an intelligence assessment which argued that Syria would seek to destabilize the Lebanon-Israel border in the event of a unilateral withdrawal.

A scenario was presented to Barak and Israeli Chief of Staff General Shaul Mofaz envisaging how an escalation could occur between Israel and Syria in the event of a unilateral withdrawal. If Israel staged such a pullout, the study argued, Syria would lose a vital bargaining chip in future negotiations over the Golan Heights.

Syria will not allow Israel to enjoy all worlds, the assessment said. There will not be a situation in which Israel will live in peace and quiet on the Lebanese border while it continues to control the Golan Heights.

The report predicts a swift escalation of violence in which armed groups in the south stage cross-border assaults, sparking retaliatory air strikes against Lebanese infrastructure targets.

In response, the groups would launch Katyusha rocket attacks as far as Haifa, Israels third most populous city, the scenario suggests.

Israels counter-strike could spark an escalation that could lead to a direct confrontation with Syria, the study said.

Barak has pledged to pull the Israeli Army out of Lebanon within the framework of an agreement with Syria by July 2000. He has refused to declare his course of action should a deal with Syria fail to materialize but has not ruled out staging an unconditional withdrawal.

Israeli military intelligence, in its regular briefings to the Knesset, consistently warns of the perils of staging a unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon, arguing that guerrilla groups would continue to launch attacks into Israel. However, the scenario presented to Barak and Mofaz is the first known time that the military has suggested that a unilateral withdrawal could lead to a direct conflict with Syria.

The assessment contradicts comments made by Mofaz two weeks ago in which he stated that the operational situation in south Lebanon had changed and that a unilateral withdrawal was not as risky as in the past.

Mofaz comments were hardly a ringing endorsement for staging such a withdrawal but came in line with several Israeli officials openly declaring that a unilateral pullout should be conducted.

Most observers believe such calls are intended to pressure
Syria into dropping its conditions for resuming peace talks with Israel, rather than a genuine desire to withdraw from the south unilaterally.

Moratinos relayed Baraks desire for a conditional withdrawal during a meeting he held with Prime Minister Salim Hoss on Tuesday.

A pullout from Lebanon should be implemented within the framework of a global political accord, both with Lebanese and Syrian authorities, in order to give a new meaning to the peace initiative, he said.

This is the position of the European Union in principle and it is the position of all those who really want a lasting and stable peace, he added.

Moratinos arrived Monday night for 48 hours of talks with officials and EU ambassadors.

Seven steps to a peace agreement

Government releases list of demands to be fulfilled before resistance ends(2)
Lebanon unveiled on Monday a seven-point list of demands which Israel must fulfill before a peace agreement can be signed between the two countries, warning that a troop withdrawal from the south was insufficient to warrant the end of the resistance.

The release of the demands, outlined in a statement released by Parliaments Defense and Foreign Affairs committees, was intended to allay the view that Lebanon opposes an Israeli withdrawal from the south for the benefit of Syria.

Speaker Nabih Berri convened a special session of the committees which gathered Prime Minister Salim Hoss and Defense Minister Ghazi Zeaiter and another 44 MPs.

The basis of any just and comprehensive settlement is Israels commitment to implement UN Security Council resolutions 242, 338 and 425 that call for Israels complete withdrawal from the Lebanese and Arab territories it occupies, the statement said.

The seven-point list of peace demands went much further than the required Israeli withdrawal. It included the rejection of a permanent settlement of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon, the return of Lebanese territory annexed by Israel, ending alleged water theft, the release of Lebanese prisoners and the right to bring South Lebanon Army* militiamen to trial.

These issues should show the whole world that Israels pledge to pull out its troops from Lebanon is simply a pullback that will not lead to any settlement or be a step toward peace, the statement said.

The committees stuck adamantly to the governments policy of rejecting a permanent settlement of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon.

Lebanon rejects solving the case of Palestinian refugees at its expense and considers any direct or indirect attempt to permanently settle the refugees here a plot against their national identity and an attack on Lebanons existence and sovereignty, the statement said.

The statement said another condition for a peace agreement with Israel is the release of Lebanese prisoners, detainees and hostages held in prisons run by the occupation forces and their collaborators. It said that the sentences imposed on some of the 200 Lebanese taken by Israel or its proxy, the SLA militia, are illegitimate and that their conditions of imprisonment violate the Geneva Convention.

The fate of the SLA militiamen must be decided by the Lebanese judiciary and not as part of a pre-withdrawal agreement with Israel. Lebanon rejects any Israeli attempt to use these people in the future for cross-border purposes, it said.

Lebanon wanted compensation for the mass murders, massacres and destruction of public and private property resulting from Israels occupation, invasions and attacks.

Lebanon also requires the return of the seven villages and all the other areas  seized from Lebanon before and after 1949 and during the June (1967) war, including the Shibaa farms and the border areas in the qadas of Marjeyoun, Bint Jbeil and Hasbaya, the committees said.

The so-called seven villages, which also include more than 25 farms, were annexed in the 1920s to British mandate Palestine from French mandate Lebanon under an agreement by the two European powers.

Their residents, who fled after the creation of Israel in 1948, were given Lebanese passports under the 1994 Naturalization Decree.

The other areas claimed by Lebanon were seized by Israel during the 1949 and the 1967 Arab-Israeli wars. After Israels invasions of Lebanon in 1978 and 1982, the barbed-wire fence separating the two countries was moved deeper into Lebanon.

Lebanon, which accuses Israel of stealing its water, wants this practice to end, especially at the Wazzani and Hasbani Rivers and the cross-border underground water, the statement said.

The committees affirm Lebanons adherence to the simultaneity of the Lebanese and the Syrian tracks and to the heroic resistance movement until Israel pulls out fully and unconditionally from Lebanon under Resolution 425, recognizes the international border and respects Lebanons national resources, mainly water, it said.

After the meeting, Berri said he had released the statement to counteract the international view that Lebanon is opposed to Israels pullout from the south and that this opposition is the result of Syrian pressures.

Lahd discusses collaborators' future with occupation officials(3)
Chief of the Israeli occupation's proxy militia Antoine Lahd discussed the fate of his militiamen with the president of the Israeli entity Ezer Weizman in the presence of the occupation's chief of staff Shaul Mofaz, commander of the northern area Gabi Ashkinazi and commander of the liaison unit Beni Gantz. The meeting was held at the Mtullah colony.

On the other hand, al-Sharq al-Awsat newspaper reported that agent Lahd has been adopting special security measures around his residence over the past few weeks.

The reports said Lahd has kept all his movements under utmost secrecy for fear of attempts on his life.

The chief collaborator sometimes spends the night in one of the Israeli colonies, the reports added.

LBR attacks Sojod outpost(4)
The Lebanese Brigades for Resisting the Israeli occupation (LBR) this morning attacked the enemy outpost in Sojod with machine guns and rocket propelled grenades scoring accurate hits in the outpost's fortifications and the enemy troops manning them. Smoke was seen billowing from the outpost.
