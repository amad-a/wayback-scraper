December Tuesday 14-1999:

Introduction
Hizbullah Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah asserted that we must cling to the Resistance more with the beginning of the talks, for the stage is complicated, and we are confronting an enemy which is betting on our weakness, surrender and retreat.

Meanwhile, a foreign ministry official said that Lebanon will demand compensation from Israel for its 21-year occupation of the South during the settlement negotiations.

In the occupied Palestine, the Israeli central court extended the detention of Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid and Hajj Mustafa Dirani until the end of April 2000.

Hizbullah Secretary General: resumption of negotiations means clinging to resistance(1)
The settlement negotiations will be tough and complicated, especially if the Lebanese and Syrian sides insist on demanding all their rights and not submitting to the Zionist conditions in spite of the American pressures, Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said.

Speaking at an Iftar organized by the Women's Society for Social Solidarity, Sayyed Nasrallah underlined that the US has always been committed to the Zionist interests.

Syrian and Lebanese delegations' going to the negotiations does not mean people should have a feeling of reassurance and the Resistance men should lay down their arms, the Secretary General said.
We must cling to the Resistance more with the beginning of the talks, for the stage is complicated, and we are confronting an enemy that is betting on our weakness, surrender and retreat, he added.

Sayyed Nasrallah noted that the negotiations are an American need in the coming presidential elections.

In turn, Hizbullah's Deputy Secretary-General his Eminence Sheikh Nahim Qassem said, "Those who believe Hizbullah is a group that will be disbanded one day are wrong, for we are too strong to be disbanded."

Speaking at the Islamic Religious Education Association's Iftar in Nabatieh, Qassem said, "Those who are looking for a solution with us must change their course, for we won't change ours, and the coming days will prove that what we are saying is true."

LEBANON TO SEEK COMPENSATION FROM ISRAEL FOR OCCUPATION(2)
Lebanon will demand compensation from Israel for its 21-year occupation of the south of the country in future peace talks, a foreign ministry official said Monday. The official gave no figures, but the daily An-Nahar said the claim totaled eight billion dollars, 3.3 billion for damage to infrastructure and 4.7 billion for reparations to civilian victims. A foreign ministry official told AFP that once a total and unconditional withdrawal by Israel had been agreed, "the talks will become negotiations on a permanent peace, to which Lebanon will only subscribe if it obtains compensation from Israel." Peace talks between Israel and Lebanon are expected to resume soon. Lebanon is also demanding the restoration of areas of land, including seven villages, which Israel has "nibbled" from Lebanese territory, especially around the source of the river Wazzani. "Lebanon will not accept any change of the border from that endorsed by the League of Nations in 1923," the official said. An-Nahar, quoting official figures, calculated that since the foundation of Israel in 1948 more than 4,000 Israeli attacks on Lebanon had killed 27,000 people and wounded 46,885, apart from the material damage. The paper said Lebanon had based its compensation claim of 100,000 dollars per death and 40,000 per injury on reparations paid to Jewish victims of the Nazi Holocaust and the sums paid by Iraq for its invasion of Kuwait.

Occupation extends detention of Sheikh Obeid and Hajj Dirani(3)
Israeli central court extended the detention of Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid and Hajj Mustafa Dirani until the end of April 2000.

The occupation forces had abducted Sheikh Obeid from his house in Jibsheit in 1988; Hajj Dirani was taken from his house also in Qsarnaba in 1994.

The Israeli entity says it holds Sheikh Obeid, Hajj Dirani and other Lebanese hostages to exchange them for Israeli troops who had gone missing during Israeli aggressions against Lebanon.

ISRAEL RELEASES THREE Lebanese(4)
Lebanese security sources said Israeli allied militia released three Lebanese men and a woman from the Khiam detention center in the zone Israel occupies in south Lebanon. Mohammed Marouni, 19, Ali Tawbeh, 16, and Mohammed Assef, 32, were detained at dawn today (yesterday) when leaving Arnoun to the village of Nabatieh where they work. The three men were released after being interrogated at Khiam and Samira Attiyeh, abducted last month from Arnoun by Israeli allied militia forces was also released. Israel and its allied militia often abduct citizens and jail them in Khiam or prisons inside Israel, accusing them of cooperating with Lebanese resistance fighters. Human rights groups have repeatedly condemned cases of torture and ill-treatment in Khiam and Israeli jails.

Israeli Aggressions(5)
In its statement, the IR reported the following Israeli aggressions:
2:05, the surroundings of Haddatha compound exposed to a volley of 12.7mm heavy machine gun bullets.
6:30, Mazraat Hamra was shelled by four 155mm missiles.
6:35, a barrage of 12.7mm heavy machine gun bullets struck the surroundings of Sojod outpost.

Military Operations(6)
A group of the Lebanese Resistance Brigades (LRB) attacked, at 6:40, Soweida outpost with the proper weapons scoring direct hits.

Meanwhile, another squad of LRB was targeting the Beaufort Castle with the suitable arms chalking up direct goals.
