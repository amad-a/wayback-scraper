Daily Report
 
 
December Saturday 11-1999:

Hizbullah promises to keep up attacks(1)
Hizbullah will continue its attacks against Israeli targets in south Lebanon despite the imminent resumption of peace talks between Syria and the Jewish state, according to Sheikh Nahim Qassem.
Our attacks will continue until Israel withdraws completely from the occupied zone to the international border, even if there is also a resumption of talks on the Lebanese-Israeli track, said Qassem, Hizbullahs deputy secretary-general.

I dont think that Lebanese and Syrian officials will ask Hizbullah to halt its attacks, he told Agence France-Presse in an interview.

Occupation's aggressions continued(2)
The Israeli occupation's artillery Friday proceeded with launching aggressions on liberated villages and towns violating, once again, the April Understanding.

The enemy bombarded the residential neighborhoods in Majdal Zoun, the woods of al-Mansouri, and the Jib Soweid area in the western sector, damaging the houses of Mohammed Salim Fayyad, Adnan Fayyad, Rida Salman, and others, besides damaging the electricity grid in the town.

The shelling was accompanied by heavy machine-gun raking from the occupation's outposts overlooking the western sector targeting the outskirts of Majdal Zoun and al-Mansouri, along with the wadis and mountainous roads leading to the occupation's outpost.

Earlier, the occupation forces at 8:00 a.m. bombarded the Sojod-Rihan woods and the Oqmata Farmstead in Iqleem al-Toffah with 155mm and 120mm rounds from the outposts in al-Rihan and al-Zaffateh.

Moreover, the enemy bombarded the outskirts of Aital El-Jabal, Haddatha, Tibneen, Jabal al-Zaatar, and Baraasheit, while enemy warplanes circled over the villages adjacent to the front lines in the middle and western sectors, staging mock air raids.

For its part, the Islamic Resistance (IR) released a statement in which it reported the following Israeli aggressions (3):
5:35, the surroundings of Salaah position exposed to a volley of 12.7mm heavy machine gun bullets.
Between 6:45 and 7:00, the perimeters of Majdal Zoun village came under twelve 120mm shells, five 155mm missiles and four 81mm rounds damaging several houses.

Hizbullah announces martyrdom of two IR men killed during Thursday confrontation(4)
The Islamic Resistance (IR) proudly announced the martyrdom of two of its men who had engaged in heroic confrontations for hours on Thursday against the Israeli occupation forces and their proxy militia at the Rob Thlathine area deep inside the occupied zone and near the international borders with occupied Palestine, an IR communiqué read.

Moussa Mahmoud Ahmed (al-Sheikh Merza), 26, was born in Meis al-Jabal, married, joined IR in 1992, participated in several military and educational training courses, besides taking part in many qualitative Jihad operations, obtained encomium from Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah. He was martyred on Thursday, December 9, during the heroic confrontations fought by IR against the occupation forces in the vicinity of Rob Thlathine.

Martyr Hassan Ali Salameh (Ammar), 23, was born in Qaaqaiyet al-Jisr, single, joined IR in 1994, participated in several military and educational training courses, besides taking part in many qualitative Jihad operations, obtained encomium from Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah. He was martyred on Thursday, December 9, during the heroic confrontations fought by IR against the occupation forces in the vicinity of Rob Thlathine.

IR had engaged in fierce clashes for hours on Thursday with the forces of the Israeli occupation and its proxy militia in the Rob Thlathine area. IR targeted patrols, motorized forces, and foot forces belonging to the occupation forces and the collaborators. The close-quarter confrontations led to definite casualties in the ranks of the enemy forces.

Following hours of silence concerning the losses, the Israeli radio acknowledged that one Israeli soldier was wounded in one of the occupation's outpost in the eastern sector.

Military Operations(5)
The IR team of martyrs Ghassan Ali Ghanem and Ali Mohammad Ashmar attacked, at 14:50, Bir Kallab outpost with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades. Direct and certain hits were scored, the mujahedeen told.

Ten minutes later, the IR squad of martyrs Ibrahim Melhem El-Bazzal and Mohammad Naameh Youssef rushed upon an Israeli infantry force at Rihan hilltops. Using the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades, the militants were able to inflict certain casualties among its rank.

Also, the IR group of martyrs Hussein Ghassani and Ahmad Jamil Farhat targeted, at 16:40, Bir Kallab outpost and its surroundings with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades chalking up direct goals.

Ambience of serenity that masks violent decades(6)
The track, gouged with ruts and flanked by gnarled dust-shrouded olive trees, meandered along the hillside for a distance of 1 kilometer from the edge of Rihan village to the back entrance of the Sojod outpost, one of the most heavily hit positions in the occupation zone. This is the road to Vietnam, chuckled Bassem, a local resident.

The outpost, manned by militiamen of the South Lebanon Army*, looked disarmingly innocuous in the soft morning light, the dome of the small tomb of Nabi Sojod beside the compound imparting an ambiance of serenity rather than violence.

Yet only hours earlier during the night, Hizbullah fighters had waged a fierce gunbattle nearby against Israeli troops and SLA militiamen. Israeli warplanes had fired missiles in six sorties into the brush-covered slopes of Jabal Safi above Rihan, while helicopter gunships prowled above the steep wadis, hunting down the resistance guerrillas as they slipped out of the zone.

This place is the source of all troubles and violence, said Ali, an off-duty SLA militiaman dressed in an olive-green Israeli uniform.

The announcement that the peace process is about to resume between Syria and Israel appears to have had as little impact on the Shiite residents of Rihan as it has on the battles waged in the remote ravines and hills of the northern sector.

When asked his reaction to the renewed peace process, Ibrahim Shoukar, 72, shrugged his shoulders indifferently.

Everyones waiting for peace. Like everyone else, I think that whatever comes is good, he said as Israeli warplanes rumbled overhead in the deep blue sky.

Like most of the 100 remaining residents, Shoukars optimism and vitality have been battered out of him by years of bloodshed and misery.

Evidence of the occupation is impossible to avoid in this bedraggled village. The Israelis occupy an outpost in the center of Rihan beside another position manned by SLA militiamen. A row of concrete T-walls was recently positioned along the entrance to the Israeli post to protect soldiers from Hizbullahs anti-tank rockets. The rusting remains of a Syrian tank destroyed in the 1982 Israeli invasion lies to one side of a road pitted by exploding mortar shells.

Ali said he was not worried for his fate following the return of state control to the occupation zone.

I dont think the government will charge me for anything because I was forced to join the militia. Maybe militiamen who killed people will be tried for murder but the only thing Ive killed is a bird, he said. I havent gone after Hizbullah to fight them but when they come for me, then I have to defend myself.

The Christian village of Aishieh, 3 kilometers south of Rihan, is in a better state of repair than its neighbor, doubtless helped by the absence of an Israeli outpost in the heart of the community.

Electricity pylons along the main street have been wrapped in Christmas lights. The villages main church is finally being rebuilt after it was destroyed in the 1982 invasion.

But hopes for a lasting peace are tinged with the fears and uncertainties that an end to the occupation could bring.

There is only 10 percent of the population here so were anxious for the occupation to end and for peace to prevail, said Nassib Aoun, a former university professor and resident of the village.

In Rihan, however, Ali expressed confidence that once a comprehensive peace had been achieved on all fronts there would be no further fighting in the south.

Once the Israelis have left there wont be a reason to fight, he said. The troubles began in the south in the 1970s with the arrival of the Palestinians. Now the troubles will end in the south with the departure of the Israelis.
