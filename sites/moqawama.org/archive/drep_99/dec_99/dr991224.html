Hizbullah: enemy's entity won't become a legal sate(1)
"No matter what the results of the negotiations are, the Zionist entity will never become, as a result of these talks, a legitimate state, regardless of the sides that recognize it, Head of Hizbullah's Political Council MP Mohammed Raad said.

"Speaking at a ceremony held by Hizbullah's Educational Mobilization Unit (EMU) to honor former Lebanese University deans, Raad said, "These negotiations will not change right. As a result of these negotiations right will not become wrong, nor will the Zionist entity become a legitimate state, even if some recognize that, for each has his own reasons and justifications."

"Betting on these negotiations, if they are to reach a conclusion and make peace in the region, is an illusory one, for peace can be made only with returning rights to their owners and not through mechanisms imposed by the usurping occupier, Raad said.

"This Ummah will never recognize the legitimacy of the Zionists' usurping of Palestine. The Zionist enemy will always be an existential enemy," he added, calling on the Ummah to confront the Zionist project with all forms of confrontation.

"The problem of the Palestinian people in diaspora will not be solved with settling them here, nor with residence permits here or temptations there, or financial grants bestowed on them by the countries marketing the settlement process, because the Palestinian people is part of the Ummah that will always have a live memory; al-Quds will never become Jerusalem, Palestine will not become "Israel", for the latter will always be the enemy which is usurping al-Quds and Palestine," he said.

Raad stressed that peace with ""Israel"" will not revive the region's economy, "nor will it pour on us capitals and investments, but will drown the region in more debts and dependence in order to enable the enemy to dominate it."

"Then, EMU official in the south Ibrahim Rammal delivered a speech and so did the former director of Law and Political Sciences School Dr. Mohammed Abdel-Raheem".

Hizbullah martyrs' funerals, another IR man remains recovered(2)
"The Islamic Resistance (IR) escorted Martyrs Youssef Baddah, Ahmed Abdel-Hussein Foani, and Hussein Qabalan to their final resting place.

"The Martyrs' bodies were recovered yesterday by the International Committee for the Red Cross (ICRC) from Wadi al-Shuhada.

"A number of Hizbullah officials, MPs, Ulama and figures along with thousands of people took part in the funeral which set out from Imam Mahdi's Mosque (Ghobeiri, Beirut) to the Shahidayn Cemetery in Shiah.

"Hizbullah Deputy Secretary General his Eminence Sheikh Nahim Qassem delivered a speech in which he praised the sacrifices of the Martyrs "who have humiliated the Zionists."

"The Martyrs are the pride of Lebanon; they are the ones who made the country strong, Qassem said.
A party that offers such sacrifices will always be firm under all circumstances and conditions, he added.
The Zionist entity is a permanent threat to our region, for it builds its existence at the expense of other peoples, he concluded.

"In Baalback, IR's supporters escorted Martyr Radwan Mohieddeen Dougha to his final resting place in a massive procession.

"Imam Khomenei's representative in Lebanon his Eminence Sheikh Mohammed Yazbek took part in the funeral along with Ulama and residents of Baalbek and the neighboring villages.

"The procession toured Baalback's main streets as mourners chanted slogans against the "Israeli" enemy and backing the Resistance's method.

"Sheikh Yazbek stressed that the Resistance will continue until the land is liberated.

Earlier, IR had recovered the remains of Martyr Abbas Youssef Rihan following a search that was conducted by ICRC teams.

"The body that arrived at al-Najda al-Shaabia Hospital in Nabatieh at 15:30 was received by a number of Hizbullah officials and ICRC's Representative in Lebanon Henri Fournier.

"Martyr Rihan will be laid to rest at 13:30 Friday in his hometown of Maifadoun".

LEBANESE VICTIMS OF "ISRAELI" ATTACKS OVER 300 THIS YEAR: PAPER(3)
"The daily Al Mustaqbal newspaper today (yesterday) reported that more than 300 people, including civilians and resistance fighters, have been killed or wounded by "Israeli" attacks against Lebanon this year. The newspaper quoted Lebanese security sources in southern Lebanon as saying one Lebanese army soldier and a peacekeeper serving with the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) were killed in "Israeli" shelling, while five Lebanese troops and eight members of the international peace-keeping force were wounded. The sources said 31 Lebanese civilians were killed and around 200 wounded in "Israeli" attacks during the same period. The sources were quoted as saying, Hizbullah resistance fighters carried out 1,600 attacks against "Israeli" occupation troops and their allied militia in the south. Hizbullah resistance attacks killed 13 "Israeli" soldiers and 20 "Israeli" allied militiamen. In 1999, 51 "Israeli" troops and 82 "Israeli"-backed militiamen were injured", the sources said.

Militiamen arrested as Lahd leaves for France(4)
"Reports from the occupied zone said a state of severe confusion is prevailing among the ranks of the "Israeli" occupation forces and their proxy militia. Such a state has been translated into increased contacts, patrols, storming, and investigations with residents of the occupied zone besides members of the occupation's militia.

"The reports said this state resulted from discovering explosive devices in the Hasbaya area before a meeting was scheduled to be held by senior "Israeli" officers in the liaison unit and Lahdi security and military chiefs.

"On the other hand, reports from Marjeyoun in the occupied zone said the collaborators' chief Antoine Lahd and his senior aides have left for France under the pretext of spending holidays there.

"Sources said the visit aims to secure houses for these officers in light of the talk about the "Israeli" withdrawal from Lebanon.

"Lahd has assigned Aql Hashem, one of the senior militia officials, to hold responsibility in his absence. Lahd has also told the occupation command that he wishes that Hashem succeeds him when he finally settles in France", the sources added.

"ISRAEL" ANNEXES LEBANESE VILLAGES, SAY UN SOURCES(5)
""Israeli" soldiers have fenced in and annexed the outskirts of four Lebanese villages on the border with "Israel" over the past few years, UN sources were quoted by DPA as saying Thursday. Over the past few years, "Israeli" occupation forces have fenced in and annexed several deserted and mined areas in the villages of Odaisseh, Maroun Al Ras, southeast of Marjeyoun, and Alma Al Shaab, the sources said. The "Israelis" claim the fences were erected for practical reasons and to apprehend infiltrators, the UN sources said. They added that the original fence demarcating the common border line is still in place and is monitored around the clock by the United Nation's 55-member Armistice Observer Group, who were deployed in 1948 to monitor the Lebanese-"Israeli" armistice pact. This latest account of "Israeli" annexation of Lebanese border villages would buttress the claim to seven other Lebanese border villages, a government source said. Lebanese Prime Minister Salim Hoss was quoted earlier Thursday as saying that the return of the seven villages which were annexed to Palestine in 1923 during the drawing of the border agreement of the French and the British will be "a Lebanese demand that will be on the agenda of the future peace talks with "Israel"". Hoss's statement followed reports quoting him as saying Beirut would probably drop demands for the return of the seven villages. Lebanon is scheduled to join in the peace talks with "Israel" after the second round of negotiations between the "Israelis" and the Syrians which are due to take place on January 3rd in West Virginia. Peace talks between "Israel" and Lebanon were broken in February 1994".

Soiled hands, spoiled lands(6)
For years, "Israelis" took soil from South Lebanon and used it for agriculture, for the gardens of public buildings and homes, and for military use. This cannot be ignored in the upcoming negotiations between "Israel" and Lebanon

"A short walk or drive on the other side of the Good Fence on the "Israel"-Lebanon border at Metulla leads to a rusted fence, hung with rusted license plates and signs that say "Border ahead," as well as gates whose signs bear names such as Oldtimers' Gate, Tank Gate, Gate 5. Metulla residents explain that this is the old fence, as distinguished from the new fence. Both visitors and residents find it hard to comprehend the differences between the new and old fence, or their significance. One way or another, the fences are wide open, to the great convenience of drug smugglers. Close to the fences is an industrial building with a big sign - "Industrial Structures Ltd., the biggest real estate company in "Israel"" - and young plantations of deciduous trees, with Thai workers working nearby.

Opposite is Kilah, a Lebanese village close to the unclear border between Lebanon and "Israel". A illager
reveals that the houses closest to the fence are empty, their inhabitants evacuated by the "Israel" Defense Forces* "for security reasons." For these same reasons, the IDF prohibited the completion of high-rise buildings, whose skeletons stand silent as they have for years. Some of Kilah's residents were forced out of their village a few years ago. Heaps of garbage and rubbish stand next to the mosque.

The naked eye can make out, between the fence and the homes of the village, pale areas -
the color of the ground cover - interspersed with wide strips of dark brown.

"The dark strips are the craters and trenches created by steam shovels and bulldozers from "Israel" that took a bite out of the red sandy earth, very fertile agricultural soil, loaded it onto trucks and brought it to "Israel"," the villager from Kilah related. "For years "Israelis" took agricultural soil from Kilah and other places in South Lebanon and brought it in big trucks to "Israel"."

Close to a few of the craters are boulders, which may once have marked the border, encircled with a stripe of brownish red, indicating the level of the ground before the steam-shovels and bulldozers took their bites.

"In 1996 I loaded soil from Lebanon, transported it to "Israel" and dumped it in various places in "Israel", including Metulla," a Golan Heights truck driver confesses. "The soil I moved was used for farming, for the yards of public institutions and private homes. The army also used Lebanese earth for 'tracker trails' - swept dirt paths it maintains on the borders in order to detect infiltrators' footprints." While he spoke, he pointed out the gardens of private homes in the town where he had dumped red sandy soil from Lebanon.
One of the companies mentioned as having trucked soil from Lebanon to "Israel" is that of Moshe Maman, of Rosh Pina. Asked this week whether earth was still being brought from Lebanon, Maman said, "the IDF has forbidden us from entering, for security reasons."

To the question of whether people had to pay for permits to take the soil, he answered, "We didn't need anything." Asked whether it was possible to just go in and take the earth, he responded, "What can I tell you, it was something like that." When did it stop? "At some point there was a newspaper article, and since then it stopped. It stopped this year," he said, and quickly ended the conversation in order to continue his work.

Maman's statements seem to corroborate Lebanese claims. According to some Lebanese, the phenomenon has slowed in the last year, but the IDF has been unable to stop it completely.

The Lebanese Parliament discussed the issue in October, 1998. In a letter to the UN Security Council, Parliament Speaker Nabih Berri complained that "Israel" was taking hundreds of tons of fertile agricultural soil from the Marjeyoun area and other places in South Lebanon. At the time, the coordinator of activities in Lebanon, Uri Lubrani, rejected the Lebanese government claims as "nonsense."

However, some days later, after reexamining Lebanese claims, defense establishment sources admitted that ""Israeli" citizens stole soil from within Lebanese territory without obtaining permits from the army." A South Lebanon Army** officer confirmed the theft of earth from an area within Lebanon, near the border fence. According to a UN spokesman, ""Israeli" trucks regularly transport topsoil from the Adassiya area to "Israel". It is inconceivable that "Israeli" soldiers don't know about it. We are talking about an area in which the "Israeli" presence is not negligible" (Ha'aretz, November 8, 1998).

The chairman of the Metulla local council, Ya'akov Katz, when asked to explain the official process that enabled the transfer of earth from South Lebanon to "Israel", including Metulla, responded, "I am not addressing this issue. Go to the authorities that deal with South Lebanon. I know nothing about it."
Both the IDF and Defense Ministry spokesmen responded laconically when faced with a detailed list of questions and asked to respond to the claims made by "Israelis" that they were involved in transporting soil from Lebanon with the knowledge or permission of the IDF and the defense establishment, as well as claims by Kilah residents regarding the relocation of the border fence westward. "We are not addressing your questions, and all border issues will be discussed in the future between us and the Lebanese," said the Defense Ministry spokesman, while the IDF Spokesman said "all issues connected with the "Israel"-Lebanon border will be discussed in the future."

Yossi Goldberg, a native son of Metulla who headed the local council for 20 years (1978-98) and served in the Knesset from 1988-92, was considerably more forthcoming than his successor and the Defense Ministry and IDF spokesmen.

Goldberg declared decisively that "the IDF never prevented the transfer of earth from the Ayoun valley because it was private land owned by Metulla residents for a century, who have the kushans [a Turkish term for property registration certificates] to prove it." To reinforce the historical connection, Goldberg added that Ayoun is mentioned in the Bible (Kings I and II and Chronicles). The Arabs call the valley Marjeyoun.

According to Goldberg, there used to be no border fence separating "Israel" and Lebanon north of Metulla. The cease-fire agreement signed with Lebanon in March 1949 stipulated that the cease-fire line would run along the international border between Lebanon and Palestine-Mandatory Eretz Yisrael demarcated in 1923 by France and Britain. (Lebanon was under the French mandate, while Palestine-Eretz Yisrael was under the British Mandate.) Goldberg says the border was not defined precisely, and from 1949 until the early 1970s there was no fence.

Then, in the wake of infiltration incidents from what used to be called Fatahland, "Israel" built a fence - the "old fence" - where it was convenient security-wise, but not necessarily along the exact lines of the border. After a truck explosion in March 1985 in which 12 IDF soldiers were killed, "Israel" moved the border fence westward and northward - the "new fence."

"The future location of the border will be determined in the expected negotiations between "Israel" and Lebanon," Goldberg pointed out. "There is no argument that soil was taken from the Ayoun valley, both by Lebanese from areas away from the border and by Metulla residents and others," he emphasized, "but that does not determine the border. I repeat, the earth that Metulla residents took is earth owned by them throughout 100 years of [Jewish] settlement. With the beginning of negotiations between "Israel" and Lebanon, the inhabitants of Metulla will demand their private lands in the Ayoun valley, some 4,000 dunam (1,000 acres) north of the fence. This is land given by the "Israeli" government in 1949 to the Lebanese after signing the cease-fire agreement. The land given in the 1980s as a temporary substitute for agricultural purposes to the farmers of Metulla in the Galilee and the Hula valley cannot replace our lands in the Ayoun valley, and we will not give them up. The border between the two countries must be clearly marked out again".
