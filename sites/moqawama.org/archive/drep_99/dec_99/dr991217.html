December Friday 17-1999:

Introduction
The "Israeli" occupation forces have once again brushed aside all the international conventions blatantly violating the April Understanding and committing a massacre against humanity and childhood by targeting the schoolchildren of the Arab Salim school. The "Israeli" rounds took the citizens of Arab Salim aback as two "Israeli" internationally-banned cluster shells slammed into the school, injuring twenty children, four of them seriously.

Hizbullah Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said that the resistance would be justified in responding with the Katyusha rockets on the settlements of northern Galilee but had chosen to de-escalate the situation. "In our opinion, it is worth this kind of reply but we are going to settle for a warning addressed to the Zionists", he said.

"ISRAEL" SHELLS SOUTH LEBANON SCHOOL, INJURES AT LEAST TWENTY
 "Some twenty children were seriously wounded today (yesterday) when "Israel" bombed a school in the village of Arab Salim, just north of the zone "Israel" occupies in southern Lebanon. An "Israeli" mortar bomb targeted the school's playground while some children, all of them less than ten years old, were on a short break. The playground and one a third grade class were full of blood and shattered windows.

Books, carefully-wrapped sandwiches, and juice cans mixed with the blood of the young students on the scene, which witnesses described as hysterical. One witness said "it looked like a stampede". Lebanese police said the exact extent of the casualties was still unclear because the wounded had only been evacuated with great difficulty to hospital in the nearby town of Nabatieh as the road was also targeted by "Israeli" gunners following the attack on the school. About fifteen children made it to the Nabatieh hospital, all suffering from shrapnels, according to doctors from "Israeli" cluster shells, which are banned by international conventions. Following are the names of children who are now in area hospitals: Mona Farhat, Hassan Saade, Hannan Moussa, Ali Shrara, Marwa Hassan, Malak Shamseddine, Hussein Harb (critical condition), Ali Hammoud, Mohammed Nathar (critical condition), Ali Nathar, Hassan Farhat, Mahmoud Moussa, Mona Shrara, Rabab Fahs, and Shadi Hatab. Hospital officials said five of the children suffered broken limbs as they tried to escape the bloodbath(1)

"The children were wounded when three mortar rounds fired from the Soweida outpost, 1 kilometer south of the school, exploded in the air beside their first-floor classroom.

The school sits on a small hill within clear view of Soweida.

Three adjacent classrooms were damaged in the attack. Gouts of dried blood and small pieces of flesh splattered the walls, desks, overturned chairs and the floor of the schoolroom where 16 children were injured.

There were 22 pupils in the schoolroom. Blood-soaked exercise books were still open to the page where they were translating a French text.

Shrapnel had gouged jagged holes in the aluminum window frames and pockmarked the walls. The staircase was streaked with blood where the injured students fled the shelling. Shards of shattered glass crunched underfoot as dazed teachers inspected the damage in the school.

Two were wounded in the neighboring geography class.

Everyone was screaming and running out of the rooms in panic, said Mohammed Farhat, who taught the geography class.

The children were herded to the ground floor. Casualties were taken to Nabatiehs Najdeh Shaabia hospital.

This is the second time the school has been subjected to direct shelling in three months. The school lacks the minimum safety element including shelters in order to put up with "Israeli" aggression, said Afif Noureddine, the school director.

Mona Shrara, 9, who was hit in the neck by shrapnel, said her teacher had instructed the pupils to hide under their desks after the first shell exploded. There was lots of screaming, she said. I felt pain in my neck, then saw blood and I started crying

One student, Hussein Harb, 10, was seriously wounded, with a shattered shoulder and a punctured lung and had to be taken to a Beirut hospital.

His mother, Maryam Hammoud, said the attack was a disaster. This isnt the first time they hit the school but its the worst. They wanted to hurt our children, she said.

Hassan Roumane, the physician attending to Harb, said the boy was lucky to be alive.

"Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said that the resistance would be justified in responding with a Katyusha attack on the settlements of northern Galilee but had chosen to de-escalate the situation.

In our opinion its worth this kind of reply but were going to settle for a warning addressed to the Zionists, he said.

"The bombardment was condemned by President Emile Lahoud, who called for an urgent session of the five-nation Monitoring Group.

Premier Salim Hoss described the attack as horrific.

This crime is the latest in a litany of outrages perpetrated by "Israel" against innocent civilians in Lebanon, he told reporters(2)

Lebanon drops call for land-for-peace(4)
 "Prime Minister Salim Hoss insisted on Thursday that Lebanon had dropped land-for-peace as a basis for its peace talks with "Israel", and would continue to demand "Israel's" unconditional withdrawal from the south.

Lebanon is not concerned with UN Security Council Resolutions 242 and 338, which call for "Israel's" withdrawal from Arab territories seized in 1967, the premier told Melhem Karam, the president of the Journalists Union, and other members of the unions council .

Resolution 242 is based on the principle of trading land for peace. We do not agree to this principle because we adhere to Resolution 425 that calls on "Israel" to withdraw completely from Lebanese territories, back to the internationally recognized borders, he added.

Since the 1991 Madrid Conference, which brought together Lebanese, "Israeli", Syrian, Jordanian and Palestinian delegates, land-for-peace has been the basis for peace talks and has been officially accepted by Lebanon.

"Earlier this month, the government endorsed seven conditions for peace with "Israel", which included the enforcement of UN Resolutions 242, 338 and 425.

Hoss said that Lebanese negotiators, who are awaiting the results of current Syrian-"Israeli" talks, will ask for compensation for the damage it suffered from "Israel's" aggression during the last 21 years. We will also insist on the return of Palestinian refugees to their homes.

Hoss argued that without a solution to the issues of refugees and Jerusalem, there will be no peace. There may be a settlement, but the two issues will remain as timebombs.

He also said that the Shibaa farms, a disputed territory in the south which "Israel" annexed in 1967, will be claimed by Lebanon during the talks, together with Lebanese water resources that are being tapped by "Israel".

"The seven conditions for peace, originally issued by Parliament earlier this month, included similar demands, in addition to a request that the seven villages, given to Palestine under a 1923 Anglo-French agreement, also be returned to Lebanon.

"Hoss, however, did not commit himself to this demand, saying only it would be another matter.
Parliament also called for the release of Lebanese detainees from "Israeli" jails and insisted that the fate of pro-"Israeli" militiamen would be decided by Lebanese authorities.

Hoss said Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa would visit Beirut on Sunday to brief Lebanese officials on the Washington talks. Sharaa and "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak began peace negotiations in Washington on Wednesday, the first such talks in four years.

Western diplomatic sources in Beirut told The Daily Star that the US and France understand Lebanons fears over the possibility of settling the refugees permanently in host countries.

However, the two countries will try to convince Lebanon to allow refugees who arrived in 1948 to choose between staying and returning, according to one source, who spoke on condition of anonymity.

The refugees who arrived later, like those from the West Bank and Gaza after 1967, will be moved to other Arab countries or to countries that are open for immigrants, like Canada and Australia, one source said.

"ISRAEL" DETAINS TWO CIVILIANS, EXPELS MAN FROM OCCUPIED ZONE(5)
""Israeli" occupation forces today (yesterday) detained two civilians from the occupied village of Arnoun. Hussein Ahmad Assaf and Ibrahim Assaf were driven from their homes to the Beaufort Castle, which overlooks occupied Arnoun and serves as an "Israeli" outpost in the region. In another development, occupation forces expelled thirty-year-old Zeid Barakat Barakat from the occupied zone. He was snatched from his home in occupied Showaya, in the eastern sector of the area "Israel" occupies and was taken to the Khiam detention camp. Local radio stations in Beirut said Barakat was tortured in Khiam before he was released and expelled from the zone. The reasons behind his detention have not been clear".
