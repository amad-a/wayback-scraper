Daily Report
 
 
March Monday 8-1999:
 
"Israel", U.S. warn against doing another Arnoun Mlikh demonstration ends after occupation forces open fire ...(1) .
 "The precedent set when Arnoun was stormed by hundreds of students appears to have spread to Mlikh in Iqlim al-Touffah, with the residents staging a sit-in Sunday and vowing to liberate their village".

"The protest by the residents of Mlikh came despite an alleged threat from "Israel" that further Arnouns would not be tolerated and demonstrators risked being shot, according to Al-Hayat newspaper.

"U.S. Deputy Head of Mission in Lebanon David Hale informed several parties that the Israelis could not stand back and do nothing if theyre faced with another Arnoun, the newspaper said.

"The paper said that Mr. Hale advised Mr. Hoss to delay new demonstrations aimed at liberating other villages in the zone. However, a source in the prime ministers office denied that Mr. Hale had been so specific.

Mr. Hale was more general than that, calling for restraint and peace in the area, the source said.
"Dozens of Mlikh residents staged a three-hour sit-in in Jarjouh to pressure the government to remove the thousands of land mines planted around the village and provide electricity and water to their homes".

"The sit-in began after a march towards the village came to a halt when occupation forces opened fire at them from their positions in Sojod and Aishiyeh".

"The residents held Lebanese flags and pictures of President Emile Lahoud while chanting slogans describing their wish to return to their homes".

"Mlikh is located 5 kilometers northwest of Jarjouh in Iqlim al-Touffah on the edge of the occupation
zone. The village is largely deserted and is only accessible by a single road from Jarjouh via Louaize.

Mlikh lies close to the "Israeli" outposts of Sojod, Bir Kallab, Rihan and Aramta and is regularly hit by "Israeli" shelling and air raids. Most of the population fled long ago, leaving only a handful of elderly residents".

We pledge to our fellow residents who remain in Mlikh that we shall have no rest until we are all back in our homes together, said Khalil Abu Zeid, a resident of Mlikh who attended the protest".

"Asked if the authorities would encourage further Arnouns, the prime ministerial source said the government had little say in the matter".

Arnoun was strictly a popular, volunteer movement. The people of Mlikh will think it over and do what they must. The army will not stop them from approaching the village and the government is not interfering, the source said.
