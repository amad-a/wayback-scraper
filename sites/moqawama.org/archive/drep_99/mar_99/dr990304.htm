Netanyahu floats idea of a unilateral pullout U-turn for "Israeli" prime minister as occupation dominates election campaign
""Israel's" leadership fractured across the board over south Lebanon Wednesday, with even Benjamin Netanyahu voicing for the first time support for the idea of a unilateral withdrawal to end the 21-year occupation.

"At the same time, he knocked down a surprise proposal by Foreign Minister Ariel Sharon ­ whose plotting and over-arching ambition led to the 1982 invasion and the horrors that ensued ­ to delay the May 17 elections and form a government of national unity to pull a demoralized "Israeli" Army out of the south".

"While these developments may bolster hopes that there finally may be an end in sight to the occupation, there was, as usual, the suspicion that the remarks by the two right-wing hard-liners were nothing more than campaign maneuvers to garner votes from among the growing body of "Israelis" who want nothing more than to get out of south Lebanon as quickly as possible.

"That the Lebanon issue has caused deepening political divisions within the government as well as the
nation as a whole was underlined when hours earlier, War Minister Moshe Arens was quoted by the Jerusalem Post as suggesting that "Israel" should abandon the U.S.-brokered April 1996
Understanding that prohibits attacks on civilians.

"He declared that the restrictions this imposed are bad for the "Israeli" Army ... The Understanding creates a situation in which Hizbullah is free to launch attacks against "Israeli" and South Lebanon Army* soldiers in the "security zone" and we are not free to attack their bases. Asked if "Israel" might abandon the Understanding, Mr. Arens, Mr. Netanyahus onetime mentor who was made defense minister last month after Yitzhak Mordechai resigned to run against the premier in May, said: Its a possibility among a number of options that we have to look at. Changing something which has been agreed on is not a simple matter. But it was Mr. Netanyahus apparent change of heart on a pullback that surprised everyone, even though he attached the usual riders regarding security guarantees.

"He told the privately run Channel Two television: The possibility of a unilateral "Israeli" military withdrawal from Lebanon exists, but it must be accompanied by adequate security arrangements.

The TV commentator termed the premiers statement as a spectacular U-turn. Until Wednesday, Mr. Netanyahu had rejected the idea of a unilateral withdrawal, saying agreement must first be reached with Beirut guaranteeing the security of both "Israel's" northern border and the militiamen of the SLA.

It may well be that it was the promise made by his main rival for the premiership, Labour Party leader Ehud Barak, Tuesday to bring "Israeli" troops home by June 2000 if he was elected that left Mr.

Netanyahu floundering amid mounting public weariness with the human costs of maintaining the so-called security zone. Lebanon became a key issue in the fiercely contested election campaign after seven "Israelis", including a brigadier general, were killed by Hizbullah in the south in less than a week.

"Netanyahu did not specify the adequate security arrangements he had in mind although the televisions commentator said it was clear the prime minister was now ready to pull out without a prior agreement with Syria".

"The prime minister would settle for undertakings that Hizbullah would not attack northern "Israel" after any withdrawal, the commentator said.

"In March 1998, Mr. Netanyahu announced that he was ready to implement UN Security Council Resolution 425 of 1978, which called for an "Israeli" withdraw following its first invasion of Lebanon.

But both Lebanon and Syria rejected his demands. They have always argued that Resolution 425s requirement for a pullout forthwith meant without conditions".

"The Labor Party dismissed Mr. Sharons proposal, in which he said that a broad-based government was needed to solve the pressing problems of the stalled peace negotiations with the Palestinians as well as Lebanon, as a ploy designed to take the punch out of Mr. Baraks pledge".

"Netanyahu also rejected the proposal for a unity government, noting: We want to leave Lebanon, but we have to make sure that Lebanon doesnt come to us. Sharon aide Shai Segal said: The issues are so complex that the minister believes we need broad national agreement to solve them all (1)

"Israel": Hizbollah sharpens up its tactics"(6)
 David Gardner, the Financial Times' Middle East Editor
"Intelligence is a commodity in which "Israel" has long believed, not without reason, that it had a ruthless edge sharp enough to outmanoeuvre all its adversaries. It has come as a real shock to learn that Hizbullah - the Lebanese Shi'ite Islamist movement long disdained by "Israelis" as ragamuffin suicide bombers from the slums of south Beirut - has developed an intelligence capacity which can outsmart "Israel's" finest".

"This was made brutally clear on Sunday when Hizbullah guerrillas ambushed a convoy ferrying Brigadier-General Erez Gerstein through the "security zone" "Israel" has occupied in south Lebanon since 1978.

"Gen Gerstein, in charge of liaison with the South Lebanon Army (SLA), "Israel's" mercenary force in south Lebanon, was the most senior officer killed on "Israel's" northern battle-front since the full-scale "Israeli" invasion of Lebanon in 1982.

"But Hizbullah's ability to identify and attack vital "Israeli" occupation targets has been evident since roughly October 1995. It was then that the "Israeli" Defence Forces** (IDF) began noticing a qualitative change in the Shi'ite movement's tactics - in particular its ability to anticipate the movements of senior "Israeli" intelligence officers and elite units.

"This was not because the guerrillas had acquired better weapons. Nor was there any change in the motivation of Hizbullah volunteers, who between 1982 and 1985 had forced "Israel's" invasion force to retreat from Beirut to the southern buffer under attrition spearheaded by suicide bombers. It was primarily because the "Party of God" had immeasurably improved its intelligence gathering.

It acquired this capacity not only because it was fighting on home soil - the treacherous mountains and ravines of Shi'ite southern Lebanon - but because it had penetrated the crumbling SLA.

"Israel" originally built this force out of a predominantly Christian faction of the Lebanese army which had disintegrated during the 1975-90 civil war. It then enticed local Shi'ite volunteers with substantial salaries in an occupation zone which offered almost no alternative employment.

But successive "Israeli" offensives after the retreat of 1985 failed to dent Hizbollah, which grew into a popular social movement and legitimate resistance organisation commanding support across the sectarian spectrum of Lebanon. "

"Along with military stalemate in Lebanon, the prospect of regional peace also eroded the morale of the SLA. Dim though this prospect now is, nobody in south Lebanon has forgotten the lynchings of "Israeli" collaborators that accompanied the IDF's retreat in 1985.

"As the SLA's ability to police the buffer diminished and its members turned to collaboration with Hizbollah, IDF units were thrust into the front line, and have been sustaining growing casualties.
From June 1985, when "Israel" finalised its withdrawal to the "security zone" to June 1996, the IDF lost 159 men in Lebanon; from mid-1996 to today it has lost 162."

"These bald figures, however, are inadequate to express the wounded pride of the legendary IDF at this rare defeat by Arab arms, which has led former senior officers to question the wisdom of "Israel's" involvement in "the Lebanese quagmire". Whereas in the past, "Israeli" commandos used to hit targets in Lebanon with ease - including Sheikh Abbas Musawi, the former Hizbollah chief they assassinated in 1992 - many of its recent ventures north of the "security zone" have been fiascos.

Inside the zone itself, the IDF is often pinned down in fixed positions, and no amount of air superiority can compensate for this weakness on the ground. "

"In this kind of conflict, many "Israelis" believe their vaunted regional power is that of a giant with feet of clay. This sentiment, along with popular disquiet at the growing casualties, is fuelling calls for a unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon."

"Eitan Haber, a former aide to Yitzhak Rabin, the late "Israeli" prime minister, summed up this sense of powerlessness in yesterday's Yediot Aharanot newspaper.

"We, whose arsenal is full of atom bombs, who extricated the hostages at Entebbe [airport], who snatched radar systems from under the noses of drowsy Egyptians, who killed terrorist chiefs in their beds [in Beirut] with soldiers dressed as women, and we - we have no solution?" he asked, concluding: "There is none. We have tried everything in Lebanon."
