Daily Report
 
 
March Monday 1-1999:
 
Introduction
The Islamic Resistance (IR) mujahedeen leveled, yesterday, one of its most painful blows to the "Israeli" enemy since its occupation of South Lebanon.

Brigadier General Erez Gerstein was the most senior "Israeli" officer to be killed in Lebanese since the 1982 "Israeli" invasion of Lebanon, when then deputy chief of staff Yekutiel Adam was killed.

Gerstein's vehicle was struck by a powerful explosive device that blew the armor-plated Mercedes
off the road as it head toward Marjeyoun. Gerstein's death came less than a week after three officers from an elite paratroop unit were killed in the IR ambush at Berkat Jabbour in Western Beqaa. This successful operation highlighted growing opposition to "Israel's" presence in South Lebanon.

Islamic Resistance strikes deep into the "Israeli"-occupied area killing an "Israeli" Brig. Gen. and three soldiers .
The IR team of Slouqi Valley martyrs detonated, at 11:50, a powerful explosive device as an "Israeli" command convoy was traveling on the road between Marjeyoun and Hasbaya. A Mercedes car was destroyed completely whereas all of its passangers were killed.

Half on hour later, the IR squad of Zimraya martyrs blew up a roadside bomb as an "Israeli" command armored Land Rover was passing on the road between Marjeyoun and Hasbaya destroying the Land Rover and killing all of its occupants .

Also, the IR team of martyrs Mohammad Hussein Mazloum and Ahmad Melhem Chahrour detonated, at 19:15, a roadside bomb as an enemy infantry force was passing near Chiheen village inflicting a number of casualties among its members.

Three hours later, the IR squad of martyrs Ali Yehia Khalil and Mohammad Hariri targeted enemy squad as it was trying to reinforce the attacked force with the rocket-propelled grenades. Direct hits were chalked up, the militants reported (1)

For its part, AFP reported that "a roadside bomb detonated by Islamic guerrillas killed the top "Israeli" general in south Lebanon Sunday in one of the worst setbacks of "Israel's" 21-year occupation of its northern neighbor".

"With "Israel's" right-wing government embroiled in a bitter election campaign, there were fears leaders here could opt for more radical retaliation against Lebanese government and civilian targets".

"Gerstein, the chief liaison with the "Israeli"-allied South Lebanon Army, was killed along with two other soldiers and an "Israeli" radio reporter when a roadside bomb exploded near their convoy in the "Israeli"-occupied buffer zone of south Lebanon".

"The 38-year-old general was the highest-ranking "Israeli" officer killed in Lebanon since another general died fighting there in June 1982".

"The killings shocked an "Israeli" public weary of the Lebanon quagmire and already reeling from the deaths last week of the commander of an elite paratroop unit and two of his lieutenants in an Hizbullah ambush."

"Dozens of protestors blocked traffic and burned tires outside the defense ministry in Tel Aviv to press demands that the army simply withdraw from Lebanon before more men are lost".

"The war in Lebanon is stupid" and "Let's get out of Lebanon" read banners carried by the protestors".
 
Gerstein and his companions were travelling in a convoy of four unmarked armoured Mercedes cars when Hizbullah guerrillas set off a remote-controlled bomb along the road leading to "Israel's" headquarters in the "security zone""(2)

Jerusalem Post, however, said: "The convoy was winding its way down the mountain road back from a visit to the village of Kafr Shaba. As the lead - bulletproof - Mercedes rounded the bend, a roadside bomb went off, sending the vehicle twisting into the air as a burning wreck".

The blast immediately killed its four occupants: Brig.-Gen. Erez Gerstein, head of the IDF's*  Liaison Unit to south Lebanon, his faithful driver, Chief Warrant Officer Imad Abu Rish, and his radio operator, St.-Sgt. Omer Alkabetz, and Ilan Roeh, "Israel" Radio's veteran northern reporter".

The rest of the vehicles in the convoy came to a halt and deployed against more bombs.

"We found two more clusters of bombs ready to go off. I don't know why they were not detonated," said a senior officer on the convoy.

"It took about four hours for IDF and SLA** sappers to neutralize the unexploded bombs. The bodies were evacuated by vehicles. No helicopters were used", military sources said.

"Ashkenazy said it was not clear when the bombs had been laid, but that a convoy had passed along the same road the day before. The blast occurred just south of the village of Kawkaba, which is inside the "security zone"".

"Gerstein had been leading a convoy of senior IDF officers in south Lebanon to a routine visit to the village and SLA position nearby".

His deputy, Col. Shaul Kamisa, and the head of civilian aid, Col. Rafik Said, were also in the convoy.

"During the evacuation mortar fire started hitting in the ridges around us," said the witness.

"In Beirut, Hizbullah claimed responsibility for the attack, which raised to seven the number of "Israeli" troops killed in south Lebanon since the start of the year (3)

Furthermore the IR men spotted, at 13:30, enemy movements at Qamha post. Immediately afterwards, the Artillery Reinforcement Unit (ARU) group of martyr Abou Ali Shahla targeted them with the proper weapons scoring direct goals.

Five minutes later, the IR militants observed enemy squad's at Ahmadieh position. Shortly afterwards, the ARU team of martyr Rida Al-Shaher attacked them with the adequate arms. Direct hits were chalked up, the mujahedeen reported.

Also, the IR squad of martyr Khalil Yassine bombarded Ahmadieh and Abou Qamha emplacements scoring certain hits.

For its part, the Lebanese Resistance Brigades attacked, at 6:25, Sojod outpost with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades chalking up definite goals (4)

Nassrallah: killing the top "Israeli" general is a turning point for future resistance operations (5)
Hizbullah's Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nassrallah hailed the Islamic Resistance's.

"victorious" killing of the top "Israeli" general as a turning point for future resistance operations".

"This new victory will undoubtedly be the start of a great turning point in the operations of the resistance and the hopes of the people," said Nasrallah in a statement in Beirut".

"It will leave important effects on the nations of the region, particularly in Lebanon and Palestine," said the group's secretary general.

"With the wretched Zionist general, falls the great myth," he said, referring to the "Israeli" army's tough image.

"It made our confidence grow in God and in the capability of the resistance to liberate (the land) and shape the future," he added.

In Beirut, more than 300 cars, filled with Hizbullah followers holding the group's flag and raising victory signs, roamed the streets during the evening, honking to celebrate the deadly bombing.

Sunday's bombing brings to seven the number of "Israelis" killed in south Lebanon in less than a week amid an upsurge of unrest.

"More than 100 "Israeli" soldiers have been killed in southern Lebanon in the last 10 years".

Who are the "Israeli" killed?  (6)
"The four members of an IDF convoy killed yesterday by a Hizbullah land mine quickly became symbols of "Israel's" multi-generational presence in Lebanon".

"From the most senior officer killed there since 1982 to the junior communications sergeant who was doing his compulsory service, from the personable Druze driver who had become indispensable to every general in the north, to the reporter who covered southern Lebanon for "Israel" Radio, the four symbolized the two generations of "Israelis" who have fought and died there".

"Born and raised in the Jezreel Valley's Kibbutz Reshafim, and a long-time resident of Moshav Yogev just up the road, 39-year-old Brig. Gen. Erez Gerstein spent most of the past 17 years in Lebanon, first as a Golani field soldier, then as an officer, and finally for the past year as commander of the critical Liason Unit, the military unit responsible for the IDF's presence in South Lebanon.

Described last night by two former chiefs of staff as "brave and creative," Gerstein was wounded in Lebanon during the 1982 war. He left a wife and son".

"His driver, Master Sergeant Imad Abu Rish of western Galilee's Yarka village, was 34 years old and spent more than ten years in Lebanon, starting as former Maj. Gen. (Res) Yossi Peled's driver.

Described by his brother last night as "not the kind of person who belonged in the army, he always sought peace," Abu Rish was known to every general who visited the north in the last decade as both personable and, as former chief of staff Ehud Barak said last night, "extremely knowledgeable" about south Lebanon. He left a wife and four children, aged four to ten".

"Gerstein's communications officer, Sergeant Omer Elkavitz, was of a younger generation. Born in Ramle, he grew up in Rehovot. According to his sister, he was "deeply affected" by Yitzhak Rabin's murder in 1995, and had become a Labor Party activist who was saddened by the fact he couldn't be at home to vote in the party's primaries. In any case, she said, he always told his family that he was just a guard in Metulla, and had good food to eat and a warm bed to sleep in, so there was "no need to worry." But he had no doubt, she added, "that somebody had to do the work," and although he could have won a medical discharge or at least a clerk's job in the army, he "fought for a combat profile, so he could be up there."

"Ilan Roeh, 32, was a reserve sergeant, but he was in the convoy yesterday as a reporter. He began his career in journalism eight years ago as an Itim stringer, moved to Hadashot, worked for Ha'aretz, and was hired away by Voice of "Israel" Radio as a reporter covering the north. With his young wife, he moved north and became a reporter respected by both his colleagues and the military he so often covered in South Lebanon".

Tension & confusion strike "Israel's" leaders
"Israeli" enemy Prime Minister was quoted as saying: "We paid a very expensive price and a painful one. We've been paying this price for 20 some years. We are searching for a way to leave Lebanon that will meet all of our goals". Netanyahu is to convene the security cabinet for further review of the Lebanon situation this morning.

"The situation on the northern border is intolerable, and we are not prepared to accept this. The reaction tonight will not be the last reaction", Defense Minister Moshe Arens told a Tel Aviv press conference at which the air strikes were announced".

"Mofaz expressed his condolences to the victims' families, before praising their many contributions to the IDF and called Gerstein "one of the best fighters in the IDF".

"Thanks to the IDF forces in Lebanon, he said, residents of the North are living a normal life, "but this has a price. Today we played a high price Everyone that's fighting there, that's located there, knows what he's fighting for To guarantee the security of the North".

"I see the chain of events over the past few days, the attack on the paratroopers, the Katyusha rockets, and the bombing of the convoy with Brig.-Gen. Gerstein as an escalation which demands a response", said OC Northern Command Maj.-Gen. Gaby Ashkenazy.

"The MK Yossi Beilin (Labor) yesterday pointed out that in less than three years more IDF soldiers had been killed in Lebanon than in all previous years since "Israel" began withdrawing from Lebanon in 1986 (7)

"However, observers believe that Mr. Netanyahu is playing a dangerous game. Any civilian casualties inccured in the air raids or artillery bombardments will prompt swift retaliation from Hizbullah in the form of Katyusha rockets attacks into northern "Israel"" (8)

"In Kiryat Shemona, the biggest "Israel" town near the Lebanese border, the streets were largely empty as most residents obeyed the army recommendation.

City officials said they would deliver bread, milk and other staples to people staying in the bomb shelters".

"Hotels across the northern Galilee region reported mass cancellations following the upsurge in fighting in Lebanon, which coincided with a school vacation for the Jewish festival of Purim, which officially begins Monday".

"While Jewish schools were closed for Purim, authorities ordered schools in northern Arab towns to close for security reasons on Monday" (9)

Leave Lebanon now, protesters demand (10)
" Some 40 angry protesters caused a huge traffic jam in Tel Aviv yesterday, when they blocked the road in front of the Defense Ministry during a demonstration to demand that the IDF get out of Lebanon."

"Chanting "There's a solution, get out of Lebanon" and waving posters with slogans like "Wake up! The next victim in Lebanon is liable to be your son," the protesters lay down in the middle of Rehov Kaplan after they set a tire on fire on the sidewalk".

"Police directed traffic around the protesters and tried to limit the protest to two lanes of the four-lane road".

"This war is going on because the citizens are stupid," declared Yona Rochlein, one of the founders of Kav Adom (Red Line), which was founded on Saturday and advocates an immediate withdrawal from Lebanon.

"As the protesters exchanged insults with annoyed drivers and asked them to join the protest, word slowly spread that three more soldiers and a reporter had just been killed in Lebanon.

"Our kids are killed every day for nothing," Zamira Poran shouted at a youth in a Purim costume who got out of a car to argue with her because he was frustrated with the delay.

Police arrested Shaul Tzedaka, a communications professor at Bar-Ilan University, after he tried to force his way into the ministry. A lawyer who joined the protest said that he managed to talk police out of arresting him after he poured gasoline over the tires.

"Police dragged, pulled, and shoved several of the other protesters out of the street, causing one woman to fall, though they helped her up afterward.

"Dganit Katz, one of the organizers, said the demonstration was set up after worried parents contacted her and others and said they had decided to take a more active role, instead of sitting idly at home.

"We are not like the Four Mothers movement. Many of those taking part today left them because they felt it was time to take action and keep Lebanon in the forefront until something is done. Not just when our boys are killed there," she said yesterday morning, before the news of the latest deaths.

Poran who took part in the demonstration said she was willing to do what ever it takes to get "our sons" out of Lebanon.

"I today am willing to play a radical role, even if it means lying in the street, in order to reach the decision makers and meet with those in charge," she said.

Poran said she recently spoke with a bereaved father who regretted that he had not taken a more active role in calling for a unilateral withdrawal and ensuring that "our children" get out of Lebanon.

Meanwhile, Gabi Kfir, 52, whose son Omer serves in Lebanon and is due to be discharged in four months wrote to Defense Minister Moshe Arens offering to exchange places with his son. He said he believes there were many missions he could carry out despite his age. Only a handful of units actually go into battle in Lebanon, he said, adding there is no reason that he can't take over jobs being carried out by soldiers there.

"Our generation entered Lebanon and it is our generation that needs to wind up the issue. Our sons don't need to become casualties there because of our incapability to solve the Lebanese problem," he stated in his letter.

"Kfir who lives in Ma'alot says he is against unilateral withdrawal but wants his son out of Lebanon.
"My son's future is before him, mine is in the past," he told Channel 1 yesterday".

"The pretext for the latest operation was the firing on the upper Galilee town of Margaliyyot early Sunday morning. Hizbullah denied having fired the rockets, and may well not have been the party responsible for the attack".

"While the attack on the vehicle of Brig. Gen. Erez Gerstein, which took four lives, cannot be defined as a violation of the understandings reached after "Operation Grapes of Wrath" in April 1996, the Katyusha fire on Margaliyyot certainly can".

"The rockets caused no injuries and inflicted minimal property damage. In "Operation Peace for Galilee" in 1982, the war against the PLO began in the wake of an attack on "Israel's" Ambassador to the U.K., Shlomo Argov, by associates of the PLO's Abu Nidal".

"The "Israeli" campaign yesterday was characterized by a focus on targets in the area controlled by
the Syrian army in the Beka'a Valley, with the central target Hizbullah's command center in the city of Ba'albek".

"That target is a clear message to Damascus, saying that "Israel" knows Syria is capable of preventing Hizbullah's military activity, and if they fail to exercise that power, "Israel" will not grant the Syrians in Lebanon any immunity".

"There are a number of key questions: Does the operation have any chance of improving the security of the Galilee? Will it improve the IDF's effectiveness within the security zone?

"It is hard to imagine that the security of the northern settlements and residents of the Galilee will be improved once the military operation is complete. It is hard to imagine that the IDF's fighting ability in the security zone will be improved, or that its losses will be kept down by this operation".

"This war of attrition will continue to wear "Israel" down, unless the present operation leads to an inclusive agreement which guarantees the IDF's withdrawal from south Lebanon and the safety of its partner, the South Lebanon Army. Without such an agreement, events like those which took place yesterday in Lebanon and the Galilee will simply continue to repeat themselves, again and again".

Did the Hizbullah know who, where, when? (11)
"The question that arose immediately after the explosion on the road to Marjayoun yesterday was: Did the Hizbullah have advance knowledge about the movement of the convoy and the identity of the people who were riding in it?"

"Hizbullah, addressing the matter in television broadcasts yesterday, claimed the answer was yes on both counts".

"Officials at the IDF Northern Command admitted they were deeply concerned about the question, but said it was impossible to answer it unequivocally at this stage. The Hizbullah is known to have collected considerable information about the movement of IDF forces in the "security zone".

The number of routes through the area - especially for vehicles - is limited. The location of the IDF and SLA outposts are well known to the guerrilla organizations. Transit is always done in convoys which are relatively easy to spot, though the convoy hit yesterday included civilian Mercedes vehicles that stand out less than military ones".

"Most of the roads at the edge of the "security zone" are under Hizbullah surveillance. Perhaps the most significant factor of all is that the IDF and SLA know that many of the residents of the security zone cooperate with the Hizbullah and pass on information about military activities".

World descends on Arnoun to celebrate liberation, as occupiers fire on children (15)
"The southern village of Arnoun saw scenes of jubilation over the weekend, as hundreds of Lebanese converged in party mood to celebrate its part-liberation from "Israeli" occupation.

The government Saturday sent two bulldozers to the village to remove "Israeli" earth barriers which had been put in place only days earlier, and install pipes in the village, giving the residents regular water for the first time in years".

"For the first time since 1982, an "Israeli" ban on vehicles inside the village was ignored as a car was driven through Arnouns narrow streets under a hail of rice from cheering residents.

But the revelry was soured Sunday when a teenager was shot and wounded by an "Israeli" machine gunner stationed in Beaufort castle overlooking the village. Mohammed Nasser, 14, from neighboring Kfar Tibnit, along with dozens of other youngsters were attempting to plant Lebanese flags close to the heavily-fortified castle when an "Israeli" soldier opened fire to warn the crowd not to approach the military base."

"The youngsters ignored the shots, and Mohammed was hit in the left arm and carried away by members of the panic-stricken crowd. He is now recovering from his injuries in al-Najda al-Shaabia hospital in Nabatieh.

"The wounding of Mohammed was the culmination of increasing tensions over the weekend between enthusiastic students and protesters and "Israeli" troops watching the proceedings from the castle.

The youngsters, most of them members of political parties, frequently tested how far they could advance past the swing gate in the center of the village which separates the liberated West from the still "Israeli"-controlled East, and up the road leading to the castle.

"Further progress came to an abrupt halt when "Israeli" troops fired several bursts from a machine gun over their heads. Perhaps believing the students were not beating a hasty enough retreat, the "Israelis" lowered their aim and shot up the road a few meters behind them.

Later in the day, hundreds of Lebanese including nearby residents, schoolchildren, bejeweled middle-aged ladies and smartly-groomed men flocked to Arnoun to celebrate".

A group of women, their faces wreathed in smiles, were asked their reaction to seeing a car in her village".

We dont have to say anything, just look at our faces, one woman said. Acting on the orders of President Emile Lahoud, Nabatiehs governor, Mahmoud Mawla, sent two bulldozers to the village to demolish the earth barricades blocking the track into the village.

"A new road was asphalted Sunday as workers from the Nabaa al-Tasa Water Authority dug ditches to connect the village to the national water supply. Electricite du Liban personnel repaired power cables on the western side of the village while civil defense troops delivered drinking water to the residents. "The lack of water was one of the main contributors to the exodus of Arnouns population last November after the "Israelis" banned the entry of water tankers into the village".

"Hordes of politicians trod the path to Arnoun over the weekend to praise the mass civil action that returned the village to Lebanese control. Greeted by warm applause and ululating women, Prime Minister Salim Hoss arrived in the village Sunday promising all government administrations are at the disposal of the steadfastness of this dear part of Lebanon. But he refused to be drawn on whether the civil improvements to the village would be accompanied by the deployment of Lebanese troops, saying this depended on a security assessment".

If the people stand up to defend their rights, then the occupation forces will have to leave, Dr. Karam said.
