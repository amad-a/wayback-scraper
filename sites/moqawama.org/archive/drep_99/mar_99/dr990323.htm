"Israelis" smell a rat in the ranks of their proxy militia(1)
"With "Israeli" troops enforcing a clampdown on residents in the eastern sector of the occupation zone, reports emerged Monday that the arrest over the weekend of a senior South Lebanon Army official appears to be connected to the killing of Brigadier General Erez Gerstein in a roadside bomb ambush last month."

"Since the death of General Gerstein and three other "Israelis" near Kawkaba Feb. 28, "Israeli" troops and SLA* militiamen have launched intense investigations to discover whether local residents aided Hizbullah in the attack"

"On Sunday, three SLA militiamen ­ including Adel Younes, a liaison officer with "Israeli" intelligence services ­ were arrested and reportedly taken to the Jewish state.

"Israeli" officials denied that any militiamen had been arrested. But a security source said the denials were clearly aimed at mollifying local residents.

"The arrests appear to be connected to General Gersteins death. Several SLA officers were reported to have been with General Gerstein when he paid a visit to a widow of a militia officer in Shebaa shortly before he was killed.

"It was unclear whether any of the group were among those arrested over the weekend.
General Gersteins convoy was hit by a roadside bomb after it left Shebaa.

Residents of Hasbaya, Ibl es-Saqi and Ain Qenya have been denied the right to leave the zone since Friday.

"The population of Ibl es-Saqi has come under close scrutiny by "Israeli" intelligence since Khaled Munzer, a local SLA chief, deserted to the Lebanese Army a week ago."

Triple salary offered after morale sinks to a new low(2)
 "The residents of Jezzine are braced for a possible withdrawal of the South Lebanon Army from the occupied enclave, with the morale of the militiamen plummeting by the day'.

"Local militiamen are refusing to man the new gate at Roum for fear of resistance attacks and have asked the government for immunity from prosecution in return for their surrender.

"Speculation of an imminent redeployment grew Monday with the arrival of a Lebanese Army armored battalion to the east of Sidon district, adjacent to the Jezzine enclave.

Witnesses said more than 50 tanks and armored vehicles made up the convoy.

Security sources, however, denied that the army had sent reinforcements to the region in anticipation of an SLA withdrawal from the region.

This is a routine rotation of troops. An infantry battalion was replaced by an armored battalion. Thats it, a source said.

But observers believed that replacing infantry with armor was in itself a sign that the army is prepared for the eventuality of a redeployment.

The army is ready to deploy fast in the region in the event of any SLA pullout to prevent any sectarian strife that "Israel" might be trying to ignite in the region while withdrawing, the source said.

The source said that in the past few weeks the SLA, through Jezzine officials, has informed the government that it was ready to pull out from the region if the Lebanese authorities would not prosecute the militiamen for collaborating with "Israel".

Of course the government has rejected this offer and refused to provide any guarantees for the militiamen, the source said.

These developments came after the new crossing at Kfar Falous was opened and closed within a few hours. The new gate is in the village of Roum, 11 kilometers from the last Lebanese Army checkpoint in Ain al-Meer.

"In December, the SLA pulled back its front line from Sfaray to Roum after 17 militiamen refused to man the Anan compound due to the threat of roadside bomb attacks. Several villages suddenly fell within a new, extended no-mans land including Anan, Sfaray, Bizri, Mazraat al-Mathaneh, Rimat, Shqadif and Saidoun".

Security sources said the SLA has been unable to find personnel willing to man the new gate at Roum unless they receive substantial pay rises.

The SLA had to bring some militiamen from Marjayoun and the neighboring region after agreeing to pay them $1,800, or three times the usual salary paid to a militiaman, the source said.

THREE DETAINED LEBANESE ASK "ISRAELI" HIGH COURT FOR FREEDOM(4)
" Three Lebanese nationals held prisoner in "Israel's" occupied buffer zone in south Lebanon asked "Israel's" supreme court on Monday to order their release, court officials said. The three, backed by two "Israeli" human rights associations, complained in their petition that they had been tortured and denied medical treatment in the "Israeli" run Khiam prison, in occupied south Lebanon. The plaintiffs said their case lies within the "Israeli" court's jurisdiction because the "Israeli" allied militia acts as a proxy militia for the "Israeli" army in south Lebanon. One of the three, Sliman Ramadan, was detained in 1985 and later had one of his legs amputee due to untreated wounds suffered at the time of his arrest, his lawyers said. The other two detainees are Mustafa Jawad Toubeh and his son Ali, who was only 14 when they were arrested in 1997. About 125 people are being held in Khiam prison on suspicion of working with Lebanese guerrillas groups battling the "Israeli" and militia occupation of south Lebanon".

 "Red Line" demands withdrawal from Lebanon(5)
"An "Israeli" army source announced that 12 activists from the "Israeli" "Red Line" organization, yesterday demonstrated for the first time in front of one of the largest "Israeli" army recruiting centers to demand an "Israeli" withdrawal from Lebanon".

LEBANON'S HIZBULLAH MORE THAN GUERRILLAS(7)
"Hizbullah's symbol may be a guerrilla fighter's raised Kalashnikov but inside Lebanon more members carry books or medical equipment than assault rifles. "If Hizbullah is not the biggest, it is among the biggest organizations," says Hassan Nasrallah, general secretary of the group. Hizbullah, which means the Party of God, is certainly best known for leading armed resistance to "Israel's" occupation of 10 percent of Lebanese territory. Its steadily increasing military competence since it was formed in 1983, coupled with a proven willingness to die for the cause, has produced a steady stream of "Israeli" casualties. Once a ragged force that had little alternative to suicide attacks, Hizbullah guerrillas are now well trained in the use of their light, but deadly, arsenal of Kalashnikovs, mortars, Katyusha rockets and roadside bombs. Their intelligence gathering seems thorough, presumably aided by a sympathetic population, which helps to explain a number of ambushes of "Israeli" troops.
The most recent, a bomb attack on February 28 that killed an "Israeli" general, re-ignited "Israeli"'s internal debate about the wisdom of continuing its 21-year-old intervention in Lebanon. Nasrallah who can be met only after being taken to an office in a car with curtains drawn, is cagey about what Hizbullah will do if "Israel" ever withdraws from Lebanon. U.N. forces based along the border area dismiss the idea that Hizbullah would carry the fighting into "Israel" -- their cross-border missile attacks are always reprisals for "Israeli" attacks on Lebanese civilians. KEEP "ISRAEL" GUESSING "We want the "Israelis" to be puzzled," Nasrallah said after arriving separately at the meeting place in the Hizbullah stronghold of Beirut's southern suburbs. "Let the "Israelis" withdraw and we will see." Despite that, Hizbullah has already laid the ground work for a role in Lebanon which it hopes will long outlast the "Israeli" occupation of the south. "Hizbullah is now a political party," said Nasrallah, whose soft voice contrasts with the organizations fierce some world image. "It has its presence in parliament and all political activities -- and probably one day will participate in the government." Its political popularity is driven not by its prowess in ambushing "Israelis", but by a web of social services that filled the void created by Lebanon's 1975-90 civil war. There are Hizbullah hospitals and clinics, Hizbullah schools, Hizbullah ambulances. "It teaches the Lebanese, it treats them in hospital, it is there when they have problems," said a foreign health worker. There is no one else doing that." It had members elected in last year's municipal elections, the first in decades, and is active in labor unions and professional organizations. Its student movement extends beyond its own schools. There is Hizbullah television, radio and an Internet site. "I don't think there is any field of social activity where Hizbullah does not exist," said Nasrallah. For all that, he will give no details of the extent of Hizbullah's structure. After years of conflict with "Israel", it is a deeply secretive organization".
