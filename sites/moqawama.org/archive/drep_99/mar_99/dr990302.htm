Daily Report
 
 
March Tuesday 2-1999:
 
Introduction
"Israel's" leaders are in really confusion and embarrassment. Within twenty four hours only their attitudes changed and varied between two extremities. No sooner had they unleashed a variety of threats and fiery statements on Monday, in retaliation for killing an "Israeli" general and three soldiers by the Islamic Resistance (IR) mujahedeen in the "Israeli" occupied area of south Lebanon, than to retract them on Tuesday morning. Ehud Barak, the leader of "Israel's" opposition Labor Party said if he wins the premiership in May elections he will withdraw "Israeli" troops from South Lebanon within a year.

"Israeli" enemy Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu asserted, in responding to his main rival in May election Barak, that "Israel" could find a way to withdraw its troops from Lebanon within a year.

Meanwhile, Hizbullah responded to the early "Israeli" hollow threats by saying that IR militants will continue staging attacks in South Lebanon and dismissed "Israeli" threats of reprisals after killing a top "Israeli" general.

"Israel's" leaders retract their hollow and fiery threats
 - Barak: If elected he will withdraw from Lebanon by June 2000.
 - Netanyahu: "Israel" could be out of Lebanon within a year.

"Ehud Barak, the leader of "Israel's" opposition Labor Party, vowed Tuesday that if he wins the prime ministership in May elections he will withdraw "Israeli" forces from Lebanon within a year.

   "I promise you that if we create the next government, by June 2000 we will be out of Lebanon," Barak said in an interview on "Israeli" radio.

   "The withdrawal will take place in the framework of a resumption of  negotiations with Syria, a change in the type of operations carried out by our  army in Lebanon and intensive contacts with the international community about deploying an interim international force in Lebanon," he said.

   It was the first time Barak, a former military chief of staff, has committed himself so precisely to withdraw from Lebanon if elected.

   But he stopped short of endorsing calls by members of the Labor left-wing for a unilateral pullout from Lebanon, stressing the need for negotiations with Syria, the real power in the country.

   "Israel" has had a military presence in Lebanon since 1978.

   Since 1985 the presence has involved occupation of what "Israel" calls a "security zone" along the southern Lebanon border with the declared aim of preventing cross border attacks.

   The right-wing Likud party of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu denounced Barak's statement as "irresponsible campaign rhetoric."

   Debate over "Israel's" presence in Lebanon has moved to center stage in the election campaign following a spate of troop losses across the border, including the killing Sunday of "Israel's" top general in the zone, Erez Gerstein.

   Gerstein was killed along with two other soldiers and a journalist when guerrillas of the Islamic movement Hizbullah detonated a roadside bomb next to his armor-plated car.

   Their deaths came less than a week after Hizbullah fighters killed the commander of an elite paratroop unit and two of his lieutenants in a night-time ambush.

   As following previous setbacks in Lebanon, the recent deaths revived a soul-searching debate in "Israel" over its Lebanon policy.

   Netanyahu, who is running neck-and-neck with Barak in opinion polls, took a hard line on Monday, vowing harsh reprisals for any future Hizbullah attacks.

   But he also reaffirmed that his government "is continuously making efforts, sometimes publicly and sometimes secretly, to find a way to remove its forces from Lebanon without endangering the northern border."

Netanyahu asserted Tuesday that "Israel" could find a way to withdraw its troops from neighboring Lebanon within a year.

  "I think that we can find a way that would allow us to withdraw from south Lebanon in the next year, but I will not set any deadline," Netanyahu said in a radio interview.

   He was responding to a campaign pledge by his main rival for the prime ministership in May elections, Labor Party leader Ehud Barak, who said earlier Tuesday that if elected he would get "Israel" out of Lebanon by June 2000.

   Netanyahu said he saw two possible paths for ending "Israel's" 21-year military presence in Lebanon.

   "One option is a withdrawal in the framework of arrangements with Syria and at a price that must not be exaggerated and the second is a unilateral pullout," he said.

   Netanyahu said his government had been "seriously discussing both options in recent days," following a spate of troop losses which included the killing Sunday of "Israel's" top general in south Lebanon.

  "Netanyahu, who refuses to consider a return of the strategic plateau which "Israel" captured from Syria in 1967 and annexed in 1981, criticized Barak for offering a specific date for a Lebanon pullout".

   "This gives an enormous advantaage to Hizbullah, which will try anything to make sure the deadline is not respected," he said, referring to the Islamic guerrilla movement which spearheads the war against "Israeli" forces in Lebanon.

   Netanyahu and Barak are running neck-and-neck in opinion polls ahead of the May 17 general elections (1)

On Monday, earlier, Netanyahu and his top security aides appeared at a loss over how to respond to the latest troop losses in "Israel's" self-declared "security zone" in south Lebanon.

  Netanyahu held more than three hours of talks with his top ministers and security officials about the killing by Islamic guerrillas of "Israel's" top officer in south Lebanon on Sunday, officials said.

   Officials said hardliners demanded harsh reprisals against Lebanese civilian targets to force the Beirut government to rein in Hizbullah which was responsible for the latest attacks.

   But the meeting yielded only a meek decision to authorize the military to "respond in a controlled and effective manner when and as it sees fit" to such attacks, in the words of one official involved in the discussions.

   With general elections less than two months away and Netanyahu's right-wing government unwilling to consider Lebanese and Syrian conditions for peace, the unanimous conclusion by military analysts here Monday was that no exit from the Lebanon quagmire is in sight.

  "Israeli" artillery and warplanes hit targets across Lebanon Sunday after Hizbullah fighters killed "Israel's" top officer in south Lebanon -- Brigadier General Erez Gerstein -- with a roadside bomb.

   But the strikes carefully avoided civilian targets and remained within the rules set down by a 1996 truce agreement between "Israel", Lebanon and Hizbullah which protected civilians from attack.

   Gerstein, two other soldiers and an "Israeli" radio reporter died in Sunday's blast, which came less than a week after Hizbullah killed the commander of an elite paratrooper unit and two of his lieutenants in an ambush.

  Before dawn on Sunday, Katyusha rockets were fired into northern "Israel", slightly damaging a house in what Netanyahu said was a clear violation of the three-year-old truce accord.

   But Hizbullah denied firing the rockets and "Israeli" military sources said the missiles appeared to have been aimed at "Israeli" positions inside south Lebanon but overshot their targets.

   Fears that "Israel" would use the rocket attack to justify a major escalation in Lebanon were fueled when the army ordered residents in northern "Israel" to remain in bomb shelters.

   But "Israel" radio military sources as saying the attacks in Lebanon would only be stepped up if Hizbullah fired more rockets into northern "Israel".

  "Israeli" military analysts were unanimous Monday in predicting that Netanyahu, struggling for reelection, would not risk a major conflict in Lebanon simply over the loss of troops in the war zone, no matter how high-ranking.

    "The truth is that "Israel" -- like other countries facing a guerrilla war -- has no real military option in Lebanon," said Amir Oren, a military expert who writes for the Haaretz newspaper.

   "If Hizbullah doesn't respond to the air raids by firing rockets at "Israeli" civilians, the government will have no excuse for launching a major operation," he told AFP.

   "Tensions will simply ease until the next crisis," he said.

   Ron Ben-Yishai, military analyst for the Yediot Aharonot newspaper, noted that former Labor premier Shimon Peres launched a massive offensive against Hizbullah shortly before the previous elections in May 1996.

   The 17-day "Operation Grapes of Wrath" resulted in the deaths of 175 Lebanese -- nearly all civilians -- and contributed to Peres' election loss to Netanyahu.

   Ben-Yishai said while Peres launched his operation in hopes of affecting peace negotiations his government had held with Syria, Netanyahu can pretend no such aim.

   "This time the "Israeli" government has no diplomatic goal that it wants to reach by means of extending military pressure on Lebanon," he said.

   Netanyahu has rejected Syria and Lebanon's central condition for a negotiated peace -- an "Israeli" withdrawal from not only Lebanon but the occupied Golan Heights (2)

Hizbullah dismisses "Israeli" threats, pledges to continue attacks... (3)
"Hizbullah vowed Monday to continue guerrilla attacks against "Israel" in occupied south Lebanon and dismissed "Israeli" threats of reprisals after the Islamic militia killed a top "Israeli" general.

   "Nothing will prevent us from pursuing our operations, which are a legitimate right," said Hizbullah deputy secretary-general Sheikh Naim Kassem, speaking at a heavily guarded press conference in one of the group's secret hideouts in Beirut's southern suburbs".

   "Attacks targeting the "Israeli" army and its collaborators in the occupied zone were legitimized by the April 1996" agreement under which the combatants in southern Lebanon pledged only to spare civilians, he said.

   "Israel" conducted mock raids over the region on Monday, a day after pounding south Lebanon with air and artillery strikes after a Hizbullah bomb attack killed Brigadier General Ezer Gerstein and three other "Israelis" in the "Israeli"-occupied zone on Sunday.

   The deaths brought to seven the number of "Israelis" killed in southern Lebanon this year.  ""Israeli" Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his top security aides decided Monday thatreprisal attacks would continue while Kassem refused comment on the threats"    "We only react to actions on the field. We do not respond to Isarel in a mechanical way. We choose the suitable time and means according to our own convictions, but we are determined to protect civilians," he said.

   He said Hizbullah has been abiding by the 1996 truce accord but "if "Israel" carries out an invasion or a large-scale military operation, it will be the one taking the initiative to abolish it."

   "We are not concerned by the US calls for restraint because if these calls concern worries about civilians, they are addressed to Isarel," he said.

   Kassem said there were "little probabilities for a large-scale "Israeli" operation because it will be an expensive adventure ... but we remain prepared for the worst."

   He reiterated that Hizbullah did not fire rockets into northern "Israel" over the weekend, refering to "Israeli" army reports saying that missiles aimed at "Israeli" positions inside south Lebanon overshot their targets.

   "Firing Katyusha rockets is only a retaliation against "Israeli" actions (against Lebanese civilians) . We believe in the direct resistance against "Israeli" officers and soldiers and their collaborators," he said.

Top secret papers on Lebanon stolen from "Israeli" general (4)
 "Top-secret military documents concerning "Israeli" operations in Lebanon were stolen from the office of "Israel's" top commander in the north, the Yediot Aharonot newspaper reported Tuesday.

   The theft took place three weeks ago from the offices of General Gabi Ashkenazi, head of the northern command which is responsible for military activity in northern "Israel" and occupied south Lebanon", it said.

  Military police, who learned of the theft only a week later, set up a special unit to investigate the crime, it said.

   "An army spokesman confirmed that an investigation had been opened into a theft at Ashkenazi's office but he would provide no further details and complained that the affair had been made public".

 ""Israeli" army radio quoted military officials saying they doubted the documents fell into the hands of the Lebanese, notably Hizbullah which spearheads the war against "Israel's" occupation of a buffer
zone in south Lebanon".

"News of the theft came two days after Hizbullah guerrillas killed "Israel's" top general in south Lebanon, Erez Gerstein, with a roadside bomb set off as his armor-plated car passed by".

"Israeli" troops parents' protest against occupation (5) "
"Israeli" demonstrators yesterday blocked traffic outside the enemy war ministry in Tel Aviv to protest ""Israel's"" occupation of south Lebanon".

"Some protesters tried to set a tire alight in the street outside the ministry headquarters but police quickly extinguished the fire and cleared the street, witnesses said.

One man was arrested after trying to push his way into the ministry building headquarters, they said.

The demonstration was organized by the Four Mothers group which has been campaigning over the past year for ""Israel"" to withdraw unilaterally from the zone it occupies in south Lebanon.

The protest was called before security sources in Lebanon announced that an "Israeli" brigadier general, Erez Gerstein, had been killed along with three other "Israelis" by an IR roadside bomb in south Lebanon."

"Although the deaths had not been officially reported in ""Israel"" due to censorship regulations, some of the protesters had heard foreign press reports of the incident".

"Don't you know about the soldiers killed today in Lebanon?" one man shouted at police as they moved into to force the demonstrators off the street.

"The demonstrators held posters saying "Out of Lebanon" and "Enough of the death roulette."

The mother of an "Israeli" soldier said she would burn herself in the enemy war ministry if the "Israeli" army does not withdraw from Lebanon".

"This war is getting longer with no aim or hope", the mother said.

Beirut informs the international community of developments (6)
" Beirut is informing the international community about "Israel's" latest aggressions in Lebanon. "Israel" has been calling on the government to deploy the Lebanese Army along the border. President Emile Lahoud said "Israel" should first withdraw and Lebanon would then tackle the situation."

" Prime Minister Selim Hoss has called on member states of the UN Security Council to intervene to
stop the "Israeli" aggressions against Lebanon. Hoss described the situation in the south as worrisome, and stressed the world should know that "Israel" is the aggressor, and it has been breaching the terms of the 1996 April truce understanding by attacking civilian areas. The premier said the resistance is waging operations against occupation forces inside Lebanon, and that does not violate the truce accord. The cease-fire permits resistance operations against "Israeli" occupation forces, but bans all attacks on and from civilian areas. Hoss also said, the resistance is a direct result of "Israeli" occupation. He also reiterated that "Israel" should withdraw its forces from Lebanon without conditions in line with Security Council resolution 425. "Israeli" Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has for long voiced disapproval to the cease-fire understanding, and last night hinted "Israel" may scrap it. His Defense Minster Moshe Arens publicly denounced the 1996 deal last week, saying he would not have conclude such an agreement, if he was in charge three years ago.
"Israeli" politicians are split on what to do in Lebanon.

There is a chorus of hard-liners calling on Netanyahu to wage a war against Lebanon and destroy the country's infrastructure, while others want a diplomatic and political way out of occupied southern Lebanon. The latest escalation in the south was the subject of discussions between President Emile Lahoud and Syrian President Hafez Assad during a telephone conversation. Lahoud told his visitors he was not surprised by the attacks, saying "Israel" had set a trap in Arnoun, by annexing the village which was later liberated by college students. Lahoud said "Israel" was seeking to gain security guarantees from Beirut"

Damascus praises resistance operations (7)
"Syria said "Israel" could either unconditionally withdraw its occupation forces from southern Lebanon or face more losses. The official press in Damascus said "Israeli" leaders were baffled by successful resistance operations in the south. It also said a string of recent "Israeli" setbacks in the
south is undermining "Israeli" Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's chances of reelection in May.

Papers also warned that Netanyahu may take the risk and escalate the situation in south Lebanon. But Syria stressed Lebanon has the legitimate right to resist "Israeli" occupation."

Front-line tense in the south as Lebanese Army declares state of high alert (8)
" A state of high alert was declared by the Lebanese army, the Hizbullah resistance movement and UN peacekeeping forces in south Lebanon. This morning, the situation there was tense but quiet. We visited some villages on the front-line."

" It was business as usual for southerners even though an "Israeli" threat to bombard their villages was looming. But residents of the front-line village of Barasheet and surrounding areas had spent the night in fear after "Israeli" warplanes staged air raids in nearby hills. But this morning southerners were out on the streets. A man said that: We were a bit shocked by the increased "Israeli" air activity and raids last night. But "Israeli" threats are normal and we are used to the daily "Israeli" bombardment and shelling. So nothing has really changed for us. But precautionary measures were taken.

Schoolchildren stayed at home. A man said that: people are cautious. They are not sure what will happen. That is why they did not send their children to school. Some people left but only a handful. But despite the heightened tension, many villagers said they will remain steadfast. A woman said that:

we are not scared and "Israel" will not force us to leave our homes, our land, when they shell we stay at home and sometimes hide in our basement. Shell warning signs were placed at positions manned by United Nations peacekeepers. The UN force which has been deployed in south Lebanon since 1978 has been placed on high alert in case "Israel" launches wide scale attacks. "Israeli" occupation troops have warned UNIFIL not to use their helicopters saying they will not guarantee their safety. In south Lebanon yesterday morning, there was an uneasy calm. Residents are taking the "Israeli" threats seriously. But these people are used to "Israeli" aggressions and vowed to remain steadfast".

Uneasy peace, nervous waiting (9)
"The war-weary residents of south Lebanon nervously awaited an "Israeli" onslaught Monday, but the threats of a massive assault against Hizbullah failed to materialize".

From the ramparts of the crumbling crusader castle in Tibnine, south Lebanon appeared to be enjoying one of the quietest days in months with not a single shot fired in anger.

"The latest upsurge in tension began after Hizbullah guerrillas killed Brigadier General Erez Gerstein, the most senior "Israeli" commander in the south, in a roadside bomb attack Sunday.

Hours after General Gersteins death, warplanes staged heavy air raids in front-line areas, with the "Israeli" government vowing to unleash an air, sea and ground offensive.

Despite the lack of violence during the day, tension remained high throughout the area facing the occupation zone".

"The skies above south Lebanon were filled with the omnipresent roar of "Israeli" F-16 warplanes and the insect-like buzz of pilotless observation drones".

"Two F-16s, their wings glinting in the sun, flew low over Tibnine, heading west before banking to the south. In the nearby village of Siddiqine, Mehdi Zaarour sipped Turkish coffee with friends in front of his house".

"For Mr. Zaarour, the threat of an "Israeli" offensive was of little consequence.

It cant get any worse for us than it already is, he said. We are used to being shelled by the "Israelis", its a daily occurrence.

Siddiqine is adjacent to the village of Qana where 107 civilians were killed by "Israeli" artillery shelling of the Fijian Unifil base on April 18, 1996.

Forty-eight people from here were killed in Qana. What more can "Israel" do to us, said Mr. Zaarour.

Warnings were prominently displayed at Unifil checkpoints, after peacekeepers were ordered to go on full alert Sunday night, with the soldiers wearing flak jackets and helmets at all times.

Schools were closed for the day and many children were told to remain close to village bomb shelters.

In Majdal Silm, which has been subject to heavy shelling in the past two months, an estimated 400 residents fled northward in fear of an offensive.

Its very tense, said Nahla Salloum, 44, one of several women watching over children outside the villages bomb shelter.

With the distant peaks of Mount Hermon shimmering above the heat and dust haze, Mrs. Salloum says she will remain in Majdal Silm.

I will stay here because this is my village, my land and my country.

Let's just get the hell out (13)
"When the IDF* was stuck in Lebanon in 1982 as a non-victor and did not know how to extricate itself, our soldiers sang a macabre tune that began, "Descend from the sky, little plane, take us to Lebanon," and ended "And we will return in a coffin." The rest of the song is not important.The prime ministers who promised us that ""Israel" will enjoy peace for 40 years," "the defense zone will protect our northern communities," etc., are no longer with us. Our defense ministers have come and gone.

Even the enemy has changed. Palestinian "terrorists" have been replaced by the Hizbullah. The "peace treaty" with Lebanon has been replaced by a hair-raising, humiliating "memorandum of understanding" with the Hizbullah that allows them to kill our soldiers but limits the scope of our reactions."

Only one thing has not changed: "Israel" is still stuck in Lebanon - or, to be more precise, Lebanon is stuck in our throat, while our leadership has been suffering from collective paralysis on everything connected to the topic.

"This week, we will be asking the two-decades-old question: What more must happen, how many more soldiers must be killed, how many more families must join the "circle of bereavement," before our leaders can free themselves from their idee fixe on Lebanon and understand that our only option is to get the hell out? Common sense tells us, or should tell us, that we can never win the guerrilla war in South Lebanon."

"First, no army in the world has ever been able to defeat guerrilla fighters. Second, the Hizbullah knows the territory better than even our best soldiers and, as an army with a routine and set procedures, the IDF is more of an observable target than an observer; its actions are more predictable than the Hizbullah's. Third, the fact that the Hizbullah operates within a friendly population gives it an edge and limits our ability to react. Fourth, the South Lebanon Army** has degenerated from an army on our payroll charged with the task of doing our dirty work to a broken-down military machine that is useless without our protection and presence".

"Fifth, our physical presence on Lebanese soil has transformed the Hizbullah in the world's eyes from a terrorist organization to legitimate freedom fighters and has thereby freed Lebanon - and, chiefly, Syria - from responsibility for the bloodshed. "Israel's" presence in South Lebanon is a godsend for Syria, enabling Assad - by means of a war conducted by a "messenger" - to pressure "Israel" to surrender the Golan Heights. Assad has rejected Rabin's and Netanyahu's attempts to initiate negotiations with Syria using a "Lebanon first" agenda. As our deterrent capacity diminishes and our weakness becomes more prominent, "Israel's" presence in Lebanon is playing into his hands".

"Lebanon is Syria's success story: a prosperous country in which law and order have been restored, which provides a livelihood for a million Syrians and which is an integral part of Syria's military superstructure.

"Assad is in no hurry to plunge into a direct military confrontation with "Israel". Right now, he has the best of both worlds: as he sheds our blood, he enjoys the protection of our status as an "occupier."

Assad gets up in the morning without a worry in the world and with the knowledge that his interests are being protected, while we find ourselves going from one funeral to the next. This is an illogical situation. Not only is our departure from Lebanon imperative, it will change the rules of the game that is serving Assad's interests".

"We did not ask for permission to invade Lebanon and we do not need it to leave. We do not need to make our departure conditional upon suitable security arrangements or upon the presence of American or French peacekeepers"

""Israel" must defend itself from within its own borders, like other sovereign countries, without paying a weekly price in blood and without stomping about in the quagmire known as the security zone. The moment we leave and others can no longer use the alibi that we are occupiers, responsibility for future developments will be placed in the hands of Lebanon - and chiefly Syria. The security zone died long ago; it exists only in our imagination."

In guerrilla warfare, small is great (14)
"Hizbullah yesterday refrained from retaliating with Katyushas on the Galilee after the IDF hit bases throughout Lebanon. But that shouldn't fool anyone about the inevitable continuation of the fighting in the "security zone". Hizbullah is avoiding a full-scale conflict with the IDF that could hurt Lebanese civilians - and by doing so, it is forcing the IDF to drop its plans for the "rolling operation" announced by Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz. The IDF operation rolled backward to a stop".

"Hizbullah is following the classic rules of guerrilla warfare: After hitting a strong regular army, guerrilla fighters should go into hiding, avoiding the army's anger and direct confrontation. The guerrillas may later return to the field, where they are much more successful. Thus, Hizbullah dictates the rules of the game in the fighting by focusing on guerrilla attacks in the "security zone" using land mines and ambushes"

"That's been its strategy from the start. After the Grapes of Wrath operation of 1996, and the written understandings reached between "Israel" and the Hizbullah, the guerrillas returned to operation very quickly, and within a short time managed to kill nine soldiers".

"From the IDF's point of view, the main difficulty is that it has neither a full answer to guerrilla warfare, nor even a reasonable one. The IDF cannot force a decisive showdown with the Hizbullah even if the guerrillas suffer heavy casualties. In such a situation, "Israel" can expect a repeat of this week's events."

"The targets struck this week by the air force were mostly destroyed, but it appears the Hizbullah suffered few casualties. Even if they suffered more casualties than they published, it's clear the Hizbullah will do all it can to avoid showing its wounded - and not only as a strategy of psychological warfare, which it emphasizes in its strategy. The top priority of the guerrilla group is not to reach a situation in which it is forced to respond by landing Katyushas on "Israel" because it's now doing everything possible to avoid direct confrontation and return to the "little fighting" of the "security zone".

The security leadership of "Israel" faced a problem yesterday. On the one hand they could tell the residents of the north to leave the shelters, but on the other hand it's clear to all that there will be new rounds of violence in the "security zone". And that's the way it will go until a better way to protect the northern border is found".
