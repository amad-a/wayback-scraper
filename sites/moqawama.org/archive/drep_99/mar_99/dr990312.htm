Sayyed Nasrallah honors heroes of recent operations: Resistance ready to deter any aggression(1)
"Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah honored the squads of the Islamic Resistance's Special Forces' Unit who had carried out the blast of the Twentieth Battalion HQ in Jezzine, intercepted the "Israeli" commando unit in Berket Jabbour, and killed enemy forces' commander in Lebanon Erez Gerstein."

"His Eminence presented each of the Mujahedeen with a copy of the Holy Quran and Imam Hussein's (A.S.) Shield in appreciation of their Jihad and great sacrifices.

"The Secretary General stressed the importance of these operations "especially at this stage", expressing his personal appreciation and that of the Ummah for the Mujahedeen's insistence and persistence to fulfill their pledges to liberate the land".

"Your blows have shaken the enemy's entity and confused its military and political institutions alike, Sayyed Nasrallah said, adding that the "Islamic Resistance is completely prepared to confront all possibilities and defend our people with all possible means."

Sheikh Qassem: the talk about understanding amendment is Nonsense(2)
"Hizbullah Deputy Secretary general his Eminence Sheikh Naem Qassem affirmed Hizbullah's refusal to amend the April Understanding "no matter how large the "Israeli" violations and the "Israeli"-US bullying are."

"Sheikh Qassem told "MBC" TV. that such an amendment is meaningless.

"There's no sense in amending the agreement. Either we have an accord to protect civilians on both sides and prevent the firing of Katyushas against occupied northern Palestine, or "Israeli" forces will attack Lebanese civilians and we will reply by attacking "Israeli" civilians, said Sheikh Qassem.

"It is meaningless to talk about amendment, for both sides are in a state of military conflict and because hurting civilians on our side will be retaliated for by the Islamic Resistance, he added.

Qassem said the US talk about amending the understanding is an attempt to seize the opportunity of "Israeli" elections and rescue the "Israeli" who are being squeezed in a quagmire, so they (the US) pressure under different headings.

"Sheikh Qassem assured that the resistance is not and will not accept to be part of his game.
"We are dealing with the situation in our occupied land, and we will go on resisting until we achieve complete liberation. Thus, it is not possible to stop roadside bombs or decrease operations, nor we can enter a political Bazar," he said.

"We also refuse to be part of the "Israeli" elections. Our decision is clear: Resistance will continue because the occupation is still there. It is our duty to keep it going on and no amendment will take place, neither partially nor temporarily, he added.

"Concerning the enemy war minister's statement that any "Israeli" military operation will aim to amend the understanding, Sheikh Qassem said, "If the "Israelis" launch a large-scale aggression against Lebanon, this means the April Understanding is no longer there, and that the "Israelis" have changed this understanding's equation; therefore, they have to bear the consequences of such a measure and bear in mind that such an adventure will be very costly at all levels."

Hizbullah: occupation is the red line, enemy will pay very dearly for any military adventure(3)
"A Hizbullah official source has commented on the "Israeli" occupation army commander Eli Amitai's remarks saying "The threats made by Amitai has nothing new or strange, for he is nowadays in a position that is not envied by anyone, especially as he took over his post after his predecessor Brig Gen Erez Gerstein was killed by the Islamic Resistance," the Hizbullah source said.

"Amitai has already experienced the difficulty of settling in the occupied zone, that is why he sounds as a general crying out to boost the morale of his frustrated troops; however, his remarks on "changing the rules of the game" unveil the nature of the aggressive decisions adopted by the Zionist inner cabinet, the source added.

"The Hizbullah source noted that "If the Zionist aggressions are to target civilians and violate their security, this means that the enemy has decided to disregard the equation which had been imposed by the Resistance following the April Understanding."

"We will then consider that the enemy has started an absolutely uncalculated adventure for which it will pay very dearly."

"Killing Gerstein was part of an ordinary operation carried out by the resistance within the framework of the April Understanding. However, the enemy's enlargement of its "plight" aims to insinuate that the enemy plans to amend the April Understanding, a change which the enemy cannot achieve, the source said.

"Continuation of the occupation is the red line. The Resistance is committed not to stop attacks until the Zionists are kicked out of our soil completely. The Resistance has also pledged to protect the civilians' security and facilities", the source concluded.

GETTING ROUND "ISRAELI" MEASURES IN OCCUPIED SOUTHERN Lebanon(7)
" If none of her family members are available, schoolteacher Alia has to drag her neighbor along to accompany her to work. Her school is only two villages away within the "Israeli"-occupied zone of southern Lebanon, but without her neighbor she risks being shot or having her car seized by the "Israeli" army or its local militia allies. Alia has found in her neighbor's kindness a way of getting round a two-passenger rule imposed by "Israel" several years ago in its occupation zone" in south Lebanon in a bid to prevent suicide-bombings. "It is prohibited to drive alone," reads a placard erected at the Kfar Tebnit checkpoint, one of five passageways leading into the zone occupied since 1978. It warns that any offenders will have their vehicle seized. More than six solo drivers have been shot dead in the last 10 years. One of them, a motorcyclist, was a member of the "Israeli" proxy militia in the zone.

He was driving round in plainclothes to tell his men to fire on sight at motorbikes, which are completely banned in the enclave. The last successful suicide-bombing dates back to 1995. Carried out by the Hizbullah resistance movement. There were conflicting theories on how the suicide bomber got round the ban on lone drivers. "Israeli" officers said he had either a dummy in the passenger seat or a young girl who jumped out of the car at the last minute. The risk of being shot is not the only hazard in moving around the zone. And at night, forget it. The area seems completely deserted, with the only visible life a few stray dogs slinking furtively along the walls. The misery is compounded by the manifold inconveniences. Under "Israeli" security measures drivers are forbidden to overtake "Israeli" patrols and have to hang well back from military vehicles. Civilians sometimes drive alone within a village, but if a patrol comes from the opposite direction, a driver must pull into the verge, step down from the car, cross the road and stand on the other side until the patrol passes. Once, I had to go urgently to Beirut, but a passing "Israeli" foot patrol made me wait half an hour. By the time I reached the crossing point it was closed and I had to wait until the next morning to get through," said an elderly woman."

"Visitors to the occupied zone, mainly those hailing from the region and a few foreign journalists, have to obtain special permits from the Lebanese army and the militia which controls the crossing points into the zone. Once the permits are checked and names are registered at the last Lebanese army checkpoint, visitors drive about a mile along an arduous mountain road toward the "Israeli" allied militia-manned crossing. Visitors walk 140 steps through a narrow concrete labyrinth to an office where militia members sign registration papers, then they and their vehicles are meticulously searched.

The drivers have to fix a special license plate to the front of their vehicles. Every resident of the border zone has his own precious permanent plate kept at the checkpoint. When leaving the zone, people are subject to the same nerve-wracking procedures. "These measures prevent us from selling our crops which often perish before even reaching the shelves. It is getting worse and many people have stopped growing anything," said a farmer. "Anyone would think we civilians are a threat to the armed forces whereas in reality we are only struggling to survive," he said. But his wife smiled, recalling the time when a mischievous nine-year-old girl wanted to play a trick on an "Israeli" patrol by filling up a bottle with a dark liquid and leaving it in the middle of the road. "They cordoned off the whole area, searched nearby fields and started shooting at the poor bottle. The situation is so tense here it is getting ridiculous," she said.
