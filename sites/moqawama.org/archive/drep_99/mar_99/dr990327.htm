Daily Report
 
 
March Saturday 27-1999:

Arrests raise tension in occupation zone(1)
"A palpable climate of fear has gripped the eastern sector of the occupation zone in recent days following a wave of civilian arrests, travel restrictions and defections of South Lebanon Army militiamen.

"The "Israeli" clampdown on the area between Ibl es-Saqi and Shebaa began during the investigations into the killing of Brigadier General Erez Gerstein last month in a roadside ambush amid suspicions that members of the SLA* are now actively working with Hizbullah or the Lebanese Army.

Although the "Israeli" restrictions began in earnest last month, the mood in the eastern sector, traditionally one of the less volatile regions of the zone, began shifting last October with the first roadside bomb attack in Hasbaya for over five years.

"The attack, which occurred beside the local SLA headquarters, left the "Israelis" stunned.
"The "Israelis" had long considered the local population harmless, having exploited the link between south Lebanons Druze community and their co-religionists in "Israel".

Druze recruits to the SLA were given key postings and better salaries while Druze clerics were allowed easy access into "Israel".

"A security source believed that the growing likelihood of an "Israeli" withdrawal has prompted the areas residents to launch a more active resistance to the occupation.

The roadside bomb ambush between Ibl es-Saqi and Kawkaba which turned General Gersteins Mercedes into a fireball was just the latest example of this new sentiment, the source said.

SLA personnel are widely suspected of involvement in the "Israeli" generals death.
Since then at least two have defected and four, including an artillery commander, Georges Karam from Qleya, have been arrested.

"The much-feared General Security Service ­ comprising Lebanese personnel and taking orders only from "Israeli" military intelligence ­ imposed travel restrictions on several settlements in the district, preventing anyone from leaving the zone".

Students who came down from Beirut for the weekend found themselves trapped in the village Monday morning, said one resident of Ibl es-Saqi, who requested anonymity.

Travel restrictions on Ain Qenya and Hasbaya were lifted a few days ago. The ban on leaving the
zone coincided with dozens of civilian arrests and detentions. Around 10 people have been detained in Ibl es-Saqi and as many as 40 in Hasbaya.

If the GSS wishes to question someone, a message is sent ­ either by telephone or word of mouth ­ ordering the suspect to appear at the SLA headquarters in Hasbaya.

"Those summoned have little choice but to obey and their families are told not to report arrests. As such, Unifils Indian battalion, which operates in some of the area, often only learns of detentions days later.

"This arrangement clearly frustrates Colonel Guru Saday Batabyal, the battalions commanding officer. What do I do, he said, when the GSS quietly sends a message to someone in Ibl es-Saqi and tells them to go to Hasbaya, which is outside my area of operations?

"One resident of Kawkaba was too nervous to comment on the situation at all. Acutely aware of a young man hovering in the background, the resident gave a strained smile and said everything was fine, before adding quietly, I am sorry. I cant answer your questions.

Clearly aware of the image the local Druze have as collaborators, Sheik Fendi Shoujaa, a leading Druze cleric in Hasbaya, insisted that the local population had always sought the return of national sovereignty to the area.

The Druze stand has always been the same as the rest of the Druze in the country but only now is this coming to be known as greater attention is paid to the south, he said.

The split between the occupation forces and the local community in the eastern sector is becoming increasingly visible.

"The Bayada clerics, the senior Druze sheiks of the Arkoub, last week issued a religious edict forbidding collaboration with the "Israelis" in any form".

Roadside bomb injures two "Israelis"(2)
"An "Israeli" Army lieutenant and a soldier were wounded Friday when a roadside bomb exploded beside a patrol in the central sector of the occupation zone.

"The "Israelis" were apparently members of a commando unit on ambush duties in Wadi Hojeir on the edge of the central sector.

"The officer was reported seriously wounded by the blast.

"The bomb is believed to have been planted by Hizbullah and was probably triggered by a trip wire or connected land mine rather than by a guerrilla lying in wait.

"The casualties bring to 13 the number of "Israeli" soldiers wounded this year.

Six soldiers have died in fighting during the same period.

Hizbullah guerrillas staged early morning attacks against the Taibe post in the central sector and also against Braachit and the Qobaa posts. No casualties were reported.

"The bomb attack will have been an unwelcome start for the new head of "Israeli" troops in south Lebanon who has been appointed to replace General Erez Gerstein who was killed in an ambush last month.

"Brigadier General Benny Gantz served with the paratroopers unit in the West Bank town of Hebron and was recently trained by the U.S. military, according to a press statement released by the "Israeli" Army.

"The Chief of Staff, General Shaul Mofaz, was responsible for appointing the new head.
Brigadier General Benny Gantz was appointed to head the liaison unit in south Lebanon because of the high esteem for his professional and personal capabilities and the need to have top "Israeli"
commanders in Lebanon, a statement issued by the army said.

The chief of staff estimates that Gantzs talents will improve the armys activities in the region and its relationship with the South Lebanon Army, the statement added.
