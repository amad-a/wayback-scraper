Daily Report
 
 
March Monday 22-1999:

"Israeli" attempts to dynamite April Understanding
"The April Understanding Monitoring Group has been in session since Friday morning to examine five complaints, including "Israeli" dynamiting of houses in Arnoun.

"Meanwhile, "Israel" on Friday night threatened to stop dealing with the group.

"Lebanese stonewalling over an incident in the village of Arnoun could spell the end of Western-backed 1996 agreements in which both sides promised not to strike at or from civilian areas, an "Israeli" security source said on Friday, according to Reuters.

"The Likud-led government is in any case unhappy with the agreements, which were reached by the previous Labor party government after some 200 Lebanese civilians were killed in its 17-day "Grapes of Wrath'' offensive against Hizbullah, the agency added.

The agreements are supervised by a monitoring group which includes representatives of Lebanon, Syria, ""Israel"", the United States and France".

An "Israeli" security source said Lebanon's representatives in the group refused to acknowledge "documented facts'' proving that there were explosives in Arnoun.

"The behavior of the Lebanese delegation significantly hurts the group's functioning and threatens to turn the Arnoun incident into one that will signal the end of the understandings,'' the source said.

The "Israeli" army claimed that its militia had found five explosive devices in Arnoun on Thursday and destroyed two abandoned houses near the place they were hidden.

Prime Minister Salim al-Hoss denounced the destruction of the houses as ""Israeli" piracy'' and called on the international community to intervene.

"Israeli" war minister Moshe Arens complained last week that the 1996 agreements put ""Israel"" at a "big disadvantage'' but would be hard to disavow as they were "tantamount to an agreement with international connotations.''

"Lebanese National News agency said discussions at the group's meeting were heated".
"Lebanon's delegate Col. Maher al-Toufaili dismissed the "Israeli" claims that there were bombs in Arnoun.

Backed by the Syrian delegate, al-Toufaili stressed that ""Israel"" aims to create confusion and tensions (through these claims).

"If the Resistance had planted the bombs, it would have detonated them against the "Israeli" forces and Lahd militia because the April Understanding protects civilians not the occupation forces and their collaborators", al-Toufaili said(1)

""Israel" was judged to have violated the understanding for an air raid on Zibqine last week which wounded a civilian and damaged houses Even before the five delegates began their deliberations, there was considerable speculation that the "Israelis" had fabricated the discovery of the bombs in Arnoun to force an urgent session of the group".

"There has been a suggestion that the "Israeli" army is seeking to re-incorporate Arnoun inside the occupation zone as a prelude to a partial withdrawal to the Litani river. Such a redeployment would lead to Beaufort castle, which overlooks the village, becoming the only "Israeli" post north of the Litani. Securing Arnoun within the zone would prevent Hizbullah from using the buildings as cover to launch close-quarter operations against the "Israeli" army".

"The "Israelis" were never likely to gain reassurances from Lebanon during the meeting that their troops would not be attacked from Arnoun by the resistance. Consequently, the "Israeli" army may soon take matters into their own hands and seal off Arnoun with barbed wire and land mines".

"Elsewhere, a shepherd was lightly wounded when one of his flock stepped on an "Israeli" land mine on the outskirts of Majdal Silm, on the edge of the zones central sector.

"Qassem Maas was treated in Tibnine hospital before being released. The incident occurred in almost the same place as an "Israeli" double roadside bomb blast which killed three civilians in Dec. 1997.

Meanwhile, a high ranking SLA ("Israeli" proxy militia) officer, Adel Younes, 30, and two other militiamen were arrested by "Israeli" troops Sunday on suspicion of aiding Hizbullah guerrillas".

"Younes, from Ain Qenya, is reportedly suspected of having helped Khaled Munzer, a militiaman from Ibl es-Saqi, to defect to the government last week. ­ with agencies"(2)

Arnoun residents fear return of "Israelis"(3)
"The joy felt by the residents of Arnoun when hundreds of students released the village from occupation last month has turned to a feeling of anxiety following the demolition of two houses by the occupation forces Thursday".

"Residents fear their new found freedom may be coming to an end with a sudden increase in "Israeli" and South Lebanon Army* militia activity over the weekend. This sense of gloom matched the weather Sunday as a cold wind moaned through the newly-strung electricity cables and icy rain spattered the pristine, black asphalt roads laid in the days after Arnoun was freed".

"Hunched over a small brazier of burning twigs, Hussein Marouni, 65, said that the mood had begun to change in the village. The situation was good for a while after the liberation. But since the demolition of these houses, we have become very worried that the "Israelis" will come back, he said.

"Israeli" troops blew up two houses Thursday afternoon after claiming to have found roadside bombs beside one of the buildings".

"Friday night, the SLA began removing the rubble from 24 houses lining the lane leading to the castle, apparently to widen the road. The buildings were bulldozed by the "Israelis" in December and January. Troops were clearly visible Sunday morning erecting concrete T-walls along the edge of the road to provide cover for soldiers in the event of an attack".

"An "Israeli" patrol, accompanied by a bomb-sniffing dog and two armored personnel carriers, inched its way down the road from the castle towards the swing gate. Mr. Marouni stiffened in case the soldiers planned to cross over into unoccupied Arnoun. As they neared the gate, several soldiers raised their assault rifles and aimed them at a group of reporters. This alarming gesture, however, was tempered by the knowledge that they were only using their telescopic sights to gain a better look".

"This time the patrol chose not to cross the swing gate, instead turning down the road running past the two demolished houses leading to Kfar Tibnit. The road is the only access route linking Beaufort castle with the rest of the occupation zone".

"Ali Ahmad Ghazal, 51, who was detained and interrogated for 56 days in Khiam last year, smiled when asked if he believed the "Israelis" had really found roadside bombs".

No, he said. I think they used the bombs as an excuse to blow up the houses. They are trying to stir things up.

"Although released last November, he returned to Arnoun only a week ago".
