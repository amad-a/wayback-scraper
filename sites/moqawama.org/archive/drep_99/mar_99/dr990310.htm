Daily Report
 
 
March Wednesday 10-1999:

Introduction
The "Israeli" war minister Moshe Arens reiterated his opposition to the three-year-old cease fire accord which prohibits attacks on and from civilian areas, but allows resistance operations against the "Israeli" occupation troops. He, also, demanded his army to re-examine the alternatives for fighting the resistance militants in the "Israeli"-occupied area of South Lebanon.

In Jerusalem, a senior security source said "I think we are coming very close to a partial withdrawal". Two redeployment options are reportedly under consideration.

Following failed "Israeli" attempts to amend April Understanding, Arens wants his occupation officers "to find new options for Lebanon"(1)
""Israeli" war minister Moshe Arens has instructed his army's general staff "to reexamine the military situation in Lebanon and present him with various alternatives."

"Arens is apparently not satisfied with the conventional "Israeli" army's view that the current situation is the least bad of the available options, "Israeli" daily Ha'aretz said.

Speaking with reporters yesterday, Arens said that "we are in the process of examining various alternatives. The current configuration [of the "Israeli" occupation forces in Lebanon] does not seem right to me."

"Arens said that all the alternatives would be examined, including a unilateral withdrawal, adding "I am not convinced that the way to deal with the Lebanese problem is necessarily through negotiations."

"We have a serious problem in Lebanon. The current situation, in terms of the structure of the security zone and the nature of the Grapes of Wrath understandings, must be compared to other alternatives. We must ask ourselves whether this is the optimal configuration. To me it is clear that this is not the best alternative." Arens made clear that he was referring both to "the territory and to the rules of the game."

"The enemy general staff and officers from the so-called northern command will work jointly to examine the options and prepare the report. "Israeli" Chief of staff Shaul Mofaz will be responsible for presenting it to Arens.

"The study will cover the deployment of "Israeli" forces in Lebanon, the army's activities in the area, and ""Israel's"" various strategic options, Ha'aretz said.

Referring to the understandings reached in 1996, Arens called them "a serious handicap, which limit the "Israeli" army's capabilities. There is a lack of symmetry here, enabling Hezbollah to attack the "Israeli" forces in the security zone, where it is relatively exposed. [The "Israeli" army] is being watched, listened to, and information of its activities is passed on by residents."

"Arens added that "abandoning the understandings, which constitute an agreement with international connotations, is not a simple step."

The war minister said he favors negotiating with Syria, but "there is no chance of reaching an agreement with the Syrians without a total surrender of the Golan. Anyone who says differently is deluding himself or the public."

"Some people are ready to pay this price. I, for my part, believe that we must not do it. The Syrians must not be given access to the shores of Lake Kinneret," the "Israeli" minister said.

"In Paris, Foreign Ministry Spokeswoman Ann Gazu Sucrieh said the April Understanding has proven its usefulness and "its implementation must continue".

The spokeswoman's comments came in response to the "Israeli" request that France mediates to modify the understanding".

"The "Israeli" demand was made during the French visit made by "Israeli" prime minister's advisor Uzi Arad two days ago".

"A French source said Arad met president Jacques Chirac's diplomatic advisor.

Prime Minister, Foreign Minister Dr. Salim al-Hoss commended the French position on the implementation of UN security Council Resolution 425 unconditionally and Paris' commitment to the April Understanding's articles".

"The French stand is honorable and appreciated. We always rely on France's understanding of Lebanon's position on the "Israeli" occupation and implementing resolution 425, al-Hoss said.

""Israeli" public security minister Avigdor Kahalani said there is no need to link withdrawal from south Lebanon to the Golan Heights".

"Kahalani said 400 Hezbollah men have planted fear and panic in the hearts of "Israeli" troops".
"Israel" election talk about a partial pullout

Two plans on table for new shape to occupied zone (2)
"With the occupation of the south dominating the electoral agenda in "Israel", the sudden decision by the "Israeli" government to order a reappraisal of the military situation in the zone reinforces the belief that a partial withdrawal from Lebanon is imminent".

"Reacting to the recent escalation in the south in which six soldiers, including a general, were killed within a week, "Israeli" Defense (War) Minister Moshe Arens has tasked the armys working group with re-assessing the territory and the rules of the game.

"With Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu facing a chorus of criticism over his south Lebanon policy
and facing a tough fight in the May 17 polls, observers believe that he may order a vote-winning tactical redeployment, which would have the support of the "Israeli" Army".

I think were coming very close to a partial withdrawal, said a senior security source. Netanyahu has a sharp brain and hes gauging public opinion. I think they will make some moves on the ground.

"Mr. Arens has made known in recent days his dislike of the 1996 April Understanding, which he believes hampers the ability of the "Israeli" Army to combat Hizbullah.

"But Monday he hinted for the first time at the possibility of altering the shape of the zone.

We have a serious problem in Lebanon, he said. The current situation, in terms of the structure of the security zone and the nature of the Grapes of Wrath understandings, must be compared to other alternatives. We must ask ourselves whether this is the optimal configuration. To me its clear that this is not the best alternative.

"Mr. Arens decision comes only three months after the "Israeli" Army was asked to provide suggestions on south Lebanon by former Defense (War) Minister Yitzhak Mordechai.

"The "Israeli" Armys broad view then was to maintain the status quo. But that assessment may no longer be politically viable with the looming elections and a public grown weary of military casualties in Lebanon".

"The army is hard pressed to come up with a suggestion that is substantial enough for the government to woo liberal voters, while not undermining the operational requirements of the troops in the south nor the security concerns of the right wing.

A partial redeployment could satisfy all parties".

"Continuing to maintain the status quo is politically risky. Further "Israeli" casualties in the short-term will merely augment opposition to the occupation and cause a backlash on Mr. Netanyahu.

"But a unilateral withdrawal is considered too drastic an alternative and is still opposed by the "Israeli" government.

"Two redeployment options are reportedly under consideration.

"The first is to develop a new security belt two to three kilometers north of the border. This would involve building an electric fence from the coast to Mount Hermon, and planting land mines and
installing surveillance equipment along the length of the new zone".

"The aim of the scheme, which is effectively an expansion of "Israel's" northern border, would be to limit the number of targets for Hizbullah.

"The proposal has reportedly gained the support of a large number of "Israeli" ministers.
"Its greatest pitfall is the logistics involved in building a fence across Lebanon. Hizbullah will not sit back and allow the electric fence and mine fields to be established unhindered".

"The second, and less drastic, scheme is to return the Jezzine enclave to the government and
withdraw "Israeli" troops from Kfar Houne to new positions on the south bank of the Litani River. Such a redeployment would reduce the front line by about 40 kilometers, and relinquish some of the most heavily hit outposts in the zone, including Sojod, Bir Kallab, Rihan and the positions overlooking Nabatieh. Beaufort Castle, due to its strategic position, would become the only "Israeli" position on the north bank of the Litani".

I think the Litani option is very much on the "Israeli" Armys cards at the moment, the security source said.

"The greatest obstacle facing such a plan is the fate of the South Lebanon Army* in the Jezzine enclave.

"The only reason the "Israeli" Army still occupies the area north of the Litani is to provide a life-line to the SLA in Jezzine".

"But it is unlikely that the "Israeli" government will forego the political and military gains of redeploying to the Litani for the sake of the 200-300 militiamen remaining in Jezzine, who no longer serve a useful purpose defending northern "Israel"".

"But the "Israelis" will have to tread carefully. The militiamen further south will be closely scrutinizing
what happens to their comrades in Jezzine. The "Israelis" cannot afford to simply dump the SLA and their families in Jezzine, as it could encourage the remaining members of the militia to throw in their lot with the government before it is too late".

"The "Israelis", by nature, are reluctant to give something for nothing, which may also have deterred the implementation of the Litani pull-back".

"Experience, however, should have taught the "Israeli" government that Lebanon is in no position to offer concessions anywhere in the south".

"Mr. Netanyahu is a shrewd operator and is morally flexible enough to go with the prevailing mood in "Israel" ­ exhaustion with the south Lebanon quagmire and demands for action, whether offensive or defensive".

"As such, a partial withdrawal would help silence detractors and win votes, thus remaining the most favorable option for "Israel's" embattled premier".

Collaborator militiamen: we are sandbags for "Israelis"(3)

"An "Israeli" army officer was among three military men injured in the accident along with one of the collaborator's militia men."

"On the other hand, Agence France Presse talked about the law which has been presented by Hezbollah offering a three-month grace period for the collaborator militiamen to repent and cease collaborating with the "Israeli" enemy.

"If this law is adopted by parliament, more than 80 percent of the members of the ("Israeli"-allied) South Lebanon Army will flee the area and surrender to Lebanese authorities the next day," a collaborator militia officer told AFP on condition of anonymity.

"We have no cause to be fighting for anymore. We have become a mere human shield, targeted by our own people from the resistance. We are wanted by Lebanese authorities and we are dead scared of the "Israelis"," he said.

Last year, 35 militiamen were killed by resistance men and hundreds of militia members and collaborators are either wanted or sentenced to death or prison terms in absentia by the Beirut military court, the agency said.

"A growing number of militiamen are fleeing the enclave and their commander, General Antoine Lahad, whose wife and children live in France, has been condemned in absentia to death for treason, it added.
