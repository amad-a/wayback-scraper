Mlikh a different story than Arnoun(1)
(Israeli aggressions)
"Although the storming of Arnoun has inspired the displaced residents of Mlikh to try to snatch back their own village from occupation, the prospect of mirroring the success of the student demonstrators remains dim".

"Mlikhs location in one of the occupation zones most violent sectors is likely to frustrate residents attempts to enlist active government support to allow them to return to their homes.

"About 100 residents Sunday staged a demonstration at Jarjouh in Iqlim al-Touffah at the junction of the single road that leads from free Lebanon to Mlikh. Any hopes of advancing toward the village ended when "Israeli" artillery shells began pounding the area.

Were awaiting an appointment with the president. Then we will contact syndicates, religious leaders and student groups, Khalil Abu Zeid said Monday."

"Mr. Abu Zeid is one of the Christian residents who make up half of Mlikhs estimated population of 2,000".

"A source in the prime ministers office has said that the government does not intend to oppose the residents wishes and the army will not stand in their way should they attempt to march on the village.

But residents face an uphill struggle. Overlooked by the "Israeli" and South Lebanon Army* outposts of Sojod, Bir Kallab, Aramta and Tel Nammali, Mlikh could be a front runner for the unenviable title of most dangerous place in Lebanon".

"What buildings remained untouched by "Israeli" bulldozers and sappers were long ago destroyed in the incessant fighting that sweeps the area on an almost daily basis.

"Hundreds of land mines have been planted in the vicinity and access is denied by an earth embankment across the single road leading to the village.

"Mlikh was abandoned in 1985 but for three months in 1991 the residents were permitted to return home. The violence that envelops the district, however, soon drove the population away.

Given Mlikhs unhealthy surroundings, why would the residents wish to return?
The old people want to live their last few years in their homes, said Mr. Abu Zeid. They just want the opportunity to rebuild their homes, farm their land and be buried in their village. Over half the residents already live in dire poverty, so what difference does it make if they go back to Mlikh.
However, one fomer resident, at least, is unimpressed with such sentiments.

I can understand why the people from my home village are trying to return. Its a feeling of nostalgia for a place they havent seen for years, said Mohammed Daoud, who left Mlikh in 1982.
The village is no longer habitable. Its surrounded by "Israeli" positions, its full of land mines. Who really wants to live there at this time? he said.

Mr. Daouds brother-in-law, Abdullah Bashir, was shot and killed by the occupation forces in 1991 when he returned to check on his house.

He walked up to the earth barricade at the entrance of the village and they killed him. We were not allowed to collect his body and as far as I know, hes still lying there, eight years later, Mr. Daoud said.

SYRIA TELLS ITS LEBANESE GARRISON TO BE VIGILANT(2)
"Syria's Defense Minister visited Syrian troops deployed in Lebanon on Sunday and told them to be vigilant in confronting what he called "Israel's" aggressive intentions, officials said. "We in Syria and Lebanon know well the aggressive intentions of "Israel's" leaders. We are ready and prepared to confront them through the cooperation of both (Lebanese and Syrian) armies and the two brotherly states," one official quoted Lieutenant- General Mustafa Tlas as saying. He said the minister's visit to a unit of Syria's 35,000 -strong garrison in Lebanon was made at the directive of President Hafez Al-Assad, who is also the commander-in-chief of the Syrian armed forces. The high-level visit coincides with mounting tension between "Israel" and Lebanon's resistance movement Hizbullah, whose fighters succeeded in killing an "Israeli" general in occupied south Lebanon a week ago. A roadside bomb killed the general, two other "Israeli" soldiers and a military journalist. "Israel" reacting by launching air attacks on Hizbullah targets. The Syrian official said that Tlas affirmed in his speech on Sunday that Damascus was still committed to a just and comprehensive peace in the region. Tlas reiterated that Syria would make no concessions to "Israel" and that peace could not be achieved without the return of all occupied Arab land, including the Golan heights, captured by "Israel" from Syria in 1967.Tlas accused Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's government of blocking the Middle East peace process and urged Arab states to unify their ranks to promote peace. "We in the armed forces should be prepared at all time," he said. Syria has held sporadic peace talks with "Israel" since 1991, but the negotiations broke off in early 1996 without making progress".
