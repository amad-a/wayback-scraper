Introduction
The "Israeli" enemy warplanes conducted a sortie into the area of Jibal El-Botom in Southern Lebanon. "ISRAEL" TARGETS AREAS IN SOUTH LEBANON AS MONITORING GROUP MEETS(1)
""Israel" has stepped up its attacks on south Lebanon by carrying out air raids and pounding the outskirts of villages with artillery fire. This as the five nation cease-fire monitoring group discusses complaints filed by Lebanon that "Israel" violated the truce by targeting civilians and property in the south".

" Early this (yesterday) morning, "Israeli" warplanes raided areas north of the zone "Israel" occupies in south Lebanon. The "Israeli" fighter bombers fired two missiles into a valley near the village of Jabal al-Boutom, north of the western edge of the zone. "Israeli" artillery pounded with more than 50 shells the outskirts of the villages of Zibkin, Yater and Kafra as well as other nearby locations east of the port city of Tyre. There were no casualties or damage in the attacks. The Hizbullah resistance movement meanwhile said its fighters had attacked several positions of "Israeli" occupation troops and their local allies in Tair Harfa and Rahrah in the central sector. In Naquora, the five nation cease-fire monitoring group is discussing a complaint filed by Lebanon. Beirut is accusing "Israel" of violating the April cease-fire understanding by injuring Lebanese civilians and inflicting property damage. The truce bans attacks on and from civilian areas but permits the Lebanese resistance to launch attacks against "Israeli" occupation troops in the south. Sunday, a 33 year old woman and her husband were wounded when "Israeli" artillery shelled the front line village of Hadatha. A number of cars and houses were also damaged when some 60 "Israeli" shells fell on Hadatha. "Israel" has also lodged two complaints to the group accusing the resistance of launching attacks from civilian areas".

Euro-MPs demand "Israeli" withdrawal(2)
"In a rare such admission by Western politicians, a European Parliament delegation chastized "Israel" Tuesday, saying its security concerns were no justification for occupying another country.

The delegates also reiterated support for Lebanons demand for unconditional implementation of UN Resolution 425."

Were intentionally making our position more explicit than before because it has to be clear: There is only one way for comprehensive peace and this is full unconditional "Israeli" withdrawal from south Lebanon as well as the Golan Heights, said Hannes Swoboda, an Austrian who is also the vice president of the EPs Socialist Group.

"Mr. Swoboda, on a two-day visit with three of his EP colleagues, was speaking at a news conference in Beirut. The delegation arrived Monday after visiting Syria for two days.

Mr. Swoboda said the EU was against "Israel" using security as an argument to occupy south Lebanon. No country in the world can use the argument of security to occupy another country, or else everybody would be doing it, he said after a visit to President Emile Lahoud.

Mr. Swoboda also said the EU believed that the Lebanese Army was capable of controlling the situation after an "Israeli" withdrawal.

Mr. Swoboda also referred to last weeks verbal note to "Israel" in which the European Union stated its support for the internationalization of Jerusalem. The letter was viewed as a severe political blow to "Israel".

Our friendship and cooperation with Arab countries is in danger, more and more, because of these interruptions of the peace process, he said. We cant allow good relations between Europe and Arab countries become distraught by the government of "Israel".

Mr. Swoboda also said the EU was working on changing attitudes in "Israel" itself by maintaining what he said were close contacts with members of the "Israeli" Knesset.

We have close contacts with civil society in "Israel", and with mothers whose sons have been killed in south Lebanon, the legislator said.

Its our obligation to have relations with "Israel", he added, and to support politically, morally and financially all those groups in "Israel" that are in favor of peace.

The delegation is also looking into the living conditions of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon and in Syria.

"Claude Delacroix, a Belgian member of the EPs Committee for Development and Institutional affairs, said the delegation visited a Palestinian camp in Damascus during their visit to Syria and were relatively satisfied with what they saw".

"The delegation is expected to visit Tyres Rashidiyeh camp Wednesday.
 Newspaper discloses attempt on Lahd's life(3)
"A Lebanese daily disclosed that there has been an attempt on the life of collaborator's militia chief Antoine Lahd".

"Ad-Diar newspaper said reports from occupied Jezzine affirmed that Lahd who visited the enclave two days ago was to be killed by a powerful roadside bomb placed near the road which is usually taken by his convoy".

"The device was not prepared completely. Nor was it ready to explode, the daily said.

Ad-Diar presumed that the charge was set days ago to go off when Lahd's convoy traveled on the road.

The chief of the collaborators was supposed to sponsor a cadets' graduation ceremony in one of the militia's barracks in Jezzine, but the ceremony was canceled after discovering the device".

13 face prison after spying for "Israelis"(4)
"Eleven men and two women were charged Tuesday with collaborating with "Israel" and spying on the Lebanese and Syrian armies and Lebanese and Palestinian leftist groups, a judicial source in Beirut said.

"Assistant military prosecutor Myassar Shokor said the 13 were believed to have been recruited by "Israeli" intelligence to collect information about the Lebanese and Syrian military forces, in addition to the Communist Party, Hizbullah, the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine and the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine".

"Mr. Shokor requested sentences from 3 to 15 years in prison with hard labor, according to the source, who spoke on the condition of anonymity.

Before the suspects are sent to court, Mr. Shokors list of charges requires the approval of military investigating magistrate Riad Talih, who has the right to produce a different list of charges and propose different sentences.

"The group was uncovered after the arrest and interrogation of an officer of the pro-"Israeli" South Lebanon Army last month. The officer, Mohammed Khalil Ibrahim Nabaa, was responsible for security in the village of Shebaa in the western sector of the occupied zone. He is believed to have spied on the PFLP and facilitated the arrest of some of the groups fighters".

"The other men are Bassem Mohammed Ghader, Khaled Mohammed Asaad, Mohammed Taysir Qaadan and his brother Majed, an army conscript, and Jamal Mohammed Rahil and his brothers Saeb and Khaled, a soldier in the Lebanese Army, police Sergeant Nabil Amin Ghannam, Omran Deeb Daakour and Mohammed Khalil Hussein Nabaa. The women are Jihad Hussein Nassar and Ikram Ali Sheet".

"Some, including Mohammed Khalil Hussein Nabaa, are also suspected of selling drugs to "Israeli" troops in the zone and smuggling European and U.S.-made cigarettes into Lebanon".

Qantara: A southern village struggling for survival(6)
"The renewed debate over a possible "Israeli" withdrawal from the south has had little impact on the daily struggle for survival experienced by the few elderly, war-weary residents of Qantara, a village tucked just inside the central sector of the occupation zone".

"On a wet winters morning, the sky a mantle of gray cloud, Qantaras single deserted road and bombed-out houses provide a bleak testimony to the hardships of the handful of residents remaining.

Only 30 elderly villagers remain in the village eking out a precarious living from planting tobacco and tending the wizened olive trees surrounding the village. Given the meager income generated by local farming, the residents are forced to rely more on occasional handouts from Unifil and the International Committee for the Red Cross".

The economic situation here is very bad, said Mohammed Atwi, 68, the only villager interviewed by The Daily Star who was willing to give his name.

If I could leave I would. I only have some olives and one cow, which gives us some yogurt ­ enough to eat but not enough to sell. Someone from the Red Cross passes by every four months and gives us some rice, butter and oil. Thats how we survive, he said.

"Outside Mr. Atwis cold, cheerless house, thunder rumbled across the rolling landscape and wind lashed the windows with rain. Mr. Atwi tugged his brown wool abaya more tightly around his shoulders".

"Just up the road from his house lies a position manned by the South Lebanon Army*. The outpost
dominates the village and is regularly targeted by the resistance, firing mortars from outside the zone.

Inevitably, "Israeli" artillery located in either Khiam or Meiss al-Jabal pound the areas from where the attacks are staged, while SLA machine gunners in the Qantara post and other nearby positions in Qsair and Salah spray the surrounding countryside with bullets".

"During the 1978 invasion, fierce battles were fought in Qantara between Palestinian guerrillas and "Israeli" troops. Several residents were killed in the conflict, including Mr. Atwis first wife.

Following the deployment of Unifil troops in the south, "Israeli" forces retreated to the border and Qantara fell under the auspices of Finnish peacekeepers".

"But following the second "Israeli" invasion four years later, the village was incorporated into the occupation zone.

"Qantara is separated from state-controlled Lebanon by the yawning depths of Wadi Hojeir, which snakes north from Wadi Salouqi to reach the Litani River below the village of Qaaqaaiet al-Jisr.

Although Qantara is only 10 minutes drive from edge of the zone, the road running between the village and Ghandourieh is open only for UN personnel. All residents wishing to leave the zone have to pass through the SLA checkpoint at Kfar Tibnit near Nabatieh, a journey of around one hour.

Qantaras isolation from the rest of Lebanon is highlighted by the proximity of the front line village of Qabrikha, lying barely 1 kilometer away on the other side of Wadi Hojeir.

We do feel its unfair when we look at Qabrikha. We have no one here to tell us God give you strength, said an elderly women as she brewed tea.

The villagers have suffered from a lack of water since the system was destroyed by shelling 14 years ago.

We had to collect our water from the spring and carry it back home on our heads just as our ancestors used to do, the woman said.

"Now, they pay LL30,000 to have water delivered by truck from Kfar Kila.

Wrapped in blankets and huddled over the stove ­ the only source of heat in their home ­ the elderly couple had clearly lost any defiance they might have had to the occupation. Amid the demands of daily survival, they suffered a personal tragedy when their daughter was killed by a roadside bomb outside the village in 1991".

She went to the SLA position to ask permission to leave the village. She was returning from the post with a friend when the bomb exploded. Both of them were killed instantly, she recalled. Then she lapsed into silence, tears trickling down the creases of her cheeks.

"On the eastern outskirts of the village lies the Finnish battalions 9-50 outpost. The 12 soldiers manning the small post spend their entire year-long tour alternating between 9-50 and 9-51 located on the hill above the village. The post is only 100 meters from the SLA position and as such the
soldiers manning 9-50 record many firings close when resistance mortar rounds explode nearby".

Weve had a number of firings close in the last 10 months. If the shells hit 200 meters or less from our post we go into the bunkers, said Lieutenant Jukka Sepata, the officer commanding the 9-50 post.

" The platoon patrols the  village twice a day to ensure there are no "Israeli" or SLA troop infiltrations and to check on the well-being of the villagers"

"The soldiers greatest enemy is boredom. Other than a gym and a collection of video tapes, the platoon has little else to do other than add to the collection of rusting shell and mortar casings, exploded Katyusha rockets and other assorted ordinance deposited in one corner of the outpost".

"But for the villagers, the presence of Unifil soldiers has imparted a degree of reassurance that at least somebody has their interests at heart".
