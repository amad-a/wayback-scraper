"ISRAEL" WILL RETREAT FROM LEBANON WITHOUT SECURITY GUARANTEES: Hizbullah(1)
" "Israel" will pull out of Lebanon without any security guarantees from Beirut, Lebanese Hizbullah leader Hassan Nasrallah predicted in remarks published Friday. "Israel" has only two options: "to stay (in south Lebanon) and suffer additional losses of human life ... or to pull out unconditionally," said sheikh Nasrallah. Hizbullah spearheads the resistance war in south Lebanon. The "Israelis" "will withdraw from Lebanon sooner or later without Lebanese government guarantees," he said, quoted by the Revue du Liban. Asked whether he thought "Israel" might launch a new military operation in Lebanon, Nasrallah said: "I think the "Israelis" have learned their lessons from their various Lebanese wars." A withdrawal from the zone it occupies in southern Lebanon has become a central theme in the campaigning ahead of "Israel's" May 17 general elections. The debate was fuelled by the killing of the top "Israeli" general in south Lebanon in a Hizbullah bombing February 28. Brigadier General Ezer Gerstein and six other "Israelis" were killed in Lebanon within a week. Since then, the two main contenders for prime minister in "Israel", incumbent Benjamin Netanyahu and Labor Party leader Ehud Barak, have been speaking about pulling out of the "Lebanese quagmire." Nasrallah refused to rule out new Hizbullah attacks on "Israel" should "Israeli" forces pull out of Lebanon. He said only that Hizbullah was benefiting from a "Syrian-Iranian understanding" in its favor, and that ""Israel" should first retreat. Then we'll see." "Israel" has offered to retreat from Lebanon in exchange for Lebanese security guarantees for the border. But Lebanon and Syria have rejected the "Israeli" proposal, saying the Jewish state must withdraw unconditionally in line with UN Security Council Resolution 425.

"ISRAEL" STEPS UP AGGRESSIONS AS MONITORING GROUP MEETS(2)
""Israeli" warplanes this (yesterday) morning carried out a series of air raids in south Lebanon and the western Beqaa. "Israeli" jets also flew at low altitude over Beirut. The raids came an hour after the monitoring group convened to discuss Lebanese and "Israeli" complaints".

" Two jets fired at least three missiles on a valley located near Birkit Jabbour in the western Bekaa. Fifteen minutes later, jets fired a missile on the outskirts of the southern village of Okmata in the Iklim al-Tuffah mountain ridge. The raids came an hour after the five nation cease-fire monitoring group convened in the occupied border town of Naquora to discuss five Lebanese and "Israeli" complaints.
The group observers a cease-fire understanding in south Lebanon that bans attacks on and from civilian areas but permits the Lebanese resistance to attack "Israeli" occupation troops and their local allies in south Lebanon. Lebanon lodged a complaint to protest the wounding of a 60 year old woman in an "Israeli" air raid on the southern village of Zibkin. Beirut also protested Thursday the incursion by "Israeli" allied militiamen into the southern village of Arnoun during which they blew up two deserted homes. Explosives detonated by remote control blasted apart the homes and damaged a third in Arnoun which "Israel" and its allies briefly occupied last month. In Beirut a security source questioned how "Israeli" troops and their allies find the explosives if they did not enter the village last night. "Lebanon's Prime Minister Selim el-Hoss denounced the explosions as "Israeli" piracy and called on the international community to intervene. He said "Israel" had violated the cease-fire by destroying the homes".

"Israel" should withdraw unconditionally(3)
"An EU parliamentary delegation wrapped up a visit to Lebanon Thursday with a call for a more active European role as a regional peace broker in the wake of mounting frustration with "Israeli" intransigence.

Were one of the biggest donors to the peace process. "Israel", with the United States by its side, does not want us to play a bigger role in the peace process, Jannis Sakellariou, a European parliamentarian from Germany said.

Weve supported "Israel" in many ways, in their quest for land and their need for security. I cant accept that they now tell me were their enemies, he said in an interview with The Daily Star before his departure.

"Mr. Sakellariou said that there was nothing new in the EUs position toward "Israel", despite recent statements advocating withdrawal from south Lebanon or the international status of Jerusalem. He added that reiteration of the EUs position was triggered by "Israeli" behavior.

Mr. Sakellariou was on a two-day visit to Lebanon with three colleagues. The delegation, which also visited Syria, held separate talks with President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss regarding negotiations on a EU partnership agreement with Lebanon.

EU diplomatic sources in Beirut said that discussions with Lebanese authorities made it clear to the EPs that the Lebanese Army was capable of filling the void after an "Israeli" withdrawal from the south.

Buttressing Lebanons insistence that implementing UN Resolution 425 involved no security guarantees for "Israel", Mr. Sakellariou pointed out that "Israel" was the best guarantor of its own security.

The 425 resolution does not say that "Israel" is to withdraw so long as the Lebanese Army has enough men or tanks to take control of the area. This is a unilateral withdrawal, there is nothing to negotiate and nothing that "Israel" is entitled to ask in return, said Mr. Sakellariou.

He added that after the "Israeli" withdrawal, Unifil would deploy in the area, thereby fulfilling the mandate it was assigned 21 years ago.

Only after a full withdrawal will there be place for any negotiations or demands, added Mr. Sakellariou.

"Israel", he continued was the only one capable of giving itself the required security guarantees.
Simultaneous withdrawal from south Lebanon and the Golan heights and application of the Oslo agreements, this is the best security guarantee "Israel" could ask for. Its in their hands and its in their interest, said Mr. Sakellariou.

But the likelihood of progress is dim as long as Benjamin Netanyahu is prime minister, as Mr. Sakellariou described him as a champion of intentionally-broken promises.

If Netanyahu is re-elected there will be no peace during his mandate, he said. Trying to avoid a war will be the only thing we can do then."
