55% favor withdrawal from Lebanon(1)
"Fifty-five percent of the public favor an immediate, unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon, according to a new survey carried out by the Jaffee Center for Strategic Studies at Tel Aviv University".

"The survey polled 1,203 adult "Israelis" (excluding those who live in the territories and kibbutzim) and was conducted between January 25 and March 7. The margin of error was 3 percent".

"Prof. Asher Arian, who wrote about the findings, said it was the first survey on security issues since 1985 to show a majority supporting a withdrawal from Lebanon.

Seventy percent believe that a solution to Lebanon can be found without giving up any of the Golan Heights, and 72% believe that the security zone can be used as a bargaining chip in talks with Syria".

"The majority of those polled supporting a pullout were women. In addition, older people favored a withdrawal more than younger, while most of those supporting a pullout were of European origin".

"A similar poll conducted in 1998 found that when asked what effect a withdrawal from Lebanon would have on the peace process, 35% felt it might move it forward, compared with 51% today".

"In 1998, 22% of those questioned believed that the IDF* would be a far stronger force after a withdrawal from Lebanon, compared with 30% today. There was a 10% rise in those who said a withdrawal would strengthen "Israel's" democracy, compared with last year".

"Arian said the survey shows the public is informed and balanced and allows the government a large degree of freedom to deal with the issues. He cited the Oslo Accords, the Hebron Agreement, and the Wye agreement, which were more or less accepted by the public despite the controversy surrounding them".

Arnoun marks Adha for first time in years(2)
"Muslims across the nation celebrated Eid al-Adha Saturday, however, residents in the south limited their festivities to just visiting relatives despite the calm security situation".

"There was one exception, however. For the first time in 14 years, Arnoun witnessed one of its most vibrant celebrations as many residents visited their hometown for the occasion".

"Most roads and the village square, which for nearly a decade-and-a-half have remained empty during important religious events, came to life as people found it hard to believe they were actually celebrating the festival in Arnoun".
