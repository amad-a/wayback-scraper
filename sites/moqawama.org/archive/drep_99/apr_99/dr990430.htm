Introduction
The echoes of Islamic Resistances (IR) storming into Sojod outpost began to appear in the Israeli-occupied zone where a state of embarrassment and perplexity had reigned over among the Israeli occupation forces and their collaborators.

The Israelis were surprised by Sojod operation, especially the timing (in the afternoon) and the heaviness of the fire used by the Resistance.

Head of Hizbullahs Politburo MP Mohammad Raad said the IR operations Tuesday were part of the continuous resistance activity against the Israeli occupation forces and their collaborators all over the occupied zone.

Confusion in occupation and collaborators' ranks following the Sojod operation(1)
A state of confusion prevails in the ranks of the Israeli occupation forces and their collaborators following the qualitative operation carried out by the Islamic Resistance (IR) against the Sojod outpost.

IR Mujahedeen on Tuesday stormed the Sojod outpost and raised Hizbullah flags over the outpost's destroyed ramparts.

Reports from the occupied zone said the Israeli command were surprised by the Sojod operation and the series of operations that accompanied it, especially the timing (in the afternoon) and the heaviness of the fire used by the Resistance.

This had led to a semi-total paralysis in the Israeli and Lahdi outposts along a 10-km front, as IR was able to directly hit the outposts and inflict serious damage on their fortifications.

The greater shock for the occupation command was the precision of hits in the major Israeli artillery emplacements at the Rihan hilltop which damaged four 155 artillery emplacements and wounded eight of the enemy soldiers, especially as Rihan is one of the most fortified outposts, besides its housing the occupation's command in the sector and its being station for armored vehicles and major center for emergency intervention and long-range artillery, the reports from the occupied zone said.

In a bid to boost the morale of the occupation troops and their collaborators, an Israeli delegation including officers from the northern area command and the liaison unit yesterday inspected the Rihan outpost, the damage in the outpost, and how the troops there were wounded.

The northern area command has formed an investigation panel to find out how IR men were able to storm into the Sojod outpost and retreat so easily.

Sojod operation proves the real initiative is in the hand of Resistance(2)
Chief of Hizbullah's Political Council MP Mohammed Raad said the Islamic Resistance's (IR) operations Tuesday were part of the continuous resistance activity against the Israeli occupation forces and their collaborators all over the occupied zone.

"It is clear, from the operation which targeted the fortified outpost of Sojod and silenced around 22 Israeli outposts in the area all at once, that Israeli troops are still there, in contrast with the what has been announced about reducing occupation troops and using Lahd militiamen in front-line positions," Raad said.

"The second issue reflected by this operation is that the actual initiative is in the hands of the resistance."

The Israelis are frustrated whenever Resistance men attack outposts of theirs or their collaborators', Raad added.

He also stressed the qualitative advancement in the Resistance men's performance, their calmness, and certainty about the operation's outcome.

Everyone has seen (in the footage of the operation) how one of the Resistance men entered the outpost unarmed with only two flags in his hands, the Hizbullah MP said.

This shows a real field superiority and brings down the unreal superiority which the Israeli enemy has tried to claim, he concluded.

1996 accord protects Israeli civilians, not Army(7)
 Three years ago, Israel launched a ferocious offensive against Lebanon to curb the Hizbullah resistance guerrilla force which ended in a cease-fire with the same forces it had intended to crush. The 1996 April Understanding was drafted to protect civilians of both sides near the last active Arab-Israeli front, providing a forum to head off a repetition of Israels bloody, 17-day Operation Grapes of Wrath that killed more than 200 Lebanese, most of them civilians. Three years and 76 dead civilians later, the two sides are still locked in conflict over Israels 21-year occupation and join residents in questioning the deal. Politicians and diplomats defend the unwritten accord and say it was never meant to end fighting. But that does not stop anger at the terms, especially in the village of Qana, forever scarred by Israels blitz on a United Nations base sheltering more than 100 men, women and children. "The April Understanding is nothing but a lie, an anaesthetic, an accord which will not stop Israel from attacking our women and children," said Jamil Salameh, the partially-paralyzed unofficial curator of the mass cemetery at Qana. WAILING WALL The April Understanding, known to Israelis as the Grapes of Wrath accord, has appeared to contain violence, including last month after Hizbullah killed an Israeli brigadier-general and three others. United Nations officials say the accord has largely succeeded in what it set out to do: protect civilians. The number killed in Israeli or guerrilla attacks fell from 47 in 1997 to 28 a year ago. But the key long-term aspect may be its recognition of the Hizbullah as a regional political force. The group is Israels formidable foe and has killed most of its 87 soldiers who have died in Lebanon since 1996. "The accord is couched as if it is between the Israeli and Lebanese governments but actually it is between Israel and Hizbullah," said a veteran observer of south Lebanon affairs. "The understanding is not a condemnation agency. It is a conflict-control mechanism but not conflict-stopping. It groups all the parties involved and acts like a sort of Wailing Wall." Former Israeli prime minister Shimon Peres launched Operation Grapes of Wrath against Hizbullah a few weeks before Israels 1996 general election. On April 18, he lost any remaining support among Israels Arabs -- key to his election defeat -- when Israel rained 17 shells on the refugees at Qana U.N. base. Images of children, their flesh burned to the bone, and piles of mutilated corpses horrified the world. Israel maintained the attack was an accident but a U.N. investigation concluded that was unlikely. After that carnage, Israel faced increasing pressure to sign a cease-fire. The accord bars attacks on civilian targets or from civilian areas, but does not protect fighters. It is monitored by Israel, France, the United States, Lebanon and Syria. "In (Prime Minister Benjamin) Netanyahu's three years in power, the Israeli government could have cancelled the Grapes of Wrath agreement," Peres said in a written response to Reuters. "They did not do so because this is the only forum in which the Syrians, Israelis, Lebanese, Americans and French are meeting. And this is the only agreement that bars all sides from targeting civilians." Hizbullah has largely stuck by the accord, launching rockets into Israel only when "seriously provoked", the guerrilla group says. In December, it fired rockets into northern Israel after warplanes blasted a Lebanese farmhouse, killing a mother and her six children in what the Israeli army said was an accident. Hizbullah has said it would like to see the monitors condemn Israel more often and apply pressure to end the occupation. But officials in Netanyahu's government, fighting a general election on May 17, have said they considered abandoning the accord, particularly after a series of Hizbullah attacks on the Israeli army and its local militia. "The understandings create a situation where Hizbullah is free to launch attacks against Israeli and militia soldiers and outposts in the occupation zone and we are not free to attack their bases which are in the villages," Defense (War) Minister Moshe Arens told the Jerusalem Post newspaper last month.

"They are bad for the IDF* (Israeli Defense Force) because, in a large measure, they tie the IDF's hands. "In Qana, grandmother Nawal Bourji hobbles among the granite tombs topped by glass shrines filled with faded photographs of the dead and garlands of plastic flowers. "I carried my daughter's limbs from the cemetery. I held her children's burned bodies," Bourji sobbed. "I have to live with that image in front of my eyes every day, so don't ask me to be pleased with a piece of paper that curbs the resistance from scarring the hearts of Israeli mothers like mine was scarred."
