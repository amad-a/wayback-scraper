Daily Report
 
 
April Saturday 10-1999:

Introduction
The Israeli occupying troops have been clamping down on its allied militia after a number of defections. Also, the Israeli forces interrogated militia commanders and Israeli patrol were stepped up in the villages of Shibaa, Hasbaya and IBL El-Saqi in the eastern sector of the occupied zone.

Meanwhile, the Israeli artillery shelled the area that has been expected to form as a site for a camp for deportees from the occupation zone, security sources said.

In the occupied Al-Quds, Israels War Minister Moshe Arens called for a reduction of the Israeli forces in the zone they occupying in South Lebanon.

Enemy's suppressive measures in occupied zone (1)
The Israeli enemy continued its arbitrary suppressive measures against residents in the occupied zone as it closed the Zimrayya crossing in both directions today (yesterday).

The Israeli occupation intensively patrolled the towns of Hasbayya and Shibaa in addition to the road between Hasbayya and Marjeyoun.

The enemy also set up fixed and moving checkpoints on the road leading to the al-Arqoub area villages, subjecting residents to check up.

On the other hand, the collaborating militiamen defections outraged the enemy forces' command which gathered the militia command in the eastern sector at the town of Zoghle to discuss the issue.

Earlier, the occupation interrogated the militia's security officer in Shibaa, Mohammed Daher, and accused him of negligence holding him responsible for the defections.

Shibaa residents staged another sit-in today (yesterday). Speeches condemning the occupation and calling for the return of the deportees were delivered, despite the presence of Israeli soldiers and their agents.

CONTACTS IN BEIRUT TO REPATRIATE SHIBAA RESIDENTS TO OCCUPIED ZONE(2)
At the Coral Beach hotel, Prime Minister Selim el-Hoss said even though the government does not control areas in south Lebanon occupied by Israeli forces, it is responsible for the well-being of its inhabitants. He made his comments during a conference called for to support the southerners living under Israeli occupation. The government he said will do its best to provide the southerners with their basic needs because their steadfastness is part of the country's struggle against occupation. Hoss said the government will also continue to work to pressure Israel to implement UN resolution 425 which calls on it to unconditionally withdraw from Lebanese territories. Transportation and Public Works Minister Najib Mikati said 6 billion Lebanese pounds of his ministry's budget has been earmarked for development projects inside the occupation zone. The conference was held just days after Israel expelled 18 residents from the occupied village of Shibaa. Conferees called on the government to ensure the deportees are repatriated. Shibaa residents meanwhile have stepped up their campaign to highlight their plight and show support to those who are living under occupation.
They converged on a mosque in Ouzai in the capital's southern suburbs. They burned the Israeli flag.

ISRAEL SHELLS PLANNED SITE OF CAMP FOR LEBANESE EXPELLEES(3)
Israel bombarded Friday area in southern Lebanon following reports of plans to set up a camp there for Lebanese expelled from Israeli-occupied southern Lebanon. Israeli artillery fired rockets, tank cannon and heavy machineguns at roads leading to the Marj al-Zouhour, located in southeastern Lebanon and adjacent to the eastern sector of the occupation zone. An Israeli MK reconnaissance plane was also seen flying over the region earlier Friday. "It seems the Israelis have taken plans to establish a camp in Marj al-Zouhour very seriously and is flexing its muscles to show the Lebanese authorities that will not allow such a camp," an official told AFP. Lebanese authorities have hinted at plans to set up a camp that could accommodate up to 300 Lebanese expelled from southern Lebanon since Israel withdrew to the current line of control in 1985. Many believe the camp would be a repeat of the Marj al-Zouhour makeshift tent camp where 417 Palestinians deported by Israel in 1992 remained stranded for a year before the Jewish state agreed to take them back. Plans provided for the projected camp to be placed under the supervision of the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC). Expulsions from the border zone have recently increased, despite repeated protests by the Lebanese authorities who consider them violations of the Geneva conventions and the April 1996 truce accord between Israel and Lebanon which prohibits the harming of civilians. Since January 7, Israel has expelled about 50 residents of the town of Shibaa which had more than 40,000 residents before Israels 1978 occupation of southern Lebanon but today counts just 3,000 inhabitants. Most of those expelled by Israel were relatives of militiamen who deserted from its local allied militia and crossed into the government-held zone.

European Union special envoy Miguel Angel Moratinos, after meeting with Lebanese President Emile Lahoud in Beirut on Thursday, said he would urge the Israeli authorities to end the expulsions of Lebanese residents from occupied southern Lebanon. Lebanon has also filed a complaint over the expulsions from Shibaa with the international committee which monitors the April 1996 truce. The committee said in a statement after a day-long session on Thursday that "there were differing views on whether this incident fell within the mandates of the April understanding." "At the same time, the group noted the issue has been raised in other (diplomatic) channels and is being addressed by appropriate authorities," said the panel made up of delegates from France, Syria and the United States as well as Israel and Lebanon.

Arens calls for reduction of Israeli troop strength in south(4)
Israeli Defense (War) Minister Moshe Arens called Friday for a reduction of Israeli forces in the zone they occupy in south Lebanon.

We must reduce the number of soldiers to minimize the dangers theyre exposed to, Mr. Arens told Israeli television during a debate on the matter but stressed that his proposal was not a unilateral retreat.

He also said he was looking at handing over Israeli posts in the security zone to the Israeli-allied South Lebanon Army.

Already some 70 percent of the positions in the security zones are in the hands of the SLA. Im looking at transferring others to them, he said.

Military sources say the Jewish state still maintains about a dozen posts in the zone.
