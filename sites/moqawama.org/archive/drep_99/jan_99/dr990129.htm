January Friday 29-1999:

Aid to resistance from Alai Industrialists (1)
"The Committee for Supporting the Islamic Resistance (CSIR) yesterday received aid in kind from the Association of Industrialists and Traders in Alai (AITA)" in the presence of a CSIR delegation headed by the committee's representative in Mount Lebanon Bilal Dagher, and the head of the industrialist association Dr. Issam Sharaf-Eddeen beside members of AITA'S executive Council."
Dagher thanked "AITA and the industrialists and traders in Alia on behalf of CSIR for their initiative which expresses their embracing of the resistance."

"For his part, Sharaf-Eddeen said "AITA has not presented an aid but done a duty which is the responsibility of all, including the economic, social, and civil institutions".

He profits most who best serves (3)
 "In whose interest was it to commission a special report on the business operations connected to General Antoine Lahad, the commander of the South Lebanon Army* (SLA)? A concise intelligence report, written in Arabic and translated into French and English, is currently being circulated around Paris. Some say the information is being distributed by some of Lahad's many enemies. Others claim the French wanted to know more about the underground economy in South Lebanon on the assumption that they are soon likely to be involved in the de facto management of the border region".

"In any event, according to the report, our ally is indeed profiting handsomely from the presence of the "Israel" Defense Forces** in Lebanon. The report does not sufficiently make a clear distinction between the money collected officially by the SLA and the money pocketed by Lahad which is unconnected to the army he commands".

"The timing of the report is obviously related to recent statements former Defense Minister Yitzhak Mordechai as well as a sarcastic comment by MK Gideon Ezra earlier this month that did not get the attention it deserved. Ezra said, "We are not in South Lebanon so that Lahad's business interests can prosper" and provoked all of the old Lebanese bugaboos that have been forever present in the City of Lights. All strands of Lebanese politics are represented in the French capital, stirring up the Lebanese cauldron from a distance of 4,000 kilometers."

"The report on Lahad's business dealings reached Ha'aretz this week, having been sent to several Western embassies in Paris. It details the main sources of income of the Lahad family divided into seven categories. The language of the report is rendered faithfully below, with some editing to reduce the length".

1. "Bribes from construction dealings. This is the most profitable branch of the Lahad family business. It is controlled by General Lahad's brother Faras Lahad, who lives in Beirut. His sons Nazam and Aziz control several construction companies in the "Israeli" security zone in South Lebanon. Various Lebanese governments invested enormous efforts in recent years "to help the residents of the south," in order to blur their lack of control over the area. Tenders were published for roads, schools, hospitals and other public buildings, for tens of millions of dollars."

"Many of these tenders were awarded to straw companies... Since these companies were unable to build even a single hut in South Lebanon, they turned to Faras Lahad and signed sub-contracting agreements with him. Lahad then gave the actual work to his sons".

"The official supervision of these construction projects exists on paper only, of course. Since every link in this chain of corruption takes its cut, the remaining budget is too small to carry out the project to a reasonable standard. This is the background to the disintegration of new roads and structural cracks in new buildings in South Lebanon".

2." Stolen cars. Thousands of cars, mostly of European make, are smuggled to the Nakoura port and sold at bargain prices in the area around the port with no documents, no insurance and no taxes. Most of these cars are stolen. Since there is no police force in the security zone, hundreds of thousands of residents can move freely on the roads without fear. General Lahad's men charge the smugglers a special fee for every car that comes off the ships".
"In addition, a brisk trade in smuggled car parts is encouraged by Lahad. The parts are sold openly in Jezzine, where the surrounding roads are under SLA control".

3. "Passage fees between Lebanon and the security zone. This is the best-known of Lahad's enterprises. For every car that passes through one of the seven entry points, Lahad's men charge 10,000 Lebanese pounds (about $7)".

4. "Passage fees between the security zone and "Israel". Here, the method is different. The thousands of Lebanese who pass the border control points between "Israel" and Lebanon every day pay a set sum at the end of every month. This method allows for adjustments in the fee by estimating the income of each worker. Lahad's financial adviser, Major Marom Abu Razak, is responsible for collecting the fees".

5. "Fuel imports. This branch is under the direct control of Lahad. He imports 150,000 liters of gasoline from "Israel" every day. Estimated profit: $7,000 per day. The Lebanese joke that Lahad makes sure to bring the lowest-quality gasoline possible in order to increase his sales of smuggled spare parts".

6. "Smuggling tobacco. This is the most dangerous of Lahad's enterprises. The price is in keeping with the risk: he charges $40,000 for every ship laden with cigarettes that docks at Nakoura".

 7. "Cellular communications network. The company that controls cellular communications in Lebanon, Libancell, is owned by Ali Daloul, the son of former Lebanese Defense Minister Mukhsan Daloul. The country set up hundreds of relay antennas in Lebanon, but was forced to pay an unspecified bribe to General Lahad to enable calls from South Lebanon as well".
The report ends here.

"This is not the first time that embarrassing information about Christian generals in Lebanon has been published in Paris. When General Michel Aoun was on the brink of war with Syrian forces, in 1989, a report was published in Paris revealing that his wife had $8 million in a Paris bank account. That report was published simultaneously in a few international periodicals, including the French weekly Le Canard Enchaine. It, too, was short and concise, written in Arabic and translated into French and English. As the French say, you don't replace a winning horse".
