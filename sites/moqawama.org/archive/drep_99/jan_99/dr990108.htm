Daily Report
 
 
January Friday 8-1999:
 
Introduction
The "Israeli" occupation troops have demolished about eighteen homes in the village of Arnoun just outside the occupied area of South Lebanon. In response, Hizbullah warned that the arm of the Islamic Resistance (IR) will twist the necks of the criminal aggressors.

Also, President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Salim Hoss decried this new aggression saying it will only solidify Lebanon's attitudes and its support to the resistance activities.

Occupation forces stormed into Arnoon, bulldozed 18 houses
"An "Israeli" force last midnight stormed into a Lebanese village and destroyed 18 house.

The 30-strong unit was accompanied by three bulldozers which were used to destroy the houses in Arnoun village which lies just outside the occupied zone. The unit withdrew at 4:30 a.m".

"The destroyed houses belong to: Khalil Hamdan, Mohammed Ali Kharoubi, Kazem Hamdan, Yehia Muslim, Mahmoud Akil Ajami, Abdullah Awada, Abed-Ali Moussa, Qassem Marouni, Moustafa Awada, Fatima Awada, Ali Aleiq, Abed al-Rahman Ajami, Nemer Jaber, Hassan Aleiq, Ali Kharoubi, Fatima Ali Sh'hadeh, Mohammed Ali Hamdan, Abed al-Hussein Qatbaie, Abdallah Yehia, Afifa Marouni, Jihad Hamdan, and Naefa Bashar".

Occupation forces ten days ago demolished six houses in the town which lies 8 kilometers to the south of Nabatieh.

"Only 35 people out of the 1200 residents now live in the town which has been deserted due to the "Israeli" blockade imposed on the villages since 1985".

"Those who are still living in the town can enter or get out of it only through a 50 cms dirt road which was built next to an iron gate at the town's entrance".

"An "Israeli" occupation forces' spokesperson acknowledged today that around 30 "Israeli" bulldozers destroyed the Arnoun houses, claiming that they were used by resistance men.

Furthermore, correspondents of various local and foreign newspapers reported that "Israeli" occupation soldiers stationed in Beaufort Castle opened fire on them during their coverage of the destroyed houses in Arnoun. The press delegation comprised correspondents of the British Times, the Daily Star, and As-Safir" (1)

"Arnoun, 5 kilometers east of Nabatieh, lies in an uneasy no-mans land between state authority and the occupation zone. The zone officially starts at a battered swing gate in the center of the village. The houses demolished Thursday stretched from 100 meters past the gate to halfway up the hill to Beaufort castle".

"The owners of the destroyed houses, none of whom were in the village at the time, visit their homes during the day but most live in Nabatieh".

"Ali Tawbe, 15, who with his mother and five siblings are the only family to live on the occupied side of the gate, said he ran out onto the balcony on hearing the rumble of bulldozers".

We were scared that they might think no one lived in our house and they would tear it down, he said.

Ali is the only breadwinner for his family, earning around LL500,000 a month as a cleaner for Nabatieh municipality. But his age and importance to his family are not safeguards against possible detention in Khiam prison.

Two days ago I was stopped by an "Israeli" officer, who asked me if I had seen any resistance guerrillas. I told him I hadnt, and he said that if I didnt tell him the truth, he would take me to Khiam, recalled Ali.

"His story was interrupted as two 120mm mortar rounds fired by the resistance exploded against the ramparts of the castle above the village. The sounds of the blasts echoed and re-echoed throughout the village, joined moments later by a disembodied but clearly audible voice barking orders in Hebrew over the castles loud speaker".

"The "Israelis" in Beaufort castle prevented reporters from taking close-up pictures of the demolished houses. Two reporters who attempted to obtain a closer look were fired upon with a heavy machine gun before they had ventured a few meters past the gate".

Look at this place, said a clearly angry and frustrated Afif Hamdan, Arnouns mukhtar, indicating the deserted streets. No one lives here any more, its become impossible.

Only around 40 residents remain in the village after most of the population walked out at the end of November, when water deliveries ceased.

The "Israelis" are not allowing water to be delivered to us, said a 50-year-old man who requested anonymity. We are having to collect rain water now for drinking and washing. God knows what well do when summer comes... (2)

Hezbollah: Resistance can punish Zionist enemy (3)
"Hezbollah underlined that the Resistance will punish the enemy for the crime of destroying houses in Arnoun village "with the appropriate means and at the appropriate time."

"Head of Hezbollah's political council MP Mohammed Raad said the Resistance which is confronting the Zionist occupation has to stress its ability to punish the invaders who are harming our people and soil; it will do so with its appropriate means and at the appropriate time".

"The "Israeli" aggressive and destructive crime of demolishing 18 houses in the brave town of Arnoun unveils the terrorist intentions of the entity which has usurped al-Quds and Palestine towards the peoples of the whole region, it also discloses the unreality of coexistence or settlement with the enemy", MP Raad said.

"He stressed that the international community has to voice its condemnation of this aggression.
The Lebanese people will not forgive those who suspiciously keep silent towards such terrorism", Raad said.

"The April Understanding Group is supposed to take a firm position regarding this Zionist barbaric act", he added.

Cabinet: "Israeli" aggressions will only increase our determination (4)
"The cabinet today stated that the recent crime of demolishing houses by the "Israeli" enemy is a blatant violation of the April Understanding".

"The government held a session which was chaired by President Gen Emile Lahoud and attended by Prime Minister Salim al-Hoss and ministers at the Baabda Presidential Palace".

"Following the cabinet's meeting, Information Minister Anwar al- Khaleel, who announced the decisions adopted during the session, said the cabinet condemned the latest "Israeli" aggressive acts against Lebanese civilians, particularly the recent "Israeli" destruction of a number of houses in Arnoun.

Al-Khalil said PM al-Hoss will hold contacts to inform the international community about the "Israeli" acts".

"The minister quoted President Lahoud as saying, "The "Israeli" repeated aggressions will only increase our determination to uphold our right and clinging to our land besides adhering to unity of Lebanese-Syrian tracks".

Torture, lice and gangrene Carl Gibeily meets four ex-detainees from Khiam and Ashkelon, who recount their experiences in "Israeli"-run jails
"The first three months: The interrogation process can last anywhere from three to four months. During that time, you are held in solitary confinement in a dark cell of roughly 90 square centimeters with a single slot in the wall, allowing you to breathe putrefied air".

"The psychological and physical torture work hand in hand. Sometimes, your "Israeli" jailers will keep you thus confined for weeks at a stretch, leaving you with nothing but your blackest thoughts and a daily bowl of gruel. At other times, they will lead you to the interrogation chamber twice a day to pound information out of you".

"They want to know everything ­ and the sooner you tell them what they want to know, the easier it will be on your miserable body. They ask, grill and badger you for the names of your comrades-in-arms, for information on how you operate and from which base, on future battle plans you may be privy to. But their quizzing does not stop at purely military intelligence".

"In those three months, they will want to know everything about you and your environment for their files. They ask you about your family, about the school you attended, about your 10-year-old best friend with whom you had played marbles, about whether youre a virgin and, if not, when and with whom you lost your virginity".

"Your "Israeli" captors are guardian non-angels and no form of torture is too base for them. They deprive you of sleep, cover your head with a hood and blast you with loud music. They strike you with nail-studded sticks. And if you still prove less than forthcoming with your replies, they resort to more agonizing techniques".

"They drench your naked body in ice-cold water and, as you tremble to recover, they throw buckets of hot water at you. They repeat this process until your bones buckle under the abrupt changes of temperature".

"Your torturers are also amateur electricians. Live cables work progressively over your body in a calculated science of increasing pain. They begin at the less sensitive buttocks, moving deliberately up the back and chest, and end at the more sensitive nose and tongue, and then down again to the genitals".

"If the short, sharp treatment fails to achieve their desired results, they opt for torture over longer periods: Starving you for days on end or hanging you by your wrists so that only your toes touch the ground. Mohammed Yassins hands and wrists turned black after he was hung in this fashion for 48 hours. Concerned that gangrene had set in, Mohammeds "Israeli" captors thrashed his hands to get the blood pumping again through the constricted arteries".

"By far the worst of interrogation techniques is for them to abduct a member of your family. If you wont talk, your torturers warn you, well beat and have our way with your mother or your sister or your fiancee. Afif Hammouds mother was held in Khiam for two months. She was physically assaulted, more often than not in front of her son".

"Then they lead you back to your pigeon-hole of a cell where, in spite of the fetid darkness, you are grateful for the brief respite. And you wait".

"The next 10 years: After the violent treatment of the first three months comes the stagnation. In this vegetative stage, every day is exactly like the last which in turn is exactly like the next. You soon forget which month, season or even year it is".

"If you are fortunate, after the interrogation, you will be moved to a jail within "Israel" proper, such as Ashkelon Prison. If you are unfortunate, you will remain in the occupied south, in Khiam.

The prison cells are equally tight ­ 2.5 square meters ­ but in Khiam you have to share your cell and a bucket for defecation with five others, whereas in Ashkelon, there are fewer prisoners per cell and rudimentary plumbing".

"There are more lice in Khiam that eat the skin raw. This is largely due to the unhygienic bucket which, at a guards whim, will not be emptied for a couple of days, adding disease to the nauseous stench. You and your cell-mates are boxed in like sardines, and you take turns sleeping because there is only enough legroom for three".

"The routine is quickly established: sit, stand, squat, lie down. Mealtimes do little to offset the routine. Bread, olives, "Israeli" halawa, and soft cheeses with expired dates on the wrappers are handed out; and you eat sitting, standing, squatting, or lying down".

The only true breaks in the monotony come twice a month. Once to wash, and once to visit the Sun Room.

"You are led naked to the washroom, and are given two minutes to wash ­ two minutes to scrub and scrape a months grime with cold water. If you take longer than two minutes, the attending guards grow impatient and begin to strike you with their sticks. After your wash, you are returned to your cell, dripping wet and with soap suds clinging to your skin".

"The Sun Room is as grandly ­ if wrongly ­ named as the prison is bleak. Once a month, you are allowed to stretch your legs for 10 minutes in a courtyard that is essentially nothing more than a larger cell without a roof. You call it the Sun Room even though the walls are too high for you to see the sun; but you do catch glimpses of a blue sky, and that reminds you that the sun must still exist".

"Back in your cell, you think of ways to occupy your mind. You make worry beads out of accumulated olive pips. And, stripping the plastic off the wire that fastens the bread bags, you use your makeshift needle to mend your clothes, and you take up embroidery, sewing designs such as birds in mid-flight, flags, family trees and romantic hearts".

"In the meantime, you wait. You wait until you die or until your release. International pressure from such groups as Amnesty has done little but raise awareness of your plight. And therefore, in the unceasing night, you are left with one dream: You hope above all else that your comrades back home will capture an "Israeli" soldier to organize a prisoner exchange. And you wait".

* Since 1985, an estimated 16 people have died in Khiam alone, some as a result of injuries sustained during interrogation, some succumbing to starvation and disease in the prison, and others in the trunks of cars, through suffocation, as they were being driven to Khiam.

High Court orders state to explain refusal to exhume commandos (7)
"The High Court of Justice has ordered the state to justify its refusal to allow the corpses of two naval commandos killed in a 1997 Lebanon raid to be exhumed for genetic identification".

"Justices Tova Strasberg-Cohen and Izhak Englard and Court President Aharon Barak heard an appeal yesterday submitted by the parents of the two soldiers - Raz Tabi and Guy Golan - and then gave Defense Minister Yitzhak Mordechai and Attorney General Elyakim Rubinstein 21 days to explain their decision. The court also ordered the state to explain its failure to hold state funerals for the two soldiers".

"The justices did not accede to the parents' demand that the army's chief rabbi, former chief of personnel and chief of casualties be ordered to explain why the soldiers' remains were rushed to burial after only a cursory identification, without the parents' having been notified".

"Hezbollah returned the remains in an exchange about six months ago, some 10 months after the foiled raid. At yesterday's hearing, the parents described the emotional difficulties of visiting the graves".
