Daily Report
 
 
August Saturday 14-1999:

Introduction
The Israeli warplanes hovered over several areas of Lebanon especially Beirut and Beqaa where they staged mock air raids and released balloons to intercept the anti-aircraft fire.

Meanwhile, the population of the occupied town of Ramia staged a sit-in in front of the Unifil HQ in Tyre to protest the two-week-long siege of their town by the Israeli occupation forces.

However, the Lebanese Army intelligence has caught a spy who works for the Israeli intelligence red-handed, a Lebanese newspaper said yesterday.

Israeli aggressions on Lebanese territories(1)
"Israeli warplanes roared over Lebanese areas all day long today (yesterday).

"Following the flights over southern areas, the enemy aircraft at 11:00 a.m. circled over Beqaa areas staging mock air raids and firing balloons to intercept anti-aircraft fire.

"Observers in Beirut said the occupation aircraft's hovering outside the military operations' areas, especially Beirut and Beqaa, aims to harm the tourism movement which is currently active in the country.

"As for the artillery aggressions, the areas of Oqmata, Sojod woods, Areed al-Zennar, the vicinity of the Bir Kallab outpost were bombarded with 155mm rounds by the occupation starting at 9:30 a.m. until the afternoon.

"Moreover, the occupation artillery shelled the Mount Abu Rkab which separates between Iqleem al-Toffah and the Western Beqaa.

"Meanwhile, the occupation's espionage drones hovered over the targeted areas".

However, the Islamic Resistance (IR) issued a statement in which it recorded the following aggressions on the Lebanese liberated areas (2):

Between 9:45 and 11:00, Mount Sojod was bombarded by fourteen 120mm shells.
9:45, three 120mm shells hit Oqmata area.
10:00, ten 81mm rounds slammed into Arid El-Zennar.
10:10, Mount Abou Rkab was targeted by two 120mm shells.
11:50, Mlikh village was targeted by three 120mm shells.

Military Operations(3)
The Islamic Resistance (IR) group of martyrs Othaina Kansou and Abbas Hammoudi attacked, at 7:00, Taibeh position with the machine guns and rocket-propelled grenades scoring direct hits. The smoke columns were seen emanating out of it.

Also, the IR team of martyrs Samer Ahmad Bshara and Ali Ismail Ollaik targeted, at 11:15, a Lahdi force of two cars and several infantry militiamen at Mohaisbat position. The mujahedeen, who used the rocket-propelled grenades, were able to chalk up direct goals.

People of Ramia protest siege of their town(4)
"The people of the occupied town of Ramia this morning staged a sit-in  front of the UNIFIL HQ in Tyre to protest the two-week-long siege of their town by the Israeli occupation forces.

In a memorandum they sent to the UN Secretary General Kofi Annan, the people underlined the suffering inflicted on the residents of the occupied town, especially when it comes to medical and food supplies.

"The memorandum also noted that the Israeli occupation has annexed parts of the town to the land of occupied Palestine; some of these annexed patches are agricultural fields whose harvests have not been collected yet.

"The countries which remain silent before such crimes perpetrated against innocent people must be considered accomplices in the crimes, the memorandum said.

"Upon his tour in the Lebanese territories occupied by the Israeli forces, enemy prime minister Ehud Barak encouraged his troops to besiege villages, and assault the Lebanese who remain steadfast in their villages".

Israeli spy held by security forces(5)
"The Lebanese army intelligence has caught a spy who works for the Israeli intelligence (Mossad) red-handed, a Lebanese newspaper said today.

"Al-Kifah al-Arabi said the Lebanese security body arrested a Lebanese citizen, identified as H.A.A., as he was talking to the Israeli Mossad on a sophisticated radio.

The suspect was born in Bayada (south) but lived in the town of Zrarieh (south). He had used his affiliation to a local political movement as a cover, security sources said.

"The Lebanese army has confiscated videotapes, documents and a radio belonging to the spy, the daily said, adding that the spy was subjected to close observance which was conducted with ultimate secrecy until he was caught in the act".

Raising a family within clear sight of Beaufort(6)
Since Arnouns occupation, the residents of Yohmor face constant Israeli Army harassment
(Israeli aggressions * )
"Seven-year-old Zeinab Hamzi is proud of her scar. Raising her T-shirt she shows off the purple welt running vertically below her belly button. The wound was caused by a piece of shrapnel from an exploding mortar round.

"Zeinab had been picking vine leaves on July 2 for her mother near her home in the frontline village of Yohmor when the shell landed three meters away.

I felt a pain in my stomach and yelled for my mum to bring me some Kleenex to wipe away the blood, she said. When my mum saw what had happened she started screaming.

Zeinab is the latest victim of the violence engulfing Yohmor which has been on the increase since the village found itself almost cut off from the rest of the country following the Israeli Armys annexation of neighboring Arnoun into the occupation zone last March.

On Thursday, the five-nation Monitoring Group blamed Israel for violating the April Understanding by damaging eight houses in Yohmor with rocket-propelled grenades and the flechette tank rounds, which discharge hundreds of 2 centimeter-long steel darts.

Theyre using flechettes a lot more than before, noted Shawki Nasr, a schoolteacher at Yohmor school, whose house was one of the eight that were damaged.

Morale is very low in the village, he said. Its very tense here. You could be sitting quietly then, without warning, there is shelling.

Nasr said his wife, Sonia, was on the verge of a nervous breakdown. Her hands tremble for no reason, he said. When you talk to people, its as if theyre not paying attention. Nobody can concentrate. Its very difficult.

The harassment the villagers face comes from the Israeli troops in Beaufort castle. Only the top of a stone turret ­ now an Israeli machine-gun post ­ are visible from Nasrs house at the edge of the village. The rest of the 12th century Crusader fortress is hidden by a hill. The soldiers manning the turret frequently fire the machine gun toward the village ­ usually indiscriminately, as testified by the bullet holes in the walls of Nasrs house.

Israeli foot patrols venture out of the castle at night to monitor the surrounding area for infiltrating guerrillas and to keep an eye on movement in the village.

One night three months ago, Hassan Alleik, 18, and his brother, Hussein, 17, were leaving their aunts house in the center of the village to return home. As they stepped into a pool of light cast from a street lamp, both teenagers were shot and wounded.

I was hit by the first shot they fired. They were firing very close by. It was not a mistake, they were aiming at us, said Hassan. He was hit in both legs and his left arm. His brother was wounded in one leg.

Nasr said that most of the shelling and shooting around the village occurred at night.

They can see us during the day. So if someone is wounded when they fire at night they can claim it was a mistake as they didnt know we were there, he noted.

Sonia Nasr said that she and her husband had trained their two children Mohammed, 8, and Ali, 7, to watch television as often as possible to prevent them from roaming too far from their home.

Its a shame. We would like them to have a healthy rural upbringing, but its too dangerous. We get worried every time theyre outside. At least if they hear fighting, they run home straight away, she said.

"The impression of isolation is reinforced by the reluctance of most residents to leave Yohmor on the usual errands because of the possibility of roadblocks manned by SLA militiamen or random shooting along the single road out of the village".

"There is no running water in Yohmor. Each household has to purchase containers of water which are transported into Yohmor by tractor. Only 10 percent of the agricultural land around Yohmor is cultivated as residents are too afraid to venture far".

With the tobacco harvest underway, many residents leave their homes before dawn to begin work.

A few days ago, some farmers were shot at as they arrived in their fields and they had to run home, said Nasr.
The tobacco season is almost over. But the olive harvest begins shortly afterwards.

Our olive trees are even further from the village than the tobacco, said Nasr. What do we do then?
