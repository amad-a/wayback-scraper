August Sunday 16-1999:

Goksel : Lack of Israeli casualties is connected to the very little Israeli movement around ... (1)
Israel cannot expect security along its northern borders following the signing of peace with Lebanon and Syria if the Palestinian refugees are prevented from returning to their original homes and are forced to remain in Lebanon, according to Unifils special advisor, Timur Goksel.

Goksel said that if there is no satisfactory outcome to the fate of the estimated 350,000 Palestinian refugees in the country, then they will become a testing problem for Lebanon. Aside from the humanitarian issue, leaving those 350,000 angry Palestinians here in Lebanon isnt going to contribute to Israels security, he said in an interview with The Daily Star.

There are a lot of youngsters in the population here, frustrated and angry youngsters, and an increasing religious influence (among them). If you leave them here, unchecked, uncontrolled and unwanted by the Lebanese its not going to contribute to the security along the borders. They could be a problem that the Lebanese and others will find difficult to control. During a trip to Washington in July, Israeli Premier Ehud Barak suggested that a resolution of the Palestinian dilemma be found in their host countries. Baraks comments triggered alarm bells in Beirut where the government adamantly rejects the possibility of settling the Palestinians permanently in Lebanon.

If the Israeli governments policy toward the Palestinian refugees is vague, its plans for the occupation of the south are very clear now, said Goksel.

They want to get out of here on the basis of an agreement. Thats there number one priority, he said. But I dont think that they have completely wiped out the possibility of withdrawing unilaterally if the peace process doesnt work. Anything radically longer than the promised 12 months (for a withdrawal) will become an internal problem in Israel.

 Goksel argued that the Israelis overcame a psychological barrier by unleashing warplanes against infrastructure targets at the end of June following Hizbullahs Katyusha attack into northern Israel.

The government of Benjamin Netanyahu had found itself impotent to respond to earlier cross-border rocket attacks because of the fear of incurring further salvoes from Hizbullah. But Goksel believed that Hizbullah has not abandoned the strategy of firing Katyushas into Israel in reprisal for Lebanese civilian casualties.

They (Hizbullah) have a promise to the people and Hizbullah is determined to keep that. For them, this is very important. If civilians get killed, the Israelis take the rap. The deterrence has not gone, its still there, he said.

Despite Israels belief that Syria ordered Hizbullah to de-escalate its operations against the occupation forces after the June infrastructure raids, Goksel argued that the lack of Israeli casualties recently was connected to the troops spending most of their time in underground bunkers. The fact that nobody is getting hurt, I dont think can be attributed to anybody restraining anyone... I think that there are certain military constraints on the resistance side because there are fewer targets on the ground... finding targets has become much more difficult than before. There is very little Israeli movement around, he said.

He pointed out that there had been some serious roadside bomb attacks since the June raids, despite the general downturn in the number of operations. The Israelis are taking new precautions against roadside bombs ­ still the deadliest weapon against the occupation forces.

A very important element is the scraping of the roadsides along all the major roads of the occupied area which will make the planting of roadside bombs that much more difficult, he said.

As part of the defensive measures underway, Goksel said he expects some further relocations of troops in the coming months similar to the SLAs abandoning of the Qantara outpost last week. But the much speculated withdrawals from the northern sector to the Litani and from Hasbaya in the east appears to be on hold, he said.

I dont expect a major revamping of the situation because of the political developments, he said. I think what we are seeing is the military requirements being put into action. We might see more adjustments to the frontline, the relocation of some of the more vulnerable posts, we are already seeing the hardening of some SLA posts, and new SLA positions.

Civilians wounded in Touline
A civilian was wounded Sunday when several mortar shells fired by the SLA militia exploded inside Touline on the edge of the central sector.

 Ali Daher, 66, was seriously wounded when a 120mm mortar round hit his sons house in Touline. The round would have been fired from either the new Mheisbat position or from the militias post at Salaa. Daher was taken to Tibnine where his condition was said to be stable.

In the Bekaa, Israeli warplanes staged mock air raids over Baalbek, flying low over the town and releasing anti-missile flares.
 Israeli artillery shelled the outskirts of the village and the surrounding areas of Habboush and Kfar Rouman.
