Hizbullah Secretary-General Vows Hizbullah Will Stay Alongside The Palestinians…(1)

Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah pledged that Hizbullah will stay alongside the Palestinian people in the common destiny battle, whether the Shibaa Farms are under occupation or not. Speaking during a loyalty to the Palestinian Intifadah rally, and the first annual anniversary of the liberation of the South, Sayyed Nasrallah stressed Hizbullah will remain the sword pointed at the enemy's neck. Hizbullah Secretary-General said when the enemy uses its F-16 warplanes, this means that the intifadah has come to threaten the existence of this cancerous Entity. He added, "160 mortar shells launched by the Mujahedeen since the start of the intifadah equals hundreds of Katyushah rockets the Resistance launches from Lebanon. And its efficiency is equivalent to years of resistance in Lebanon." Sayyed Nasrallah promised the Palestinians that the “Israelis” will never see the faces of their prisoners as long as Palestinians detainees remain in “Israeli” jails. Nasrallah said that contrary to “Israeli” allegations, Hizbullah has no ambitions in Palestine. Nevertheless, he made it clear that all that Hizbullah wants is to see all Palestine recovered and the Zionist invaders to return to where they came from. Hizbullah chief added, "all our members, leaders, activists and means are at service of Hamas, the Islamic Jihad, and all Palestinian groups fighting the “Israeli” occupation." The rally was attended by various political personalities who voiced the need to support more efficiently the intifadah and criticized the lack of Arab efficiency in defending the Palestinians and support their struggle to recover their rights. Among the attendants were representatives from the Islamic Jihad, Hamas, the Islamic Jamaa, and Amal Movement.

“Israeli” troops on alert for possible attack one year after Lebanon pullout…(3)

““Israeli” troops were on alert in the north on Thursday for possible border attacks by the Hizbullah guerrilla movement on the first anniversary of the Jewish state's pullout from Lebanon.

Tensions have been running high in recent weeks after bellicose statements by the leader of Hizbullah, with “Israel” issuing stern warnings to Beirut's political master Syria that it would retaliate in case of attack.

Media reports said troops were on full alert in case of missile attacks, roadside bombs or kidnapping of soldiers.

Hizbullah, which spearheaded the armed campaign against “Israel’s” 22-year occupation, has launched numerous attacks against “Israeli” forces since “Israeli” troops pulled out on May 24, 2000.

Since October, three “Israeli” soldiers have been detained and three more killed by Hizbullah in the Shibaa Farms area.

"The sword can strike at any time and in any place and we need give no more details," Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said at a rally in Beirut on Tuesday, according to press reports here.

"We hold the Syrians solely reponsible for what is happening in Lebanon. This is why we will continue to fire on them in the event of Hizbullah attacks," Defence (War) Minister Binyamin Ben Eliezer said earlier this week.

 

“Israel” wonders aloud: did Lebanon pullout prefigure intifada?…(4)

“ One year after extracting itself from its bloody occupation of south Lebanon, “Israel” finds the example of the Lebanese Hizbullah guerrillas is transforming the Palestinian territories into a new quaqmire.

"What now bleeds like Lebanon, sounds like Lebanon and looks like Lebanon will end like Lebanon," read an editorial in the top-selling “Israeli” daily Yediot Aharonot.

Rightly or wrongly, a growing portion of the “Israeli” public and the country's leadership now perceive “Israel’s” unilateral withdrawal from south Lebanon last May 24, ending 22 years of occupation, as helping to inspire the ongoing fighting in the West Bank and Gaza Strip.

In an interview Tuesday with the Haaretz daily, “Israeli” Defence (War) Minister Binjamin Ben Eliezer hypothesized that Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat "was impressed by what happened in Lebanon and Kosovo and wants to replicate those models here."

Ben Eliezer was alluding to the success of Hizbullah's guerrilla campaign against “Israel” in occupied south Lebanon and the ability of Albanian guerrillas to attract international intervention on the behalf of Kosovar Albanians in Yugoslavia in 1999.

Evidence of the Hizbullah influence in the occupied territories can now be seen at any anti-”Israel” demonstration or funeral for an intifada victim where Hizbullah flags are often held aloft.

In face of the Palestinian uprising, many “Israelis” now question the wisdom of then prime minister Ehud Barak's decision to withdraw from south Lebanon before the end of his first year in office in July 2000, as he had promised during his election campaign.

Originally, Barak had hoped to exit Lebanon as part of a peace deal with Syria, the main power broker in Beirut. But when peace talks broke down, Barak viewed a unilateral pullout as a way to stop Damascus from pressuring “Israel” in south Lebanon.

Nonetheless, the “Israeli” military worried that a unilateral withdrawal would make “Israel” appear weak in the eyes of the Arab world and some officers advised Barak not to withdraw.

Retired general Effi Eitam, who commanded a battalion in south Lebanon, told Haaretz Wednesday about warning Barak on the imminent dangers.

"I told him (Barak), 'it's not that you're taking the IDF* (“Israeli” Army) out of Lebanon, you're bringing Lebanon into “Israel’s” territory," Eitam said.

"The immediate result is what we see today in Judea, Samaria and Gaza," he added, using the “Israeli” names for the West Bank.

In Yediot Aharonot, General Antoine Lahad, the former chief of “Israel’s” Lebanese proxy army in south Lebanon, called the retreat a huge mistake.

"I often warned: 'don't make a unilateral withdrawal, don't appear to be fleeing Lebanon without an agreement," Lahad said. "Whatever you do against Hizbullah without an agreement will cost you dearly with the Palestinians.'

He added: "That was a bad precedent. After all, both Lebanon and the territories get 'inspiration' and funding from the Iranians, and Syria's fingerprints are also evident."

For the Arab world, the lesson of the May 24 departure was the “Israeli” army, considered the region's most powerful, could be defeated by a tiny guerrilla force, although its retreat was precipitated by the collapse of Lahad's South Lebanon Army.

Raanan Gissin, spokesman for Prime Minister Ariel Sharon, told AFP he considered the retreat strategically sound, but he believed it had helped convince the Palestinians to renew their armed struggle.

Lebanon was "a model to be emulated," Gissin said.

Using Lebanon as a template, the Palestinians think "if they continue to put pressure on “Israel”, to use violence, perhaps they can drive “Israel” out of the West Bank, out of the settlements, remove the settlements," Gissin said.

 

Hizbullah Shows Katyusha Launchers Trained at Jewish Settlements…(5)

Hizbullah has for the first time given the media a glimpse of its missile arsenal, displaying loaded multi-barreled launchers right on “Israel’s” northern flank in a show of force that marked the first anniversary of south Lebanon's liberation.
"This is the sword that is hanging over the enemy's neck along the border. It is ready to strike any time," said Hizbullah leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah in a mass rally in south Beirut.
He was referring to the Katyusha launchers that his border fighters showed off to some 140 reporters and cameramen representing 65 local, Arab and international media outfits during a daylong guided tour on Tuesday.
"These Katyushas are trained at Jewish settlements. They will go when Sayyed Nasrallah sends the order," said a local Hizbullah commander who goes by the nom de guerre of Abu Mustafa.
He was standing next to a launcher loaded with 10 Katyusha missiles and mounted on an old U.S.-made Chevrolet van. "The Jewish settlements are at the mercy of these projectiles. Sharon knows as much," Abu Mustafa said.
Abu Mustafa, however, said his Katyushas had a maximum range of 23 kilometers. Other Katyusha rockets were seen mounted on separate platforms under oak and olive trees.
As Abu Mustafa was speaking, two “Israeli” jets roared past overhead at a low altitude. "Take cover and please hurry," he yelled at reporters as he dived for cover in the bush.
Abu Mustafa told reporters the sworn mission of the Hizbullah fighters was to liberate the Shibaa farms, irrespective of the U.N.-drawn Blue Line and of UN resolutions."
"There are 220 square kilometers of Shibaa farmland occupied by the enemy. They simply will have to be liberated, and liberated they definitely will be," Abu Mustafa said.
In his south Beirut speech, Nasrallah pledged continuous support to the Palestinian uprising.
"We are fighting the same battle," Nasrallah said, addressing the Palestinians. "We shall continue to support you by all means until you establish your independent state on the land of Palestine."
Nasrallah also declared that Hizbullah was ready to take Sharon on the battlefield. "If Sharon wants war, we say Ahlan Wa Sahlan. We are ready."

 

A year after Lebanon pullout, “Israelis” ask: Did it backfire?…(6)

“ A year after “Israeli” troops pulled out of Lebanon, “Israelis” still debate whether it was a good idea.

The withdrawal ended an entanglement that cost thousands of “Israeli” and Arab lives, and Katyusha rockets have not landed in northern “Israel” since last May. The hasty retreat on May 24, 2000, also hardened Palestinian positions during peace talks that broke off in January.

Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat told “Israeli” negotiators at the time that the pullout put him in a difficult position, said former “Israeli” Foreign Minister Shlomo Ben-Ami, who headed the “Israel” team.

"Arafat told me, `Hizbullah were our pupils and look, 400 of them kicked you out of Lebanon,"' Ben-Ami said in an interview Wednesday. "It had a tremendous effect."

Despite such misgivings, the Yediot Ahronot daily wrote in an editorial Wednesday that former Prime Minister Ehud Barak was correct to order the pullout.

"A year later ... it seems to have been a brave and wise decision to pull out," Yediot wrote. "The hardest question is: Why didn't they decide on a pullout sooner?"

However, others said it was too early to draw conclusions.

Gerald Steinberg, director of the BESA center for strategic studies at Bar-Ilan University, said the situation remains volatile: "There's a pretty high level of alert along the border and the pullout did not end the threat from Lebanon."

“Israel” had hoped the Lebanese army would take the 440-square-mile zone it evacuated and guard the border. “Israel” also expected Hizbullah to be disarmed and with “Israel” no longer an occupier cease trying to fight it.

Instead, the border area is controlled by Hizbullah, and its leader frequently proclaims his group's continued dedication to battling “Israel”.

Clashes continue at one point along the border a hill known as Chebaa Farms. Three “Israeli” soldiers were kidnapped there last fall, and they remain missing.

Also, Hizbullah is trying to help the Palestinians in their renewed struggle with “Israel” attempting to send in weapons, train Palestinian fighters and even claiming a few attacks itself.

What “Israel” hoped would be seen as a gesture "was interpreted by the Arabs as weakness," said military analyst Zeev Schiff. "The Arab side is convinced now that there is no need to reach accords with “Israel”, that only strength will achieve their ends. That's why the Palestinians abandoned agreements in favor of a low-intensity conflict and terror."

In the negotiations, “Israel” had offered the Palestinians a state on 97 percent of the West Bank and Gaza, land compensating for the remaining 3 percent in “Israel” proper, the dismantling of most Jewish settlements and a share of Al-Quds.

Talks broke off days before Barak lost an election. The main sticking points at the time were the fate of Palestinian refugees and control over a disputed Al-Quds shrine.

Many Palestinians say they are inspired by Hizbullah.

"Only the Hizbullah strategy is the way," said Ali Saleh, a shopowner in the West Bank town of Ramallah. "“Israel” is not interested in having an independent Palestinian state beside her which might have its army and power."

Still, the circumstances are different: “Israel” had no territorial claim in Lebanon, whereas for many Jews the West Bank is their biblical birthright. Some 200,000 Jewish settlers live in the West Bank, whereas there were none in Lebanon, and the West Bank abuts Al-Quds and is just a few miles from Tel Aviv.

Yet polls show growing numbers of “Israelis” perhaps a majority now favor a unilateral pullout from much of the West Bank and Gaza.

“Israel’s” Lebanon imbroglio began after 1970, when Jordan expelled thousands of Palestinian guerrillas to Lebanon, where they helped spark civil war in 1975. Palestinian guerrillas turned south Lebanon into a staging ground for infiltrations and rocket attacks.

This prompted two “Israeli” invasions, in 1978 and 1982, that succeeded in expelling Arafat's Palestine Liberation Organization. “Israel” continued to occupy half of Lebanon after the Palestinians left, then found itself fighting Hizbullah.

It relinquished most of the territory in 1985, keeping only the border strip. But casualties almost 1,000 “Israeli” soldiers killed from 1982 until the pullout brought public pressure to withdraw.

“Israeli” newspapers this week republished a photo that has became a symbol of the pullout, showing soldiers waving an “Israeli” flag atop an armored vehicle returning to “Israel”. Dominating the photo is Sgt. Sharon Shitubi, his features locked in a smile of satisfaction and relief.

Six months later, while guarding a Jewish settlement in Gaza, Shitubi was shot and killed by a Palestinian gunman.

 

IDF* alert on northern border…(7)

The IDF is taking seriously Hizbullah threats to escalate the fighting along the northern border, particularly in the Shaba Farms area near Mount Dov, on the anniversary today of the IDF's withdrawal from south Lebanon.
Troops are reported to have been put on full alert in case of missile attacks, roadside bombs, or even kidnap attempts by Hizbullah gunmen.
The alert follows increased rhetoric by Hizbullah leaders and particularly the organization's leader Sheikh Hassan Nasrallah that the organization is the "extended sword on the neck of the enemy along the fabricated Lebanese border."
"The sword can strike at any time and in any place, and we need give no more details," said Nasrallah, at a Beirut rally Tuesday night in support of the Palestinian intifada and to mark the first anniversary of the "liberation" of south Lebanon.
Earlier Tuesday, Hizbullah took dozens of foreign journalists to its "front-line" positions in the Shaba Farms district to show off its arsenal of Katyusha rockets, Sagger anti-tank missiles, and other weapons aimed at IDF troops and positions in the Mount Dov region.
Reports from Lebanon said IAF helicopters and fighter planes staged low-flight sorties over south Lebanon, including Shaba Farms, apparently in a show of strength by “Israel” on the eve of the anniversary.
According to local reports, the Hizbullah gunmen wore brand-new American-style camouflage uniforms. Some wore ,Australian-style bush hats, and others helmets.
Senior Hizbullah commander Haj Mustafa told reporters at the scene that Hizbullah was limiting its operations to the Shaba Farms district, and would continue its struggle until all Lebanese land and prisoners held by “Israel” were liberated.
Hizbullah has declared the Shaba Farms district an ongoing battle zone, claiming that the land belongs to Lebanon even though it was captured by “Israel” from Syria in the 1967 Six Day War.
The area was placed on the “Israeli” side of the UN-delineated withdrawal line - known as the Blue Line - on the basis of available documentation. To date, neither Lebanon or Syria have provided the UN with documentation to substantiate their claims that the district was once part of Lebanese territory.
Prof. Gabriel Ben-Dor, head of Haifa University's National Security Studies Center, said Hizbullah's show of strength was a "classic deterrence gesture" aimed at trying to dissuade “Israel” from retaliating if and when the organization strikes.
Nevertheless, Ben-Dor stressed that Hizbullah had its own agenda, and was amply motivated to attack “Israel”.
"Hizbullah is serious and certainly means business. It probably feels that this is an opportune moment for initiating further action along the northern border, and particularly in the Mount Dov region," said Ben-Dor.
"[Hizbullah] thinks that “Israel’s” strength and deterrent capability have weakened, and I do not rule out the possibility that it might take the opportunity at this time to once again try and test “Israel’s” resolve.
"If anything, it would most probably [execute] a carefully calculated and limited operation, designed to show that Hizbullah can still fight and win and thereby justify the continued existence of its military arm and the organization itself."
Ben-Dor said that although Syrian President Bashar Assad was enamored of Hizbullah, it was unlikely that he was deliberately encouraging it to step up operations to the point where it would provoke a harsh “Israeli” response against Syrian interests in Lebanon.
"If anything were to materialize, Syria would no doubt claim that this was its promised response to “Israel’s” attack on its radar outpost... I don't think Syria, or Hizbullah for that matter, want an all-out confrontation in which Hizbullah might be wiped out and Syria badly mauled," he said.
Meanwhile, UN Undersecretary-General for Peacekeeping Operations Jean-Marie Guehenno visited south Lebanon yesterday and toured the Blue Line on foot and in a UN helicopter.
Guehenno, who was accompanied by UNIFIL's senior political adviser and veteran spokesman Timur Gosel and senior officers, flew over the Shaba Farms district, and also visited the former Fatma crossing point north of Metulla, in what was described as an orientation visit. He is due to visit Damascus today and will come to “Israel” at the beginning of next week

Pomp greets opening of May 25 Hall…(9)
A host of officials gathered Wednesday to inaugurate the Information Ministry’s “May 25 Hall” as part of its drive to shore up state-run media and celebrate the first anniversary of “Israel’s” withdrawal.
Information Minister Ghazi Aridi said he was “happy to open the Liberation Hall at the National News Agency, about which much has been said and at which much has taken place,” referring to the government’s policy of reforming state media.
The government of Rafik Hariri has trimmed the ranks of surplus NNA staff in a process overseen by Aridi.
“But the NNA plays a vital role in providing the Lebanese press with most of its news and photographs, and many people don’t know this,” Aridi said.
He also paid tribute to a series of news supplements published by the NNA which chronicle last year’s “Israeli” withdrawal. Aridi said that the victory by the resistance over “Israeli” occupation forces “is a victory for all Lebanese, and not a single political group.”
The minister pledged to ensure that the NNA became a forum for “the views of all politicians and the airing of everyone’s concerns.”
Liberation Hall is located on the ministry building’s third floor, and will serve as a multi-purpose meeting place for staff.
 

40 Palestinians Injured in More “Israeli” Aggressions, Despite…(10)
Twenty Palestinians, including a baby, were injured when “Israeli” tanks pounded a refugee camp in the Gaza Strip as confrontations flared in the Palestinian territories despite a declaration by “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon of a unilateral ceasefire overnight. The Palestinians, who have branded the ceasefire called by “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon a “trick,” said “Israeli” army operations continued against them in the Gaza Strip. The occupation army mounted five armed incursions into areas under the full Palestinian Authority control, despite “Israel” saying it would not carry out such operations under the terms of its ceasefire. In one raid, Palestinian security and medical sources said 20 civilians, including five children, were wounded by shrapnel and bullets when the “Israeli” occupation army fired shells on the Yebna refugee camp, near Rafah on the border with Egypt. At least three houses were damaged in the raid, which saw the tanks move some 50 metres (yards) into Palestinian territory. Among the injured were an 18-month old baby girl. Security sources also reported another armed raid into a village east of Khan Yunis in the southern Gaza Strip, with tanks moving in some 250 metres (yards) into autonomous land under a hail of gunfire. Earlier, the head of general security in the Gaza Strip, Abdel Raseq al-Majeida, reported three separate tank raids: on the eastern outskirts of Gaza City, near the Mintar crossing, and the towns of Qarara and Absan al-Kabeera, near Khan Yunis. A senior “Israeli” occupation official claimed that under the terms of a “unilateral ceasefire” in force in the Palestinian territories since late Tuesday “the army will not initiate operations and will not mount incursions into Palestinian autonomous zones.” The latest raids bring to at least 22 the number of “Israeli” incursions into self-rule areas of the Gaza Strip in around 11 days, in contravention of the 1993 autonomy accords, according to the Palestinians. In other aggressions Wednesday, Majeida said “Israeli” tanks fired two shells on a Palestinian security post near on the Mediterranean coast south of Gaza City. He said the shells were fired from an occupation army post near the Jewish settlement of Netzarim, severely damaging the police building but causing no injuries. “This shelling was unprovoked and it proves that “Israel’s” call for a ceasefire is a lie,” he said.

 

“Israeli” killed in shooting near Ariel…(12)
 Asher Iluz of Modi'in was shot dead by Palestinians near Ariel and another man was lightly wounded yesterday evening.
An 86-year-old Gilo resident was shot in the chest and seriously wounded when Palestinians began firing at the Al-Quds neighborhood from nearby Beit Jala. For the first time, Palestinians also shot at the Al-Quds neighborhood of Malha, a kilometer west of Gilo.
Iluz, 33, died on the operating table at Beilinson Hospital in Petah Tikva from wounds sustained in the fighters shooting. Iluz and Yitzhak Goldstein, 29 , of Ariel, were returning from near Rehalim and were travelling on a dirt road near the village of Isaskar in Area B, when at least three fighters ambushed their vehicle and sprayed it with bullets.
Despite his wound, Iluz continued driving, heading for Ariel, as Goldstein returned fire and alerted security forces. The terrorists continued shooting at the car and shot also at soldiers who came to the assistance of the two.
The IDF imposed a curfew on Isaskar for several hours and searched the village. Trackers, however, discovered footprints leading to Salfit, which is under total Palestinian control.
A police spokesman said the ambush had been carefully prepared and that at least two different types of weapons had been used.

 

Iran Says Muslim Nations Must Bankroll Palestinian Uprising…(13)
Iran’s Foreign Minister Kamal Kharazi called on Islamic countries Wednesday to bankroll the Palestinian uprising against the Zionist Entity, state radio reported. “We must support the intifada, and the most important concrete decision will be to help finance the Palestinians so that they can continue with their uprising,” Kharazi said, cited by the report. “There is no hope for peace as long as the racist and aggressive regime exists in the heart of the Middle East,” he said, calling once again on Muslim nations to break all political and economic ties with the Jewish state. “There is no longer any acceptable excuse for maintaining ties with a regime which does not stop committing crimes,” he said. Kharazi also expressed Tehran’s disappointment over the US veto of a UN security council resolution on sending an international force to the occupied territories. He said Saturday’s ministerial meeting of the Organisation of the Islamic Conference (OIC) in Doha would focus on adopting a stiffer stance towards the Zionist Entity. Kharazi said the Palestinians have “no other choice but to defend themselves” against the “violence perpetuated by “Israel”.” Gulf diplomats said Tuesday they expected many Gulf Arab foreign ministers not to attend the OIC meeting because Qatar has allowed the Zionist Entity to maintain its trade office in Doha. “Certain Gulf countries are dismayed at Qatar’s refusal to close the “Israeli” trade office in Doha ... and have decided to lower the level of their representation” at the meeting, one diplomat told AFP. He said the foreign ministers of Kuwait, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates would not travel to Doha.
