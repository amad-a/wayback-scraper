Berri warns “Israel” not to hit Syrians here…(1)

Speaker Nabih Berri said Friday that any military action against Syrian troops here would give the resistance “the right to attack all “Israeli” settlements.”
“The South and the resistance will consider any retaliation against Syrian troops in Lebanon or in Syria as aggression against the South. This gives the resistance the right to attack all “Israeli” settlements,” said Berri, during a ceremony held at UNESCO Palace on Friday to mark the first anniversary of liberation.
Among those present were President Emile Lahoud, Premier Rafik Hariri, Hizbullah leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, Deputy Premier Issam Fares and former President Amin Gemayel.
Berri said the time has passed when “Israel” could impose its agenda on the rest of the region, and that the country was entitled to continue the resistance until “Israel” withdraws from the Shibaa Farms.
“These are the rules of the new game,” he said. “The time when “Israel” could dictate the rules of the game in the region is over. Lebanon has the right to continue the resistance as long as the Shibaa Farms are occupied and so long as international resolutions do not include them in UN Resolution 425.”
Berri also called for speeding up the de-mining process, as well as greater efforts to rebuild infrastructure and boost development in the liberated areas.
For his part, Hariri said “Israeli” threats against Syrian troops here are threats against the entire country, and that they will not stop the government from “fully coordinating” with Syria.
He also said the country will continue efforts to find “a comprehensive peace.”
He also paused to tell the audience that he did not favor using the term “just” when discussing a possible settlement, since the majority of Mandate Palestine would not be returned to Palestinians under the current peace formulas.
Nasrallah said “Israel” was still threatening the entire region with war and that Hizbullah had received offers aimed at getting it to stop its resistance.
“Hizbullah has received very attractive propositions through various channels,” he said. “Why all this intimidation and enticement? Do they want to serve the interest of Lebanon or “Israel”?”
Nasrallah also said that the only way to face “Israel” or the current economic crisis is for the people to close ranks.
He urged the government to assume its responsibility toward the liberated areas, the families of those who were killed during resistance operations, and the former detainees who made victory possible.
Also on Friday, approximately 10,000 people turned out in Sidon for afternoon prayers to mark the anniversary. Some 18 mosques closed their doors to worshippers to encourage them to gather publicly at Martyrs Square to mark the anniversary of liberation.
 

Lebanon "complex" haunts Sharon in Palestinian territories: analyst…(2)

“ “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon will prove to be a tenacious fighter in the Palestinian territories because he is haunted by the ghost of the war in Lebanon, Palestinian analyst Mohammed Hamza warns.

Sharon's forces will also prove tougher to beat in Gaza and the West Bank than in south Lebanon because the Jewish state is more committed for religious, strategic, and economic reasons, Hamza and other analysts added.

Their notes of caution were directed at Palestinian fighters and others who are inspired by “Israel’s” pullout from south Lebanon on May 24 last year, following a long guerrilla war led by the Hizbullah.

"Sharon has a complex, which is named the Lebanese war complex," according to Hamza, who heads the MAQDS center for strategic studies in Gaza City.

"He is starting at the point where the ... Lebanon war stopped," said Hamza who was an advisor to Abu Jihad, the chief Palestinian military strategist assassinated by the “Israelis” in 1988.

It was Sharon who masterminded the invasion of Lebanon in 1982 in a bid to crush the Palestine Liberation Organization only to have a full victory slip away from him and see the war turn into a political defeat for the right wing.

"He thought the “Israeli” left prevented him from completing his war aims," Hamza said, citing articles written by Sharon.

He finally turned the tables on the left by becoming prime minister in a landslide victory in February over incumbent Labor premier Ehud Barak, who had pulled out of Lebanon in a final repudiation of Sharon's war.

In winning the election, Sharon also took many votes from the left who were disillusioned with Barak's peace policies.

On the flip side of the coin, “Israelis” now are preoccupied with the Lebanon example as one that carries great risks for them.

In the face of the Palestinian uprising, many “Israelis” question the wisdom of Barak's decision to withdraw from south Lebanon before the end of his first year in office in July 2000, as he had promised during his election campaign.

Hamza's main concern is that Sharon -- if the Palestinians fail to create international or domestic “Israeli” pressure to stop him -- might actually succeed in imposing his solution on the Palestinians.

It is a solution where they only keep land they have already -- no sovereign state in Gaza and the West Bank with east Al-Quds as capital.

Nor do the Palestinians have the backing that Hizbullah had in Syria and Iran, Hamza added.

Brigadier General Saeb al-Ajezz, director of national security in the northern Gaza Strip, agreed that Sharon and his right wing would fight hard for the West Bank as part of biblical “Israel”.

And Sharon will also cling to the Jordan Valley for strategic reasons -- such as the early warning stations that can be deployed there -- while many settlers will be motivated by the financial incentives of cheaper housing.

But he believed that the Palestinians would eventually triumph because Sharon has failed to deliver the security he promised “Israelis” when he came to office and will further diminish security by pursuing his military escalation.

Hamza also saw some optimism in Sharon's failure to improve security.

Mohammed Essam, the alias of a Palestinian fighter for the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine, admitted that the obstacles were higher than those in south Lebanon, an example that inspired him nonetheless.

His source of confidence in an eventual Palestinian victory was one of deep "conviction" that his cause was just, he said.

 

“Israeli” planes fly over Lebanon for 10th straight day…(3)

“ The “Israeli” air force crossed into Lebanon for the 10th straight day Sunday and broke the sound barrier over Beirut, Lebanese police said.

The planes made several passages at low altitudes over towns in south Lebanon, stirring panic among residents. Many motorists, travelling to south Lebanon for the weekend, stopped briefly out of fear.

After ripping a sonic boom over Beirut, the “Israeli” jets flew over the Bekaa valley, a stronghold of the thousands of Syrian troops deployed in Lebanon.

“Israeli” jets have been flying over southern Lebanese villages and cities regularly since October.

Hizbullah killed an “Israeli” soldier last month in an ambush, prompting “Israel” to bomb a Syrian position in Lebanon, also killing a soldier. “Israel” has threatened to take further action if attacks continue.

On Thursday, UN special envoy to south Lebanon Staffan de Mistura chastised the “Israeli” air force for repeated violations of Lebanese airspace since “Israel” ended its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon last May.

"We should ... not underestimate the almost daily violations by the “Israeli” air force, across the blue line (dividing “Israel” and Lebanon) and the unjustified sonic booms deep inside Lebanon, which in turn contribute to increase tension," he said.

"All sides need to exercise maximum caution and restraint during the forthcoming critical days," Mistura added.

 

Coptic pope urges Arab unity to face “Israeli” threat…(4)

The head of the Egyptian Coptic Church, Pope Shenouda III, urged all Arab countries on Sunday to unite in order to combat “Israel’s” policy of aggression.
Shenouda, who spoke at a news conference at the Bristol Hotel, is here at the invitation of the Armenian Orthodox Church, which this weekend commemorates 1,700 years since Christianity was taken as that state’s official religion.
Shenouda said Arab unity is vital for facing “Israel”, as the Jewish state “depends on any Arab division, or any kind of weakness, more than its own weapons to fight us.”
““Israel’s” aggressive policy has no limits,” Shenouda said. “Their greed and expectations are very high.”
Asked if he had discussed any of the country’s domestic issues with religious officials here, Shenouda refused to comment, saying: “No foreign personality outside your country should judge your internal relationships.”
However, he said countries “like Lebanon and Syria” should solve their differences, as problems between them can only benefit “Israel”. He also said he was aware of the special nature of the relationship between Beirut and Damascus. “All I want to say is that I hope your problems are solved,” he said, “especially since there is a common enemy.”
Shenouda then thanked God for last May’s “Israeli” withdrawal, adding that he hoped “it will be permanent and complete.”
He also said he hoped “Israel” would withdraw from the Golan Heights, Palestine and other occupied Arab countries.
Asked about his own evaluation of the present status of churches in the country, he said its importance continues to be the same as in the past.
 

Southern de-mining proceeds apace…(5)

The army said Sunday that it had dismantled and detonated more than 250,000 mines, shells and bombs in the South with the Syrian Army’s help since liberation last year.
The statement said a 114,842 square-meter area was cleared of mines throughout eastern Sidon, Nabatieh, Bint Jbeil, Tyre, Marjayoun, Hasbaya and Jezzine. According to the statement, about 4,900 mines were dismantled and 244,000 bombs and shells detonated.
Main roads in Eastern Sidon, Jezzine, Nabatieh and the Western Bekaa were also cleared of mines, the statement said, adding that two officers and eight soldiers were injured in the process. The statement said that two Syrian soldiers were also injured during the operations.
It said the de-mining process was continuing in the liberated areas. The National De-mining Office has organized awareness campaigns in the South and the Western Bekaa on the danger of mines.

 

Palestinians Stage 2 Lebanese-Style Car Bombings in Al-Quds…(6)

Palestinian militants copied Lebanese civil war methods in staging two car bombings that ripped through a major “Israeli” neighborhood of Al-Quds nine hours apart on Sunday, injuring 31 “Israelis”, “Israeli” police said.
Both car bombs were made up of mortar shells, nails and bullets like the explosive-laden vehicles that were used by Palestinian, Muslim and Christian militias in Lebanon's 1975-1990 civil war, the police said.
The first blast at around midnight was claimed by George Habash's Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine.

The second explosion was claimed by the Palestinian militant Faction Islamic Jihad whose hard-core activists were trained by Hizbullah. The Party of God spearheaded the guerrilla war that forced “Israel” to terminate 22 years of occupation in south Lebanon a year ago.
The PFLP responsibility claim was faxed to news agency offices in the West Bank and the Islamic Jihad's to agency bureaus in Damascus, the Syrian capital. Police said the two car bombs were equipped with timing devices.
Both blasts came hard on the heels of two back-to-back suicide martyrdom car bombings by Hamas and Islamic Jihad near Gaza and in the central “Israeli” town of Hadera to mark the first anniversary of south Lebanon's liberation.
The latest explosion went off at 9 a.m. (0600 GMT) Sunday, near the Russian Compound area, where the Al-Quds police headquarters and magistrates court are located.
It cast a damper on the debut of newly appointed U.S. Middle East envoy, William Burns, who met with Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat at his headquarters in Ramallah and was due to meet “Israeli” Prime minister Ariel Sharon in Al-Quds later Sunday.
Police said 31 people were lightly injured, most of them suffering from shock in the second explosion. The street, normally crowded at that hour, was relatively empty because Sunday is the eve of the Jewish holiday of Shavuot, and many people were on a weekend vacation.
Police raced to the scene and closed off the area as fire fighters put out the blazing car. Al-Quds police commander Mickey Levy said there were other explosives in the area. He and Al-Quds mayor Ehud Olmert went on “Israel” radio to appeal to residents to stay away.
One “Israeli” shop owner on the scene, Eliaho Lieberman, said "I know we are in big trouble," he said. "They (Palestinians) will not beat us and we will not beat them. Each side will suffer and count its dead."
The first car bomb exploded just after midnight, about 100 meters (yards) away from the scene of the second.
It went off near the entrance to the police headquarters and a block away from a popular entertainment district, where hundreds of young people were partying in discos and bars. No one was seriously injured.
Police spokesman Shmuel Ben-Ruby quoted witnesses as saying two men parked the car in a parking lot a few minutes before the explosion. The two suspects then left the scene in a second car.
The parking lot is next to a police station and the magistrates' court where nine people were appearing before a judge in connection with a wedding hall collapse that killed 24 people.
The blast was heard inside the courtroom and disrupted the proceedings, sending security guards scrambling outside. The judge ordered the nine held for several more days.

 

Quds Mufti Says Sharon Will Not Force Palestinians To Their Knees…(7)

The highest Palestinian religious authority, Sheikh Akram Sabri, has accused the “Israeli” government of wanting to use war to turn Palestinians into “compliant slaves no longer willing even to think about an own state and about freedom.” Sheikh Sabri, said an end to the Palestinians Intifada would be possible only after “Israel’s” oppression apparatus" had been stopped and when “Israeli” settlers changed their “wild west” mentality. The Mufti saw no end to the confrontation, in view of the increasingly difficult living conditions for Palestinians. He added that enemy Premier Ariel Sharon will not force them to their knees.
