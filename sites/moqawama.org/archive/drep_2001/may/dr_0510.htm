President and speaker oppose reduction of UNIFIL strength…(1)

President Emile Lahoud and Speaker Nabih Berri agreed Wednesday on the need for UNIFIL to retain the necessary number of soldiers to enable it to fulfill its mandate.
A statement from Baabda Palace said that a report by UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan recommending a phased reduction of UNIFIL’s troop levels was discussed during Berri’s visit to Baabda.
According to the release, Beirut’s position was conveyed to Lebanon’s ambassador to the United Nations, Salim Tadmori, with instructions to follow up the matter with the international body’s secretariat.
The Security Council is due to discuss Annan’s report on May 15 amid Lebanon’s strong opposition to the proposed reduction of the UN force.
Lahoud and Berri also discussed infrastructure and development projects in the South.
Separately, Berri said the UNIFIL troop reduction was a “setback” not only for the state’s policies, but for peace in the Middle East.
He accused the United States of being behind the decision, adding that the move puts the country in a dangerous situation.
“Such a reduction would increase the economic pressure on our people in the South because the livelihood of hundreds of families depends on the UN’s presence,” Berri asserted.
South MPs, groups and local figures also asserted their opposition to the UNIFIL reduction.
Marjayoun-Hasbaya MP Ali Hassan Khalil, Tyre MP Ali Khreis, and Bint Jbeil MP Ali Bazzi issued a statement Wednesday calling for the withdrawal of Annan’s recommendation, saying that the reduction of UNIFIL troops “served “Israel’s” interest.”
Marjayoun-Hasbaya MP Qassem Hashem said Annan’s report exposes the UN’s “double standards.”
“Such a move exerts pressure on Lebanon … the UN shouldn’t reduce its numbers because it must continue to document “Israel’s” violations and aggressions.”
 

Fadlallah: US is ‘subservient’ to “Israel”…(2)

A leading cleric accused the United States administration Wednesday of “subservience” to the Jewish lobby and of trying to “please” it by fabricating excuses for “Israel’s” crimes against the Palestinian people.
In reply to a question on “total solidarity” with “Israel” expressed by President George W. Bush, Sayyed Mohammed Hussein Fadlallah said such a stand “demonstrates his ignorance of the region’s history, specifically that of Palestine. It also points to the extent of his administration’s subservience to the Jewish lobby … and fabrication of excuses for the enemy for all crimes it commits against the Palestinians.”
He added that “Israel”, which Bush claimed had always lived in danger, “is not part of the region and cannot become a natural body in it as it is a fabricated entity established on the ruins of a whole people.”

 

“Israeli” artillery fires on southern Lebanon, no casualties: police…(3)

“ “Israeli” artillery opened fire on a hill outside the southeastern Lebanese village of Shibaa Wednesday without causing casualties, Lebanese police said.

Twelve rounds hit the Seddana hill outside Shibaa at 2:30 pm (1130 GMT), the police said.

At the same time, an AFP correspondent witnessed “Israeli” helicopters overflying the nearby Shibaa Farms area, captured by “Israeli” in 1967 and claimed by Lebanon.

He reported hearing explosions and automatic arms fire in the area.

There were no reports from the Lebanese side of any military operations against the “Israelis”.

Since “Israel” withdrew its troops from southern Lebanon a year ago, guerillas of the militant Islamic group, Hizbullah, have carried out a number of attacks on “Israeli” army units in the Shibaa Farms. They have killed three soldiers and captured three more.

 

US ‘frowns’ on compensation case over “Israeli” occupation…(4)

The US opposes the government’s filing a compensation case with the International Court of Justice against “Israel” for 25 years worth of damage caused by military attacks on civilians and vital installations.
According to well-informed sources, Washington raised the issue through its diplomats, not only with Lebanese leaders but also through other unofficial channels preparing the case.
The diplomats indicated filing such a case would at present not help bring about “pacification”, but spread “intransigence.”
In the sources’ view, this explains Lebanon’s delay in submitting its case, although the related files are ready.
One source expressed surprise at the “hesitation” in defending Lebanon’s rights despite the considerable human and material losses caused by “Israeli” attacks.
He also wondered why UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan has not responded to Beirut’s complaint regarding “Israel’s” alleged use of depleted uranium in its attacks on sites in the South and other regions.
Lebanon had asked the UN to send experts to the South to investigate, but Annan postponed a UN response to the request pending results from commissioned reports on the health hazards posed by depleted uranium.
The source recalled that Speaker Nabih Berri had hinted he would not hesitate to form a special parliamentary committee to look into the matter if current investigations failed to achieve “tangible results.”

 

Italy finances demining conference…(5)

Italian Ambassador Guiseppe Cassini said Wednesday that 300 people would arrive from abroad for a conference this month on de-mining the former occupied areas.
Cassini made the comments after meeting President Emile Lahoud at Baabda Palace, where they discussed the May 21 conference. Cassini told reporters after the meeting that approximately 300 people were scheduled to attend the conference, to be financed by Italy.
Asked about his country’s stand on reducing the size of UNIFIL’s size, Cassini said Italy, which provides UNIFIL’s air wing, had been requested to withdraw two helicopters. He said his country would carry out any request of the UN whether it involved decreasing or increasing the number of UN troops.

 

Labor of love produces history of lost village
Reem Haddad interviews the man who preserved the memory of a Palestinian village erased in 1948…(6)

At first he didn’t think much about it. The passing away of an elderly person was sad, but a fact of life.
Slowly, however, Ibrahim Othman realized that every death meant the loss of history and the only remaining evidence that once there were residents of a Palestinian village called Deir al-Qassi in northern Palestine.
Suddenly, Othman, a school teacher in Tyre who fled the village with his family in 1948, felt compelled to track down the elderly and get their stories.
Soon it became an obsession. Morning and night, it was all he could think about: “Then I felt I had a mission,” said Othman. “And that is to collect every detail I could and write a book about Deir al-Qassi.”
Starting in 1997, after work and sometimes until morning, Othman, now 59, traveled throughout Lebanon going from village to village asking if Palestinians above the age of 70 resided in them ­ specifically Palestinians from Deir al-Qassi or thereabouts.
Altogether ­ from his travels from the North, to the Bekaa, and to the South, he was able to locate 51 elderly villagers ­ the eldest being 102 years old. But it was a race against time.
“Some passed away a few days after the interview and others died before I had a chance to reach them,” he said.
Othman spent hours with each of the residents he was able to find, bringing out every memory.
“I wanted to know everything,” he said. “From customs, to who owned what shops, to the games children played.”
Armed with hundreds of loose papers, he diligently wrote down every word. He found himself overwhelmed with information.
“I checked each fact against another and made sure everything was correct,” he said.
Based on the elder villagers’ memories, he was able to draw as “accurate a map of the village as possible,” he said.
Othman was also able to depend on his own memories.
He clearly remembers the fateful day in 1948 when, as a 6-year-old, he was playing in a street with some friends.
In only three days, he would be going to school for the first time, and he was very excited about it.
He also recalls that his parents were especially pleased that season, as the harvesting ­ which was to begin in the next few days ­ was exceptionally good.
“It was a Thursday afternoon when the planes came whooshing above us and began to bomb the village,” he said.
“I started running in panic along with everyone else. On the way, some children were blown to pieces.”
Joining his family, he hid in some fields. But the attacks did not cease and villagers fled to nearby Lebanon. A few days later, some of the villagers sneaked back into Palestine and made their way to the village.
“They wanted to get some belongings out of their homes,” he said.
Some never came back. They were shot and killed.
One of  them was the mother of a man Othman located and interviewed in Tyre. The last thing Mohammed Maarouf, then 25-years-old, heard was his mother calling out to him as “Israeli” soldiers riddled her body with bullets.
Maarouf’s story is one of those included in the book, which is named after the village.
Othman spared no details. From statistics to the name of the coffee seller down the road “they’re all in the book,” he said.
Included with full explanations are the types of food eaten, clothes worn, water sources, land owners, mosques, cemeteries, agriculture products, flora and fauna in surrounding valleys and type of trees, animals that lived in nearby woods, various roads leading into the village, number of livestock in the village, names of the shepherds, ancient ruins found in the village and the way houses were constructed and furnished.
Customs and traditions were discussed in full, among them births, marriages, beliefs, superstitions, astrology, Arabic medicine, religious commemorations, welcomes for strangers, proverbs, schools and students, women’s traditions, folklore dances, poets, songs, relationships with the Bedouins, and intermarriages between those from the village and those from surrounding ones. Names of all villagers, shop owners, and regular passers-by were included.
“It’s a living book,” said Othman. “Everything has been reconstructed exactly as it was.”
Finally, last year, the book went to print. Othman put much of his savings in producing over 1,000 copies and distributed them free of charge to offsprings of Deir al-Qassi.
Fifty copies were left aside for “my children, their children and grandchildren,” he said.
His one regret was that he didn’t interview his parents before they passed away over 10 years ago. His perseverance in his book wouldn’t have surprised them: “I’ve always been like that,” said Othman. “When I put my mind to something, I don’t let go.”
After fleeing from their village, the family settled in a refugee camp in southern Lebanon. At the age of 13, Othman decided he wanted more.
He left the camp and made his way to Tyre, where he continued his studies. In 1971, he was admitted to the Arab University as a history major.
At the same time he enrolled in a vocational school to obtain a teaching diploma and simultaneously took French lessons. After graduation and since then, Othman has been teaching in schools for the United Nations Relief and Works Agency.
But memories of his village stayed alive.
“I had this need to make sure that my children and other young people knew where they came from,” he said as he pointed to a painting hung up on a wall depicting a few houses scattered around some hills set in a picturesque village.
He painted it himself. It was how he remembered his village 54 years ago.
“This is my legacy to them.”

 

Mofaz admits excessive force lead to Palestinian baby's death…(7)

Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz yesterday told a group of officers in Gaza that responding to mortar fire with tank shells had been excessive last week, when a Khan Yunis baby was killed.
On a routine swing through command posts hosted by Southern Command Maj. Gen. Doron Almog and Brig. Gen. Yair Naveh, Mofaz met units posted along the southern border with the Palestinian Authority. He spoke with the tank regiment that fired shells at PA targets in response to a mortar attack on “Israeli” settlements.
Mofaz addressed the rules of engagement under which the tank regiment operated. It became apparent the “Israeli” Army’s standing order is for any mortar attack on “Israeli” positions to be answered with tank shelling of pre-selected Palestinian positions - pre-selected because identified as the origin of the Palestinian fire.
Mofaz heard that 25 hollow payload shells were fired - the baby was killed by shrapnel from one of them. He did not criticize the regiment directly but made it clear that in the context of what happened, the response was "exaggerated.

 

“Israeli” troops enter Palestinian camp, face resistance…(8)

Six Palestinians were wounded, one critically, and an “Israeli” soldier was lightly hurt in the fighting near Rafah in the southern Gaza Strip early on Thursday, hospital officials and security sources said.

Palestinian security officials said “Israeli” troops on foot also briefly entered a Palestinian-controlled area near the West Bank town of Ramallah where they exchanged fire with Palestinian gunmen before retreating.

Erekat told reporters “Israel’s” use of force "demonstrates clearly that this government has a programme not to revive peace but to destroy it".

At least 409 Palestinians, 79 “Israelis” and 13 “Israeli” Arabs have been killed in more than seven months of fighting.

“Israeli” Finance Minister Silvan Shalom defended on Wednesday a plan to allocate additional sums to settlements that has angered Palestinians and drawn rebuke from the United States.

The Palestinians say “Israel’s” policy of building settlements in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, lands they want for a future state, has fuelled its uprising which erupted in late September in the vacuum of deadlocked peace negotiations.

Shalom told “Israeli” television during an official U.S. visit that the plan was not final and had been misunderstood.

"When it is released it will show that it comes to answer real needs that came about in the last six months," he said, referring to expenses for security, health and education.

Some 200,000 Jewish settlers live in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, home to three million Palestinians. Settlements built on occupied land are illegal under international law. The status of Jewish settlements is to be determined in a final peace treaty.
