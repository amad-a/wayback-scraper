Some 2.5 million “Israelis” in range of our missiles: Hizbullah…(1)

“ An official in the Lebanese armed movement Hizbullah has warned that 2.5 million “Israelis” are in range of the group's Katyusha rockets.

Nawaf Moussawi, a member of Hizbullah's political leadership, told a rally Sunday in the southern seaport of Tyre, that "2.5 million “Israelis” are now in range of our missiles".

Hizbullah's press office confirmed the quote reported in the Lebanese press Monday but said they were "not a threat but a statement of fact."

Moussawi added that Hizbullah "considers itself a partner to the Palestinians and not simply a supporter in the intifada, " the eight-month Palestinian battle against “Israeli” occupation of the West Bank and Gaza Strip.

During “Israel’s” occupation of south Lebanon, which ended last May, Hizbullah frequently pounded northern “Israel” with Katyusha rockets.

Since October, Hizbullah has launched attacks on the Shibaa Farm territory along the “Israeli”-Lebanese border, kidnapping three “Israeli” soldiers and killing three more.

“Israel” has threatened to strike Syrian military positions in Lebanon, if Hizbullah, backed by Damascus, Tehran and Beirut, carries out any further attacks on the Shibaa Farms.

“Israel” already bombed a Syrian radar station on April 16 in revenge for Hizbullah's killing of a soldier.

On Friday, Nabih Berri, Lebanon's parliament speaker and chief of the political party Amal, said the whole of northern “Israel” could come under fire in case of further “Israeli” attacks on Syrian positions in Lebanon.

 

Sharon Seeks A Way Out of Occupied Lebanese Shibaa Farms Through International Coverage…(2)

“Israeli” sources uncovered that enemy Prime Minister Ariel Sharon approved a plan to withdraw from the occupied Shibaa Farms. According to the “Israeli” press, Sharon is willing to evacuate the Farms, but in a different manner than last year’s defeatful withdrawal from southern Lebanon. He will rather turn towards international institutions to find a way out so that the “Israeli” forces will not be accused once again of fleeing Hizbullah. The sources said Sharon has the intention of instituting legal proceedings to the International Court of Justice in the Hague, concerning the status of the Shibaa Farms, and this as a prelude to withdrawing from the Farms in a political, and judicial manner.

 

Leabnese president blasts “Israel’s” "extremist policies"…(3)

“ Lebanese President Emile Lahoud slammed Monday what he called "the extremist and aggressive policies of the “Israeli” government" at a Paris dinner held in his honour in French President Jacques Chirac's residence.

“Israel” "wants to get out of its international obligations," he charged, urging a rethink of the strategies and methods currently being used to try and impose peace on the troubled Middle East.

"We think that a clear and courageous position from the international community reminding “Israel” of its obligations and the requirements of peace ... would likely force “Israel” to revise its choices and to maybe resume negotiations."

France in particular should use its clout in the European Union to get the 15-nation bloc to be more active in resolving the violence.

Lahoud's first official visit to Paris comes amid efforts by France to play a more central role in resolving the Middle East crisis.

French and Lebanese officials said mounting tensions between “Israel” and Lebanon over the Shibaa Farms border area and the overall situation in the Middle East would be high on the agenda during Lahoud's two-day visit.

The Lebanese government insists that “Israel’s” pull-out from south Lebanon a year ago after some 20 years of occupation will not be complete as long as a portion of the border region known as the Shibaa Farms remains under “Israeli” control.

 

PM: “Israel’s” threats for ‘local consumption’…(4)

Prime Minister Rafik Hariri said in an interview published on Monday that “Israeli” threats against Syria were for “local consumption” and he did not foresee war breaking out in the region.
Speaking to the Kuwaiti newspaper Al-Anbaa, Hariri said “Israeli” Premier Ariel Sharon was in fact “launching war” on the Palestinians “and accordingly we have to be cautious.”
He added that Lebanon always respected UN resolutions while “Israel” turned its back on them. “Even if we have reservations on UN resolutions, this doesn’t mean we don’t respect them,” he said.
Hariri went on to say that Lebanon sought to “strengthen its credibility” before the international community.
Replying to questions on a letter he received from US Secretary of State Colin Powell, Hariri said it dealt with the Mitchell Commission’s report, adding ““Israel” doesn’t want peace and is launching war on the Palestinians ­ a losing war.”
For his part, Speaker Nabih Berri told a delegation representing the Arab media that had there been “true solidarity” among Arabs “none of what’s happening at present in occupied Palestine” would be taking place. He also said had it not been for Syria, the resistance movement would not have been able to exist and endure. Berri reaffirmed that the Shibaa Farms were Lebanese territory and the resistance would continue “until every inch occupied by “Israel” is liberated.”

 

Syrian Daily Says Lebanese Resistance's Experience An Inspiration for Golan Height Inhabitants…(5)

Syrian daily Tishreen, said in its editorial that the occupied Golan Height might witness an armed resistance against the Zionist occupation. The daily added “ For sure the national Lebanese resistance's experience is a great inspiration for Golan inhabitants, and the Palestinians.” It said the “Israeli” defeat in south Lebanon can be repeated elsewhere.

 

Land Mines Leftover by “Israeli” Occupation Claim At Least One Lebanese Victim Per Week Says UN…(6)

The Lebanese army has dismantled some 6,000 land mines and explosives in south Lebanon since the Zionist Entity's pullout from the area in May 2000. During the mine-clearing operations ten Lebanese soldiers, including two officers, and two Syrian soldiers have been injured from blasts. UN Secretary General Kofi Annan’s Special Representative in South Lebanon, Steffan De Mistura said that the mines claim at least one victim per week in the south, which counts some 70,000 inhabitants, and scare people from returning to their homes abandoned during the “Israeli” occupation. It is worth noting that, since the withdrawal of the Zionist occupation, 13 Lebanese civilians have been killed and more than 70 others injured or maimed by mines and other explosive devices in the area.

 

The Lebanon pullout…(7)

” The unilateral pullout from Lebanon, a year ago last Thursday, was in the nature of a grand experiment: What would happen if “Israel” simply acceded fully to Arab and international demands, without asking anything in return? Would “Israel”, as the Left hoped, thereby eliminate the cause of the conflict with Lebanon, paving the way, if not for peace, then at least for non-belligerency? Or would the pullback show, as the Right claimed, that the territorial dispute was merely a pretext for the conflict, and that territorial concessions could not solve it?

One year is too short a time in which to assess the significance of any historical event. Nevertheless, the trends that have emerged in the first year of this experiment are highly disturbing.

By far the most serious consequence of the Lebanon pullout was the blow to “Israel’s” deterrence. The withdrawal convinced much of the Arab world, and particularly the Palestinians, that violence does pay: Despite the IDF's* vast technological and numerical superiority, Hizbullah was able to cause a majority of “Israelis” to want to leave Lebanon merely by inflicting a relatively small number of casualties over a period of several years. The immediate result of this lesson was the bloody war of attrition launched by the Palestinians last September - which most Palestinian militia leaders say openly was inspired by Hizbullah's success. In eight months, this war has already caused roughly the same number of “Israeli” casualties as about four years in Lebanon. Worse, most of the casualties have been civilians rather than soldiers, and normal life has been disrupted all over the country rather than only along the northern border.

But even if we look only at the situation in the North, the results are far from encouraging. To start with, the theory that the pullout would put a stop to Hizbullah attacks has proven nonsense. True, the organization refrained from such attacks for all of four months. But since October, it has kidnapped four “Israelis” and killed three others - a damage level not far below the pre-withdrawal average for that period of time. Furthermore, it has redeployed its forces within meters of “Israel’s” northern border - meaning that should it choose at any point to resume the Katyusha attacks it has suspended for the past year, it is in a position to do far greater damage to “Israel’s” civilian population than it ever was before.

The idea that the pullback would end “Israel’s” dispute with the Lebanese government has also proven false.

Despite official UN certification that it has withdrawn from the last centimeter of Lebanese territory, Beirut still insists that “Israel” has not completed the pullback. It justifies this insistence through the ingenious method of suddenly asserting that a whole new region, to which it had never before laid claim, is in fact "occupied Lebanese territory." Thus far from ending the conflict, the withdrawal has actually spurred Lebanon to make new territorial demands.

The Lebanese government has also made a mockery of the idea of non-belligerency. The one request “Israel” made in exchange for the pullout, which the entire international community backed as reasonable, was that Lebanon then deploy its army in the south to control Hizbullah. But Beirut has refused. Instead, it has given Hizbullah free rein to continue “terrorist” actions along the border.

Finally, there was the hope that “Israel” would at least gain international support through the withdrawal. This, too, has proven to be largely a delusion. For instance, even though the international community agreed that the Lebanese army should be required to deploy in the south in exchange for the pullback, it has done nothing to back up this demand. Lebanon has suffered no diplomatic repercussions at all on account of its refusal.

Worse, however, is the speed with which Lebanon's new territorial claims have been given international legitimacy, primarily by the media. For the first few weeks after the pullback, international media reports did acknowledge that “Israel” had withdrawn to the last inch, as certified by the UN. Now, however, virtually every report about IDF-Hizbullah skirmishing in the area of Har Dov (Shaba Farms) simply refers to the area as "disputed territory." Not territory to which Lebanon has no conceivable claim, according to the UN and all known maps of the international border. Just "disputed territory." And disputed territory, of course, is legitimate grounds for military action - the very justification that “Israel’s” withdrawal was supposed to have removed.

An assessment of the Lebanon pullout is far from an academic exercise, because the “Israeli” Left is now advocating the same panacea of unilateral withdrawal in the West Bank and Gaza. The majority of “Israelis” should therefore study the lessons of this failed experiment carefully. Otherwise, we are doomed to repeat it.
 

Ex-detainees describe hard time in “Israeli” prisons…(8)

Two ex-detainees shared their experiences at student-sponsored presentations at the Lebanese American University on Monday, comparing their time at “Israel’s” Ashkelon Prison and the Khiam detention center.
Both Nabih Awada and Afif Hammoud were detained in 1988 and released in 1998, but their 10 years of detention differed considerably.
Awada, 16 at the time, was arrested after an operation against “Israeli” forces near Marjayoun, and was subjected to many interrogations before his transfer to Ashkelon.
Hammoud, who was then 22, was arrested in Khiam after being detained and interrogated over 21 times.
“After enduring various methods of torture during interrogation sessions, I was taken to Metulla settlement (in “Israel”) for medical examination,” Awada told the audience.
He was then transferred to Beir Sabaa Prison in “Israel”, where he said torture methods were more psychological than physical.
“The “Israelis” used to deny us sleep by banging on the prison doors several times throughout the night.”
He said interrogation sessions used to concentrate on questions that he could not answer.
“They asked me to explain why Hamra Street is famous, and wanted me to interpret Lebanese proverbs,” he said.
“Sometimes, I tricked them and told false stories, pretending I couldn’t tolerate further torture,” he added.
Awada was sentenced to 15 years in prison and, upon reaching 18 years old, was transferred to Ashkelon, where he spent eight years with sentenced Palestinians and Lebanese detainees. While there, the administration did not interrogate the detainees.
“Prison life there was different,” he said.
There was a committee for the detainees headed by one detainee who represented the group before the prison’s administration.
“Although our demands took about a year to be approved,” he said, “we secured a few of them, like watching television and reading newspapers.”
Awada learned to speak and read Hebrew from his fellow Palestinian prisoners.
Hammoud recounted the experience of his years in Khiam.
“More dangerous than the interrogator was the prison agent,” he said. Prison agents are detainees used to draw out information from detainees.
They take information from interrogators, pretending to know the detainee’s village and people to win their confidence in an effort to obtain details of any activities against the occupation.
“Their duty is to get information from the detainee and to accuse him of doing something,” Hammoud said.
He described the torture methods he was subjected to, such as electric shocks and being doused in cold and hot water.
“The “Israelis”, and particularly “Israeli”-allied SLA members, used to tie us to a pole and beat us,” he said, mentioning that one of his colleagues died on the pole.
He said that during prolonged interrogations lasting several hours, SLA members would threaten to arrest the detainees’ mothers, sisters and loved ones.
“They arrested my mother for three months and would make me hear her voice from time to time (from an adjacent room) to psychologically torture me,” Hammoud said.
He recalled the only way SLA members granted detainees’ parents access to see their detained children was through bribes.
“Our parents used to have to bring … valuable gifts in exchange for seeing their son or daughter for a few minutes,” he said.
Hammoud recounted one incident in 1987 in which SLA members took a newborn baby into their custody.
“Mohammed Ahmad, 7 days old, was arrested with his mother when SLA members couldn’t find his father,” he said.
His father, Omar Ahmad, turned himself over to the SLA, but his wife and son remained imprisoned for another five months as a tool to force him to confess.

 

9 Palestinians, including 2 Children, Injured in Continuous “Israeli” Aggressions, More Palestinian…(9)

A seven-year-old Palestinian boy was shot and wounded in the leg when “Israeli” occupation forces opened fire early Monday on Palestinian homes in the southern Gaza Strip. Mohammed Jabr Abu Samhadana was hit by a live round from an M-16 assault rifle. The forces on the Rafah border post with Egypt also opened heavy machine gun fire on the nearby Yebna refugee camp, damaging several homes and wounding 4 Palestinians, including a boy. In Nablus two Palestinian shepherds were wounded after “Israeli” settlers opened fire at them. Another two Palestinians were injured when an “Israeli” settler opened fire at students at the Tokouh village near Beitlaham (Bethlehem). Palestinian security forces said a 60 -year-old Palestinian man suspected of collaborating with the occupation forces was killed in Tolkarm. Earlier on Sunday, a car bomb exploded in the heart of west Quds (Al-Quds). The explosion left more than 20 injured. The Islamic Jihad movement claimed responsibility. A leader of the Islamic Jihad movement vowed at a rally of 1,000 university students in Gaza City that his group would pursue martyr attacks deep inside the occupied territories, which will topple enemy Premier Ariel Sharon. and called on the Palestinians to continue the resistance until the occupation ends. “Israeli” enemy radio said that a bomb exploded near an “Israeli” military patrol close to the Baida village in Jenin. Overnight Palestinians targeted an “Israeli” post near Rafah with machine guns and bombs. They shelled mortar bombs at Neve Dekalim settlement in the Gaza Strip vowing to continue fighting. The Zionist entity launched another incursion Monday as “Israeli” tanks and bulldozers penetrated autonomous Palestinian territory to a depth of 200 metres in the Najjar village close to Rafah. They also opened fire at Palestinian homes. The “Israeli” military's bulldozers flattened the land and destroyed trees. Tanks and bulldozers penetrated several meters in Jougaia east of Gaza City.

 

“Israeli” Killed in Drive-By Shooting…(10)

An “Israeli” motorist was killed in a drive-by shooting Tuesday on a West Bank road near the Palestinian city of Nablus, the army said.

According to the army, the gunmen fled into Palestinian-controlled territory after shooting on the car.

The driver died of massive head wounds from the shooting, the army said without identifying him.

Jewish settlers living in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip have come under repeated attack by Palestinian militants.

Twenty-one of the 86 “Israelis” killed since the Palestinian uprising began eight months ago have been settlers who died in roadside ambushes or drive-by shootings. The fighting has claimed 478 lives on the Palestinian side.

The shooting came as new U.S. mediation efforts appeared to be yielding some results Monday with an agreement between “Israel” and the Palestinians to resume talks on security cooperation.

 

Two shot in West Bank ambush…(11)

“ Two “Israelis” were wounded, one seriously, when shots were fired at their minibus last night as it traveled between Migdalim and the Tapuah junction in Samaria. Following the shooting, the car overturned, coming to a stop on the side of the road.
A 40-year-old woman was airlifted to Sheba Hospital at Tel Hashomer, suffering from gunshot wounds to her abdomen and hip, and was in moderate to serious condition.
Yehuda Goshen, 50, of Gatit in the Jordan Valley, was taken to Beilinson Hospital and was in moderate condition, after being shot in the back.
Security forces have yet to determine whether the shooting came from a passing car or was a roadside ambush. Judea and Samaria police spokesman Rafi Yafe said up to 10 bullets apparently fired from Kalashnikov rifles penetrated the car.
Palestinian officials yesterday accused “Israel” of using the cease-fire for propaganda, and charged that since last week over 90 violations of the cease-fire had been committed by “Israel”.
"“Israeli” allegations of ceasing fire are only for propaganda. “Israel” did not commit itself to the cease-fire, and the occupation army intensified its aggression and shooting against innocent civilians," said Abdel Razek Majaydeh, head of the Gaza Public Security Service.
 

Estimates: Intifada has cost economy two billion dollars…(12)

According to various economic estimates, the eight-month-old Intifada uprising in the West Bank and Gaza Strip has cost the “Israeli” economy some two billion dollars, which amounts to two percent in annual growth, “Israel” Radio reported Tuesday.

The two sectors of the economy that have been hardest hit are construction and tourism. The violence has also badly affected investments and exports.
