“Israel” presses UN and ICRC for release of soldiers captured by Hizbullah…(1)

““Israeli” Foreign Minister Shimon Peres has accused Damascus of preventing Beirut from deploying the Lebanese Army along the border with the Jewish state. Peres claimed after talks with UN Secretary General Kofi Annan in New York there are three armies in Lebanon, the Syrian, the Lebanese and Hizbullah. Annan had also met the fathers of three “Israeli” soldiers captured by the Hizbullah resistance movement from the occupied Shebaa Farms last October. They urged the UN Chief to secure some news on the three soldiers and a fourth Mossad colonel Hizbullah had later captured. Hizbullah has not allowed the International Red Cross or UN staff to see the soldiers. It has also not provided any information on the four. “Israel” slammed the International Committee of the Red Cross for not establishing contact with the “Israelis” and called on the UN to intervene. Hizbullah wants to exchange them for at least nineteen Lebanese detainees “Israel” has been holding without trial or charge. The group also wants “Israel” to release other Arab and Iranian detainees and provide landmine maps for explosives the Jewish state left behind in the former occupied zone in southern Lebanon.

Sharon under pressure after settler is shot dead…(2)

“Israel’s” Prime Minister, Ariel Sharon, was under domestic pressure yesterday to adopt still harsher measures to crush the Palestinian uprising after a settler was shot dead on the West Bank ­ three months after his father died the same way.

Assaf Hershkovitz, 31, was shot dead in a drive-by ambush claimed by Hamas guerrillas, who said they were avenging explosions that killed five Palestinians, including two children ­ separate attacks which Palestinian officials believe were by “Israeli” death squads.

As they prepared to bury Mr Hershkovitz yesterday in a grave by his father, West Bank settlers called for tougher measures from Mr Sharon, including further construction of illegal new settlements in the occupied territories.

The settler's death was part of a wave of Middle East violence coinciding with a visit to Washington by “Israel’s” Foreign Minister, Shimon Peres, who will discuss Egyptian-Jordanian truce proposals, which already appear doomed to fail. Victims included Mohammed Musa Abu Jabber, a Palestinian policeman who was hit yesterday by an “Israeli” tank shell in the south of the Gaza Strip, as well as Malak Barakat, aged four, and her brother Shahid, who were killed in an explosion on Monday night which flattened a two-storey flat in Ramallah, near Mr Arafat's West Bank headquarters.

Suspicions grew when it was found that the blast also killed a leader of the Fatah movement, Hassan al-Qady. “Israel” denied it, though it has admitted previous assassinations.

Only a few hours earlier in Gaza, a large explosion destroyed a house, killing Hamdi Salim al-Madhoun, 18, and Muhammad Abdel Karim Abu Khaled, 17. Palestinian witnesses said they saw “Israeli” helicopters overhead; the blast severely damaged a human rights centre next door. And in Gaza City yesterday, hundreds of Hamas supporters mourned two members killed on Monday when a bomb exploded in a van.

 

Eight die as Peres heads for Washington…(3)

A renewed attempt to secure a truce in the Middle East appeared doomed last night amid fresh outbreaks of violence which left eight Palestinians dead.

As Shimon Peres, “Israel’s” Foreign Minister, set off for Washington to discuss the Egyptian-Jordanian peace proposals, he left behind a region engulfed in scepticism.

A series of explosions dampened hopes of an end to the latest round of violence, which saw four killed in a West Bank bomb blast, two in an explosion in Gaza, one in a bomb blast inside a settlement, and another in a clash with “Israeli” forces.

The bomb blast flattened a two-story building in the West Bank town of Ramallah, killing at least four Palestinians and injuring five others. Rescue workers said four people were trapped under the rubble.

One of the dead was Hassan el-Qady, whom “Israel” suspected of shooting dead a 16-year-old “Israeli” boy who had been lured to the area. Palestinians said an eight-year-old boy and his six-year-old sister were killed, with another man. The mother of the two children and her five year-old daughter were injured but were in stable condition, a hospital official said.

The apartment building is not far from Yasser Arafat's headquarters. Mr Arafat was in his building at the time of the explosion. Sources at the scene said that two men wanted by “Israel” were making an explosive device in the building when it blew up, but the governor of Ramallah, Mustafa Eissa, said a bomb was placed under the stairs, and he believed the “Israelis” were responsible.

The Arab world will be watching the talks in Washington to see if the Bush administration applies any meaningful pressure on “Israel” to change its stance on one of the biggest obstacles ­ settlement-building. Americans and Europeans have condemned “Israel” for continuing to put up homes in the occupied territories.

But Ariel Sharon, the “Israeli” Prime Minister, has refused to accept a demand in the truce proposals for a freeze on settlements, whose growth through the Oslo negotiations severely undermined Palestinian faith in peace-making.

Palestinian officials insisted an agreement on a ban was crucial. If Mr Arafat is to sell a truce to his embittered population he must be able to show the seven months of bloodshed that has left more than 400 Palestinians dead have not been without results. Mr Sharon, a fervent supporter of the settlements through his political career, has insisted “Israel” should be allowed to continue the construction to allow for "natural growth" in existing settlements.

Palestinians and “Israel’s” "Peace Now" group say that almost 4,000 settler homes are vacant in the West Bank, enough to accommodate demand for two years, without building a single new home.

So far, Mr Sharon has not budged. "I don't see a problem with the issue of settlements," he told The Al-Quds Post, in an interview published at the weekend.

"In Oslo it says, I think, that until there is a peace agreement, we won't talk about settlements. If we make peace, what's the problem with there being settlements? Who does it bother?" he said.

Mr Peres is expected to tell the Americans that “Israel” will not negotiate until there is an end to Palestinian violence.

In earlier incidents yesterday, a member of the militant group Hamas was one of three Palestinians killed in the Gaza Strip. The “Israeli” army said it believed the first explosion, a car bomb, had been planted by Palestinians targeting a Jewish man and his daughter, but instead killed a Palestinian working in a nearby greenhouse, and wounded another. A Palestinian wanted by “Israel” was shot dead by “Israeli” troops near Qalqilya, in the West Bank.

 

Muslims ask Jewish group to delay release of book on Islam…(4)


 A national Islamic advocacy group today asked the American Jewish Committee (AJC) to delay the release of a new book on Islam for Jews until Muslim concerns about the book's content and cover image can be addressed.
SEE: http://www.ajc.org/pre/muslimjew.htm
In April, the Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR) questioned AJC's choice of a controversial author for the book, called "Children of Abraham: An Introduction to Islam for Jews," scheduled for release this week. CAIR says the author of that book, Khalid Duran, has a mysterious identity, associates with infamous "Muslim bashers" and was once convicted of defaming an Islamic center in Germany.
CAIR now says it also objects to an image of the Prophet Abraham ("Ibrahim" to Muslims) on the book's cover. (For Muslims, created representations of prophets are inappropriate in that they distract from God's message and could lead to a kind of idol worship, something forbidden in Islam. Just recently, a similar image of the Prophet Muhammad in Time magazine resulted in an apology to Muslims. [Reuters, 4/21/2001])
Duran's book is one of two being published by the AJC. Rabbi Reuven Firestone wrote the companion publication, "Children of Abraham: An Introduction to Judaism for Muslims."
In a letter to AJC Executive Director David Harris, CAIR Executive Director Nihad Awad wrote: "Given the AJC's stated intent of promoting 'understanding and mutual respect,' it is puzzling as to why your organization would first of all engage an author who has little credibility in the Muslim community and then add insult to injury by using a cover image that many Muslims will find offensive. This book has the potential to do more harm than good in terms of interfaith relations."
Awad added that the table of contents for Duran's book reads like a "laundry list of stereotypes and hot-button issues." He cited the book's apparent description of the anti-Muslim political ideology of Kemal Ataturk as a "successful synthesis" and its focus on "Islamism," "Jihadism," "gender segregation" and "female circumcision," issues often used to stereotype Muslims and Islam. Based on its table of contents, Rabbi Firestone's companion book on Judaism for Muslims focuses on ordinary religious issues and lacks the Duran book's critical tone.
CAIR has offered to have recognized scholars on Islam review Duran's book for stereotypical or inaccurate content. Despite repeated requests, the AJC denied CAIR a preview copy of the book.
As evidence of Duran's lack of credibility, CAIR noted that he is a close colleague of infamous Muslim bashers such as Steven Emerson, an author who Muslims say has a long history of attacks on Islam and on the American Muslim community. Duran was a consultant for Emerson's now-discredited 1994 PBS program "Jihad in America."
Reports also indicate that Duran will co-author a book called "Muslim America" with Daniel Pipes, another writer with an even longer history of Islamophobia than Emerson. Pipes, Emerson and Duran were among the first commentators to accuse Muslims of bombing the Murrah Federal Building in Oklahoma City in 1995.
SEE: http://www.fair.org/extra/9901/emerson.html and http://www.cair-net.org/misc/people/daniel_pipes.html
In 1997, a Canadian Broadcasting Corporation (CBC) report responding to complaints about accusations made by Duran said, "A...factor which does not contribute much to Mr. Duran's credibility is the mystery surrounding his true identity. His real name is not Duran. 'That's my mother's name,' he told me. His father, whose name he wishes to withhold, was Moroccan, but Mr. Duran was born in Berlin, which is the reason for his German passport...When asked about his real name, he remains vague."
"A third factor that undermines his credibility...is that he was sentenced in 1993 for making defamatory comments about the Islamic Center of Aachen, (Aix- la-Chapelle) in Germany. His sentence was to retract his statements or pay a heavy fine or, failing that, to go to prison..." Duran challenged the report's findings, but has not explained the mystery surrounding his name or his conviction for defamation in Germany.
There are an estimated seven million Muslims in America and some 1.2 billion worldwide.
