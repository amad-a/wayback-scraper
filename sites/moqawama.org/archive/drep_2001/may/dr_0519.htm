Sharaa ‘extremely satisfied’ with Beirut talks

“Israel” may strike again, Syria will not “accept humiliation: Sharaa…(1)

Amid mounting regional tensions, Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk Sharaa paid a day-long trip to Beirut on Friday to coordinate the two countries’ stands in the face of “Israeli” threats.
Sharaa summed up the results of his short visit by expressing “extreme satisfaction” with talks held with the country’s top leaders with regard to the Middle East conflict.
Speaking to reporters after a meeting which grouped President Emile Lahoud, Speaker Nabih Berri, Premier Rafik Hariri and Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud, Sharaa said: “I feel extreme satisfaction with the outcome of the gathering. I will convey what I heard to President Bashar Assad and the Syrian leadership, and believe we’re following the right track in our relationship which “Israel” cannot harm, no matter how great the challenges and threats.”
On his arrival, Sharaa said that he was carrying a message from Assad to Lahoud dealing with the various regional and international developments.
Asked whether Assad had cut short his visit to Egypt because of “Israeli” threats to strike Syria, Sharaa said the threats were nothing new as since its creation, “Israel” had been threatening its neighbors.
“As for the visit to Egypt, it was very successful and achieved its aim … and as usual, we and our brothers in Egypt exchange views and hold consultations on latest developments.”
He also said the issue of the Shibaa Farms, the UN-demarcated Blue Line and “Israeli” threats to Lebanon were covered in his Beirut talks.
Sharaa told reporters at Baabda that his visit was within the framework of the “brotherly cooperation” between the two countries.
He added that Assad’s message dealt with “all issues of interest to both countries and governments that may come to your mind, topped by the serious developments in occupied Palestinian territory and “Israel’s” aggressive military operations against unarmed people.
“This issue was discussed in detail and we also looked into the various possibilities, something which was also raised with our brothers in Egypt.”
Other topics included the “Israeli” attack on a Syrian position in Lebanon, “the dangerous consequences of such an aggression and the possibilities of its recurrence,” Sharaa said.
The Syrian minister said there was “no enthusiasm for peace” within “Israel” and “this serious development reflects negatively on the peace process.”
“Each day that passes is not in “Israel’s” interest and Westerners are discovering that the government of (Ariel) Sharon is dangerous, not only to peace in the Middle East, but also to security and stability in an important and sensitive part of the world.”
Turning to Syrian-Lebanese relations, Sharaa said that discussions with Lahoud established that “closer rapprochement” between the two countries and peoples in various domains would “consolidate national cohesion within both states.”
“One should not belittle at all the effect of this special Lebanese-Syrian relationship on the region’s security and stability. It is such a relationship that will bring about a just and comprehensive peace one day,” he said.
Later in the day, after a meeting at the Foreign Ministry with his counterpart Hammoud, Sharaa declined to say whether a blistering attack on anti-Syrian politicians by Syrian Defense Minister Mustafa Tlass represented the official stand of Damascus.
He said he could “not understand” the UN decision to reduce the number of peacekeepers in the South, nor the US Congress’ vote to block financial aid to Lebanon.
“Not all US decisions are made to uphold the American interests, especially where the Arabs are concerned. These decisions are often motivated by pro-”Israeli” sentiment,” he said.
Asked whether there would be a joint plan to retaliate against any possible “Israeli” attack on Syrian military positions, Sharaa said his country was observing a “deep sense of responsibility, but at the same time will not condone humiliation. If “Israel”, as some newspapers reported, tries to change the rules of the game, we will change the rules of the game, and time is on our side.”
In reply to further questions, he said an extraordinary Arab follow-up committee meeting in Cairo Saturday would discuss the situation in occupied territories, especially “Israel’s” “aggressive and provocative operations against the Palestinian people.”
The committee, set up by an Arab summit held in Cairo, will also discuss “Israeli” threats against Syria and Lebanon, and Beirut’s opposition to a reduction in UNIFIL’s strength, he added.
However, the main purpose is to back the intifada, Sharaa said.
Asked whether a recent explosion in the occupied Golan Heights was a prelude to launching resistance operations in the region, Sharaa said: “I can’t say it’s the beginning or the end, but the rules of the game are not for “Israel” to impose.”
Referring to the gathering, a presidential statement said Lahoud had affirmed that a united Lebanese-Syrian position was capable of confronting “Israeli” threats against both countries.
The statement also said the joint evaluation of events in the region had shown that Syrian-Lebanese “expectations,” regarding “Israel’s” “clear decision to undermine the peace process in the region, are correct.”
The statement said the two sides expressed satisfaction with the unanimous Arab support for the Lebanese-Syrian stand in facing potential threats, and affirmed the importance of consolidating bilateral cooperation in various fields.
The president handed Sharaa a message in reply to Assad’s in light of discussions held.
Lahoud earlier received the president of the Journalists Union, Melhem Karam, who later told reporters that the head of state had expressed surprise at a US Congress decision to block some $35 million in aid to Lebanon unless the government deployed troops along the “Israeli” border.
“Israel” was meanwhile demanding $450 million in so-called compensation for withdrawing from Lebanon, Lahoud remarked.
Diplomatic sources said that following intensive contacts over the last 48 hours, Lebanon and Syria succeeded in persuading other members of the Arab follow-up committee of the “gravity” of “Israeli” threats against the two countries.
The two states called for amending the Cairo meeting’s agenda by noting that the “Israeli” threats were as dangerous as the situation in the region in general, the sources added.
The sources said Beirut and Damascus drew the attention of other committee members to threats made twice within 24 hours by “Israeli” Defense (War) Minister Binyamin Bin-Eliezer that his country would strike at Syria in retaliation against any resistance operation by Hizbullah.

 

Hoss says Lahoud accepted the Blue Line temporarily…(2)

Former Premier Salim Hoss affirmed Friday that President Emile Lahoud had indirectly endorsed the UN-drawn Blue Line in a memorandum delivered to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan in June last year.
But Hoss stressed that approving the Blue Line did not contradict Lebanon’s claims to the Shibaa Farms area.
Hoss was responding to the assertion of US Ambassador David Satterfield that Lebanon had “formally agreed” to the Blue Line.
“It did so a year ago,” Satterfield said Thursday. “This issue should not be reopened.”
The ambassador’s comments sparked an uproar, with officials at the foreign ministry scrambling through archives to check whether any such approval had been relayed to the UN.
In a statement released by Hoss, the former premier said that a memorandum delivered by Lahoud to Annan on June 9 inferred that Lebanon would recognize the Blue Line despite its misgivings.
The memorandum in question was in fact a scathing attack against the UN’s handling of the “Israeli” withdrawal verification process and contained strong criticism of the Blue Line itself.
But Hoss quoted a key paragraph in the memorandum which he argued could be taken as Lebanon’s endorsement of the Blue Line.
The memo recalled that the Blue Line in the Shibaa Farms district ran between the areas of deployment of UNIFIL in south Lebanon and the UN Disengagement Observer Force in the Golan Heights. The memo noted that the Blue Line was not a substitute for a legal international boundary.
“Lebanon agreed to this evaluation while awaiting a common formula for the Shibaa Farms area, signed between Lebanon and Syria to submit to the UN,” Lahoud’s memo said.
Hoss said that this represented a recognition of the Blue Line but not a decision to drop Lebanon’s demand for an “Israeli” withdrawal from the Shibaa Farms.
“It is clear that Lebanon has never dropped its claim on the Shibaa Farms. And it never regarded the “Israeli” withdrawal as complete for precisely that reason” that it did not include the Shibaa Farms, Hoss said.
Hoss’ statement indicated that “the Lebanese government also contests three other sites covered by the Blue Line.”
The other locations are between Addaisseh and the “Israeli” settlement of Misgav Am, a 4-kilometer stretch east of Metulla to the Hasbani bridge and in a valley near Yaroun.
 

Two wounded in Fatima Gate shooting…(3)

“Israeli” troops on Friday shot and wounded two Lebanese civilians at the former border crossing of Fatima Gate in Kfar Kila, amid intense air activity by “Israeli” warplanes over South Lebanon.
Riad Younis, 38, and Hassan Yassin, 21, both from Sidon, were with two other men, when an “Israeli” soldier fired 14 shots at the group with a rifle.
Younis and Yassin were taken to the South hospital in Nabatieh with leg injuries. The other two members of the group were unharmed. Yassin was seriously wounded in the shooting and was transferred to a Sidon hospital after suffering heavy blood loss.
The two men were the first Lebanese civilian casualties at the hands of the “Israelis” along the border since the beginning of the year. One civilian was killed and eight others wounded at the former crossing in 2000.
“Israeli” military officials said their troops opened fire as a group tried to destroy the border fence at the Fatima Gate.
Younis denied having thrown stones at an “Israeli” outpost or attempted to damage the fence.
The shooting took place on a day of heavy “Israeli” air activity over Lebanon, the fourth consecutive day of such action since Hizbullah fighters attacked an “Israeli” outpost in the Shibaa Farms.
“Israeli” jets flew low over the South and also circled over the Bekaa.
Witnesses said a pilotless reconnaissance plane, a “drone,” circled over the Shibaa Farms and Kfar Shuba. “Israeli” troops were also seen reinforcing outposts they hold in the Shibaa Farms area.

 

“Israeli” warplanes violate Lebanese airspace for third consecutive day…(4)

“ “Israeli” jets violated Lebanese air space Friday for the third consecutive day, following an anti-”Israeli” attack by Hizbullah four days earlier.

Two “Israeli” fighter planes broke the sound barrier over the northern resort of Tripoli, near the Syrian border, before heading towards the south of the country.

Ten minutes later, “Israeli” fighters flew over the cities of Sidon and Tyre at low altitude, near the “Israeli”-Lebanese border.

On Thursday, “Israeli” warplanes flew over the southern region of Lebanon from which the Jewish state pulled out in May 2000 after 22 years of occupation, and also over the eastern Bekaa plain near the Syrian border.

Warplanes also overflew the country on Wedndesday.

 

Lebanon pullout led to Palestinian intifada – Lahd…(5)

The former head of the now-defunct “Israeli”-backed militia in southern Lebanon said in an interview on Friday that “Israel’s” lightning pullout from south Lebanon a year ago had paved the way for a Palestinian uprising.     Antoine Lahd told “Israel’s” largest newspaper, Yedioth Ahronoth, that he had warned the “Israeli” army against unilaterally ending its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon and that he felt betrayed by then-Prime Minister Ehud Barak.  "I warned them more than once: 'Don't unilaterally withdraw, don't look like someone who is fleeing Lebanon without an agreement. Others are watching every move you make and drawing conclusions'," Lahd said from his Tel Aviv exile. "'Everything you do against Hizbullah without an agreement will cost you a high price with the Palestinians.' It was a bad precedent," he added.  Barak came to power in May 1999 vowing to end “Israel’s” occupation of south Lebanon within a year. A month before the scheduled June 2000 withdrawal, “Israeli” troops left south Lebanon under cover of darkness. A Palestinian uprising against “Israeli” occupation of the West Bank and Gaza Strip erupted in September, just four months after “Israel’s” pullout. Lahd found out about the withdrawal in a telephone call while he was in Paris visiting his wife and children, who have lived in the French capital for the past 11 years.  When he came to “Israel”, he was told he could not return to Marjayoun. Lahd, who still wonders if Barak feared he would tell Hizbullah the withdrawal plans and for this reason did not coordinate with him, was "shocked" that nearly two decades of cooperation ended so quickly. "Barak forgot how for 16 years I worked with you hand in hand and never betrayed your trust," Lahd said.     "At the end of the shared path you stabbed me in the back. There is no other way to describe the way we worked hand in hand, and you left me and Lebanon without peace or security."
 

No papers on Resistance-Liberation Day…(6)

The Press Federation and the Journalists’ Union announced Friday that all newspapers will close on May 25 to mark Resistance and Liberation Day.
Federation president Mohammed Baalbaki and union president Melhem Karam said that the liberation of the South was a national achievement for all Lebanese from all confessions and affiliations. Both men urged the government to continue performing its duties in the fields of development and security in the South. They also called on all parties to maintain the political debate on a “patriotic” level.
Lebanon will celebrate its first Resistance and Liberation Day to mark the first anniversary of the liberation of the South last May.

 

UN officials to look at South, de-mining…(7)

The UN Under-Secretary-General for Peacekeeping Operations, Jean Marie Guehenno, and UN Special Coordinator for the Middle East Peace Process Terje Roed-Larsen will arrive in Beirut on Sunday, according to a UN press release.
Guehenno, Larsen and the Personal Representative of the Secretary-General for Southern Lebanon, Staffan de Mistura, will meet  President Emile Lahoud, Speaker Nabih Berri, Prime Minister Rafik Hariri and Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud Monday.
The UN officials also will participate in the two-day de-mining conference, which opens Monday at UNESCO Palace. The conference will see the launching of de-mining activities for which the United Arab Emirates has earmarked $50 million. The UN officials will also next week tour the Blue Line area in south Lebanon to visit UNIFIL’s positions and look closely at its activities on the ground, including the international force’s de-mining efforts in the area.

 

Serious help demanded for ex-detainees…(8)

The Follow-up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in “Israeli” prisons welcomed on Friday all projects to help former inmates but feared their proliferation would defuse the campaign of demands.
The committee said in a statement that proposals to offer health, social and psychological care for 1,500 former detainees was still awaiting international funding. It said Beirut MP Nasser Qandil’s recent proposal to treat former detainees as retired army soldiers was an “old suggestion” submitted by the committee more than 10 years ago. The committee urged the government to give priority to former detainees who have sat for examinations for vacancies in the Internal Security Forces. It called on the government to employ 500 former detainees as a gift for resistance on the occasion of Resistance and Liberation Day.

 

11 Palestinian Martyrs, Tens Wounded in Fierce “Israeli” Offensive, 6 “Israelis” dead, About 100 Injured…(9)

In a fierce “Israeli” aggression against Palestinians, “Israeli” helicopter strikes Friday on a Palestinian Authority security post and prison in the northern West Bank town of Nablus, left five Palestinians martyrs and tens of wounded. Meanwhile, the Palestinian resistance once again struck the heart of the Zionist Entity, when a Palestinian blew up himself at the entrance to the busy shopping Asharon center in the coastal town of Netanya. According the latest death toll 6 “Israelis” were killed and around 111 injured, several were in serious condition. In a phone call to al-Manar Channel, the Izzeddine al-Qassam Brigade, armed branch of Hamas movement, claimed responsibility for the blast, saying that 20-year-old martyr Mahmoud Ahmad Marmash from Tulkarm carried out the operation. The group said the bombing was number seven of 10 promised bomb attacks. The attack is the third in Netanya since the beginning of the Palestinian uprising and the deadliest since “Israeli” enemy Premier Ariel Sharon took office 10 weeks ago. An “Israeli” occupation policeman said the bomber detonated the bomb in sight of the guards at the entrance of the shopping center and witnesses said the guard's suspicion, and that of the shoppers, was aroused when they spotted the bomber dressed in blue suit and long jacket despite the heat. Alerted the police blocked the Palestinian, who in turn detonated the bomb. Following the blast, Sharon held urgent talks with war minister Benjamin Ben Eliezer and Foreign Minister Shimon Peres to study possible responses to the blast. This attack came a day after the “Israeli” occupation army intensified its aggressions against Palestinians with helicopter attacks and raids into Palestinian areas leaving several Palestinians wounded. Earlier Friday, two “Israeli” missiles exploded in the Mediterranean Sea, close to where Palestinian Chairman Yasser Arafat's offices are located in Gaza City. General Saeb al-Ajezz said the missiles appeared to have been fired from a position near the Nahal Oz kibbutz. A third missile landed at martyr's cemetery on the eastern outskirts of Gaza City. In another incident, one Zionist settler was shot dead and two others injured, one seriously, when the car they were traveling in came under Palestinian fire near Ramallah. Meanwhile, Palestinian resistance fighters intensified mortar attacks against occupation targets. Early Friday a mortar bomb was fired into the Southern Gaza Strip Jewish Settlement of Gadid. No casualties were reported. On Thursday night, a mortar shell hit a field near the Nir Am Kibbutz inside Palestinian territories occupied since 1948. Also the Islamic Jihad Movement claimed responsibility for 2 mortar shells at the Netzer Hazami settlement in the central Gaza Strip and a third shell at Kfar Aza, inside occupied Palestine. On the other hand, “Israeli” authorities stopped a group of Palestinian officials, including top negotiator Saeb Erakat, information minister Yasser Abed Rabbo and parliament speaker Ahmed Qorei, from entering the Gaza Strip for a meeting with Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat, a Palestinian official said. They were coming from the West Bank for a cabinet meeting.

 

Saudi Crown Prince Refuses to Visit Washington Unless the US Intervenes Against “Israeli” Attacks…(9)

Saudi Crown Prince Abdullah Ben Abdul Aziz refused US President George W. Bush’s official invitation to visit Washington, indicating Saudi Arabia’s discontent from the United States low-level involvement in the Arab-”Israeli” conflict. The New York Times daily reported a Saudi official as saying that Prince Abdallah will only visit the United States after the Bush administration does something to halt "“Israel’s”" military attacks against the Palestinians.

 

Moroccan Parties Call For Urgent International Intervention to Stop “Israeli” Massacres Against…(10)

Four Moroccan parties called on the international community to put an end to Zionist crimes against the defenseless Palestinian people, and demanded an urgent intervention to stop the massacres. The call came during a solidarity gathering for the Palestinian. For his part, the Moroccan Prime Minister, Abdul Rahman al-Yussufi, said the Zionists are committing crimes against humanity, and urged Arab countries to live up to their promises by aiding the Palestinians financially, and working on ending the economic closure on the Palestinian territories. He also said “Israeli” repression, destruction and aggressions should be stopped. Also, Abbas al-Fassi, the Secretary-General of al-Istiqlal Party, criticized the silence of other Moroccan parties towards the war of extermination led by the “Israelis”.

 

Foreign press association "outraged" by “Israeli” shootings…(11)

The Foreign Press Association (FPA) has expressed "outrage" over “Israeli” gunfire targeting a French television correspondent while covering clashes in the occupied West Bank. The FPA has written to “Israel’s” Foreign Minister Shimon Peres calling for official investigations into the many shootings by “Israeli” soldiers of correspondents. Peres said “Israel” has a "problem" with foreign press, echoing charges by right-wing “Israeli” officials who have been accusing journalists of being quote accessories to murder. FPA board members rejected the charges and voiced concern that international journalists are being put at risk by right-wing “Israelis” portraying them as "enemies" of the Jewish state.
