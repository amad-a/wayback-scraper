Lebanese President Says the Solution for Regional Conflict Is An “Israeli” Withdrawal from All Occupied Lands…(1)
On the second day of the Lebanese President Emile Lahoud's first official visit to Paris, France appeared to soft-pedal on its previous demand that the Lebanese government deploy the army all the way to the southern border, with Prime Minister Lionel Jospin saying this was purely internal Lebanese matter. As for regional tensions, Jospin expressed optimism over the impending visit of Syrian President Bashar Assad to France next month, while Lahoud explained the importance of preserving tight Lebanese-Syrian relations. The Lebanese President also reiterated that the solution for the regional crisis is a return to the negotiating table and a full “Israeli” withdrawal from all occupied Arab lands. He stressed once more that the occupied Shibaa Farms are Lebanese.

 

Tribunal sentences 8 for collaboration…(2)

The Military Tribunal sentenced eight people Wednesday to jail terms of 18 months on charges of collaborating and entering “Israel”.
The eight were Layla Mazraani, Fadi Ghasham, Tanios Makhoul, Antoine Milan, Zaki Abu Seif, Samir Abdoush, Ali Ramadan and Jalal Joumaa. The court also sentenced Samir Ali Hassan to hard labor for life in absentia for forming a gang, conducting terrorist acts against Syrian troops, assault and vandalism.
 

Palestinian Resistance Carries Out More Operations Against “Israeli” Occupation, 4 Palestinians Injured in and “Israeli” incursion…(3)
A car bomb exploded in the coastal town of Netanya this afternoon. “Israeli” enemy army radio claimed six people were lightly injured. Al-Quds Brigade, the armed branch of the Islamic Jihad Movement, claimed responsibility for the attack in a phone call to al-Manar Channel. Four Palestinian civilians were injured on Wednesday during an “Israeli” army incursion into a Palestinian-ruled area in the southern Gaza Strip, leaving one in a critical condition after being hit by an “Israeli” live bullet. Palestinian security officials said confrontation erupted between occupation forces and Palestinians after two “Israeli” tanks and a bulldozer penetrated several dozen metres into an area of Rafah and razed land. “Israeli” tanks also shelled a Palestinian national security post in Rafah, causing damage. The nearby Yebna refugee camp came under fire as well. Two mortar bombs hit the settlement of Slaf in the southern Gaza Strip on Wednesday. The Islamic Jihad in Palestine said in a statement that members of its military wing, the “al-Quds Brigades,” are responsible for the shelling. The statement added that it's the first time this settlement comes under fire, but the Zionist Entity said that the mortar bombs hit the Rafah Yam settlement. “Israeli” enemy army said a military convoy also came under automatic weapons fire on a road near the settlement of Netzarim in the central Gaza Strip. Shots were also fired near Ganei Tal in the Gush Katif Jewish settlement bloc in the southern Gaza Strip. Enemy officials said a bomb was also found in the Gilboa region near the northern West Bank city of Jenin. Earlier Wednesday, “Israeli” tanks and bulldozers entered a Palestinian controlled area near al-Mintar crossing east of Gaza City in the northern part of the Strip. In a statement received by al-Manar channel, Fatah Movement's General-Command of the Assifah forces said resistance shall continue as long as a single Zionist remains in Palestine. It added the Palestinian mujaheddeen will prevent Zionist settlers from enjoying security, and stability. The statement also said that martyrdom operations is a source of pride to our people, and these operations will continue until victory and the liberation of all Palestine is realized.

 

“Israeli” dead in northern Samaria attack…(4)
An “Israel” motorist was shot and killed in an attack a short time ago near the Samaria village of Bakka al Sharkia, north of Tulkarm.
The man, a resident of one of the “Israeli” communities in the vicinity, sustained a severe head wound in the shooting, in what was apparently a drive-by shooting attack, according to Army Radio.
He died en route as an “Israeli” helicopter evacuated him to Rambam Hospital in Haifa.
The Jerusalem Post will provide further details on this story as they become available.
 

Car bomb explodes in Al-Quds…(5)
A car bomb that exploded in the coastal city of Netanya yesterday caused no serious injuries but threatened to unravel a newborn US effort to put a lid on eight months of Palestinian-”Israeli” violence.

Hospital officials reported no serious injuries, but at least six people were treated for shock and hearing problems caused by the blast.

The Islamic Jihad Palestinian group claimed responsibility for the blast in a faxed message to The Associated Press in Lebanon and Syria.

Netanya, on “Israel’s” seacoast, is just 15 kilometres from the West Bank and is a frequent target for Palestinian bombers. On May 18, a suicide (martyrdom) bomber hit a mall there, killing himself and five “Israelis”.

The bombing, on the day two “Israelis” killed in a Palestinian drive-by shooting attack were buried, darkened the atmosphere around a renewed US effort to quell the violence.

A second meeting of “Israeli” and Palestinian security commanders was held late yesterday in Gaza, after an inaugural session on Tuesday aimed at improving security cooperation in the West Bank.

Major General Abdel Razek el-Majaidah, chief of Palestinian public security in Gaza, said afterwards the Palestinians demanded that “Israel” remove restrictions and evacuate eight Palestinian houses seized in Gaza in the past month. Other participants said the meetings would continue next week.

“Israel” demanded an end to Palestinian violence, according to a statement from the Defence (War) Ministry, which said the talks would continue next week.

US officials cancelled a meeting earlier yesterday with Palestinians about implementing the report of an international commission aimed at stopping the violence, said an official close to the negotiations.

Requesting anonymity, the official said the Americans were unhappy that the Palestinians had sent only mid-level security officials to the Tuesday security meeting, the first such meeting in several weeks.

US embassy spokesman Larry Schwartz declined to comment on the talks.

Palestinian negotiator Saeb Erekat said there can be no division between discussions about improving security and advancing political talks, especially a halt to construction in Jewish settlements in the West Bank and Gaza.

"To stop violence means lifting the closure (on Palestinian territories), stopping all settlement activities, implementing signed agreements and resuming (peace) negotiations," he said, listing Palestinian demands of “Israel”.

“Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon came under withering criticism from Jewish settlers, once his most enthusiastic supporters, for calling what “Israel” termed a unilateral ceasefire last week. Palestinians have dismissed the policy - in which “Israel” says it is initiating no actions beyond self-defence - as a publicity stunt.

At the funeral of one of the victims of a drive-by shooting in the West Bank on Tuesday, Shlomo Riskin, a well-known New York rabbi who moved to the settlement of Efrat in 1983, blasted Sharon's policy.

"A unilateral ceasefire is surrender," he thundered. "It is forbidden for us to surrender to evil."

The family of the other victim, Sarah Blaustein, 53, a recent immigrant from Lawrence, New York, asked the government not to send a representative to her funeral because of its policy of restraint in the struggle with the Palestinians.

The settlers are upset over a wave of attacks against them without strong “Israeli” retaliation and Sharon's acceptance of a report by the international commission, headed by former US Senator George Mitchell. The report calls for a freeze of all construction in the settlements, though Sharon has said he does not accept such a halt.

Sharon heard vocal opposition from his own party during a speech in the parliament about the conflict with the Palestinians. When he defended his call for a unilateral ceasefire, members of his own party interjected shouts of protest.

"I see the big picture," said Sharon, explaining that the ceasefire helps in “Israel’s” effort to win world support.

Warning of a long struggle, he counselled his people not to ask when the conflict will end. "They (the Palestinians) don't ask when this will end," he said. "The person who asks every day how long this will last, will lose."

 

Zar family to Sharon: We are like lambs led to slaughter…(6)
Prime Minister Ariel Sharon was subjected to a fierce verbal attack by incensed mourners at the Zar family household during a condolence call at the Samaria community of Karnei Shomron last night.
"We have to go to war against the Palestinians," one of the family members screamed at Sharon.
"Get to work, we are being led like lambs to the slaughter," another family member, wracked with sobs continued, her voice breaking.
"When in the past I said that Arafat was primitive, there were those that replied, "No, it's you who are primitive," Sharon said, continuing, "The eyes of many of those people are now open.
"My blood is boiling. I must decide when to do the things I think must be done," Sharon replied to the assembled group of mourners.
Gilad Zar was shot to death in an early morning ambush by Palestinian gunmen two days ago.
Chief Infantry and Paratrooper Officer Brig.-Gen. Yisrael Ziv said yesterday that the “Israeli” Army needs to be prepared for all-out war erupting.
However, he warned against sensationalizing the matter, insisting that this was, is, and will be the obvious task of the “Israel’s” Army.

 

“Israel” Economy Hurt by Few Tourists…(7)

 The number of foreign tourists visiting “Israel” has plummeted since “Israeli”-Palestinian fighting erupted eight months ago, forcing job losses, hotel closures and cutbacks in flights, officials said Wednesday.

The finance ministry estimates the losses from tourism will cost “Israel’s” economy $1 billion this year. Tourism last year brought in $3.2 billion.

''This is a major hit on the tourism industry. The damage to tourism is a long-term problem,'' said finance ministry deputy director Vered Dar. ''It may not be over for years, depending on the situation.''

In the first three months of this year, 485,000 tourists came to “Israel”, 50% fewer than the number of visitors in the same period a year ago, the tourism ministry said.

Twenty-five hotels have closed and about 12,000 hotel workers - 35% of the hotel work force - have lost their jobs since the fighting began Sept. 28, according to the Hotel Association in Tel Aviv.

El Al, the “Israeli” national airline, has seen 20% fewer passengers than normal since last year. The biggest drop was in passengers from Italy, Germany and Spain, airline officials said.

To cut expenses, El Al is using smaller planes and is halting some flights, spokesman Nachman Klieman said.

The fall in tourism accounts for much the economy's decline since the Palestinian uprising began. The “Israeli” Chamber of Commerce reported Tuesday that losses in tourism, exports and construction had cost the economy $2 billion.
