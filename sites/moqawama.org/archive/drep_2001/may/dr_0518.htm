ASSAD CALLS LAHOUD AFTER CUTTING VISIT TO CAIRO SHORT AS SYRIAN-”ISRAELI” TENSIONS MOUNT…(1)

Syrian President Bashar al-Assad held telephone discussions with Lebanese President Emile Lahoud on regional developments hours after the Syrian leader cut short talks in Cairo with Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak. Informed Syrian sources said Assad returned home after “Israeli” Defense Minister Benjamin Ben Eliezer vowed to respond to Hizbollah operations on the occupied Shebaa Farms, by targeting those he said are behind the attacks. Ben Eliezer said Damascus is responsible for recent Hizbollah strikes. Press reports in Beirut said Assad will dispatch his Foreign Minister Farouk Sharaa to Beirut on Friday to deliver a letter to Lahoud over regional tensions. 

 A senior Syrian official told Reuters Assad held three meetings with Mubarak, one conducted behind closed doors. They focused on efforts "to stop the brutal actions being committed against the Palestinian people." The unidentified Syrian official said talks also covered ways Arabs can overcome their differences to face prevailing challenges.  The official did not give a reason for the President's quick return, recalling Egyptian Information Minister Safwat el-Sherif's comment that there was nothing wrong in the talks between the two leaders and nothing happening in Syria to explain the quick return. So-called informed Syrian sources were widely quoted as saying Assad returned home following “Israeli” threats against Damascus. “Israeli” Defense Minister Benjamin Ben Eliezer held Syria responsible for Hizbollah operations against “Israeli” posts in the occupied Shebaa Farms. During a visit to the occupied Golan Heights, Ben Eliezer said Syria was responsible for Monday's Hizbollah attack on an army position in the occupied Shebaa Farms. The “Israeli” hawk said nothing will deter the Jewish state. 

“Israeli” warplanes violate Lebanese airpace for second day ..(2)

“Israeli” jets violated Lebanese air space Thursday for the second consecutive day, triggering an alert by Lebanese, Syrian and Hezbollah forces, AFP correspondents witnessed Thursday.

Lebanese army troops and Syrian forces deployed in eastern Lebanon were restricted to their bases, while anti-aircraft batteries were on alert, they said.

Hezbollah also advised farmers and shepherds not to approach the “Israeli”-occupied border Shebaa Farms area where the group had staged an attack on Monday, they said.

Earlier Thursday, “Israeli” warplanes had broken the sound barrier over the ancient city of Baalbek, an AFP correspondent said.

The fighter-bombers also flew at medium altitude over the border zone in southern Lebanon that “Israel” had occupied for 22 years before its May 2000 troop pullout.

On Wednesday, “Israeli” warplanes broke the sound barrier over southern Lebanese cities and Beirut.

“Israeli” Defence Minister Binyamin Ben Eliezer warned Syria Tuesday against new attacks from southern Lebanon on “Israeli” positions, after rocket fire on the Shebaa Farms by Hezbollah.

Since the “Israeli” withdrawal last year, Beirut has complained to the United Nations of dozens of “Israeli” violations of its air space.

 

“Israeli” maps ‘cover 70 percent of minefields’ …(3)

A senior UN official affirmed Thursday the need to start de-mining the South, saying that 70 percent of the minefields’ maps had been obtained from “Israel” and given to Lebanese authorities.
Staffan de Mistura, personal representative of UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, spoke to reporters after reviewing with Information Minister Ghazi Aridi preparations for a de-mining conference opening in Beirut on Monday.
“We have 70 percent of the maps already received from the “Israelis”, checked and proved to be correct,” De Mistura said. “We handed over the maps to Lebanese authorities and we insisted on receiving the remaining 30 percent and expect “Israel” to respond to our request.”
Acknowledging the danger the mines posed, de Mistura said the absence of some of the maps was no reason to delay addressing the matter.
“On the contrary,” he said, “our strategy is to begin clearing the mines and we won’t be alone in this, the whole international community stands on our side.”
De Mistura expressed hope that the conference would be “very successful,” recalling that the United Arab Emirates had already pledged $50 million to de-mining operations.

Sentences handed down to collaborators …(4)

A military court sentenced a former member of the “Israeli”-proxy South Lebanon Army to death in absentia and another to five years in jail for killing a Lebanese during “Israel”’s occupation of south Lebanon.
The verdicts were issued late Wednesday and posted on the court’s bulletin board Thursday.
The name of the victim was not made available.
Marwan Abdullah Olayyan, who was sentenced in absentia, and Mohammed Hassan Sweid were among nine people on trial for entering “Israel” and collaborating with the occupation forces.
The others received prison terms ranging from three weeks to one year and fines of LL200,000 to LL1 million. ­  

 

Palestinian kids send message to Annan …(5)

With maps of Palestine around their necks and banners in their hands, some 250 Palestinian refugee children turned up in front of UN House on Thursday to deliver a solemn letter to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan.
Aged between four and six, the children came from 11 schools in the Shatila, Bourj al-Barajneh and Mar Elias refugee camps to protest.
The protest was organized by the Coordination Forum of NGOs Working Among the Palestinian Community as part of activities marking the 53rd commemoration of Palestinians’ exile from Palestine in 1948.
The letter, written by forum members, condemned the “Israeli” Army’s use of force against children in the intifada and demanded the UN and the international community support Palestinian refugees’ right of return.
“We have spoken up. Now it’s time for the UN to listen and act,” the letter read.
Aida Jammal, UNICEF’s communication officer in Lebanon, met with the children and promised to deliver their message to Annan, along with a photograph of their protest.

 

 

“ISRAEL” OCCUPIES GAZA TERRITORIES "INDEFINITELY" AND WARNS OF "ALL OUT" WAR…(6)

“Israel” has warned it may launch an all out war against Palestinians if the uprising continues. “Israeli” troops occupied autonomous areas in Gaza and said they plan to stay there indefinitely. Palestinians warned Prime Minister Ariel Sharon his repeated invasions of self-rule regions may herald his downfall. 

“Israel” intensified its armed crackdown against the Palestinians with helicopter attacks and raids into Palestinians areas, saying it will hold on to the autonomous Palestinian territory it captured indefinitely for quote "military reasons." In what have become systematic operations under hardline “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon, the “Israeli” army continued its bombardments Thursday. Rockets rained down on the Khan Younes refugee camp in the occupied Gaza Strip, wounding four Palestinians, one seriously. “Israeli” troops also staged a new brief incursion Thursday- the sixth in twenty-four hours- into an area under Palestinian control, destroying residential homes in the town of Qarara near Khan Younes. In a separate incident, a remote-controlled bomb exploded in the West Bank as an “Israeli” vehicle was passing a road near Nablus, causing no injuries. Overnight “Israeli” helicopters pounded Palestinian security buildings at the Jabalaya refugee camp in Gaza and in the self-ruled West Bank town of Jenin, injuring at least 14 civilians. The attacks plunged the town into blackness after targeting the electricity instillations. “Israeli” forces had thrust into five areas under Palestinian control Wednesday in violation of the 1993 autonomy accords. But unlike past invasions the “Israeli” army announced it would stay indefinitely in homes it has occupied. Palestinians accused “Israel” of terrorizing civilians, saying the incursions prove “Israel”'s intention to reject peace efforts." In New York, “Israel”'s hardline Public Security Minister Uzi Landau admitted “Israel”'s killing of five Palestinian policemen earlier this week in the West Bank was a mistake. Landau, speaking after meeting Jewish leaders in the US, warned if the situation between the two sides continues to deteriorate “Israel” will feel the necessity for "all out war" against the Palestinians who warned Sharon against digging his "political grave and downfall" by re-occupying any towns or villages. 

 

Five killed by suicide (martyrdom) bomber in Netanya mall…(7)

Initial reports are indicating that a suicide (martyrdom) bomber blew himself up in a crowded Netanya shopping mall just a few short moments ago.

 Magen David Adom emergency services are reporting at least 40 wounded at this moment, according to “Israel” Radio.

The casualties have been evacuated to the city's Laniado Hospital. Their conditions have not been reported at this time.

The blast took place at the entrance to the Sharon Mall at the northern end of the coastal resort city, two kilometers inland from the beachfront.

Security forces and police bomb squads have sealed the area and are searching for additional attackers or explosive devices at this time.

The country's central coastal region has suffered more than 10 attacks over the past several weeks. Netanya has borne the brunt of these attacks.

Eyewitnesses reported seeing a large black cloud in the area of the mall, located near the city's courthouse.

 

Settler missing….(8)

Police, army, Shin Bet and Border Patrol units are to resume their search this morning for a 64-year-old Nokdim man with a history of disappearing for a few days on hikes.

Shaul Zmanenko, missing since Tuesday, has taken long hikes alone in the desert before, so his family only reported him as missing yesterday, saying he often took overnight hikes in the Nokdim and Herodian area.

The family reported him missing to the Hebron police and because of the recent murders of the two teens in Tekoa, not far from Nokdim, the security services, including police, army, Shin Bet and Border Patrol immediately took up the search, fanning out through the area on a hunt for him.

According to a Nokdim settler, Zmanenko got a ride out of the settlement on Tuesday morning, toward Jerusalem. A shepherd in the Herodian area reported seeing him there, heading in the direction of Nokdim.

In the past, Palestinian police found the man on hikes into Palestinian territories in the desert and returned him safely to “Israel”. Settlement sources note Zmanenko has a tendency to "forgetfulness.

 

ICRC slams “Israeli” settlements, deaths…(9)

The head of the International Red Cross Delegation in “Israel”, Rene Kosirnik, slammed “Israel” Thursday for establishing settlements in the occupied territories and for killing civilians who were not in the midst of active fighting, but noted "abnormal acts and wrong doings" on both sides.  While briefing reporters in Jerusalem, Kosirnik noted his organization was unable to visit four “Israelis” the militant group Hezbollah has been holding in Lebanon and that “Israelis” have denied visits to abducted Lebanese Mustafa Dirani and Sheikh Obeid.  Kosirnik called “Israel”'s policy of establishing settlements in the occupied territories "a war crime," saying it violates article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention that stipulates an occupier "shall not transfer parts of its own civilian population into the territory it occupies."  He said his organization also has discussed with “Israeli” authorities "the use of force and the proportionality of the use of force."

In an obvious allusion to several cases in which the “Israelis” targeted members of the paramilitary Fatah Tanzim and others accused of involvement in attacks, Kosirnik said: "The killing of one civilian willingly is a war crime."

 A civilian who bears arms and fights is a combatant but regains his rights as a civilian the moment he stops fighting, he maintained. "One does not forfeit rights for all the time," he said.  He acknowledged, however, that there are "gray areas." According to Kosirnik, the ICRC prevented “Israel” from carrying out some demolitions and also has approached the “Israeli” authorities about its practice of leveling areas in the Gaza Strip.  He based his approach on article 53 of the Fourth Geneva Convention that prohibits any destruction of real or personal property "except where such destruction is rendered absolutely necessary by military operations."   Humanitarian law "takes into account security imperatives of the occupying power so sometimes you have to balance those things," he said. Destruction can be justified if it is necessary "for the survival" of the occupation forces, he added.

 

EU Says “Israeli” Settlement Policy Violates International Law…(10)

European officials said their patience is running out concerning the expansion of “Israeli” settlements in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, and warned that a tough stance may be taken towards imports originating from these settlements. In an unusually strong criticism of the “Israeli” policy, the EU Foreign Relations Commissioner Chris Patten said “ the settlements constitute a violation of international law, and it is impossible to allow its expansion from going on like that”. For her part Swedish Foreign Minister Anna Lindt, whose country is holding the EU presidency, said that the European Union has called on "“Israel”" to start evacuating the settlements. Lindt held Sharon the prime responsibility for the violence in occupied Palestine.

 

Malaysia Calls For Deployment of UN Forces in Palestinian Territories…(11)

Malaysia on Thursday urged the United Nations to station peacekeeping troops in the Palestinian territories in response to “barbaric acts” committed by ““Israel””. Malaysian Foreign Minister Syed Hamid Albar said “Something needs to be done to stop the barbaric acts by the “Israelis” ... many children and women have died because of them.” He said the Organisation of Islamic Conference would meet in Doha on May 26 to condemn ““Israel”” and to pressure the United Nations to place peacekeepers in the occupied territories.
