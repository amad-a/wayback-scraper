Hizbullah ‘will keep fighting’ for Shibaa…(1)

Nasrallah: US trying to make contact

Hizbullah’s secretary-general  warned “Israel” against threatening Syria on Sunday, vowing to continue fighting “Israeli” troops in the disputed Shibaa Farms.
“Threatening to attack Syrian targets and launch war will not scare the young Syrian leadership,” Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said during a ceremony in Bidnayel in the Bekaa.
“If they (“Israelis”) are betting on scaring (Syrian) President Bashar Assad, then they are gambling and playing with fire because neither Syria nor its armed forces will accept insult or humiliation,” he added.
In April, “Israel” destroyed a Syrian radar post in Dahr al-Baidar, killing three Syrian soldiers, in retaliation for an attack by Hizbullah.
“Israeli” officials have repeatedly warned Syria that they will hold Damascus accountable for its support for Hizbullah and any attacks it carries out against “Israeli” forces.
Nasrallah also vowed to drive “Israel” from the Shibaa Farms and predicted the Jewish state would be forced to pull out of the area, as it did from the South.
“We’re running out of patience before the continued “Israeli” attacks on our national dignity,” he said.
“When we decide to punish this enemy for those crimes, no one should blame us.”
Nasrallah said the resistance would impose the rules of the game on “Israel”, reiterating that resistance operations would continue in the Shibaa Farms.
“The resistance will decide the timing and the venue of the battle, it will continue to enjoy the initiative,” he said.
He asserted that Hizbullah was not bound by UN resolutions and that it would recover the Farms “unconditionally.”
“The resistance is determined and willing to continue and nothing will stop it,” he said. “We ask the United Nations: Did it implement UN Resolution 425 or did we?”
He said that people who wanted sovereignty would not accept a piece of land remaining occupied, stressing that ““Israel’s” occupation of the Shibaa Farms is a violation of sovereignty and an insult to Lebanon.”
Nasrallah called for supporting the resistance because it would force “Israel” out of the occupied area.
He said that counting on a Lebanese split over the issue of the resistance was a “losing wager,” adding that the resistance defeated “Israel” when the Lebanese people were divided.
The Hizbullah leader also argued that “Israeli” mothers were more affected by losing their sons in resistance attacks, while
Lebanese mothers were “imploring us to send their children back as martyrs.”
“And we’re seeing Palestinians handing out sweets to celebrate martyrdom,” he added, referring to the intifada.
Nasrallah also said the US Congress’ vote to deprive Lebanon of “ridiculous” economic aid was an explicit expression of the US threat that Lebanon will not receive any aid unless resistance activities stopped and the army was sent to the border.
“If you give all the money in the world in return for giving up our land,” he said, “we will not accept even if you sent armies and fleets.” ­

The United States treats us as terrorists while they are trying to establish contact with us, working towards that night and. It is us who are refusing, as we do not want to have the honour of sitting at the same table as them”, he said.
 

 Hizbullah vows retaliation after “Israelis” wound Lebanese at border…(2)

“ Hizbullah said Saturday it would retaliate for the wounding by “Israeli” troops a day earlier of two Lebanese at a border post.

"The terrible “Israeli” attack against Lebanese civilians Friday is a new Zionist crime that will not pass without a response," Hizbullah said in a statement following Friday's shooting incident.

"The Islamic resistance (Hizbullah's military branch) will choose the opportune time to punish the criminals."

Two Lebanese, Hassan Yassin, 21, and Riad Yunis, 38, were shot in the foot when an “Israeli” soldier opened fired on them from an army post on the border between Lebanon and “Israel”.

Local newspapers An-Nahar and As-Safir quoted Yunis as saying "the soldier fired 14 bullets without any warning."

Neither of the two men had thrown stones at the “Israeli” position, which looked out on a Lebanese road linking the southern villages of Kfar Killa and Aadaisse, the newspapers reported.

Yassin was in serious condition after his wound started to hemorrhage, the newspapers said.

Lebanese security sources said two Lebanese civilians were wounded when “Israeli” border guards fired on a group of visitors at the Fatima Gate.

Since “Israel” ended its 22-year occupation of southern Lebanon in May 2000, Hizbullah has launched strikes on “Israeli” forces in the Shibaa Farms border territory.

The group showered the Shibaa Farms region with rockets last Monday and has kidnapped three “Israeli” soldiers and killed three others since October.

Lebanese and other Arab nationals regularly group at the border, often throwing stones across the fence.

At least 12 civilians have been killed and more than 40 have been injured by “Israeli” fire at the border since the withdrawal.

 

Raad claims conspiracy in border debate…(3)

Views in resistance

The head of Hizbullah’s politburo, Nabatieh MP Mohammed Raad, accused the United States and “Israel” of stirring domestic trouble to force the government to send the army to the South.
Speaking on Sunday in the southern village of Rihan to commemorate the first anniversary of the South’s liberation, Raad said “it is increasingly obvious that the Shibaa Farms will be liberated only by the force of arms, not through diplomacy.”
He added that both Washington and Tel Aviv “are behind the local gossip about the need to deploy the Lebanese army on the border.”
He claimed there was “no point in continuing the present public debate about the usefulness of the armed resistance in the South, which succeeded in getting UN Resolution 425 implemented after 20 years of being ignored.”
Raad also deplored the fact that, almost 12 months after its liberation, the South still lags behind other regions as far as the rebuilding of infrastructure is concerned.
 

Officials to lobby UN over   proposed troop reduction
Lahoud, Hariri and Berri will argue against modifying UNIFIL…(4)

A government source said Sunday that officials would reiterate their call to the UN’s under-secretary for Peacekeeping Operations, Jean Marie Guehenno, to maintain UNIFIL’s size.
According to the source, officials will not conceal the fact that they blame UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan’s non-compliance with the Lebanese government’s demand, and his insistence on proceeding with the scheduled reduction despite Beirut’s warning about the dangerous regional situation.
UNIFIL troops will be scaled down to 3,600 after July 31, and to 2,000 exactly a year later.
The source said that President Emile Lahoud, Premier Rafik Hariri, Speaker Nabih Berri and Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud will inform Guehenno about the danger of modifying UNIFIL’s role.
Despite the fact that Annan’s report linked the reduction to the regional situation, the source said, the three leaders find it dangerous to see UNIFIL’s status transformed from peacekeepers to observers without taking into account possible “Israeli” aggressions against Lebanon.
The source said that Guehenno’s visit here showed the importance given by Annan to the de-mining campaign and his desire to help it in its task.
Guehenno arrived in Beirut late Sunday and will participate with Annan’s personal representative for south Lebanon, Staffan de Mistura, in the two-day de-mining conference, which opens Monday at UNESCO Palace.
UN Special Coordinator for the Middle East Peace Process Terje Roed-Larsen was expected to accompany Guehenno, but he postponed his visit “due to the increasingly tense and violent escalation taking place currently in the West Bank and Gaza,” according to a UN statement.
The statement added that the special coordinator “hopes to visit Lebanon within the next two weeks.”
The UN officials will also tour the Blue Line area in the South to visit UNIFIL’s positions and look closely at its activities, including the international force’s de-mining efforts in the area.
The source said that local officials would also draw Guehenno’s attention to the severity of the UN’s view that Lebanon had accepted the Blue Line.
The source added that Lebanon would warn of the seriousness of Thursday’s Security Council press statement, which the source described as “aggressive and critical.”
The press statement said: “The council members expressed their deep concern for declarations that the Blue Line was not suitable with regard to the Shibaa Farms.
“This region is subject to UN Resolutions 242 and 338 related to the occupied Syrian Golan Heights.”
The source said that officials would assert that such a position would encourage “Israel” to attack Lebanon and Syria in retaliation for each resistance operation to liberate the farms.
According to the source, the three Lebanese leaders will take advantage of European Union Foreign Affairs Chief Javier Solana’s visit to Beirut on Monday to underline the EU’s “mistaken” policy of adopting the UN’s call to stop resistance activities in the Shibaa Farms.
The source also said the officials would inform Solana that such a position would provide a “cover” for any “Israeli” aggression on Lebanon and Syria.
“Such a European stand will not help the EU play a political role in the Middle East,” the source said.
Solana will be accompanied by EU envoy to the Middle East Miguel Angel Moratinos.

 

UN hands Beirut ‘very limited’ maps of “Israeli” minefields…(5)

Defense Minister Khalil Hrawi confirmed over the weekend that the government has finally received maps of “Israeli” minefields in the South.
However the maps, which authorities received from “Israel” via the United Nations, are “very limited,” Hrawi told the National News Agency on Saturday.
Staffan de Mistura, personal representative of UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, announced on Thursday that maps covering 70 percent of minefields in the former occupied territories had been obtained from “Israel”.
Hrawi said the government had asked countries that have ties with “Israel”, such as the United States, France and Italy, to pressure “Israel” to hand over the rest of the maps.
“We should have the maps, but I find it difficult because planting mines has a military objective,” said Hrawi.
“If we obtain the maps, it would be better and if not, we’ll continue our work.”
Hrawi said that countries participating in the two-day de-mining conference, which opens Monday at UNESCO Palace, were willing to contribute to dismantling “more than 130,000 mines and unexploded bombs.”
He said the majority of countries with diplomatic representatives here will participate in the conference. Joining them will be representatives from the Syrian Army, which assisted the National De-mining Office and was “willing to help.”
Hrawi said several countries will also send military delegations or experts, including the United Arab Emirates, the US, Italy and Mines Advisory Group, a British charity organization.
He said that the conference’s first day will include a “comprehensive explanation” of the scale of the de-mining project, after which participants are expected to state their country’s contribution.
On Tuesday, delegates will attend a mock de-mining operation at the army barracks in Nabatieh. Also participating will be Syrian Army representatives and an American team, which will train officers for the task.
The demonstration will take place in a specially-prepared 3,000-square-meter field, which has been divided into squares and rectangles.
UNIFIL’s Ukrainian battalion will provide mine sweepers for the mock operation, while trained dogs will also participate.
Hrawi also announced that starting Wednesday, the NDO will be “better prepared” to carry out de-mining operations.
“If countries fulfill their promises, we expect to accomplish a lot within three or four years,” he said.
“If help remains limited ... it will be a long process. It would take about 15 or 20 years because our capacities are limited and primitive.”

 

When Qantara finally came back to life
Nicholas Blanford reports on the day “Israel’s” 22-year
occupation of the South came to an end in one village…(6)

The former residents of Qantara had been considering returning to their occupied homes for at least nine months, ever since the (Israeli-proxy) South Lebanon Army militia had vacated its outpost in the center of the village and moved to a new position on Mheisbat hill, 3 kilometers to the east.
But access to the narrow rutted lane that led from Ghandourieh through Wadi Hojeir to Qantara was blocked by a checkpoint manned by Finnish UNIFIL peacekeepers. Civilians were forbidden from entering the front-line area, the scene of bitter clashes between the resistance and “Israeli” troops.
“Every time there was a funeral for someone from Qantara we would meet in Ghandourieh and ask UNIFIL to let us back,” said Ahmad Beshara, the mukhtar of Qantara. “But they told us that the village was still occupied and it was too dangerous. We were waiting for the smallest light to allow us back.”
Their chance came in mid-May 2000, when “Israeli” troops handed over the Taibe outpost to the SLA. The militia abandoned the Mheisbat and Qsair outposts at the same time, leaving a large chunk of the central sector outside the direct control of the occupation forces.
Mohammed Rammal, a construction worker and a member of Amal, sneaked into Qantara three days before the liberation with a couple of comrades to see for himself if the SLA had departed.
“We saw that there was no militia in the area so we began calling our friends on our cellulars and told them to come back. ‘Are you really in Qantara?’ they asked. ‘Yes,’ I told them. ‘Everything’s fine.’”
Sabaha Awada and her two daughters, Norma 17, and Sawsan, 19, were among the crowd of around 40 residents who had gathered in Ghandourieh to offer condolences to the family of Zeinab Deeb, who had died a week earlier. Awada had not set foot in Qantara for 15 years.
“We just couldn’t believe we were about to go back to our village. It was like a dream come true,” she said.
Also in Ghandourieh was Marjayoun/Hasbaya MP Nazih Mansour, a member of Hizbullah’s parliamentary bloc. He was familiar with the area, having been born and raised in Taibe.
“When I arrived at noon, one of our people said: ‘Do you want to go to Qantara?’ I had my bodyguard and two others with me. We headed toward Qantara immediately.”
The UNIFIL soldiers manning the checkpoint at the entrance of the road to Qantara were unable to stop the crowd. A mass of cars and residents on foot, cheering and waving flags, followed Mansour as he sped down the twisting, potholed lane into the depths of Wadi Hojeir.
In Qantara, Hajj Ali Atwi, 70, was working in his fields while his wife, Wahiba, 50, washed clothes on the doorstep. They were among the 30 residents still living in the village.
“We had heard rumors that the village would be liberated,” Wahiba said. “In the afternoon, UNIFIL blocked the road leading to Taibe with four tanks (armored personnel carriers).
“My son, Mustafa, who speaks English, asked the Finnish soldiers what was going on. But they said nothing was happening. Mustafa came to the house and said people were entering the village. I asked him what he was talking about. He pointed to the west and said many cars were heading toward the village.
“Then I saw 12 SLA men heading cross-country from the direction of Mheisbat. My husband went into the village and told people to be careful. We didn’t know at the time that the militiamen were deserting.”
Mansour was the first civilian to enter Qantara. He stopped outside a house at the entrance of the village.
“I entered the house and saw an old man and woman,” Mansour said. “I recognized the man as Abdo Saghir. He used to take me to school from Taibe to Nabatieh. He was scared and didn’t recognize me. I told him who I was and he yelled and burst into tears. I began crying too, and we both hugged each other.”
The word was spreading rapidly and hundreds of people from all over the South began heading toward Qantara.
“I started shaking when I saw all these people,” Wahiba said. “It was a huge surprise. It was like we had been dead and then reborn.”
For Rammal, the Amal fighter, the feeling of joy at the liberation of the village was tinged with sadness.
“The village was in ruins, barely any buildings remained standing. I remembered my friends and family who had died over the years,” he said.
Amid the scenes of celebration, Mansour debated whether to continue to other villages or remain in Qantara.
“We prayed first,” he said. “Then I decided to continue. The road was jammed with cars so I jumped on the back of a motorcycle and we drove toward Qsair. Others began following us.”
Upon reaching the entrance of Qsair, Mansour found his path blocked at a UNIFIL checkpoint. An APC was parked across the narrow road and armed soldiers stood on the other side of a locked gate.
“We told them these were our villages. They said we couldn’t go any further. The people were waiting to see what I would do. The decision wasn’t easy. We didn’t know for sure if the militia had left the area completely. But we felt ourselves compelled to continue. So I grabbed the gate and began shaking it with my hands. Everyone joined me and within 15 minutes we had knocked the gate over. The UNIFIL soldiers saw we were not going to stop and moved the (APC) off the road.”
The crowd surged into Qsair, then headed east along the single road running parallel to the Litani River gorge, through the abandoned village of Alman and on to Deir Sirian.
The jubilant residents greeted the throng by throwing handfuls of rice. Sheep were slaughtered in the street to honor their liberators.
Finally, the cavalcade entered the outskirts of Taibe, the largest village in the area. SLA militiamen were still manning the heavily-reinforced outpost on the hill overlooking the village.
“The scenes in Taibe were indescribable,” Mansour said. “People were running out of their houses barefoot to greet us. It was a truly historic moment.”
The militiamen in the outpost fired a few warning shots over the crowd but were unable to quell the triumphant advance. Using a loudhailer, Mansour called on the militiamen to surrender. About 15 of them did.
The others began abandoning the position and heading down a back road to Addaisseh, 2.5 kilometers east of Taibe.
By early evening, the “Israelis” were scrambling to check the headlong civilian advance. A bulldozed earth barricade blocked the road to Addaisseh and tanks manoeuvred into position on the outskirts of the village.
The occupation zone was in danger of being cut in two. Only the road through Addaisseh still connected the northern and eastern sectors of the occupation zone to the central and western sectors.
Hizbullah’s leadership was as stunned as everyone else at the turn of events. But the resistance also recognized the potential advantage in exploiting the sudden development.
As night fell, resistance fighters went on stand-by throughout the South as Hizbullah’s local commanders turned their attention to another occupied village lying 8.5 kilometers south of Qantara ? Houla.

Palestinian refugees in Lebanon stage protest sit-in…(9)

“ Several hundred Palestinians gathered in the refugee camps of Beirut and southern Lebanon Saturday calling for international protection against "the (“Israeli”) massacres" carried out against their people in the autonomous Palestinian territories.

At Lebanon's largest refugee camp -- Ain el-Helweh, near Sidon in southern Lebanon -- supporters of the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine (DFLP) burned a model of an “Israeli” settlement.

"With our blood, with our soul, we will avenge you O Palestine !," they chanted.

Further south, refugees from camps near Tyre gathered in front of the UN offices there in response to a call from Fatah, the main faction of Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat's PLO (Palestine Liberation Organisation).

"Even if our children were born in countries of the exodus, they always breathe the smell of the land of Palestine," they chanted.

Similar gatherings were held in Shatila camp in Beirut, and Bourj el-Barajneh, in the southern suburbs of the capital.

At all the rallies, the demonstrators read a memorandum addressed to the United Nations Secretary General Kofi Annan, claiming their right of return, international protection for the Palestinian people and "recognition of its legitimate right to fight and to defend itself to achieve its independent state."

Lebanon has 367,000 Palestinian refugees according to UN figures, nearly half of whom are in 12 poverty-stricken camps scattered around the country.

 

Arab Ministers Recommend Moratorium on Political Contact with “Israel”…(10)

Arab foreign ministers have recommended a collective severance of all political contacts with “Israel” until it ceases all forms of military action against the Palestinians.
The recommendation was taken by the foreign ministers during a meeting at Arab League headquarters in Cairo that was attended by Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat.
The ministers met in their capacity as members of a follow-up committee formed by an Arab summit conference held in Amman in February to oversee ways of assistance to the Palestinians against “Israel’s” savage aggression.
The recommendation is technically not binding on the Arab governments. But reporters at the Arab League headquarters said the delegates were in constant touch with their higher authorities to clear the recommendation in advance, lending it extra weight.
"The meeting has called for severing all Arab political contacts with “Israel” as long as the aggression and the siege of the Palestinian people and their national authority continues," a statement said after the day-long deliberations Saturday.
Anger in the Arab world shot up to fever-pitch over “Israel’s” use of F-16 fighter-bombers to bludgeon Palestinian cities for the first time since the 1967 Middle East war from dusk Friday to dawn Saturday, killing 12 Palestinians and wounding more than 100.
The recommendation has thrown in doubt the fate of a joint initiative by Egypt and Jordan, the only Arab countries that have peace treaties with “Israel”, to broker a Palestinian-”Israeli” cease-fire.
"Our intention is not to talk about or fall in the trap of talking about peace proposals while we see that the “Israeli” government does not mean it," Arab League new Secretary-General Amr Moussa told reporters.
"“Israel” is Systematically exterminating the Palestinian people. The attacks against them have to stop," said Moussa,, renewing a pan-Arab demand for the dispatch of an international force to protect the Palestinians.
On his return to Gaza, Arafat told reporters the League position "reflects the Arab solidarity " with the Palestinians following the latest “Israeli” escalation of air and ground operations. He said the Palestinian losses from the “Israeli” aggression have surpassed $5 billion.
The Cairo meeting was attended by delegations from Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Egypt, Morocco. Tunisia, Saudi Arabia and Yemen.
“Israel” said the Arab move would backfire. 'It is self-defeating," said “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon's spokesman Raanan Gissin.

 

Rajoub: “Israelis” 'bloodthirsty gangsters'…(11)

"“Israel” is governed by a group of gangsters, bloodthirsty people," said West Bank Preventive Security Service chief Jibril Rajoub to a group of “Israeli” reporters who visited his headquarters in Ramallah yesterday.
"Your government destroyed all chances of peace or reconciliation with the Palestinian people. We seek a Palestinian state with east Al-Quds as its capital, whether you like it or not. The “Israeli” government cannot dictate its conditions to the Palestinian people by using force. Sooner or later justice will defeat “Israeli” power."
The worst kind of terror is the occupation and the settlements, he said, adding, "the first step must be to stop all occupation. You have to decide whether you want occupation or security."
According to Rajoub, the PA has made every effort to halt the violence. The ball, he said is now in the “Israeli” court.
"Netanyahu had his own idea about how to solve the conflict and implement agreements. Then Barak came with another idea. Now there is Sharon. I don't think we can live forever under the mercy of the “Israeli” government's aspirations."
The shooting at settlements is not terror, he said, as the settlements are perceived as a kind of aggression.
Rajoub's colleagues said the shelling was an assassination attempt.
Rajoub's home has been shot at twice before, but this was the first time his home was attacked by tank shells.
Defense Minister Ben-Eliezer later said the circumstances of the shootings were not fully clear, but he was sure the shelling was an accident.
Three of his bodyguards were injured, including his personal guard, members of his security service told The Al-Quds Post.
Rajoub was at home resting when three tank shells hit his home, the senior preventive security officer said. His bodyguards rushed him out of the collapsing building, he added.
Other sources said Rajoub himself was not at home at the time.
The house sustained moderate damage from the shelling. Witnesses said one shell exploded inside the house and two others landed outside.
Standing outside the damaged building, he told reporters, "This is a criminal attack. Nothing similar has happened here before. I call on all the international community to come and see the amount of destruction that happened here in my house, which is not an office nor a military base."
Rajoub's wife and three children were not at home at the time of the attack.
The meeting with Rajoub came after a scheduled open panel discussion set up by PA Minister of Information Yasser Abed Rabbo with “Israeli” reporters at the Palestinian Media Center in Ramallah. It was the first time that Palestinian officials had officially invited a group of reporters to discuss the situation, and was perceived by many either as a distress call or an attempt to convey the PA's message across to the “Israeli” public.
Abed Rabbo said that in order to reach an agreement, all the West Bank and Gaza Strip settlements must be evacuated, without exception.
Furthermore, "If negotiations are to be renewed then it must be done in the presence of a third party," he said.
"If peace means the end of occupation, then we will say yes. But if it means “Israel” will define the borders of the cages we are to live in, then we will fight."
PA Minister for Al-Quds Affairs Faisal Husseini, however, believes that “Israel” is unable to accept the concept of two separate states with a clear border between them, according to the 1967 lines. "Without that, we won't reach anything," he says.
Between meetings, the reporters were taken to Beituniya to view the result of the F-16 shelling of the Force 17 headquarters there. The site, a mass of concrete rubble and twisted steel, lies opposite the Grand Hotel, which in the past was the venue of meetings between Palestinian and “Israeli” government officials. Several black-shirted Palestinians touting Kalashnikov assault rifles stood nearby.
In the Grand, work was underway to clear the smashed glass from the windows. The hotel's swimming pool, the only one in the city, was also damaged.
Reporters were later taken to the roadblock where five Palestinian policemen were killed by soldiers just over a week ago. "We have yet to receive an explanation from the “Israelis”," said Rajoub. The soldiers shot at the policemen from the roof of an apartment block overlooking the road on the opposite hill, said Abed Rabbo. The soldiers, he said, took over the roof a month ago.
The actual post was empty, floral wreaths of dried flowers hung on the open door.

 

“Israeli” helicopters strike Palestinian targets…(12)

“ “Israeli” helicopter gunships fired at least seven missiles at Palestinian targets in the Gaza Strip on Monday, prompting new Palestinian demands for international intervention which “Israeli” quickly dismissed.

At least 441 Palestinians, 13 “Israeli” Arabs and 87 “Israelis” have been killed since the uprising flared last September against “Israeli” occupation of the West Bank and Gaza Strip.

The air strikes, which followed fighting on Sunday, were the latest in a series of raids which “Israel” says are needed to protect its people but which have heightened international concern that the conflict could rapidly spin out of control.

Palestinian officials said the helicopters also opened fire with machineguns in the Gaza Strip but reported no casualties other than a night watchman who was slightly hurt.

The blasts rocked Gaza City, damaging a car mechanic's workshop, a marble factory and library. The army said the helicopters hit a factory used to make mortar bombs.

The Palestinians, in the midst of an eighth-month-old uprising against “Israeli” rule, have stepped up calls for international protection since “Israel” deployed F-16 fighter jets in air strikes on Friday after a Palestinian suicide martyrdom bomber killed five “Israelis”.

The Palestinians have also been incensed by the shelling of West Bank Preventive Security chief Jibril Rajoub's house in the Wets Bank city of Ramallah on Sunday. He was unhurt.

"This is part of the war on the Palestinian people and the Palestinian Authority. “Israel” is acting as a state above the law," Palestinian cabinet minister Saeb Erekat said of the air strikes in Gaza.

"It is time for the world to stop this state before it is too late," he told Reuters.

Despite the growing international criticism of “Israel”, cabinet minister Danny Naveh said the government still opposed the dispatch of any international peace force to the region.

"International intervention and international forces won't solve the problem. They can only serve the Palestinian interest to try to impose pressure on “Israel” and that's something we cannot accept," he told Army radio.

GUNBATTLES IN WEST BANK

At least 10 Palestinians were wounded by “Israeli” tank fire near Ramallah, Bethlehem and Qalqiliya in the West Bank late on Sunday. “Israeli” troops and Palestinian gunmen also exchanged heavy fire in the West Bank town of Hebron.

The escalation reached a new peak on Friday when “Israel” responded to the Palestinian suicide martyrdom bomb attack in the town of Netanya by deploying the F-16 fighters against Palestinian security targets in the West Bank.

At least 12 Palestinian police were killed in the raids, which brought new calls for an end to the violence and the heightening of a security alert in “Israel”.

“Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon pledged his government would use as much military might as necessary against Palestinians despite scathing criticism of the F-16 raids.

“Israeli” newspapers called the decision to unleash the U.S.-built aircraft "pointless" and "stupid." U.N. Secretary-General Kofi Annan branded “Israel’s” response "disproportionate."

In an interview with NBC television, U.S. Vice President Dick Cheney was asked whether “Israel” should stop using F-16s.

"Yeah, I think they should stop, both sides should stop and think about where they are headed here," he responded.

 

EU threatens “Israel” over its exports from settlements…(13)

Europe will today step up pressure on the “Israeli” Government over human rights and threaten a clamp down on exports from illegal settlements in the West Bank and Gaza.

Swedish officials will use an EU-”Israel” Association Committee meeting in Brussels to make what was described yesterday as a "direct" statement covering human rights.

The European Commission will follow up by upping the ante in a dispute over "rules of origin", accusing “Israel” of breaking a trade-deal by selling goods made by settlers to Europe while concealing their origin.

The move reflects growing criticism of “Israel”. On Saturday, the Swedish presidency of the EU issued a statement saying it is "appalled by the rapid increase in the level of violence in recent days".

Chris Patten, EU Commissioner for external relations, told the European Parliament last week that all settlement activities in Gaza, the West Bank, including East Al-Quds, and the Golan Heights "constitute a major obstacle to peace". He added that these areas "fall outside the territorial scope" of the EU-”Israel” trade agreement and that the "EU must uphold the rule of law".

EU customs officers suspect a range of “Israeli” exports, including wine, flowers, carpets, cosmetics and soda machines, of coming from illegal settlements. Brussels says that such goods are not entitled to tariff concessions granted by the trade agreement. The EU has already demanded changes to the agreement to ensure that orange juice from settlements is not sent, but 10 of its 15 members, including the UK, have queried other “Israeli” import certificates.

According to “Israel”, at stake is relatively insignificant $200m, or three per cent of its total $7bn of exports to Europe ­ though Brussels is cautious about these figures. Customs in some EU countries are already demanding deposits to cover tariff duties that may be applied.

The Commission has stress-ed that it has no intention of imposing economic sanctions on “Israel”. However, some officials are clearly irked at “Israel’s” disregard for EU attempts to help broker peace in the Middle East. They argue that “Israel” takes the benefits of links with the EU while refusing to engage in political dialogue.
