Nasrallah blames crisis on debt…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general said Thursday that the government ­ and not the resistance ­ was responsible for the economic crisis.
Speaking to the Kuwaiti daily Al-Ray al-Aam, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said there was no “direct link” between the country’s economic “bottleneck” and his group’s continuing activities in the South.
“It is not the resistance forces that are responsible for the crisis,” he said, “but rather the state officials who contracted billions of dollars worth of debt for the country during the years they were in office.”
As for the Shibaa Farms, Nasrallah stressed that “the issue is not about a few square kilometers of land, or whether this land has Lebanese residents or not. It is an issue of national pride that we cannot afford to overlook.”

 
“Israel: Army price for “Israeli” soldiers information…(2)
“Israeli” Defense (War) Minister Binyamin Ben-Eliezer said Thursday that he would be ready to pay "any price" for information on three “Israeli” soldiers and a colonel whom the Lebanese Hizbullah detained.
"I would pay any price for a lead, at least a cassette," in which the men would appear, he told United Press International shortly after raising the issue in an address to his Labor Party's Central Committee.
Hizbullah last year arrested the three soldiers from a U.N.-delineated borderline in the Shibaa Farms of South Lebanon that they claim. Several days later, they said they were holding an “Israeli” colonel whom they accused of spying. 
Hizbullah rejected International Red Cross demands to visit the four. It demanded “Israel” free several Lebanese prisoners, including Sheikh Abdul Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani, whom “Israel” had abducted in an attempt to free a missing airman. 
Ben-Eliezer told his party all he wanted was "a lead, a sign, be it a cassette that we will hear, that we shall see, a handwritten letter. I am ready to release whoever can be, I should just have a sign (of life), and there is no sign."
Asked later whether he would release Obeid and Dirani, Ben-Eliezer told UPI, "If have information, I am willing to discuss every possibility."

 

Fadlallah defends martyrdom missions…(3)

A recent fatwa on suicide martyrdom missions in the name of Islam was criticized Thursday by Sayyed Mohammed Hussein Fadlallah, who insisted that such operations were legitimate.
“Suicidal martyrdom operations, including those mounted by Palestinians against “Israelis”, are allowed in struggles against the enemy,” the senior cleric said in a statement. “It is the duty of all Muslims to engage in Islamic jihad if it ensures the ultimate goal, which consists in inflicting losses on the enemy.”
Fadlallah said that “according to Islam, it is wrong to commit suicide, but this does not apply to jihad operations.”
“What was said against the suicide martyrdom operations was meant to undermine the anti-”Israeli” struggle,” Fadlallah added.

 

‘Cutting UNIFIL violates 425’…(4)


Speaker Nabih Berri said Thursday that UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan’s report recommending a phased reduction in UNIFIL’s size violated part of Security Council Resolution 425 on “Israeli” withdrawal from occupied areas.
Berri held a meeting with Annan’s personal representative in the South, Staffan de Mistura, who briefed him on certain points in the report.
Information leaked from the meeting said Berri reiterated his country’s opposition to any reduction in UNIFIL’s strength and added that the report contained a change in UNIFIL’s mandate.
The government has launched intensive diplomacy to affirm its stand on the issue of the proposed reduction, calling for the need to retain UNIFIL’s strength at 4,500, the level preceding “Israeli” withdrawal last May.
The moves come as the UN Security Council is due to consider Annan’s report on May 15 and decide whether to adopt it or introduce a change, as demanded by Lebanon.
President Emile Lahoud was notified on Thursday that Annan had received a message containing the country’s position on the issue, urging the need for UNIFIL to “carry out its role in full.”
De Mistura told reporters after meeting Berri that defending the UN-demarcated Blue Line “is not done through the number of soldiers deployed but through the moral authority of the secretary-general, his representatives and the Security Council.”
He said the proposed gradual reduction of the force to 2,000 men at the end of July 2002 was conditional on “no radical changes occurring in the region.”
“I’m aware of fears that this number may be reduced further, but such fears are unfounded because the report was clear about reducing the figure to 2,000,” he added.
Referring to the need to develop the South, de Mistura said the “few dollars spent by UN troops is not the solution to the present or the future. We’re in need of economic development, providing job opportunities and the infrastructure as well as ensuring investments.”
He called on the government to provide more loans to the South, despite economic difficulties, and said additional care should be attached to the region following its liberation from occupation. He also said demining the South would help “confront the economic situation.”
De Mistura was referring to an Italian-financed conference in Beirut on May 21 to discuss possible participation by various countries in the demining campaign. Those participating, numbering about 300, will go to the South the next day to inspect mine clearing tests.
Sources outlined the conference aims, which include mobilizing countries and UN organizations and seeking their assistance in moves to clear mines planted in over 1,500 fields.
Lebanon has already notified various countries, including Arab states, of the requirements for implementing the plans and there have been positive responses, including some financial contributions, the sources said.
They also said a main obstacle facing the plans was “Israel’s” refusal to hand over maps of minefields in the South.
Lebanese Army officers and UNIFIL representatives toured mine-infested areas in the South on Thursday. They watched from within a Finnish armored car as a mine-sweeping vehicle removed 12 mines from one spot in the former occupation zone.

 

Lebanon sentences another 18 more people for collaborating with “Israel”…(5)

“ Beirut's military court sentenced 18 more Lebanese for collaborating with the "“Israeli” enemy," judicial sources said Thursday.

Four of them were sentenced in absentia to 15 years each.

The remainder received prison sentences ranging between two weeks and five years.

The trials against those accused of collaborating with “Israel” started 10 days after the May 24, 2000 pullout of “Israeli” troops from south Lebanon. Since then, Beirut's military court has tried 2,613 suspected collaborators.

So far, 30 have been sentenced to death, all of them in absentia.

 

“Israel” fires rockets at Arafat's police HQ in Gaza…(6)

“Israel” blasted surface-to-surface rockets into the headquarters of Yasser Arafat's Fatah movement and national security headquarters in Gaza City yesterday.

Palestinians in Gaza said the weaponry was the most powerful yet used against them by the “Israel” army in the seven and a half months of the conflict in which more than 500 people have died.

Both sides hurled accusations at each after a week that began as a four-month-old girl was killed by a tank shell in Gaza and culminated in the discovery in a West Bank cave of two 14-year-old Jewish settlers who had been stoned to death.

Three missiles hit the Fatah offices, a few hundred yards from Mr Arafat's headquarters, and several struck the Palestinian Authority's security headquarters, which have an intelligence department and a jail. Palestinian doctors said 20 people were wounded.

There were also fierce exchanges between Palestinian gunmen and “Israeli” troops in Rafah, a scene of regular violence in the south of Gaza.

The Palestinian Planning Minister, Nabil Shaath, said “Israel” had declared a "war of terror" against the Palestinians. “Israel’s” Prime Minister, Ariel Sharon, referring to the rocket attack, said "this strategy will continue until the Palestinian violence stops".

Two of the rockets punched large holes in the Fatah offices, causing balconies to crumble. "We are not afraid of death," chanted a crowd that gathered after the strike. An office containing the Palestinian Public Security, Military Intelligence and General Intelligence departments, and surrounded by housing, was also hit.

Earlier, “Israel’s” armed forces dispatched armoured bulldozers inside the strip to flatten Palestinian farmland and a security post. It was the “Israeli” army's fifth invasion of Palestinian territory in two days and, according to Palestinian officials, its 14th since Mr Sharon became Prime Minister just over two months ago. On all occasions troops have withdrawn after the demolitions.

The army argues that such wrecking missions ­ which have been criticised by “Israel’s” closest ally, the United States ­ are "engineering activities" aimed at preventing Palestinian paramilitaries from firing guns, grenades and mortars into Jewish settlements, at military positions, or into “Israel”.

The Palestinians say they are part of the pattern of collective punishment of the 1.2 million Arabs in Gaza.The latest Palestinian figures show 180 homes and 44 water wells have been ruined, and 25,000 trees uprooted by “Israel” in the occupied territories since September.

An “Israeli” army spokeswoman said the roadside bomb went off near the Kissufim crossing as three Romanians were working on the fence that separates the Gaza Strip from “Israel”. It killed two of the workers and injured the third. They were among hundreds of thousands of foreign workers brought in to replace Palestinians, who are banned from entering “Israel” because of the crisis. The 250,000 foreign workers in “Israel” are mostly from the former Soviet Union, the Philippines, Romania, South America and Thailand. Kavla-Oved, an “Israeli” labour rights group, says many are paid far less than the minimum wage of 3,266 shekels a month, about £560.
