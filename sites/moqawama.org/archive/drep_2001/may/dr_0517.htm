“Israeli” warplanes break sound barrier over Beirut…(1)

“ The “Israeli” air force violated Lebanese air space Wednesday and broke the sound barrier over Beirut, 48 hours after an anti-”Israeli” attack by the Lebanese militant group Hizbullah.

Supersonic bangs shook the windows of homes in Marjayoun, Tyre and Sidon, before “Israeli” jets again broke the sound barrier over the capital, leaving white plumes of smoke high in the sky, AFP corresondents said.

“Israeli” Defence Minister Binyamin Ben Eliezer warned Syria Tuesday against new attacks from southern Lebanon on “Israeli” positions, following rocket fire from Hizbullah, which is supported by Damascus and Tehran, on the Shibaa farms area.

The attack on the area occupied by “Israel” did not cause any casualties or damage.

In the aftermath of the “Israeli” withdrawal from southern Lebanon last May after 22 years of occupation, Beirut has complained to the United Nations of dozens of “Israeli” violations of its air space.

 

Suspected collaborator found dead near Kfar Kila…(2)

A suspected collaborator with “Israel” from the border village of Kfar Kila who went missing 17 days ago was found dead Wednesday in an olive grove on the edge of the village.
But according to a source at the Nabatieh government hospital, Hassan Daher, 65, was probably not killed by abductors but died from natural causes about a week ago.
Daher went missing at the end of April and was thought to have been kidnapped as part of an escalating campaign against former “Israeli”-proxy South Lebanon Army militiamen and other residents of the South suspected of having collaborated with “Israel”. There was no claim of responsibility for Daher’s disappearance.
His body was discovered noon Tuesday by farmers from Qlaya. Alerted by the smell, the farmers discovered the partially decomposed corpse lying on some rocks in the middle of the grove.
The authorities were alerted and Daher’s body was removed and taken to the Nabatieh government hospital for an autopsy.
Daher was originally from Lebbaya, in the Western Bekaa, just north of the former occupation zone.
Kfar Kila residents said he had been involved in a dispute with another branch of the Daher family in the mid-1980s. The details were unclear but the residents believed the feud had involved the killing of two people, one of them Daher’s daughter, and the rape of his niece. The result was that Daher fled and sought refuge in then-occupied Kfar Kila.
He found a job in “Israel” and allegedly also smuggled drugs across the border. His two sons, both former SLA militiamen, are in Roumieh Prison.
The residents said that his enemies in Lebbaya attempted to abduct him at least once from Kfar Kila during the occupation.
Daher went missing April 30, apparently snatched by unknown men and taken to Nabatieh.
A doctor at the Nabatieh hospital, who spoke on condition of anonymity, said that no trace of foul play had been found on Daher’s corpse. He speculated that Daher had died of a heart attack. He added that from the rate of decomposition, Daher had probably died a week ago.
Since the beginning of the year, the border district has witnessed a growing number of attacks against ex-SLA militiamen.

 

“Israel” invades Gaza, kills Palestinian schoolboy; blast rattles east Al-Quds…(3)

An explosion shook an area near the Old City of occupied East Al-Quds but not casualties were reported. “Israeli” police said the cause of the blast, which occurred near the main bus station in East Al-Quds's Damascus Gate, remains unclear. “Israeli” police told AFP a Palestinian passerby noticed a suspicious bag in the area, picked up and threw it into a bush, where it exploded. Earlier, “Israeli” soldiers raided the autonomous Palestinian areas for the fourth time on Wednesday. They also shot and killed a Palestinian schoolboy schoolboy as he passed an army watchtower near the Netzarim Jewish settlement in the occupied Gaza Strip. There were no confrontations at the time. In an overnight attack in Gaza, a mother was seriously wounded and her little daughter hurt when “Israeli” tanks attacked residential areas in the Rafah refugee camp. Palestinians mourned new dead in the aftermath of the most emotional day on the Palestinian calendar, the al-Naqba, marking the expulsion of hundreds of thousands of Palestinians from their nation when the state of “Israel” was created in 1948. “Israeli” soldiers killed four Palestinians during Naqba commemorations and wounded more than 200 when some 700,000 Palestinians took to the streets. Two foreign journalists were also wounded, apparently by “Israeli” gunfire. “Israeli” troops again invaded Gaza. They occupied Palestinian President Yasser Arafat's Palestinian Authority's main dairy plant and searched homes in other autonomous areas. “Israeli” tanks and bulldozers for the second time this week razed fruit orchards. The last time “Israeli” troops invaded autonomous regions they killed five Palestinian policemen. A senior unnamed “Israeli” official admitted Wednesday the army had committed a quote "error" caused by poor intelligence when it shot dead the policemen. “Israel’s” Foreign Minister Shimon Peres expressed regret over the killing.

 

“Israeli” helicopters attack West Bank, Gaza sites…(4)

The “Israeli” army launched an air strike on the northern West Bank village of Jenin, sources on both sides said.

“Israeli” helicopters also attacked the Palestinian police station at Jabalya in Gaza.

Palestinian security sources said the Jenin attack -- four missiles fired at the offices of the Palestinian Authority -- was unprovoked.

"Every day, they are hitting any town, any city, any suburbs," said Marwan Kanafani, an Arafat adviser. "This is something that's becoming a daily practice of the “Israeli” army."

The Palestine Red Crescent Society said seven people were "lightly injured" in the Jabalya attack. Jabalya is a Palestinian refugee camp.

Palestinian security officials also said “Israeli” troops had entered Palestinian-controlled territory in Gaza with tanks twice overnight into Wednesday.

Earlier, the Palestine Red Crescent Society said a 15-year-old Palestinian was shot and killed in clashes with “Israeli” soldiers overnight Wednesday in Gaza.

 

“Israel’s” Heavy Hand

Flak Jacket Saves French Journalist…(5)

Aguirre's flak jacket saved his life. He and the cameraman who say they were far away from the stone-throwers. They have no idea why the “Israeli” army would target them.

Ten days ago, “Israel’s” defense (war) minister said he was giving his field commanders a "free hand" to pursue Palestinian gunmen anywhere. But that free hand has become a heavy one.

'Not What We Intended'

In the past 10 days, “Israeli” troops have bombarded, shelled and machine-gunned dozens of Palestinian areas. Twenty-two Palestinians have been killed, including five policemen who were killed after “Israeli” soldiers opened fire on their checkpoint.

At first, “Israeli” spokesmen said the soldiers had been responding to Palestinian fire. Now, even the army admits there was no fire. “Israel’s” chief of staff told a government committee last night that "the result was not what we intended."

Just hours after voicing its regret, the “Israeli” army launched another attack on a Palestinian police station Wednesday.

"There is entire immunity here," says Akiva Eldar, a columnist for the Ha'Aretz newspaper. "The message is: OK, excuse me, I killed five soldiers but mistakes happen."

And it's doubtful, he says, that in this climate, many “Israelis” will even care.

This morning, “Israel’s” army killed 14-year-old Muhammad Salim during a clash at Netzarim Junction in central Gaza, according to officials at Shifa Hospital. He was throwing stones, the army says, and the soldiers shot him in the legs. But Muhammad died of chest wounds.

And the Palestinians who buried him today say he was simply walking home from school.
