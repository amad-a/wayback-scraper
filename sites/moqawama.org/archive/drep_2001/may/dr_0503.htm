Hizbullah says US report on terrorism has no credibility…(1)

“ The Lebanese movement Hizbullah said Tuesday that the annual report on terrorism released by the US State Department has no credibility since it makes no mention of "Zionist terrorism" in the Palestinian territories.

The report "takes on the point of view of the “Israeli” government and describes as terrorists the Palestinians who fight for their survival and their land, while it omits the daily slaughtering of children..., the razing of houses and the daily bombardments", the fundamentalist movement said.

"By omitting Zionist terrorism, perpetrated with US approval, the report loses all credibility", said the statement published by Hizbullah, which carried out the vast majority of anti-”Israeli” attacks in southern Lebanon before the May 2000 pull out. Hizbullah also criticized the fact that Washington described as "hostages" the three “Israeli” soldiers it captured in October in the Shibaa Farms area. The kidnapping was aimed at dealing a prisoner exchange with “Israel”, which still detains some twenty Lebanese.

"The US government seems to have forgotten that the heroes detained in Zionist cells who were fighting for the liberation of their land, while the soldiers of the invader are not hostages but aggressors", the movement added.

The US report pointed out that Hizbullah has not carried out any attacks against US targets since 1991, but said however that the group "still represents a significant terrorist threat to US interests in general from its bases in Lebanon".

 

Armenia backs Shibaa bid as Lahoud arrives with team…(2)

“ Armenian President Robert Kocharian received President Emile Lahoud for a state visit Wednesday, expressing support for Lebanon’s attempts to force “Israeli” troops out of the Shibaa Farms area.
Lahoud, who was accompanied by his wife Andree and an official delegation, arrived in the Armenian capital of Yerevan at noon, where he received an official welcome at the airport.
“I am deeply grateful for Armenia’s support for (our) attempts to liberate land from “Israeli” occupation,” he said. “And it is only natural for Lebanon to express similar support to Armenia in its own efforts to assert its sovereignty over its entire land.”
After talks held later, Kocharian spoke favorably about the “crucial” economic role played by Armenians in Lebanon and called for greater cooperation between the two governments.
He also congratulated Lahoud on the liberation of the South.
“We look forward to consolidating peace and justice in the Middle East area and for full compliance with United Nations resolutions,” he said, adding that his government supported Lebanon’s stand regarding the “Israeli”-occupied Shibaa Farms.
 

Addoum sets sights on Sharon and Peres…(3)

State Prosecutor Adnan Addoum issued criminal complaints Wednesday against “Israeli” Premier Ariel Sharon and Foreign Minister Shimon Peres.
Addoum told army intelligence on Wednesday to begin an “immediate” search for the two men and any of their known accomplices.
A source close to legal authorities in Beirut said that arrest warrants for the pair “will follow soon.” The warrants are then expected to be passed on to INTERPOL, and from there to all countries with which Lebanon has extradition treaties.
The documents, which charge both men with murder, as well as “acts of aggression” against Lebanon, are nonetheless expected to have only a limited effect.
Although the source admitted that little is expected of the move, “even if the arrest warrants are no implemented, they will cause the two international embarrassment.”
Addoum’s action followed recent legal proceedings begun by local attorney May Khansa.
Khansa’s case cited “Israel’s” 1982 invasion ­ a move which was spearheaded by Sharon, as well as his role in the 1982 Sabra and Shatila massacres, and last month’s air raid against a Syrian radar position in Dahr al-Baidar.
Khansa blamed Peres for the 1996 Qana massacre of more than 100 civilians in south Lebanon. She also accused him of making threatening remarks against the country during a recent news conference in Cyprus.

 

Lebanon civilian injured by “Israeli” mine blast…(4)

“ A Lebanese civilian was injured Wednesday by the explosion of a landmine left by “Israel” after its May 2000 withdrawal from southern Lebanon, police said.

Hassan Ali Ismail, 27, was driving his tractor in a field in the village of Baraasheet, east of the southern port city Tyre, when the mine exploded, they said.

On Sunday, two Lebanese civilians were killed in the separate explosions of an “Israeli” mine and a bomb in the formerly “Israeli”-occupied border zone of southern Lebanon.

Eleven Lebanese civilians have been killed and about 70 others injured or maimed by mines and other explosive devices left over since “Israeli” troops withdrew from southern Lebanon on May 24 last year.

The mines have also wounded a dozen members of the Lebanese army engaged in mine clearance.

According to United Nations estimates, “Israel” left at least 130,000 mines and unexploded shells in the former 850 square kilometer (330 square mile) occupied zone.

 

Mines are ‘obstacle’ to state in South…(5)

Baalbek-Hermel MP Marwan Fares said Wednesday that the state would be “unable to asserts its authority” in the South due the presence of more than 130,000 mines.
After a meeting of Parliament’s Human Rights committee, Fares said that UN Resolution 425 had yet to be implemented due to the presence of the mines, the occupation of the Shibaa Farms, and “Israel’s” failure to release around two dozen Lebanese detainees.
“Even if “Israel” hands over the maps of the minefields, I expect these maps to be ‘mined’’’,” Fares said, indicating that they would be misleading. The MP also praised initiatives by various countries, such as the United Arab Emirates, to help rid the former occupied areas of mines.

 

Exhibit at UNESCO will document trying years of “Israeli” occupation…(6)

More than 500 documents, maps, and photographs telling the story of occupation and liberation from 1978 to 2000 will be on exhibit at UNESCO Palace starting May 12.
Artist George Zeenny, the exhibition’s organizer, announced the event, titled “Liberation, a Lebanese victory for all Arabs” during a news conference at the Press Federation.
The event will begin roughly two weeks prior to the first anniversary of the liberation.
“I dedicate this exhibition to the martyrs, wounded, detained, kidnapped, tortured and surviving Lebanese,” Zeenny said.
The exhibition documents the crucial events Lebanon passed through from March 14, 1978, to May 25, 2000, particularly in the South.
Pictures of forces deployed in Lebanon during the war years, including the multi-national forces, “Israelis”, US Marines and others will be on display, featuring caricatures from Lebanon, Syria, Palestine and Iraq.
Zeenny told The Daily Star that the exhibition included documentary films such as Hanin, directed by Maroun Baghdadi, written by Unsi Hajj and narrated by Nadia Tueni; Khiam, directed by Juana and Khalil Jreije; Wild Flowers of the South, directed by John Chamoun and May Masri; and Qana, directed by Sayed Kaado.
Other exhibits relating to the Palestinian nakba (catastrophe) of 1948 will also be on display.
“The exhibition is designed in such a way as to attract the visitors’ sight, move their emotions and excite their minds,” he said, referring to his goals for those who visit his work.
He said that the exhibit would also contain a number of “rare” documents, which will occupy two large rooms measuring 25 by 13 meters each.
He urged young Lebanese, especially those under 40 years old, whom he said “don’t know much about their country,” to visit the exhibition. He argued that Lebanon’s history had to remain fresh in citizens’ minds.
“This is a new goal in my work, to refresh the Lebanese memory and strengthen the sense of national belonging.”
Zeenny currently heads the Middle East Culture League in London, where he has hosted Arab artists and writers such as Mahmoud Darwish, Nizar Qabbani, Amin Maalouf, and Marcel Khalifeh.
Zeenny has organized exhibitions on Lebanese history for more than a quarter-century, including exhibits at Beiteddine and Deir al-Qamar, and said he made every effort to remain objective in collecting and exhibiting documents.
He said funding for the exhibition’s $68,000 cost came from various sources, none of which was the government.
“People are helping me gain the Culture Ministry’s support,” he said. But “the government hasn’t responded and isn’t supporting my activities.”
The exhibition will last through May 16. It will then move to Sidon on May 25 and open in Damascus on June 5.

 

“Israeli” troops bulldoze Palestinian homes in Gaza; settlers criticize Sharon…(7)

“ “Israeli” bulldozers and tanks rumbled into a Palestinian refugee camp in Gaza on Wednesday, demolishing 20 houses said to have provided cover to gunmen and waging a nighttime firefight that killed a teenager.

Still, “Israel’s” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon came under sharp criticism that he's doing too little to halt Palestinian attacks as he visited a Jewish settlement where a resident was killed in a shooting.

Sharon travelled to the West Bank Jewish settlement of Ofra to pay condolences to the family of Assaf Hershkovitz, who was gunned down Tuesday while driving on a road repeatedly targeted by Palestinian militants. Hershkovitz's father was killed on the same road in January.

"Where's the peace?" said one placard brandished by residents at the settlement. "Prime minister, you promised security," said another.

Sharon told Hershkovitz's widow, Hila, that "people thought there was a solution that would take one day. There's no solution in one day."

She responded: "But there's not even a ray of light. We're in the same situation."

In Gaza, “Israeli” tanks fired shells and machine-gun rounds to cover the pre-dawn operation in Rafah, in southern Gaza along the border with Egypt, said Brig. Gen. Abdel Razek el-Majaida, a Palestinian security commander. Doctors said Mahmoud Aqel, 17, was killed and at least 15 Palestinians were wounded in a shootout.

Five “Israeli” bulldozers levelled 20 houses, three chicken farms and greenhouses, the Palestinian Authority said. The Palestinians fired automatic rifles and anti-tank rockets at the “Israeli” forces.

At daybreak, Palestinian residents went through the remains of their homes. A 13-year-old boy was shot in the cheek by “Israeli” soldiers positioned hundreds of metres away, Palestinian witnesses said.

Khalil Ibrahim, 37, said he told his family to flee their home when he saw the bulldozers coming at 2 a.m. Wednesday. The house was severely damaged but still standing when the troops withdrew.

"I don't think I can live in my house again because sooner or later they are going to attack it," Ibrahim said.

On several recent occasions, “Israel” has sent in bulldozers to tear down buildings that it says Palestinian gunmen used for cover. However, the moves have drawn international criticism, and “Israel” has withdrawn each time after carrying out the demolitions.

The actions illustrate Sharon's dilemma as he seeks to halt attacks against “Israelis”, but without attracting widespread condemnation from critics who accused the former general of using excessive force during his long career as a soldier and a politician.

In Maariv, a leading daily, editor Yaakov Erez wrote that Sharon, who assumed power two months ago, has not fulfilled his pledge to strengthen “Israel’s” security.

"The fighting with the Palestinians has turned from being a bothersome nuisance into a near war," Erez wrote. "This situation in which Sharon (does) nothing is a powder keg that could explode at any moment."

However, the Palestinians and many Arab supporters accuse Sharon of escalating the conflict and say he has no intent of hold serious peace negotiations. In recent interviews Sharon angered Palestinians by suggesting that even if peace talks resume he opposes more land transfers or any dismantling of settlements.

Syrian President Bashar Assad, in an interview with Spain's El Pais daily, called Sharon a "war criminal" who "likes to kill" and wants to push the region to war. Tensions with Syria have increased since “Israel” bombed a Syrian radar station in Lebanon last month.

Since fighting began last September, 431 people have been killed on the Palestinian side and 72 on the “Israeli” side.
