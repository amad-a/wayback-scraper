Two Lebanese arrested for spying in “Israel”: courts…(1)

““Israeli” authorities claimed they arrested two Lebanese and an Arab-”Israeli” on suspicion of spying for quote "hostile elements" in Lebanon. A publicity ban that had been imposed on the case by courts in Haifa was partially lifted but the identities of the men have not been revealed. “Israel” radio said the two Lebanese were members of “Israel’s” now defunct proxy militia in south Lebanon. Some 6,000 militiamen and their families retreated with withdrawing occupation troops from the south last May. The three men are suspected of passing on information to their alleged contacts in Lebanon, and were reportedly arrested in April. 
 

“Israeli” MPs denounce government bill to legitimize detention of Lebanese hostages…(2)

 The follow up committee for the release of Lebanese detained in “Israeli” prisons have urged the government to arrange meetings between families of detainees and UN Secretary General Kofi Annan. The group also called on Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud to file an official protest with the Security Council over “Israel’s” continued detention of Lebanese. Meanwhile, “Israeli” members of parliament harshly criticized a government-sponsored bill known as the "Dirani and "Obeid" law. It seeks to legitimize “Israel’s” continued detention of Lebanese resistance figures Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid and Mustafa Dirani, who were kidnapped from Lebanon in the late 1980s and early '90s. They are being held as hostages for use in any negotiations on the fate of an “Israeli” airman who went missing over Lebanon in 1986. “Israel’s” Supreme Court has already ruled in favor of releasing the two Lebanese figures but state and security forces are struggling to legalize their continued detention. Opponents of the bill warned it would only result in international condemnation. International human rights organizations have condemned “Israel’s” detention of Dirani, Obeid, and at least 17 other Lebanese without trial or charge, in clear breach of human rights conventions. The Hizbollah resistance movement has been holding three “Israeli” soldiers and a Mossad colonel to exchange them for the Lebanese and other Arab detainees. 
 

 Lebanese parties back Assad's comments on Jews…(3)

“Lebanese parties and groups welcomed Tuesday the comments made by Syrian President Bashar al-Assad during the visit of Pope John Paul II, which were slammed as antisemitic by “Israel”.

A statement from the parties and groups said they held a meeting at the Lebanese movement Hizbullah's information bureau to "assess the results of the pope's visit to Syria".

"The participants paid homage to President Assad's speech, who only reminded us of the truth about Zionism, its hostility towards the Christians and Muslims and its insistance on aggravating the suffering of the Palestinian people", the statement said.

Assad faced accusations of anti-semitism, particularly from “Israel” itself, after he accused the “Israelis” of "trying to kill religions in the same way (the Jews) betrayed Jesus Christ, in the same way they tried to kill the prophet Mohammad."

His remarks were criticised by France and the United States.

 

 “Stop the killing of children. What have we done to deserve death?”…(4)

Several hundred Palestinian schoolchildren demonstrated at the Ain al-Hilweh refugee camp near Sidon Tuesday, holding a silent march to protest the death of a Palestinian girl killed by “Israeli” shrapnel.
Carrying a small coffin, the children mourned 4-month-old Iman Hajjour, a Palestinian girl who died Monday when “Israeli” forces shelled a refugee camp in the Gaza Strip.
Hajjour, who became the youngest victim of the seven-month long intifada, was killed in an “Israeli” attack on the Khan Younis refugee camp.
The children also carried a banner that read: “Stop the killing of children. What have we done to deserve death?”
Other banners called on the international community to stop the killing of innocent children and send United Nations observers to protect them from “Israeli” attacks.
Children wearing kuffieh raised black flags and Palestinian flags and sang the Palestinian anthem.
The General Union of Palestinian Women, which had organized the demonstration, issued a statement condemning the killing and additionally demanding that the international community judge “Israelis” as war criminals.
The union also organized a similar protest in Tyre, outside the headquarters of UNIFIL, while in the Beirut suburb of Bourj al-Barajneh, some 300 children shouted anti-”Israel” slogans before burning and stomping on an “Israeli” flag.

 

­Popular Resistance Committees Continues Operations Against “Israeli” Occupation Despite Arafat's Freeze…(5)

In response to the mounting “Israeli” aggressions against the Palestinian people, Fatah Movement decided to reactivate the Popular Resistance Committees to confront the “Israeli” occupation army’s incursions into the Palestinian territories, and other aggressions. Following serious “Israeli” tanks and artillery attacks, which caused several fatalities - including a 4-month-old Palestinian baby - on Monday, Fatah held an urgent meeting to discuss the issue. Palestinian Chairman Yasser Arafat had ordered last week a freezing of the committees’ activities. However, the Popular Resistance Committees, which include various Palestinian factions, affirmed it shall continue its operations against the Zionist occupation, and promised the Nazi government of Sharon to turn all occupation spots into unsafe places. The committee said it remains independent from affiliation to any Palestinian faction, and takes its decisions as an independent body. It called on the various Palestinian factions including the Fatah Movement to respect this independence.

 

Palestinians bury infant killed by “Israeli” fire

“Wrapped in a white shroud, a 4-month-old Palestinian girl killed by “Israeli” shelling was buried Tuesday as thousands of mourners chanted "Long live Palestine."

In the Gaza Strip refugee camp of Deir el Balah, about 7,000 mourners laid to rest 4-month-old Iman Hijo. Her body was wrapped in a white shroud and draped with flowers. About 3,000 mourners chanted "Long live Iman" and "Long live Palestine." Many carried posters of Iman. Dozens of gunmen fired in the air.

Mourners carried the baby's father, Mohammed, 21, a Palestinian policeman who has been using crutches since being injured three months ago in clashes with “Israeli” troops. "I want to spit in the face of this ugly world," the father said before the funeral cortege left the family home.

Late Monday, Arafat visited some of the injured from the shelling attack, kissing or patting their hands.

"The “Israelis” have to know that we are the strongest nation, the strongest people," he said. "And this criminal, dangerous “Israeli” military escalation will not ... force us to accept any conditions or make compromises on any single meter of land and our Christian and Islamic holy sites."

 

Two “Israeli” Teenagers Found Dead in Cave…(7)

“The bodies of two teenagers from a Jewish settlement were found in a cave in the Judean Desert on Wednesday, and “Israeli” police said they suspected Palestinians had stoned them to death.

The circumstances of the deaths of the two 14-year-olds were not clear and there was no immediate comment from Palestinian officials. Some local officials said the killings may have been linked with the theft of about 100 goats during the night.

A caller to Reuters claimed responsibility for the killings in the name of an Islamic militant group. But the call could not be authenticated.

“Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon expressing shock at the deaths, called them an ``additional escalation'' in the seven-month-old Palestinian uprising against “Israeli” occupation of the West Bank and Gaza Strip.

``Prime Minister Ariel Sharon has expressed his deep shock and condemns the criminal murder of the two youths from Gush Etzion and sends his sincere condolences to the two families,'' Sharon's office said in a statement.

The teenagers' bodies were found by a search party near the Tekoa settlement, in the Gush Etzion settlement bloc about eight miles south of Al-Quds.

The youths disappeared on Tuesday while hiking along a river bed about 765.5 yards from the settlement.

A police spokesman said an initial examination of the bodies indicated that the youths had been stoned and beaten to death.

``It appears that Arabs, Palestinians killed them,'' he said.

An anonymous caller told Reuters by telephone that the killings were to avenge the death of a four-month-old baby killed by shrapnel during an “Israeli” barrage of fire in Gaza on Monday and the killing of an Islamic Jihad militant on Saturday.

He gave no details and the authenticity of the call could not be confirmed.

 

Settler shot dead guarding West Bank outpost…(8)

One “Israeli” was killed and several Palestinians were injured in another day of violence in the territories yesterday.
Arye (Orlando) Agranouni, 48, a resident of Itamar, was murdered early yesterday morning on the outskirts of the West Bank settlement. Agranouni was standing guard over a lone caravan on the settlement's eastern hill, police said, when at about 1 A.M., two Palestinians approached and fired several times with a Kalashnikov rifle. His body was found half an hour later. Contrary to initial reports, which said the body had been stabbed and abused, it was unmarked. Agranouni's M-16 rifle was apparently stolen by the killers.
Police investigators found footsteps leading from the site of the murder toward the nearby Palestinian village of Hirbat Yanoun. But an “Israeli” Army source said it seems likely that the killers picked up a car on the road and drove elsewhere.
Both police and “Israeli” Army sources said the outpost Agranouni was guarding, located 7.5 kilometers from the main settlement, was illegal. Itamar residents rejected this claim, saying the caravan was within the settlement's legal boundaries.
Several settlers demonstrated on the roads yesterday to protest the murder, blocking the Tapuah Junction for an hour with burning tires. Agranouni was buried yesterday afternoon.
Palestinian sources said that in response to the murder, the “Israeli” Army beefed up its presence in nearby Palestinian villages.
 

Senior officer: Mortars will be fired at W. Bank…(9)

 It is only a matter of time before Palestinians in the West Bank begin firing mortars and RPGs at Jewish targets in Judea and Samaria, a senior “Israeli” Army officer warned yesterday.
He noted that vast quantities of arms and ammunition have been stockpiled by various Palestinian groups in the West Bank, especially in the northern area.
"We know they have the equipment, and I wouldn't be surprised if we see such attacks, " he said. "Everyone has mortars, whether within the ranks of the Palestinian Authority or in other Palestinian groups. But it is not clear whether it will be in their interest to fire them."
He warned, however, that in the event of such an attack “Israel” must retaliate harshly and not restrict itself to pinpointing the specific site of the firing. "Our response... must be more than just deterrence," he said.
Prime Minister Ariel Sharon disclosed yesterday that three seaborne weapons shipments may have reached the Palestinians before a fourth was intercepted by the navy on Sunday.
 

“Israeli”: U.S. afraid to invest in “Israel” until violence ends…(10)

“ American companies are afraid to invest in “Israel” because of months of fighting in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, “Israel’s” finance minister said Tuesday.

However, Silvan Shalom said a recent slump in “Israel’s” high-tech sector which accounted for about a third of “Israel’s” growth last year had more to do with the decline of the Nasdaq stock market than an upsurge in bloodshed.

"Nasdaq affects us more than Nablus," he told the Associated Press in an interview, referring to a town in the West Bank where much of the violence of the Intifada, or Palestinian uprising, has taken place.

"The Intifada, of course, affects us as well. But not like it looks when you are watching television," he said.

Shalom arrived in the United States on Monday for a three-day visit to try to win back U.S. investors. He has met with American companies, investment banks and credit agencies.

He said “Israel”, which has 120 companies listed on the Nasdaq, was "a super power in high-tech" and American firms were still interested in investing. However, he said they were cautious due to the violence.

"They want to wait a little bit and see what will be in the near future," Shalom said.

Before leaving “Israel”, Shalom said the government was working on a series of tax incentives to draw investors back to the high-tech sector, which employs about one in 20 “Israelis”.

Venture capital firms in “Israel” raised a record $3.2 billion last year, but analysts predict that figure will fall by half this year. “Israel’s” Manufacturers Association recently forecast that growth in the high-tech industry would drop from 45 percent last year to 10-15 percent in 2001.

The high-tech slowdown is triggering the start of a recession in “Israel”, according to economists, and unemployment is around 9 percent.

During his visit, Shalom also met with officials from the World Bank in Washington. He said he pressured the bank to delay disbursing $750 million in new loans to Iran.

He said that although the money was intended for infrastructure projects, it would be used to build nuclear weapons, which may be targeted at “Israel”.

Last May, the bank ignored U.S. opposition and approved $232 million in loans to Iran for the first time in seven years.

Since September, “Israeli”-Palestinian fighting has claimed 437 lives on the Palestinian side and 73 on the “Israeli” side.

 

Denmark Called on the Zionist Entity to Halt "Unacceptable Violence" Against Palestinians…(11)

Danish Foreign Minister Mogens Lykketoft sharply called on "“Israel”" to end “unacceptable violence,” halt settlement activity and lift its blockade of the Palestinian territories. This came after talks with “Israeli” enemy Foreign Minister Shimon Peres at a meeting of European Social Democrats in Berlin. Embarrassed, Peres replied by claiming there was no blockade but rather only a closure which was now being lifted and that no new settlements were being built. He said a four-stage process was needed to restart talks with the Palestinians: Reduce what he called violence and terror; Confidence building measures; Cooling off; Return to negotiations.
