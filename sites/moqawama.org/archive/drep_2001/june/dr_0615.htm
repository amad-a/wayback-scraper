Hizbullah ‘will fight’ to ensure security…(1)

A Hizbullah official said Thursday that firing on “Israeli” warplanes flying over Lebanon was “the beginning of a new phase,” asserting that the resistance movement was still in a strong position.
Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, the group’s southern commander, made the remarks at a celebration in Bint Jbeil marking the graduation of computer trainees.
On Tuesday, Hizbullah’s anti-aircraft batteries fired on “Israeli” warplanes flying over the western sector of the border district in what a party source described as the beginning of a “new retaliatory policy.” The source said the party would also retaliate against any “Israeli” troops who shoot civilians on the Lebanese side of the border.
“Even though Lebanon urged the international community to see to it that “Israel” stop its aggression, nobody has heard a voice calling on the enemy to put an end to this challenge of Lebanese sovereignty,” Qaouk said. “The resistance movement had to retaliate to tell the world that we in Lebanon, following Hizbullah’s victory, do not beg for our security, but instead extract security and protection for civilians by force.”

 

“Israelis” fire on Lebanese villages in Shibaa Farms: police…(2)

“ “Israeli” occupation forces in the disputed Shibaa Farms area opened heavy machine-gun fire Thursday on Lebanese villages, causing no injuries, the Lebanese police said.

“Israeli” soldiers posted in Sammapa and Roueisset Naaman fired for half an hour Thursday at dawn on Shibaa and Kfarshouba, villages near the eastern sector of the "blue line" drawn by the UN to separate Lebanon from “Israel”, police said.

Meanwhile, the “Israeli” army continued the fortification work it started Wednesday on five positions in the Shibaa Farms.

 

BBC film on Sharon's role in massacre angers “Israel”…(3)

“Israel” is seething over a BBC documentary investigating whether Prime Minister Ariel Sharon should be tried for war crimes over the massacre of hundreds of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon two decades ago.
Justice Minister Meir Sheetrit said the Panorama programme, due to be aired on Sunday, was proof of the BBC's "anti-”Israel”" and "pro-Palestinian bias," “Israeli” media reported. Sharon, then defence (war) minister, was found indirectly responsible for the massacre at the Palestinian refugee camps of Sabra and Shatilla during “Israel’s” ill-fated 1982 invasion of Lebanon, and forced to resign.
“Israeli” army radio said efforts were being made to prevent the airing of the programme, titled "The Accused." However, an “Israeli” official said that while the government had voiced its displeasure over the programme, no formal protest had been raised with the BBC. "We are expressing our dismay at the programme, considering the fact that Mr. Sharon is engaged now in peace efforts on behalf of “Israel” and that no direct responsibility in the Sabra and Shatilla massacre was attributed to Mr. Sharon, never," the official told AFP on condition of anonymity.
"It is obvious it is not going to be objective and journalistic but rather a speedy trial by television," the official said, adding that “Israel” considered the BBC had prepared the programme in such a way that the government was not able to "react properly." The BBC said it "regrets" the “Israeli” reaction "but will continue to report events in “Israel” and the surrounding region to the best of our ability. "We are proud of our record of impartial reporting of Middle East affairs and stand by the journalism in this Sunday's Panorama," it said.
The BBC's website said the Panorama presenter talks to key witnesses and survivors of the "rampage of murder, torture and rape" that left at least 800 people dead and provoked international outrage.
"In the light of developments in international war crimes prosecutions the programme asks whether the evidence should lead to indictments for what happened in the camps," it says. In January, before his landslide election as prime minister, the 73-year-old former general expressed his regrets for what he said was a "terrible tragedy," but refused to apologise. "To apologize for what?" Sharon told the Arab “Israeli” weekly newspaper Kul-al-Arab.

Lebanon jails Palestinian for spying for “Israel”…(4)

“ A former senior Palestinian official who turned spy for “Israel” after being held for 11 years in the Jewish state, and later spent six years in a Syrian prison, was jailed for 15 years by a Beirut military court Thursday, a judicial source said.

Khaled Mahmud Yassin, 57, was handed over in December 2000 to the Lebanese among some 50 Lebanese and Palestinians carrying Lebanese documents who had been arrested by Syrian intelligence.

He had originally been jailed for life in “Israel” after being captured in 1973 during an abortive attack he carried out as a "colonel" in the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine (DFLP).

He confessed to the Beirut court that he had been turned by “Israeli” intelligence when the FDLP failed to include his name on a list of prisoners exchanged in 1984.

“Israel” announced that he had been hospitalised when in reality he was being trained for four months in espionage methods before being expelled to Lebanon "for health reasons".

Yassin sent back information by short wave radio to “Israeli” intelligence, in particular on Palestinian activists in Lebanese refugee camps.

He also admitted feeding false information to Lebanese intelligence.

In 1990 he secured the expulsion of Palestinians from Libya after informing Egypt's embassy in Bulgaria that some of them were planning an attack in Cairo, the judicial source said.

Under Lebanese law the length of time he spent in a Syrian prison will be deducted from his 15-year sentence in Lebanon.

Jewish group claims responsibility for Palestinians’ murder…(5)

In a courageous and challenging operation, Palestinian Mujahed Hassan Abou Sha'ra persuaded the lieutenant-colonel in the Shin Bit “Israeli” security apparatus to a tunnel road and opened fire killing him and wounding his guard seriously in the neck. Exclusive information to al-Manar Channel reported that the officer is called Yehuda Afri, nick named Boody. He is responsible for what is called zone 504. He masterminded a number of assassination operations against Palestinian resistance fighters. Later the “Israeli” army rushed to the place and shot dead Abou Shar'a. Martyr Abu Shar'a is father of 3 children and used to work in a hotel in Gilo settlement in occupied Quds (Al-Quds). Badr Forces, Martyr Hussein Obeyat Brigades and Al-Aqsa Martyrs Brigades claimed responsibility for the operation. No more than a few hours after the ceasefire was announced, a group of settlers, named Shelhevet-Gilad opened fire on four Palestinians, killing one and wounding the other three. Martyr Auni al-Haddad, 42, was driving his van overnight when the enemy fired more than 15 bullets on the car. His three sons were wounded as the vehicle diverted from its path northeast of Quds. Palestinian mujaheddeen earlier Thursday bombarded with mortar shells the “Israeli” settlements of Rafah Yam in the Gaza Strip, while they shelled Netzarim settlement with automatic weapons. Late Wednesday they opened fire on 3 “Israeli” patrols in West Bank towns of Ramallah and Nablus and targeted an enemy outpost in Neve Dekalim in the Gaza Strip.

 

Senior Palestinian Officials Blocked At an Occupation Checkpoint…(7)

The Palestinian Authority issued a statement following a late Wednesday meeting in the West Bank town of Ramallah, saying the “understanding on a ceasefire constitutes part of a comprehensive plan that includes application of all the recommendations of the Mitchell Commission report, consisting of a freeze in (Jewish) settlement, application of signed accords (and) the resumption of negotiations toward a final settlement based on resolutions 242 and 338." The statement added: “We hope to see this understanding applied on the ground with the lifting of all restrictions” that "“Israel”" has imposed on the Palestinian territories and “a return to the situation before September 28.” It did not specifically confirm a statement earlier by “Israeli” enemy war Minister Binyamin ben Eliezer that the ceasefire had formally gone into effect at 3:00 pm (1200 GMT). Ben Eleizer said the army has started redeployment in the Palestinian territories and some roadblocks had been removed in line with a US-brokered ceasefire. However, Palestinian Housing Minister Abdel Rahman Hamad, said nine senior Palestinian officials were blocked for an hour late Wednesday at an “Israeli” military checkpoint as they tried to re-enter the Gaza Strip after a meeting in the West Bank. Hamad described the incident as a “provocation”. Witnesses said the concrete blocks had started being removed at some checkpoints, while the army removed two tanks from a roadblock on the Gaza Strip’s main highway. But two jeeps were still in place, holding up Palestinian traffic when settler and army traffic passed through the crossroads to the Netzarim Jewish settlement. Palestinian commander of forces in the northern Gaza Strip Saeb al-Ajez did not consider the removal of the tanks to be a lightening of the blockade as cement blocks and iron speed bumps had not been removed. The tanks had been removed to around 300 metres (yards) away, adding that they might return any time. Meanwhile, CIA director George Tenet will hold a meeting 48 hours after the start of the ceasefire to review the status on the ground since then. Five days later, the “Israelis” and Palestinians would meet again to review steps that each had taken to consolidate the ceasefire. That would be followed by a period of six weeks, during which the two sides would apply confidence-building measures. Security cooperation would also resume at the level of liaison offices. At the end of the six weeks, the so-called peace talks should resume in line with the recommendations of the US-led Mitchell commission.

 

Factionalism threatens “Israel’s” survival, warns British chief rabbi…(8)
Rabbi Sacks set on repairing the divisions between “Israel” and world Jewry

Visiting “Israel” today reminds British chief rabbi Prof. Jonathan Sacks of the Second Temple period, when Jewish sovereignty collapsed for the second time. He finds the same kind of factionalism present now, and he is worried.
The religious, cultural and ethnic divisions in “Israel” disturb Sacks greatly. "The real rifts in “Israeli” society which existed in 1948 still exist today, and have rarely been addressed," he says. "In many ways," says Sacks, the feeling in "“Israel” today is not unlike the climate in the Second Temple period."
Sacks - whose official title is Chief Rabbi of the United Hebrew Congregations of the Commonwealth - prefers to travel to “Israel” as an academic, not as a rabbi. "I feel I can communicate with people more freely if I come as an academic," he says. He was in “Israel” to celebrate the opening on Tuesday of a center for Jewish study in honor of his predecessor, Lord Immanuel Jakobovits. The inauguration took place on British Day, during the 46th Bar-Ilan University Board of Trustees meeting.
The factionalism within “Israeli” society is a great irony, Sacks continues, because “Israel” has always been surrounded by massive empires. All of them disappeared, yet "Am Yisrael hai" (the Jewish people lives). "Only one people have ever been capable of threatening the Jewish people," he says, "and that's the Jewish people." He believes "our historic inability to live together without deep division" has been "our greatest weakness" in the past and it is "appalling that it continues today without alarm bells ringing."
Sacks had expected a "major national conversation" to be sparked in “Israel” by the murder of former prime minister Yitzhak Rabin. "I thought the religious and secular would sit together and say 'we've been strangers for too long and we need to understand each other better.'" He grants this did occur in small, local ways, yet failed to "capture the national imagination."
Addressing this issue has been something of a project for Sacks. A year ago, he was the driving force behind a large conference at the Hebrew University of Al-Quds, which drew 90 leading academics from “Israel” and 16 from Diaspora communities. Its goal was to create dialogue between religious, cultural and secular figures both inside and outside “Israel”. He admits to being unequivocally disappointed with the outcome. Last month, he took a different tack. At Bar-Ilan University, Sacks engaged in a public dialogue - in Hebrew - with author Amos Oz, whom he describes as "a secular prophet in the great prophetic tradition."
Not that Sacks' own community is without its share of division. Five years ago, a national storm erupted around Sacks himself, when he failed to attend the funeral of a highly respected leading Reform rabbi and Holocaust survivor, Hugo Gryn.
"We are trying to heal things," says Sacks, who has held his position for close to a decade. He asserts that the atmosphere between the different denominations of Anglo Jewry has "improved markedly after going through some difficult points." He credits quiet consultative procedures and his own links with non- Orthodox rabbis with "sending a signal out to most members of the community." But this shift, he says, is "going against the tide of the Jewish world."
Sacks has identified another change within world Jewry: "the exposure of a certain ambivalence about “Israel”" in the Diaspora. Like many others, he cites the Six-Day War as a time when it was easy for Jews around the world to identify with “Israel”. But now that the Western media portrays “Israel” as Goliath, Sacks says there is no doubt that some sectors of the community have become more ambivalent about supporting “Israel”. He was not willing to elaborate on the degree of ambivalence that applies in each sector, nor on the factors that affect this dynamic.
Nevertheless, it is a shift that Sacks is aiming to fight. Since the Al-Aqsa Intifada broke out last September, he has led two missions to “Israel”. One, with 52 British rabbis, was aimed at allowing them to return to their communities and offer alternatives to the "hammering" “Israel” has taken from the British press. His second mission intended to address this point directly by bringing a group of non-Jewish journalists to “Israel”. The experience, he says, highlighted to him the "weakness of hasbara [government PR] in “Israel”."
Sacks seems pleased that no British groups organizing visits of Jewish teenagers to “Israel” this summer have canceled en masse - to date. He attributes this to the fact that many of teenagers' parents came to “Israel” as kids - unlike, in the main, their American counterparts. He firmly believes in sending Jewish youth for serious study experiences in “Israel”, "as nothing creates as strong a bond as actually being here." For the last 50 years, he says, “Israel” was the "city of refuge" for the Jewish world. He hopes it will become "an ongoing seminar in Jewish identity" because "you can't understand what it is to be a Jew until you've come to “Israel”."
But in “Israel”, other qualities are required, say Sacks. “Israel” has needed strength - military, political and economic - since before the inception of the state. But the next generation will need spiritual power; he warns that the nation's strength of character will be "tested to the limits" in coming years. Sacks points out that in Hebrew, there are two words for strength: koah, the strength to overcome your opponent, and gvura, the strength to control yourself. Koah, he continues, is the strength to wage war and gvura is the strength to wage peace. He says “Israel” has shown "extraordinary strength of both kinds," which is why he believes it will eventually make peace.
Meanwhile, however, he cites his favorite prayer, of East European origin, which goes something like this: "Oh Lord, I know you will help me, but could you please help me until you help me?.
