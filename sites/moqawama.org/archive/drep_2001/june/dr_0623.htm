“Israel” frets as reporters enter Ghajar…(1)

The “Israeli” Army may be reviving a plan to annex Ghajar, according to security sources, a move prompted by five local reporters entering the Lebanese end of the divided village for the first time since “Israel’s” withdrawal.
Ali Shaib, a reporter with Hizbullah’s Al-Manar television station and one of the five to enter Ghajar, said they had seen “Israeli” tourists in the Lebanese two-thirds of the village.
“We saw their tour buses with Hebrew writing on the sides,” he told The Daily Star.
“The residents were scared and told us we should leave before the “Israelis” came to take us,” he added.
Ghajar is split by the United Nations-delineated Blue Line with two-thirds falling on the Lebanese side and the remainder inside “Israeli”-occupied Syria.
After protests by Ghajar’s residents last summer, the “Israeli” Army agreed not to physically divide the village with the sophisticated electric fence being constructed along the 110-kilometer frontier.
Access to the Alawite village from the Lebanese side is limited. The northern end is surrounded by an old border fence erected by the “Israelis” during the occupation of south Lebanon. Minefields adjacent to the fence also hamper access to Ghajar.
The only gate in the old fence lies 1.5 kilometers to the east at Abbassieh. But Indian UNIFIL troops man an observation post by the gate and block anyone from passing through.
Reporters in the past have approached Ghajar from Abbassieh by walking along a narrow footpath between the old fence and the minefields.
Shaib said he and his colleagues climbed through a hole in the fence to enter the village.
There is an understanding between the government, UNIFIL and the “Israelis” that Lebanon will not exert its authority over the village so long as the “Israelis” keep out.
But the “Israeli” Army believes that the absence of a fence makes Ghajar a weak link in its chain of defenses along the frontier.
“Israel’s” Radio speculated the reporters’ visit was part of a plan by Hizbullah to test the reaction of “Israeli” forces stationed in the southern end of the village.
“Ghajar is a recognized breach of the border,” the radio said. “This no doubt will continue to cause anxiety to security officials on the northern border, as well as on the political level who do not know how to prevent this.”
Experienced security sources in the South suspect that the “Israeli” Army has not abandoned its plans to seal off the village from the rest of Lebanon.
One foreign observer, familiar with the “Israel” radio report, suggested the “Israeli” Army “might be exaggerating the case to create a pretext for physical measures on the Lebanese side of the village.”
“They are going to put up something there, no doubt about it,” commented a UNIFIL officer. “Otherwise, why all this fuss?”

 

Lebanon to present resolution to OIC…(2)

A draft resolution will be presented to the Organization of the Islamic Conference meeting opening Monday in Mali to gather support for Lebanon’s stand in the conflict with “Israel”, government sources said Friday. The draft will demand the release of detainees held in “Israeli” jails and affirm Lebanon’s right to compensation for human and material losses resulting from “Israeli” attacks.
It will condemn “Israel’s” continued aggression against Lebanon, including the recent attack on a Syrian military radar post. In addition, the draft will urge the international community to exert pressure on “Israel” to hand over maps of land mines planted during its occupation.

 

Settlers torch Arab land as US steps up ceasefire effort…(3)

Dozens of Jewish settlers blocked several key West Bank roads and set a Palestinian field ablaze yesterday in anger at killings which have threatened a delicate US-brokered Middle East ceasefire. Settlers also stoned the Palestinian village of Sinjil in the West Bank, witnesses said.

Palestinians have shot dead four Jewish settlers on West Bank roads and six Palestinians have been killed in the nine days since the sides agreed to the plan of the CIA director, George Tenet, to end nearly nine months of bloodshed. At least 461 Palestinians, 116 “Israelis” and 13 “Israeli” Arabs have been killed since the Palestinian revolt against “Israeli” occupation began in September last year.

Yesterday the US special envoy William Burns and European Union foreign policy chief Javier Solana met the “Israeli” Foreign Minister, Shimon Peres. The two are also to meet the Palestinian President Yasser Arafat. The US Secretary of State, Colin Powell, is due in the region next week to throw his weight behind the truce.

The “Israeli” army said a roadside bomb exploded near a tank in Gaza yesterday but no one was wounded. Nine petrol bombs were thrown at troops in Hebron, on the West Bank. Again there were no injuries.

Villagers in Silet al-Daher on the West Bank said “Israeli” soldiers firing rubber-coated metal bullets wounded three stone-throwing Palestinians protesting at a curfew imposed since the killing of a settler at the nearby Homesh settlement earlier this week. The army said it used only stun grenades. Medical officials said a Palestinian woman was hit in the back by a teargas grenade.

Some 200,000 settlers live among three million Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, on land “Israel” captured in the 1967 Middle East war. The international community considers the settlements illegal.

 

2 “Israeli” Soldiers, Bomber Killed…(4)

Two “Israeli” soldiers and a Palestinian bomber were killed in an explosion in the Gaza Strip on Friday when the troops were lured to a booby-trapped jeep by Palestinians pretending to be in distress, the army said. Elsewhere in Gaza, two Palestinian teens were critically wounded by “Israeli” fire.

The Islamic militant group Hamas claimed responsibility for the explosion, announcing the name of the suicide martyrdom bomber over mosque loud speakers in Gaza City. The bomber was also killed.

Prime Minister Ariel Sharon's adviser, Raanan Gissin, said the Palestinian Authority had to take immediate action to prevent further attacks on “Israelis”. ''Our patience is running out. If no action will be taken by them we will have to draw necessary conclusions and take the action ourselves,'' Gissin said.

The two soldiers were killed near the Jewish settlement of Dugit in the northern Gaza Strip.

The army said a patrol was passing an abandoned house and heard Palestinians calling for help. Two soldiers got out of their jeep and when they approached the house, a jeep parked nearby exploded, killing them and the Palestinian inside. A third soldier came under Palestinian fire and was lightly hurt, the army said.

The blast was so powerful that only an axle was left of the boobytrapped jeep. A bloodied helmet lay in the sand nearby. One soldier standing near the wreckage fought back tears as he was comforted by a friend.

Palestinian security officials said soldiers in the area shot tank and heavy machine-gun fire in response, injuring three Palestinians, two moderately and one lightly. Hamas said in an announcement read in mosques in the Shata refugee camp in Gaza Strip that the bomber killed was Ismail Maoussabie, 27.

In a video clip taped before the attack, Maoussabie made a speech while holding a Kalashnikov gun with Hamas flags draped behind him.

''I am one among hundreds of martyrs in waiting who are waiting to meet God and who are able to reach the Zionists where ever they are,'' Maoussabie said.

The explosion occurred a few hundred yards from a beach where about 250 “Israelis” were bathing at the time, Adam said.

Near the Jewish settlement of Ganei Tal in southern Gaza, two 18-year-old Palestinians were seriously wounded by army fire, one with a shot to the chest and another with a bullet in the head, Palestinian doctors said. Palestinian residents said they heard an explosion before the shots. The circumstances of the incident were not clear and the army did not immediately comment.

As the bloodshed persisted, both sides remained skeptical the truce would stick. “Israeli” and Palestinian security commanders reported no progress in a meeting late Thursday.

Since the outbreak of hostilities in late September, 495 people have been killed on the Palestinian side and 117 on the “Israeli” side.
