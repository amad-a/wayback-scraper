Belgian court considers charges against Sharon…(1)

Survivors of the 1982 massacre at the Sabra and Shatila refugee camps opened legal proceedings against “Israeli” Premier Ariel Sharon in Belgium on Monday, accusing him of crimes against humanity.
The 28 survivors presented their case against Sharon and others alleged to have been involved in the massacres to an investigating magistrate under Belgian laws that permit the country’s courts to try war crimes committed abroad.
The judge will now determine whether Sharon, then “Israel’s” defense (war) minister, and the others should face charges.
The launch of the case coincides with uproar in “Israel” over the airing of a BBC program which discussed whether Sharon would be indictable for war crimes over Sabra and Shatila.
Souad Srour, who was paralyzed in the attack, read a statement to reporters of the horror she suffered at the hands of “Israeli” soldiers.
“We heard someone knock at the door. My father asked ‘Who is it?’ And they said, ‘We’re “Israelis” and we want to search the house.’ My father opened the door and there were 13 armed soldiers,” Srour said. “Afterward they started firing at us. My little sister was shot in the head, and my father was hit in the chest but he was still alive … . But me, I was paralyzed right away and I could no longer move.
“They raped me, one after the other, right in front of my father. They shot at me ­ I was hit on the left hand ­ and then they left,” she added. “They came back later that night and were furious to see I was still alive … They shot me twice more.”
Srour, now 36, walks with two canes and still has nightmares about the incident.
Sharon had to resign as defense minister after an “Israeli” inquiry found him indirectly responsible for the killings.
Belgian lawyer Michael Verhaeghe, who is representing the survivors, said there was “sufficient evidence” to convict those responsible. “The facts in this case undeniably reveal crimes against humanity,” he said.
If the judge decides to press charges, Sharon could be arrested if he enters Belgium. But lawyers said that as a serving head of government, he would likely enjoy immunity.
The plaintiffs said they could also press similar charges against Sharon in other countries.
The move was praised on Monday by Palestinians living in refugee camps. Author Raafat Murra, who has written about the killings, said he supported the case even though he doubted it would lead to charges.
“It is enough that this would make international noise, annoy Sharon and expose him as the murderer and butcher that he really is,” Murra said.
Meanwhile, the “Israeli” Foreign Ministry said that the BBC’s program was an attempt to “tarnish “Israel” and its leader.”
The BBC said it asked Sharon to comment for its program, The Accused, part of its Panorama series. His aide, Ranaan Gissin, gave an on-camera interview on Sharon’s behalf, saying the “Israelis” had never expected their Phalangist militia allies would massacre the refugees.
One of the experts interviewed was Richard Falk, professor of international law at Princeton University in the United States, who had been vice-chairman of an international commission that investigated the 1982 invasion.
“There is no question in my mind that he (Sharon) is indictable for the knowledge he had or should have had,” Falk said. “Sharon’s specific command responsibility arises from the fact that he was the minister of defense in touch with the field commanders, that he actually was present there in Beirut.”
Sharon’s lawyer, Dov Weissglass, was evasive when asked by “Israel” Radio whether the premier would consider filing a libel suit against the BBC.
“Israeli” officials have in the past accused the BBC of being slanted against “Israel” and occasionally even anti-Semitic.
The BBC said it stood by the program. The reporter, Fergal Keane, wrote in The Independent newspaper Saturday that “I have nothing but contempt” for those who suggest investigating the massacres indicates a pro-Palestinian bias. ­

Sharon's role in Lebanon massacre returns to haunt him 20 years on…(2)

“Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon's dark past returned to haunt him Monday as a controversial British documentary revisited his role in the massacre of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon two decades ago and a survivor sought to bring war crime charges against him.

In a courtroom thousands of miles away from the scene of her ordeal, Suad Srur el-Marai relived her terror when Lebanese Christian militiamen went on the rampage in the Sabra and Shatilla refugee camps during the “Israeli” invasion of Lebanon in 1982.

El Marai, who was raped and left paralysed in the attack on her family, is one of a group of survivors seeking to press charges in a Brussels court against Sharon for crimes against humanity.

"We heard someone knock at the door. My father asked 'Who is it?' And they said, 'We're “Israelis” and we want to search the house.' My father opened the door and there were 13 armed soldiers," Srur said in a statement to reporters at the court.

"Afterwards they started firing at us. My little sister was shot in the head, and my father was hit in the chest but he was still alive ... But me, I was paralysed right away and I could no longer move," she continued.

"They raped me, one after the other, right in front of my father. They shot at me -- I was hit on the left hand -- and then they left.

"They came back later that night and were furious to see I was still alive ... they shot me twice more," said Srur, now 36, who walks with two canes and still has nightmares about the incident.

The complaint hinges on a 1993 law that makes it possible for Belgian courts to try cases of genocide and other crimes against humanity that took place in foreign countries.

Sharon, a hawkish former general known as the "Bulldozer," was declared unfit to be defence minister after an “Israeli” commission of inquiry in 1983 found him indirectly responsible for the killings during the invasion he orchestrated.

His career was blighted for years by the scandal, and his landslide win in the February election in “Israel” over Labour prime minister Ehud Barak had seemed all but impossible only weeks beforehand.

But Sharon has never apologised for his role. And his government slammed a BBC "Panorama" documentary, which ran Sunday, for suggesting Sharon should be indicted for the massacre.

"“Israel” takes a grave view of the slanted and unfair nature of the Panorama programme," the foreign ministry said in a statement. "The timing of the programme, 19 years after the events ... constitutes an attempt to sully the name of “Israel” and its leader by any means."

On the programme, former International War Crimes Tribunal chief prosecutor Judge Richard Goldstone said Sharon, now 73, should be held responsible for the massacre.

"In the case of Sabra and Shatilla, clearly the Kahan Commission found that very serious crimes had been committed and I have no doubt that any decent person would regret the fact that not a single criminal prosecution followed," Goldstone told the BBC programme, entitled "The Accused".

Richard Falk, professor of international law at Princeton University, said of Sharon: "There is no question in my mind that he is indictable for the knowledge he had or should have had."

In a statement, the BBC said it stood by the story, which it called "a legitimate analysis of a human rights issue."

In January, just a month before his election, Sharon expressed his regrets for what he said was a "terrible tragedy," but refused to apologise.

"To apologise for what?" Sharon told Arab “Israeli” weekly newspaper Kul-al-Arab.

 

Atrocity survivors ask Belgian court to indict Sharon…(3)

Palestinian survivors of a massacre in Lebanon nearly 20 years ago asked a Belgian court yesterday to indict the “Israeli” Prime Minister, Ariel Sharon, for crimes against humanity over his role in the carnage.

Taking advantage of Belgium's unusually broad laws on war crimes, 28 of those who survived the killings at the Sabra and Chatila refugee camps outside Beirut filed a legal complaint with an investigating judge in Brussels. He will probably take one month to decide whether Mr Sharon and others should face charges for involvement in the murders.

In 1982 up to 2,000 unarmed Palestinians in the camps were slaughtered by a Lebanese Christian militia allied to the “Israelis”. An “Israeli” inquiry found Mr Sharon, who was then Defence Minister, "indirectly responsible" and he resigned.

Even if the case goes ahead, Mr Sharon would almost certainly not be arrested if he went to Belgium. But the attempt to purse a war crimes case has already caused a diplomatic incident, and the “Israeli” Foreign Minister, Shimon Peres, has told the Belgian government that none of his fellow ministers will risk visiting Brussels if the action proceeds.

That creates a headache for Belgium, which takes over the European Union's presidency at the end of the month and will be expected to play a leading role in Europe's efforts to help broker peace in the Middle East.

One of the survivors, Souad Srour Al-Marai, gave harrowing details of the massacre, which took the lives of most of her family. She said: "My little sister, aged 18 months, raised her hand and asked her mother to pick her up, she was frightened. Then they started shooting at us.

"My little sister received a bullet in the head, my father was hit in his chest but was still alive. My brothers and sisters, Chadi [3], Farid [8], Bassam [11], Hajar [7], Chadia [18 months] [and] also our neighbour were all killed by the first bullets."

The woman then said she was raped in front of her dying father and left for dead.

The basis for the case lies in a law dating from 1993 that gives Belgian courts jurisdiction over violations of the Geneva war crimes convention no matter where they happened, even if they involve no Belgian nationals. Earlier this month the legislation was used to convict two nuns who played a role in the genocide in Rwanda in 1994, fuelling a debate over the whole process of war crimes prosecutions.

The Belgian government, concerned at the prospect of dozens of new cases from around the world, and fearful of the political implications, now wants to amend the law.

Vincent Van Quickenborne, a Belgian senator who is sponsoring the case, said that "for Belgium as a state these facts cannot be acceptable. We as a state should indict people and try them to find out whether they have committed these acts."

Michael Verhaeghe, the lawyer who is representing the survivors, said there was "sufficient evidence" to convict those responsible, adding that "the facts in this case undeniably reveal crimes against humanity".

Yesterday's legal action is the second against Mr Sharon to be lodged in Belgium, but it is thought to stand a greater chance of leading to a trial.

The 1982 massacre unfolded over three days when “Israel” allowed its Lebanese Christian Phalangist militia allies into the refugee camps.

The subsequent “Israeli” Kahan commission report stated that “Israeli” troops surrounding the camps knew what was happening. Throughout the killings many of the victims were stabbed and a large number of women were murdered after being gang-raped. Mr Sharon was in overall command of the “Israeli” forces.

More than 400 of the dead were buried in a mass grave just inside the entrance to the camps. Hundreds of others were buried secretly during the massacre, many of them beneath Beirut golf course.

 

Sharon, Mofaz urge passage of law to hold captives…(4)

Prime Minister Ariel Sharon and Chief of General Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaul Mofaz urged the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee yesterday to conclude discussions on legislation that would give the state legal grounds to hold captives as bargaining chips for the release of “Israeli” hostages.
The state drafted the legislation as a result of legal challenges in the case of Hizbullah's Mustafa Dirani and Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, who were abducted from south Lebanon to help obtain the release of “Israeli” captives. They are being held at present as administrative detainees.
The committee yesterday held its eighth discussion of the legislation amid continued objections out of concern for problems with international law and human rights. The government recently submitted a revised version of the bill, but the committee is still undecided.
Sharon said that it is important for soldiers to know that if they go to fight that the country will do everything possible to bring them home. "Therefore we must fulfill our obligation using every means at our disposal in order to return our soldiers home," he said.
Sharon also said that in the past, missions to obtain hostages to bring about the release of captives in Arab states had resulted in greater losses than the number of captive “Israelis”. However, he said that no one had hesitated to launch them. He noted that he had taken part in such missions when he was a soldier and was wounded.
Missing IAF navigator Ron Arad's brother, Chen, also testified before the committee yesterday. He said the state must be able to hold the captives until there are signs of life.
Under the legislation, the government could legally hold captives who would be defined as "illegal fighters." Several members favor defining such captives as prisoners of war instead, to ensure there is compliance with international law.
Mofaz said he objected to the idea of defining such captives as prisoners of war because it would be tantamount to recognizing terror organizations and giving "legitimization to the continuation of hostilities." Meretz MKs Zehava Gal-On and Naomi Chazan called for defining the captives as prisoners of war to avoid the state becoming the first to pass a law allowing for the holding of hostages. Chazan said the prisoner of war status would enable the state to hold the captives until the end of conflict or a prisoner swap.
Likud MK Yuval Steinitz and Labor MK Haim Ramon also supported the idea. Steinitz said that if the Hizbullah captives are defined as prisoners of war, “Israel” can respond against Hizbullah attacks by attacking Lebanese soldiers.

Moussa vows to stand with Lebanon…(5)

Arab League Secretary-General Amr Moussa arrived in Beirut Monday, promising to support Lebanon against “Israeli” threats.
“Any threats addressed to Arab countries …are serious,” he told reporters at Beirut Airport. “All efforts should be made to defend Arab rights in any country where those rights are threatened or where security and sovereignty are threatened.”
Asked what organizations such as the Arab League could do for Lebanon, he replied: “We will support what Lebanon wants because Arab rights should be supported without any exception.”
Moussa, who was previously Egypt’s foreign minister, was met at the airport by Information Minister Ghazi Aridi. During his 48-hour trip, he is scheduled to meet President Emile Lahoud, Speaker Nabih Berri and Prime Minister Rafik Hariri.

 

“Israeli” warplanes violate Lebanon's skies despite UN appeal…(6)

“ “Israeli” warplanes violated Lebanese airspace again Monday, ignoring calls from UN chief Kofi Annan for them to stop.

They broke the sound barrier over Beirut as well as the southern regions of Marjayoun, Jezzine and Hasbaya, correspondents reported.

The United Nations secretary general, who has just ended a tour that took him to both Lebanon and “Israel”, criticised “Israeli” overflights as violations of the UN demarcation line drawn following “Israel’s” withdrawal from southern Lebanon in May last year.

In talks with Annan, Lebanese President Emile Lahoud said that “Israeli” planes and ships had violated Lebanon's airspace and waters 600 times since the end of the occupation.

Hizbullah, which has killed three “Israeli” soldiers and captured another three in the Shibaa Farms, said last week its forces would fire on “Israeli” warplanes.

On June 3 Lebanese anti-aircraft batteries fired on the intruders for the first time since “Israel’s” withdrawal, but scored no hits.

 

Three more killings put CIA-brokered truce under pressure…(7)

A week after a CIA-brokered ceasefire between “Israel” and the Palestinians, the list of the dead is ticking steadily upwards ­ rising by three more yesterday.

Two “Israelis” were shot in the occupied West Bank by Palestinian gunmen and a 16-year-old Arab youth in the Gaza Strip died of his wounds. A nine-year-old boy was also buried. The spate of killings brings the number to die since the ceasefire was finally formalised by the CIA director George Tenet last week to eight ­ five Palestinians and three “Israelis”. It will strengthen mutual allegations that the truce is being routinely broken, and bring more political pressure on both sides to step up the conflict.

“Israel’s” government publicity machine has successfully promoted the view that the violence is committed only by the Palestinians. In reality, violations of the truce have come from both sides.

On Sunday two Palestinian youths were shot by the “Israeli” military, which says its soldiers are under orders to shoot only when their lives are threatened. The youths had reportedly been throwing stones.

There were three shootings of “Israeli” motorists yesterday. A 35-year-old Jewish settler was shot dead on the West Bank. A faction of Fatah claimed responsibility. A second person was shot dead last night in a convoy of cars approaching the Jewish settlement of Einav in the northern West Bank.

The violence was reflected in hostile verbal exchanges. “Israel’s” Prime Minister, Ariel Sharon has said a "cooling-off" period will not begin until the Palestinian attacks have been brought to a complete halt.

Speaking during a trip to Jordan, Yasser Arafat accused “Israel” of breaking international commitments and warned that the ceasefire deal would not last unless “Israel” ended its blockade of the occupied territories and froze settlement building.

“Israel” is sticking to what it calls a policy of "restraint", maintaining much of its punitive siege, but using less excessive military measures than earlier in the conflict. Yesterday, Mr Sharon reiterated this intention: "I am not willing to accept calls ... saying I have to take this nation to war."

Two bombs were discovered and safely detonated inside “Israel” in the Mediterranean city of Haifa yesterday. One bomb was attached to a motorcycle and was set to be detonated by an attached cellular telephone, a police spokesman said.

The events have placed Ariel Sharon under further pressure from the far-right to launch a major military strike against the Palestinians, a move which he publicly resisted.

"There are many things we must do ... but to take the people now to war, in my view, is a mistake of the first order from every perspective," the “Israeli” Prime Minister told members of his Likud party.

 

Divisions on Agenda of Coming “Israeli”-Palestinian Security Meeting…(9)

A day after a 12-year-old Palestinian boy was shot dead by “Israeli” occupation forces, the fifth fatality since the US-brokered ceasefire deal came into effect Wednesday, “Israeli” and Palestinian security officials are to hold another meeting later Monday. However, divisions also over the agenda for the security talks. An official from enemy Prime Minister Ariel Sharon's office said the “Israelis” demand the meeting covers violations of the ceasefire by the Palestinian and the arrest of Hamas and Islamic Jihad activists. But the head of Palestinian preventive security in the West Bank, Jibril Rajjoub, clearly stated that the Palestinian Authority will not arrest any Palestinian who participated in the resistance prior to ceasefire. He also said the meeting should be devoted to discussing a timetable for the lifting of the choking blockade on the West-Bank and the Gaza Strip.

 

Occupation Forces Use Internationally Banned Bullets Against Palestinians Says Zionist Daily…(9)

The Zionist Ha’aretz daily reported “Israeli” police were equipped this year with internationally banned soft-point bullets, commonly known as Dum- Dums, for use with small-calibre carbine rifles. The bullets expand on impact and are designed to increase damage when hitting a body. A Zionist police spokesman refused to comment on the type of ammunition in its possession and how it is used. Legal groups representing Palestinians living in territories occupied since 1948 as well as the Palestinian Authority accused the occupation forces of using such ammunitions. Doctors who conducted autopsies on the dead and treated the wounded said some of the injures showed that the bullets used had expanded on impact.

 

Saudi Arabia calls for “Israel” boycott if peace talks falter…(10)

Saudi Arabia has urged Arab countries to consider reviving a boycott of foreign firms doing business with “Israel” if Middle East peace efforts fail. Defense Minister Prince Sultan said if the UN and superpower nations fail to quote take a positive stand in the peace process then Arabs must reconsider positions towards companies dealing with “Israel”. A boycott of foreign companies doing business with “Israel” has virtually collapsed since the start of Arab-”Israeli” peace talks in 1991. But calls for a revival of the boycott have been growing since “Israel” stepped up its attacks on Palestinians more than eight months ago.
