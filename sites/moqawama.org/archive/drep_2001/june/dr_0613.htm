Nasrallah: Time is running out for “Israeli” prisoners…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general  warned “Israel” on Tuesday that time was running out to win the release of four “Israelis” held by the group, accusing the “Israeli” government of “complicating” a possible swap by including missing airman Ron Arad in the deal.
Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah’s comments came alongside reports that US Secretary of State Colin Powell intends to play a greater role in securing information on the four “Israelis” being held by Hizbullah.
Powell’s decision is in response to growing irritation in “Israel” at the lack of movement in winning the release of the three soldiers and a suspected Mossad intelligence agent detained in two operations last October.
Nasrallah made the comments during a ceremony to commemorate the seventh anniversary of the kidnapping by “Israeli” commandos of Mustapha Dirani, the head of the Believers’ Resistance.
“I say to Ariel Sharon, the prime minister of the enemy: You are not (only) linking the fate of Mustapha Dirani to the fate of Ron Arad, who became a mystery,” Nasrallah said. “You are (also) linking the fate of your four soldiers with the fate of Arad.”
Dirani was kidnapped by the “Israelis” in the hope that he would shed light on the circumstances of Arad’s disappearance after his F-4 Phantom crashed in Lebanon in 1986. Arad was briefly held by Dirani’s group.
Addressing the families of the four “Israelis” detained by Hizbullah, Nasrallah said: “Your leaders are not telling you the truth … . They are not doing anything to solve this.
“Time is not on your side … situations might change and living persons might die, ” Nasrallah said without elaborating.
All three “Israeli” soldiers were wounded, at least one of them seriously, during the detaining operation near Shibaa village last October.
No information on their condition has been released since the soldiers were snatched.
The failure to resolve the issue has prompted close to 80 officers in the “Israeli” Army reserves to send a letter to Sharon and “Israeli” Defense (War) Minister Binyamin Ben-Eliezer, calling upon them to “increase the efforts to obtain the release of our missing and captive soldiers in Lebanon.”
The letter calls upon the two leaders to “storm on all fronts” to attain the goal, as “the continued captivity of the soldiers hurts the inner strength and stability of “Israeli” society.”
According to “Israel’s” Maariv daily, “Israeli” President Moshe Katsav complained to US officials recently that the United Nations and the Red Cross were not doing enough to obtain information on the well-being of the captives. Katsav asked Powell to use his influence on Beirut and Damascus to achieve results.
Powell reportedly has decided to await the outcome of UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan’s visit to Lebanon and Syria this week before taking further action.
Germany has been mediating on the prisoners, but negotiations are not believed to have proceeded far. ­

Hizbullah intensifying psychological war against “Israel”…(2)

Hizbullah is intensifying its psychological war and is even trying to deter tourists from visiting the country.
The extremist organization is now using its Internet site, its radio station, and its Manar television channel to try to demoralize the “Israeli” public and raise Palestinian expectations.
Its latest gimmick is to screen excerpts of Prime Minister Ariel Sharon's condolence visit to the family of Gilad Zar, who was killed in a shooting several weeks ago.
The aim, apparently, is to try and send a message to the Palestinians that Jewish residents of the territories have lost their sense of security.
Manar television has also been running pictures from the Dolphinarium suicide martyrdom bomb attack.
The pictures include subtitles in Russian and Hebrew warning immigrants from the former Soviet Union of the dangers of remaining in “Israel”. Russian-speaking immigrant youngsters made up the bulk of the bomber's 20 victims.
Hizbullah is also apparently trying to harm “Israel’s” economy by turning its attention towards potential tourists and warning them what could happen to them if they visit the country.
The organization's television station shows pictures of the attacks that have occurred in the country with the following warning in English: "Tourists Beware. . .Tourism in “Israel” Puts Your Life in Danger."
The pictures are accompanied by the sounds of police and ambulance sirens to give added impact to the message - even though it is primarily being seen by Hizbullah's faithful in Lebanon and its Palestinian acolytes in the Gaza Strip and the West Bank.
According to senior analysts such propaganda is unlikely to have any effect on tourists, certainly far less than the coverage given of attacks by international news organizations.
Nevertheless, Hizbullah is aware that its broadcasts are monitored in “Israel” and they should be seen in the context of the organization's ongoing psychological war against what it describes as the "Zionist Enemy," the analysts said.

Resistance targets “Israeli” planes…(3)

Hizbullah anti-aircraft gunners opened fire Tuesday on “Israeli” warplanes flying over south Lebanon for the first time since “Israel’s” troop withdrawal last year.
“Israeli” aircrafts were fired on twice during the afternoon in two separate sorties over the western sector of the border district.
“At 3.30pm, the anti-aircraft units of the Islamic Resistance confronted the enemy’s planes after they violated Lebanese airspace and flew over Bint Jbeil,” a statement from Hizbullah said. “At 4.10pm, the enemy’s planes violated the airspace over Zibqine and the anti-aircraft units took action again.”
Hizbullah’s anti-aircraft units are equipped with twin-barreled 23mm automatic cannons mounted on the back of flatbed trucks, as well as shoulder-fired SAM-7 missiles.
It was the first time Hizbullah had fired at warplanes since overflights resumed last October.

 

Lahoud says Syrian presence  is vital due to Shibaa dispute…(4)

President Emile Lahoud affirmed that Syria provides vital assistance to Lebanon, and defended its controversial military presence as pivotal as long as parts of southern Lebanon remained occupied.
Lahoud made the statement during an interview with the Moroccan News Agency on the eve of a two-day visit to Morocco, which starts Wednesday.
Lahoud praised Syria’s role in ending the Lebanese civil war and in laying the foundations of the new Lebanese state based on the Taif Accord.
“But Syria’s most significant contribution toward Lebanon was its help in liberating the South,” he said.
He added that “just like Syria has always stood by Lebanon, it will continue to do so until the South is fully liberated and a comprehensive regional peace is achieved.”
Lahoud stressed that “with Syria’s support, such a comprehensive peace would restore stability to the Middle East area and end the Arab-”Israeli” struggle.”
The Lebanese president pointed out that “any partial peace is in fact a surrender to the “Israelis”, which we and the Syrians have always rejected despite the political, economic and military pressures exerted on us.”
Lahoud pledged that “the Lebanese-Syrian cooperation will continue until the Golan Heights are liberated, just like the Lebanese south was, and until “Israel” withdraws to the June 4, 1967, lines.”
Lahoud predicted that “there will be no stability in the region until the Shibaa Farms are returned to Lebanese sovereignty. There is no compromise on this issue. Lebanon is entitled to resort to all the methods at its disposal to liberate the Shibaa Farms.”
He added that “no matter what method we employ, the Shibaa Farms will be liberated from “Israeli” occupation just as the South and the Western Bekaa were in May 2000.”

Two sentenced for collaboration…(5)

A military court has sentenced two people in absentia to 15 years in prison for contact and collaboration with “Israel” during its occupation.
The court also ordered the two men, Naji Mahmoud Honein and Hussein Ali Abbas, to pay a fine of LL2 million each.
The sentences were issued late Monday and posted on the court’s bulletin board Tuesday.
The court also sentenced Hatem Ratib Attieh to a two-year jail term and ordered him to pay a fine of LL400,000. A fourth man, Fouad Tanios Abou, was sentenced to a one-year jail term. Charges were dropped against four other men.
The defendants were the 121st batch of collaborators tried by the court since it began its work last June in the wake of “Israel’s” May 2000 withdrawal from south Lebanon. Its verdicts cannot be appealed.

 

Collaborators ‘want to battle’ intifada…(6)

Former “Israeli”-allied South Lebanon Army militiamen who fled to “Israel” hope to make a living combatting the intifada in the Occupied Territories, according to reports from “Israel”.
“Israeli” Deputy Minister Yuri Stern, who is responsible for the absorption of SLA refugees and their families, has promised to work toward securing the ex-militiamen places in the Jewish state’s police force or in its Border Police, “Israel’s” Yediot Aharonot newspaper reported on Monday.
In a recent meeting with members of the SLA community in Netanya, Stern requested a preliminary list of those wanting to serve in the Border Police. “The deputy minister was attentive,” said Saad Issa, who is responsible for the education of SLA children. “We have 400 candidates for the Border Police.”
One former SLA militiaman, Robert Fares, 26, explained: “I want to go anywhere there’s a war on. I have no problem clashing with the Arabs… .Quite the opposite, it’s what I want to do.”

 

“Israeli” Occupation Forces Wound Several Palestinians In More Aggressions…(7)

Several Palestinians were wounded in the Yebna refugee camp in Rafah at the Palestinian-Egyptian border, when “Israeli” occupation forces blew up six cement cubes deliberately at the camp. This barbaric policy which was pursued since Monday has put the life of many Palestinians in danger, and led to the death of several ones including little children. Palestinian sources said that occupation forces have set up a military post in Qarara town in Khan Younis, and in Nablus, “Israeli” settlers set fire to vast areas of land close to the Bourin crossraod, and usurped several acres of land in the Samouh town. The “Israeli” occupation forces shelled again Palestinian homes in Nablus, using heavy and ordinary weapons, and destroyed several homes and possessions. In the Gaza Strip, a 20-year-old Palestinian boy from Beit Lahya city died after being shot earlier by enemy bullets at northern al-Bira in Ramallah. On another level, Palestinian mujahedeen fired mortar shells on Ates Mouna settlement, and overnight, a bomb exploded at the entrance of Atrout airport in northern occupied Quds (Al-Quds). Enemy sources did not elaborate on the operation. Also in Rafah, Palestinian mujahedeen threw a bomb at a troop carrier on the Egyptian border. Meanwhile, the Arab Human Rights organization in Jordan condemned the Zionist authorities for banning more than 850 Palestinian students from taking their official exams in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. The organization assured that this measure violates the article 94 of the Geneva convention, and the international declaration of the human rights. Voice of Palestine radio reported that Imad Abu Diab, a member of the Jihad Islami movement, who faced an assassination attempt yesterday, has improved after being seriously wounded. The Islamic Jihad movement accused the Zionist army of exploding Abu Diab‘s car in Tolkarm.

 

Two “Israelis” wounded in shooting attack near Ofra…(8)

 Two “Israelis” were wounded a few minutes ago by Palestinian gunmen in a shooting attack near the entrance to the Samaria community of Ofra.
One person, a 17-year-old woman, was hit in the neck and pelvic region by the gunfire, sustaining moderate to serious wounds in the attack, according to first reports.
She was transferred to Al-Quds's Hadassah-University Hospital, Ein Kerem, for treatment.
The second victim sustained lighter wounds and was treated at the scene by medics.
Several mobile homes in the community, close to the vicinity of the attack, were also damaged in the shooting.
The Palestinians fired from a house in the nearby village of Ein Yabrud, according to “Israel” Radio.
A Kalashnikov and an M-16 semi-automatic rifle were used in the attack, fired in long bursts, according to the spent shell casings found at the scene, Army Radio reported.
Large “Israeli” force are engaged in a door-to-door search in the area in pursuit of the assailants.

Palestinian Forces Reject to Halt the Intifadah, Praises the Refusal to Arrest Resistance Fighters…(9)

The National Liberation Movement- Fatah- issued a statement saying that it fully rejects any arrest for political reasons, or because of resisting the occupation and participating in the uprising. Fatah assured that its impossible to separate between the security issues and the political ones, adding that we should be primarily concerned about the safety of Palestinians- a demand which will not be granted until the occupation, aggressiveness and colonialism ends. The movement stressed on preserving national unity and hold on to the Palestinian right to resist. For his part, Sheikh Abdullah Al-Shami, one of the leaders of the Islamic Jihad movement, said during a phone call with al-Manar channel late Monday, that the security meetings will negatively affect the Palestinian people. Sheikh Al-Shami prized the statement issued by Fatah movement, and the Palestinian Authority announcement that it rejects the arresting policy. Meanwhile, the Palestinian National and Islamic Forces said the American demand that the so-called violence be stopped means a halt of the intifadah and the arrest once again of resistance fighters. The forces added in a statement that these demands are totally rejected by the Palestinian Authority. They vowed to continue and develop the intifadah, and consolidate national unity to protect the intifadah and confront the Zionist pressures exerted via the US administration in the aim of imposing the so-called security coordination.

 

The Impact of the Palestinian Intifadah on the Zionist Entity is Growing…(10)

The Zionist Channel Two Television said that the International Jewish olympic games might be postponed or canceled for the first time since WW2, because of the insistence of participating delegations, which fear the lack of security in the Zionist Entity. The American and Canadian delegations particularly requested that the games be postponed for a year. In a vain attempt to reverse their decisions, the Zionist Minister of Sports Natan Velnaee explained that canceling the olympic games would be a reward for what he called terrorism and said that their support for "“Israel”" should be shown during difficult times. However, more than half of the Jewish sportsman and women canceled their participation, therefore, holding the games would be meaningless, according to the Zionist sports officials. The Channel Two reporter said concerning the swimming competitions in which 200 sportsman should take part in, only about 30 persons from outside "“Israel”" confirmed their participation. "The calling off of the Olympic games is a direct result of the sportsmen fear to come to "“Israel”". Talks about Zionist Jews coming to "“Israel”", in addition to the cancellation of flights to "“Israel”" probably shows that "“Israel”" is seen as a dangerous place. Does this add to the critical situation we are facing?" asked an “Israeli” journalist the Zionist Foreign Minister Shimon Peres. Peres answered, "I think that the postponement of the (Jewish) olympic games would be a great mistake, even though half of the players attended. It is better to hold the games with less participants." The Zionist Minister of Transport Ephraim Sneh was asked about the airline companies which reduced the number of flights to the Zionist Entity and prevented plane crews from staying in Tel Aviv. "As far as I know, Air France, Alitalia, and Lufthansa are the three companies the media spoke of," answered Sneh. Sneh was also asked to comment on the decision of the Jewish Reform Movement in the United States not to send its young members to the Zionist Entity. He said, "The Reform Movement, in my opinion, has received a negative image from the media after its decision. I called the director of the movement and conveyed how gravely we take this decision. I repeat we can not give them orders, but this is the time they have to show solidarity with "“Israel”"." A journalist said, "When a mother in the United States tells herself why should I put my children's life in danger for no reason, for at every moment an operation may occur. Can't this opinion be understood?" "We are neither telling those youths to serve (as soldiers) at the Termit post along the Egyptian border, nor are we asking them to sit on Mount Dove (Shibaa Farms)," said Sneh. Concerning the calling off of the Jewish Olympic games he said, "Unfortunately, and I say with sorrow that this is a triumph for terrorism. They were able to cause confusion and split us from the Jews outside "“Israel”". We have to take a lesson from that."

 

Probe slams “Israeli” Army tank killing of 3 Palestinian women in Gaza…(11)
Incident called a 'mistake which should not have happened'

 Serious flaws have been discovered in the army's operations in the West Bank and Gaza Strip during an investigation into the deaths of three Palestinian women, when an “Israeli” tank fired on their tent in Gaza on Saturday night, military sources said yesterday.
The sources flatly declared: "This was a serious, professional mistake which should not have happened."
It is highly likely that disciplinary steps will be taken against the commanding officers involved in the incident, though it may only be junior officers who are punished.
Defense (War) Minister Benjamin Ben-Eliezer and Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz received an initial report on the incident from the head of the Southern Command, Major General Doron Almog during a high-level meeting yesterday afternoon.
Ben-Eliezer posed a number of difficult questions to the senior army commanders present, and even criticized the decision to deploy tanks in the area.
The three Palestinian women were killed and three others, all from the same family, were wounded shortly after midnight Saturday, when the “Israel’s” Army was engaged in a shoot-out with Palestinian gunmen. The Palestinians had fired at “Israeli” outposts in the settlement of Netzarim and the Netzarim Junction, as well as at a nearby “Israeli” tank.
In response, the tank fired at two figures, apparently armed, who seemed to be fleeing from the site. However, the three shells missed their target, and one hit the tent which housed the Malalhas, a Palestinian Bedouin family. The tent, in a neighborhood of Gaza city, is more than a kilometer from Netzarim.
The tank round that killed the women contained hundreds of tiny, metal "Flechette" arrows, that disperse over a wide area when the rounds explode.
The army refused to comment on the incident or to take responsibility for it in its immediate aftermath, prefering instead to say that the matter was being looked into. The report blames "lapses in communication" for the incident. It says that “Israeli” soldiers spotted the suspected perpetrators a few hundred meters away, but somehow the tank team understood the targeted perpetrators were actually a kilometer and a half from them.
The investigation has revealed a worrying phenomenon of instructions not being followed through, problems in communication between the various forces in and around the Netzarim area and commanders failing to grasp the circumstances surrounding field operations.
A senior security source talked last night about "grave findings which point to failure to follow orders." The investigation has not yet been completed, and it has thus not yet been decided what steps to take against those involved in the fatal incident.
Head of the Palestinian security forces in the northern Gaza strip, Saeb al-Ajaz, yesterday blasted “Israel” for using various weapons against Palestinian civilians, in particular the use of Flechette rounds.
The “Israeli” Army spokesman confirmed Monday's report in the Hebrew Ha'aretz that the army was using Flechette rounds in the Gaza Strip. The “Israeli” Army insisted the use of the Flechette rounds was legal, but al-Ajaz argued that international conventions forbade their use.
”Israel’s” Army previously used these rounds in the security zone in Lebanon against Hizbullah cells. The round is considered to be most effective in an open area, where its dispersal of hundreds of tiny metal arrows increases the strike radius.
However, the use of the Flechette, which scatters the arrows around the target rather than hitting it precisely, is much more problematic in Gaza, which is a densely-populated area.
