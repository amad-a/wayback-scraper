Hamas 'will not be bound' by Middle East ceasefire…(1)

The Hamas militant Islamic group is not bound by the cease–fire called by Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat, its spiritual leader said today as the fragile truce held.

Hamas support for the cease–fire called last Saturday is seen as vital to its success – the bomber who blew himself up outside a Tel Aviv disco on Friday night, killing 20 settlers, was a Hamas member.

"When we are talking about the so called cease–fire, this means between two armies. We are not an army. We are people who defend themselves and work against the aggression," the group told the AP.

“Israeli” Foreign Minister Shimon Peres said a reduction of Palestinian violence in recent days shows a convincing cease–fire had begun. "You can congratulate us on the beginning, but not on the completion," Mr Peres said.

“Israel” has demanded eight weeks of quiet before peace negotiations can be resumed while the Palestinians want only four weeks, he told Army radio.

Despite international efforts to calm tempers, several Palestinians were injured today in exchanges of fire in Hebron and Ramallah in the West Bank, Palestinian witnesses said.

Also in the West Bank, Ashraf Mahmoud Bardawil, 27, a Fatah activist in the Tulkarem area, was critically injured in an explosion in his car, according to a local hospital director and Bardawil's family. The cause wasn't clear.

Mr Arafat called a meeting of his Fatah leadership and Hamas representatives last night. A joint statement said they would halt attacks in “Israel” as of midnight (2100 GMT) to give “Israel” a chance to "stop assassination and stop killing and destruction."

"We are going to stop our military actions in our lands," read the leaflet released in the names of the military wings of Hamas and Fatah.

Such a joint statement is unusual and a Hamas leader in Ramallah, Hassan Yousef, said he was not sure it was authentic. The secretive military wing could not be contacted to verify it but issued no leaflets denying it.

Another militant group, Islamic Jihad, did not take part in Monday night's meeting, and the radical Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine, formally part of the PLO but opposed to peace with “Israel”, issued conflicting statements.

North of Ramallah, “Israeli” soldiers opened fire Tuesday with rubber–coated steel bullets on Palestinians throwing stones after the army refused to let them by a checkpoint, Palestinian witnesses said. Ten Palestinians were injured. The army said it fired on 600 demonstrators to disperse them.

In Hebron, an exchange of fire erupted between security forces of the two sides and one Palestinian officer was injured in the leg. The “Israeli” army said soldiers shot after they were fired on.

In the West Bank, Palestinian gunmen fired at “Israeli” cars at two points early Tuesday but no injuries were reported.

Three Palestinians, including a 7–year–old boy, also were injured in an explosion while playing near the Egyptian–Gaza border, according to doctors and security sources. The boy had chest injuries; the two others, ages 16 and 17, were reported in stable condition. Palestinians claim “Israeli” soldiers have booby–trapped the area.

Despite the relative reduction of violence in the eight months of fighting, Palestinian leaders insisted a cease–fire would not end their struggle against “Israel”.

West Bank Fatah leader Marwan Barghouti, who attended the meeting with Arafat, said the cease–fire applies only to areas under full Palestinian control. In other areas, he said, "resisting occupation is a legitimate right of the Palestinians."

West Bank Palestinian security chief Jibril Rajoub pledged a "100 percent effort" to enforce the cease–fire, but warned only a political solution would end the conflict.

U.S. Secretary of State Colin Powell urged Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat in a telephone call late Monday to arrest those responsible for the Tel Aviv nightclub bombing. Powell leaned on Arafat to go beyond his offer of a cease–fire and his order that attacks on “Israel” be halted, a U.S. official said on condition of anonymity.

German Foreign Minister Joschka Fischer, who extended a stay in the region to continue intensive talks, told “Israel” television that U.S.–led, international monitoring would be necessary for any “Israel”–Palestinian accord.

 

Barghouti Says Palestinian Factions Agreed to Continue Resistance, Intifadah…(2)
Marwan Barghouti, West Bank leader of Fatah faction, said in a television interview that the Palestinian Intifadah (uprising) against the Zionist Entity would continue, encompassing protests, stone throwing, and the armed struggle in the West Bank and Gaza Strip against the “Israeli” occupation. Following a meeting between Palestinian Chairman Yasser Arafat and 13 Palestinian factions Monday evening, Barghouti said, " We understood that the ceasefire ordered by Arafat is only to be implemented in the territories under full Palestinian control. Fire is not to be opened from these territories. But the intifadah shall continue ." Barghouti told the Manar Channel the factions agreed to continue and strengthen the resistance, and intifadah.

 

Palestinian Fatah Member Seriously Wounded in “Israeli” Assassination Attempt, At Least 12 Palestinians…(3)
The Zionist Entity resumed its assassination policy of Palestinian officials, after targeting the car of Ashraf Bardaweil a Fatah member, leaving him in serious condition. The attack took place near the village of Dhanabeh near Tulkarem in the West Bank. Fatah official Faeq Kanaan described the attack as an “Israeli” assassination attempt, adding that it was either a missile fired from a helicopter or an “Israeli” tank that destroyed the car. Meanwhile, the Zionist forces arrested 10 Palestinians, among them Majadi Alawaneh, a senior liaison officer in Nablus. Elsewhere, eight Palestinians were wounded as occupation forces opened fire with rubber bullets at a crowd of students trying to break through a roadblock . Several hundred students, wanting to reach their university at Bir Zeit near Ramallah in the West Bank, hurled stones at troops who responded with rubber-coated metal bullets and tear gas. In other incidents, three Palestinian children, including an eight year old boy, were seriously injured by shrapnel in the southern Gaza Strip in a land mine explosion while playing near the border with Egypt. In the al-Khalil (Hebron) area, shots were fired at an “Israeli” vehicle near the West Bank village of Halhoul. The Fatah Al-Aqsa brigades claimed responsibility of the attack, which left them injured or killed. Also in Halhoul, Palestinian security source said “Israeli” forces had opened fire on a Palestinian police roadblock, wounding one of them. Thousands of Palestinians commemorated the 34th anniversary of the June 1967 Arab-”Israeli” war on Tuesday with protests around the West Bank and Gaza Strip. Around 1,500 people were seen demonstrating in central Ramallah, the largest town in the West Bank, shouting “national unity” and demanding an “Israeli” withdrawal from their towns and villages. In the flashpoint southern West Bank town of al-Khalil, three people were lightly injured in clashes with “Israeli” troops after a demonstration of some 300 people to mark the anniversary of the war, witnesses said. At least 1,000 people also took to the streets in Gaza City.

 

Intifadah Inflicts harm on Tourism Sector in the Zionist Entity…(4)
The number of tourists visiting the Zionist Entity shrank, while many flights were canceled due to the ongoing eight-month Palestinian Intifadah. The number of tourist visitors reached about 130 thousands only on March this year - dropping to as much as 50% compared to the same time of last year. Air France and American Delta Airlines called off their flights to Tel Aviv after Friday’s martyrdom operation. The two companies said they would resume flights Monday. Meanwhile, British Airways announced that the revival in business trips compensated for the decreasing number of tourist flights to Tel Aviv due to tourism decline. The “Israeli” enemy is seeking every means to stop the harm that afflicted tourism and ordered an investigation of why Delta Airlines and Air France had canceled flights, saying this would violate their daily flights licenses.

 

Middle East Stage to Intensive International Efforts to End the Palestinian Intifadah…(5)
Intensive international efforts to end the Palestinian Intifadah are going on following the announcement of a ceasefire by both Palestinian chairman Yasser Arafat and “Israeli” enemy Premier Ariel Sharon. Commenting on Arafat’s ceasefire, “Israeli” Foreign Minister Shimon Peres said it will take several weeks to be applied. He added, Arafat's powers were limited; nevertheless , he is in control of the situation and could arrest those responsible for the attacks. As to enemy war minister Benyamin ben Eliezer, he considered that the ceasefire is artificial and will not hold water. The Palestinian Authority had also labeled Sharon’s ceasefire a sham. On the ground, the intifadah operations continued along with “Israeli” occupation aggression against the Palestinians. Meanwhile Russian and the United States, co-sponsor of the failed Mideast so-called peace process, were leading efforts to ensure the fragile ceasefire. Russia jumped back into the fray Tuesday with special envoy Andrei Vdovin meeting Peres and Sharon. Vdovin warned the region was on the brink of a very serious situation, calling for maximum restraint and an implementation of the Mitchell report recommendations according to a clearly established timetable. In separate phone calls US Secretary of State Colin Powell urged Arafat to make a 100 percent effort against what he called violence and Sharon to continue a policy of restraint, the US State Department Spokesman Richard Boucher said. He said encouraging signs of reducing "violence" had been seen. Sharon spokesman Raanan Gissin said that CIA chief George Tenet was expected in the Zionist Entity very soon as part of a US initiative aimed at promoting security meetings. While in Cairo, Egyptian Foreign Minister Ahmed Maher announced the “Israelis” and Palestinians will resume very soon their security talks in a bid to restore calm. Last week there were inconclusive security talks between the two sides. Maher was speaking after Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak held talks with German Foreign Minister Yoshka Fisher who is on a tour in the region and had met with Sharon, and Arafat earlier. Fisher said there is an agreement to continue combined efforts with Cairo to stabilize the ceasefire and start the implementation of the Mitchell report. Sharon has already ignored calls by the report to freeze settlement building in the Palestinian territories. And Maher warned the Zionist Entity against ignoring the report.
