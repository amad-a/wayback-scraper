Sharon trial campaign gathers momentum…(1)

Elie Hobeika and other former Lebanese militiamen involved in the Sabra and Shatila massacres could cut a deal to escape future indictments by offering to testify against “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon in court, a leading Lebanese lawyer said Sunday.
The lawyer, who spoke on condition of anonymity, said that Hobeika and other senior members of the Phalange militia which participated in the 1982 massacre of Palestinians may eventually find themselves coming under closer scrutiny by judicial authorities.
His comments came as the campaign to indict Sharon mounted over the weekend, with a leading US-based human-rights group calling for a criminal investigation into the “Israeli” premier’s role in the Sabra and Shatila killings.
“There is abundant evidence that war crimes and crimes against humanity were committed on a wide scale in the Sabra and Shatila massacre, but to date, not a single individual has been brought to justice,” said Hanny Megally, executive director of Human Rights Watch’s Middle East and North Africa division.
Survivors of the massacre filed a case in Brussels last week, accusing Sharon of crimes against humanity. The case was brought under a 1993 Belgian law which allows war criminals to be tried in Belgium regardless of their nationality or where the crimes were committed.
The BBC aired a report last week which examined whether there was a legal basis for putting Sharon on trial for the killings of over 1,000 Palestinian refugees. Richard Falk, a professor of international law at Princeton University and one of the experts interviewed on the program, said that since the BBC report was broadcast, he and his wife have received threatening phone calls.
Falk, who is Jewish, said on the program that “there is absolutely no question in my mind” that Sharon was indictable “for the knowledge he had or should have had.”
“Israeli” officials have accused the BBC of anti-Semitism and taking an anti-”Israeli” editorial slant in its reporting.
In its statement, Human Rights Watch urged US President George W. Bush to discuss the matter with Sharon during their scheduled meeting at the White House on Tuesday, and urge him to cooperate with any investigation. The group said the United States had a substantial interest in the case because the “Israeli” occupation of West Beirut followed written US assurances that Palestinians remaining there would be safe.
“Israel’s” Kahan commission, which investigated the slaughter, concluded that Sharon “bears personal responsibility” for his role in the massacres.
Human Rights Watch said the Kahan investigation could not substitute for proceedings in a criminal court in “Israel” or elsewhere that would bring to justice those responsible for the killings.
Megally said that the Lebanese government “should instigate a similar investigation regarding the role of the Phalange militia in this massacre.”
The Lebanese lawyer said that if Sharon finds himself being indicted, then Hobeika and other Phalange militiamen may be next. Asked if it was theoretically possible for Hobeika to cut a deal and testify against Sharon, the lawyer said: “I think it is, especially if no indictments have been filed.
“They are more likely to get a better deal if they come forward now,” the lawyer added, “rather than after indictments have been issued.” ­

 

Hizbullah gunners open fire on “Israeli” jets over weekend…(2)

Hizbullah anti-aircraft batteries fired at “Israeli” warplanes on two consecutive days over the weekend, fulfilling a promise to retaliate to all “Israeli” violations of Lebanese airspace.
A Hizbullah spokesman said anti-aircraft units opened fire 11.30am at “Israeli” aircraft flying over Bint Jbeil, Hanin, Zibqine and Sojod. On Saturday, “Israeli” planes flying over Markaba, Houla and Bint Jbeil were fired at. The planes flew along the Bekaa and broke the sound barrier over Beirut. An “Israeli” Army spokesman said “the army does not detail its operational activity.”
On June 12, Hizbullah shot at “Israeli” warplanes for the first time since the “Israeli” troop withdrawal. The party has promised to target any “Israeli” planes breaching Lebanese airspace as well as retaliate against “Israeli” soldiers that shoot Lebanese civilians along the border.
“Israeli” jets have been flying regularly over Lebanese villages and towns since October, when members of Hizbullah snatched three soldiers patrolling the Shibaa Farms territory.
Lebanon has repeatedly complained to the United Nations over what the UN says are violations of the UN-delineated Blue Line marking “Israel’s” line of withdrawal from south Lebanon.
Meanwhile, a government source asserted on Sunday that the “unofficial ceasefire” in the Shibaa Farms does not mean Lebanon has abandoned the option of armed resistance to regain the Shibaa territory.
The source said that the present calm along the Shibaa Farms front was an opportunity offered to the international community to focus efforts on stopping the “massacres and bloodshed” in the occupied Palestinian territories.

 

Hizbullah ‘won’t be provoked’…(3)

Nabatieh MP Mohammed Raad said Saturday that Hizbullah would not get involved in any confrontation with any parties as long as “Israel” is still occupying Lebanese soil.
“Nobody should provoke us or lead us on into a political battle while our land is occupied,” he said during a ceremony in Marwanieh, Zahrani on Saturday.
Raad, who was commenting on recent developments at MEA, said Hizbullah had its own files on people responsible for squandering and embezzling public funds. However, he said he would not expose such information to maintain stability in the country.
Separately, he told Sawt al-Shaab radio on Sunday that the party rejected a meeting with UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan earlier this month because of certain “conditions” imposed by the UN.

 

Prosecute “Israeli” premiers and Lahd’…(4)

An organization which rehabilitates victims of torture called on Sunday for the prosecution of present and former “Israeli” prime ministers, as well as the head of the former South Lebanon Army, for allowing prisoners to be tortured.
The call occurred during the inauguration of the “No to Torture” exhibition at Unesco Palace, marking the anniversary of the United Nations International Day in Support of Victims of Torture on June 26.
Speaking on behalf of The Khiam Rehabilitation Center of The Victims of Torture, Suzan Zein said that “all who supervised torture” should be punished. She singled out “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon and former “Israeli” premiers Ehud Barak and Shimon Peres. Antoine Lahd, the former commander of the SLA was also accused.
In a separate conference, the Bar Association of Tripoli and the North called for a ban on the use of torture in Lebanon.
“A new law on the basics of criminal judiciary will be issued and exercised soon in Lebanon,” said Bar Association President George Mourani. “This law protects human rights during interrogation.”
Zghorta MP Qaisar Mouawad said the parliamentary Human Rights Committee had witnessed the use of torture through its visits to various prisons this year. “MPs, lawyers and social committees share the responsibility of protecting the basics of the June 26, 1987 UN agreement against torture,” he said.

 

A Palestinian Killed, 22 Palestinian Houses Demolished in New “Israeli” Occupation Aggressions…(5)

The Zionist Entity translated its so-called respect of the US-brokered ceasefire plan by carrying out new incursions into Palestinian territories, and destroying 22 Palestinian houses. Palestinian security sources said early Saturday that the “Israeli” enemy army destroyed 17 houses with two bulldozers, escorted by 4 tanks, which penetrated 200 meters into the Al-Barahna area on the edge of the Rafah refugee camp in the southern Gaza Strip. Three other Palestinian houses near the Netzarim Settlement outside the Gaza City were also leveled by occupation bulldozers. 
And in further aggressions, “Israeli” occupation troops shot dead a 20-year-old Palestinian, Mohammed Jamal Swaidan, and injured 2 others near the Gaza Strip refugee camp of Maghazi. The troops arrested the two injured and took the body of the martyr, who is a member of Hamas and the student's council in the Islamic University. 
Meanwhile, Palestinian resistance fighters opened fire at an occupation army position near the Gaza Strip Settlement of Neve Dekalim. “Israeli” enemy patrols and positions in the West Bank also came under fire around dawn, while four anti-tank grenades were fired on an army position in Rafah along the Gaza Strip border with Egypt, according to an “Israeli” army spokesman. 
Concerning Friday's heroic martyrdom operation, the Ezzedin al-Qassam Brigades, armed wing of Hamas movement, claimed responsibility for the attack which killed two “Israeli” occupation soldiers and wounded a third. In a statement released, the group said the operation was carried out by the martyr Ismael Beshir al-Maassuabi, 27, from al-Shati refugee camp. 
This in response to "the policy of assassination and repression adopted by the Zionist occupiers". The Palestinian mujahedeen in a jeep blew up himself on a road the Zionist soldiers were patrolling near the settlement of Dugit north of the Gaza Strip. 
On the other hand, a Palestinian source said the occupation army set up lately new military posts in the various locations of the Gaza Strip. The source added that occupation troops prevented students in the town of Silat al-Thaher in Jenine, from reaching their school to sit for their exams. The area is under curfew since last week.
