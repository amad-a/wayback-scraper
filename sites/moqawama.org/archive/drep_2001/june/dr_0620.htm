Nasrallah meets with PFLP delegates…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, met a delegation of the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine on Tuesday.
A delegation official, Abu Ahmad Fouad, said afterward that talks had focused on the latest political developments, especially the intifada and the pressure being exerted on the Palestinian people by “US imperialism and the Zionist enemy.”
Fouad briefed Nasrallah on the climate of national unity in Palestine, and attitudes toward the cease-fire plan put forward by CIA chief George Tenet.
“All organizations have asserted the continuation of the intifada. This choice is that of the whole Palestinian people,” he said. “All other options proposed by the US administration serve the interest of the Zionist enemy.”

 

“Israel” frets more Hizbullah kidnappings…(2)

Hizbullah may attempt to kidnap “Israeli” soldiers along the border and “Israeli” citizens traveling overseas, a senior “Israeli” intelligence officer told the Knesset’s Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee Tuesday.
The officer warned that Hizbullah would no longer limit itself to attacks and kidnapping attempts in the Shibaa Farms district, but expand its operations along the entire border with “Israel”.
The officer also warned that Hizbullah may attempt to kidnap “Israeli” citizens traveling overseas.
Last October, Hizbullah detained three “Israeli” soldiers from the Shibaa Farms. Days later, Hizbullah announced it had captured an “Israeli” Mossad agent after luring him to Beirut.

 

Hebrew broadcasts ‘would help confront Jewish state’…(3)

A united Arab satellite channel broadcasting in Hebrew must be created to confront “Israel’s” biased media, Arab Information ministers meeting in Beirut suggested at the opening of their 34th regular session on Tuesday.
Participants also called for a consolidation of efforts, an opening up to Western markets, and moves to confront international prejudices against Arabs with clear arguments.
Eleven information ministers gathered at the Bristol Hotel for the two-day conference to discuss ways of strengthening Arab media and encourage cooperation to defend Arab rights in the struggle against “Israel”.
Information Minister Ghazi Aridi, who was handed the presidency of the Arab Information Ministers’ Council from his Sudanese counterpart, came up with the idea of creating the Hebrew-language station.
“Comprehensive action by the media sector is needed to confront “Israeli” dangers whether on security, political or economic levels,” Aridi said at the opening ceremony.
If the Arab satellite station to be broadcast in Hebrew was difficult to achieve, Aridi suggested the option of producing special programs on various Arab channels geared toward “Israeli” audiences. He also suggested the creation of an Arab satellite channel to broadcast in three foreign languages “so that the space will not be limited to “Israeli” media,” to provide communication not only for the Arab diaspora but to the international community.
Aridi said it was time for Arabs to clarify the meanings of “terrorism, violence and violation of human rights” and who is really committing them.
“We have to factually reveal that the real violence and terrorism is that committed by “Israel” and not by the Arab resistance,” he said, suggesting that Arabs coordinate a common program to support the intifada and teach the younger generation the Arab cause.
Aridi said that the Lebanese media, and especially that of the resistance, were significant in helping liberate the country from “Israeli” occupation.
“The Lebanese media followed the attacks of the resistance against “Israeli” targets step-by-step and provided communication with Lebanese detainees in “Israeli” prisons,” he said. “All these means helped in devastating the image of a strong “Israel”, the same way the resistance destroyed the myth that “Israel’s” army cannot be defeated.”
Aridi said this attests to the real capabilities and potential of Arabs, “if only they knew how to invest them.”
Listing the massacres at Sabra and Shatila, Qana, and Mansouri as well as the killing of Mohammed al-Durra, the 12-year-old Palestinian boy who was shot by “Israeli” soldiers at the beginning of the intifada, Aridi said the Arab media succeeded in highlighting these events to expose “Israel’s” “terrorist actions and reveal its true racist and criminal nature.”
He said that for the first time “Israel” fears Arab media, driving it to create an Arabic satellite station “to present a negative image of the intifada.”
Praising the report produced by BBC-TV that condemned “Israel’s” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon for his role in the Sabra and Shatila massacre, Aridi asked why the Arabs did not produce a similar documentary, especially that they have the necessary information.
Jordanian Information Minister Saleh al-Qallab told his colleagues that Arabs must not at all fear the satellite channel that “Israel” is developing.
Supporting Aridi’s suggestion to create a united Arab channel targeting “Israel”, Qallab warned that this step would not find its way if there were no overall coordination among Arab countries and each state used this channel to expose its own points of view. Qallab said that a successful Arab media is the one “that does not talk to itself” but influences the international community and convinces it with its points of view.
Syrian Information Minister Adnan Omran said Arab media has always been “local” in the sense that they did not extend outside the Arab world to reach the international community.
Omran criticized some Arab media, without naming any specifically, for exposing “Israeli” points of view in press reports while the “Israeli” media, he claimed, was completely void of the Arab point of view.
“These Arab media groups are unknowingly contributing to and supporting the acts of terrorism and killing of the Palestinian people,” Omran said.
He was likely referring to the Qatari-based Al-Jazeera satellite news channel, which regularly interviews “Israeli” officials to obtain their point of view on various events.
Omran expressed his appreciation for what he called the “objectivity and courage of producers” of the BBC report in which they documented Sharon’s involvement in the 1982 Sabra and Shatila massacre.
“This program showed Sharon does not belong in the capitals of the world as a prime minister but rather in an international criminal court to be punished for his crimes,” Omran said.
Palestinian Information Minister Yasser Abed-Rabbo suggested scheduling a media conference to bring together Arab public and private stations.
Prominent media personalities should be invited to this conference where a comprehensive dialogue about the Arab cause will be held, Abed-Rabbo said.
Egyptian Information Minister Safwat Sharif said the Arab media was capable of “mobilizing international public opinion” in supporting the intifada and bringing forth charges against “Israeli” leaders for crimes against the Arab people.
Arab League Secretary-General Amr Moussa admitted that the Arab media failed to successfully convey the Arab cause to the international community while the “Israeli” media succeeded in controlling the scene and attracting the minds of Westerners to their policies.
Moussa warned that the Arabs will not be able to achieve unity without modern media able to adopt new technologies.
He said that the league will play an important role in the united media strategy, suggesting that he will ask a “prominent Arab political figure” to head this new strategy and to talk on behalf of Arabs in international forums.
After the opening ceremony, ministers held a closed meeting to discuss their work plan before announcing the adopted resolutions on Wednesday.
They are scheduled to visit the South and meet with President Emile Lahoud, Speaker Nabih Berri and Prime Minister Rafik Hariri on the same day.

 

Italian sappers destroy “Israeli” mines in final act of liberation…(4)

Hundreds of lethal land mines that ringed a former “Israeli”-proxy South Lebanon Army outpost have been located, dug up and destroyed by a team of Italian sappers.
Reporters were invited by the Italian Embassy to the final clearing of the area around the Teir Harfa outpost in the former occupation zone’s western sector.
As part of its “Italy for South Lebanon” project, the Italian government donated $225,000 to fund a 35-day project to help clear land mines from the South.
Fabrizio Gensini of Sogelma, the company appointed by the Italian government and the National Demining Office, said the team was able to clear more than 220 anti-personnel mines, in addition to a large number of live bullets, from the 1,000 square meter post.
He said the mines were usually found at a depth of 10 to 15 centimeters, though some were found 50 centimeters deep.
After their fuses had been removed, the 51 mines collected on Tuesday were placed in a hole with 3 kilograms of TNT. At precisely 3pm, they were detonated by remote control. The deafening explosion sent up a tall plume of smoke and dust.
Gensini said the precision timing of the explosion was agreed upon by the Lebanese Army and UNIFIL.
“The “Israelis” as well are aware of this understanding,” he said. “That’s why they don’t worry when they hear the explosion.”
Project supervisor Silvio Ronaldi, who has previous de-mining experience in Bosnia and Somalia, said the operation here was made easier because the mines were installed by a regular army and not by a militia ­ even though no maps were available.
“Locating the first two land mines was quite difficult, but after that it became easy because the “Israelis” have used the international standard,” he said.
He said the team had located four lanes of mines. Each mine was one meter apart, and each lane was four meters apart.

Zionist settlers carried out attacks of vandalism late Monday in the Palestinian village of Umm Safa near the West Bank town of Ramallah, arresting two of its residents, and razing land and groves. The settlers attacked the town and opened fire in the air. Neither the “Israeli” enemy police nor its forces intervened to stop the attacks. In southern Nablus, Zionist settlers stormed through the Palestinian village Madama overnight. Dozens of settlers torched two cars, uprooted dozens of trees and hurled stones at houses. The “Israeli” occupation army announced that it had imposed the blockade on the autonomous town of Tulkarem and instated several roadblocks in the region. This comes after the “Israelis” killed a 9 year-old Palestinian boy. Palestinian mujaheddeen retaliated by killing the former security official of Homesh settlement, Danny Yehuda, and Doron Zisserman, who was shot dead as he drove in the Einav settlement Monday evening. The Al-Aqsa martyr brigades claimed responsibility for the attack. In Rafah, Palestinian mujaheddeen threw grenades at an “Israeli” post. Earlier, Palestinians shelled five mortar bombs on the Neve Dekalim and Kadid settlement in the Gosh Katif bloc. Also in Nablus three other soldiers were injured when their armored troop carrier turned upside down after it was targeted by Palestinian mujaheddeen grenades.

 

Arafat Blames Settlers for Latest Mideast Violence…(6)

Palestinian President Yasser Arafat Tuesday blamed “Israeli” settlers acting with the protection of “Israeli” forces for the violence that has threatened a six-day-old cease-fire.

``We do not commit acts of violence,'' Arafat told a news conference in Madrid. ``The violence today is committed by the settlers, according to what has been recognized by “Israeli” political leaders and “Israeli” media.

``Regrettably some “Israeli” forces, not all of them, protect these settlers,'' Arafat said.

The two sides have blamed each other for cease-fire violations in which at least four Palestinians and three “Israelis” have been killed since the U.S.-brokered truce took effect last Wednesday.

Arafat, who met Spanish Prime Minister Jose Maria Aznar and then held a joint news conference, renewed his call for more international observers, saying calm has returned to areas where they are stationed.

Spain, which hosted the 1991 Madrid peace conference, has been seeking a more active role in Middle East peace efforts.

Aznar, who spoke by phone with President Bush Monday after meeting him in Madrid last week, backed Palestinian demands for “Israel” to halt Jewish settlement building in the occupied West Bank and Gaza.

Palestinian anger over the settlements has helped fuel an uprising against “Israeli” occupation that erupted last September after peace talks deadlocked.

Asked what advice he would give “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon on how to respond to “Israeli” calls to avenge attacks on settlers, Arafat asked: ``Are there no Palestinians being murdered? They are murdering Palestinians every day.''

Sharon has faced public demands to abandon the cease-fire and strike at the Palestinians following Monday's killings of two Jewish settlers in the West Bank. Arafat is due to hold talks with Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak in Alexandria Wednesday.

 

Palestinians take control of their heritage in Al-Quds’s Old City…(7)

Political turmoil and security problems have not succeeded in preventing Palestinians from continuing to restore and rehabilitate the Old City of Al-Quds, according to Palestinian architect Shadia Toukan.
Toukan, who is director of the Old City of Al-Quds Revitalization Program, presented a lecture on attempts and hurdles of restoration of the Old City at the Order of Engineers on Monday.
Initiated in 1995 by the Arab human resources and cultural development organization Welfare Association, the Old City of Al-Quds Revitalization Program is dedicated to preserving Al-Quds’s historic monuments and reviving its cultural heritage.
While most of its old buildings remain neglected, the Old City is experiencing a growing cultural isolation from remaining Palestinian towns as a result of the rapid expansion of “Israeli” settlements, said Toukan.
“I’m taking advantage of my visit here to tell people about our program,” she said.
With a $4 million donation, $3 million of which came from the Arab Fund for Economic and Social Development, experts have conducted studies and surveys of the Old City, which measures roughly 2 square kilometers and cradles monuments from over 20 historic periods.
According to Toukan, the studies revealed 48 historic sites requiring immediate rescue restoration from decay and protection from “Israeli” urban development. The city’s old Moroccan quarter has expanded over the past 30 years to become a large Jewish settlement, said Toukan. Another 78 “Israeli” enclaves have also been built around the city, she reported.
In recent years, Jewish settlers have been known to deliberately burn down wooden scaffolding, which the program’s restoration experts use, said Toukan.
Nevertheless, last year, the program set up a community outreach program to encourage the city’s residents to participate in the restoration activities and thus heighten awareness of their historic and cultural heritage.
The program also trains craftsmen and professionals to restore historic buildings using the methods of carpentry, tiling, iron mongery and other skills, with which the buildings were originally built, Toukan said.
“Our main objective is to preserve the cultural heritage of the city, but by building people’s capacities we’re also creating job opportunities,” she said. “We’re hitting two birds with one stone.”
This, she said, is what she implied in the title of her lecture protecting man and monument.
Polishing stone, reconstructing demolished sections and removing modern additions have been some of the techniques that the restoration experts have used to return the ancient monuments to their original form.
Since 1996, the Old City of Al-Quds program has restored more than 60 houses and monuments, including the Dar al-Aytam boys’ orphanage and vocational school, the al-Aqsa Library and Museum and the Suq al-Qattanin market. The oldest monument restored thus far dates back 1,200 years.
Other restored sites include monuments that have been placed under Islamic Trust, which means they cannot be transferred to new owners and must be used for their original function.
One way in which the program selected houses for restoration was if their owners or other local organizations filed an application for maintenance.
“Residents of the old town have no alternative to living in these deteriorating houses because the “Israeli” state won’t issue building permits and newer flats are far too expensive for them,” Toukan asserted.
Through restoration, the program hopes to improve living standards of the Old City’s residents, who lack social, economic, health and educational services, said Toukan.
This year, the Arab Fund for Economic and Social Development has promised to slate an additional $2 million grant, which the Old City of Al-Quds program will use for additional studies and renovations.
