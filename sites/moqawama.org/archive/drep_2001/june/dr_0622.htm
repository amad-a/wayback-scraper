 Thurs2ay 29-2000

 

“Israeli” TV won’t run BBC show on Sharon…(1)

” “Israel’s” state-owned television decided not to air a BBC documentary discussing whether Prime Minister Ariel Sharon is indictable for war crimes, an official said Wednesday.
The documentary dealt with the 1982 massacre of hundreds of Palestinians in Beirut during the 1982 “Israeli” invasion of Lebanon.
In the BBC program, entitled The Accused, an international law expert said Sharon would be indictable on war crimes charges.
“Israel” TV’s Channel One bought the broadcast rights for the documentary. The head of the TV’s news department, Rafiq Halabi, said in a radio interview that the program was of considerable public interest. But Uri Porat, director of the “Israel” Broadcasting Authority, which controls Channel One, overruled Halabi. ­

 

Intifadah Operations Injure an Occupation Soldier, Hit With 120 mm Mortar Bomb at Zionist Roadblock…(2)

A heavy 120 mm mortar bomb was fired at an “Israeli” road block on the edge of the Gaza Strip at Nahal Oz crossing. A military spokesman said the explosion damaged cars and blew in windows. It was the first time a mortar of this calibre had been fired since the beginning of the Palestinian intifadah, nearly nine months ago. Later, “Israeli” enemy radio reported that a bomb exploded at a treasurer close to the Netzarim settlement. The radio also reported that an “Israeli” enemy soldier was wounded when Palestinian mujahedeen threw 11 grenades at enemy posts close to the Egyptian border. A military post also came under fire near the Sufa crossing point. Elsewhere, Palestinians damaged a settler's car at Tolkarm, and overnight Palestinian stone throwers attacked a settler while she drove close to the Aroub camp in al-Khalil (Hebron). A Palestinian youth was moderately injured during clashes with occupation forces close to the Homesh settlement.

 

Palestinian Official Says Attacks Against Zionist Settlers Justified Since They Are armed Militia …(3)

Palestinian Authority Secretary-General Ahmed Abdel Rahman defended attacks against Zionist settlers in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. “The Jewish settlers in the territories are not civilians, but are an armed militia that need to be fought,” Abdel Rahman said. He said that the settlers were attacking Palestinians with the backing of the “Israeli” occupation army, adding that the Palestinians had no choice but to defend themselves. Meanwhile, Jose Bove, the French farmer who campaigns against globalization, has been released following his arrest by “Israeli” occupation troops in the West Bank. He was arrested Wednesday with several others for demonstrating against a Jewish settlement south of Beit Laham (Bethlehem). The French anti-globalisation icon has told a Al-Quds news conference that peace between Palestinians and “Israelis” will not be achieved as long as Jewish settlers remain in the West Bank and Gaza. The Confederation Paysanne, that he heads, said Bove and representatives of 11 other human rights organizations had begun a four-day visit to the West Bank and Gaza Strip on Monday.

 

Palestinian Dismisses “Israel’s” Demand for Arrests…(4)

Palestinian negotiator Nabil Shaath, in Washington to see U.S. leaders, contemptuously dismissed on Thursday an “Israeli” demand that the Palestinian Authority arrest hundreds of alleged militants without evidence that they were planning attacks on “Israelis”.

Shaath said that if the “Israelis” wanted the Palestinians to arrest someone, they would have to submit a court order giving basic information about why the suspect was wanted.

Arresting militants is one of the requirements of the agreement that CIA Director George Tenet negotiated between “Israel” and the Palestinians last week, but the two sides continue to disagree over details of the requirement.

“Israel’s” security Cabinet accused the Palestinian side on Wednesday of failing to carry out its obligations under the deal.

"The “Israeli” government determines that the Palestinian Authority has yet to fulfill its obligations under the Tenet document -- stopping terror, arresting terrorists, halting incitement and foiling attacks," it said.

Shaath told the Washington Institute for Near East Policy that there had to be a balance between saving lives and respecting the human rights of potential detainees.

"To say, 'Go back and arrest everybody who is known to have been a sympathizer of Hamas and all those quote unquote usual suspects that “Israel” has in its annals that you should collect every time “Israel” says so' -- I'm sorry," he said.

"When there is absolutely no suggestion that these people are planning something, but they are to be arrested because “Israel” says they are to be arrested, that is the bulls--t. We are not going to arrest," he added.

The text on arrests in the Tenet agreement does not specify any arrangements for deciding who should be arrested.

It says: "The PNA (Palestinian Authority) will move immediately to apprehend, question and incarcerate terrorists in the West Bank and Gaza and will provide the security committee the names of those arrested."

Shaath, who is minister of planning and international cooperation in the Palestinian Authority, said he made the Palestinian position on arrests clear to U.S. National Security Adviser Condoleezza Rice at a meeting on Thursday.
