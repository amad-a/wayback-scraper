Local lawyer files suit against “Israeli” PM over massacres…(1)

Local lawyer May Khansa filed a lawsuit against “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon Wednesday on charges of committing massacres in the Palestinian refugee camps of Sabra and Shatila in 1982.
Khansa, who said the Lebanese judiciary had the authority to try Sharon because the killings took place here, claimed that Sharon’s crimes were not subject to the statute of limitations because Lebanon was still exposed to “Israeli” aggression.
Local authorities said early this week they were planning to re-open an investigation into the massacres to indict Sharon for his role in the slaughter of at least 800 Palestinians.
Khansa feels that Sharon himself had admittedly turned to the Phalange Party for help in order to carry out the massacres.
Khansa also based her suit on reports and documentaries broadcast by Al-Jazeera Television which revealed the roles of both former MP Elie Hobeika, the commander of the Phalange militia forces in Sabra and Shatila, and former Phalange commander Fouad Abu Nader.
The lawyer submitted a list of 24 witnesses, Lebanese and Palestinians, who sustained total or partial damage due to the crimes.

Ex-detainee’s death ‘caused by torture’…(2)

The Follow-Up Committee for the Support of Lebanese Detainees in “Israeli” Prisons announced Wednesday the death of former inmate Hani Noureddine.
The committee said that the death of the former detainee, aged 53, was due to various ailments caused by torture when Noureddine was detained in the Ansar detention center.
The Ansar prison in south Lebanon was used by the “Israeli” Army to hold prisoners between 1982 and 1985. The prison was closed when the “Israelis” withdrew to the perimeters of the former occupation zone.
Noureddine was detained in July 1982 and released a year later.
The committee said that torture led to Noureddine developing a severe case of rheumatism and an infected lung.
He had undergone extensive hospital treatment for his ailments until his death.
The committee partially blamed government neglect for Noureddine’s death and highlighted the lack of serious attention to the needs of former detainees.
Noureddine was a resident of Jbaa in Iqlim al-Touffah and was a member of the village’s municipal council. A commemoration will be held Sunday in Jbaa.

 

Lebanon court hands down 22 sentences for links with “Israel”…(3)

Beirut's military court handed down Wednesday another 22 sentences against Lebanese accused of collaborating with the "“Israeli” enemy," judicial sources said.

Four people received sentences in absentia ranging between 15 years and life for "collaboration with the enemy, giving information about resistance fighters and kidnapping," they said.

Two people were sentenced to one month in jail for "entering enemy territory."

The remainder received sentences of between six months and five years in jail for collaboration with or enrollment in the now-disbanded “Israeli”-allied South Lebanon Army militia.

The trials against those accused of collaborating with “Israel” started 10 days after the May 24, 2000 pullout of “Israeli” troops from south Lebanon. Since then, Beirut's military court has tried 2,698 suspected collaborators.

So far, 31 have been sentenced to death, all of them in absentia.

 

Authorities probe letter from “Israel”…(4)

Security personnel have intercepted a letter posted from “Israel” containing a report by an “Israeli” human-rights group to its local counterpart, based in Qornet al-Hamra.
The letter, written in Hebrew, details “the sufferings of the “Israeli” people” during the intifada, at the hands of Palestinians.
State Prosecutor Adnan Addoum, who received a copy of the letter on Wednesday, has ordered the Central Criminal Investigations Department to look into the case.

 

Assad warns “Israel” to stop warmongering
Incursion into Occupied Territories would ‘end’ chances for agreement…(5)

Syrian President Bashar Assad wound up his first official visit to France on Wednesday with a stern warning that an “Israeli” incursion into Palestinian territories to quell the intifada would kindle a new war.
But as he painted a gloomy picture of the region, Assad also was careful to make an appeal on behalf of Lebanon, “which Syria, the Arabs and the international community must support” to reverse its economic crisis.
Assad spoke on the final day of a three-day visit to Paris that was tarnished by protests from Jewish lobbyists and Lebanese expatriates opposing the Syrian military presence here.
“If “Israel” enters the Occupied Territories, it would end the peace process,” Assad said.
“Israeli” Premier Ariel Sharon, he added, is “pushing the region toward war. Syria does not want war and neither do other Arab countries. But “Israeli” behavior is going in the direction of war.”
He criticized President Bush’s administration for failing to lean on Sharon to salvage the peace process, and said that at present he had no intention of visiting Washington.
“The new American government has not really acted strongly … to defend the peace process,” he argued.

Palestinian Mujahedeen Hit Several “Israeli” Occupation Targets, Amid More Clashes…(6)

Confrontation between the Palestinians and the enemy soldiers took place Wednesday in the West Bank town of Khalil (Hebron) in defiance of the ceasefire agreement as long as the land is usurped and the Palestinian people made to suffer. During a crowded meeting in Al-Nussayrat Refugee Camp in the Gaza Strip, Islamic movement Hamas affirmed that it will not yield to any American-brokered plan and promised to take revenge from the “Israelis” and to proceed in the Intifadah. In this context, the Palestinian mujahedeen carried out several operations against “Israeli” occupation outposts and patrols in several areas of the Gaza Strip and the West Bank. Palestinian mujahedeen opened fire on a settler’s car when passing near the Neve Dekalim settlement. The enemy Radio reported that the settler fled to a nearby building afterwards. The mujahedeen, moreover, projected anti-armor shells and grenade bombs on “Israeli” occupation outposts in Rafah near the border with Egypt. The same outpost, along with other “Israeli” positions and patrols were earlier in the morning targeted by the intifadah men with bullets and grenade bombs. The mujahedeen also detonated roadside bombs in Nablus when an “Israeli” army patrol was passing by. Palestinian sources said that an “Israeli” occupation force armed with five tanks headed toward the village of Syla which is subject to curfew for 8 days. The enemy troops clashed with the villagers who emerged to defend their land. In the West Bank, Hassan Abu Shayreh, who killed an “Israeli” military intelligence officer 12 days ago, was buried hours after the “Israelis” returned his body, which bore signs of torture what suggests that the martyr may not have died immediately after killing the “Israeli” officer. Earlier Tuesday, Mahmoud Mumtir, 15, died in a Jordanian hospital 6 month after being wounded in clashes with the “Israeli” occupation troops in Kalandia refugee camp in the West Bank.

 

“Israeli” Police admit rubber bullet killed Nazareth man in riots…(7)

While testifying about the shooting to death of Iad Lawabneh, 21, in central Nazareth last October 2, members of a police anti-terror unit yesterday told the Or Commission they had fired rubber bullets at close range.
Lawabneh was shot in the chest, apparently by rubber-coated steel bullets fired from 30 meters. He died en route to hospital.
Yesterday's hearing marked the first public airing of details in Lawabneh's killing. Arab residents of Nazareth say he was shot while picking up a tear gas cannister that had fallen close to him to hurl it back at policemen.
The director of Nazareth's Anglican hospital, Dr. Bashara Nahla, testified yesterday that Lawabneh was wounded around the heart. In his examination of the wounds, Dr. Nahla judged that Lawabneh had been shot by rubber-coated bullets because there were bullet entry wounds, but no exit marks.
Witnesses said Lawabneh was part of a group of stone-throwing young people in an alley off Nazareth's main Paulus III street. Several policemen tried to stop this group approaching the main street - local residents say they used tear gas and rubber bullets to disperse the group.
The eyewitness descriptions indicate that police fired at the youths from around 25 to 30 meters in apparent disregard of police regulations prohibiting the firing of rubber bullets from less than 40 meters.
Four policemen at the scene of this clash told their stories to the Or panel yesterday and police officer Rafi Cohen's testimony was especially revealing. Cohen appeared reluctant to cooperate with the commission - appointed to investigate the October clashes in which 13 Arabs died.
Responding to questions from the panel, Cohen said "at first, I didn't want to speak. If there is a way to exempt me from this disgraceful [commission] process, it would make me happy." He said the establishment of the Or Commission was a "shame" and a political sop to the Arab population in the period before the election for prime minister.
In past testimony given to Or Commission investigators, Cohen criticized the "policy of restraint" he said was imposed on himself and his police colleagues during the October clashes. He told the investigators that while on the run, he had fired rubber bullets at a crowd of demonstrators. He said "firing rubber bullets at a specific individual simply isn't effective."
Cohen's admission went against policy procedures outlined by police officers who have previously testified to the Or panel. They said firing rubber bullets on the run, and firing indiscriminately into a crowd, is prohibited by rules of engagement. They said rubber bullet aim must be precise and directed at the legs of an individual who poses a clear threat to police or others.
Asked about the contradiction between admissions he had made in past testimony to Or investigators, and the procedures outlined in testimony by senior police officers, Cohen said the Or investigator had failed to understand him.

 

Druze of Occupied Palestine to Support Intifadah by Not Serving the Occupation Army…(8)

A delegation of Druze personalities in the Zionist Entity called on the the Druze living in the enemy state to refuse serving in the Zionist occupation army and support the Palestinian intifadah, the delegation informed Walid Jumblatt, a Lebanese Druze deputy and chief of the Progressive Socialist Party, in a letter sent to him.

 

Intifadah Gives a Heavy Blow to Zionist Tourism…(9)

The Zionist Entity tourism sector received a heavy blow when two big Swedish tourism agencies announced that the trips of around 15 thousand tourists to the town of Eilat this week have been canceled due to security reasons. Worth mentioning the Palestinian uprising has severely harmed tourism in the Zionist Entity. Officials there admitted that tourism has decreased by 60 percent. Meanwhile, the “Israeli” enemy Radio uncovered today that tough security measures are being taken by the the “Israeli” occupation authority to protect its interests, its embassies and diplomatic missions all over the world against any potential attack. “Israeli” daily Ha’artez highlighted today that these speculations are based on specific information and not only on threats.
