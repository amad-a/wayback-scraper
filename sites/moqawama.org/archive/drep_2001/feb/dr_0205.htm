Lebanese teenager loses foot after stepping on mine in former “Israeli” area…(1)

“ A Lebanese teenager's foot was blown up Sunday when he stepped on a mine in the region formerly controlled by the “Israeli” army, police said.

Ali Hani Srur, 18, stepped on the mine in the Aita al-Shaab village, some 500 meters (yards) from the “Israeli” border. He was taken to a hospital where his foot was amputated, the police said.

The incident brings to nine dead and 67 injured the number of civilian victims from mines left over since “Israel’s” 22-year occupation of south Lebanon.

Another 12 demining experts from the Lebanese army have also been hurt.

The United Nations estimates that “Israel” left behind at least 130,000 mines in its former self-styled security zone before leaving in May.

UN peacekeepers, who complain that “Israel” has not been forthcoming with maps of the mines, said January 15 that they have dismantled 2,159 mines and explosives from the area.

Hariri says “Israeli” violations is a provocative act before election…(2)

Lebanese Prime Minister Rafik Hariri confirmed the “Israeli” violation of the "blue-line" that marks the border line between Lebanon and occupied Palestine, according to the U.N..

Hariri said "“Israel”" is trying to take advantage of Ghajar village divided by the blue-line, and perhaps would try to provoke Lebanon and the resistance on the eve of the “Israeli” election.

Hariri said we will not be dragged by this provocation and the government is performing its duties to confront this issue.

Earlier, the United Nations acknowledged the violation and requested that occupation forces remove it promptly.

A joint Lebanese and UN field observation team will examine the site in the coming hours.

The United Nation Secretary General representative Steffan de Mistura said after meeting Lebanese Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud, that the violation is not acceptable to the United Nations, and added he saw the violation with his own eyes when he flew by a helicopter over the area.

He received a confirmation from “Israeli” Foreign Minister Shlomo Ben Ami to remove the violation.

Withdrawal was more of a rout, says Lahd…(3)

The man who commanded the now-defunct “Israeli”-proxy said Saturday that “Israel’s” military withdrawal last May was a rout. Antoine Lahd said in an interview on “Israeli” television that the ““Israeli” withdrawal was a stampede in the face of Hizbullah.”
Caretaker “Israeli” Prime Minister Ehud Barak, running an uphill battle to be re-elected next Tuesday, has portrayed the May 24 pullout in his campaign as an accomplishment of his government. “The withdrawal from southern Lebanon has brought neither security nor peace,” said the 73-year-old Lahd, who has been living in a luxury Tel Aviv hotel since fleeing Lebanon during the pullout.

Facility for victims of Khiam opens its doors
‘We heal the wounds, visible and invisible … suffered as a result of torture’…(4)

A rehabilitation center equipped to heal the physical and mental wounds of former Khiam detainees opened its doors for the first time on Saturday.
Undeterred by heavy rain, hundreds of people, including Social Affairs Minister Asaad Diab and Minister of State Nazih Beydoun, attended the inauguration of the Khiam Medical and Psychological Center. Social and health workers, as well as former detainees, were also on hand.
“We heal the wounds, visible and invisible, which detainees suffered as a result of torture and the violation of their human rights,” said France Arrestat, head of the French delegation of Medecins du Monde.
The international relief group, along with the Amel Association, used a 310,000 euro grant from the European Union’s European Community Humanitarian Office (ECHO) to set up the center.
Its team of local and foreign psychologists and social workers will provide psychological support to the 3,000 former Khiam detainees and their families. Physicians, physiotherapists, and nurses will also be employed to treat detainees’ physical ailments.
The majority of Khiam detainees developed severe health problems due to the abominable conditions within the detention camp. In addition to frequent torture and maltreatment, factors such as darkness, humidity, and poor hygiene in the prison cells contributed to the former detainees’ poor health.
According to Mahmoud Khsheish, who spent 13 years incarcerated in Khiam, many detainees’ prisoners’ illnesses only surfaced after their release.
Abdullah Attieh, who spent four years in detention, is near-sighted, a condition he attributes to the pitch darkness of seven months in solitary confinement.
“I have one degree of myopia for every month of solitary confinement I spent,” he said jokingly, adding that he would also seek treatment for his rheumatism at the new center.
Because many of its patients cannot afford to travel to expensive hospitals, the center has kept its costs extremely low. Former detainees and their families pay only LL2,000 for doctors’ consultations and physiotherapy sessions, while the center will charge them nothing for psychotherapy.
“I have no psychological wounds whatsoever,” said Ahmad Yehia, a Hizbullah member who spent nine years in Khiam.
Although he readily listed his chronic physical ailments ­  including rheumatism, chronic bronchitis, stomach infection, and a slipped disc, Yehia was adamant that his mental state was “completely normal.”
“I’m totally fine,” he insisted, adding that any mental suffering he underwent only strengthened his devotion to Islam and jihad.
Amel president Kamel Mehanna emphasized the South’s need for  socio-economic development following “Israel’s” pullout.
“We have to transform the military victory into a developmental victory,” he said.
According to Mehanna, although development is the government’s responsibility, NGOs also have an important role to play as “motivators” of social change.
“We need to work,” he said, “so that others will join us.”
Rene Mimas, the European Commission’s charges d’affaires, said the new center was the eighth project ECHO has financed in the South since liberation. The total sum of EU funds spent on these projects is 3 million euro.
Mimas also pointed out the symbolism of the center’s proximity to the Khiam detention camp, which he said “marked the blackest hours of a still- recent past.”
Also symbolic was the center’s location in a building previously used by both the “Israeli” Army and its militia as a recreational center.
“Do we have to destroy everything the occupation built?” asked Mehanna, who opposes the suggestion that all buildings erected during the occupation and subsequently abandoned should be torn down. His point was emphasized by having the center occupy one of these buildings.
Although the center gives priority to former detainees and their families, it also serves the surrounding population, whom it will charge a slightly higher fee. In doing so, the center will compensate for the shortage of health services and improve the quality of primary healthcare in Khiam and the vicinity.

“Israeli” agressions continue against Palestinians…(5)

Gunfire crackled in the Gaza Strip on Saturday, three days before “Israeli” elections.

The “Israeli” army claimed troops fired at Palestinians trying to enter an “Israeli” controlled area in Southern Gaza, wounding two.

Soldiers also wounded another Palestinian trying to set a roadside bomb in same area, the army said.

Palestinian hospital officials reported a 35-year-old man was brought to hospital suffering gunshots wounds inflicted by “Israeli” soldiers in Gaza, and a 14-year-old boy was also wounded by “Israeli” bullets near the Kfar Darom Jewish settlement.

Another Palestinian, 21-year-old Abdullah Abu Karesh died of wounds five days after being shot in the head by “Israeli” troops near the Mintar crossing in Gaza.

Also several Palestinian buildings were damaged in an overnight “Israeli” attack in the divided West-Bank town of al-Khalil, following a day of Palestinian protests during which “Israeli” troops wounded 10 Palestinian.

Meanwhile, the “Israeli” occupation army increased security measures before the elections and imposed further restrictions on the movement of Palestinians in and out of the West Bank and Gaza Strip.

Head of the Central Orthodox Council for Jordan and Palestine, Raouf Abou Jaber, called on Palestinians, living in the territories occupied since 1948 to boycott the “Israeli” elections to be held on February 6.

Jaber said Barak and Sharon should not be given the votes of Palestinians amid the critical situation and the ongoing “Israeli” aggressions and racist behavior, calling Palestinians to unite.
