Hizbullah ready to step up attacks on “Israel”: Haaretz …(1)

Hizbullah has deployed Katyusha missile-launchers in southern Lebanon, sparking fears of an upsurge in violence at the “Israeli”-Lebanese border, the “Israeli” daily Haaretz said Friday, quoting a military officer.

According to the newspaper's military source, Hizbullah has set up a strategy enabling it to launch an attack "within a few minutes", firing from all along the border on “Israeli” targets deep inside “Israel”.

The “Israeli” army officer added the new long-range missiles used by the movement could reach the outskirts of the northern “Israeli” port city of Haifa.

The “Israeli” army had taken measures to prepare for such an attack, Haaretz said without elaborating.

The “Israeli” air force flew at low altitude over the sector Thursday , including the eastern tip of southern Lebanon, near the Shebaa Farms area, Lebanese correspondents said.

Hizbullah, which spearheaded armed opposition to “Israel”'s 22-year occupation of southern Lebanon, has led several attacks on “Israeli” positions in the Shebaa Farms, capturing three “Israeli” soldiers October 7 in the area seized from Syria by “Israel” in 1967 and now claimed by Beirut.

Caretaker Prime Minister Ehud Barak has often threatened to retaliate with large-scale attacks on Lebanese infrastructures and Syrian positions in Lebanon, but has refrained from carrying out heavy military operations against Lebanon since the May 24 “Israeli” pull-out.

His right-wing rival and frontrunner in the February 6 election for prime minister, Ariel Sharon, has taken a much firmer stance against Hizbullah's attacks, accusing Barak of being weak.

UN officials says “Israel” violating blue line with Lebanon

Army will stop building fence this side of Blue Line

 “Israel” is violating the UN-drawn "blue line" border with Lebanon, in violation of its earlier promises, Staffan de Mistura, UN Secretary General Kofi Annan's representative to south Lebanon, said Friday.

"This is something that is not acceptable to the United Nations," said De Mistura, saying he would send "urgent reports" on the matter to Annan and to UN official Terje Roed-Larsen, who supervised the drawing of the blue line after “Israel” ended its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon in May.

"I visited the region by helicopter yesterday and flew over several times, slowly and at low altitude, and I was able to notice with my own eyes that there was work well beyond the blue line, around 400 meters (yards)," De Mistura said after a meeting with Lebanese Foreign Minister Mahmud Hammud.

De Mistura said he saw "non-Lebanese workers in the midst of constructing a fence identical to that set up to mark the line" that designates the border.

He said the work stopped when he visited. "It is necessary at all costs to verify that this was not a pause but a halt."

"We are working to block this work and to return to the previous situation, as the blue line is for us a reference point," he said. …(2)

Later on, “Israel” informed the United Nations on Friday that it had ceased constructing a new fence on Lebanese territory in the divided village of Ghajar.
The decision came after Washington and UN headquarters in New York stepped up pressure on the Jewish state to end the violation of the UN-delineated Blue Line.
Staffan de Mistura, UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan’s personal representative for southern Lebanon, met Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud Friday and informed him that “Israel” had pledged to halt the violation.
“We have made our concerns know to senior “Israeli” officials who have indicated to us that this construction will stop,” de Mistura told reporters.
“Israeli” Foreign Minister Shlomo Ben-Ami reportedly relayed his assurance that the violation would end to the UN Middle East special envoy Terje Roed-Larsen.
“We will be watching the situation closely over the coming days to ensure that indeed the construction has stopped and that the status quo ante in the area is returned to in order to ensure the respect of the Blue Line by all concerned parties,” de Mistura added.
UNIFIL’s commander, Major General Seth Kofi Obeng, received similar assurances from the “Israeli” Army.
“The “Israeli” Army informed the force commander that work on the fence at Ghajar has stopped,” UNIFIL spokesman Timur Goksel told The Daily Star. “This was verified by UNIFIL through observations on the ground and by air.”
The Daily Star revealed last week that the Israelis had resurrected a plan to incorporate the Alawite village of Ghajar within the “security fence” presently under construction along the Blue Line. The line  divides Ghajar, leaving two-thirds inside Lebanon and the remainder in “Israeli”-occupied Syria.
Ghajar’s residents strongly oppose having their village divided by a fence. But the “Israeli” Army believes that the lack of a high-tech fence in the area poses an unacceptable security risk.
As a compromise, the Israelis began building a new section of fence along the eastern flank of “Lebanese” Ghajar as part of a move to seal off the village in its entirety. The Israelis initially denied any responsibility, however, claiming that the village’s residents had undertaken the work. The UN was unimpressed with the plea of innocence..
 
There was no sign of construction activity on Lebanese soil Friday, although work continued on the new fence along the Blue Line east of Ghajar.
A UNIFIL helicopter slowly circled the scene of the violation before continuing on its daily aerial reconnaissance of the border. Soldiers from UNIFIL’s Indian battalion kept the village under observation from the Abbassieh outpost, two kilometers to the east.
The Israelis had bulldozed a dirt track some 500 meters long parallel to the village, running from the Blue Line to an old fence on the northern end of “Lebanese” Ghajar. Concrete foundations had been laid for about 350 meters.
The Daily Star was able to approach Ghajar and strike up a conversation through the old fence with Adel Shemali, a teacher living in the village.
Shemali was cagey over the
violation of the Blue Line. “Whether there is a breach or not, it’s hard to tell,” he said. “And even if there is a breach, we can’t really talk about it.”
Shemali said the villagers were adamant that they would not permit their village to be  physically divided by a fence. His friends agreed: “We are Arabs from Syria and we do not want our town to be split,” said another resident, Mohammed.
The “Israeli” Army originally proposed annexing Ghajar behind the new fence in November. But the scheme was dropped after UNIFIL warned the military that violating the Blue Line in Ghajar would provoke unnecessary tensions in the area.
“This looks like the typical bloody-mindedness of the “Israeli” Army,” said a Western diplomat. “They knew they couldn’t get away with it but went ahead anyway.”
The diplomat said it was plausible that the “Israeli” government had been unaware of what was happening in Ghajar.
“The military causes the problem and the civilians have to bail them out,” the diplomat said.
Last summer, the process of verifying “Israel”’s dragged on for two months, in part due to numerous violations ­ almost all of them petty in nature ­ by “Israeli” troops.
The diplomat warned that failing to respect the Blue Line was “asking for trouble.”…(3)

 

Anti-normalization Institution calls for release of anti-”Israeli” activists …(4)

The National Institution to resist Normalization with ““Israel”” condemned both Jordan and Mauritania for arresting anti-”Israeli” activists.

Prominent Arab personalities who met at the residence of the institution's secretary-general, Brigadier Yasseen Suede, discussed actions to be taken in solidarity with the Jordanians and Mauritanians arrested because of their opposition to any normalization of relations with the “Israeli” enemy.

They considered the arrests as an attempt to suppress the struggle of the Palestinian people, and demanded the release of Jordanian union leaders who were recently arrested.

409 Palestinian martyrs, 21,343 wounded since start of intifadah …(5)

“Israeli” occupation troops shot a young Palestinian ,wounding him in his leg ,near an “Israeli” school in Jordan valley in the West Bank .The “Israeli” army claimed that the Palestinian was observing the school and that they shot the man when he tried to escape after being arrested by “Israeli” Soldiers.

Meanwhile, “Israeli” Prime Minister Ehud Barak headed a meeting with senior army generals to discuss security measures to be taken following Thursday's killings of the “Israeli” doctor, Shmuel Gillis ,and Leor Attiyeh ,a member of a special “Israeli” intelligence unit.

Barak said in a statement ,that those behind the killings will be punished.

Intifadah fighters went on with their operations and opened fire on an “Israeli” army outpost in the West Bank Jewish settlement of Gosh Katif.

Earlier, another occupation outpost was hit by grenades and gunfire in Nablus.

In Gaza, Palestinians buried 22-year-old Ahmad Mheisan, shot by “Israeli” troops near near the Karni crossing point.

The Palestinian health minister Riyad Al Zanoun said the number of Palestinian martyrs since the start of the intifadah ,in September 28, rose to 409 ,while 21,343 were injured by “Israeli” troops.

Zanoun said that 35% of those injured are below eighteen ,20 % were shot in the chest ,20 % in the stomach ,24 % in their legs and hands ,while 18 % were shot in the head.

The Palestinian health minister said “Israeli” troops used live bullets ,rockets ,and bombs in their attacks against the Palestinian people.

Oslo agreement no more applicable,says National Palestinian Council …(6)

The National Palestinian Council held a two-day session in Cairo, during which it decided to form the National Independence Board, given the aim of working for the total independence of occupied Palestine according to international resolutions.

The head of the council, Salim  Zaanoun, said the Independence Board principally aims at having international
resolutions on Palestine as the main reference for any coming negotiations with the Israelis.

The board also calls for the recovering of Quds (Jerusalem) as the capital of the future Palestinian state.

The National Palestinian Council confirmed that Oslo's principles were used up since the 5-years schedule to implement the agreement ended on May 1999.
