Sayyed Nasrallah, Muslim delegations discuss Muslim's condition…(1)

Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah received in the secretariat a number of delegations and personalities, who participated in the Quds Conference that was held in Beirut.

Sayyed Nasrallah discussed with the delegations ways of supporting the intifadah in Palestine, including the condition of Muslims in the world, and the responsibilities of the Islamic and National Movements.

The Chief of the Islamic Jama'a in Pakistan, Hussein Ahmad, who headed a delegation, said after the meeting that he congratulated Sayyed Nasrallah for the great triumph of the Islamic Resistance over the “Israeli” enemy, asserting that this victory brought pride to the Islamic Nation.

Other guests of Hizbullah Chief was Ahmad al-Shami, Secretary-General of the Yemeni party, Haqq, and the Algerian Speaker Abdel-Qader Samadi who headed a delegation of the Algerian Peace Society Movement.

Samadi said to journalists in Hizbullah's General-Secretariat, "The entire Islamic Nation is honored by the victory realized by Hizbullah, in the scope of the historical conflict between the nation and the Zionist Entity project. We consider this experience to pioneer a new route to regain the rights of Arabs and the Muslim Palestinian people. By God's will, this (victory) will be a positive overture to recover Beit al-Maqdes and the Aqsa Mosque. We truly value the efforts of Hizbullah and its Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah."

Foundation outlines plans for Al-Quds 
Conference outlines strategy for Arabs and Muslims to liberate Holy City…(2)

Where the Arab League failed in mobilizing boycotts and gaining international sympathy for the Palestinian cause, the Al-Quds Foundation hopes to triumph.
However, organizers of the three-day Al-Quds conference, which culminated in the foundation’s creation, still stressed the importance of jihad  as the only route to liberation.
“A necessary strategy … based on the premise that all of Al-Quds is for Palestinians … is the rejection of cohabitation with the Zionist enemy,” the newly elected head of the foundation, Hamas official Musa Abu Marzouq, told a news conference at the Carlton Hotel
on Wednesday.
“Our only choice is jihad in all its forms and using all its tools,” he said.
According to Abu Marzouq, the foundation, which will be “temporarily based in Beirut until the liberation of Al-Quds,” aims to save the city of Al-Quds and reverse recent moves toward normalizing ties with “Israel”.
“We completely reject the normalization of ties with (“Israel”), on a governmental, parliamentarian and popular level,” said the speaker of Yemen’s Parliament, Sheikh Abdullah Bin Hussein al-Ahmar. “The president has refused the visit of any Jew to Yemen.”
Until the foundation opens in a month’s time, Ahmar, who is also the foundation’s vice-president, plans on circulating memorandums among Arab leaders explaining the conference’s recommendations.
“When the foundation opens, we will personally visit Arab nations and coordinate our efforts in refusing negotiations,” he said.
The conference partly focused on reviving the idea of an Arab boycott of “Israeli” and US goods.
“Every pound, dirham or riyal we use to buy their goods is transformed into a bullet shot into the chest of our Palestinian brothers,” organizers said in a concluding statement.
“So we must weaken them any way we can and boycotting is our only method for doing so. With 1.3 billion Muslims and Arabs in the world, we can really harm America and its partners.”
Abu Marzouq also stressed the importance of defining ties with nations based on their stance on “our rights in Palestine and legitimate right to liberate it and on the enemy’s policies and criminal practices.”
Ahmar advocated using the media to gain sympathy, and plans on creating films and programs discussing Al-Quds and correcting misconceptions.
“I’m sure if the American people understood the cause, the situation would no doubt change because they influence their country’s policies,” he said.
The conference’s recommendations also included the introduction of a file on Al-Quds and Palestine to be introduced into education programs in the Arab world, to “make new generations aware and sympathetic of the Palestinian cause.”
A conference on the Palestinian right of return will soon be planned and efforts will focus on highlighting UN Resolution 3379, equating Zionism with racism.
Hizbullah delegate Hassan Hodroj fielded a question on the party’s role in the Palestinian resistance, referring to last Friday’s shelling of Bastara following attempts by armed Palestinians to cross into the disputed Shibaa Farms.
“Of course we support resistance as a necessary strategy in the struggle and we support the Palestinian people in their insistence on the right of return. With regards to operations, we have the best interest of the Lebanese nation as a whole in mind,” said Hodroj.
The conference, organized by the Islamic National Conference, drew several hundred representatives from over 40 Arab and Islamic countries.

 

Delegates tour South in show of support…(3)

Two separate delegations from the Al-Quds Foundation conference toured the South on Wednesday, paying tribute to resistance fighters whom they said should serve as examples to liberate all occupied holy lands.
“It is resistance which will liberate Palestine and, with God’s will, holy Al-Quds,” Iranian MP Ali Akbar Mohtashemi, who headed one delegation, told reporters.
The MP, who was greeted by Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, Hizbullah’s commander in the South, and other party leaders, visited Fatima Gate on the border and toured Khiam prison. He was briefed by former inmates on torture methods used by their captors.
Mohtashemi described the prison as “the fierce Zionist enemy’s detention camp,” and said resistance against the occupation “should serve as a lesson to all Muslims and to the Palestinian people.”
Addressing “our brethren in occupied Palestine and all Palestinians,” Mohtashemi said Arabs and Muslims who gathered in Beirut for the conference on preserving the Arab character of Al-Quds were unanimous in declaring they “will not leave you alone, but will be with you in liberating Palestine.”
Qaouk paid tribute to Iran’s aid, noting that Mohtashemi had joined the resistance movement in its early stages and was wounded. “The Lebanese people will not forget Iran’s huge sacrifices and role in the liberation, this great achievement,” he said. “Iran nowadays, standing on Syria’s side, has considerably changed the existing equation and “Israel” will have to take the Iranian role into consideration in any possible war.”
Mohtashemi later visited Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, whom he invited to attend an international parliamentary conference in support of the Palestinian intifada, to be held in Tehran in April.
“If the new (US) administration wants to follow the same course adopted by the previous one, it will inflict harm on the national interests of the American people and state more than hitherto,” Mohtashemi told reporters afterward.
He said the “enmity between our people and American rulers will not change unless the American administration changes its policies.”
Meanwhile, another delegation comprising dozens of Arab and Islamic participants in the Al-Quds conference also visited Khiam and Fatima Gate, arriving in mini-buses.
They included the leader of Pakistan’s al-Jamaa al-Islamiya, Hussein Ahmad, and his counterpart in Kashmir, Abdel-Rashid Tourabi.
The delegation visited the former “Israeli” outpost of Dabshe, accompanied by the vice-president of Hizbullah’s political council, Mahmoud Qmati, and the head of the party’s politburo in the North, Mohammed Saleh.
““Israel’s” defeat in the South proves that we can liberate all occupied land sacred to us,” said Ahmad.
“We’re very proud of the resistance and Hizbullah’s accomplishments in the South,” Tourabi said, adding that the people of Kashmir supported the resistance in both Lebanon and Palestine.
Accompanying them, Algerian MP Hassan Araybi said Hizbullah’s resistance showed that “strength comes from faith, not weapons.”
“It is an honor to every Arab and Muslim to visit places which gave birth to heroes who accomplished the greatest victory in our modern history,” said Syrian historian Ahmad Houeis.
Houeis suggested that political decisions should be made by the mujahiddin, rather than the Arab League.
Those “who have sacrificed their lives should decide the Islamic nations’ fate,” he said.
However, the visitors shouted anti-”Israeli” slogans, such as: “death to “Israel”,” “we will return,” and “Palestine is ours.”

 

Trigger-happy “Israelis” open fire on ‘nothing’ in Shibaa…(4)

“Israeli” helicopter gunships targeted a “suspicious” object in the Shibaa Farms area Wednesday, but nothing was found.
Heavy machine gun fire and explosions were heard at dusk in Kfar Shuba and several helicopters were seen flying over the Shibaa Farms.
In Al-Quds, an “Israeli” army spokesman said the troops had seen “something suspicious but it was found to be nothing.”
The “Israelis” reportedly decided to destroy the object with helicopters rather than send troops to investigate.
The shooting, which occurred on the “Israeli” side of the United Nations-delineated Blue Line, came four days after two Palestinian fighters were killed and one other was wounded by “Israeli” tanks and helicopters.
The “Israeli” Army is in a state of heightened alert in the Shibaa Farms following several attacks and a renewed threat by Hizbullah to capture more soldiers if “Israel” fails to meet the party’s demands for a prisoner swap. Three “Israeli” soldiers were snatched along the Blue Line last October.
“Israel’s” military intelligence expects further attacks from Lebanon in the coming weeks, claiming Iran has stepped up weapons and funds to Hizbullah and Palestinian groups opposed to the peace process.
In connection with “Israel’s” tightening of security in the area, work has continued uninterrupted on what is thought to be a new fence on Lebanese territory in the divided village of Ghajar.
UNIFIL spokesman Timur Goksel said that a team from the Observer Group Lebanon, part of the UN Truce Supervision Organization, plans to visit Ghajar Thursday to investigate the construction work at first hand.
“There is nothing significantly different,” Goksel said. “There continues to be more work on the new fence.”
Ghajar is divided by the Blue Line with two-thirds falling inside Lebanon and the remainder in “Israeli”-occupied Syria.
Construction work began last week in the Lebanese part of the village on what appears to be the foundations of a new security fence. So far, the construction activity extends around 50 meters into Lebanon.
UNIFIL suspects that the “Israelis” intend to cordon off the village as part of its security precautions along the border.

Nineteen more Lebanese sentenced for collaboration with “Israel”…(5)

“ Beirut's military court tried 25 more Lebanese for offenses connected with the “Israeli” occupation of southern Lebanon that ended last spring, convicting 19 of them for collaboration, court officials said.

The 19 received sentences ranging from nine months to two years.

Another two of the defendants were convicted of violating a boycott on “Israel” by smuggling five kilos (11 pounds) of cocaine from the then “Israeli”-held town of Naqoura to Cyprus.

One was jailed for 15 years and fined 10,000 dollars. The second was sentenced to seven years and a fine of 5,000 dollars.

The four remaining defendants were freed, either for lack of proof or because of extenuating circumstances.

Wednesday's rulings bring to 2,343 the number of people tried since June 5, just two weeks after “Israel’s” withdrawal from southern, when Lebanon began trying more than 2,500 Lebanese accused of collaboration.

Many of those charged have been former pro-”Israeli” South Lebanon Army (SLA) or other Lebanese accused of colaborating with “Israel”.

Eleven people have been sentenced to death and three others to life in prison, all in absentia.
