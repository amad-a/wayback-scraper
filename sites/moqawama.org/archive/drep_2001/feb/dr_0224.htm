Delegation supports Lebanon (1)

A British delegation said Friday it supported Lebanon’s stand that the Shibaa Farms are Lebanese and that “Israel” needs to pull its troops out of the area.
The delegates said they urged both sides to maintain calm in the South but that they supported Lebanon’s position on the Shibaa Farms after meeting President Emile Lahoud.
“We support an “Israeli” withdrawal from the Shibaa Farms because the Shibaa Farms are Lebanese,” said Chris Doyle from the United Kingdom-based Campaign for the Advancement of Arab-British Understanding.
Doyle arrived in Lebanon Wednesday with Lord Phillips, a lawyer and founder of three charities in the UK, and Richard Spring, MP with the Conservative Party and opposition spokesman on foreign affairs.
Doyle said the purpose of the visit was to “look closely” at the way people were living, especially in the liberated areas.” He said Lebanon needed a period of “just and comprehensive peace” to revive its economy.

 

UN mine-clearing team wants to speed up South operations (2)

A team of de-mining officials from the United Nations wrapped up a three-day fact-finding visit here Friday with a call to speed up clearing land mines from in the South.
Martin Barber, the head of the UN’s Mine Action Service, called mine clearance a “precondition” for the development and growth of the South.
“We want to accelerate and increase the capacity, to speed it up and see it through,” Barber told a news conference in UN House.
Clearing the district of mines, he added, would allow social and economic agencies to help “local people return to their land and make it prosperous.”
Barber arrived here last weekend for a six-day visit, during which he met with Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud, Defense Minister Khalil Hrawi, Army Commander General Michel Suleiman, and Mahmoud Othman, the president of the Council for Development and Reconstruction.
Earlier Friday, Barber observed a unit of UNIFIL’s Ukraine battalion clearing land mines near Labboune on the border.

 

A Palestinian Martyr, 49 wounded in a Day of Fierce Clashes (3)

Clashes broke out between “Israeli” occupation troops and Palestinians in the West Bank on Friday.

21-year-old Raed Mahmoud Hussein Moussa was shot dead by occupation soldiers in the West Bank town of al-Khodr near Beitlaham. Three other Palestinians were also wounded and 20 inhaled gazes hurled by the “Israelis”.

In the town of al-Khalil, Palestinian protesters hurled tens of Molotovs at the enemy troops, who fired bullets and tear gas at the demonstrators.

In the town of Ramallah, “Israeli” soldiers were targeted by gunfire, following a demonstration which turned into clashes and during which 40 protesters were wounded.

Heavy gunfights also erupted Friday in various towns of the Gaza Strip between Palestinian resistance fighters and “Israeli” troops.

At least 8 Palestinians were wounded near the Palestinian-Egyptian border crossing of Salahhedine, where the fiercest fighting broke-out.

One of the injured, Talal Abu Arida, was shot in the head.

Hospital sources said a lot of the wounded bled for several hours while trying to reach the hospital because enemy troops blocked the main roads.

Near the Khan Younis refugee camp in the Gaza Strip, “Israeli” occupation soldiers shot and seriously injured a 17-year-old Palestinian.

A source from the Palestinian liaison office said the occupation army shelled a vocational school and a Palestinian house near Deir al-Balah in the Strip.

The clashes came as the occupation army tightened its blockade in the Gaza Strip effectively cutting it in two by blocking its main roads.

Outgoing “Israeli” Premier Ehud Barak ordered the division of the Gaza Strip just two days before the first visit of U.S. Secretary of State Colin Powell to the region.

 

Intifadah Fighters Targets “Israeli” Troops,Settlements (4)
The outbreak of clashes in the Palestinian territories followed a series of intifadah operations against the “Israeli” occupation.

Earlier in the morning, Palestinian resistance fighters detonated two roadside bombs, one on the road between the Mintar checkpoint at the eastern end of the Gaza Strip and the Netzarim Settlement and another directed at soldiers patrolling near an “Israeli” settlement.

The occupation army admitted an “Israeli” soldier was wounded.

Palestinian fighters also fired mortars Thursday night at the Jewish settlement of Aley Sinai. This is the fourth such attack at Jewish settlements.

The occupation army Radio said that “Israeli” military outposts in the Nevi Dekalim settlements was also subject to heavy gunfire.

 

“Israel” Seized Samples of Uranium to Cover Up its Crimes (5)

In order to wrap its crimes, the “Israeli” occupation forces confiscated uranium samples while they were being transported by the Palestinian Authority from Gaza Airport to some international laboratories for tests procedures.

The Palestinian Environment Minister,Youssef Abu Saffiyya, said he ministry is planning to send samples of the harmful and toxic materials that the enemy used against the Palestinians to other nations.

However, the ministry is unable to do so because of the “Israeli” enemy blockade.

A  7-month pregnant Palestinian woman lost her embryo Thursday after inhaling the black gas “Israeli” troops used in the West Bank town of Jenine.

Tens of Palestinians also suffered from health problems because of that gas.

 

“ISRAEL” VIOLATES BORDER AND WARNS OF "INITIATING" ATTACKS ON HIZBOLLAH (6)

“Israel” has again violated Lebanon's border. An “Israeli” infantry unit was seen crossing the UN-demarcated border at Tallet Meghar near the occupied Shibaa Farms. “Israeli” troops also opened gunfire along the border. The latest frontier incident coincides with calls from “Israel’s” top army brass to "strike out at Hizbollah" even at the risk of drawing Katyusha rocket attacks on northern settlements. The Jerusalem Post daily quoted senior military officials as saying the army should restore what they described as its "deterrent power" by initiating attacks on Hizbollah near the Shibaa Farms. They said any Hizbollah response should result in strikes deep inside Lebanon. Military officials said a quote policy of restraint must end. “Israel” had vowed to retaliate at the "appropriate time" following a Hizbollah resistance attack on the Shibaa Farms that killed an “Israeli” soldier last week. "Bloodshed on massive scale" looming: UN envoy (7)

The United Nations special Middle East envoy Terje Larsen has warned of the threat of bloodshed and instability throughout the region unless the Palestinian Authority's financial situation is improved. Larsen said chaos and anarchy could grip the region if Palestinian public employees like teachers, nurses and security personnel are not paid. “Israel” has been withholding Palestinian funds, effectively forcing the shut down of many institutions. The US and the UN have been urging “Israel” to release the money. The UN relief agency for Palestinian refugees has also been appealing to donor countries for contributions to alleviate worsening hardships. UNRWA unveiled that half of the population of Gaza and one third in the West Bank now lives below poverty line. UN envoy Larsen also warned of "bloodshed on a massive scale" if the Middle East peace process crumbles due to the Authority's financial weakness.
