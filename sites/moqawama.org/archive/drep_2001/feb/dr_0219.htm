Nasrallah slams US Ambassador for denouncing Hizbullah’s attack
Qassem we shall liberate Shibaa Farms by armed force…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, accused the United States on Sunday of blocking foreign investment to pressure Lebanon into signing a peace agreement with “Israel”.
Nasrallah made the comments at a memorial gathering for the late president of the Higher Shiite Council, Sheikh Mohammed Mehdi Shamseddine, who died last month.
The Hizbullah leader’s remarks came one day after US Ambassador David Satterfield spoke of the harmful economic effects of Hizbullah’s attack.
“This is not a question of the Shibaa Farms,” Nasrallah said. “Only when Lebanon gives in and signs a settlement agreement based on the Zionists’ conditions, the American master might, just might, extend a helping hand.”

Also, Hizbullah’s deputy secretary-general, Naim Qassem, rejected “Israeli” and UN charges that the Friday attack violated the Blue Line, which was drawn after “Israel’s” May withdrawal from the South.
“We have never heard, in our entire history, of a ‘Blue Line’ being regarded as an international border,” Qassem said during a ceremony in Beirut’s southern suburbs.
“This (line) was written and made public to serve Zionist interests,” the resistance leader argued. “The Shibaa Farms are Lebanese and we shall liberate them by armed force.”
Replying to Satterfield, Qassem said: “Are the “Israeli” crimes against the Palestinians not provocative? And were the “Israeli” attacks against Lebanon not provocative? As for the Shibaa Farms, is their continued occupation by the “Israelis”, and the continued detention of Lebanese political prisoners in “Israel”, not provocative?”
He stressed that the attack was “in defense of our land and it was a legitimate right to the resistance forces to launch it.”
He dismissed what he said was linkage between economic aid for Lebanon and halting resistance actions in the South.
“We are not among those who seek economic aid through selling their pride and independence, even if this costs us assistance from donor states,” he declared, adding that “the Americans are the main source of trouble for Lebanon. Their economic aid is nothing but a political and cultural trap. Thus, we should continue with our struggle.”

Information Minister Ghazi Aridi said Saturday that attacks against the Jewish state “had not stopped,” and that the resistance would make the decision to halt its operations. In an interview with Egyptian radio, Aridi noted that “Israeli” warplanes had violated Lebanese air space in recent weeks “on an almost daily basis.”
Also on Saturday, Speaker Nabih Berri visited Damascus, where he conferred with Assad on the latest local and regional developments. Berri later lunched with Syrian Vice-President Abdel-Halim Khaddam.

Jewish state debates retaliation…(2)

“Israel” responded to Friday’s deadly attack with mixed signals over the weekend with calls for attacks on Lebanon’s infrastructure balanced by a desire for restraint.
The chief of staff, Lieutenant General Shaul Mofaz, blamed Lebanon and Syria for Hizbullah’s ambush of an “Israeli” convoy, in which one “Israeli” soldier was killed and at least two others wounded. Mofaz hinted that “Israel’s” policy of restraint may not last much longer.
“In the days to come we will hold deliberations on the circumstances surrounding the attack, and when we reach the conclusion to act, we will act,” Mofaz warned.
“Israel’s” outgoing premier, Ehud Barak, said: “Anyone who thinks “Israel” will not retaliate for the Hizbullah missile attack on Har Dov (the Shibaa Farms) is mistaken.”
“Israeli” Deputy Defense (War) Minister Ephraim Sneh blamed Iran for Hizbullah’s deadly ambush but said that “Israel” “had no intention” of responding to Iran’s “provocative” efforts to create a conflict along the Lebanese border.
“(With) the situation that exists today, our retaliation a massive one, not a limited one like we did Friday afternoon is liable to lead to a widening of the conflict, including a limited conflict with the Syrians,” Sneh told “Israel” Radio.
“Israel” limited its retaliation for the ambush to shelling aimed at hitting the retreating guerrillas. Hizbullah has threatened to rocket settlements in northern “Israel” if “Israel” escalates.
“This is the reality. Therefore … we don’t have any interest in opening another front, in addition to the fronts we already have today,” Sneh added.
An “Israeli” military source was quoted in the Haaretz daily as saying that “Israel’s” retaliatory shelling on Friday was “not intended to cause a deterioration in the situation along the northern border.”
Senior “Israeli” Army officers have criticized the  lack of response to Hizbullah’s operations in the Shibaa Farms, which began with the kidnapping of three soldiers last October. Since then, Hizbullah has staged three ambushes in the disputed area, killing two soldiers and wounding five others.
Avigdor Lieberman, an “Israeli” MP, argued that the Hizbullah ambush “is worthy of a significant attack on Beirut’s infrastructure.”

Faithful send up balloons along border…(3)

About 80 Hizbullah boyscouts released over 250 balloons into the skies along the Lebanese-”Israeli” border during a demonstration at the Sheikh Abbad Hill post on Sunday.
The balloons were covered with Hizbullah slogans including, ““Israel”: This evil will definitely disappear” and “Al-Quds, we are coming to help you.” Other balloons read “Beware, beware, Zionists, our party is on its way” and “Islamic Resistance.”
Demonstrators chanted the name of Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, while armored personnel carriers and more than 20 “Israeli” soldiers took up defensive positions at the northern and southern entrance to the post.
Some balloons flew over the “Israeli” settlements of Kfar Galadi, Mtulleh and Kiryat Shmona, while others fell on the Abbad post.
Scout Ismahan Najda said: “We will show the enemy that Hizbullah’s flags will fly over Palestinian territories and that they don’t scare us.”
Najda said she was happy to frighten the enemy, who would think that the balloons contained explosives.
Some 500 balloons carrying the same messages were also released from Labboune near the coastal town of Naqoura on the southern border. Many landed in the northern settlements of Shlomi, according to reports. Five “Israeli” patrol cars and some 50 soldiers took the balloons for further examination.
Hizbullah’s sports department organized a 2-kilometer sprint for over 150 students at the Fatima Gate. Spectators said they did not notice any “Israeli” presence or patrols during the race.

Hizbullah's game of cat and mouse with UN force…(4)

By Robert Fisk in Kfar Shouba, Lebanon

At the Clinic for the Children of the Martyrs of Kfar Shouba, Dr Ali is quite straightforward. "The United Nations wanted to put a new post on a hill opposite the “Israeli”-manned border fence and the villagers asked if the UN would protect them. The UN told the villagers that they moved at their own risk. So there was a consensus that the villagers didn't want the UN to have a position there."

Now in Kfar Shouba – a 1,000-year-old, dirt-poor hamlet that lives off its olives, figs and corn – "consensus" is hard to come by when both the village mukhtar (leader) and his deputy have long since died.

But the United Nations have a pretty shrewd idea that "consensus" means "Hizbullah" and that the warriors of the Party of God had their own reason to object when the UN's Indian battalion turned up at Kfar Shouba with two portable cabins for a makeshift observation post opposite the Hassan Gate, a great steel door in the “Israeli” wire. According to UN records, a car load of armed men blocked the narrow road close to the fence and gently informed the Indian soldiers that they really didn't need a post opposite the “Israeli” army. Of course, the UN departed.

Now it should be said at once that Kfar Shouba occupies a rather strategic location. High above the village, in the snow line of Syrian-occupied Golan, two massive “Israeli” telecommunications stations tower over the valleys of Lebanon. The “Israeli” frontier wire that curls through the drab, stony hills separates Lebanon from Shibaa farms, that fraction of territory that belonged to Lebanon when it was overrun and occupied – and annexed – by “Israel” in the 1967 Middle East war. In other words, the frontier above Kfar Shouba separates Lebanon from Lebanon.

And Shibaa farms – in which guerrillas captured three “Israeli” soldiers last year, and killed a soldier last week – is the very location chosen by Hizbullah as an offensive target.

In Kfar Shouba, Heikel Abdullah, a retired 30-year veteran of the Lebanese army, insists the problem is very innocent. "The “Israelis” patrol behind the Hassan Gate and there's a slope from there down to a pond where the villagers used to water their animals," he says. "Then there's a little hill on the other side of the pond – and that's where the UN wanted to build their observation post. We wanted them to build their post at the gate, right on the Blue Line [the UN-delineated demarcation line between “Israeli” forces and Lebanon]. When they refused, we took the view that they were saying to the “Israelis”, 'you can move forward ... down to the pond and we won't stop you'."

The UN Indian battalion take the view that they couldn't see anything if they set up their post by the pond, and precious little else if they parked themselves cheek-by-jowl with the “Israelis”. They wanted what soldiers call a "feature" – a prominent hill from which they could observe the skulduggery going on between “Israelis” and Hizbullah. Which, the UN suspect, is what the guerrillas didn't want.

There's no lack of sympathy for the Hizbullah in Kfar Shouba. It's a Sunni Muslim village and the Hizbullah are an essentially Shia organisation.

But a chance cup of coffee with an elderly family near the mosque proved that among just four elderly residents, eight sons and one daughter had been imprisoned by the “Israelis” during their 22-year occupation of Kfar Shouba. Which takes one back to the little road leading to the Hassan Gate.

According to Heikel Abdullah, just last week an “Israeli” armoured vehicle moved through the wire and on to Lebanese sovereign territory, driving right down to the pond – with infantry support – before returning back through the wire 45 minutes later. Was there, perhaps, a guerrilla plan afoot to attack that armoured vehicle, an operation that was cancelled when the UN arrived with their two portable cabins?

No one knows. But for four days, the UN kept away from the Hassan Gate. Now they are sending patrols back through the village and an armoured vehicle sits on the knoll above the pond during the hours of daylight. Then, each night, it conveniently returns to base, leaving the Hizbullah to move through the darkness. Another dangerous little southern Lebanese compromise.

Four Palestinian martyrs in two days of “Israeli” aggressions…(5)

While clashes continued between the “Israeli” occupation troops and Palestinian people, medical reports said Mohammed Khodr al-Hawamidah was killed when an “Israeli” army vehicle deliberately hit the victim's car.

Another Palestinian, an old man, passed away at an “Israeli” checkpoint near Tolkarm after the “Israeli” soldiers prevented him from reaching the hospital.

Earlier, “Israeli” night shelling of Palestinian neighborhoods in the West Bank town of aL-Khalil(Hebron) Friday night left two Palestinians dead and 10 wounded.  A heavy trade of fire took place across the city between “Israeli” soldiers and Palestinian fighters.

6 Palestinians were also wounded during “Israeli” shelling of Palestinian refugee camps in the Gaza Strip.

On the other hand, Palestinian fighters executed several attacks on “Israeli” patrols and posts.

A patrol near Kadim settlement at the southern eastern side of the West Bank town of Jenine, was targeted with machine guns.

And south of al-Khalil, Palestinians hit an “Israeli” patrol. A similar attack was launched against a patrol in Halhoul town, and a post at the Arial settlement near Nablus was gunned.

Palestinian anger after court frees “Israeli” killer…(6)

Human rights campaigners warned “Israel” of a Palestinian backlash yesterday after the “Israeli” Supreme Court released a Jewish settler who had served eight years of a life sentence for murdering a bound Palestinian prisoner.

Basem Eid, director of the Palestinian Human Rights Monitoring Group, said: "This decision sends a clear message that Arab blood is cheaper than “Israeli” blood.

"The “Israeli” judicial system would not let a Palestinian get off so lightly for killing an “Israeli”. The lack of symmetry will make Palestinians more and more angry. “Israelis” will go on suffering as a result of their own policy."

The Arab fury, stoked by Friday's Anglo-American air strikes on Baghdad, was making itself felt even before the court's verdict. Palestinian gunmen stepped up their attacks on settlers and soldiers over the weekend. “Israeli” troops returned fire, killing six Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza.

Yoram Skolnick, 32, the murderer released yesterday, shot dead Musa Abu Sabha in 1993 after the 20-year-old Palestinian had tried to stab a West Bank settler. When Skolnick fired nine bullets from an Uzi sub-machine-gun into his back, Abu Sabha was already under arrest, tied and blindfolded.

Former president Ezer Weizman reduced his sentence to 11 years, and a parole board recommended he be freed for good behaviour after completing two-thirds of his sentence. Yesterday, by four votes to three, the Supreme Court rejected an appeal by “Israel’s” Public Committee Against Torture to delay the release, which the human rights group said would send a dangerous message to "ideological Jewish criminals".

Skolnick's wife, Sigalit, said her husband regretted the murder. He had, she claimed, shot Abu Sabha in a "storm of emotion" and was no longer dangerous. However, the Attorney General, Elyakim Rubinstein, concluded earlier that Skolnick had not grasped the severity of his crime.

His release comes less than a week after another settler, Nahum Korman, began a stint in an old people's home, doing six months' community service for kicking to death a 10-year-old Palestinian boy who had been throwing stones at “Israeli” traffic.

B'tselem, an “Israeli” human rights watchdog, condemned the verdicts in these two cases as an example of “Israel” closing its eyes to the excesses of Jewish settlers, while imposing long sentences, with no prospect of parole, on Palestinians who kill Jews. "There is persistent discrimination against Palestinians," it said. "The message of today's decision is that it's not very bad to kill Palestinians."
