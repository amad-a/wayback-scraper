Sayyed Nasrallah, Mufti Qabalan discuss regional situation…(1)

Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah received in the Secretariat headquarters the deputy president of the Higher Islamic Shiite Council (HISC) Mufti Sheikh Abdel-Amir qabalan, who was accompanied by Sheikh Abdel-Amir Shamseddine, and the son of late HISC president Ayatollah Sheikh Mohammed Mahdi Shamseddine.

Nasrallah discussed with Qabalan the local and regional situation, especially with the election of Ariel Sharon as “Israeli” Prime Minister.

Following the meeting, Qabalan said he thanked Nasrallah for offering his condolences for Shamseddine's death.

Concerning the next HISC president, the Mufti said it is too early to discuss it before 40 days pass after Shamseddine's death.

Soueid supports resistance…(2)

Jbeil MP Fares Soueid has offered his full solidarity with Hizbullah in its confrontation with “Israel”, the party said in a statement on Thursday.
Soueid, who met the party’s deputy secretary general Sheikh Naim Qassem, was quoted by Hizbullah as saying his visit with Qassem was “to get to know the party’s leadership, and Sheikh Qassem especially, in order to express my full solidarity with the party’s position concerning its confrontation with “Israel”.”
Souied thanked Hizbullah for its “good conduct” in the immediate aftermath of last year’s liberation and for “its respect for the idea of coexistence, and the rights of communities.”
The statement said Soueid and Kesrouan MP Mansour Bone met with Qassem at the party’s headquarters in Haret Hreik, where they discussed a range of topics, such as national reconciliation, the image Christians have of Hizbullah and ways of creating permanent channels of dialogue between Hizbullah and Christian leaders.
In their meeting, the leaders  discussed the Shibaa Farms, as well as ways of discussing Syrian relations with all sects.

President urges unity after Sharon victory…(3)

President Emile Lahoud on Thursday urged the country to focus on correcting the economy and avoiding divisive domestic issues to confront Ariel Sharon’s election as premier of “Israel”.
Lahoud made the remarks at the weekly session of the Cabinet, which put off dealing with several controversial issues, but will press ahead with reforming the state-run media.
Lahoud opened the session by saying that the results of the “Israeli” elections confirmed that “Israel” “has never desired achieving a just and comprehensive peace.”
He added: “Our strategic choice lies in a just and comprehensive peace, while the “Israeli” strategic choice is limited to achieving security.”
Lahoud said the situation required a “united Lebanese internal stand and seriousness in dealing with current issues, notably the economic situation.”
In turn, Hariri said the situation was “not dangerous, but delicate and demands solidarity by all sides.”
“Sharon’s stand and behavior will complicate his activities and he will fall into many contradictions and problems,” Hariri said. “Those who elected him will suffer most.”

Suleiman: Lebanon stays firm…(4)

Army commander General Michel Suleiman said Thursday that “Israeli” intimidation would not make Lebanon change its firm position until a just and comprehensive regional peace is reached.
“This peace includes “Israel’s” withdrawal from occupied Arab territories and the Palestinians’ right of return,” Suleiman said during meetings with Arab military officers at his office.
“If Lebanon succeeded in ousting “Israel” through its resistance, its army, its national unity, and Syrian support, Arabs united can regain all their rights through international resolutions,” he said.
Batroun MP Butros Harb said Sharon’s win meant “the Middle East has entered a new era of conflict and suffering. Sharon’s victory means that most “Israelis” approve of his political and ideological orientations, which contradict all peace projects that have already been discussed,” Harb said in a statement.
He asserted that Sharon’s victory also meant that the “Israeli” public was aware of possible confrontation with “Israel’s” neighboring countries, including Lebanon.
Harb called for an evaluation of domestic politics so that Lebanon could be in a better position to face “Israel’s” new right-wing leadership.
“The internal situation Lebanon is going through is its worst political, economic and social crises,” he said.
He urged the government to form a policy which would “avoid turning Lebanon into the only battlefield.”
The president of the Syrian Social Nationalist Party, Gebran Araiji, said that Sharon’s victory as premier of “Israel” was “a challenge to the Western values that condemn racism.
“Sharon’s words and acts are racist,” Araiji asserted, asking “how will the US and Europe react to such a challenge?”
Araiji praised a statement by the Council of Maronite Bishops in which the clerics called on people to close ranks to confront Sharon’s election.
Araiji said that the SSNP “favored everything that strengthened internal unity in the face of the “Israeli” enemy.”

Security forces go on alert in South…(5)

Security forces in the South have been placed under a state of alert following unconfirmed reports about a number of “Israeli” commandos and paratroopers gathering in Nahariya, close to the Lebanese border.
Security sources said the reason for the reported buildup was not known, but said the alert was in line with Lebanon’s policy of being ready to face any possibility, no matter how remote.

UN sets up monitoring post on “Israeli”-Lebanese border…(6)

“ The Indian contingent of the UN peacekeeping force in Lebanon set up a new monitoring post Thursday on a disputed sector of the border with “Israel”.

Ukrainian mine sappers also set up two pre-fabricated units near Hassan gate in the eastern sector of the border area, following mine clearance work around its perimeter, an AFP correspondent observed.

"This post has been set up to reassure the inhabitants of this very isolated region," a spokesman for the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) told AFP.

The post is right next to the Shibaa Farms area, in the border zone between Lebanon and Syria, which has been occupied by “Israel” since 1967 and is claimed by Beirut.

It is around 800 metres (870 yards) from two “Israeli” positions at Sammaqa and Rueissat al-Aalam on the border with Lebanon.

People living in southern Lebanon demonstrated at Hassan gate recently, accusing UNIFIL of trying to change the position of the blue line mapped out by the UN after the “Israeli” withdrawal from southern Lebanon in May 2000.

However, barbed wire fencing has not been set up over 1.5 kilomtres (one mile) of the new line in the sector, which is still marked out by the old border line dating back to the 1949 armistice.

Cattle from “Israel” frequently cross into this no-man's land, prompting protests from Lebanese villagers.

10 “Israelis” injured in Quds explosion…(7)

A car bomb exploded in the western part of  Al Quds, injuring up to ten people.

Al Quds enemy police chief Mickey Levy said a large explosive device had been used in the bomb, which had totally destroyed the car it was in.

He said nine of the wounded were suffering from shock.

Initial reports said the bomb damaged an adjacent store window and a nearby car.

The attack today is the fifth bombing attack since last November, and comes less than 48 hours after Ariel Sharon was elected
next enemy premier.
