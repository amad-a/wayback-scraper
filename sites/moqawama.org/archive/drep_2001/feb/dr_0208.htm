"No difference between Sharon or Barak",says Hizbullah…(1)

Political assistant of Hizbullah secretary-general, Hajj Hussein Khalil, said that Hizbullah sees no difference between Likud leader, Ariel Sharon, and his rival Ehud Barak, asserting that the party will remain on high alert to confront “Israel”.

"For us there is no difference in our position between Sharon and Barak or others. Both of them represent the same vision and anti-Arab and anti-Islamic project based on occupation, settlement, and atrocities.Both of them have to do with all massacres carried out not only in Lebanon but across the Arab world," said the Hizbullah official.

Khalil added, "There is a hostile “Israeli” project and Hizbullah shall confront this project, may Sharon or Barak be behind it."

On the other hand,a Hizbullah source said that Sharon's election will "complicate" the German mediation for the swapping of Lebanese and Arab detainees held in “Israeli” prisons with “Israeli” soldiers captured by Hizbullah.

The source said that the exchange issue is "now postponed".

"This case will be frozen until further notice," said the Hizbullah source.

“Israel” held up detainee swap by refusing to release Palestinians: Hizbullah…(2)

“Lebanon's Hizbullah resistance group said “Israel’s” refusal to free Palestinian detainees had been holding up German-mediated talks to swap four captured “Israelis” for Lebanese and Arab prisoners. The deputy head of the group, Nawaf al-Mousawi, said “Israel” had agreed to hand over Lebanese and Arab detainees, as well as the maps of minefields in the former occupied zone, that Hizbullah had requested. But Moussawi said “Israel” refused to release the Palestinian prisoners, which brought negotiations to a halt. Hizbullah, whose resistance war drove “Israel” to end its occupation of southern Lebanon last May, captured three “Israeli” soldiers in an October raid on the occupied Shibaa Farms. A few weeks later, it captured an army colonel who worked for the Mossad and has been holding the four ever since. The group said it would release the captives once “Israel” frees all Lebanese and Arab detainees and returns the remains of resistance fighters killed during its occupation of the south. It also wants “Israel” to give Lebanon maps of landmines it had planted in the south and to provide information on the fate of Lebanese and Palestinian fighters who went missing during “Israeli” invasions of Lebanon. “Israel” has described the demands as unrealistic.

Lebanese angry at vote for "criminal"…(3) 

“Outraged Lebanese condemned Ariel Sharon's expected election as “Israel’s” new prime minister on Tuesday, saying he should be tried for orchestrating the 1982 invasion of their country and killing Palestinians.  "In any decent country, this man should not become prime minister. He should be tried as a war criminal," said Kamal Ibrahim, 61, whose wife and three children died during “Israel’s” 1982 bombardment of Beirut. "If there is international law, if there is justice in the world and respect for human rights, this man must be executed." As defense minister, Sharon was the architect and spearhead of “Israel’s” invasion, which killed about 30,000 civilians.  His reputation was further stained when he ordered the massacre of hundreds of Palestinian refugees  in the Sabra and Shatila camps in Beirut.  Many of the Palestinians and Lebanese who lived through the traumatizing invasion huddled together to watch live footage  of the “Israeli” poll, incredulous that the “Israelis” seemed to have chosen the man Arabs revile most as prime minister. "Look at these people, they are applauding for war," said Palestinian Othman Ibrahim, 28, as he watched Likud supporters cheering for Sharon.     "These elections show that whoever kills more Palestinians wins," said Abdallah Salhani, 77, a survivor of the Sabra and Shatila massacre.     "Sharon has been exposed as a war criminal, so what hope do we have as Palestinians?"  he added.   But Lebanon's Hizbullah resistance group, revered by Arabs for ending “Israel’s” 22-year occupation of south Lebanon in May, said Sharon and Barak were cut from the same cloth.  "They are both butchers but the difference between them is that Sharon is a butcher in wolf's clothing and Barak is a butcher in sheep's clothing," Deputy Secretary-General Sheikh Naim Kassem told Reuters in an interview this week.  In the Palestinian camps, Sharon's election brought back painful memories for the survivors of the invasion and massacre.   Many talked about the loved ones they lost and until now Sharon's name is synonymous with terror in the refugee camps.     "He is a butcher and can a butcher possibly change?" asked 40-year-old Palestinian Mohammad Afifi. "He is a reflection of the real image of “Israel”, he is a shame on his people. By voting for him, they have exposed their true colors." Afifi, like many Palestinians, argues that Sharon could actually do good for the Palestinian quest for independence. "I actually support Sharon because his terrorist image will make the world sympathize with us," he said. 

Lebanon between wait-and-see and fear from Sharon "revenge"…(4)

“ Lebanon adopted Wednesday a wait-and-see attitude toward the victory of “Israel’s” prime minister-elect Ariel Sharon, who once drove the “Israeli” army into Beirut, while fearing "revengeful" attacks.

Information Minister Ghazi Aridi said Sharon was "preparing himself for revenge, knowing that some people in “Israel” lived with humiliation from the troop pullout from southern Lebanon last May," after a 22-years occupation.

"Sharon, who never hid his intransigence toward Lebanon and Syria, ... would probably not launch total war, but there is a risk of “Israeli” military operations" against the Palestinians, Lebanon or other Arabs, Aridi told AFP.

"Sharon and Lebanon are like a film noir (one with a bleak view of life). Everyone knows what he did here," he said, referring to the Sabra and Shatila massacres of hundreds of Palestinian refugees by Lebanese Christian militias protected by the “Israeli” army during its 1982 invasion of Lebanon.

An “Israeli” investigation commission ruled that Sharon, who was defence minister at the time, was "personally and indirectly" responsible for the massacres. He was forced to resign.

Asked about possibilities of launching armed attacks to back Lebanon's claims for the liberation of the “Israeli”-occupied Shibaa Farms, Aridi said: "This is a decision pertaining to the Lebanese government and the resistance."

The leading An Nahar newspaper said Wednesday that "now that the ogre (Sharon) has arrived and that the “Israelis” have again said 'yes' to war..., the confrontation seems inevitable and any settlement impossible."

The al-Kifah al-Arabi radical daily even thanked “Israeli” voters for putting an end to the "lie of peace."

The al-Mustaqbal daily, owned by billionaire Prime Minister Rafiq Hariri, run a more moderate front-page headline announcing that "“Israel” has decided to postpone the peace process."

"But the tight majority that Ariel Sharon will have in the Knesset will only give him a limited margin of manoeuvres, this is why the best attitude to adopt is a wait-and-see attitude," said the daily.

A European diplomat here also told AFP that "for the time being, all eyes should be turned toward the Palestinian Territories and Jordan."

"Damascus and Beirut do not seem to wish to test the “Israelis” militarily from Lebanon for now," said the diplomat who did not wish to be identified.

'I want an aggressive “Israeli” to get the reins of power and go to war'…(5)

By Robert Fisk in Beirut

"I hope Sharon gets elected." It seemed extraordinary, to hear these words from a survivor of the 1982 Sabra and Chatila massacre. But Amin Taha wasn't joking. His logic was as cold as the wind that blasted down the filthy alleyway outside his home.

"I want an aggressive “Israeli” to get the reins of power and initiate a war, because then there will be a reaction," he said. "There should be either peace or war. And there's no peace."

Among the tens of thousands of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon, those who would not be allowed the "right to return" to their original home villages in northern Galilee by Ehud Barak – and certainly not by Ariel Sharon – the feeling is much the same: if the Palestinians could be demonised when resisting the army of that darling of the West, Ehud Barak, let the world see how the “Israelis” behave under Sharon.

It was put in more intellectual fashion by a leftist Palestinian intellectual who still works in the camps each day. "You people support the “Israelis” and the Americans support the “Israelis”," he said. "All right, you made Barak out to be a nice guy, even though he wouldn't give us back Arab Al-Quds, built more settlements than his Likud predecessor and refused our 'right of return'. So we were blamed for destroying peace.

"You were embarrassed when you had to deal with Netanyahu [“Israel’s” previous prime minister].

"Now see if you can manage to support Sharon. See if you can approve his behaviour and claim we are the ones who destroy peace."

It's an argument – as dangerous and shrewd as it is perverse – that has spread through the West Bank and Gaza and even through the “Israeli” Arab community inside “Israel”, who were urged by their leaders to boycott yesterday's vote.

If the world gives such uncritical support for “Israel”, goes the theory, let it give the same unquestioning support to Sharon; then see how the world copes when its friend commits another Sabra and Chatila massacre.

Palestinian refugees await "warmonger" sharon, “Israel’s” "true face"…(6) 

Palestinian refugees took “Israeli” right-winger Ariel Sharon's victory in stride Tuesday, calling the man behind the bloody 1982 invasion of Lebanon a true representative of “Israel” who may indirectly boost the Palestinian cause.   At the announcement of Sharon's crushing defeat of incumbent Ehud Barak, the narrow, winding streets of the shantytown of Ain Helweh refugee camp remained deserted as families gathered around warm stoves, gazing at their television sets.   Ruhi Shaaban, 30, shook his head in disbelief while listening to the news bulletin at his grocery shop at the camp located at the outskirts of the southern port city of Sidon.   "Sharon is better for the Arabs and the Palestinians because he really says what the “Israelis” think," he said, drawing nods from half a dozen young men following the news from the grocer's small TV. "It is high time for the Arabs and the Palestinians to ready themselves for war," he said. The elderly Abu Ahmad, sitting on the sidewalk and wearing the traditional checkered headscarf, shook his head and said simply: "There is no difference between Sharon and Barak."   "Both of them committed massacres against the Palestinians and the Arabs. But one of them, Barak, showed a false face of peace and the other, Sharon, shows the real face of “Israel”: a face of aggression."   Maher Shabayta, an officer of Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat's Fatah movement at Ain el-Helweh, also said Sharon represented the true “Israel”: "He is a warmonger and does not want peace." "His victory encourages the Arabs to unite and the Palestinians to escalate the intifada" that erupted after Sharon visited Al Aqsa Mosque in occupied East Al-Quds. Shabayta, still sitting in his office late in the evening, said Sharon's victory "will further legitimize the right of the Palestinians to use arms on a large scale to defend their rights."   "Sharon will never grant the Palestinians their rights; their only alternative is to escalate armed struggle," added one of his aides.   Farther south at the Rashidiyeh camp, near the port of Tyre, refugee Zuheir Kassem agreed that "there is no difference between Sharon, the criminal of Sabra and Shatila, and Barak, the criminal of the Verdun operations."   Barak, “Israel’s” most decorated soldier, headed the commando unit that killed three Palestine Liberation Organisation (PLO) leaders and a number of other people in Beirut's Verdun neighborhood in 1973.  "With Sharon's victory, we can see that the “Israeli” society is moving further toward more extremism and aggression against the Palestinian people," Kassem said.   "We, as refugees, are afraid that Sharon will further block our right to return home," he said, lying down on a futon with his wife and four children while zapping channels to follow the news.   At the nearby Burj Shmali camp, a dozen young men gathered in front of a television set. Everytime the name of Sharon or Barak was mentioned they hurled insults. 

Lebanese army looks forward to liberate Shibaa Farms…(7)

Representing Lebanese President in a graduation ceremony of Lebanese soldiers, Brigadier General Naiim Farah said Lebanon is still living the great joy of liberation of its South, which was achieved by the unity of the Lebanese poeple and the cohesion of the army and the resistance, which astonished the world, in addition, to the support of the Syrian army.

"You will be honored with protecting and fortifying the liberation (of the south), as well as, with liberating still occupied (Lebanese) soil in Shibaa Farms. You shall also take part in the reconstruction of what was destroyed, in order to wipe out the remains of the occupation," said Brigadier-General Farah to the soldiers.

A herdsman escapes death after “Israeli” mine explosion…(8)

Shadi Nader is one of few Lebanese citizens to have escaped from a land mine explosion in the surroundings of former-”Israeli” outpost, Dabshe, facing the southern city of al-Nabatieh.

Nader, a herdsman, was herding his livestock when the mine exploded, killing and wounding a number of  his animals.

On the other hand, Lebanese security sources said that the “Israeli” occupation army shelled Wednesday night the outskirts of Lebanese villages close to occupied Shibaa Farms with flechette bombs. No casualties was reported.
