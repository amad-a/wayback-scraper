A superpower called Hizbullah (1)

It's sometimes difficult to put up with remarks made by senior IDF officers, and the logic behind them. It's as if they try to build up threats that aren't really there, just to get the public's mind off failures, creating alibis for future failures. What's worse, they often ignore how their declarations have a negative impact on the most basic of “Israel’s” interests. That's what happened this weekend when we learned of "a new strategic threat." The senior command, in a well-orchestrated media blitz, once again campaigned to frighten the citizens of the country.This time, out of the blue, it appears that the Hizbullah "is readying for a wide-scale confrontation with “Israel”." And, adds the top staff at GHQ, there's a new twist: the organization, the officers "reveal," has katyushas and some are even long-range rockets. The Hizbullah, they warn, is positioned to fire "across the entire sector." The danger is so great that the Shiite organization "can, if it wants, engage in wide-scale conflict within minutes."
Those who don't know the facts and listen only to the generals' threats, at least according to the reports from the Lebanese border that appeared in the weekend press and on TV, could have become convinced that the IDF is facing a massive military superpower across the border, and not a picayune organization that can mobilize maybe 300 fighters. The Hizbullah, it should be remembered, is a small organization armed mostly with light weapons. It has always had katyushas and the decision to use them is mostly dependent on “Israeli” policy and particularly on IDF activity inside Lebanese territory.
But beyond the facts, where's the wisdom of the commanders of the Middle East's strongest and most advanced army glorifying the threat of the Hizbullah, broadcasting to the public a sense of fear in the face of a threat from such a tiny organization? After all, these are the same officers who complained that the IDF withdrawal from Lebanon harmed “Israel’s” deterence, because it was perceived by the Arabs as a retreat in the face of Hizbullah successes. Don't the declarations that name the Hizbullah as the IDF's main strategic enemy, attributing to powers it doesn't have, seriously damaging “Israeli” deterence?
Over the weekend it turned out that the 70 meters of northern border that the IDF still hasn't fenced is the Achilles' heel of the army, placing the entire country in danger from Hizbullah. Besides the fact that the failure is the IDF's, for not finishing the security fence, presenting it as a threat at the national level is simply an insult to the intelligence. There's concern that more soldiers might be kidnapped by the Hizbullah, the generals keep warning. Of course the danger is there, but isn't the IDF capable of protecting the soldiers on patrol inside “Israel”, and why does it have to declare from the rooftops that there's the possibility of more kidnappings? It seems that the reason for the warnings is that it's difficult to explain the disgrace of the kidnapping of the three soldiers, and now they are trying to present kidnappings as acts of God, emanating from a fantastically powerful organization, and impossible to prevent.
The generals' declarations about the dangers of the Hizbullah cast a difficult light on the IDF. It is as if the commanders are admitting that an army with the most sophisticated weapons in the world, taking a huge bite out of the state budget, is incapable of providing reasonable defense on the border against a few hundred fighters.
One of the generals went so far as to warn that cooperation between Hizbullah and the Palestinians could lead to katyushas from Gaza to Ashkelon. It's not clear what motivated him to tie the Hizbullah to Gaza, but it's obvious the Palestinians don't need the Hizbullah to get katyushas. If they decide to change their tactics and to strike at “Israeli” towns inside the Green Line, they can do it without the Lebanese organization. The Palestinians are not firing katyushas at Ashkelon and not using mortars because they don't want to widen the scope of the violence, and not because they don't have the appropriate weapons.
In the debate over the withdrawal from Lebanon, it was the senior commander that tried to frighten us with warnings that a unilateral withdrawal would lead to a catastrophe, placing the northern settlements of “Israel” in an untenable position. More than eight months have passed and that nightmare scenario has failed to materialize. It's doubtful that the current developments in Lebanon, including the changes Hizbullah is going through, justify the renewed threats.
The absurdity of attributing to the Hizbullah a strategic threat is symptomatic of a certain routine on the part of the senior command. It can't operate without identifying - and nurturing - new threats. The senior officers need the shadow of threats, whether real or rhetorical. That's how they justify ever increasing budgets for new weapon purchases. And that's how they tilt public opinion away from demands to properly investigate the failures that sometimes get exposed.
If there's no threat from Lebanon, they'll point to a missile test in Iran. If the Iranians aren't doing anything, there's a Iraqi tank division moving west. And if the Iraqis are busy with their own problems, it's the Syrian army going on high alert. Thus, “Israelis” are doomed to live in the constant shadow of a campaign of fear. It's too bad, because it's not really necessary.

Sharon remembered in Sabra and Shatila camps (2)

“Palestinian refugees living in Beirut's Shatila refugee camp predicted there will be no peace in the Middle East if Likud leader Ariel Sharon is elected prime minister. While Lebanese and Palestinian refugees fear a Sharon victory is certain to wreck any peace prospects in the region, Hizbollah said both Sharon and Barak have Arab blood on their hands. 

“ Known to many Arabs as the "Butcher of Beirut," Ariel Sharon orchestrated “Israel’s” invasion of 1982, eventually occupying the capital Beirut. In that year Sharon, who served as defense minister explained the purpose of the invasion. [Ariel Sharon, “Israeli” Prime Minister Candidate, saying: "We came here to fight and destroy the terrorist organizations that have been killing and murdering our people for 18 years. This is our goal here."] Some 30,000 people were killed in the invasion, almost all innocent civilians. Much of Lebanon was reduced to rubble. Since then Sharon's name remains synonymous with the worst atrocities of Lebanon's 1975 to 1990 civil war. Sharon masterminded the slaughter of more than 1,000 men, women, and children in a three-day killing spree in Sabra and Shatila refugees camps in Beirut. The massacre, which began in the afternoon on September 16, happened as “Israeli” troops under Sharon's orders encircled the camps. Sharon resigned as defense minister in 1983 after an “Israeli” inquiry found him indirectly responsible for the bloodbath. According to Palestinian survivors of the massacre, a Sharon victory is ominous. Souad Srour al-Mere'eh was 14 when three militiamen raided her home, lined her and 12 member family against the wall and machine-gunned them, leaving them bleeding and dying. Her wounded father died but not before he saw the militiamen return to rape his daughter. Souad survived the massacre but became partially crippled. The past 18 years could not erase the painful memories or quell the anger she feels. "I know that if he becomes a prime minister he will do more than what he did," Souad says, and there will be no peace. None of the survivors believe Sharon, with his record of hostility against Palestinians, will change. Mohammad Abu Rodeina was five years old when eleven members of his family were killed. [Mohammad Abu Rodeina, survivor, saying: "You're asking me about the one who killed our families, destroyed our houses and made us refugees, all the “Israelis” and Jews by choosing someone like Sharon are killing the peace process."] Meanwhile, Hizbollah Deputy Secretary General Sheikh Naim Kassem said both Ehud Barak and Sharon had committed crimes against Palestinians and Arabs. "Sharon is a butcher in wolf's clothing", Kassem said, and Barak "is a butcher in sheep's clothing." He urged Palestinians who became “Israeli” citizens after the creation of the Jewish state in 1948 to boycott the elections. Refugees in Lebanon and Palestinians in the occupied territories consider Hizbollah a role model for ousting “Israeli” occupation troops out of southern Lebanon last may, in a rare Arab victory against the Jewish state. 

Palestinian refugees in Lebanon demand right of return (3)

“Hundreds of Palestinian refugees demonstrated in front of the United Nations offices in the southern Lebanese port city of Tyre, calling both candidates in “Israel’s” leadership elections quote "butchers." They handed a letter to the UN interim force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) addressed to Secretary-General Kofi Annan, demanding the implementation of UN resolutions calling for their right of return home to what has become “Israel”. The fate of refugees is one of the thorniest issues in peace-making. Lebanon wants all 360,000 Palestinians living in the country extradited home. Both Barak and Sharon have ruled that out. Demonstrators recalled operations led by both candidates against Palestinians in Lebanon. Barak headed a commando unit in 1973 that killed three Palestinian Liberation Organization (PLO) leaders in Beirut, while Sharon is remembered by Lebanon refugees for his central role in a number of massacres and attacks against Palestinians. 

Palestinians clash with “Israelis” troops on the Day of Anger (4)

The Day of  Palestinian Anger erupted as “Israelis” cast their ballots. A Palestinian intifadah fighter hurled a bomb on the Jewish settlement Kfar Darom, in the Gaza Strip.

A gunfight also broke out near the settlement between “Israeli” troops and Palestinian fighters.

The “Israeli” army, which imposed a strict siege around the Palestinian territories, shelled Deir al-Balah Palestinian  refugee camp, wounding a Palestinian women.

The bombing also destroyed a dairy products company and caused damage to a number of houses.

While “Israeli” troops continued to arrest Palestinians in villages north of West Bank town of al-Khalil (Hebron), clashes erupted between students and “Israeli” soldiers in al-Khalil and Halhoul village.

A number of Palestinians were wounded in other fierce clashes in Ramallah.

In the western entrance of Hares town in Salvit province, Palestinian citizens hurled stones at “Israeli” soldiers who answered by firing bullets, injuring a number of youths.

 

Facility for victims of Khiam opens its doors

‘We heal the wounds, visible and invisible … suffered as a result of torture’ (5)

A rehabilitation center equipped to heal the physical and mental wounds of former Khiam detainees opened its doors for the first time on Saturday.
Undeterred by heavy rain, hundreds of people, including Social Affairs Minister Asaad Diab and Minister of State Nazih Beydoun, attended the inauguration of the Khiam Medical and Psychological Center. Social and health workers, as well as former detainees, were also on hand.
“We heal the wounds, visible and invisible, which detainees suffered as a result of torture and the violation of their human rights,” said France Arrestat, head of the French delegation of Medecins du Monde.
The international relief group, along with the Amel Association, used a 310,000 euro grant from the European Union’s European Community Humanitarian Office (ECHO) to set up the center.
Its team of local and foreign psychologists and social workers will provide psychological support to the 3,000 former Khiam detainees and their families. Physicians, physiotherapists, and nurses will also be employed to treat detainees’ physical ailments.
The majority of Khiam detainees developed severe health problems due to the abominable conditions within the detention camp. In addition to frequent torture and maltreatment, factors such as darkness, humidity, and poor hygiene in the prison cells contributed to the former detainees’ poor health.
According to Mahmoud Khsheish, who spent 13 years incarcerated in Khiam, many detainees’ prisoners’ illnesses only surfaced after their release.
Abdullah Attieh, who spent four years in detention, is near-sighted, a condition he attributes to the pitch darkness of seven months in solitary confinement.
“I have one degree of myopia for every month of solitary confinement I spent,” he said jokingly, adding that he would also seek treatment for his rheumatism at the new center.
Because many of its patients cannot afford to travel to expensive hospitals, the center has kept its costs extremely low. Former detainees and their families pay only LL2,000 for doctors’ consultations and physiotherapy sessions, while the center will charge them nothing for psychotherapy.
However, despite the obvious mental agony they suffered, many former detainees expressed reluctance to break the social taboos that still surround psychological illness.
“I have no psychological wounds whatsoever,” said Ahmad Yehia, a Hizbullah member who spent nine years in Khiam.
Although he readily listed his chronic physical ailments ­  including rheumatism, chronic bronchitis, stomach infection, and a slipped disc, Yehia was adamant that his mental state was “completely normal.”
“I’m totally fine,” he insisted, adding that any mental suffering he underwent only strengthened his devotion to Islam and jihad.
Amel president Kamel Mehanna emphasized the South’s need for  socio-economic development following “Israel’s” pullout.
“We have to transform the military victory into a developmental victory,” he said.
According to Mehanna, although development is the government’s responsibility, NGOs also have an important role to play as “motivators” of social change.
“We need to work,” he said, “so that others will join us.”
Rene Mimas, the European Commission’s charges d’affaires, said the new center was the eighth project ECHO has financed in the South since liberation. The total sum of EU funds spent on these projects is 3 million euro.
Mimas also pointed out the symbolism of the center’s proximity to the Khiam detention camp, which he said “marked the blackest hours of a still- recent past.”
Also symbolic was the center’s location in a building previously used by both the “Israeli” Army and the SLA militia as a recreational center.
“Do we have to destroy everything the occupation built?” asked Mehanna, who opposes the suggestion that all buildings erected during the occupation and subsequently abandoned should be torn down. His point was emphasized by having the center occupy one of these buildings.
Although the center gives priority to former detainees and their families, it also serves the surrounding population, whom it will charge a slightly higher fee. In doing so, the center will compensate for the shortage of health services and improve the quality of primary healthcare in Khiam and the vicinity.

Nasrallah meets with De Mistura to discuss South’s redevelopment (6)

The moribund economy and outdated infrastructure in South Lebanon topped discussions on Tuesday between the United Nation’s coordinator for the South and senior clerical figures.
Staffan de Mistura, UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan’s personal representative for south Lebanon, met Hizbullah’s leader, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah in the party’s secretariat in Haret Hreik.
A statement from ESCWA headquarters at UN House said that De Mistura and Nasrallah discussed “the pressing need to increase Lebanese activities in the area in order to promote economic and social development.”
“In this connection, we concurred that in the South, poverty does not luckily kill people, but mines do every week. Hence the need is to focus on demining,” De Mistura said.
The “Israeli” occupation created a mini economy in the South, generating revenues of around $40 million a year. At least 3,000 residents were employed in the South Lebanon Army militia and its civilian administrative wing. Another 3,000 inhabitants crossed into “Israel” each day to work in factories, shops, restaurants, and farms. “Israel’s” micro-economy ended overnight last May when the SLA collapsed and the “Israelis” retreated from the South.
Other than some infrastructure work on rehabilitating the water and road networks there has been little investment in the area and jobs are scarce. The employment difficulties are compounded by the absence of almost 6,000 residents, suspected collaborators, who either fled to “Israel” or handed themselves over to the authorities last summer. Most of them were the chief family income earners.
The dire economic outlook is not confined to the villages in the former occupation zone. The village of Yater, 17 kilometers south-east of Tyre, is witnessing an exodus of around 60, mainly young, people every month due to the lack of job opportunities.
De Mistura also raised the plight of the South with the Maronite patriarch, Cardinal Nasrallah Butros Sfeir.
The UN statement said that both “stressed the urgency of some initiatives to bring international confidence in investing in activities against poverty.”
Clearing the district of an estimated 130,000 land mines was considered “a first step to open up for rural development activities in South Lebanon.”
Nasrallah also received Dutch Ambassador Jan-Piet Kleiweg de Zwaan on Tuesday, the first time the European diplomat has met Hizbullah’s secretary-general.
A source close to the meeting said a broad range of subjects was discussed including sectarianism in Lebanon, the Syrian presence, and the Shibaa Farms.
Nasrallah reportedly said he saw no difference between “Israeli” Premier Ehud Barak and the man certain to replace him at the polls, Ariel Sharon, the leader of the Likud party.
“We know how to cure the “Israeli” disease: by resistance,” Nasrallah was quoted as saying.
