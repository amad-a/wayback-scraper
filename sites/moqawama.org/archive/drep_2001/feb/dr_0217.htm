Nasrallah: We are not taking part in intifada
‘It appears that Israel is haunted by a ghost called Hizbullah’..(1)

Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah Friday rejected Israel’s claims that its members were fighting in the Palestinian intifada.
“When we carry out an attack we admit to it, like what happened today, but it appears that Israel is haunted by a ghost called Hizbullah,” Nasrallah said during a mass rally in Nabi Sheet, Baalbek.
The rally commemorated  the anniversary of the death of the party’s former secretary-general Sheikh Abbas Musawi, who was assassinated by Israel in 1992.
On hand were a score of political and religious figures, including Defense Minister Khalil Hrawi, Deputy Speaker Elie Ferzli, and many MPs.
Nasrallah spoke hours after Hizbullah said it ambushed an Israeli patrol in the Shebaa Farms. An Israeli soldier was killed and two were wounded in the attack, the first since Israel elected Ariel Sharon as its new prime minister. The Israeli Army confirmed the report.
Nasrallah confirmed that his group had carried out the attack, but insisted it was not taking part in the Palestinian uprising.
“We’re honored and pleased by these accusations, but we wish they were true,” he said.
He insisted that his party always claimed responsibility for its operations and rejected recent Israeli accusations that Hizbullah is masterminding Palestinian attacks on Israeli forces in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. “We cannot take the place of the Palestinian people and factions.”
Nasrallah also warned that there was no future for the peace process. “Our people have made the decision and the choice of the resistance will grow.”
He warned Israel against considering striking brutal blows against Palestinians. “If you (Sharon) think about carrying out any acts of genocide or mass exodus against the Palestinian people, you will find that they are not alone in this battle.”
Nasrallah, who urged Arabs last week not to fear Sharon, likened the new Israeli leader to a toad “who looks ugly, sounds irritating and threatens and thunders all the time.”
“Sharon is like (his predecessors) Shimon Peres, Ehud Barak, Yitzhak Rabin, and Benjamin Netanyahu,” said Nasrallah, “He does not scare us.”
Nasrallah also praised the Palestinian bus driver, Khalil Abu Olbeh, who rammed a crowd of Israelis, killing seven soldiers and a civilian. “He is a hero, a legend,” he said. “Abu Olbeh’s attack proved that courage will defeat weaponry.”
“The Zionists can confiscate your arms and seal the gates of your borders … but can they confiscate your cars, your trucks, your knives?” he asked.
Nasrallah also asked: “Why is the state’s administration absent from the South? If there is a real problem, let them solve it.”
He asserted that Hizbullah had never been a substitute for the government “neither in security issues nor in economic and administrative issues.”
He said his party was still determined to recover the Shebaa Farms and have Lebanese detainees freed from Israeli prisons.

Hizbullah hits Shebaa Farm
Israeli soldier slain in deadly message to Sharon…(2)

Hizbullah threw down the gauntlet to Israeli Premier-elect Ariel Sharon on Friday by killing one Israeli soldier and wounding two others in the first attack in the Shebaa Farms district for over two months.
The ambush occurred at the southern end of the mountainous Shebaa Farms district near the hamlet of Fashkoul.
Hizbullah dedicated the attack to “the lord of martyrs,” Sayyed Abbas Musawi, a former party secretary-general who was assassinated by an Israeli helicopter gunship in 1992, and “the sheikh of martyrs,” Sheikh Ragheb Harb, the founder of the resistance who was killed in a bomb blast in Jibsheet in 1983.
A Hizbullah statement said the “Al-Aqsa martyrs” group attacked at 12.15pm an Israeli motorized patrol on the Moghr Shebaa outpost road.
The statement claimed a direct hit on a ‘Humvee’ vehicle.
An eyewitness in Ain Arab village, 4 kilometers away, said he heard the explosion and saw the vehicle burning. He said that the vehicle was hit by a missile and not a roadside bomb as was initially believed. Israeli troop reinforcements came under small arms fire from the Lebanese side of the Blue Line shortly after the initial attack. It was unclear whether the missile was fired from the Lebanese side of the Blue Line or from inside the Shebaa Farms.
Israeli retaliatory shelling began around 30 minutes after the ambush. More than 90 heavy artillery and mortar rounds pounded the area between Kfar Shuba, Halta and Majidieh, a former training base for the Israeli-proxy militia.
The shelling spread west toward Meri, Ain Arab and the eastern edge of Khiam. Phosphorous rounds were fired at the single road linking Abbassieh, on the Blue Line, with Meri and Majidieh.
Israeli warplanes and helicopters flew overhead. The Israeli Army confirmed that one soldier was killed and two others were lightly wounded when their vehicle was struck by an anti-tank missile.
The attack was the first mounted by Hizbullah since Nov. 26 when fighters ambushed an Israeli patrol with a roadside bomb, killing one soldier and wounding two others.

Lebanon warns Israel against over-reaction to Hezbollah attack …(3)

A Lebanese official has warned that any excessive Israeli retaliation over a Lebanese guerrilla attack in the border area that killed an Israeli soldier Friday would endanger the security of northern Israel.

The unidentified official, quoted in the liberal daily An-Nahar, was referring to an attack by guerrillas of Hezbollah in the Shebaa Farms, controlled by Israel but claimed by Lebanon.

"Any Israeli response to the Shebaa attack that goes beyond the Shebaa Farms, occupied Lebanese territory, would torpedo the rules in force and, consequently, the Jewish settlements (in northern Israel) would not be safe from a Lebanese response."

The official was quick to add that Lebanon "has no intention" of escalating the situation, but "retains its right (to act) as long as the Shebaa Farms remain occupied."

The Shebaa Farms, a mountainous region at the Lebanese-Syrian borders, was occupied by Israel in the 1967 Middle East war.

While Lebanon claims them, Israel says it captured the territory from Syria and that its future status will only be determined in the context of a peace treaty with Syria.

Hezbollah has said it will continue its guerrilla operations until the area is liberated.

Commenting on the attack itself, the official characterized it as a "message" to Israel and to those Lebanese calling for the territory to be gained by diplomatic means.

"This is a message to Israel from Lebanon, which wants to underscore the fact that it will not provide any guarantees for the security of its border ... except within the framework of a comprehensive resolution."

It is "also a message to local parties who want to separate the Shebaa Farms (issue) from the conflict (with Israel) and call for a freeze in the resistance" to Israel in southern Lebanon.

Lebanon has refused to deploy its troops along the border after Israel withdrew its troops from southern Lebanon last May after a 22-year occupation.

Sharon faces dilemma as Hezbollah raises chance of regional conflict …(4)

The killing of an Israeli soldier and the wounding of two others Friday by Hezbollah raises the chances of a regional conflict if Israel retaliates against the guerrilla group's sponsor Syria, analysts said.

Israel has said it holds Beirut and Damascus, the main powerbroker in Lebanon, responsible for the incident.

It was the first attack by Hezbollah against Israel since hardliner Ariel Sharon was elected prime minister in a landslide victory on February 6.

The attack comes at a time of political transition for Israel with caretaker Prime Minister Ehud Barak tending the government until Sharon announces the formation of his government, which will probably include Barak as defence minister.

The Hezbollah attacks constitute a test for Sharon, who has vowed to restore security to Israel after almost five months of the Palestinian uprising or intifada, which has left more than 410 dead, most of them Palestinians.

Now, Sharon's challenge is to find the proper response in this game of brinkmanship. Israeli military officers have openly warned of the risk of a regional conflict due to the failure of the new Syrian President Bashar al-Assad to rein in Hezbollah.

"The incident originates on Lebanese territory, and those who protect Hezbollah are Syria and Iran," said military spokesman Lieutenant-Colonel Olivier Rafowicz.

The Israeli leadership believes Assad is playing with fire in comparison with his late father, strongman Hafez al-Assad, who died in June.

"He does not understand the dangerous implications of his policies," said an Israeli military officer, who asked for anonymity.

"In a certain manner, we are able to find ourselves in a war that nobody wants," he said. "The risk of a regional conflict has not been higher since the 1991 Gulf War."

Hezbollah has carried out three similar attacks against Israeli troops in the past four months, including a bombing that killed a soldier and wounded three Israeli soldiers on November 26.

On October 7, a Hezbollah commando ambushed an Israeli patrol in the same area and captured three Israeli soldiers.

Those soldiers, and a fourth Israeli captured a few days later, were taken in a bid to arrange a swap for Lebanese and other prisoners held by Israel. They are all still being held captive.

The Shebaa Farms, a mountainous region at the Lebanese-Syrian borders, was occupied by Israel in the 1967 Middle East war and are claimed by Lebanon.

However, Israel says it captured the territory from Syria and its future status will only be determined in the context of a peace treaty with Syria.

Hezbollah has said it will continue its guerrilla operations until the area is liberated.

Israelis have expected Sharon to act with caution due to his sensitivity over his past history as defence minister during Israel's disastrous 1982 invasion of Lebanon.

But since Sharon is on the verge of forming a unity government with Barak as defence minister and leading dove Shimon Peres as foreign minister, the alliance with Israel's left will grant him political coverage for any serious military campaign.

Gerald Steinberg, a defense expert and professor at Bar Ilan University near Tel Aviv, said it is unknown when Israel will offer "a major response."

"My guess, from everything that we know, is that it's not going to be the type of response that we were used to in the last few years," Steinberg cautioned. "It will be a more serious response when it takes place."

Resistance expects status quo to go on…(5)

The timing of Friday’s fatal ambush appears to be a clear signal to the incoming Israeli government of Prime Minister-elect Ariel Sharon that Hizbullah has not abandoned its bid to eject the Israeli Army from the Shebaa Farms, despite the lull of the past two months.
Since Hizbullah recommenced military operations against the Israeli Army last October, a “balance of terror” has existed between the two. It theoretically enables Hizbullah and the Israeli Army to trade blows in the Shebaa Farms without the conflict escalating beyond control.
Hizbullah has deliberately confined its attacks to the Shebaa Farms and to military targets only in an attempt to weaken Israeli justification for harsh retaliation. The party leadership is aware that retaliatory raids against Lebanese infrastructure would likely result in nationwide opprobrium against them.
Hizbullah doesn’t want to sacrifice the plaudits it won for its role in ousting the Israeli Army from the south last year.
Similarly, Ehud Barak has refrained from ordering massive attacks in retaliation to previous incidents in the Shebaa Farms to prevent escalation. Hizbullah has warned that it will fire rockets across the border if Israel responds to its attacks disproportionately. Syria appears to be content to allow Hizbullah to dictate the pace of operational activity. Despite Sharon’s past military record, Hizbullah seems to believe that the “balance of terror” will continue to shackle the next Israeli government.
“If Barak was able to do anything in the South, he would have done so,” Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, Hizbullah’s southern commander told The Daily Star recently. “The reality is that they’re in a weak position. The coming of Sharon doesn’t change the reality that northern Israel is within (our) range.”
But there are complications threatening to upset the balance. The first is that Sharon was elected on a “security” rather than “peace” platform. Hizbullah’s deadly ambush may allow Sharon to make good on his electoral vow and restore the deterrence factor that many Israelis believe was lost by the unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon.
Punishing Lebanon for attacks on the Shebaa Farms which would have the understanding of the international community would also serve to deflect some attention from the crisis in the West Bank and Gaza. In such a scenario, the residents of northern Israel would certainly suffer from Hizbullah’s Katyushas, but Lebanon would fare considerably worse.
Secondly, since Israel’s withdrawal last May, Hizbullah has concentrated on fulfilling more of a regional role as exemplar to  Muslims, especially Palestinians.
Hizbullah may thus care a little less about the opinions of the divisive Lebanese when it knows that its military operations allow the party to maintain a high profile in the Islamic world.

Israelis justify draconian strategy by creating false links to Hizbullah…(6)

There has been a noticeable tendency in recent days for Israel to widen the circle of blame for planned acts of violence in the Jewish state, beyond that of the Palestinians, to include Hizbullah and by extension Iran.
The first incident was the announcement last weekend that Israel has been holding since Jan. 5 Jehard Shuman, a Lebanese-born British national, on suspicion that he was planning guerrilla attacks inside Israel on the orders of Hizbullah.
Hizbullah has refused to comment on Shuman’s arrest, neither confirming nor denying that he was working on behalf of the party.
The second incident was the assassination of Massoud Ayad, a Lieutenant Colonel in Force 17, the personal security unit of Palestinian President Yasser Arafat.
Ayad was killed in Gaza when his car was struck by a missile fired from a helicopter gunship.
The Israeli Army promptly issued a list of accusations against Ayad, charging him with having planned and carried out bomb and mortar attacks against the Netzarim settlement in Gaza, smuggling arms and drugs, and heading a Hizbullah cell in the Palestinian territories.
The Israeli Army said that Ayad visited Lebanon last summer for training and to receive instructions from Hizbullah.
“He maintained continuous contact with Hizbullah officials in Lebanon and was activated by them to carry out terrorist attacks inside the Gaza Strip in the name of Hizbullah,” the Israeli military said. “In addition, (Ayad) was involved in the planning and attempted kidnapping of Israeli soldiers,” it added.
The third, possibly related, incident were comments from the US State Department aired on CNN regarding its unease at a meeting of anti-Israeli and anti-American militant groups in Beirut last month.
In particular, one US official said that the presence of Iranian delegates to the meeting was a “cause of concern.”
Since the Al-Aqsa intifada began last September, Iran has “stepped up its terrorist activity … and stepped up its planning for terrorist attacks,” one US official maintained.
The meeting in question was a well-publicized conference in which some 200 Arab and Muslim figures met to discuss measures to protect Jerusalem from Judaization.
The gathering was organized by Hizbullah, Hamas, Palestinian Islamic Jihad, and the Islamic National Conference. Among the participants was Ali Akhbar Mohtashemi, an Iranian MP, and a former Iranian ambassador to Damascus who played a major role in the founding of Hizbullah in the 1980s.
Ironically, the flurry of accusations against Hizbullah comes at a time when the resistance has been noticeably quiet on the Shebaa Farms front. The last operation carried out by Hizbullah in the area was a roadside bomb ambush on Nov. 26, in which an Israeli soldier was killed.
“There’s been too much mentioning of Hizbullah lately,” said a senior Western military source. The source suggested that the Israeli military was deliberately implicating Hizbullah and Iran in planned attacks in the Palestinian territories and Israel to serve as a pretext for any military action against the resistance in Lebanon. “They are setting up the stage, just in case,” the source said.
Neither outgoing Israeli Premier Ehud Barak nor his hawkish successor, Ariel Sharon, are expected to launch pre-emptive raids into Lebanon for the time being. Given the sentiment in the Arab world, Israel is unlikely to exacerbate hostilities by provocative actions outside the main theater of conflict in the West Bank and Gaza.
However, linking Hizbullah and Iran to acts of violence in the Palestinian territories and Israel planned or executed serves to defuse any misgivings that exist over the Israeli government’s controversial policy of assassinating Palestinian activists.
Ayad may or may not have had links with Hizbullah, but that is beside the point, said a European diplomat.
“Palestinians come in all shapes and sizes, from those you negotiate with in five-star hotels to stone-throwers,” the diplomat said. “But by renaming them collectively as Hizbullah terrorists you are discrediting them to the fullest and gaining a 100 percent acceptance rate among the Israeli public” for the Israeli Army’s actions.
Prime Minister Rafik Hariri echoed that view in his dismissal on Thursday of Israel’s claim that Ayad was working for Hizbullah.
“It’s easier for the Israelis to say that they have assassinated a Palestinian belonging to Hizbullah than an aide to Arafat,” he told reporters in Paris.
Hizbullah and Iran are regarded in Israel, correctly, as being implacably hostile towards the Jewish state. References to Hizbullah and Iran lend the impression that the Israeli Army is not only contending with the Palestinians whose plight draws sympathy domestically and internationally but also a broader, more sinister, pan-Islamist front.
The Israeli Army has long used Hizbullah as a “bogeyman” to its own advantage usually to win increases to the defense budget.
This point was recently illustrated by an Israeli columnist, Reuven Pedatzur, in an unprecedented opinion piece in the Haaretz daily newspaper.
“Where’s the wisdom in the commanders of the Middle East’s strongest and most advanced army glorifying the threat of the Hizbullah, broadcasting to the public a sense of fear in the face of a threat from such a tiny organization?” Pedatzur asked. “The absurdity of attributing to the Hizbullah a strategic threat is symptomatic of a certain routine on the part of the senior command. It can’t operate without identifying and nurturing new threats.

 Israel brings in military build up to South Lebanon…(7)

Lebanese President Emil lahoud on Thursday reviewed with the representative for the UN Secretary General to South Lebanon Stavan Domistora the general conditions in South Lebanon and the work of the UN forces ( UNIFIL) in coordination with the Lebanese security systems.

The Lebanese President learned from the UN envoy about the moves carried out by the UN after Israel's attacks on the Lebanese part of al-Ghajar village on the international borders.

The two sides reviewed the general conditions in light of recent developments and Lebanon's position regarding them.

Meantime, Israel has brought in human and armored fortifications towards its positions facing the South Lebanon borders.

The Lebanese national news agency quoted eye-witnesses at the lines drawn for the Lebanese borders with Israel as saying that Israeli summoned more of its forces into the Shebaa farms.

The Lebanese agency added that more that 40 armored vehicles of M 113 and Meikava tanks arrived in the occupied lebanese Shebaa farms.

The agency continued that 10 trucks equipped with ammunition accompanied the armored vehicles.

It also added that on Thursday afternoon these israeli fortifications were distributed on the Israeli positions in Rweiset al-Sammaqa, Rweisat al-Alam, tallet al-Ramtha, Mazraat Zibdin and al-Marsad in Jabal al-Sheikh.

The Lebanese agency indicated that this is the first time since the pullout of the Israeli forces from the Lebanese territories in May 2000, in which Israel brings in such number of fortifications to the Lebanese borders.

On Thursday at dawn, the Israeli forces opened its machine- guns fire against al-Arqoub heights in the eastern sector of South Lebanon. 

Palestinians show support for Abu Olbeh’s bus attack..(8)

More than 5,000 Palestinians marched in Ain al-Hilweh refugee camp Friday to show support for the intifada and to glorify Wednesday’s killing of eight Israelis by a Palestinian bus driver.
“Faster, faster, Khalil, don’t spare any pigs,” the crowd shouted as they marched through the camp.
The protesters were referring to Khalil Abu Olbeh, a driver from Gaza City who rammed his bus into a crowd at a bus stop south of Tel Aviv, killing seven Israeli soldiers and one settler. Olbeh was shot and captured after a highway chase.
“With our blood, with our souls, we redeem you, Palestine,” the crowd chanted, carrying hundreds of Abu Olbeh’s pictures.
Men, women and children took part in the demonstration, many clad in military fatigues and wearing headbands imprinted with the words, “Palestine is Ours.”
Members of the military police of the Palestine Liberation Organization were carrying assault-rifles while “lion cubs,” children wearing military fatigues, held plastic toy weapons.
Speakers expressed support for the Palestinian uprising and urged Israel to withdraw from Arab territories in compliance with UN resolutions.
In a speech aired over loudspeakers, the president of the Palestinian National Council, Salim Zaanoun, asserted from Gaza that “the Palestinian leadership remains more than ever before devoted to the refugees’ right of return.”
“The right of return is one of the prime objectives of the intifada,” he added.
Zaanoun said the Palestinian leadership had “rejected an offer of $30 billion in return for giving up the refugees’ right to return” at the Camp David peace summit in the United States last July.
Palestinians demand the right of return for the 3.7 million  Palestinians who lost their homes when Israel was created in 1948, and their descendants.
Separately, the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine in the Central Bekaa urged Palestinians to “unify ranks” to defeat Israeli Premier-elect Ariel Sharon’s projects.
