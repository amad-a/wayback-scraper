UN buzzes “Israeli” Blue Line violation at Ghajar…(1)

A senior United Nations official inspected “Israeli” construction work on a new security fence on Lebanese territory in Ghajar on Thursday as moves to halt the Jewish state’s latest violation of the UN-delineated Blue Line shifted into gear.
Steffan de Mistura, the personal representative of the UN secretary-general for southern Lebanon, flew over the divided village of Ghajar to check reports that “Israel” is building a security fence north of the Blue Line. De Mistura reportedly flew over the village at low level several times.
“He wanted to let the “Israelis” know that the UN is watching them closely,” a UNIFIL officer said.
The officer said that the concrete foundations of the new fence now reach 350 meters into Lebanon.
“Israeli” engineers were spotted last week working north of the Blue Line in Ghajar, raising suspicions that “Israel” had resurrected a scheme to cordon off Ghajar behind the new security fence being erected along the length of the Blue Line.
Ghajar is split by the Blue Line, with two-thirds of the village in Lebanon and the remainder in “Israeli”-occupied Syria. Although the Alawite residents of the village are permitted access to both ends of the village, “Israelis” are forbidden from entering “Lebanese” Ghajar.
Ghajar’s residents have voiced strong opposition to having their village physically split by the fence. To appease the local population, the “Israelis” are running the new fence up the eastern flank of “Lebanese” Ghajar until it meets an old security fence some 500 meters north of the Blue Line.
The old fence was built by “Israel” during the occupation. It skirts the northern and western ends of “Lebanese” Ghajar. When the new stretch of fence is completed, “Lebanese” Ghajar will have been incorporated inside “Israeli”-occupied Syria.
The Blue Line violation was raised during the Cabinet meeting. The Cabinet condemned “Israel’s” latest violation and called on UNIFIL to end the construction work.
De Mistura is expected to brief US Ambassador David Satterfield on the construction at Ghajar in the hope of enlisting Washington’s support in pressuring “Israel” to end the violation.
“We have to make a fuss and a noise over this,” the officer said. “We need to find a way to prevent this from becoming a dangerous flashpoint.”

UN condemns “Israel’s” latest border violation with Lebanon…(2)

By Robert Fisk in the Wazzani river valley, southern Lebanon

2 February 2001

With the naked eye, you can see the yellow bulldozer and the long trail of dust as it pushes the foundations for a new frontier fence north into Lebanon. The Indian UN soldiers watch it through fieldglasses. "How can we tell how far it will go?" Major Chhahal asks. "They've been doing this work for three days, right on the edge of Gaghar."

It all looks innocent. But it is not. Just days before the elections that may turn Ariel Sharon into a prime minister, the “Israeli” army is building a new border fence on Lebanese territory and in clear violation of UN Security Council Resolution 425.

The UN has condemned the "violation of the Blue Line," the UN's own south Lebanon frontier beyond which the “Israelis” agreed not to go. The Hizbollah guerrillas who hounded the “Israeli” army out of Lebanon less than a year ago, and who have their men watching scarcely half a mile away, are well aware of what is happening in Gaghar – and of the excuse it gives them to strike once more at “Israeli” troops.

Like all Lebanese dramas, this one has a beautiful complexity all its own. For when the “Israelis” retreated last May, the UN drew its blue line through the middle of the Arab village of Gaghar. Two-thirds of the village is on Lebanese territory, the lower third is in occupied Syria; the town's few hundred inhabitants are Syrian Alawites – the same sect as Syria's President, Bashar al-Assad – who cling to their Syrian citizenship but hold “Israeli” identity papers. And since the “Israelis” could not build their frontier fence through the middle of the town – and since the residents refused to accept a frontier gate on the “Israeli”-occupied Syrian side – it looks as though the “Israelis” are willing to violate the UN's demarcation to mark a border at least 150 feet inside Lebanese territory.

Because they cannot run barbed wire through the village, the “Israelis” call Gaghar a "soft underbelly" on the Lebanese border, through which guerrillas could infiltrate into Lebanon. Already, “Israel” has violated the blue line by sending ambush patrols into the northern, Lebanese end of Gaghar at night.

But that is hardly the fault of the people of Gaghar. They did not ask “Israel” to occupy their village when it captured the Golan Heights from Syria in 1967 and then annexed the land against international law. The “Israelis”' security problem, they say, is of “Israel’s” own making. If “Israel” left Syrian territory, it would not have to worry about this stretch of frontier between Syria and Lebanon.

The UN, many of whose soldiers are positioned only metres from “Israeli” bunkers, are well aware that they will be caught in a firefight if the Hizbollah decide to use this violation as a justification for attack; which is why Major General Seth Kofi Obeg, the UN's commander, went to complain to General Gaby Ashkenazi, the head of “Israel’s” Northern Command, on Sunday. Not to worry, General Ashkenazi said, it is not an annexation of Lebanese territory. It is the people of Gaghar who are building the new fence, he went on, not the “Israeli” army.

This does not fool the UN. Indeed, there is as much chance that “Israel” would allow Syrians under occupation to decide the route of the border fence as there is that General Ashkenazi keeps a crocodile in his bathroom.

So, earlier this week, Major- General Obeg's adviser, Timur Goksel, took a helicopter along the blue line to look for himself. Flying over the gorge of the Wazzani river, he hovered over the construction. "They are putting in concrete foundations to 50 metres [inside Lebanon] in what looks like preparation to extend the line northward," he said. "The “Israelis” are creating another point of friction along the border."

Gaghar lies perilously close to Chebaa farms, Lebanese territory that was policed by Syria after 1947 and then captured and, in effect, annexed by “Israel” in 1967, with Golan. Already, three “Israeli” soldiers have been captured by Hizbollah on the farmlands and there have been two infiltration attempts in the past two months.

How far will the new fence stretch into Lebanon? In all, it might take in several square miles; big enough to cause a major crisis in southern Lebanon. Meanwhile, beyond the soft waters of the Wazzani, over the long grass and buried minefields north of the village, the bulldozers roar on. In three days, the UN says, it will have a better idea of where the fence is going. Until then, Major Chhahal and his men go on watching. So do the Hizbollah.


“Israeli” warplanes violate Lebanese airspace…(3)

“ “Israeli” warplanes flew over south Lebanon at low altitude on Thursday, the third such incursion this week, AFP reporters said.

The flights came one day after “Israeli” helicopters fired machine guns on valleys of the eastern sector of southern Lebanon after an explosion was heard near an “Israeli” position.

No explanation has been given for Wednesday's explosion, which occurred in the Farms of Shibaa, captured by “Israel” from Syria in 1967 but claimed by Lebanon.

“Israeli” warplaness also flew over southern Lebanon on Sunday and Monday.

Lebanon has lodged several complaints with the United Nations in recent months about “Israeli” planes breaching its airspace.

 

Two Palestinian martyrs,one injured, an “Israeli” killed…(4)

Palestinian fighters shot dead an “Israeli” settler on the outskirts of the West Bank town of Jenin on Thursday.

An “Israeli” army spokeswoman said the man was shot in the chest in the Sabah al-Kheir district, in an area of the West Bank that is under “Israeli” security control and Palestinian civil rule.

The army had earlier located the shooting in Jenin itself.  However, a Palestinian security source said unidentified gunmen had shot the “Israeli” while he was driving on a bypass road near Jenin in an area under total “Israeli” control.

On the other hand, a Jewish woman settler ran over a Palestinian woman with her car, seriously wounding the Palestinian woman.

Late on Wednesday “Israeli” forces shot to death a Palestinian civilian, Shaher Saber (37), near the Jewish settlement Netzarim in the Gaza Strip.

Another Palestinian was gunned down by the “Israeli” army in the Strip.

In Nablus, an old man called Falah Ayash died of a heart attack after waiting for three hours at an “Israeli” check point.  Worth mentioning that the deceased is the uncle of the martyr Yehia Ayash.

The death raised to at least 335 the number of Palestinians in a four-month-old  uprising. 

 

Palestinian Ulama Association invalidates any agreement not securing right of return…(5)

The Palestinian Ulama Association issued a fatwa which invalidates any agreement or treaty that doesn't guarantee the unconditional return to all Palestinian refugees to their home land they were expelled from.

Moreover, it considered the Palestinian people and the successive generations unbound to any such treaties.

The Palestinian Ulama Association considered any financial compensation is an acquired right for the refugees but is not a substitute for the right to return, and accepting this under these conditions is considered illegal from a religious point of view, and everyone who accepts any compensation as a substitute to their return will be considered as a seller of his nation.

The fatwas was concluded with an assurance that liberating Palestine and Quds and the return of millions of Palestinian refugees cannot be achieved by the humiliating negotiations but by struggle and resistance.
