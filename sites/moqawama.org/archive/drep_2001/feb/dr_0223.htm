Introduction

Hizbullah’s Secretary General H.E. Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah and Prime Minister Rafic Hariri  held a face-to-face meeting from late night Wednesday to pre-dawn Thursday. The reports said it has laid down the framework of an agreement to maitain constant cooperation and it was described as “positive and successful”.

Meanwhile, President Emile Lahoud offered a riposte Thursday to the US ambassador to Lebanon who had recently cautioned Beirut not to antagonise “Israel”. “Those who advise not to provoke “Israel” must know that “Israeli” occupation is in itself a daily and permanent provocation”, Lahoud said.

Elsewhere, Iran has offered to sign a 100-million-dollar financial accord to help fund various projects in Lebanon, mainly to rebuild the devastated south after 22 years of occupation, papers reported Thursday.

 

Hizbullah Leader, PM held a meeting…(1)
Nasrallah: Resistance is the people’s right
Hariri: I’ll support operations in Shibaa Farms

A rare meeting between Premier Rafik Hariri and Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, was described on Thursday as “positive and successful,” having apparently ended in an agreement to maintain constant cooperation.
But Nasrallah is believed to have insisted that no restrictions be imposed on Hizbullah’s operations in the Shibaa Farms, despite Hariri’s reported concerns.

Hariri met with Nasrallah for two-and-a-half hours at the Hizbullah leader’s home in Haret Hreik late on Wednesday night. The meeting, which was apparently called by Hariri, came amid reported tension between the prime minister and Hizbullah stemming from last Friday’s deadly ambush of an “Israeli” convoy in the Shibaa Farms in which one “Israeli” soldier was killed and three others wounded.
Hariri and Hizbullah agreed to cooperate with each other, they said, but the resistance said it would not agree to “a joint operations center with the government.”
Hizbullah cloaks its activities amid tight secrecy to prevent details of pending operations being leaked to “Israel”.
According to Hizbullah sources, Nasrallah insisted that the resistance was “the people’s right,” and should as such be supported by the government. In exchange, Nasrallah said that Hizbullah was ready to help the authorities in any way possible. Nasrallah said that Hizbullah had no intention of allowing its military operations to impact negatively on Hariri’s government. Hizbullah’s target was not Hariri, but “Israel”, the resistance leader stressed.
Nasrallah argued that it was not the resistance that was threatening investments in Lebanon, but the “Israel’s” occupation of the Shibaa Farms.
US Ambassador David Satterfield had described last Friday’s fatal attack as “provocative” and warned that Lebanon could suffer economically if such actions continued.
Nasrallah had responded by condemning what he described as attempts to submit Hizbullah to “Israeli”-US conditions and provoke a rebellion against Syria and the Taif Accord.
Nasrallah explained to Hariri during their meeting that his comments had not been directed at Hariri but were an attack against the US.
It was only the second time that Hariri has met with Nasrallah one-on-one. The first occasion was three months ago, when the two talked for four hours while paying condolences to Hajj Hussein Khalil, Nasrallah’s political adviser, on the death of his father.
Sources close to Wednesday’s meeting said Hariri had assured Nasrallah he was not yielding to pressure from the US.
“He came to say: ‘Look, I’m not surrendering to American pressure. I still support you and support operations in the Shibaa Farms,’” the source said.
Hariri said he wanted to maintain contacts with Hizbullah to develop a “unified political language” between the government and the resistance.
The source said that Hariri and Nasrallah agreed to hold further meetings.
A Hizbullah spokesman said that military operations would continue in the Shibaa Farms.
“This is a red line for us,” the spokesman said.
“Israel” has reportedly asked Egypt to help persuade Hizbullah not to open a second front on the Lebanon-”Israel” border.
““Israel” asked Egypt to ask Syria and Lebanon to stop the activities against “Israeli” soldiers by Hizbullah,” an “Israeli” official source said on Thursday.
Egypt so far has supported Lebanon’s attempts to regain the Shibaa Farms through military force.

Lebanon's Lahoud says “Israeli” occupation "permanent provocation"

“ Lebanese President Emile Lahoud offered a riposte Thursday to the US ambassador to Lebanon who had recently cautioned Beirut not to antagonise “Israel”.

"Those who advise not to provoke “Israel” must know that “Israeli” occupation is in itself a daily and permanent provocation", Lahoud was quoted as saying in a statement read by Information Minister Ghazi Aridi after a meeting of the government's Council of Ministers.

Lahoud's retort alluded to the US ambassador David Sutterfield's condemnation of anti-”Israeli” attacks in southern Lebanon.

On Saturday, Sutterfield branded the February 16 attack by Hizbullah on “Israeli” soldiers in the Shibaa Farms area-- killing one “Israeli” soldier and injuring three others-- as a "provocation".

The Shibaa Farms were captured by “Israel” in the 1967 Middle East war and are claimed by Lebanon.

However, “Israel” says it captured them from Syria and their future status will only be determined in the context of a peace treaty with Syria.

Lahoud condemned what he called "a coup by “Israeli” society against the peace process". According to the Lebanese president, "facts prove that “Israel” has no desire whatsoever to reach a fair and comprehensive peace and that it is satisfied with having safety belts ... on the Golan Heights and the Shibaa Farms".

The Golan Heights were seized from Syria by “Israel” in 1967.

The statement quoted Lebanese Prime Minister Rafiq Hariri as saying the February 6 victory of “Israeli” Prime Minister-elect Ariel Sharon "did not give an optmistic vision of the peace process".

"Everybody is waiting to see what the new “Israeli” government will do. For our part, we will continue working for the liberation of occupied Lebanese land and pursue our efforts with our Arab brothers for a fair and comprehensive peace", he added.

 

Iran offers 100 million dollars to help rebuild South Lebanon…(3)

Iran has offered to sign a 100-million-dollar financial accord to help fund various projects in Lebanon, mainly to rebuild the devastated south after 22 years of "Israeli" occupation, papers reported Thursday.

Iranian Housing Minister Ali Abdal-Ali Zadeh discussed the accord with President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Rafiq Hariri in separate meetings on Wednesday, they said.

The financial protocol is meant to help finance projects related to the infrastructure, tourism, cooperatives, agriculture, trade, air transport, industry, tobacco growing and assistance for the south.

"The Iranian people and government have struggled alongside the Lebanese people and government in fighting against the "Israeli" occupation and we are in Lebanon today to seek reconstruction," Zadeh was quoted as saying.

"Everyone has to take part in the reconstruction of the south, particularly after its liberation," he said.

Iran has already helped in rebuilding the devastation inflicted by "Israeli" military attacks on Lebanon's infrastructure before the Jewish state ended its 22-year occupation of southern Lebanon in May 2000.

The Islamic republic has also provided financial, political and logistic assistance to Hezbollah, which spearheaded the guerrilla war against the "Israeli" occupation.

Also, Lebanon and Iran signed a cooperation agreement on Thursday aimed at boosting tourism ties between the two countries.
The agreement was signed at the Ministry of Tourism by  Minister Karam Karam and Irannian Housing Minister Ali Abdal-Ali Zadeh.
“I hope that more Iranians will be able to visit Lebanon thanks to this agreement and that more Lebanese will visit Iran,” Ali Zadeh said.
He invited Lebanese to visit Iran, which contains “historical and religious monuments for all religions, especially the Armenian community.”
Karam praised “the historical humanitarian and cultural ties” that bind the Lebanese and Iranian people while thanking Iran for its support for Lebanon during its war with “Israel” and the liberation of the South.
Ali Zadeh, who is accompanied by a 31-member delegation from Iran’s public and private sectors, is on an official visit here to look into ways of boosting economic ties between the two countries.
Speaking at the opening session of the fifth ministerial meeting of the Lebanese-Iranian Economic Committee, Economy and Trade Minister Basil Fuleihan said both countries had decided to boost economic ties to match their political relations.
“Undoubtedly, the political relations between the two countries are very strong, but everyone concedes that economic ties are much lower,” Fuleihan said.
He said that boosting economic cooperation would require work by the committee, which is devoted to this issue.
“We are confident that the expertise of Lebanese businessmen and merchants coupled with Iran’s economic know-how will generate more profit for both our countries,” Ali Zadeh said.
Fuleihan said strengthening economic cooperation would include the fields of banking, trade, agriculture, infrastructure and industry.
According to Fuleihan, “the delegation also discussed with customs officials the prospects of Iran’s use of Lebanon’s free-trade zone to stock Iranian products and then selling them through Lebanese merchants.”
Talks between Iranian and Lebanese officials also centered around the prospects of funding several development projects in liberated areas in the South, Fuleihan said.
The issue also topped discussions between Ali Zadeh and Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah.
The Iranian delegation also held a series of meetings  on Thursday with officials from the Council for Reconstruction and Development, the Industrialists’ Association, the Union of Lebanon’s Chambers of Trade and Industry, and the Beirut Merchants’ Association.
It also met with Minister of Public Works and Transport Najib Mikati and Minister of Industry George Frem, who said talks focused on means of developing Iran’s trade with Lebanon through investments.
“I hope the delegation’s visit will help open Iran’s markets to Lebanese products, to encourage Lebanese industry,” Frem said.
Iran’s exports to Lebanon total $28 million annually compared to $1 million of Lebanese exports to Iran.
Ali Zadeh said that Iranian investors were interested in working in Lebanon which they consider “a gate to African and European countries.”
On Wednesday, the Iranian official also discussed prospects of the Islamic Republic’s financial assistance to Lebanon in separate meetings with President Emile Lahoud and Prime Minister Rafik Hariri.

 

Gabonese PM pays tribute to South…(4)

Gabonese Premier Jean-Francois N’Toutoume Emane expressed admiration Thursday for what has been achieved in the South.
Emane, who arrived on Tuesday for a three-day visit, toured the South with Minister for Administrative Reform Fouad Saad.
He was shown the site of the 1996 Qana massacre, where 107 civilians who were taking refuge in a UN-protected shelter were killed in “Israeli” shelling.
“I am pleased to welcome you and your party to Sidon, which endured a lot under the “Israeli” occupation. We also appreciate Mr. Emane’s insistence on visiting Qana, which witnessed even more horror than Sidon,” MP Bahiya Hariri said in an address in Sidon prior to the Qana visit.
“Our presence here shows our respect and appreciation for this city’s deep-rooted history. We pay tribute to the residents of this great city and, through them, to the South as a whole,” Emane said.

Liberation of Lebanese soil not over, says Berri…(5)

Lebanese parliamentary Speaker Nabih Berri considered that the liberation of Lebanese soil is still not over and should not end without the return of the occupied Shibaa Farms to Lebanon’s sovereignty.

Speaking in a dinner organized by the lawyers syndicate in honor of lawyers deputies, Berri called on Lebanese lawyers to work with international law institutions in the aim of regaining Lebanon’s legal rights and oblige ““Israel”” to compensate Lebanon for the damages it caused during the 22-years of occupation.

 

“Israeli” army expects more shelling of Jewish settlements…(6)

“Israeli” daily Haaretz reported the “Israeli” occupation army reserve general, Avi Fein, as saying it is expected that Palestinians in the Gaza Strip will target nearby “Israeli” cities with rockets in the coming weeks.

The daily said the general was speaking in a meeting with the heads of worldwide Jewish communities, visiting the Zionist Entity.

The “Israeli” general added that the launching of mortar shells by the Palestinians on the Jewish Settlement of Netzarim in the Gaza Strip, is part of the preparations being done for larger scale attacks.

He considered the “Israeli” deterrence capability at its lowest and said firm action should be taken to enforce it.

Also, senior “Israeli” army officers are recommending that forces be sent to Palestinian controlled areas, in addition to undercover units, in order to prevent shooting attacks against “Israelis”.

The officers warned that preparations should be made for an increase in Palestinian operations in the West Bank.

On the other hand, Intifadah fighters opened fire on an “Israeli” settler motorist at an occupied Quds(Jerusalem) intersection early on Thursday.

The motorist, from Maale Adumim Jewish settlement, was wounded in the shoulder.

In another operation, the “Israeli” radio reported that a bus carrying settlers, under army protection, fell in an ambush near Mefdalim Settlement, close to the West Bank town of Nablus. The enemy soldier answered by opening fire at the Palestinian resistance fighters.
