The only way to recuperate occupied lands is through resistance, Qasem…(1)

Hizbullah Deputy Secretary-General Sheikh Naiim Qassem called on the Arab Summit to adopt  firm and decisive resolutions putting aside the option of negotiations, supporting the Palestinian people, and the Arab rights and sanctities.

Speaking in a speech in the Faculty of Arts in the Lebanese University, Sheikh Qassem said the “Israelis” want to force the Palestinians  through negotiations to lay down their arms and oblige them to give in large concessions.

The Deputy Secretary-General said negotiations wholly serve the interest of the “Israelis”, for negotiations is a tool used to strengthen “Israel” with the signature of others.

He affirmed the sole way to recuperate the occupied lands is by exercising all forms of resistance.

 

Lahoud demands united front against Jewish state…(2)

President Emile Lahoud urged Arab leaders Tuesday to provide “total backing” for the intifada and help Lebanon and Syria confront “Israel”.
The president’s comments came at the opening of the Arab summit in Amman, amid reports that next year’s gathering will be held in Beirut. Diplomatic sources said the move had apparently come at the request of the UAE, next year’s scheduled host, “in appreciation and support of Lebanon’s policies.”
Lahoud thanked the UAE for its initiative and expressed his country’s readiness to host the summit, the sources said.
Lahoud appealed “in the name of Arab dignity” not to leave his country “economic prey to the policy of siege and punishment” and to conduct an overall review of the “so-called” peace process.
He added that each time Arab heads of state met they affirmed a just and comprehensive peace as their strategic choice, while “Israel” “persists in its crimes from bad to worse, just as its leaders … go from bad to worse.”
““Israel” is an entity not interested in peace,” he said. “It is an entity opposed to peace and which seeks only security, even at the expense of peace. When “Israel” withdrew from south Lebanon last year under the blows of the resistance, the withdrawal was only designed to meet “Israel’s” need for security.
“There were those who told us the withdrawal was an important step on the road to peace … and there were those who accused us of abandoning our responsibilities … if we did not deploy the army there,” he added. “When we sent it in in a manner that served our interests ­ not “Israel’s”و ­ an economic war was launched, depriving us of the planned donor countries’ conference.”
Lahoud complained that “Israel” continues to occupy the Shibaa Farms and hold Lebanese detainees, “while Palestinian refugees live in tragic conditions … and “Israel” refuses to hand over maps related to thousands of mines. What is needed now is an overall review of the so-called peace process …We have to admit that this process does not exist in the first place, as far as “Israel” is concerned.”
In fact, he said, “there is no peace process but only an “Israeli” security plan, under well-known international patronage, with the sole aim of ensuring “Israel’s” security. This calls for adopting a clear and unified Arab stand on the peace process … and a clear Arab strategy on the diplomatic, political, military, security and economic levels.
“It also calls for the need to provide total backing for the intifada, for Lebanon, and for Syria in confronting occupation and its consequences,” he added.
Lahoud concluded by saying that the siege of Lebanon was crueler than the occupation of the West Bank and Gaza.
“Accordingly,” he said, “I appeal to you in the name of Arab dignity, illustrated through the resistance, not to leave Lebanon economic prey to the policy of siege and punishment.”
Earlier in the day, Lahoud
met UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan. The two discussed the continued detention of Lebanese in “Israel” and the president also raised “Israeli” threats, violations of Lebanese airspace, Shibaa, and the Jewish state’s refusal to hand over minefield maps.
Lahoud then reminded the UN of its promise to hold a donor conference to aid reconstruction. He then conveyed his country’s support of Annan’s nomination for a second term in office. Annan described the talks as “useful.”
Prime Minister Rafik Hariri held talks with Lahoud and Syrian Vice-President Abdel-Halim Khaddam.
Lahoud also received Syrian President Bashar Assad after the opening session, in the second such meeting since the two presidents’ arrival Monday.

Assad: “Israelis” are worse than Nazis…(3)

Syrian President Bashar Assad said “Israelis” who voted Prime Minister Ariel Sharon to power last month were the product of a society that was "more racist than the Nazis."
"Sharon is described in the world as a man of massacres, a killer and a man who hates Arabs. It's true that he is a man who hates everything to do with Arabs," Assad said. "But Sharon did not come (to power) through his will, but through the will of the “Israeli” street. How do they expect us to convince ourselves and our people that this “Israeli” street which elected a man like Sharon wants peace, or that it is not hostile to Arabs and Muslims?" he asked in a speech that often strayed from his prepared text. Assad dismissed the idea that Sharon, elected in February, should be given time to show his peaceful intentions or that his hostility to Arabs did not reflect “Israeli” public opinion.
"Will Sharon turn suddenly into a peace dove?" he asked. "Does this mean giving him time to kill more Arabs?"

 

“Israeli” jets cause sonic booms over Lebanon…(4)

“ “Israeli” war planes buzzed Lebanon Tuesday, breaking the sound barrier over Beirut and several Lebanese coastal towns after midday, AFP reporters said.

The jets flew at low altitude over the seaport towns of Tyre, 80 kilometers (49 miles) south of Beirut, and Sidon 40 kilometers (24 miles) to the north, where they made mock attacks.

The planes climbed to fly over Beirut, making two sonic booms which caused windows to shake.

Since “Israel” ended its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon last May, Lebanon has lodged frequent complaints with the United Nations against “Israeli” violations of its airspace.

 

Palestinians rally to seek Arab backing for intifada…(5)

Several hundred people rallied in front of Parliament Tuesday, urging Arab leaders meeting in Amman to give full backing to the Palestinian intifada.
The rally was organized by the Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine and other Palestinian factions and groups to coincide with the start of a two-day Arab summit in Amman. The meetings will discuss ways to support the Palestinian uprising and end United Nations sanctions against Iraq.
“We urge Arab leaders to support the intifada of our brothers and to come up with a unified stand and boycott the “Israeli” government,” the groups said in a joint statement.
Speaker Nabih Berri, who addressed the group from Parliament, asked Arab leaders to provide financial support for Palestinians and called for the implementation of international resolutions supporting their cause.
“Arabs must also show their support by maintaining their position on the return of Palestinian refugees, their right for self-determination, and their right to establish their independent state,” Berri said.
Information Minister Ghazi Aridi, Deputy Speaker Elie Ferzli, and Beirut MP Nasser Qandil also joined the rally.
“Arab people have to support the Palestinian people and their intifada, and provide them with means of resistance,” Ferzli said.
Elsewhere, approximately 100 refugees from the Nahr al-Bared refugee camp in the North staged a demonstration in front of the United Nations Relief and Works Agency offices in Tripoli. The protesters were seeking support from Arab leaders.

 

Suicide martyrdom bomber kills two in “Israel” petrol station attack…(6)

A Palestinian suicide martyrdom bomber has detonated explosives near a group of “Israeli” teenagers waiting for a ride to school, killing himself and two others. Four people were hurt, one critically. In two other “Israeli” towns, bombs were discovered and safely exploded.

The Islamic militant group Hamas claimed responsibility for the deadly blast, and said seven more bombers were ready to strike.

“Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon called an emergency session of his security Cabinet for later today.

Sharon, elected on a promise to restore security to “Israelis”, is under growing pressure to respond harshly. However, “Israel’s” police commissioner said little more could be done to prevent suicide attacks.

Today's blast went of shortly after 7:30am (0530 gmt) in the parking lot of a gas station in northern “Israel”.

"I saw three young fellows lying on the ground. They looked in really bad shape," said a customer at the station, who was only identified by his first name, Maoz. "Another was wounded, but was still standing up."

White plastic bags covered the three bodies lying on the ground near the gas pumps. Troops sealed off the area, searching for additional explosives.

Also today, two bombs were discovered and safely exploded in the open–air markets of the towns of Netanya and Petah Tikvah.

Earlier this month, four people, including a Palestinian suicide bomber, were killed in a blast in Netanya. Yesterday, Palestinian militants carried out back–to–back attacks in Jerusalem, including a bombing near a bus that killed the assailant and injured more than two dozen by–standers.

Police suggested they could do little else to prevent bombings. "We are faced with a very big offensive of the terrorist organizations to carry out attacks," said Police Commissioner Shlomo Aharonishki. "The last few days show that in spite of all our stepped up efforts ... they succeeded in getting in."

Police have said they expected an increase in the pace of bombings during the Arab League summit which was to conclude in Jordan later Wednesday.

In a phone call to a Western news agency, Hamas claimed responsibility for today's blast and yesterday's suicide martyrdom bombing in Jerusalem.

In the Wednesday morning attack, a group of teenagers got off a van at the gas station, called the "Meeting Place of Peace," near the village of Sdeh Hemet, close to the West Bank. From there, the youngsters planned to board an armored bus for a ride to their Jewish seminary in Kedumim, a Jewish settlement in the West Bank.

One of the students, Shmuel Friedman, said he stood a few meters away from the other students at the gas station when the blast went off.

"Suddenly, there was a big explosion. I turned around. I saw wounded, and several people lying on the ground. Afterwards, I called the police. We started taking the wounded to the gas station," Friedman said.

The police chief of the nearby town of Kfar Saba, Shimon Buchbut, said the blast was apparently set off by a suicide martyrdom bomber. A doctor, who arrived at the scene shortly after the blast and administered first aid, said one of the dead was an Arab.

The attacks put growing pressure on Sharon to take action. Sharon was elected last month on a promise to restore security to “Israelis” rattled by a 6–month–old Palestinian uprising. However, the government has suggested it would not retaliate during the summit.

Uzi Landau, the minister for public security, said Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat was ultimately responsible for the violence and that the “Israeli” people no longer harbored any illusions about him.

"After many years, during which we told the public we are headed toward peace, that Arafat is a partner, that we can talk to him, we now discover something else, that he is an enemy and a terrorist," said Landau, of Sharon's Likud Party, a long–time opponent of land–for–peace deals with the Palestinians.

 

Palestinian elevate their resistance as Arab Summit goes on…(7)

Palestinian rage against ongoing “Israeli” aggressions and occupation were translated into two new bomb blasts in occupied
Quds (Jerusalem), the latest in a series of attacks in the Zionist Entity since Ariel Sharon came to power.

A powerful car bomb wounded 7 “Israelis” when it blew up in the morning rush hour in the commercial and industrial area of
Tapliot in occupied Quds.

Six hours later another blast ripped through a bus in the French Hill district on the edge of east Quds, killing one person, who the occupation police say may be the suspected bomber, and injuring around 31 “Israelis”. One was reported to be seriously wounded. The “Israeli” police closed off the area and launched a search for other explosive devices.

The Islamic Jihad Movement claimed responsibility for the car bomb attack in Tapliot, saying in a statement that they are
retaliating for crimes committed by the Zionist enemy against the Palestinians of al-Khalil. The Islamic Jihad added that the
operation which will not be the last aims at showing “Israeli” “Israeli” premier Ariel Sharon that the determination of the Palestinians is stronger than the steel of his tanks.

Meanwhile Sharon convened an urgent cabinet meeting to discuss the deterioration of the situation following the two bomb blasts.

In the Palestinian territories, hundreds of Palestinians took to the streets to highlight their plight as an Arab summit opened in Amman.

In Khan Younis, in the Gaza Strip, “Israeli” occupation troops shot a Palestinian during clashes that erupted with stone-throwing Palestinian demonstrators, protesting the six-month-old “Israeli” blockade. The demonstrators called on the Arab Summit to support their uprising against the “Israeli” occupation.

In the West Bank town of Beit Fourik, hundreds of children were targeted with rubber-coated bullets and tear-gas while they were calling the summit to support them.

Clashes also flared in the hot point of al-Khalil, where enraged jewish settlers sought to revenge the killing of a baby girl
Monday. They torched Palestinian offices and stormed an Arab hilltop district. Offices of the Waqf, the Islamic religious authorities were destroyed.

Palestinian sources said at least one Palestinian resident was injured when the “Israeli” occupation army opened-fire Monday night on the Abu Sneinah district.

On the other hand, eyewitness and Palestinian security  sources reported that the “Israeli” army swept kilometers of Palestinian lands in  Wadi Salqa in Dair al-Balah, southern of the Gaza Strip.

The sources mentioned that five  “Israeli” bulldozers barricaded with  tankers swept since early morning vast areas pulling out about 200 olive and  almond trees.

Palestinian  security general official, Abed  Arrazek al-Mujaideh called upon the  enemy authorities to compensate for the  damages estimated by millions of  dollars caused by the water leaking  from “Israeli” reservoirs, drowning  their plantations and injuring six  Palestinians among which were a  woman and a child after water swept  the ongoing cars.
