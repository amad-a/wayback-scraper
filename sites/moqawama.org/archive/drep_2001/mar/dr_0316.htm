Hizbullah examines remains of shelter…(1)

Hizbullah members examined on Thursday the shattered remains of a concrete shelter beside the Fatima Gate in Kfar Kila that was destroyed by “Israeli” troops a day earlier.
Around 10 “Israeli” soldiers crossed through gate early Wednesday morning and spent 15 minutes bulldozing the shelter and shoveling the debris across the border into “Israel”, a Hizbullah member who witnessed the incursion said.
The “Israeli” soldiers remained 15 minutes on Lebanese soil before retreating back through the gate, he said.
The shelter, which was used by “Israeli” troops during the occupation, was bulldozed to make way for a new section of border fence.
The government has not said whether it intends to protest the incursion to the United Nations.

The “Israeli” Occupation Campaigns Against Lebanon's Use of Hasbani River…(2)

The “Israeli” enemy continued its campaign to stop Lebanon from using  its water resources at al-Hasbani River which runs in Lebanese land before  reaching the Tabarayah Lake.

Shlomo Droor, an “Israeli” war ministry spokesman, said "“Israel”" will ask the United Nations to stop Lebanon from  building a Water Pumping station,  claiming that the station will divert the  river's water from the Jordan River and the Tabarayah Lake.

“Israel’s” T.V Channel  Two reported that messages on this  regard were dispatched to Syria  and that the issue was being discussed  by Ariel Sharon and the Infra-Structure  Minister Avigdor Lieberman, who  according to the Channel tried to  stimulate the “Israeli” government to take an immediate response, but  Sharon's decision was to act decisively  but only through diplomatic channels.

“Because of water, wars break out,”  Lieberman said.

For his part, “Israeli” occupation army chief  General Shaul Mofaz said, “I suggest that we act in a calm and measured manner, and not talk of war,”  told public radio. “At the moment it is in the process of clarification ... If it doesn’t turn out well we will have to assess the situation and judge again what to do.”

Meanwhile, T.V Channel One's  military correspondent reported that the  pumping station on al-Hasbani River  does not have a significant impact on  water levels gushed to the Palestinian occupied territories and from there to the Jordan River.

A United Nations spokesman Wednesday denied "“Israel’s”" accusations, saying he was “puzzled” by "“Israel’s”" response.
“The workers are building a four-inch pipe to carry water from the Hasbani river to a poor village lacking water. You don’t divert a river with a pipe so small,” said Timour Goksel, spokesman for the UN Interim Forces in Lebanon (UNIFIL).

Hasbani river flows back into controversy…(4) 

For such a small and innocuous-looking river, the Hasbani has generated considerable fuss over the past 40 years.
During “Israel’s” occupation of the South, the Lebanese regularly accused “Israel” of illegally pumping water from the Hasbani. The accusations were correct. The “Israelis” had installed two small pumps at the Wazzani springs on the west bank of the Hasbani which fed drinking water into the occupied village of Ghajar on top of the east bank. The water was carried via two 4-inch pipes slung across the river.
The Wazzani springs contribute 20-25 million cubic meters (mcm) to the Hasbani’s total annual flow of 125mcm. The springs continue to flow during the summer months when the river above Wazzani runs dry.
The Hasbani naturally flows into “Israel”, 3 kilometers south of Ghajar. The river is joined by the Banias, which rises in the “Israeli”-occupied Golan Heights, and the waters of the Dan springs.
The combined river, known as the Upper Jordan, eventually flows into the Sea of Galilee. The Jordan river emerges from the southern end of the Sea of Galilee.
Now that Lebanon has decided to insert its own 4-inch pipe into the Wazzani springs to supply much-needed drinking water to the impoverished village of Wazzani (also known as Arab Louaize), “Israel” has begun threatening harsh retaliation.
“There is no water in the Middle East. Therefore, understandings must be reached. If not, it can turn into a war or a forceful confrontation,” thundered Uri Saguy, the head of the “Israeli” Mekorot water company and a former head of “Israeli” military intelligence.
Despite the rhetoric, the “Israelis” are fully aware of the limited extent of the work being undertaken at the Wazzani springs.
UNIFIL informed the “Israeli” Army that Lebanese construction workers would be installing a pump before the project began on Feb. 20.
“Israel’s” bellicose public response three weeks later has little to do with objections over a Lebanese village receiving drinking water and everything to do with warning Lebanon of the repercussions of considering a more ambitious water diversion plan for the Hasbani.
This is not the first time that “Israel” has locked horns with its northern neighbors over the waters of the Hasbani.
In January 1964, Arab governments met to block an “Israeli” plan to divert water from the Jordan river to the coastal areas of “Israel”.
The options available were to complain to the UN, prevent the Upper Jordan tributaries (the Hasbani and Banias) from reaching “Israel” or go to war.
Eight months later, it was decided to divert the Hasbani into the Litani river and the Banias down the length of the Golan Heights to the Yarmuk.
The scheme threatened to disrupt “Israel’s” own water plans. In March, May and August 1965, “Israeli” tanks attacked Syrian construction works near Banias.
On July 14, 1966, “Israeli” aircraft and tanks halted for good the Syrian diversion efforts.
However, the water skirmishes led to further border incidents between Syria and “Israel”, which culminated in the June war of 1967 and the occupation of the Golan Heights.
After Ghajar was occupied in 1967, “Israel” destroyed a pumping station and an 8-inch pipe beside the Wazzani springs on the Lebanese side of the border.
The pump and pipe were installed four years earlier by the government to supply drinking and irrigation water to Wazzani village. The new pump house is located beside the derelict remains of the original building.
In 1979, a year after “Israel” established the occupation zone in the South, the Mekorot water company examined the possibility of diverting the springs to irrigate parts of “Israel”. The plan entailed extending the border northward by about 1 kilometer, but the scheme failed.

Looking for trouble…(5)

 Goksel, the UN spokesman in south Lebanon, was refreshingly candid over “Israel’s” claim that Lebanon is ‘diverting’ the Hasbani River: “Suddenly, they’re worried that they won’t have enough water for their swimming pools.”
The facts are these. The Council of the South is constructing a four-inch pipe to carry water to the small village of Wazzani, where residents are trying to reconstruct their lives and livelihoods after more than two decades of “Israeli” military occupation.
The water is coming from a small stream, also called the Wazzani, which flows into the Hasbani. It will be pumped to a water tower that can supply the needs of the villagers ­ not for swimming pools, but for more basic needs.
“Israel’s” argument that this amounts to “diverting” water is astonishing hypocrisy. During the occupation, the “Israelis” and their proxies set up schemes that pumped water into “Israel”. They also levied illegal charges on Lebanese villages for the supply of water.
They have left their occupation zone, but it seems the “Israelis” want to keep control of the water. “I hope that “Israel” will defend its interests,” said Uri Saguy, the head of Merokot, the national water company. “We have the means to do it.” And Avigdor Lieberman, the minister for infrastructure, left no doubt whatsoever what those means were. “Nobody heard me say wars break out over water,” he told “Israel” Radio. “But factually that is correct.”
So where is the causus belli, Mr. Lieberman? According to the UN’s Food and Agricultural Organization, “Israel” uses an annual total of 138 million cubic meters of water that has passed into the Sea of Galilee from the Hasbani. Yet Lebanon is taking only around 10 million cubic meters from the river. So it is absurd for Saguy to claim that the pumping of water to Wazzani is illegal.
How can “Israel” disregard its neighbors’ rights? Sadly, water is part of a wider picture. “Israel” continues to occupy land illegally in the Golan and Shibaa Farms. It continues to send its warplanes into Lebanese airspace. Border violations are commonplace: on Wednesday, for example, “Israeli” soldiers crossed into Lebanon and bulldozed a concrete shelter.
And now “Israel” asserts its control over a stream in Lebanon. As Lebanese construction teams work on the project, they are watched from across the border by armed “Israeli” security guards. For fear that Lebanese children in Wazzani might have water in their homes, “Israel” threatens us with its military might.
Bishop Elias Kfoury spoke very well for Lebanon and the people of the South: “Let “Israel” leave us in peace.” Issues of water can be resolved by neighbors who respect each other and abide by the law.

Storm in a glass…(6)

A month ago, separate messages sent via American and United Nations intermediaries informed “Israel” that the Lebanese intended to pump water from the Hatsbani River to residents of two southern Lebanon villages which have dire needs, including a lack of water. The U.S. position is that the Lebanese want to divert the water to these villages for humanitarian reasons; the Americans cannot promise that the volume of water pumped will not increase in the future.
Harsh, war-like threats issued in recent days by “Israeli” officials, including Infrastructure Minister Avigdor Lieberman's life or death characterization of the affair as a "to be or not to be" matter, are, to a large extent, a storm in a glass of water. More precisely, the issue is liable to become a problem if dialogue is not conducted soon between “Israel” and Lebanon over the use of water from northern rivers flowing across the territory of more than one nation. At this stage, the issue is one of dialogue; and there is presently no justification for comparing the Hatsbani issue with the decision reached in 1964 by Syria and other Arab states to deliberately divert water from the Jordan River so as to harm “Israel”. Attempts to divert water sources during the mid-1960s were one of the factors that contributed to the outbreak of the Six Day War.
Also exaggerated are reports implying that “Israel” has asked Washington to warn Lebanon against daring to pump water from the Hatsbani. Such a warning was never transmitted via diplomatic channels; the Americans already know the facts.
The Americans themselves refer to the small, four inch, diameter of the pipeline used by the Lebanese to transport the water around the Rajar village as proof that the project is local in character and orientation.
The Lebanese have learned of the Hatsbani storm in “Israel” from television broadcasts and newspaper coverage in this country. But a different sort of problem could arise from this “Israeli” coverage: Hizbullah might try to exploit the tempest to promote its own goals.

“Israeli” Aggressions Continue Against Palestinians…(7)

Seven Palestinian children were injured  Thursday when “Israeli” soldiers fired a  stun grenade into a school yard in the West Bank city of Al-Khalil(Hebron).

Medical officials said the children were  taken to hospital after the grenade exploded near them.

Earlier, the body of a Palestinian teenager  Mortaja Amr-17, was found Thursday  morning near Kalkiliya after he was  shot in clashes with the “Israeli” occupation  forces.

And Overnight, Palestinian Intifada  men launched two bombs on “Israeli”  outposts close to Nevi Dekalim  settlement.

The enemy forces said it deactivated   two mines near Moraq settlement  close to Rafah.

Another 11 year old Palestinian was  injured during clashes in northern Ramallah.  His condition is not stable yet.

In western al-Khalil, witnesses said the occupation army razed several hectares (acres) of Palestinian-owned land.

“Israeli” soldiers also closed an office of the Palestinian Islamic endowments in the town.

Also, “Israeli” bulldozers destroyed two Palestinian homes and a workshop in a Palestinian village north of al-Khalil on Tuesday, witnesses said.

A spokesman for the occupation's civil administration in the West Bank said the action was taken because the structures were near an area where a Jewish settler had been killed and had been built “illegally.”

Meanwhile, the “Israeli” occupation said it loosened its siege on  the Palestinian territories Thursday,  in a bid to escape the international pressure  that called on it to lift the blockade on  the West Bank and Gaza Strip. It said it allowed  goods to enter the territories and fishing to resume off the Gaza coast.

But “Israeli” prime minister Ariel Sharon  said he would not lift the siege unless clashes are stopped.

Palestinian information Yasser Abed Rabbo said the easing of the closure was a “lie.”

“The Palestinian territories have witnessed a continuation of intensified steps in terms of blocking roads and cutting off hundreds of Palestinian towns and villages,” he said in a statement.

Palestinian Mujahed Tells “Israeli” Court He Acted in Revenge for the Palestinian Martyrs…(8)

Ala Khalil Abu Olba, the Palestinian bus driver who carried out the hit-and-run bus attack near Tel Aviv a month ago that killed eight “Israelis”, was charged with murder on Thursday.

Courageously facing the “Israeli” occupation authorities, Abu Olba said he had no remorse for the action. He said he wanted to revenge the killing of more than 345 Palestinians killed by the occupation troops since the start of uprising.

Abu Olba, 35, who worked with "“Israel’s”" state-owned Egged bus company, was charged with premeditated murder for the killing of seven soldiers and a settler and the wounding of 21 others.

He was injured during a high-speed chase by police after the attack, which came a week after Prime Minister Ariel Sharon’s election victory.

The prosecution asked that Abu Olba be detained until the trial is concluded, but a Tel Aviv district court judge extended his detention until the beginning of April.

“Israeli” Army Reservists Flee Service in the West Bank, Gaza Strip…(9)

Some 600 reservists from the “Israeli” army have been in jail since October for refusing to serve in the Palestinian  territories, the “Israeli” Maariv daily reported  Thursday.

They have been sentenced to varying jail terms for not answering call-ups to serve either in military bases or  Jewish settlements in the West Bank and  Gaza Strip.

An “Israeli” army spokesman said that  between October 1998 and March  1999 "nine hundred army reservists did  not answer call-ups".

"“Israel”" imposes mandatory military  service of three years for men and 21  months for women. The men can  also be called up as reservists for periods up to more than a month per year until  the age of 49.
