Qaouk accuses “Israel” of border violations…(1)

Hizbullah’s southern commander, Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, accused “Israel” on Friday of continuing to use land inside Lebanon for military purposes.
He claimed the “Israeli” Army has been using the Saddana hill, the Bastara farmstead and the Tebbin hill as military areas, “although they are located in Lebanese territory.”
All three locations face the Shibaa Farms and lie just within the Lebanese side of the UN-delineated Blue Line.
Qaouk said that the “Israelis” occupied the land by shooting at anyone attempting to enter the area.
He added that even UNIFIL troops were prevented from accessing the area.
“This represents a direct occupation, a flagrant violation to Lebanon’s sovereignty and a direct obstruction to the UN’s movement,” he said.
Qaouk said he was “surprised” at the lack of reaction from the international community over “Israel’s” territorial violations “which will only make the enemy sink deeper in the dark tunnel of its crisis.”

Missing Iranian Diplomats Surface in “Israeli” Jail?…(2)

The four Iranian diplomats who disappeared in Lebanon upon “Israel’s” 1982 invasion may be held in an “Israeli” jail, Egypt's official Middle East News Agency (MENA) said in a report published in Beirut Friday.
There was no immediate reaction from the Iranian Embassy in Beirut or Hizbullah to the report which, if accurate, would raise the possibility of further complications in Germany's mediation efforts to broker a prisoners swap between the Party of God and the Jewish State.
A Palestinian humanitarian organization based in the West Bank town of Ramallah said in a statement released on Thursday that recently released Arab prisoners have seen by naked eye the names of the four missing Iranians engraved in the walls of prison cells in “Israel’s” Atleet prison.
The report implied that the diplomats had personally inscribed their names in the hope that someone else would read them and tell the outside world that they were still alive, languishing at the Atleet jail.
The engraved names were listed by the report as:
1-Mohsen Musavi, charge d'affaires of the Iranian embassy in Beirut,
2-Ahmed Motevaselian, commander of an Iranian Revolutionary Guard unit that was based in Baalbek
3-Kazem Allaf, a reporter-photographer for Iran's official news agency Irna
4-Mohammed Taghi Rastegar Moqadam, an Iranian embassy driver.
The four went missing after they were stopped at a Christian civil war militia checkpoint north of Beirut as they were fleeting the “Israeli” army's occupation of Beirut.
Spokesmen for the Lebanese Forces militia, currently outlawed, have contended that the four were killed when a mortar barrage fired from Beirut's Muslim sector hit the LF's central prison in the Karantina.
But the Iranian government still contends the four were sent to the “Israeli”-occupied border enclave in south Lebanon and then to “Israel” proper.

‘Pro-”Israeli” espionage ring’ uncovered…(3)

A pro-”Israeli” spy ring was recently uncovered in the South after its members were found to have links with “Israel”, security sources reported Friday. The ring is said to comprise both Lebanese and Palestinians, including two high-ranking members of the Fatah faction of Palestinian President Yasser Arafat. The members of the ring are being questioned by local authorities. They reportedly include Sami Akkawi and Mohammed Ali Obeid as well as former members of the “Israeli”-proxy militia and the Lebanese Forces.

Hizbullah’s ‘center of operations’ ? as alleged by “Israel” ? currently inhabited by Bedouin workers…(4)

The news that the former “Israeli”-allied camp in Majidieh is regarded by “Israel” as Hizbullah’s “center of operations” has come as a surprise to the 20 Bedouins who having been living there for the past month.
“There are no Hizbullah here. There’s no one here but us,” said a puzzled Omar Harati, surveying the deserted former SLA barracks.
But, according to the “Israeli” Army, the sprawling complex is home to “dozens of Hizbullah terrorists” who do “various jobs at the base,” as reported this week by “Israel’s” Yediot Ahronot daily.
It said that the Hizbullah fighters deploy different types of anti-tank weapons in the vicinity of Majidieh as well as conduct intelligence gathering operations.
“Israel’s” Radio has also described Majidieh as Hizbullah’s “headquarters.”
Yediot noted that the missile attack against the “Israeli” Army convoy two weeks ago, in which a soldier was killed and two others wounded, was launched from Majidieh.
It is true that the road where the “Israeli” convoy was attacked is clearly visible from the tree-lined lane that leads to Majidieh. The vehicle, a ‘humvee,’ was hit just before reaching an “Israeli” Army outpost at Tallet Ghazale, also visible from Majidieh.
The Hizbullah squad probably launched the missile, believed to have been a wire-guided Sagger, from near its old observation post at the junction of the lane and a main road which terminates at an Indian UNIFIL position on the UN-delineated Blue Line, 300 meters to the south.
The distance between the former post and the “Israeli” convoy is a little over 2 kilometers, just within range of a Sagger. The location beside a main road would have enabled the Hizbullah team to escape by vehicle immediately after the attack, evading “Israeli” retaliation.
Two gaping holes in the road, a few hundred meters north of the entrance to Majidieh, testify to the “Israeli” attempts to hit the fleeing Hizbullah attackers.
Since Hizbullah resumed operations against the “Israeli” Army last October, none of its fighters would be foolish enough to live in an isolated cluster of buildings located only 1 kilometer from the Blue Line and the “Israeli” Army.
Hizbullah fighters spent the summer months in Majidieh after it was vacated by the SLA during the May withdrawal.
But Hizbullah left Majidieh along with numerous other border posts following the capture of three “Israeli” soldiers from the Shibaa Farms on Oct. 7.
Instead, a group of Bedouin from Qabb Elias in the Bekaa valley  have settled in the former barracks to work for the Arslan family, which owns the Majidieh estate.
Majidieh is named after Majid Arslan, one of the figures of Lebanon’s independence and a long-serving politician. His country estate at the foot of the Shibaa Farms hills was taken over in 1977 by the militia of Saad Haddad, the founder of what was to become the South Lebanon Army.
Harati said that the Bedouin grew vegetables ? “not drugs,” he insisted ? in the fertile soil around Majidieh.
Asked if he was concerned for his safety living in such an exposed location beside the Shibaa Farms, Harati said there was no reason for fear.
“There are no Hizbullah here, there are no soldiers here, just us workers. Why should anyone cause us trouble?” he asked.
Harati said that Indian UNIFIL soldiers patrolled Majidieh by vehicle six times a day.
“We are protected by the UN,” he said. “The UN have told the “Israelis” that we are here.”
The “Israeli” Army is in a good position to know exactly who is living in Majidieh. Other than the regular overflights by unmanned reconnaissance planes and warplanes, Majidieh can be observed by three “Israeli” military outposts, Abbassieh, Tallet Ghazale and Ramta.

Islamic Jihad Claims Responsibility for Roadside…(5)

The Islamic Jihad Movement military branch, al-Quds Brigade, claimed responsibility for the roadside bomb attack which targeted Friday morning an “Israeli” occupation army patrol in the Gaza Strip.

In a statement, a copy of which the Al-Manar Channel received, the al-Quds Brigade said, " The unit of martyr Shadi al-Kahlout detonated a 30 kg bomb as an enemy jeep vehicle passed on the road of the Nahhal settlement east of Gaza city. Following the explosion, fire was opened on the patrol and several casualties were recorded in the ranks of the Zionist soldiers."

The statement also revealed that the brigade carried out a number of operations against the “Israeli” occupation during the last two weeks, and asserted that there were casualties in the ranks of “Israeli” soldiers.

On the other hand, the “Israeli” occupation army said  various gun battles with Palestinian  fighters in the Gaza Strip continued into early hours of Friday morning.

Also  intifadah fighters opened fire Thursday  night on an enemy military outpost along  the Egyptian-Palestinian border  near Rafah town.

In the West Bank town of Nablus , the  mujahedeen opened gunfire at “Israeli”  outposts in Tel al-Raas.

An “Israeli”  border guard unit was also targeted by  Palestinian fighters near the Qalendia  refugee camp South of Ramallah.

Three Palestinian Martyrs, 7 wounded by “Israeli” Army…(6)

In one of the deadliest days in the Palestinian territories in recent weeks, “Israeli” occupation troops shot dead Friday two Palestinian children and a mentally-ill vagrant.

Witnesses said none of them was involved in any confrontation incidents, although clashes occurred elsewhere in the West Bank following Muslim prayers.

“Israeli” troops shot and killed a nine-year-old boy in the West Bank town of El-Bireh after opening fire in the direction of a group of children who had been playing with cap guns beneath his family’s apartment, medical officials and witnesses said.

Ubey Darraj was dead on arrival in hospital after he was hit in the chest by a bullet from a heavy caliber machine-gun, hospital officials said.

Shortly afterwards, hospital officials in Gaza announced that a 13-year-old Palestinian boy, shot by “Israeli” troops on Tuesday, had died Friday.

Mohammed Mahmoud Hellis, 13, was hit in the head with a live round while walking home from school near the flashpoint Mintar crossing point in the Gaza Strip. Witnesses said there were no clashes at the time.

Earlier Friday, the “Israeli” Occupation army shot  dead a Palestinian described by  Palestinian officials as a poor, mentally ill  vagrant who was walking on a road  junction near the Jewish settlement of  Netzarim in the Gaza Strip.

The “Israeli” army said the man was  preparing to detonate a bomb but later  conceded its troops made a mistake.

Palestinian hospital officials said the body of Mustafa Al-Ramlawi, 42, from  Buraij Refugee camp, was mangled after  being hit by heavy caliber machine-gun  fire and explosive shells.

Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat lashed out at what he called "“Israel’s”" dangerous objectives, saying Friday’s deaths exposed "“Israel’s”" violence against the Palestinians for all the world to see.

"Their (“Israel’s”) ways have become exposed to the truth in front of international public opinion, as has been exposed their dangerous objectives against human rights,” Arafat told reporters in Gaza City after returning from Libya.

Following the  Muslim day of prayer seven Palestinians were  injured when “Israeli” soldiers fired rubber-coated steel bullets at them at the northern edge of the West Bank city of Ramallah following anti-”Israel” demonstrations.

Dozens of youths and soldiers clashed after some 1,500 people marched through Ramallah, demanding continuation of the Palestinian intifadah, or uprising, against “Israeli” occupation.

Friday’s deaths bring to 347 the number of Palestinian martyrs.
