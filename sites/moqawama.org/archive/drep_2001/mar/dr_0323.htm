Iran Confirms its 4 Missing Beirut Diplomats are Alive in “Israel”…(1)

Iran's Foreign Minister Kamal Kharazi has given the first official confirmation that four missing diplomats of the Iranian embassy in Beirut were alive in an “Israeli” prison.
"Their fate is rather still a mystery. But the information we now possess and the testimony of witnesses available to us show that all four are alive in “Israel”," Kharazi told reporters in Beirut.
Winding up a two-day visit to Lebanon, Kharazi said before leaving to Syria on Tuesday that Iran was hopeful the four diplomats would be part of any future prisoners swap between Hizbullah and “Israel”.
Kharazi's remark was the first official corroboration of a report by Egypt's official Middle East News Agency (Mena) three weeks ago, which said the names of the missing four were seen by Palestinian inmates engraved on the wall of a cell in “Israel’s” Atleet Prison.
Naharnet excusively relayed that report from Beirut. It was attributed to a Palestinian human rights organization in the West Bank town of Ramallah on March 3.
The report implied that the diplomats had personally inscribed their names so that other inmates may be able to tell the outside world when the opportunity arises that the Iranians were still alive.
The engraved names were listed by the report as:
1-Mohsen Musavi, charge d'affaires of the Iranian embassy in Beirut.
2-Ahmed Motevaselian, commander of an Iranian Revolutionary unit that was based in Baalbek.
3-Kazem Allaf, a reporter-photographer for Iran's official news agency Irna.
4-Mohammed Taghi Rastegar Moqadam, an embassy driver.
The diplomats went missing at a checkpoint of the Lebanese Forces civil war Christian militia in northern Lebanon as they were fleeing “Israel’s” 1982 invasion army in Beirut.
They were driving for Damascus in an embassy car with a diplomatic plate when they were stopped at the LF's Madfoun checkpoint.
Initial media reports said all four were executed on the spot, but an LF official statement said later the diplomats perished from a shell that blasted their cell in an LF seaside prison in Beirut fired from the Muslim sector of the city.
A third version surfaced in the Beirut media that the four Iranians were handed over to “Israel” in exchange for fresh arms supplies to the LF.

Lawyers plan legal action against “Israel”…(2)

“Israel” could get a taste of its own medicine if Arab lawyers follow through on the pledge they made Thursday to prosecute the state before the International Court of Justice.
More than 150 political figures and lawyers from the region discussed bringing a case against “Israel” during a conference in Beirut organized by the Federation of Arab Lawyers to support the Lebanese people and the Palestinian intifada.
Forum organizer and Lebanese lawyer Michel Tueni explained that “Israel” was one of the first to demand compensation for crimes committed against Jews during World War II. Tueni said Lebanese have the same right to compensation and argued that part of the reason Lebanon is so deep in debt is because it faced more than 20 years of aggression from “Israel”.
“Lebanon is begging for funds when it’s owed money from (“Israel”),” he said. “It doesn’t make sense.”
Tueni serves on a committee formed under the previous government that looked into filing a case against “Israel” at the International Court of Justice in the Hague. He said the current government was supportive of the idea but has not followed through on the lawsuit.
“I’m astonished that governments haven’t done anything with regard to this matter,” Tueni said. “This must be made a priority for us and anyone who was harmed by “Israeli” aggression.”
Tueni advocated the creation of a ministry dedicated to “demanding our rights, the first obligation of elected officials.”
Even though thousands of resolutions have been passed condemning illegal “Israeli” actions ­ the first one in the country following the 1968 commando raid on Beirut Airport in which several MEA airplanes were blown up ­ only a few have been implemented.
Former President Elias Hrawi was one of the first to take a step toward holding “Israel” responsible for its actions when he approached both the United Nations and the United States following the massacre at Qana in 1996 in which 107 people were killed.
Although 130 countries supported a UN resolution requiring compensation for Lebanon, Hrawi had less luck with the US, which, along with “Israel”, was one of the only nations to reject it, according to Tueni.
Hrawi told a cheering crowd at the conference: “When the US president (Bill Clinton) reacted to my visit by donating money, I told him that I didn’t come to extend a begging hand. I told him to find another country to send their old clothes to ­ I was there to get his help politically in implementing Resolution 425 (calling for a withdrawal of “Israeli” troops from Lebanon.)”
He received his second standing ovation at the end of his speech when Farouq Abu Issa, the secretary-general of the Federation of Arab Lawyers, answered chants from participants and granted the former statesman an honorary law degree.
Any official case must be initiated by the government because the International Court of Justice in The Hague is a tribunal for governments rather than individual plaintiffs.
Many lawyers in attendance from all over the Arab world, however, volunteered their services enthusiastically and joined on to Tueni’s campaign.
Mohammed Shaddo, a lawyer from Mauritania, described the lawsuit as one of the many weapons available in the resistance against “Israel” and volunteered to lead a case to try “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon for war crimes.
Sharon, whom he described as “a general who committed crimes on Lebanese land,” was the architect of the 1982 “Israeli” invasion of Lebanon.
Hussein Araji, an Iraqi lawyer, listed the destruction wrought on his country by US and British air raids, school by school and hospital by hospital. But a similar census has yet to be conducted in Lebanon.
Tueni called for the creation of government centers around the South to collect data on destruction and deaths as a first step in the campaign.
“If we keep quiet, we’re the losers. If we keep quiet we die,” Araji said. “We have conference after conference but have to put the voice of the Arab people into action.”

20 collaborators sentenced…(3)

A military court has sentenced 20 people to prison terms ranging from three weeks to seven years for collaborating with “Israel” during its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon.
The court, whose verdicts cannot be appealed, also slapped fines of up to LL5 million on the 20.
Mitri Issa Abu Mrad, a former guard at the Khiam detention center, was sentenced to seven years in jail. He was convicted of torturing inmates and also fined LL400,000.
Since June, more than 2,300 people have been sentenced to jail terms of up to 25 years. At least 14 collaborators have been sentenced to death in absentia for killing resistance fighters who fought “Israel’s” occupation forces.

Arab League Asserts Occupied Quds Is A Red Line


Arab League Secretary General Esmat Abdel Meguid said Thursday that no country should cross the “red line” of occupied Quds(Jerusalem) after US President George W. Bush promised to move the US embassy there.

“The question of Al-Quds is a red line that no state should cross,” Abdel Meguid told journalists at the Arab League ahead of next week’s summit of Arab leaders in Jordan.

“This question is governed by UN Security Council resolution 242, which the United States recognizes and supports,” he said.

Bush, during a Tuesday appearance with “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon, stood by an election campaign pledge to move the US embassy from Tel Aviv to occupied Quds.

A Palestinian Martyr, Several Wounded Amid Continuation of Clashes…(5)


The “Israeli” occupation  army said  today it killed a Palestinian  man overnight it claimed he was trying to  infiltrate into the “Israeli”-occupied territory  from the northern Gaza Strip armed  with a bomb.

The body of the martyr remained on  the ground while sappers attempted  to defuse the explosive device, an  army spokesman said.

Another Palestinian was seriously  wounded after a group of zionist  settlers beat him.

Also fierce clashes erupted today  between Palestinian fighters and “Israeli” occupation troops near the Neve Dekalim and Karid settlements in the Gaza Strip.

Clashes began after an  “Israeli” bulldozer attempted to set up a military post near Neve Dekalim  settlement.

And overnight, Palestinian and  “Israeli” occupation troops exchanged shelling.

“Israeli” troops shells rained  down Khan Yunis' northern refugee  camp south of the Gaza Strip.

According to Palestinian medical  sources three Palestinians were  wounded as a result of the shelling, one  was a 15-year old boy, while houses  in the area were heavily damaged.

Meanwhile, Palestinians  returned by firing three mortar shells  at the Nezarim settlement and two  more at the Morag settlement.

The  “Israelis” claimed there were no  injuries on their part.

A Palestinian  official said as far as he knew there  were no mortar shells in Palestinian  arsenals adding that the “Israelis” fired the shells at the Palestinian position  without justification.

On Wednesday night, the “Israeli”  army shelled a training base for  Palestinian Chairman Yasser  Arafat's bodyguards, killing  one officer and seriously wounding  three others, Palestinian sources  said.

On the other hand, Marwan Barghouti, Palestinian Fatah  leader in the West Bank, said he was  seeking to broaden the Palestinian  uprising, intensifying both popular  demonstrations and the armed  "resistance."

Barghouti said there will be an escalation of the intifada in the coming weeks to confront Sharon's new policy of  cracking down on the Palestinians,  launched to prove he can give “Israel”  security.

UN, Human Rights Group Condemn “Israeli” Use of Excessive Force…(6)


“Israel” came under fire from human rights activists again Thursday for turning a blind eye to Jewish settlers who kill Palestinians, a day after being pilloried for using excessive force against the Palestinian uprising.

“Israeli” human rights group B’Tselem said settlers have killed six Palestinians during the current Palestinian intifada, with “Israeli” law enforcement agencies investigating only two of the deaths.

“In addition, over the past few months, settlers fired at and wounded Palestinians, threw stones at Palestinian vehicles, damaged property, uprooted trees, burned a mosque, harmed Palestinian medical teams, attacked journalists, prevented farmers from reaching their fields and blocked roads,” it said.

Around 200,000 Jewish settlers live in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, land occupied by “Israel” in war 34 years ago, but their presence there is illegal under international law.

In Geneva, a three-member UN human rights team called Wednesday for the immediate establishment of an “adequate and effective” international presence in the West Bank and Gaza Strip, saying it was beyond dispute that “Israeli” security forces had used “excessive and disproportionate force.”

“Even in life-threatening situations, minimum force should be used against civilians,” it said.

However, in a meeting with UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan on Wednesday, Sharon underscored his opposition to the deployment of UN observers, echoing his Foreign Minister Shimon Peres.

150 “Israeli” Collaborators Captured by Palestinian Authority…(7)


Rashid Abu Shabak, deputy  head of Palestinian preventive security  in the Gaza Strip said that over 150  “Israeli” collaborators have been arrested  since the beginning of the six-month- old Palestinian uprising.

Abu Shabak said that among them  was one who provided information  that led to the February  assassination of 50-year-old  Massud Ayyad, a high-ranking official  in Arafat's Force-17 private bodyguard.

He asserted interrogation of the  alleged agents revealed "“Israeli”  plans to assassinate a  number of other Islamist and  nationalist figures as well as  Palestinian Authority and security  personalities.

The “Israeli” occupation army has liquidated around  20 intifadah activists since late last September.
