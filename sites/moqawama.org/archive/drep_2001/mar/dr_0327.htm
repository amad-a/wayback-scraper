Nasrallah refuses to confirm deny possession of Iranian missiles…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, refused on Monday to either confirm or deny reports that the party has acquired new longer-range missiles from Iran.
In an interview with “Israel’s” Maariv newspaper Friday, “Israeli” Premier Ariel Sharon accused Syria of helping Iran arm Hizbullah in a massive airlift of rockets, arms and ammunition.
“We are not concerned to either confirm or deny such reports. Sharon assumes that we in Lebanon will be worried when he says that we have rockets. Instead, we would be happy if the rockets he talked about were in our possession,” Nasrallah told a rally in a suburb south of Beirut late Sunday.
Up until “Israel’s” withdrawal last May, Hizbullah had occasionally fired into “Israel” 122mm Katyusha rockets with a range of around 20 kilometers. The weapon in question, the 240mm version of the Katyusha, reportedly has a range of 70 kilometers.
Dismissing Sharon’s claim as an “old boring tune,” Nasrallah said: “I want to tell Sharon and all (members) of the enemy government that we will do what is our right and certainly, what is our duty. It is our right to defend our country and defend every drop of blood and every drop of water and every iota of soil.
“It is our right to be strong to repulse the enemy,” he said. “Can you imagine that “Israel”, which possesses nuclear weapons and arms of mass destruction, is protesting against the resistance on charges that the resistance possesses a rocket with a 70-kilometer range?”
Reports that Hizbullah has acquired longer-range rockets have been routinely aired since June 1996. In February last year, “Israeli” security sources were quoted as saying that Hizbullah had been given 240mm Fajr rockets with a 70-kilometer range, which were being guarded in the Bekaa by a unit of Iranian Revolutionary Guards.
Hizbullah is not thought to have fired any such rockets into “Israel” during the years of occupation and the reports that it possesses Fajr rockets have remained unconfirmed.
Hizbullah leaders have repeatedly denied receiving military aid from Iran, insisting that Tehran’s support is political and moral and that they buy their weapons on the global market.
The guerrillas have not fired into “Israel” since last May.
“Sharon is not worried about Kiryat Shmona or Nahariya,” Nasrallah said in reference to “Israeli” towns near the border, “but he is worried about Haifa and Tiberias and about the whole of occupied northern Palestine.”

Hizbullah Calls on The Arab Summit to Uphold the Palestinian Cause…(2)

Hizbullah called on the Arab Summit to consider the Palestinian cause and its symbol- occupied Quds (Jerusalem)- the central cause of the nation, especially amid the continuation of the heroic Aqsa intifadah, which revived the Palestinian cause and placed it on the right track.

In a statement its, Hizbullah asserted its support for the option of struggle taken by the Palestinians, and reiterated its call on the Arab states  not to stake on negotiating with the “Israelis”, but rather adopt the choice of the Palestinians by supporting them and by cutting all ties with the Zionist Entity.

Hizbullah urged the Arabs not to yield to pressure exercised by the United States, which is trying to sow dispute between the Arabs themselves in order to distract them from their duties towards the intifadah and occupied Quds.

The statement also called on the Arab Summit to take a firm stand concerning the “Israeli” Premier Ariel Sharon's threats against the Arab world, especially against Lebanon and Syria, through affirming Lebanon's right to resist the occupation, and Syria's right to recuperate its occupied land. This stand should be translated into practical measures by the Arab states.

Civilian injured in south Lebanon mine explosion…(5)

“ A Lebanese civilian was injured near Tyre Monday when he stepped on a landmine left by “Israel” after its May 2000 withdrawal from south Lebanon, police said.

Nazem Messelmani, 31, was hunting near the Al-Shaytiya village, 80 kilometres (50 miles) south of Beirut, when the incident happened, the police said.

Ten Lebanese civilians have been killed and 70 injured by mines left over since “Israeli” troops withdrew from southern Lebanon on May 24 last year.

According to UN estimates, “Israel” left at least 130,000 mines and unexploded shells in the former 850 square kilometer (330 square mile) occupied zone.

Mystery group declares war on collaborators…(6)

A car belonging to a suspected collaborator with “Israel” was blown up in the border village of Houla overnight Monday, causing damage to a nearby house but no casualties.
A group called Strike Force 513 claimed responsibility for the bomb attack and warned that “the gates of Hell” would open for any collaborators who remain in the village.
The bombing is the latest in a growing number of incidents in the former occupation zone targeting ex- “Israeli”-allied South Lebanon Army militiamen and people suspected of working for “Israel”.
The bomb ­ consisting of 200 grams of TNT, according to the police ­ was planted beneath the car and detonated early Monday morning. The car was gutted and the windows of a nearby building were smashed.
The car belonged to Mohammed Abboud, 47, who lives and works in the village. According to a Houla resident who requested anonymity, Abboud is suspected of having worked for the “Israelis”.
A hand-written statement was found beside the wreck of the car, warning “collaborators and traitors” to leave the village.
“This is a warning to all collaborators and traitors that they should leave the town of Houla immediately, or the response will be very harsh and the gates of Hell will be opened on you. This is just a message,” the statement said.
The warning was signed “The Revolutionaries and Avengers for all Detainees and Martyrs and all those who Suffered During the Occupation ­ Strike Force 513.”
The communique was headed “Statement Number One,” indicating the possibility of further attacks by the group against suspected collaborators.
Since “Israel’s” withdrawal last May, local parties, Hizbullah in particular, have used their influence to curb incidents of revenge. Hizbullah distanced itself from the attack, saying that security in the area was the government’s responsibility.
“Hizbullah has no links with this incident,” a spokesman told The Daily Star. “We are not the ones to maintain security in this part of Lebanon. The government is responsible for security.”
The bombing is the latest in a series of increasingly violent incidents over the past two months directed at the collaborators and their families.
In the last week a house in Shemaa near Naqoura belonging to a former militiamen village chief was blown up and a house in the village of Hebbarieh belonging to a former spy in Khiam prison was firebombed.
Many residents of the villages have expressed deep unhappiness at what they consider lenient sentences handed down to former militiamen by the Military Tribunal.

A medical source in the Palestinian  Health Ministry reported that  Hassan Mansour, 15,  was shot in the abdomen by live  “Israeli” gunfire as he passed by “Israeli”  tanks at the Mintar crossing point.

Sources in Gaza hospital said  Mansour was in a serious condition.

The Palestinians were partaking in a  protest against the closure policy still  practiced by the “Israeli” authorities on the city that caused a great damage to  tourism and agriculture .

On the other hand, exclusive  information to ALMANAR Channel  reported that a water reservoir in  the settlement of Nahal Oz was  bombed.

Some three million m3 of  water leaked from the reservoir which  ended up in drowning the  plantations in the village of Ashuja'ah  and Eastern Beit Hanoun.

The enemy evacuated the settlers in the village which was recently targeted by  the mortar bombs of the Intifadah men.

Later, a Palestinian driver ran over a “Israeli” border guard. The location was still not mentioned.

A Jewish settler was also seriously wounded in the Jewish section of al-Khalil (Hebron) following clashes between Palestinian intifadah fighters and occupation troops.

In the Arab camp, a Palestinian resistance fighters opened fire on an “Israeli” army patrol.

Clashes also erupted in Tel al-Raas region in the West Bank town of Nablus.
