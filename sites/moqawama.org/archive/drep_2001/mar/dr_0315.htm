“Israel” Quarrels Red Cross Over Hizbullah-Held Soldiers…(1)

“Israeli” President Moshe Katsav on Wednesday lashed at the International Committee of the Red Cross for failing to bring enough pressure to bear on Hizbullah to provide information about the three “Israeli” soldiers taken captive last Oct. 7.

The ICRC rejected the charge as "unwarranted and regrettable" and said “Israel” has ceased giving the Red Cross access to two high-profile Lebanese prisoners in “Israel”, Sheikh Abdul Karim Obeir and Mustafa Dirani.

Katsav told state-run Radio “Israel” that the jewish state could not obtain any bit of information about the conditions of the three captives although at least one of them was wounded in a firefight that led to the abduction.

"If we were talking about a different country, I'm sure the international community would be in an uproar and would demand at least a visit to the captured soldiers by the international Red Cross," Katsav said.

An ICRC spokesman in “Israel”, Uriel Masad, said Katsav's suggestion of international bias against “Israel” was "regrettable."
Germany, which has been trying to broker a Hizbullah-”Israel” swap of prisoners, has given up its mediation bid after the recent change of government in “Israel”.

“Israelis” make veiled threats over Hasbani water project…(2)

“Israel” will ask the United Nations to stop Lebanon building a small pumping station along the Hasbani River, a move it claims could seriously deplete its water supply.
Defense (War) Ministry spokesman Shlomo Dror claimed that in addition to the pump house, Lebanon planned to build a dam to divert the entire flow away from the River Jordan and the Sea of Galilee, reducing both “Israel” and Jordan’s water supplies.
Dror said that due to a second year of low rainfall, water is “scarcer than ever,” and “Israel” could not allow Lebanon to divert the Hasbani.
“We really expect the Lebanese to understand that this is very important to us and that we won’t be quiet over it,” he said.
Despite the protests, a United Nations spokesman maintained that the “Israeli” claims were vastly overstated.
“Workers are building a four-inch pipe to carry water from the Wazzani springs to a poor village lacking water. You don’t divert a river with a pipe so small,” said Timur Goksel, spokesman for the UN Interim Forces in the South.
Nonetheless, an “Israeli” military source claimed on Tuesday that Lebanon was trying to change the course of the Hasbani, one of three tributaries of the Jordan River, which feeds the Sea of Galilee. The sea is “Israel’s” main source of fresh water.
Goksel said the “Israelis” had little reason to raise the issue now, particularly as they had been briefed well in advance.
“When work started on Feb. 20 we informed the “Israeli” Army that UNIFIL would be providing security to the workers and they were fully aware of what the work was about. I really don’t know why it became a story today. I’m a bit puzzled.”
Speaker Nabih Berri endorsed the pumping project Wednesday, saying the water was “necessary” for the liberated areas.
Asked if he considered “Israeli” threats a prelude to future aggression, Berri replied only that caution was required because ““Israel” withdrew defeated and not as a result of a peace process.”
Uri Saguy, head of the “Israeli” national water company Merokot and a former military intelligence chief, told public radio, “the pumping is illegal and I hope that “Israel” will defend its interests.”
“We have the means to do it,” he added.
However, Saguy refused to say if he was recommending military action. “Whoever takes unilateral action will find himself with a unilateral reaction,” he replied. “There is no water in the Middle East. Therefore, understandings must be reached. If not, it could turn into a war or a forceful confrontation.”
“Israeli” legislator Michael Kleiner, a member of Prime Minister Ariel Sharon’s Likud Party, urged his government to destroy the station. “A change in the water distribution is a provocation and “Israel” must respond,” Kleiner told “Israel” radio.
Goksel however, said the residents of the nearby village of Wazzani had already complained earlier this year about lack of water before resorting to the Council for the South.
“The council took action and decided to carry out a project to carry water from the river to a reservoir in the village. The council approached UNIFIL to provide security for the workers because it is a controversial region.”
Goksel said the project, due to be completed on April 20, involved “opening a dirt-track road down to the river side, and installing a pump and four-inch pipe to carry the water to a water tower in Wazzani.”
The council’s workers have completed building a pump house on the riverbank. It faces a similar “Israeli” pump which was erected in 1965 to supply a similar amount of water to the nearby occupied village of Ghajar.
A Council for the South team has also installed 13 electricity poles to supply water pumps with power.
An engineer supervising the project said that “we will work here day and night to show them that we don’t care about their threats.”
Wednesday’s construction efforts were observed by a number of plainclothes “Israeli” security agents, who were deployed on a hill overlooking the river. One of the agents was armed. A UNIFIL patrol positioned in two armored personnel carriers also observed the construction, while about seven other “Israelis” were deployed along the banks in Ghajar.
Despite the UN protection, however, Wazzani residents are still fearful that “Israel” will try and bomb the water pump.
Council President Qabalan Qabalan said works will continue, despite the presence of “Israeli” forces, especially as “the water is ours.”
Elias Kfoury, Greek Orthodox bishop for the areas of Sidon, Tyre and Marjayoun, also condemned the “aggressive “Israeli” statements.”
““Israel” doesn’t have the right to violate our rights because the land and the water are ours,” he said. “Let “Israel” leave us in peace. We know how to manage our interests.” ­ with agencies

“Israel” crosses border to destroy shelter…(3)

“Israeli” troops crossed the UN-delineated Blue Line onto Lebanese soil at Fatima Gate early on Wednesday to bulldoze a concrete shelter that has been occasionally used by Hizbullah members.
The incursion occurred at 1am and involved an unknown number of soldiers crossing through the gate with a bulldozer. The concrete shelter ­ originally used by “Israeli” troops during the occupation ­ was removed to the “Israeli” side of the border.
Hizbullah confirmed the incident to The Daily Star. A UNIFIL officer said that a team of peacekeepers was dispatched to Fatima Gate to investigate the incursion.

“Israeli” soldiers kill Palestinian; woman dies after “Israelis” prevent her from seeking medical treatment…(4)

“Israeli” soldiers have shot and killed a 19-year-old Palestinian near the Karni border crossing between the Gaza Strip and “Israel”. He was shot in the back although no clashes were taking place in the area. In the West Bank, a fifty-year-old woman died after “Israeli” soldiers refused to let her through a checkpoint to receive emergency medical treatment. At least ten Palestinians were injured in “Israeli” gunfire during protests marking a day of rage across the West Bank and Gaza. 

“ Arab-”Israeli” members of parliament have marched on the main checkpoint into the Palestinian-controlled West Bank city of Ramallah to protest “Israel’s” continued siege of the territories. Palestinians have been protesting in a "day of rage" against “Israel’s” crippling blockade imposed on Ramallah, an economic and cultural center.  Officials said tens of thousands of civilians are facing serious-health related problems due to “Israel’s” closures. A 50-year-old Palestinian woman died after “Israeli” troops refused to allow her into a hospital outside the West Bank town of Jenin. “Israel” claimed it had bowed to international pressure by opening some roads and removing roadblocks that served a harsh blow to an already ailing Palestinian economy. The United States, European Union and Russia sharply criticized “Israel” for its tightened closure on the territories, saying it will not bode well for peace making. Palestinians described “Israel’s” perceived easing of the blockade as cosmetic. President Yasser Arafat's Fatah movement called on Palestinians to end the siege "using all means." “Israel’s” military said it is preparing for an escalation of fighting towards the end of the month when Arab leaders convene for a summit in Jordan. The Palestinian uprising and “Israel’s” carckdown on the territories will top the gathering's agenda. “Israel’s” hardline leader Ariel Sharon convened his security cabinet for talks on the blockade ahead of a visit to Washington next week. The US and European states have been warning “Israel” against the continued clamp down on the Palestinian territories. European Commissioner for External Affairs Chris Patten and Sweden's Foreign Minister Anna Lindh who met Sharon and Arafat called for ending the blockade to prevent an economic collapse of the Palestinian Authority, which they warned will devastate peacemaking.
