Army arrests 12 who ‘spied on Hizbullah’…(1)

Twelve spies who were gathering intelligence on Hizbullah and planning bomb attacks on behalf of “Israel” have been arrested, the army announced Thursday.

The army said the 12 suspects confessed they had been trained in “Israel”, where they sent information about anti-”Israeli” resistance forces in Lebanon.

"The network had enrolled a Palestinian officer with the grade of lieutenant colonel who has also been arrested, and its members carried out a number of car bombings in Sidon and its suburbs" in southern Lebanon, an army statement said without providing details.

Judicial sources told AFP that Lebanese authorities started to arrest members of the "terrorist network, which includes at least one woman, about three weeks ago."

The arrests would mark the first time Lebanon has announced the detention of an “Israeli” agent since the Jewish state ended its bloody 22-year occupation of south Lebanon in May.

During the occupation, Lebanon regularly announced the arrests of “Israeli” spies.

The army statement did not mention the detainees' identities or nationalities, besides the Palestinian officer.

Security sources told AFP that two Palestinian officers from the Fatah movement of Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat had been arrested in the Sidon region about two weeks ago. But the sources could not confirm whether the arrests were linked to the "terrorist network."

The army statement said Lebanese authorities "confiscated sophisticated remote-controlled equipment and secret cameras" at the time of the arrests.

"Members of the network have confessed that they underwent training inside “Israel” and had been ordered to enroll people to work for the enemy and carry out bombings in the region of Sidon and nearby areas," the statement said.

"They have also confessed that they had gathered information about the activities of the resistance and its positions and had also sent such information to the enemy," the army added.

It said the case had been "referred to the concerned judicial authorities." Cases of espionage are tried at military courts in Lebanon.

A number of Hizbullah and Palestinian officials had been targetted by car bombings in the Sidon region in the months before the “Israeli” pullout.

But security sources told AFP that since the “Israeli” troop pullout in May, no car bombing has been reported near Sidon, including at the Ain al-Helweh camp where about 60,000 Palestinian refugees reside.

Lebanon sceptical over "bizarre" new “Israeli” government…(2)

“ New “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon's overtures for peace in the Middle East have aroused great skepticism in Lebanon, where his government was described Thursday as "bizarre" and "dangerous."

Lebanese Prime Minister Rafiq Hariri, reacting to the investiture of his “Israeli” counterpart Ariel Sharon on Wednesday expressed doubts on the future stability of the “Israeli” government, which has eight parties ranging from the left to the extreme right.

He did however refuse to dramatise the return to power of Sharon, who is hated in Lebanon since he led the “Israeli” invasion in 1982, which was marked by the massacres of Palestinians in the Sabra and Shatila refugee camps.

"We have to wait and see what the program of the Sharon government will be. But I do not think there will be war, even if there is no peace either," Hariri told AFP.

"No Arab country is for escalation," he added.

Hariri observed that "we will be suffering less than the “Israelis”, knowing the problems that they have with the Palestinian issue, the intifada (Palestinian uprising) and other challenges."

Sharon called Wednesday for Syria and Lebanon to make peace with “Israel”, saying “Israel” wanted "neighborly" relations with them along the lines of peace treaties signed with Egypt and Jordan.

"We want realistic political arrangements and a real peace with Syria and Lebanon," Sharon told parliament in his investiture speech.

"Lebanon and the Arabs desire peace with “Israel”, if they regain their occupied territories and if the “Israeli” government respects international laws, United Nations resolutions and human rights," Hariri told AFP in response.

The Jewish state occupied southern Lebanon for 22 years, before pulling out May 24, 2000.

Beirut, which proclaims its solidarity with Syria on the question of the Golan Heights, does not however consider the retreat from its country to be have been complete.

“Israel” has not pulled out of the Shibaa Farms, a contested area adjacent to the Golan it captured from Syria since 1967 but which Lebanon now claims.

The Lebanese press virtually ignored the Sharon call for peace Thursday but attacked the make-up of the “Israeli” coalition, in more severe terms.

The influential daily As-Safir called the government "most bizarre and most dangerous," seeing it as "an attempt to patch together the fragments of “Israeli” politics."

The Sharon coalition "is the result of horse-trading and compromise between those who are calling for a mass expulsion of the Arabs" from the occupied territories "and for them to be killed one by one, and those who say there can be a deal," it said.

For another daily, Al-Anwar, Sharon has formed "a government of political and military violence", one of contradictions where "all the options are open except a comprehensive peace."

"Despite its contradictions, this government is agreed on one thing, that peace accords should be piecemeal and not comprehensive," it said.

The mass-circulation Ad Dyar said Sharon had spoken "fine words, as if he had become a man of peace."

But it added: "Even if they deceive the rest of the planet, they will not deceive Lebanon and the Arab world."

Recalling Sharon's role during “Israel’s” 1982 invasion of Lebanon in the slaughter of Palestinians in Beirut refugee camps by Christian militia, Ad Dyar said: "His massacres are always in our minds and his peace is only an attempt to impose on the Arabs a surrender on “Israeli” terms."

The paper claimed Sharon "incarnates the “Israeli” snake which wraps itself gently around its prey, in order to stifle it and swallow it once it gets a good hold."

Kach vows to return to Hebron grave today…(3)

Hundreds of activists from the outlawed Kach movement held celebrations last night near the grave in Kiryat Arba of Baruch Goldstein, the man who massacred 27 Palestinians in Hebron in 1994. The police prevented the extreme right-wing activists from honoring Goldstein at the grave itself, although members of the movement said they intended to hold their celebration near but not at the site of the grave.
Kach activists Noam Federman and Baruch Marzel asked the police a few days ago for permission to hold a Purim party near the grave. Extreme right-wing sources say they hold the party every year without special permission from the police, but this year they wanted to put the issue on the public agenda.
In response to the request, the police set down several conditions, including a ban on coming within 100 meters of the grave and on holding any form of political parade. Yesterday morning, senior police officials pressured the Hebron station to cancel the Purim party, and the Judea and Samaria (West Bank and Gaza Strip) district police subsequently announced that it would not be permitted to take place.
Later, the police declared the area around the grave a closed military area and arrested right-wing activist Michael Ben-Horin, when he refused to leave the area. In response, Kach activists promised to return to the grave today.
