Hammoud will head delegation to Arab League (1)

Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud will lead a delegation to the Arab League Council’s 115th session opening in Cairo on Monday.
The session’s agenda will include two items entered by Lebanon ­ the first deals with Arab solidarity with Lebanon to complete last year’s liberation from “Israeli” occupation. The item also includes a request for $50 million in assistance to help clear mines in the South.
Beirut will also raise the issue of Lebanese hostages and detainees in “Israeli” jails.
On Sunday evening, Cairo will also host a monthly meeting of an Arab follow-up committee to back the Palestinian intifada. The committee was set up at the extraordinary Arab summit held in Egypt late last year.
The committee will review a report by Arab League Secretary-General Esmat Abdel-Meguid on recommendations adopted by a meeting held in Amman last month. It will also discuss preparations for an ordinary Arab summit, which will take place in Amman on March 27.
Hammoud will be accompanied at the Cairo meetings by the Foreign Ministry’s director of political affairs, Naji Abi Asi, his office director, Mohammed Deeb, and the Arab affairs director, Hisham Dimashqieh.
It will be Hammoud’s first trip abroad after recovering from a health setback, which prevented him accompanying Prime Minister Rafik Hariri on his recent official visits to Tokyo, Paris and Amman.

Cuban speaker tours South, pledges solidarity (2)

Cuban Speaker of Parliament Ricardo Alargon de Quesada expressed his country’s solidarity with Lebanon and all just Arab causes and criticized the international community “for failing to implement UN resolutions.”
De Quesada, who arrived here Tuesday and met with his Lebanese counterpart Nabih Berri, toured the South on Wednesday, starting at Naqoura and moving on to Qana, where town officials briefed him on “Israel’s” killing of more than 100 civilians there in 1996.
The Cuban speaker was accompanied on his tour by Cuba’s Ambassador to Lebanon Enrique Hernandez.

More Intifadah Operations against the  “Israeli” Occupation (3)

A small bomb exploded in a rubbish skip at an industrial  zone near West Occupied Quds on Wednesday. No  casualties were reported.

The “Israeli”  police suspected the bomb was in a  garbage bag.

In the Gaza Strip, Palestinian resistance  fighters opened fire at an “Israeli” army outpost near Rafah and at other outposts  near the Jewish settlements of Nevi  Dekalim and Gosh Katif.

Gunfire clashes also erupted near the  Netzarim Settlement, after which “Israeli”  troops shelled Palestinian houses.

Meanwhile, hundreds of 1948 Palestinians  demonstrated at the Beit Hanoun crossing  point angry at the “Israeli” army which  prevented them from crossing into the Strip  to visit relatives.

On the other hand, an “Israeli” military spokesman reported  that a Jewish settler was wounded  Tuesday night when Palestinians hurled  stones at his car, near the West Bank  town of Beit Laham.

Also  Palestinian resistance fighters attacked an  “Israeli” military camp and the Psagot  Settlement near Ramallah in the West  Bank. While in the Gaza Strip  Palestinians opened fire at an occupation outpost near the Nevi Dekalim settlement.

De-Mining South Lebanon Needs 30 Years (4)

The government is displeased with the failure of the international community to help defuse the minefields the “Israeli” army left behind when it withdrew from south Lebanon last May, An-Nahar reported on Wednesday.
The Beirut authorities are also unhappy about the U.N. reluctance to allow the peacekeeping force in south Lebanon take a bigger role in de-mining the region, An-Nahar said.
The peace force, known as UNIFIL, is de-mining only its zone of operations, leaving the rest of the region for the Lebanese government to cope with.
Unifil's Ukrainian contingent has been handling the de-mining operation, "But the pace is so slow that it would take 30 years to remove the 130,000 mines in the south," An-Nahar quoted a Foreign Ministry official as saying.
Many donor nations have said Lebanon should sign the Ottawa Convention that bans the use, production and transportation of anti-personnel mines to be entitled for assistance.
But Lebanon was reported to have notified the United Nations that it could not sign because “Israel” hasn't sign the convention, yet.
None of the countries possessing mine-detecting aircraft or radar was willing to extend this equipment to Lebanon and “Israel” has been adamantly refusing to provide maps of the minefields, foreign ministry sources in Beirut said.
“Israel” claims it had given the maps to the South Lebanon Army, which has ceased to exist.
