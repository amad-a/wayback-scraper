Hezbullah Secretary-General Says “Israelis” Considering Withdrawal From Shibaa Farms

Hezbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said the “Israelis” began seriously discussing withdrawing from the occupied Lebanese Shibaa Farms, asserting that their presence in this area is costing them more and more, including the loss of lives.

Speaking during a ceremony organized by the Department of Women's Activities in the Islamic Resistance Support Foundation in honor of women who supported the Resistance morally and financially, Sayyed Nasrallah responded to the “Israeli” Premier Ariel Sharon's threats saying that since the liberation of the South on 25 May, northern occupied Palestine is within the resistance's grip.

He said if the “Israelis” plan to attack Lebanon, then they would go through lots of considerations, fearing  the response of Hezbullah.

"Those believing that the issue with the Zionist Entity has ended with the withdrawal of the occupation forces from a large part of our land is wrong and wrongly perceives the local and regional circumstances," added Nasrallah…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general  said that even if “Israel” pulls out from the Shibaa Farms, the resistance will keep its weapons to prevent future attacks.
“The resistance ­ its weapons, Katyusha rockets and fighters ­ will remain on the border until further notice because this serves Lebanese interests and security. “I want to tell you now and later that the country needs resistance.”
He also denied claims that his group’s continued presence on the border served the interests of Syria. “The presence of the resistance on the border serves the Lebanese national interests, first and foremost,” he asserted.
Nasrallah was referring to calls made by foreign countries and much of the country’s Christian population for the deployment of the army in the South ­ a move which the government has so far refused.
The Hizbullah leader said no one could guarantee that “Israel” would not attack the country “in a month or two” ­ a reference to recent “Israeli” threats to bomb a local pumping station being erected along the Hasbani River…(2)

Lebanon's Hariri: Hizbullah has right to attack “Israel”…(3)

Even though “Israel” pulled its forces out of his country, Lebanese Prime Minister Rafiq Hariri said Lebanon has the right to fight for the Shaba Farms.
"The Lebanese government is fully convinced that the Shaba Farms area is a Lebanese territory and must be liberated by all means available," Hariri said in an interview with the London-based Jane's Defense Weekly to be published today.
"One way to regain Shaba Farms is through diplomatic contacts, while another way is through armed resistance. Only circumstances determine which course we should follow," Hariri was quoted as saying.
Hariri also gave Hizbullah a free hand in striking at “Israel”.
"Things are not as they used to be in the 1980s. Wars in that era did not solve the problems, but made things worse," said Hariri. "Now if [the “Israelis”] wage a war, Hizbullah will escalate its attacks."
Still, Hariri says a political settlement is possible with Prime Minister Ariel Sharon "if the new “Israeli” government has the true desire to do so." But he quickly added that statements by Sharon and other “Israeli” officials "lead to a pessimistic conclusion about peace prospects in the region."
Hariri blamed a lack of peace with “Israel” on the Jewish state.
"The peace process has not reached its objective because the “Israelis” are not yet ready for peace... and we cannot wait," says Hariri. "When “Israel” is ready [for peace], we'll be ready."
As to the possibility of “Israel” launching air strikes against Syrian military bases in Lebanon or Lebanese infrastructure in retaliation for future attacks by Hizbullah, Hariri said: "If “Israel” carries out military operations against Lebanon, it will only complicate the situation because these operations will increase the scale of violence. The solution cannot be reached through violence but only by resorting to political dialogue."
However, Hariri denied reports of differences between the government and Hizbullah on the productivity of continued guerrilla operations in the south, and declined to directly address the issue of the Lebanese Armed Forces disarming the guerrillas and deploying to fully secure the entire border with “Israel”.
"There are no problems between Hizbullah and the Lebanese government. When “Israeli” forces withdraw from all of south Lebanon, Hizbullah guerrillas will disarm themselves and weapons will naturally go to the Lebanese authorities," Hariri said.

Beirut takes “Israeli” threats to UN…(4)

The Foreign Ministry sent a message to the United Nations on Tuesday expressing its objection to “Israeli” threats over the waters of the Wazzani springs in the South.
The message was sent to New York at the request of acting Foreign Minister Mohammed Abdel-Hamid Beydoun.
“Israel” recently threatened Lebanon with military force if it decided to change, in any way, the normal courses of the Hasbani and Wazzani rivers.
Lebanon called on the UN to take the necessary steps to halt further “Israeli” threats.

“Israel” collaboration convictions continue…(5)

A military court convicted 12 people of collaboration and contact with “Israel” during its occupation of south Lebanon and sentenced them to prison terms ranging from three weeks to life.
The verdicts and sentences, handed down late Monday, were posted on the court’s bulletin board early Tuesday. The court, whose rulings cannot be appealed, dropped charges against five other defendants for lack of evidence.
The court sentenced Faris Qassis in absentia to life in prison with hard labor for working with a security branch of the former “Israeli”-allied South Lebanon Army militia and torturing a Lebanese man after kidnapping him in the former “Israeli”-occupied zone.
Two brothers were also sentenced in absentia to 15 years in jail and fined LL2 million each for joining the SLA.
The court sentenced one defendant to seven years in prison for collaborating with the “Israeli” military police and another  defendant to one year in jail for serving with the SLA.
Four defendants received six-month prison terms for joining the militia, while another three were sentenced to three weeks in jail for entering “Israel”, according to the court statement.
Another defendant was fined LL 500,000 for visiting “Israel”.

Hezbullah, Amal Movement For a Resistance Society Against the Occupation…(6)

The Amal Movement and  Hezbullah leaderships in south Lebanon considered that the threats of  “Israeli” Premier Ariel Sharon's  government against Lebanon calls for strengthening the unity in directing  the resistance against the “Israeli”  enemy so as to establish a society  able to undertake the burdens of  confrontation, continue the  liberation of occupied Lebanese  Shibaa Farms, and free the detainees in “Israeli” prisons, in addition to put a stop to “Israeli” threats against  Lebanon's use of its own water  resources.

During a meeting, the southern  leadership of both parties praised the  national stand against “Israeli”  attempts of infiltrations,
especially concerning the  issue of water, after the “Israeli”  occupation army withdrew from  Lebanon defeated.

Amal and Hezbullah's  leaderships also stressed on the  necessity of working for demining the  formerly occupied zone and reminded  the Lebanese state of its obligations  towards the reconstruction of  the liberated south.

Mofaz Says Palestinian Authority Coordinates with Hezbullah…(7)

“Israeli” occupation army chief  Tuesday accused Palestinian security forces of complicity in nearly half  “Israeli” deaths since “Israeli”-Palestinian violence erupted in late September.

“Of the 67 “Israelis” to have died since violence broke out, more than 40 percent, both among security forces and settlers, have, to our great distress, resulted from activity of the Palestinian security forces,” chief of staff Lieutenant-General Shaul Mofaz told public radio following a meeting of the parliament’s foreign affairs and war committee.

Mofaz told the meeting that the Palestinians were stockpiling weapons in territories under their control and that in the Gaza Strip, home weapons-making factories were emerging capable of assembling mortars and grenade launchers, the radio reported.

He said Palestinian security forces have been working with Hamas and Islamic Jihad to carry out attacks against “Israeli” targets and that Hizbullah has been preparing the forces for attacks against "“Israel”", the radio added.

Mofaz’s comments came just one day after "“Israel”" accused Yasser Arafat’s Palestinian Authority of deepening its involvement in “terror” and working with Hamas and the Hizbullah.

Three Palestinian Youths Shot, Wounded by “Israeli” Troops…(8)

Palestinian resistance fighters continued their attacks against  “Israeli” occupation army  locations  injuring an “Israeli” officer and a soldier  in the Jordan Valley.

Also in the northern West Bank, an “Israeli” settler was injured in a drive-by shooting attack, the army said.

It said he was hit in the leg when his car came under fire from another vehicle while he was travelling near the Jewish settlement of Alei Zahav southeast of the Palestinian-ruled town of Qalqilia.

Meanwhile, two Palestinian youths  were injured after “Israeli”  troops  fired rubber bullets on Palestinians  throwing stones at a checkpoint to their  closed-off village.

Witnesses said, dozens of  Palestinian youths came under fire  while throwing stones in protest to  the closure of Beit Laham  at the  southern entrance to the village of  al-Khoder in the West Bank.

Near al-Sawiya village south of Nablus, a Palestinian boy was shot by a Jewish settler.

The Palestinian boy injured Tuesday, Amin Ahmed, 13, was shot in the stomach by a settler after a group of schoolchildren threw stones at his car near al-Sawiya village south of Nablus, witnesses said.

“The settler got out of his car and shot at the group of children with an Uzi (machine pistol) before driving away,” one witness said, adding that the children were walking home from school when the incident took place.

According to the “Israeli” army radio,  there was an exchange of fire  overnight between a Palestinian police  position and an “Israeli” army patrol  near the Erez border crossing  between the Gaza Strip and the  occupied territories; no casualties were  reported.

The army also said Palestinians had  fired anti-tank rockets towards  “Israeli” soldiers in the south of the Gaza Strip, but the army claimed that  no one was injured .

“Israeli” civilian bulldozers meanwhile uprooted dozens of olive trees belonging to Palestinian villagers south of al-Khalil to clear the way for a road for Jewish settlers living in the Otniel settlement, witnesses said.

Residents of Khirbet al-Karma village in the southern West Bank confronted the bulldozers shouting “Allahu Akbar” (God is great).

Hanan Ashrawi injured by “Israeli” grenades…(9)

“Israeli” soldiers threw stun grenades at protesters and injured Palestinian lawmaker Hanan Ashrawi during a demonstration on Monday near East Jerusalem, witnesses said.
Witnesses said another three Palestinian demonstrators were also slightly hurt during the march as they passed an “Israeli” military checkpoint north of Jerusalem.

"They attacked us violently. We are unarmed, then they come and say we initiate violence," Ashrawi said. She was wounded in the leg and treated at the scene by bodyguards.
Ashrawi, who served in the past as Minister of Higher Education in the Palestinian Authority, is one of the best known spokespeople for the Palestinians. She participated in the 1991 Madrid peace talks between the Arabs and “Israel”.
Dozens of Palestinian women carrying Palestinian flags and banners participated in the protest against “Israeli” occupation and military blockades in the West Bank and Gaza Strip.
“Israel” sealed off the West Bank and Gaza after a Palestinian uprising against “Israeli” occupation began in late September after peace talks stalled.
“Israel” maintains the closures are needed to prevent militant attacks. But Palestinians say the measures amount to collective punishment, barring tens of thousands of Palestinian workers from reaching jobs inside “Israel” and stifling their economy.
"The siege (closure) is racist and should be lifted," one protest banner read. "The occupation is doomed to an end," said another.
Bowing to international pressure, “Israel” has eased some of the restrictions on Palestinian travel inside the West Bank and Gaza but Palestinians say the changes were cosmetic only.
Later on Monday, additional Palestinian demonstrators threw stones at “Israeli” soldiers near Al-Ram after the funeral of a 10-year-old Palestinian boy whose body was found close to the Neve Yaacov settlement on the edge of Jerusalem on Saturday.
Relatives of the boy accused Jewish settlers of killing him.

At least 387 Palestinians, 66 “Israelis” have been killed since the start of the Palestinian revolt.

No leads in Menara murder, weapons-theft case…(10)

Police said yesterday they had no leads or suspects in the largest weapons theft in the country's history and the murder of the security officer on Kibbutz Menara.
A large hunt is on for over 60 weapons, mostly M-16 assault rifles, stolen from the armory at the kibbutz near the Lebanon border. A special team has been set up to work with military police detectives and the General Security Service.
Police are confident that the theft of such a large number of weapons cannot remain hidden for long and their intelligence will turn up a lead.
Meanwhile, kibbutz security officer Yitzhak Kvartatz, 54, was laid to rest yesterday at the Kfar Sava Military Cemetery following his autopsy. His beaten body was found Monday a few kilometers from the kibbutz along the Lebanon border, with a bullet wound to the back of his head.
He was buried at the military cemetery since he was employed by the Border Police as the head of kibbutz security. The regional Border Police commander eulogized him.
It is not yet clear whether Kvartatz was killed before the weapons were stolen or afterwards, though Galilee police chief Yehuda Solomon said he may have known his killers.
"The investigation is just at its beginning, but it is a very grave and exceptional case and we are treating it as such," Solomon said at a press conference yesterday.
"We don't have any suspects at this stage. We still don't have any breakthrough," Solomon said. "Our intelligence efforts are very large now." He said security forces and police swept the area for kilometers around the kibbutz.
Kvartatz was last seen on Thursday but there were conflicting accounts of people spotting him later. The autopsy reportedly estimates that he was killed on Friday.
A day after his body was found and the locked armory discovered stripped of its large cache of weapons, more questions than answers have been raised on the perpetrators' motive. Police said they could not say yet whether the murder was criminally or nationalistically motivated.
There was also no question raised of Kvartatz's integrity. A veteran of the battle for Jerusalem in 1967 who was later wounded in the 1973 Yom Kippur War, the single man was utterly devoted to the kibbutz's security and had served in his position for over two decades.
"He was pedantic and devoted to security with a rich past in the Border Police. I can't say into whose hands the weapons have fallen," said Solomon. "We are not ruling out any motive and are making all our efforts to get our hands on the murderers and on the weapons."
He said the perpetrators must have known Kvartatz and his routine well, as well as being able to blend into the kibbutz without raising the suspicions of the guards. Police questioned dozens of people on the matter yesterday, including the Golani soldiers who were guarding the gate near the armory over the weekend.
"We are very concerned about this and are making a great and wide-scale effort, even beyond our district," Solomon said.
Security guards in the North, meanwhile, have been put on alert and briefed to take precautions.
Police in the North began carrying out security checks in all armories in the Galilee to make sure a similar incident doesn't happen. Village security chiefs and security officers in all the settlements in the Galilee region were asked to recount the weapons in their armories and re-examine their locks and alarm systems to make sure the store houses were still in working order.
According to police sources, police are considering installing a closed circuit TV camera in armories with over 50 weapons.
"You can say that most of the armories are adequately protected but what happened at Menara was a failure that is just too hard to accept," one police official told Itim.
The incident sparked a wave of suspicion at the many foreign workers and heavily populated Arab villages in the north.
"It is not hard today to grab a foreign worker or an Arab, give him some money to collect information on the armory and the secret code, and be helped by nationalist elements to steal the weapons," one unnamed GSS agent was quoted by Itim as saying.
At Menara, members gathered late Monday to discuss the murder and yesterday psychologists sent by the Galilee Regional Council came to talk about the fear among members and children over the murder.
