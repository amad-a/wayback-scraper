Tehran calls for more attacks by resistance
Imam Khamenei hails Hizbullah’s outstanding role

Iran’s supreme leader claimed over the weekend that Israel has been weakened, urging Hizbullah to continue attacking the Jewish state’s troops in the Shebaa Farms.
“Hizbullah, which has a heavy responsibility, must take the initiative against the Zionist enemy,” state-run radio quoted Ayatollah Ali Khamenei as saying.
Hizbullah killed an Israeli soldier on April 14 in a missile attack, the third to die in the Shebaa Farms since the party resumed military operations against Israel last October.
 Khamenei saluted the dead Syrian soldier as a “martyr of the intifada,” the nearly seven-month-long Palestinian uprising against Israel.
Iran’s spiritual leader paid tribute to Syria for “its resistance faced with the Zionist threats.”
Hizbullah should also strengthen cooperation with other Lebanese groups “to stand against the plots of the Zionist regime,” Khamenei said.
He described the intifada as “a direct result of the devoted efforts of Hizbullah and is proof of the awakening of Palestinian Muslims.”
Nasrallah and other Hizbullah officials are in Iran to take part in a conference Tuesday and Wednesday on the intifada….(1)

 On the other hand, Iran will host a two-day major  international conference from  Tuesday to support the Palestinian  intifadah against the Israeli enemy.

Hezbollah Secretary General Sayyed  Hassan Nasrallah along with other  Hezbollah officials will attend the conference. Some 35  Arab and Islamic nations, as well as all  Palestinian opposition groups are expected  to take part in the conference, which  was entitled “The International  Conference of Support for the  Intifadah and the Islamic Revolution in  Palestine”…(2)


Hizbullah MP vows to extract steep price if Israel launches new attacks…(3)

Baalbek-Hermel MP Hussein Hajj Hassan said Sunday that Hizbullah will attack Israeli settlements in Northern Palestine if the Jewish state continues to target Lebanese infrastructure.
“The resistance is ready to turn the Israeli settlers’ lives in northern Palestine into hell,” said Hajj Hassan, who said Hizbullah, not the Israelis, would “choose the time of the attack.”
The Hizbullah MP made the comments while attending a UNESCO Palace ceremony marking the 23rd year of the detention of Samir Qantar.
Qantar, the longest-serving detainee still in Israel, was given a 542-year sentence in 1979 after being convicted of killing five Israelis in the town of Naharya.
More than 400 attended the ceremony, which was organized by the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of the Lebanese Detainees in Israeli Prisons.
“We are equipped with the right weapons,” said Hajj Hassan, vowing that the resistance would liberate the Shebaa farms.
Hajj Hassan also praised the Palestinian resistance, especially Sunday morning’s suicide (martyrdom) bombing which killed one person and wounded dozens in Tel Aviv.
He also warned US President George W. Bush to be wary of the resistance’s capabilities.
“We say to President Bush ­ you should read and analyze the resistance well. We are neither weak nor afraid.
“Sharon should also understand that resistance will struggle for the return of the Palestinian refugees in Lebanon,” he continued, pointing out that “Sharon is afraid of their return.”
To relatives of detainees, Hajj Hassan said: “I can assure you, it won’t involve too much time before you see your detainees arriving ... (home) because the rules of the game have changed.
“In the past, liberating our detainees was a tenuous dem­and,” he added, “but today it has become an attainable possibility since our enemy has detainees, as we do.”
He also admonished authorities here for poor treatment of ex-detainees, saying Israeli prisons “would be better than living with unfulfilled promises.”
Aley MP Akram Chehayeb echoed the call, arguing that the state should treat the liberated detainees as heroes.
“The state should take good care of them,” he said, “so they don’t feel they were first imprisoned by occupation and then by their own country’s negligence.”
In a letter to his brother, Qantar said the detainees issue should not be separated from peace negotiations, saying the two were linked by “necessity.” The letter also urged all Arabs to defend the Palestinian intifada.
“We have to support the Palestinians ... and defend Palestinian refugees’ human rights in Lebanon,” it said.
Social Affairs Minister Asaad Diab, who spoke on behalf of President Emile Lahoud, said Israel did not respect international agreements and violated them at will, accusing the international community of failing to “distinguish between an attacker and its victims.”
Diab also promised to continue working to free both the detainees and all remaining Lebanese territory.
Mohammed Safa, the head of the follow-up committee, said he hoped that UN Resolution 425 contained provisions for the release of all detainees, rather than leave the issue up to international “promises.”
Safa spoke of the families of three Israeli soldiers captured by Hizbullah last fall, saying they are traveling the world in an attempt to enlist support for their cause, asking Lebanon, Syria and Iran to allow International Committee of the Red Cross delegates to visit their relatives.
“I ask the government to resist this campaign,” said Safa, urging the authorities to create their own campaign to seek the release of Lebanon’s 19 detainees.

 

Damascus supports the resistance against Isreali enemy..(4)

 Syria’s  Baath Party said Saturday that Damascus continued to support Hizbullah’s operations.
Syria “stands by the Lebanese national resistance to liberate the occupied Shebaa Farms and free the detainees in Israeli jails,” the official Syrian Arab News Agency (SANA) quoted  Suleiman Qaddah, the party’s assistant regional secretary, as saying.
Qaddah’s statement came in the wake of a request from Washington for Syria to use its influence on Hizbullah to restore calm in Lebanon.
Qaddah, who was speaking at the opening of a conference on information technology and economics, also renewed Syria’s support for the Palestinian uprising and called on the “international community and United Nations bodies to check Israel’s bloody drive.”
He said that “the escalation of Israel’s aggression” would do nothing “but increase the Arabs’ determination to stick to their rights, make more sacrifices and give more martyrs.”
Syrian President Bashar Assad was quoted Thursday by SANA as telling his US counterpart, George W. Bush, that “calls for restraint have become useless, as it is necessary to diagnose the problem,” meaning Israel’s occupation of Arab lands, “to find effective solutions.”

 

Lebanese suspected of collaborating with Israel targeted by bomb attacks..(5)

The cars of two Lebanese suspected of collaborating with Israel were blown up in the formerly occupied southern Lebanon village of Houla, Lebanese police said Saturday.

Two unknown bombers blew up overnight a car belonging to Ali Yaakub, a Houla resident who used to work in Israel. Yaakub house was damaged, but no one was injured by the blast.

Simultaneously, a similar attack targeted the car of Ali Hammud, suspected of having had contacts with Israel during its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon, police added.

The vehicle was destroyed but no injuries were reported.

On March 26, a booby-trapped car exploded in the southern Lebanese village, and a leaflet left at the scene threatened "all collaborators and traitors" with reprisal, urging them to "leave immediately".

A similar attack took place on April 9.

When the Israeli army pulled out of southern Lebanon last May, 6,000 Lebanese collaborators left the country, in fear of retaliation by the local population.

Several hundreds of them have now returned to Lebanon and some of them have been tried by a military court in Beirut for collaborating with the Israeli enemy.

 

 

Israeli dies in suicide (martyrdom) attack on bus station…(6)

Israel denounced Yasser Arafat's Palestinian Authority for failing to prevent a suicide (martyrdom) bombing which killed one Israeli civilian and wounded more than 40 others yesterday at a bus stop in the commuter town of Kfar Saba, north-east of Tel Aviv, during the morning rush hour.

According to witnesses, an Arab waited in the queue on the busy Weizmann Street until a No 29 bus pulled in, and blew himself up as soon as the driver opened the automatic doors. The bomber died instantly. So did an Israeli man in his mid-fifties who was going to work.

One of the wounded, a 14-year-old boy, was in a serious condition. Sunday marks the start of the working week in Israel. Kfar Saba, which is three miles from the Palestinian town of Kalkilya, has been a frequent target. A suicide (martyrdom) bomber killed himself and two Israeli schoolboys at a bus stop just outside the town three weeks ago.

No one claimed responsibility for yesterday's bombing, though militant Palestinian groups have taken responsibility or been blamed by Israel for recent attacks. After an emergency session of his inner cabinet, Prime Minister Ariel Sharon condemned what he called "this horrendous attack", which came just hours after Israeli and Palestinian security chiefs had resumed a dialogue designed to reduce the violence that has claimed 478 lives over the past seven months.

Before the suicide bombing, the toll had grown at the weekend with the death of a Palestinian security officer from wounds sustained last Monday when Israel shelled a police compound in the Gaza Strip.

Abdel-Aziz Rantissi, said yesterday: "The continuation of the Israeli occupation and Zionist aggression will be met by resistance. This is the legitimate right of the Palestinians."..(6)

Also, a 38-year-old Beit Shemesh man was murdered somewhere between Neveh Ya'akov and northern Ramallah over the weekend, police said yesterday after the man's body was recovered.

The body of Stanislav Sandomirski, 38, was found bearing signs of violence, after he had been missing since Friday afternoon.

Sandomirski had been visiting friends in Neveh Ya'akov and was due home Friday afternoon to pick up his daughters from community center activities. Saturday night, Palestinian security forces reported they found an abandoned Israeli car at the entrance to Sudra village, north of Ramallah. An Israeli patrol found Sandomirski's body in the trunk of the car. His funeral is scheduled for today…(7)

 

Meanwhile, a bomb exploded near a bus in the West Bank early on Monday but no one was injured, the Israeli army said.

An army spokeswoman said the bomb was activated near a bus close to the Jewish settlement of Ariel in the West Bank. She blamed the explosion on Palestinian militants.

Police said an explosion late on Sunday in the northern Israeli coastal city of Haifa was also caused by a bomb and also blamed the blast on Palestinian militants.

A police spokesman said three police officers were slightly hurt when the bomb exploded as police were clearing people from the scene. A civilian had called when he found a suspicious bag.

"Based on the type of explosive, it was a nationalist incident," the spokesman said…(8)

 

 

Hamas takes responsibility for Kfar Saba blast…(9)

Hamas this morning took responsibility for yesterday's (martyrdom)bombing in Kfar Saba that killed Dr. Mario Goldin, 52, and wounded 50 others.
Omar Salim Abu Atieh, 22, from the Sajaeih refugee camp in Gaza carried out the lethal attack, a statement published earlier in Gaza said.
"The Hamas will continue in its efforts to send suicide (martyrdom) bombers into Israeli territory," the statement concluded, according to an Army Radio report. Security measures are being further beefed up on the eve of Independence Day following yesterday's suicide (martyrdom) bombing in Kfar Saba.
One of the wounded, a 14-year-old boy, was still in critical condition in the city's Meir Hospital last night. Two others were moderately hurt and the rest were suffering from shock and minor injuries.

 

 

No signs of fatigue

By Danny Rubinstein


The Palestinian media gave play this weekend to Defense(War) Minister Benjamin Ben-Eliezer's comment that, "The question is: Which side will get tired first. It won't be us." Hearing about this remark, Palestinians responded in kind. Nor will we be the first to wear out, they said.In fact, insofar as public stamina is reflected by events on the ground in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, the Palestinians are not showing any signs of fatigue or collapse. The opposite is true. Despite all the obstacles, sacrifices and woes, it appears that the Palestinian population in the territories can forbear and continue the Intifada uprising for some time.

True, their quality of life has dropped. The Palestinian economy is stagnant and crippled, both in whole and in part. Yet the small sums of money that continue to reach the Palestinian Authority (PA) suffice to pay minimal salaries to almost 200,000 PA workers, along with officials on local councils and various public agencies in the territories.

One way or another, the Palestinians are getting by. There isn't starvation. Public morale remains fairly high. Throughout the seven months since the Al-Aqsa Intifada uprising erupted in September, not a single Palestinian has demanded that the Intifada be brought to a halt because it carries too high a cost. Calls for its continuation are sounded across the board. The Palestinian public mood is not swayed by a brief lull here or there, which lasts a mere few days, or the renewal of security contacts with Israel; breaks in the fighting or resumed contacts don't prompt anyone in the PA to speak in favor of making concessions.

Last week's events - particularly U.S. and European criticism of Israel, and the Israel Defense Forces'* (IDF) subsequent withdrawal from Beit Hanoun - reinforced a perception among Palestinians that the PA has gained a kind of balance of power with Israel. Israel's current government, which faces virtually no opposition at home, is compelled to accede to limitations on the use of force.

Of course, there is no doubt which is the stronger side in this Israel-PA confrontation. But under current diplomatic circumstances, Israel's power cannot be fully utilized in the stand-off with the Palestinians. The State of Israel cannot (or does not want to - the distinction is irrelevant) re-conquer the West Bank and Gaza, or parts thereof.

Nor can the State of Israel enforce a protracted or permanent closure in the territories, forcing the Palestinians to go hungry and cutting them off from vital services. In other words, despite Palestinian weakness and the clear asymmetry between the two sides, a kind of balance of power has taken root. PA Chairman Yasser Arafat's slogan, which he propounds day and night, sums up the situation: "We won't be subordinate to them; we won't get down on our knees."

In recent months, the lack of support from Arab states has vexed and infuriated Palestinians. The big money hasn't come to the territories; meantime, messages of solidarity with the PA are articulated by Arab public figures and intellectuals, who pressure their governments to furnish support. Last weekend, the East Jerusalem newspaper Al-Quds carried a poem written by Persian Gulf poet Razi Kusaibi, which ridicules Arab regimes for persisting with peace rhetoric at a time when "(Prime Minister Ariel) Sharon butchers us, and the wise sage (Rabbi Ovadia Yosef) curses us."

Buoyed by this sense that a balance of sorts has been reached with Israel, Palestinians advanced an ultimatum. Negotiations with Israel cannot be resumed, they say, unless there is a complete cessation of Israeli construction in the settlements and East Jerusalem. Though the Jordanian-Egyptian initiative has other clauses, members of the PA leadership say this no-building section is the one unconditional component of the plan to revive the peace process. Some Palestinian spokesmen go further, predicting that the Al-Aqsa Intifada uprising will set in motion a process of dismantling the settlements, and will go down in history as the "Settlements Intifada."

"Sharon withdrew from Beit Hanoun, and in the end he'll send his bulldozers out to raze the settlements. Their end will be akin to that of (the evacuated Sinai settlement) Yamit," Hafez Barghuti, editor of the Palestinian journal, Al Hayat Al Jedida, wrote this past weekend.

Responding to IDF efforts to divide the Gaza Strip into three sections, other Palestinian spokesmen observed that, in order to guarantee the presence of 12 Jewish families at the Netzarim settlement, a siege was enforced against 600,000 residents of Gaza City.

Taking a hard line, PA parliament speaker Abu Ala said that, this time around, the Palestinians will not assent to any Israeli ploy or trick: they won't put up with settlement construction or expansion carried out under the pretext of "natural growth." Abu Ala demanded an absolute settlement freeze.

Insisting that a total settlement freeze is a precondition for resumed negotiations, Arafat and his colleagues are aware that Israel's government will never assent to the demand. That means that an end to the Intifada is nowhere in sight.
