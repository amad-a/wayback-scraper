Striking at “Israel’s” support with a FIST…(1)

With bags under her eyes, Lana Khalil leans back into the sunlight and recalls how she started an anti-Zionist “divestment campaign” on campuses across the United States.
Khalil, a Lebanese who grew up in Nigeria, has only spent two years in Lebanon ­ enough time to witness firsthand what “Israeli” aggression is all about.
It was her first year here when “Israel” began its two-week bombing campaign in April of 1996, what it named operation Grapes of Wrath. The operation was responsible for the Qana massacre, when “Israel” shelled a UN base and killed more than 100 women, children and elderly seeking shelter.
“When I moved to Lebanon, the images I saw epitomized the dispossession and imperialism of a nation. Che Guevara once said that sympathy with suffering somewhere in the world is the root of revolutionary work,” says Khalil, sitting cross-legged in her sandals and loose tank top on a bench in Sanayeh park.
Witnessing the aftermath of the Qana attack prompted her
to join a student group called Fight “Israeli” State Terrorism (FIST) at the American Community School, where she was a student, that year.
It wasn’t long before she extended her sense of compassion to the Palestinian community when she began volunteering as a teacher at the Sabra and Shatila refugee camps.
“When I started developing such close ties with my students, I wasn’t fighting simply for inalienable rights but for these people whom I was so emotionally attached to,” she says.
It was only natural for her to establish FIST at Sarah Lawrence College in New York in her freshman year. The organization focused on educating students on the Middle East and “deconstructing myths systematically propagated in the media,” Khalil explains.
FIST then began action campaigns that included teach-ins, lectures and a recreation of Khiam prison on campus.
The original 15 members were mainly comprised of Jewish, “Israeli” and other international students. Khalil is the only Arab in the organization.
The group’s dialogues with Jewish student groups even succeeded in recruiting another 10 members who considered themselves to be Zionists, according to Khalil.
With the start of the intifada  FIST began working toward starting a divestment campaign, similar to that of the anti-apartheid movement in the 1980s that occurred on college campuses nation-wide.
The group plans on lobbying the university’s administration in May to issue a statement that Sarah Lawrence supports the intifada and the Palestinian right to self-determination and condemns “Israeli” brutality.
Sarah Lawrence would then actively divest (as opposed to invest) in any company that has shares in “Israel”, as with the parallel anti-apartheid movement that managed to oust South Africa’s racist regime.
If the school doesn’t agree, FIST will occupy the university president’s home and start a hunger strike, says Khalil. Because of the level of awareness her group has created, she is expecting the majority of students on campus to participate.
“This year, we’ve become the most active group on campus,” she says. “Before people came to Sarah Lawrence, they didn’t know anything about Palestine. Now it’s become part of their everyday lives.”
With the start of the intifada, Khalil has taken FIST’s activism to a new level by focusing on a “complete bombardment of the anti-Arab and anti-Palestinian system,” she adds.
Every so often the group stands on tables in the campus cafeteria and tells diners about those who have died in the fighting, where and how, just in the time-span of their meal. Long lists of the victims are distributed at the same time.
Occasionally, these lists have also been represented through chalk outlines of corpses all over campus.
Khalil says they were especially successful in what she called “guerrilla theater.” The idea is to attract people’s attention by staging a fistfight in a public space and then breaking into an argument about stealing land and other policies of the Zionist state.
Khalil even succeeded in ensuring two hours of radio time per week and two pages in the campus’s daily newspaper dedicated to the intifada. They also started an underground bi-monthly “zine” (a small, informal magazine) that includes Palestinian poetry, political analysts’ articles, and student opinion pieces and has a circulation of about 3,000.
Their group has gained so much clout that they were able to set up two projection screens on the main campus green for a full day that displayed pictures of “Israeli” brutality.
“Everything is building up to our action in May,” she said. “It’s gotten so that FIST is a very big name on campus with two implications ­ either we’re a strong activist group fighting for a just cause, or we’re a fanatic anti-”Israeli” group.”
Student groups at 15 other universities, including Princeton University, New York University, Michigan University and Columbia University, plan on participating on their campuses on the same day.
Khalil is hoping to have another 60 universities, including some in the Arab world and Europe, join the movement. But even a handful, according to Khalil, will be enough to make the news and a statement that resistance to “Israel’s” apartheid policy does exist.
“I’m working 19 hours a day on this,” she says. “I’m completely fueled by the need to do this, because it’s still happening, and by seeing everyone else in the same struggle and giving so much to this cause.”

 

Jewish woman accused of aiding Arab bomber…(2)

” Less than a week after the arrest of Jewish contractor Jean Elraz on suspicion of murdering Kibbutz Menara security officer Yitzhak Kvartatz and selling the kibbutz's weapons to Palestinians, it has been revealed that a Jewish woman is suspected of aiding the Palestinian responsible for the Wadi Ara taxi bombing last month.
Angelica Yosepov, 19, of Holon, is suspected of being an accomplice to Zeid Kiliani, of Siris village near Jenin, in two other attempted bombings as well as the taxi blast on March 1, in which Tiberias resident Claude Knap was killed and nine others were wounded.
Police and the General Security Service (GSS) yesterday released details of the case and the identities of the two suspects.
It coincided with the decision of the Acre Magistrates Court to lift a publicity ban on the identities of Elraz and his alleged “Israeli” Arab accomplices in the murder of Menara security officer Kvartatz, 54, and the theft of over 50 M-16 assault rifles and ammunition from the kibbutz armory.
Elraz, 48, who had previously held sensitive positions in the security forces, is suspected shooting Kvartatz in the back of the head and of orchestrating the theft of the guns, which were later sold to Palestinians in the territories.
Police said Elraz and the Arab suspects, four of them members of the same family from Kafr Salem and another resident of the same village, had all confessed to their respective roles in the crime, which sent shock waves throughout the country.
Three other suspects were also arrested, two of whom are still being held pending further inquiries.
Police are reportedly seeking to establish whether Elraz was involved in other crimes, including the murder of a Lebanese collaborator and the theft of a heavy machine gun and other weapons.
The Arab suspects are believed to have been involved in the previous theft of guns and of selling them to Palestinians.
They had allegedly planned to carry out at least three other thefts of scores of weapons from armories of communities along the northern border.
Elraz's lawyer, Shmuel Saf, asked the court to send his client for a psychiatric examination into his allegedly erratic behavior. The police did not object. Elraz reportedly told the court that he had no knowledge of the crimes of which he is suspected and does not understand why he was arrested.
Elraz, a contractor from Kiryat Ata, has a rich background in security. He served as a captain in an elite “Israeli” Army intelligence unit and was also in the personal protection branch of the General Security Service, where he was a bodyguard to senior politicians, including then defense (war) minister Ariel Sharon.
He attended cabinet meetings and guarded other leading figures, including former prime minister Menachem Begin and former foreign minister David Levy.
His career in the security forces reportedly ended after he was convicted by a military court of smuggling drugs into “Israel” from Lebanon and related offences.
Elraz told Yediot Aharonot yesterday that he had killed 80 people as part of his service in the security forces. He apparently relayed his story to the newspaper through his lawyer.
He is divorced with three children, but was reportedly still living with his family at the time of the Menara murder and theft.
After leaving the security forces, he set up as a contractor and had been working in that capacity for several years. Last year, as a sub-contractor working on the construction of the security fence along the border with Lebanon, he had stayed a lot in the North, in Kiryat Shmona and Moshav Margaliyot.
Residents of northern communities who knew Elraz during his work on the border fence were as shocked as friends and colleagues over his arrest on suspicion of murder and selling weapons to Palestinians purely for financial gain.
In the case of Kiliani, the police and GSS yesterday said that he was involved in two attempted bombings in Tel Aviv, as well as the attempted murder of Col. (Res.) Ya'acov Ben-Dayan in the city's Carmel market in February.
He allegedly slashed Ben-Dayan's throat with a knife, seriously wounding him.
Kiliani had a criminal background and had been staying in “Israel” illegally. He reportedly met Yosepov a year ago, originally representing himself as a Druse who had served in the “Israeli” Army.
According to the allegations, the couple lived together in various places in the Tel Aviv region. Kiliani's brother was killed in clashes with the Army at the beginning of the intifada and he decided to take revenge with the help of Hamas.
After the attack on Ben-Dayan, Kiliani left “Israel”, but later contacted Yosepov. They met and it was at that point she allegedly learned that he was a terrorist planning to carry out attacks in “Israel”.
In February, she reportedly helped him plant bombs near a cafe in Rehov Allenby and the Dolphinarium beach.
Subsequently he boarded a taxi heading north. It is alleged that Yosepov was aware that he was carrying another bomb.
Kiliani allegedly detonated the device when the taxi was stopped at a police checkpoint - killing Knap and wounding nine people.
Kiliani himself was badly wounded and was taken to Haifa's Rambam Hospital, where he was arrested. His girlfriend was arrested the same day and reportedly confessed to aiding and abetting Kiliani and not preventing attacks.

 

Seventy-five injured as “Israel” launches helicopter attacks…(3)

“Israel” and the Palestinians tentatively agreed to resume limited contacts with the United States as chaperone, after “Israel” fired dozens of rockets at Palestinian military bases in the Gaza Strip and a Palestinian mortar shell critically injured an “Israeli” infant.

“Israeli” and Palestinian officials said they expected a three–way meeting to take place in the coming days, but that a time and place had not been set.

The decision came after a week of steady escalation, including “Israel’s” shelling of bases of Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat's security forces in the Gaza Strip late yesterday. Seventy rockets were fired at five areas in Gaza, injuring 77 people, including policemen, said Tayeb Abdel Rahim, a senior aide to Arafat.

In a seaside compound of Force 17, a Palestinian security service, rockets ripped through the metal roof of a large garage, burning a car. Other vehicles had been moved from the compound in anticipation of an “Israeli” attack, said Maj. Emad Mustafa of Force 17. The wall of an empty, two–story arsenal adjacent to Arafat's home was pierced by a missile. Arafat was in the West Bank town of Ramallah at the time of the attack.

The shelling began after 8pm (1800 GMT). At one point, seven helicopter gunships hovered above Gaza City. The rockets lit up the night sky, followed by loud booms.

Abdel Rahim, the Arafat aide, reiterated Palestinian claims that “Israel” was implementing a 100–day plan of gradual escalation. Gissin and other Sharon aides have denied there was such a plan.

Since the fighting began in September, 458 people have been killed, including 375 Palestinians, 64 “Israeli” Jews and 19 others.
