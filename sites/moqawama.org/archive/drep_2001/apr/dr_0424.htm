Hizbullah: no visits for captured “Israelis”…(1)

Hizbullah’s deputy secretary-general said Monday that the party had rejected the latest request by the International Committee of the Red Cross to visit “Israeli” military personnel captured by Hizbullah.
Sheikh Naim Qassem made the announcement after receiving the ICRC’s Middle East and North Africa operations head, Werner Kaspar.
“We have reiterated our request to visit the (“Israeli”) prisoners, but the position of Hizbullah has not changed,” Kaspar said after meeting Qassem, adding that the “discussions were very constructive and it is necessary to have these discussions from time to time.”
Hizbullah is refusing access to four “Israeli” prisoners captured on Oct. 7 in the Shibaa Farms. A week later, Hizbullah announced it had also detained a retired “Israeli” colonel working for “Israeli” intelligence.
Hizbullah has demanded that, in exchange for information on the the “Israeli” prisoners’ well-being, the ICRC be allowed to visit a Hizbullah official, Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, and the head of the Believers Resistance, Mustafa Dirani, who are being held in “Israeli” jails. Obeid was kidnapped by “Israeli” commandos in 1989 , while Dirani was captured in the same manner in 1994.
Asked whether there was any coordination with German mediators, Kaspar said that he “did not want to intervene or coordinate with other mediators.”
“We, as a humanitarian organization, have requests based on international law,” he asserted. “We take our own initiatives and offer our services. It’s up to decision-makers to accept our calls or not.”
Hizbullah has said on several occasions that the German government is also engaged in mediation efforts between the group and “Israel” to secure a prisoner swap deal.
The group has demanded the release of all 19 Lebanese prisoners still held in “Israeli” jails and all other Arab detainees in exchange for the release of the four “Israelis”.
It has also called on “Israel” to provide Beirut with maps of mines it planted in the South.

 

Arab ministers will appeal to stop attacks…(2)

A Foreign Ministry source said Monday that a delegation representing Arab foreign ministers would be formed this week in compliance with an Arab League recommendation.
The league convened Saturday in Cairo to discuss the “Israeli” attack on a Syrian radar position in Dahr al-Baidar.
The source said that the league’s secretary-general, Esmat Abdel-Meguid, was expected to contact Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and Egypt to talk to them in his capacity as chairman of the regular Arab League session, which has been devoted to discussing last week’s “Israeli” air raid.
According to the source, the delegation’s main task would be to contact US President George W. Bush, UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, and the European Union, to ask them to exert pressure on “Israel” to halt its attacks on both Lebanese and Syrian positions.

 

Islamic Republic of Iran to Host International Conference on  Palestinian Intifadah…(3)

The International Conference on the Palestinian Intifadah is to be held in Tehran Tuesday with the participation of  more than 30 officials from the Arab and Islamic world. Imam Sayyed Ali Khamenei and Hizbullah Secretary General Sayyed  Hassan Nasrallah are to give speeches during the conference, which  aims at bolstering up the Palestinian Intifada.

On the eve of the International Conference on the Palestinian Intifadah, non governmental organizations from various parts of the world met in Tehran to voice support for the Palestinian people's rights.

Iranian Parliament Speaker, Ayatollah Mehdi Karrubi, who opened the NGO conference, said it is the responsibility of the public opinion, including non-governmental organizations to defend human rights and condemn the ongoing “Israeli” aggressions against the Palestinian people.

He reminded the participants that Palestinian children are killed on daily basis by “Israeli” occupation forces, which is carrying out
an economic blockade on the Palestinians, preventing medicine and food from reaching them.

Karrubi hoped that the conference will establish the basis for coordination among the different organizations so that the right
steps be applied to protect Palestinian civilians.

Meanwhile, more officials were arriving at Tehran ahead of the International Conference of the Palestinian Intifadah. Lebanese
Speaker Nabih Berri said the holding of such a conference reflects the importance Iran gives to the Palestinian cause, hoping
that the conference will not be another occasion for more speeches but a time to respond firmly to the ongoing “Israeli” aggression.

For his part Karrubi said the conference should enlighten the world on what is going on in occupied Palestine, and called the
influential countries to exert pressure on the Zionist Entity. He added that the conference will call for the holding of a
referendum for all Palestinians to decide on the future of their state.

President of the Islamic Republic of Iran, Sayyed Mohammed Khatami maintained that “Israel” is the "biggest threat posed to the Islamic and Arab world. And Moslems should defend the rights of the Palestinians and mobilize their capabilities for the return of the Palestinian refugees and their rights of self-determination."

 

Palestinian Resistance Fighters Carry Out More Bomb Attacks Against the Occupation…(4)

The Palestinian Mujaheddeen executed a series of operations against the enemies posts and patrols the latest of which was in
occupied Qods. At least four Jewish settlers were injured when a bomb exploded in the afternoon southeast of Tel Aviv. The
enemy radio said the bomb was placed under a car parked in the town's market.

Early Monday, a bomb exploded in front of a bus carrying “Israeli” soldiers in the West Bank. The explosion occurred near the
Barkan Settlement on a main road between Jewish Settlements. The “Israeli” army sealed off the area after the operation.

Moreover, the “Israeli” occupation army said that Palestinians targeted an “Israeli” car while it was passing south of Nablus.

Mohammed Nazar Mohareb, 12-year-old was shot dead with an “Israeli” bullet in his head, while another 12 Palestinians were wounded when clashes between the Palestinians and “Israeli” enemy soldiers broke-out during a funeral in KhanYunis, south of Gaza Strip.

The funeral was for another Martyr, Madi Khalil Madi, who was wounded earlier last week during the “Israeli” shelling in Deir
al-Balah.

On the other hand,  Palestinian Mujaheddeen sabotaged Monday morning an “Israeli” attempt to break into a refugee camp near
Rafah in the Gaza Strip. The Mujaheddeen used machine guns and mortar shells to prevent 7 “Israeli” tanks and 2 bulldozers from
destroying several houses in the round-about of the camp. The fierce battle against the enemy lasted for 2 hours.

“Israeli” enemy mentioned that “Israeli” soldiers arrested overnight a Palestinian carrying a bomb primed to go off in the village of
Kfar Khalil in the West Bank, near Nablus.

Witnesses said that the occupation army stormed into the village of Nahalin near Beit Laham and arrested a Fatah official
Mosfah Aref Sbatin, 30. Two Palestinian youths were also arrested from their homes at around the same time.

Meanwhile Hamas claimed its responsibility for Sundays self-sacrifice bomb operation in Kfar Saba near the West Bank

Al-Qods daily, reported that Hamas circulated a statement including the Martyr's name, Omar Salim Abu Atieh, 22, from Hai Al-Shujaa in Gaza. An “Israeli” was killed and more than 50 wounded when Abu Atieh blew himself up at crowdy bus stop.

Another bomb went off Sunday in Haifa wounding an “Israeli” officer who was trying to defuse it..

 

“Israeli” U-turn raises doubts over 'Bulldozer'…(5)

Seven weeks after he took office, “Israelis” are wondering if Ariel Sharon ­ the fiercely hardline former general known by them as "Mr Bulldozer" because of his ruthless and decisive manner ­ is showing signs of uncertainty.

In his second major volte face within a week, the “Israeli” prime minister has switched course. This time he has indicated he may be willing to consider a previously rejected package of proposals for returning to negotiations with the Palestinians.

The turnaround ­ following swiftly after the “Israeli” army's bungled re-occupation of part of the Gaza Strip and its hasty departure less than a day later ­ concerns peace plans drawn up by Egypt and Jordan.

Until this week, aides to Mr Sharon, 73, had dismissed the proposals outright, describing them as a "non-starter" either as a means of ending the seven-month-old intifada or as laying the grounds for a resumption of peace talks.

They have changed their minds. Now they say that, although “Israel” continues to have "serious reservations" about the plans, the package has not been rejected outright.

Mr Sharon's predecessor, Ehud Barak, left office after 21 months under a barrage of right-wing derision over his sudden changes of tack. The habit earned him the nickname "Mr Zig-Zag". Whispers have begun to suggest that Mr Sharon is showing symptoms of the same affliction.

The apparent softening comes despite the fact that Mr Sharon has repeatedly said he will not negotiate "under fire". However, no reduction of violence, by either side, has been apparent since his landslide victory in early February. By late yesterday, three bombs had exploded inside “Israel” in just over 24 hours, including a suicide martyrdom device which killed two. “Israelis” also shot dead a Palestinian boy, 12, at a funeral in the Gaza Strip yesterday.

The proposals ­ backed by the Palestinians ­ include an end to the siege on the Occupied Territories, a withdrawal of “Israeli” military forces to the positions prior to the start of the intifada, and a freeze on all Jewish settlement building. In return, the Palestinians would end incitement to violence by their officially controlled media and renew security co-operation. They would also push for final-status talks to resume.

“Israel’s” Foreign Minister, Shimon Peres, is said to be looking over the proposals, and about to submit a list of reservations to Egypt and Jordan.
