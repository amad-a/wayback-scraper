 Thurs2ay 29-2000

 

Qassem pledges resistance will continue until Shibaa is freed…(1)

Hizbullah’s deputy secretary-general said Friday that the campaign to evict the “Israeli” Army from the Shibaa Farms would continue regardless of political considerations.
“We will strive to liberate the Shibaa Farms without taking into consideration the political situation or regional, local and international considerations,” Sheikh Naim Qassem said. “We will not succumb to political blackmail whether from the United States, “Israel” or the United Nations.”
But Qassem added that Hizbullah’s operations would not be the cause of a deterioration in the region, blaming instead the actions of the United States and “Israel”.
“The “Israeli”-American decision in the region can deteriorate or calm the situation,” he said, adding that everybody would support the resistance.
Qassem said that Lebanon’s economy was ailing because of its political positions and not because of the resistance.
He dismissed the United Nations-delineated Blue Line as merely a “creation” by the UN for “Israel’s” benefit.
This would allow “Israel” to keep the Shibaa Farms under its occupation to “pressure Lebanon to take what it (“Israel”) couldn’t take when it pulled out defeated,” said Qassem.
Meanwhile, Agriculture Minister Ali Abdullah said that the “Israeli” attack on the Syrian radar position in response to Hizbullah’s operation in the Shibaa Farms was a cycle which could not be imposed on the Lebanese.
“Resistance is the result of the occupation, it will continue until all the land is liberated ... the Lebanese and Syrian governments have asserted that the Shibaa Farms are Lebanese,” Abdullah said in Amal’s office in Hermel.
The minister said the
debate on the Syrian presence had stopped “after everybody was assured that such a presence was an insurance for Lebanon and was costing Syria a lot of martyrs.”
He said the issue should be discussed by the governments of both countries.
Also on Friday, Higher Shiite Council vice-president Abdel-Amir Qabalan urged the Lebanese to unify ranks and resort to dialogue to prevent “Israel” from taking advantage of their disagreements.
“Lebanese should not allow the “Israeli” enemy to profit from their disputes and debates about national or political issues,” said Qabalan during Friday prayers. “They should always resort to dialogue as the right way to reach an internal consensus.”
He said “Israel’s” attacks on Lebanon, Syria and Palestine would not achieve their goals and that the Jewish state would be unable to separate the Lebanese-Syrian track.
He also called on the Arabs to fully support Lebanon and Syria by boycotting commercial and diplomatic ties with “Israel” and pressuring the international community to force it to comply with Security Council resolutions.

 

Calls for restraint "useless": Assad…(2)

Syrian President Assad has told his US counterpart George W. Bush the peace process with “Israel” is now at an "intensive care" stage. Assad urged Bush to quote take a courageous stance and consider “Israel’s” attack on the  Syrian radar station an aggression. 

“ US President George W. Bush urged his Syrian counterpart Bashar al-Assad to show restraint amid rising Middle East tension, but Damascus said Assad told the US leader such calls were "useless."  Bush's call to Assad, placed as the US Assistant Secretary of State for Near Eastern Affairs Edward Walker visited Damascus, came after “Israel’s” raid on Monday on the Syrian radar station. The White House said Bush telephoned Assad to discuss the situation in the region, particularly in Lebanon, and "both agreed on the urgent need to restore calm to the area," as well as "the need for all parties to use restraint." But Syria's official news agency said Assad told Bush the "repeated calls for restraint have become useless," and quote "it is necessary to diagnose the problem to find effective solutions." Assad reserved the right to respond to the “Israeli” strike at the appropriate time and held “Israel” responsible for the consequences of its attack on the radar post. “Israel’s” Monday attack was the first on a Syrian target in Lebanon since 1982. “Israel” held Damascus responsible for the Hizbollah operation in the Shibaa Farms, which was occupied by “Israel” in 1967 and was not included in the Jewish state's pullout from southern Lebanon last May. Beirut and Damascus have rejected UN and “Israeli” claims that the Lebanese region belongs to Syria and can only be returned as part of a peace agreement. 

Beirut ‘disappointed’ by Security Council’s treatment of air strike…(3)

A government source said Friday that Lebanon was disappointed with the weak statement from the UN Security Council regarding the “Israeli” air raid on Dahr al-Baidar.
The source explained that officials were upset at the Security Council’s statement treating “the aggressor” (“Israel”) and “the victim” (Lebanon) alike.
“While the resistance operation took place in Shibaa, a Lebanese land occupied by “Israel”, the “Israeli” air raids targeted an area deep within Lebanon proper,” the source said. “Moreover, the Security Council’s statement omitted any allusion to the fact that the “Israeli” air raid breached Lebanon’s sovereignty and the Jewish state used the resistance operation as an excuse to attack the radar positions in Dahr al-Baidar.”
The source recalled that UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan was upset at the “escalatory “Israeli” response” in targeting the Syrian radar position.

UN protests “Israeli” violations of Lebanese airspace…(4)

“ The UN representative in southern Lebanon called Friday for “Israel” to stop violating Lebanese airspace, as “Israeli” warplanes broke the sound barrier over the country for the fifth consecutive day.

Staffan de Mistura said the overflights Friday were the 342nd time the “Israelis” had violated its northern neighbor's airspace since August.

He said the flights violated the UN "Blue Line" which separates Lebanon and “Israel” and said they "are particularly unhelpful at this time of tension in the region."

"Such actions cause unfair concern among the population and should be stopped," De Mistura said in a statement.

Lebanese Foreign Minister Mahmud Hammud praised De Mistura's statement, calling it "the first time the United Nations has clearly noted the “Israeli” violations and aggressions."

Hammud told AFP he hoped the violations would figure into UN Secretary General Kofi Annan's report on the Lebanese-”Israeli” border situation due out at the end of the month.

The UN envoy reported two sonic booms over Beirut on Friday, while security sources said “Israeli” planes broke the sound barrier throughout the country, including over southern Lebanon and the main northern town of Tripoli.

 

“Israeli” troops fire across border…(5)

“Israeli” soldiers fired machine guns on Friday at a group of stone-throwers near the Fatima Gate in Kfar Kila without causing any casualties, witnesses said.
They said that one of four “Israeli” soldiers positioned in an observation post near the gate started firing at nine young men who were throwing stones and a handful of workers carrying out reinforcements near the border.
Members of Hizbullah who had been watching the site instructed the group of men to leave the site, which they did immediately after the shooting.
Earlier Friday, “Israeli” workers reinforced the border from Hamames Hill south of Khiam to the “Israeli” settlement of Misgav Am on a hill above Addaisseh. A Hizbullah member who refused to be identified said “Israelis” have been carrying out reinforcement works for two days.
He said “Israelis” workers always started their work at nightfall and went on until sunrise, to avoid the stone-throwers.

 

Hamas supporters in Lebanon stage sit-in at UNIFIL headquarters…(6)

“ Three hundred Palestinian supporters of the Islamic resistance movement Hamas staged an anti-”Israeli” sit-in Friday outside the information office of the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL) in the southern Lebanese city of Tyre, an AFP correspondent witnessed.

The demonstrators waved banners calling on UN Secretary General Kofi Annan "to act to protect the Palestinian people from the daily crimes carried out by the government of (“Israeli” Prime Minister) Ariel Sharon."

One of the Hamas leaders in Lebanon, Abu Bilal, said his movement "had changed the rules of the game, going from stone throwing to mortar fire on Zionist settlements, and suicide attacks."

He called for the fight to go on, and for "a refusal of any negotiation."

The demonstrators burned “Israeli” and US flags before dispersing.

 

“Israel’s” neighbors say raids will spark greater violence…(7)

“ Thousands of Syrians turned their independence day Tuesday into a mass protest of “Israeli” airstrikes that killed three Syrian soldiers posted in the Lebanese mountains, and Hizbullah guerrillas in Lebanon promised to retaliate.

Hizbullah's deputy leader, Sheik Naim Kassem, vowed to retaliate against “Israel” at an "appropriate time and manner . . . contrary to “Israeli” expectations."

Speaking in Beirut, Kassem said Hizbullah will continue to fight “Israeli” troops in the Shibaa Farms until “Israel” vacates the area, where Lebanon and the “Israeli”-occupied Golan Heights meet.

“Israel” said the raid Monday -- its first deep inside Lebanon since its forces withdrew from southern Lebanon in May 2000 and the first on a Syrian target since 1996 -- was a signal to Syria that “Israel” would not tolerate Hizbullah attacks.

Syria and Iran back Hizbullah, “Israel’s” toughest opponent during its 18-year occupation of Lebanon.

Syria has not indicated how it might respond, though Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa vowed Monday during a visit to Moscow that “Israel” "will pay a heavy price . . . at the convenient and appropriate time."

Thousands of Syrians rallied Tuesday on the Syrian side of the Golan Heights, demanding “Israel” return the rest of the plateau it has held since the 1967 Middle East war. "The [“Israeli”] aggression will not frighten us!" they chanted.

The Syrian government had lifted its normal restrictions on travel to the Golan Heights because of a holiday celebrating the 55th anniversary of Syria's independence from France.

At a rally in Damascus, Suliman Qaddah, a regional official of the ruling Baath Party, said “Israel” alone "bears responsibility for this aggression and its adverse consequences."

In an editorial Tuesday, the official Syrian newspaper Tishrin said the strike heralded an era of "provocation, extremism, and racism led by the terrorist [“Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel] Sharon."

Iran's official Islamic Republic News Agency said Sharaa told Iranian Foreign Minister Kamal Kharrazi in a phone call Tuesday that “Israel” was seeking a pretext for a confrontation.

On the streets of Damascus, Syrians expressed concern about the possible consequences of such a confrontation. Salim al-Ahmad, a 45-year-old merchant, called the airstrike a "provocation . . . and an attempt to drag the region into a new war."

Meanwhile, Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak told reporters in the Egyptian resort of Sharm el-Sheik that the strike on the Syrian army reminded him of the climate during “Israel’s” 1982 invasion of Lebanon, when Sharon was defense minister.

"If “Israel” thinks that it can stop violence by this policy, I am saying that it won't and, to the contrary, it will backfire and violence will increase everywhere," Mubarak said after meeting with Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat.

"I urge Sharon to be realistic because the policy of strikes is useless and . . . all the people of the region, including the “Israeli” people, will suffer more."

 

Ain al-Hilweh urges ICRC to action…(8)

Palestinian popular committees at Ain al-Hilweh in Sidon urged the International Committee of the Red Cross to pressure “Israel” to stop its “massacre” of the Palestinian people Friday.
Abed Maqdah, the committees’ secretary, called on the head of the ICRC in Lebanon, Henri Fournier, who visited the camp Friday, to make every effort to send international observers to protect the Palestinian people and release Palestinian, Lebanese, and other Arab detainees in “Israeli” prisons.
Fournier said that the purpose of his visit was to inform the committees about the ICRC’s activities in the Occupied Territories and the problems Palestinian refugees face in Sidon’s camps.
He also said the ICRC maintained a constant presence in the Occupied Territories through its centers in Gaza, Al-Quds, and most Palestinian towns, adding that the committee had established a logistics center in Amman, Jordan where food and medical supplies were gathered and sent to Palestine.
Fournier said the ICRC’s “main partner” was the Palestinian Red Crescent. He also distinguished between the ICRC and UNRWA’s activities, saying that the latter facilitated the dispatch of Red Crescent ambulances to wounded people and their treatment “through necessary contacts.”
Fournier said the work of the ICRC and Red Crescent faced “certain obstacles” due to the tense situation in the Occupied Territories.”

 

Palestinians launched 98 mortar shells on Occupation Targets for the Last Four Months, 75 of Which in the Past Two Weeks…(9)

A number of Palestinian stone throwers have attacked “Israeli” forces that have barged into the area surrounding the Quds(Al-Quds) holy shrine, after the Friday prayer. Two of the “Israeli” forces were injured.

Meanwhile, “Israeli” explosive experts dismantled a bomb at the borderline between Gaza Strip and the occupied territories.

In the besieged Gaza Strip, residents have complained Friday that little has changed since “Israeli” enemy ordered an easing of internal restrictions imposed earlier this week, after a heavy assault that saw “Israeli” troops occupy a chunk of Palestinian ruled land for 24 hours, triggered heavy international condemnation.

Witnesses said the tanks still stationed on the main highway. Concrete blocks remained positioned on the road, cutting the wide highway down to just one lane of traffic, while dozens of vehicles waited in line to pass through the checkpoint.  “Israeli” tanks stationed at the Gush Katif settlement bloc had been blocking the road linking Rafah with Khan Yunis and closed major highways linking the south and north of the Gaza Strip. Palestinian civilians suffer daily while trying to go home. They might spend days in the Gaza strip, where they work, before they are allowed to leave.

Top Palestinian minister Saeb Eraekat complained that "“Israel’s”" order to ease the restrictions were a “public relations stunt” and
that the territory remained cut in three parts. Ereakat demanded the roads to be opened completely and not selectively.

In the meantime, Palestinian Chairman Yasser Arafat ordered to end mortar attacks against Jewish settlements. The Zionist Entity gave a cautious welcome to Arafat’s order, but said it wanted first to see his instructions taking effect on the ground.

Amid this verbal conflict, Palestinian mujahedeen opened fire friday at the Jewish zone of Al-Khalil. At the same time there was an exchange of fire between the Palestinian resistance fighters and the occupation forces.

Hamas, for its part, vowed to continue resisting occupation of Palestinian land by all available means.

The latest statistics showed that palestinians have launched 98 mortar shells for the last four months, 75 of which were launched during the past two weeks.

On the ground Thursday, a powerful explosion rocked an old building being renovated to serve as a local base for Force 17,
Arafat’s elite security force, in the West Bank city of Ramallah. In the Gaza Strip, two Palestinian teenagers were shot by
“Israeli” troops, one of them seriously, in separate incidents.

 

“Israeli” troops open fire on journalists in occupied territories; “Israeli” police storm al-haram al-sharif…(10)

“Israeli” soldiers have shot and wounded five Palestinians including a television journalist in the occupied territories. Four civilians were injured in the West Bank city of Ramallah. A Palestinian journalist working for Abu Dhabi television came under “Israeli” fire as she was covering damage caused by recent “Israeli” raids on the southern Gaza Strip. “Israeli” troops had shot at another journalist working for Palestinian television late on Thursday. “Israeli” police armed with assault rifles also mounted a show of force at Al-Quds's most sensitive holy place, al-Haram al-Sharif, where the current Palestinian uprising began. “Israeli” police stormed the site, Islam's third holiest site. A police spokesman claimed “Israel” was only responding to Palestinian stone-throwing. 

“ “Israel” claimed it lifted an internal blockade it imposed on the occupied Gaza Strip earlier this week after “Israeli” troops re-occupied parts of the peninsula for 24 hours, action that triggered swift international condemnation. Witnesses said the army removed roadblocks but tanks remained positioned at checkpoints and traffic was only trickling through. Senior Palestinian negotiator Saeb Erakat, touring the West Bank town of Jericho which sustained heavy destruction due to “Israeli” shelling, said “Israel’s” declaration was only a "public relations stunt" because the Gaza Strip continued to be divided into three chunks. Erakat unveiled that “Israel” has turned down proposals to resume contacts to end fighting and resume peace talks. Top UN Mideast envoy Terje-Roed Larsen, who along with European Union envoy Miguel Moratinos held talks with Palestinian President Yasser Arafat in the West Bank city of Ramallah, said “Israel’s” slight easing of the siege helped but denounced “Israeli” restrictions on UN personnel. The world body complained to “Israel” after troops blocked movement of a UN convoy led by UNRWA chief Peter Hansen in the occupied Gaza Strip. Larsen said the restrictions are in violation of international obligations. “Israel’s” easing of the blockade followed an order by Arafat to end mortar attacks on “Israeli” targets and Jewish settlements. “Israel” cautiously welcomed Arafat's instructions but said it is waiting to see if they are effective. The hardline Hamas movement vowed it would continue to resist “Israel’s” occupation by "all means available." “Israel’s” Haaretz newspaper said “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon has rejected a proposal by Arafat for the two to come out on television and call for a halt to violence and a resumption of peace talks. “Israeli” troops shot two Palestinian teenagers in Gaza, injuring one of them seriously on Thursday night. Calls are mounting from leftist “Israeli” politicians for peace contacts to resume with the Palestinians, warning Sharon that his hardline position of rejecting any political dialogue before the uprising ends will not resolve the conflict. The international community has also been denouncing Sharon's policies. France's Foreign Minister Hubert Vedrine hailed what he called the "first small steps" towards peace after “Israel” partially eased the blockade on Gaza but stressed “Israel’s” siege of the territories and the construction of Jewish settlements are stumbling blocks to any peace efforts.  Moscow for its part said Mideast peace making has been set back by a decade and urged the international community to solve the crisis. 

 

Anger at “Israeli” plans to expand homes in Old City…(11)

“Israeli” peace campaigners and Palestinian officials angrily condemned a building plan yesterday, drawn up by an “Israeli” government company to boost the Jewish presence in Al-Quds's tinderbox Old City.

The scheme is seen by critics of Ariel Sharon ­ a keen supporter in the past of building in the occupied territories ­ as more evidence that his hardline rule is fuelling a Jewish settlement drive, undermining possible future peace negotiations.

The Palestinians are demanding a freeze on all settlement construction as a condition for returning to talks after seven months of uprising in which nearly 500 people have been killed.

The Old City, with its shrine the Haram al-Sharif/Temple Mount, is one of the most volatile and sensitive patches of land in the world. Mr Sharon's visit to the mount before he became Prime Minister provided the spark that detonated the long-brewing Palestinian intifada last September.

Yesterday there was fresh tension in the Old City after “Israeli” armed police stormed the mount to quell stone-throwing by Palestinian youths after Friday prayers at the al-Aqsa mosque. No one was hurt.

The building project was drawn up by the Jewish Quarter Development Company, run by the “Israeli” government, in the hope of connecting Jewish west Al-Quds with the 3,000 “Israelis” who live inside the Old City's Jewish quarter and also with the Western (Wailing) Wall, Judaism's holiest spot.

The Old City was occupied by “Israel” in the 1967 war along with all east Al-Quds and later annexed ­ a move that has never been recognised by the international community.

The $36m project includes a tunnel to the Western Wall plaza, and hundreds of houses outside the Old City's southern walls ­ part of which would stretch into Silwan, a Palestinian neighbourhood.

The scheme ­ which has yet to win approval from the “Israeli” government ­ has emerged at a time of growing international concern about a general surge in settlement activity under the Sharon government, despite thousands of settler homes being empty.

It will deepen Palestinian suspicions that, as the conflict drags on with no sign of resolution, “Israel” is steadily strengthening its grip on Palestinian land by "creating facts on the ground" in defiance of international law.

“Israel” drew criticism from the United States and European nations recently after plans were revealed for 8,000 new homes ­ including a new settlement south of Al-Quds.

Ziyad Abu Ziyad, a Palestinian minister, criticised "elements in the “Israeli” establishment who are trying to undermine any future possibility of peace in the region".

"This is directed against a future compromise. The “Israeli” right wing is leading and dictating their positions to their government. We will oppose it by all possible means."

A Palestinian television journalist was wounded in the Rafah refugee camp in Gaza yesterday, in what she said was an unprovoked shooting by “Israeli” troops. The army says it is investigating the incident.

“Israeli” enemy Foreign Minister Shimon Peres met today in Tel Aviv the United Nations Middle East envoy Terje Roed- Larsen and UNRWA’s General Commissioner Peter Hansen.

Sharon's tough actions follow familiar pattern…(13)

Daring and creative. Ruthless and unpredictable.

All these adjectives have been applied to Ariel Sharon by friends and enemies alike in his past incarnations in “Israel’s” political and military life. Now, they say, the same qualities are coming to the forefront in his six weeks in the prime minister's job and help to explain the intensifying conflict in the region.

The “Israeli” army's incursions into the Gaza strip in recent weeks, the bulldozing of Palestinian houses and orchards, extrajudicial killing of suspected terrorists, the bombing of a Syrian radar station in Lebanon -- all these moves strike many “Israelis” as vintage Sharon.

``He is 73 years old, and at this age nobody can change his character,'' says a former “Israeli” official who knows him well. ``As long as I know him, he didn't believe in peace with the Arabs, not from now to eternity.''

Galia Golan, a prominent “Israeli” peace activist, said she was reminded this past week of “Israel’s” invasion of Lebanon in 1982. Sharon was defense minister at the time and is widely considered the architect of the unpopular war that ensued from that invasion.

What struck Golan was the bombing of the Syrian targets early Monday morning in retaliation for an attack by Hizbullah, an Islamic guerrilla movement Sharon's predecessors had frequently bombed Hizbullah targets, but refrained from hitting Syrian interests for fear of dragging Syria into the conflict.

``This is the same old Sharon,'' Golan said. ``He is pulling out all the same old tricks. It is Sharon's way of doing things to escalate not just to the next level but to go 10 steps down the road.'' Yet it is this tough, stubborn side of Sharon -- his fearsome reputation for fighting Arabs -- that attracts so many “Israelis” and allowed him to win election in a landslide by an “Israeli” public weary of Palestinian uprising.

So far, most of Sharon's tactics are the same as those of his predecessor, Ehud Barak.

The assassinations of suspected terrorists, the use of helicopter gunships against Palestinian security forces, the road closures -- all were all used by Barak with little success in trying to quash the Palestinian uprising that began in late September.

The biggest difference is that Barak did not send the army into territory under Palestinian sovereignty, for fear of jeopardizing the peace process.

Some “Israelis” say they believe Sharon has exercised more restraint than they would have imagined.

``It is a surprise. He is proving to be far more responsible and careful than you would imagine from his public image,'' said Shlomo Gazit, a former “Israeli” intelligence chief who went to high school with Sharon. 41

Last week, “Israeli” tanks and bulldozers stormed into Gaza's Khan Yunis refugee camp in the middle of the night and flattened more than two dozen houses from which Palestinians were shooting mortar shells at Jewish settlements. And Tuesday, the army began razing orchards in an attempt to clear a half-mile deep buffer zone inside Gaza to prevent Palestinians from shelling “Israeli” towns just outside.

The army quickly pulled out after U.S. Secretary of State Colin Powell criticized the moves as ``excessive and disproportionate.''

Another technique that Sharon is rolling out from the 1970s entails dividing up Palestinian areas into small sectors, clamping down hard on sites known as focal points of violence, and rewarding the population where calm exists. The “Israeli” army this week carved Gaza into three parts, through which it is virtually impossible for Palestinians to pass.

Another former “Israeli” security official said he is surprised that Sharon has not tried to scrap the peace process altogether. He credits the influence of Sharon's 36-year-old son, Omri Sharon, a supporter of the peace process who has held secret meetings with Palestinian leaders throughout the last month.

In a series of interviews with the “Israeli” press last weekend, Sharon offered the most detailed explanation so far of his thinking since becoming prime minister. As in the past, he expressed doubt that there could be anything better than a truce with the Palestinians. ``I don't believe it that it is possible to resolve a conflict that has lasted 120 years in one jump. And I don't think we have to put forward such a pretentious goal of signing peace immediately,'' said Sharon.

``My whole life has passed in this conflict.'' As for himself, Sharon said, ``There is nothing new. I have always been the way I am . . . I have not changed one iota. There is no new Sharon.''
