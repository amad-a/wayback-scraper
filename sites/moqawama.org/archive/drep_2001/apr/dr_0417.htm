Lebanese Hizbullah vows to hit back at "“Israeli” enemy"…(1)

“Hizbullah said Monday that it was personally targeted by the “Israeli” raid on a Syrian military position in Lebanon and vowed to hit back at the "“Israeli” enemy" at the right moment.

"We are going to cut off the hand of the enemy who dared to attack our land and our brothers", the movement said in a statement, denouncing hardline “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon.

"We will turn the Sharonian dream of terrorizing our people into a nightmare for the routed army of the Zionist occupation forces", said Hizbullah, which spearheaded resistance against the 22-year “Israeli” occupation of south Lebanon, which ended last May.

"The Islamic resistance (Hizbullah's armed branch) knows where, when and how to deliver a thunderous blow to the enemy and how to hurt him", the statement added.

At least one Syrian soldier and five others were wounded during the “Israeli” air raid on the Syrian military position east of Beirut early Monday morning.

 

Lahoud Extends Condolences to Assad, Denounces Sharon's 'Blood-Thirst'…(2)

President Lahoud called his Syrian counterpart Bashar Assad Monday and extended Lebanon's condolences over the death of Syrian soldiers in “Israel’s” pre-dawn raid on a Syrian radio position east of Beirut.
"The Syrian martyrs have joined the long list of the martyrs of the Lebanese army and the resistance," Lahoud said, according to a statement released by the Baabda palace.
The statement said Lahoud labeled the raid as a "manifestation of the bloody policies Ariel Sharon has followed since he came to power against the Palestinians and the Lebanese."
The President said these policies could lead to an all-out confrontation in the Middle East and asserted that “Israel” would take full responsibility for the consequences of its spiraling belligerency.
Monday's raid on the Syrian radar station in Mdeirej would not stop Lebanon from pursuing by all means the full liberation of the Shabaa Farms and the release of all Lebanese prisoners in “Israeli” jails.
Informed sources said Lahoud and Assad "have reaffirmed the resolve of Lebanon and Syria to persist in confronting “Israel”, without submitting to its conditions and pressures until a comprehensive Middle East peace is accomplished and all occupied Arab territories are restored."
Lahoud later held crisis talks with Premier Hariri over the ramifications of the first direct “Israeli” air strike on a Syrian military target in Lebanon since the 1982 invasion. No statement was issued after the meeting.
Hariri, who has earlier denounced the raid as a "grave aggression," is due to leave for a visit to Qatar Monday and then to Saudi Arabia Tuesday.

Lebanon holds “Israel” responsable for consequences of raids in letter to UN…(3)

“ Lebanon said Monday in a letter to the United Nations that the “Israeli” raids on Syrian targets on its territory were "an attack on its sovereignty" and that it held “Israel” "responsible for the dangerous consequences of this aggression".

In a letter addressed to UN Secretary General Kofi Annan and made public in Beirut, Lebanon said "this “Israeli” aggression is a blatant violation of Lebanon's sovereignty and an attack on the integrity of its territory".

The letter said Lebanon holds "“Israel” responsible for the dangerous consequences of this aggression" and adds that the policies of “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon's government "do not serve the fair and comprehensive peace which the region needs".

But the letter also said "this new aggression will not stop it from claiming its rights and from pursuing legitimate measures to recover the Shibaa Farms".

"These farms are not, as “Israel” itself admits, “Israeli” territory and “Israel’s” presence there is therefore illegitimate and amounts to an occupation", the letter went on.

 

Syria: "“Israel” Will Pay Heavy Price at Proper Moment"…(4)

Syria has pledged to exact a heavy price from “Israel” "at the the proper time" for its air assault that levelled a Syrian army radar position east of Beirut, calling the attack a "dengerous escalation" in the Middle East.
This stance was outlined by Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk Al Sharaa when he arrived late night Monday in Moscow for talks with Russian officials about spiralling Middle Eastern tensions in the wake of the radar-devastating air raid and the worsening hostilities in the Palestinian territories.
"“Israel” will pay a heavy price at the right time for its attack on a Syrian military position in Lebanon," Sharaa told reporters as he climbed down from the plane at Moscow airport. "The attack is a violation of international laws. It has creating a dangerous excalation that widens the scope of violence in the Middle East."
"Sharon's “Israel” is killing the peace process exactly as it is killing Palestinian children in Gaza and the West Bank," Sharaa concluded.
In Damascus, a high-ranked Syrian official was quoted by the official Syrian news agency SANA as saying the attack on the Syria radar site was a "dangerous escalation bound to shake the security and stability of the region."
In the first official Syrian version about the attack, the unnamed official was quoted as saying the raid was a "manifestation of the aggressive policies of the “Israeli” government under Ariel Sharon."
He said the attack was in line with "the barbaric and racist crimes against the Palestinian people in the occupied territories."
The official gave this version of the air assault:
"Formations of “Israeli” warplanes violated Lebanon's airspace 20 minutes after midnight (early Monday) and rocketed one of our positions in the Lebanese territory. Our air defense systems intercepted the raiding jets and forced them to retreat.
One of the Syrian radar station was hit and one of our elements was martyred and four others were wounded."
Earlier, several Arab television stations quoted an anonymous senior Syrian official as saying, "Syria reserves its right to self-defense." But the SANA report made no reference to the self-defense notion.

 

“Israeli” violation of blue line with Lebanon "serious": UN…(5)

“ “Israel’s” raid Monday on a Syrian target in Lebanon was a "serious" violation of the UN "blue line" dividing the two countries, the UN representative in southern Lebanon said.

"What happened overnight is serious," Staffan De Mistura said after a meeting with Lebanese Foreign Minister Mahmud Hammud.

"It's once more a violation of the blue line, which we are trying to protect because it remains, despite all, a guarantee for everyone," he said.

The blue line marks the line of “Israel’s” retreat from southern Lebanon last May, ending 22 years of occupation.

"All this pushes toward an escalation and we judge that dangerous. We insist on the fact that it's the moment, in the Middle Eastern context and the Lebanese situation in general, to continue to control the situation and not proceed toward an escalation," he said.

 

Egypt condemns “Israeli” raid on Lebanon…(6)

“ Egyptian Foreign Minister Amr Mussa on Monday condemned “Israel’s” air strike on Syrian positions in Lebanon and accused the Jewish state of creating "turmoil" in the region.

"We strongly condemn this aggression ... against a sovereign state," said Egyptian Foreign Minister Amr Mussa during a visit to the United Arab Emirates.

"This aggression robs the “Israeli” withdrawal from Lebanon of its substance ... and amounts to a confirmation of the occupation of the Shibaa Farms," he said, referring to “Israel’s” withdrawal from south Lebanon last May after 22 years of occupation and the continuing dispute over the Shibaa Farms region on the border between Lebanon and “Israel”.

Mussa, in a statement to the Emirati daily Al-Ittihad to be published Tuesday, a copy of which was obtained by AFP, accused “Israel” of stirring "turmoil amongst the Arab peoples of Palestine, Lebanon and Syria".

He pledged Egypt's "total solidarity" with the Lebanese people and said “Israel” had no right to interfere in the issue of Syria's military presence in Lebanon, something which concerned only Beirut and Damascus.

Arab League says “Israel” trying to "set region ablaze"…(7)

“ “Israel” has chosen "the path of bloodshed" by launching a deadly attack on a Syrian position in Lebanon Monday, the Arab League said, calling on the international community to prevent the Jewish state from "setting the region ablaze."

"This aggression constitutes a dangerous escalation in “Israeli” policy and shows the path of bloodshed, which destroys the peace process, that has been adopted by “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon," the 22-member Arab League said in a statement.

The League called on the international community to take action against "“Israeli” attempts to set the region ablaze and force it into a circle of violence and instability."

"Sharon is an advocate of escalation who will lead the region to the edge of the precipice," the League said, saying the situation "threatens not only the security and stability of the region, but also the interests of all countries of the world."

It said the raid will boost Lebanon's will to "defend its legitimate rights and to regain its sovereignty over its territories with the withdrawal of “Israeli” forces from the Shibaa Farms," the “Israeli”-held area where Hizbullah attacked.

"“Israel” will never succeed in materializing its illusions of separating the Syrian and Lebanese fronts," it added.

 

Palestinians denounce “Israeli” raid on Lebanon as "dangerous escalation"…(8)

“ The Palestinian Authority on Monday condemned “Israel’s” deadly bombardment of a Syrian military target in Lebanon as a dangerous escalation that risked provoking a regional conflict, saying it was appealing to the US, Europe and the UN to intervene quickly.

Palestinian leader Yasser Arafat slammed the “Israeli” raids on a Syrian radar station east of Beirut, which left at least one soldier dead and five wounded.

"The “Israeli” aggression against the Syrian radar in Lebanon represents a serious violation which puts the region in an extremely dangerous and critical situation," Arafat told the press on his arrival in Amman for a short visit for talks with King Abdullah II.

Separately, Arafat's top advisor Nabil Abu Rudeina branded the raids a threat to regional stability and demanded international intervention.

"The Palestinian Authority ... calls on United States and the international community to act to halt the “Israeli” aggressions against the Palestinians, the Syrians and the Lebanese," he said.

Also, in a statement carried by the official WAFA news agency, the Palestinian Authority said the “Israeli” raids "dangerously threatened security in the region" and affirmed "its solidarity with the Syrian and Lebanese brother peoples faced with “Israeli” aggression."

"The Authority calls on Arab countries to unite and calls on the (UN) Security Council to condemn this dangerous attack and force “Israel” to stop its aggression against the Palestinian people and against Syria and Lebanon," the statement said.

 

Jordan fm, on rare visit to “Israel”, calls Lebanon raid “unjustified escalation”…(9)

Jordan’s Foreign Minister Abdel Ilah al-Khatib, in “Israel” for talks with his “Israeli” counterpart Shimon Peres, said the Jewish state’s attack in Lebanon was an unjustified escalation that could threaten regional stability and security.

Khatib, speaking at a joint press conference with Peres following three hours of talks, said “Israel’s” overnight bombardment in Lebanon against a Syrian position could affect the overall stability and security in the region. Khatib, the first high-ranking Arab official to visit “Israel” since “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon took office in March, said the aggression made his mission more difficult, referring to efforts by Jordan to try and quell the deadly intifada or uprising raging across the occupied West Bank and Gaza Strip. Jordan’s Foreign Minister said what “Israel” did overnight east of Beirut was unacceptable and unnecessary. Khatib had said in Amman he would “firmly and directly” denounce “Israel’s” raids in Lebanon overnight that left at least one Syrian soldier killed. Khatib is also the first Arab official to visit the Jewish state since the outbreak of the intifada or uprising in late September. An activist in Palestinian President Yasser Arafat’s Fatah movement was shot and injured Monday by an “Israeli” sniper in the divided West Bank city of Hebron. In a move likely to increase tensions “Israel” said it will not ease the crippling siege on Palestinian areas. A hardline Palestinian group has claimed responsibility for two pipe bombs, which exploded Sunday in “Israel”, leaving one “Israeli” injured. A little known group called the al-Aqsa Martyrs claimed responsibility for the blasts in the “Israeli” city of Kfar Saba, not far from the Green Line separating the West Bank from “Israel”. In a statement faxed to Beirut, the group said it carried out the attack to “salute” Lebanon’s Hizbollah resistance movement and it retaliation for “Israeli” attacks on Palestinian areas and southern Lebanon.

“Israel” violates Lebanese airspace…(10)

““Israeli” jets staged mock raids over Lebanon on Monday, hours after the Jewish state carried out a deadly attack on a Syrian radar position. “Israeli” jets flew low over the Bekaa Valley, southern Lebanon and Beirut, breaking the sound barrier amid high tensions in the area. In a related security development, an Egypt Air flight from Cairo to Beirut turned back early Monday after Lebanese aviation authorities advised the pilot not to land during the “Israeli” bombardment. Authorities at Beirut International Airport told the pilot of the plane, carrying 116 passengers, not to land. It headed into Cypriot airspace before returning to Cairo.

“Israelis” seize Palestinian areas and attack security installations…(11)

“Israeli” troops seized Palestinian–controlled areas in the Gaza Strip early today – a first in seven months of fighting – and rocketed Palestinian security installations in retaliation for mortar fire on an “Israeli” town.

One Palestinian was killed and 36 hurt in the attack from the ground, air and sea – one of the most extensive “Israeli” military operations since the fall.

“Israeli” troops also divided Gaza into three parts, barring north–south traffic in the crowded strip of more one million Palestinians.

The attack came in response to the firing of five mortar shells from Gaza at the “Israeli” Negev Desert down of Sderot, about 2.5 miles from the Gaza Strip and about five miles from a sheep farm owned by “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon.

The Palestinians fired dozens of mortars on “Israeli” targets in recent weeks, mainly on Jewish settlements in Gaza. The attack on Sderot caused no damage or injuries, but was the first on an “Israeli” town and considered a major provocation by the Sharon government which has promised to restore a sense of security to “Israelis”.

A top Palestinian security official, Brig Gen Abdel Razek Majaidah, denied “Israeli” accusations that the Palestinian Authority was involved in the attacks.

In response to the “Israeli” strike, Palestinian security officials canceled participation in US–sponsored security talks that were to have taken place late yesterday.

Army bulldozers also levelled farmland near the northern town of Beit Hanoun.

In the past few months, “Israeli” troops briefly entered Palestinian–controlled areas from time to time, including last week when army bulldozers razed part of a Gaza refugee camp. However, today's operation marked the first time soldiers recaptured land with the intention of remaining there for some time.

Palestinian officials have said considered a recapture of Palestinian–controlled areas a major provocation. “Israeli” troops withdrew from two–thirds of the Gaza Strip in 1994, as part of interim peace accords.

As part of the “Israeli” strike, tanks and helicopter gunships also shelled 10 buildings across the Gaza Strip, including eight security installations, among them the Gaza City headquarters of Palestinian police chief Brig Gen Ghazi Jabali.

Rockets punched holes in the facade and ceiling of Jabali's compound, and shattered windows in the third–floor conference room. Families in adjacent buildings fled their homes.

Rockets also hit buildings in the towns of Deir el Balah, Rafah and Beit Hanoun. In Hanoun, the body of a Palestinian policeman was discovered in the rubble this morning. Palestinian doctors said 36 Palestinians were hurt, most by shrapnel.

Since fighting erupted in late September, 472 people have been killed, including 389 Palestinians, 64 “Israeli” Jews and 19 others.
