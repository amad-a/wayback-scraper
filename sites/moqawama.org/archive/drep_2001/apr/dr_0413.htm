Cabinet to plan Liberation celebrations…(1)

The Cabinet took a series of decisions in its weekly meeting Thursday, including the creation of a special committee to plan a program for May 25 celebrations marking Liberation Day.
The committee, headed by Deputy Premier Issam Fares, includes the ministers of defense, education, sports and youth, information, culture, and interior and municipalities.
The Cabinet also called on the special committee established to review cases of prisoners and detainees liberated from “Israeli” prisons to submit its proposals before May 25.

”Israeli” troops fire in the air to ward off students…(2)

About 150 secondary school students from North Lebanon demonstrated at Fatima Gate on Thursday, throwing stones and lightly injuring one “Israeli” soldier.
“Israeli” troops fired into the air to disperse the students, who had gathered at the gate and were hurling rocks at an “Israeli” bulldozer working on the other side.
The bulldozer was being operated by Palestinian workers who were digging a channel to install cables. The workers were being guarded by four “Israeli” soldiers.
As the students pelted the men with rocks, the soldiers fired shots in the air. The workers were forced to take shelter under nearby trees after the bulldozer’s windshield was broken by rocks. An “Israeli” reinforcement unit was sent to the gate, after which the workers and the soldiers withdrew.
In Al-Quds, a spokesman for the “Israeli” Army acknowledged that one soldier was lightly wounded by a rock and that the troops had fired into the air to disperse the crowd.

 

Cleric wants “Israeli” rabbi eliminated…(3)

The head of the Association of Jabal Amel Ulama urged Palestinians and Muslims on Thursday to execute the chairman of “Israel’s” ultra-Orthodox Shas Party, Rabbi Ovadia Yossef.
At a news conference at his Sidon office, Sheikh Afif Nabulsi called the rabbi, who had urged the annihiliation of Palestinians with rockets, a “nasty Satan and a big terrorist.”
“This man does not hesitate to kill children and innocent people and says crazy and hysterical things,” Nabulsi said.
He called on “Palestinians first and Muslims second” to execute “God’s sentence” on him by killing him “in any way possible.”
The cleric added that “the “Israeli” culture, which is supported by American culture, is designed to produce only aggression and mass killings.”

 

Lebanese military court convicts security official of Jewish collaboration…(4)

A Lebanese military court has convicted a security official of a former “Israeli”–allied militia of collaboration with the Jewish state and sentenced him in absentia to life imprisonment.

Alameddin Faris Badwi, whose whereabouts were not known, was convicted of serving as a security official of the now–defunct South Lebanon Army militia in the town of Hasbaya in the former “Israeli”–occupied border zone in southern Lebanon. He was also fined 10 million Lebanese pounds (US$6,666).

In the sentences and verdicts posted on a bulletin board, the court also convicted 21 people of collaboration and contact with “Israel” during its occupation of the south Lebanon border strip and sentenced them to prison terms ranging from three weeks to two years.

Most of the defendants were also fined 400,000 pounds (US$266), according to the court statement.

The court, whose verdicts cannot be appealed, sentenced 15 defendants to prison terms ranging from six months to two years for serving with the SLA and collaborating with “Israel”. Six men received jail terms ranging from three weeks to two months and were fined one million pounds (US$666) each for entering “Israel”.

Lebanon is technically at war with “Israel” and bans any dealings or contacts with the Jewish state.

The SLA helped “Israel” police its occupation zone in southern Lebanon. The militia collapsed after “Israeli” forces withdrew from south Lebanon last May, ending an 18–year occupation.

About 2,400 people have since June been sentenced by the military court to jail terms of up to 25 years. Hundreds more are awaiting trial. About 30 former SLA militiamen have been sentenced to death in absentia for killing Lebanese guerrillas who fought “Israeli” forces in south Lebanon.

Some 2,200 members of the SLA, which once numbered 2,500 fighters, have surrendered to Lebanese authorities or have been captured since “Israel’s” withdrawal. The rest, mostly ranking officers, fled with their families to “Israel” or other countries.

 

Spain to train Lebanese troops in mine removal…(5)

A group of Lebanese Army officers will travel to Spain next week to attend a land-mine clearing course given by a joint Spanish and Russian military team.
The squad of 20 officers and non-commissioned officers are scheduled to depart for Madrid on Monday for a six-week course involving all aspects of humanitarian demining.
“They won’t learn just how to remove land mines but other issues such as topography, communications … it’s an integral program,” said Guillermo Ardizone, consul at the Spanish Embassy.
The scheme for a joint training program was conceived by the Russian government which approached Spain as a partner due to that country’s considerable experience in demining.
The Spanish Army has cleared mines in Peru and Ecuador in South America and Angola and Mozambique in Africa. Some 1,200 Spanish troops are presently deployed in Kosovo on land-mine clearing duties.
Russia and Spain drew up a profile of the project which was then presented to the Lebanese authorities.
“The Lebanese reacted very fast in accepting and choosing the people. It’s been a very smooth cooperation,” Ardizone said.
Since “Israel” withdrew its forces from the South last year, at least 10 people have been killed and another 35 wounded in land-mine explosions.
The United Nations estimates there are around 130,000 land mines in South Lebanon as well as 288 booby-trap devices and an unknown amount of unexploded ordinance.
“The knowledge gained by the Lebanese officers will allow them to act as trainers when they return,” Ardizone said.
The diplomat said that there were no immediate plans to repeat the course in the future.
“We will evaluate the project when it is completed and see what is possible,” he said.
A senior military source said that the demining course in Spain was one of several overseas training programs on landmines for Lebanese soldiers. Other countries that have taught demining techniques include the United States, the United Kingdom, Italy and Syria.
The source added that a Syrian Army demining unit is presently clearing minefields in Jabal Bir al-Dahr and Kawkaba al-Arab in the Western Bekaa.

“Israeli” Occupation Aggressions Leave One Palestinian Martyr, Several Injured, A Mosque Destroyed…(6)

In the latest developments in occupied Palestine, 10 Lao anti-tank rockets of the “Israeli” occupation posts in the Gush Katif Settlement were missing. The “Israeli” forces are running investigations on the issue.

Just a day after a massive “Israeli” ground assault into the Khan Younis refugee camp, which left two Palestinians dead and dozens more wounded, “Israeli” tanks shelled Thursday a Palestinian refugee camp at Rafah in the southern Gaza Strip, destroying a mosque.

Palestinian Resistance fighters had thrown 23 grenades and 20 molotov cocktails at an occupation army outpost at Rafah on the border between the Gaza Strip and Egypt.

Also, “Israeli” enemy soldiers fired tear gas into a girls' school in the West Bank village of el-Khader triggering clashes that left two Palestinians injured.

A seven-year-old Palestinian girl was shot in the face by an “Israeli” rubber bullet while at school.

Several teenage school girls and teachers were treated for tear gas inhalation at the scene and the school was closed after the incident.

Children started throwing stones at “Israeli” soldiers posted next to a girls’ school in the village after the “Israeli” occupation army fired tear gas canisters into the school “for no reason,” a school employee said.

Two Palestinian teenage boys were also shot by rubber bullets outside the school during the demonstration, medical sources said.

In the Khan Younis refugee camp, “Israeli” enemy forces shot dead the Palestinian civilian Hafez Rushdi Khalil.

The 35 year-old man, was killed as he drove his taxi past an “Israeli” army post in the Khan Yunis area and another Palestinian man with him was shot in the foot.

Two anti-tank-grenades were fired at the Ganei Tal Settlement that forms part of the Gush Katif Settlement bloc west of Khan Yunis, causing no damage or death.

There were also exchanges of fire between “Israeli” troops and Palestinians near the settlement of Netzarim in the central Gaza Strip.

The “Israeli” forces also fired around 10 shells on the southern suburbs of Gaza City, wounding one Palestinian and damaging a position of the Palestinian national security forces.

An “Israeli” soldier was moderately wounded in the West Bank village of Beit Jala, near Beit Laham, when Palestinians opened fire on a military force between the Jewish settlement of Gilo in east al-Quds and the village.

A heavy exchange of fire erupted between “Israeli” troops and Palestinian fighters. “Israeli” tanks fired at least three shells on to the village.

 

Death by remote control as hit squads return…(7)

By Robert Fisk in Gaza

When the “Israelis” came for Abu Jihad exactly 13 years ago, they employed up to 4,000 men for his assassination. There was an Awacs plane over Tunis, a squadron of jets to protect the Awacs, two warships in the Mediterranean, a submarine to guard the warships, a 707 refuelling aircraft, 40 men to go ashore and surround the home of Yasser Arafat's PLO deputy commander, and four men and an officer to murder their victim.

Abu Jihad's son Jihad al-Wazzir recalls: "First they killed the bodyguard who was asleep in the car outside. Then they killed the gardener and the second bodyguard ... My dad was writing in his office and went into the hall with a pistol. He got off one shot before he was hit. My mother remembers how each of the four men would step forward and empty an entire clip of bullets from an automatic weapon into my dad ­ like it was a kind of ritual. Then an officer in a black mask stepped forward and shot him in the head, just to make sure."

Today, “Israel’s” murder squads come cheaper: a computer chip that activates a bomb in a mobile telephone, a family collaborator, or even a splash of ultra-violet paint on the roof of a car to alert an “Israeli” Apache helicopter pilot to fire a Hellfire missile into the Palestinian's vehicle.

It's long-range assassination. But some things don't change. Palestinians have long believed ­ and Jihad al-Wazzir Jnr is convinced ­ that the “Israeli” who delivered the coup de grâce to his father on 16 April 1988 was an intelligence officer called Moshe Yalon. And today, one of the principal instigators behind the policy of murdering “Israel’s” Palestinian military opponents is the deputy chief of staff, a certain major general called Moshe Yalon.

It's a cruel, vicious, internationally illegal war in which the Palestinians have themselves been guilty in the past. Back in the Seventies, “Israeli” and PLO agents murdered each other in Europe in a policy of retaliation and counter-retaliation that drove European security forces insane with anger. "In the end, these murders led to a ceasefire," Mr al-Wazzir explains. "The whole thing ended."

It continued, however, in Beirut where two of the men involved in murdering PLO leaders were called Ehud Barak and Amnon Shahak. Shahak would later become the “Israeli” military commander in Lebanon in 1982. And it was Mr Barak who as Prime Minister last year relaunched “Israel’s” murder squads.

Historians will one day debate the worth of such killings.

But “Israel’s” killers take innocent lives too. An Apache helicopter attack on a Palestinian militant tore two middle-aged Palestinian women to pieces; the “Israelis” did not apologise. The nephew of a man murdered by the “Israelis” in Nablus later admitted to the Palestinian Authority that he had given his uncle's location to the “Israelis”. He told his interrogators: "They said they were only going to arrest him. Then they killed him."

If it's a dirty war ­ which it is ­ it's also a developing one. Mr al-Wazzir, now an economic analyst in Gaza, explains: "It's small-scale now and in known locations. People who did not think of themselves as targets are killed. There's a network of “Israeli” army intelligence and air force intelligence, and Mossad and Shin Bet that works together, feeding each other information.

"They can cross the lines between Area C [under “Israeli” control] and Area B [shared control] in the occupied territories. They can penetrate these borders. Usually, they carry out operations when the IDF [“Israeli” Defence Force]* morale is low. When they killed my father, the IDF was in very low spirits because of the first intifada. So they go for a 'spectacular' to show what great warriors they are. Now the IDF morale is low again because of the second intifada."

Palestinian security officers in Gaza have been intrigued at the logic behind the “Israeli” killings. One of the Palestinian officials says:"Our guys meet their guys and we know their officers and operatives. I tell you this frankly ­ they are as corrupt and indisciplined as we are. And as ruthless.

"After they [the “Israelis”] targeted Mohamed Dahlan's convoy when he was coming back from security talks, Dahlan [the head of Palestinian 'preventive security' in Gaza] talked to [the “Israeli” Foreign Minister Shimon] Peres. 'Look what you guys are doing to us,' Dahlan told Peres. 'Don't you realise it was me who took Sharon's son to meet Arafat?'"

Was this a threat? Mr al-Wazzir understands some of the death squad logic. "It has some effect because we Palestinians are a paternalistic society," he says. "We believe in the idea of a father figure. But when they assassinated my dad, the intifada didn't stop. It was affected but all the political objectives failed; rather than demoralising the Palestinians, the assassination fuelled the intifada.

"They say there's a list now of 100 Palestinians on the murder list. No, I don't think the Palestinians will adopt the same type of killings against “Israeli” intelligence. An army is an institution, a system. Murdering an officer just results in him being replaced."

The “Israelis” have murdered up to 20 Palestinians they claim to be "terrorists" ­ with no concrete evidence and no court hearings. It's a practice they honed in Lebanon where guerrilla leaders were blown up by hidden bombs or shot in the back by Shin Bet execution squads, often ­ as in the case of an Amal leader in the village of Bidias ­ after interrogation. All this was, and still is, in the name of "security". And that is something the murders have clearly not produced.

 

“Israeli” Soldiers Kill Palestinian…(8)

““Israeli” soldiers shot and killed a Palestinian teen-ager, and Palestinian police safely exploded a car bomb they said was an “Israeli” attempt to kill an activist. Both sides followed a security meeting with tough talk.

Countering a trend of daily barrages, Palestinians did not fire mortar shells at “Israeli” settlements Thursday, but other violence continued.

A 14-year-old Palestinian boy was shot and killed Thursday night on a main West Bank road at the village of Beit Umar, north of Hebron. Palestinians said there were no clashes in the area, but the “Israeli” military said soldiers shot at someone trying to throw a firebomb at a civilian bus, setting off an exchange of gunfire.

In Ramallah, Palestinian security agents foiled what they said was an “Israeli” attempt to kill a local activist with a car bomb. Nasser Abu Humaid, leader of the Tanzim militia in the Amari refugee camp, said two men befriended him and offered him a car. Humaid said he became suspicious and called security agents, who discovered a bomb in the car.

They towed it to a safe location where it exploded. Humaid claimed the two men were collaborators working for “Israel” and said the car bomb was an attempt to kill him.

''This shows how serious the “Israelis” are about security,'' said West Bank security chief Jibril Rajoub, complaining that the car bomb attempt came a day after “Israeli” and Palestinian security commanders met. ''This is an “Israeli” invitation to ... send a car bomb to Tel Aviv.''

The “Israeli” military said it did not know about the car bomb incident.

Since hostilities erupted on Sept. 28, “Israel” has targeted and killed at least 15 activists, Palestinians say. “Israel” has acknowledged some of the attacks and refused to comment on others. Overall, 469 people have been killed in the conflict, including 386 Palestinians, 64 “Israeli” Jews and 19 others.

Palestinian Information Minister Yasser Abed Rabbo said that the Palestinian Authority has ''not yet used all its tools in response to “Israeli” aggression.''

The rhetoric remained heated despite a meeting of security commanders that ended early Thursday. It was the second session after months without any meetings, but there were no apparent results. U.S. diplomats took part in an effort to defuse the crisis and restart peace talks.

 

Three soldiers wounded…(9)

Three soldiers were wounded in two separate incidents, one in the West Bank and the other in the Gaza Strip, near the border with Egypt yesterday morning.
Shortly after 8 a.m. Palestinian snipers shot at a soldiers manning a roadblock at the entrance to the Al-Quds - Gush Etzion (tunnel) road. A soldier was shot in the jaw and taken to Hadassah-University Hospital in Ein Kerem, where he underwent surgery and was listed in satisfactory to serious condition.
Two soldiers were lightly wounded yesterday morning when Palestinians shot at an army post near the border with Egypt, one was transferred to Soroka Hospital in Beersheba suffering from a gunshot wound to his right arm and shoulder.
Following the shooting at the entrance to the tunnel road, the army closed the road to traffic, and a tank fired a shell at a building on the slopes of Beit jala that was identified as being the source of the sniper fire. Shortly after Palestinians once again fired shots at soldiers at the roadblock but no one was hurt.
Throughout the day there were heavy exchanges of fire between soldiers in army posts near Rafah and Palestinian gunmen. In addition to the shooting Palestinians threw 23 grenades and 18 firebombs at the posts. Towards noon there were intermittent exchanges of gunfire between Palestinians in Khan Yunis and soldiers deployed at the Neveh Dekalim industrial zone.
