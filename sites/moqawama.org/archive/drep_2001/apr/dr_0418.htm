Hizbullah's Constant Rule Is Resistance Operations Shall Continue Until Liberation Is Achieved…(1)

Responding to the “Israeli” enemy leaders words about changing the rules of the game following the “Israeli” air raid on a Syrian position in Lebanon, Hizbullah deputy Secretary-General Sheikh Nahim Qassem asserted that the resistance has one unchangeable rule, which is the continuation of its activities for the sake of liberation- of the occupied Lebanese Shibaa Farms and detainees in “Israeli” prisons.

Sheikh Qassem affirmed the military operations aim at liberation and are not carried out for political uses, nor are the operations directed against any side but the Zionist occupation.

 Commenting on Lebanese daily al-Mustaqbal's words, belonging to Premier Rafiq Hariri, that Hizbullah's Shibaa Farm operation on Saturday was ill-timed, Sheikh Qassem said the daily's comment was ill-timed.

He also said that the recent “Israeli” pounding of the Syrian position shows the extent of the Zionist irritation from the Lebanese-Syrian coordination and cohesion.

 

“Israel” wants Syria to quit Lebanon, says Hizbullah and its allies…(2)

“ The main political bloc of Hizbullah and Amal, in addition to other parties, charged Tuesday that “Israel’s” recent air raid on Syrian positions in Lebanon was aimed to pressure Damascus to withdraw its estimated 35,000 troops from the country.

"The attack had the goal of changing the balance of power imposed on “Israel” by the resistance and to create pressure to push the Syrian troops to exit Lebanon," Hizbullah and its allies said in a press statement issued at the movement's weekly meeting.

The meeting also brought Hizbullah together with Amal, headed by Parliamentary Speaker Nabih Berri, the Lebanese branch of the Baath party, groups and small Nasserist and Muslim parties.

“Israel” wants "to separate Syria's destiny from Lebanon and make it surrender Lebanon and at the same time revenge its stinging failures at the hands of the heroic resistance," the statement said, alluding to “Israel’s” withdrawal from south Lebanon last May after a 22-year occupation.

Hizbullah, Lebanon and Syria have pledged to continue attacks until the "liberation" of the Shibaa Farms. But “Israel” says it will only return the territory as part of a comprehensive agreement with Damascus.

 

Assad issues warning after “Israeli” attack
Beirut asks Annan to ‘deter’ further ‘aggression’…(3)

Syrian President Bashar Assad warned “Israel” on Tuesday that it “alone will bear the price of its aggressive actions in the region,” as Beirut mounted a diplomatic campaign to prevent further “Israeli” strikes in Lebanon.
In a telephone conversation with UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, Assad called the situation “extremely complicated” and said a solution required “important efforts.”
He was speaking after “Israeli” planes destroyed a Syrian radar installation in Dahr al-Baidar early Monday, killing one Syrian soldier and wounding six.
The raid came after Hizbullah fighters on Saturday attacked a tank in the Shibaa Farms area, killing an “Israeli” soldier.
US Secretary of State Colin Powell said he was “deeply concerned,” urging all sides to exercise “maximum restraint” and end the violence “immediately.”
Beirut called for an “emergency” meeting of the Arab League ministerial council and demanded implementation of resolutions adopted by the recent Arab summit in Amman.
President Emile Lahoud received a telephone call from Saudi Arabia’s crown prince, Abdullah bin Abdul-Aziz, who affirmed that the kingdom would provide “all possible help” for Lebanon and denounced the “Israeli” attack.
Lahoud, who maintained close contact with Assad on the developments, described the raid as yet another “expression of the bloody course adopted by (“Israeli” Premier Ariel) Sharon since coming to power.”
Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud conveyed Beirut’s position during talks with ambassadors from UN Security Council countries.
“We consider “Israel’s” action to be a dangerously aggressive escalation,” Hammoud said.
He vowed that the government would not drop its demand to recover all “Israeli”-occupied territory, including the Shibaa Farms, describing the “Israeli” strike as “aggression against both Lebanon and Syria.”
He also stressed that resolutions adopted at the Amman summit said any “Israeli” aggression against Lebanon or Syria would be taken as aggression against all Arab countries.
Hammoud said he sent a letter to Annan, asserting the need for the UN to “deter “Israel”.”
For its part, Hizbullah pledged to continue resistance operations, saying its fighters would continue to attack “Israeli” troops in the border zone “at the time we choose and in the appropriate manner.”
“You may hear of a (new) operation (in Shibaa) very soon,” said Sheikh Naim Qassem, Hizbullah’s deputy secretary-general. “It may be delayed for a while due to on-the-ground factors rather than political conditions.”
In a statement issued earlier, Hizbullah also suggested that it might target settlements in northern “Israel” to avenge the raid.
“We will chop off the hand of the enemy which reached into our land and our brothers,” said the statement. “We will turn Sharon’s dream of terrorizing our people into a nightmare.
“Hizbullah knows when, where and how to strike at the enemy,” the statement said.
The raid was the first direct attack on a Syrian military position here since 1996, and the most serious since “Israel’s” 1982 invasion, when “Israeli” forces destroyed Syrian anti-aircraft missile batteries in Lebanon.
“Israeli” warplanes broke the sound barrier Tuesday over eastern Lebanon. Planes also carried out reconnaissance missions over the South and the Western Bekaa.
The ambassadors of Russia, Britain and France all expressed concern about the situation after talks with Hammoud, while Qatari Ambassador Mohammed Ali Nuaimy denounced the “flagrant “Israeli” aggression.”
In Saudi Arabia, the Kingdom’s second deputy prime minister and minister of defense, Prince Sultan ibn Abdel-Aziz, said the “Israeli” attack was “totally unacceptable.”
Gulf states, including Kuwait and Oman, condemned the “Israeli” strikes as “blatant aggression” and called for international pressure to stop “Israel’s” attacks.
Jordan’s King Abdullah telephoned Assad to express solidarity with Damascus after the “provocative” raid.
Palestinian President Yasser Arafat told reporters in Amman that the strike was a “dangerous escalation,” and Egyptian President Hosni Mubarak denounced “this aggression against an Arab force that hasn’t attacked anyone and hasn’t violated the law or international resolutions.”
In Tehran, First Vice-President Hassan Habibi called on all Islamic and Arab countries to support Lebanon.
The Chinese government expressed concern over the rising tensions in the Middle East and appealed to “Israel” to “immediately cease its infringement upon Lebanon’s sovereignty.”

Lebanese FM calls for "deterring" further “Israeli” attacks…(4)

“ Lebanese Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud started meeting Tuesday with foreign ambassadors to ask the international community to deter “Israel” from repeating attacks into Lebanon, an official said.

“Israeli” warplanes raided a Syrian radar position in eastern Lebanon on Monday, killing at least one Syrian soldier and wounding four others.

On Tuesday, Lebanese and Syrian army troops, as well as Hizbullah guerrillas, remained on a state of high alert, although the situation in the country has remained calm since the raid.

"The foreign minister has invited the ambassadors of the five permanent members of the United Nations Security Council to inform them that “Israel” is attempting to escalating the situation in the region," he said.

"He wants to ask the international community to deter “Israel” from repeating such aggression because it is a dangerous escalation for the entire region," said the official, who did not wish to be identified.

Hammoud spoke with the US ambassador David Satterfield by telephone on Monday, but no details were disclosed on that meeting.

He met with Russian Ambassador Boris Bolotine at the foreign ministry on Tuesday and was also due to receive the ambassadors of France, China, Britain and Arab countries.

"Russia is extremely concerned about the latest developments," Bolotine told reporters after the meeting. "The last (“Israeli”) raid is a dangerous escalation to the situation in the region, and this is unacceptable."

"We call on all sides to observe maximum restraint to avoid increasing the escalation in the region," he said.

 

Southern Lebanese, not scared by renewed “Israeli” raids…(5)

“ “Israeli” air strikes Monday did not scare the people of south Lebanon who lived with the dual attrocities of both the country's civil war (1975-1990) and a 22-year “Israeli” occupation that ended last May.

Although “Israeli” fighter planes bombarded a Syrian radar station east of Beirut, killing at least one Syrian soldier and wounding four more, in the first direct “Israeli” attack on the Syrian military since 1982, many south Lebanese residents appeared surprisingly calm in the aftermath.

Lebanon's turbulent history with “Israel” of ground invasions and pulverising air raids seemed far away.

"The attacks and counter-attacks were 'messages' and the issue is over now. I don't think that “Israel” is now interested in getting into another long-term military adventure in Lebanon," said Georges Lahoud, 31.

Lahoud is well placed to know about armed conflicts in the region. He lives in the Christian town of Marjayoun, the main locality in the border zone occupied by “Israel” for 22 years until its troop pullout in May 2000.

"It is true, as “Israeli” leaders said yesterday, that these are new rules for the game. During the occupation, we were constantly awaiting the next round of “Israeli” shelling. Now they responded with a very tactical hit," said Ahmad, a resident of Shaqra located just outside the former occupied zone.

But while this usually sleepy southern region was even more quiet during the long Easter weekend, the Lebanese and Syrian army troops, as well as Hizbullah guerrillas, remained on a state of high alert.

"It is all an issue of messages being sent, and this one was for Syria, more than for Lebanon, this is why the general situation is rather normal," said housewife Samia Ayoub, referring to Syria's status as Lebanon's chief powerbroker.

"This is why people are generally not scared, although there is always a fear that “Israel” would want to escalate the situation," she added.

Jiryes Nohra, sitting behind the counter of his pharmacy in the town of Qlaiaa, said "the “Israeli” direct strike against a Syrian target was meant by “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon to defuse internal tensions inside “Israel” over the Intifada, or Palestinian uprising."

"We, in the south, are not concerned and we are not scared, because “Israel” is very much worried about Hizbullah's presence on the border, a few yards away from villages in northern “Israel”," said Nohra, 38.

"“Israel” had already been worried about the resistance guerrillas during the occupation when they were relatively far away, so we can imagine how it is now when “Israeli” towns are just a stone's throw away from the arms of the resistance," he said.

Kamal Khreis, 40, a baker from the nearby village of Khiam, said "we are not scared from “Israel” and not from its reactions because it has recently suffered more dangerous blows and it did not respond."

"“Israel” is now scared of the resistance guerrillas and I do not think that “Israel” would dare bombard civilian regions in southern Lebanon because it knows that the resistance's response will be harsh."

But if the Lebanese have their views about Hizbullah, Syria's continuing 35,000-strong military presence in the country and the latest “Israeli” attack, all breathed a sigh of relief when they woke up to see that, this time at least, the country's power grid was spared.

"The “Israelis””, when they couldn't hit back at Hizbullah, they used to unleash their anger at our power stations," said a woman named Fatima, sitting behind the counter of her candy store.

"At least, this time our summer is saved," she said, handing over ice cream cones to a group of young girls.

 

Iran urges Arab, Islamic states to support Lebanon…(6)

“ Iran's first vice president, Hassan Habibi, called on all Islamic and Arab states Tuesday to support Lebanon in its resistance against “Israel”, the official IRNA news agency reported.

Habibi, in a meeting with visiting Lebanese Economy Minister Bassel Fleihan, urged support for Lebanon, "a country situated in the front line of the war against the Zionist regime," IRNA reported.

He said there were "many great possibilities in various fields to develop cooperation between the two countries," and reaffirmed Tehran's determination to "deepen and develop" ties with Lebanon.

Fleihan, whose arrival and departure dates were not announced, said his country was ready to strengthen relations with Iran in the economic, commercial and transport sectors.

Fleihan also extended to Habibi an invitation by Lebanese Prime Minister Rafiq Hariri for a visit to Lebanon.

Tehran condemned “Israel’s” raids Monday on Syrian positions in Lebanon as a dangerous "adventurism," that are "destined to fail."

Foreign Ministry spokesman Hamid-Reza Asefi underlined Tehran's support for Syrian and Lebanese claims, as well as those of the Palestinians, to territory occupied by the “Israelis””, affirming that “Israeli” raids would fail to "block the Lebanese and Palestinian people from securing their rights."

 

Arab League to meet Saturday to discuss “Israeli” strike in Lebanon…(7)

“ An emergency meeting of the Arab League ministerial council will be held Saturday, following a request by Lebanon and Syria, to discuss the recent “Israeli” strike on a Syrian military position in Lebanon, a League source said Tuesday.

Cairo-based delegates of the 22-member organisation will attend, the same source added.

The Omani foreign ministry said the attack was "a flagrant aggression against Lebanon" and warned that "“Israeli” policy will plunge the whole region into more tension".

In Sanaa, a spokesman for the foreign ministry underlined Yemen's support for Lebanon and Syria in comments condemning the "aggression" carried by the official SABA news agency.

And OIC secretary-general Abdulwahed Belkeziz said in a statement published from the body's Jeddah headquarters that "the new “Israeli” aggression is a violation of the sovereignty and territorial integrity of Lebanon."

In Bahrain, the foreign ministry deplored what it called “Israel’s” "flagrant violation of the territorial integrity of Lebanon" and the "dangerous escalation" in regional tension.

Slain brothers remembered in Dearborn

1996 “Israeli” attack killed boys on visit…(10)

During spring break in 1996, two of Haidar Bitar's sons visited their grandmother in Lebanon.

They never came back.

Five years ago today, an “Israeli” bomb slammed into a United Nations compound, killing them and tearing off their grandmother's arm.

On Tuesday, the Dearborn resident joined more than 150 other concerned Arab Americans to commemorate the deaths of about 100 civilians in a UN camp for refugees in Qana, Lebanon, on April 18, 1996.

"I lost a part of my body when they died," said Bitar at the Lebanese-American Heritage Manor. "My other son keeps asking me 'When are my brothers coming back?' "

“Israel” maintains it launched the artillery attacks in retaliation for attacks from Hizballah guerrillas in northern “Israel”.

But those who attended Tuesday's commemoration say it was an unjustified shelling that killed many innocent civilians seeking refuge in a UN facility.

The commemoration has become an annual event in Dearborn. After the attack, the city became an official sister city of Qana in symbolic support of its people.

State Rep. Gary Woronchak spoke at the event, as did former Dearborn Police Chief Ron Deziel, City Councilman Doug Thomas and Mayor Michael Guido.

"We're here to remember two sons of Dearborn," said Mayor Guido. "We're here to remind people."

The recent turmoil in the Middle East added to the sense of frustration for many who attended, and they questioned the United States' support of “Israel”.

Since violence erupted on Sept. 28 between “Israelis”” and Palestinians, almost 500 people, mostly Arabs, have been killed. On Monday, “Israel” launched an airstrike against Syrian targets in Lebanon -- its first raid deep into Lebanon since its troops withdrew last year.

From Dearborn cafes to local e-mail discussion groups, anger has been steadily growing.

"Qana is just one example of so many acts of “Israeli” aggression," said Imad Hamad of Dearborn, regional director of the American-Arab Anti Discrimination Committee. "It's a reminder to all of us that peace cannot be achieved on the bodies of the innocent civilians."

 

“Israel” re-occupies Gaza territories and kills three Palestinians…(11)

The “Israeli” army has shot and killed three Palestinians as troops re-occupied Palestinian self-rule areas in the Gaza Strip. Top “Israeli” generals said troops could remain for months in territory they have reoccupied. Palestinian President Yasser Arafat slammed “Israel’s” raids and its reoccupation of Palestinian land as an "unforgivable crime". A short while ago, Egypt's President Mubarak issued a blunt warning to Sharon not to go quote beyond the limit of what is acceptable. Mubarak said after meeting with Arafat violence will increase everywhere if “Israel” continues its attacks on Palestinians. Mubarak also warned “Israel” against trying to draw Cairo into the conflict following allegations that Palestinians have been smuggling arms though the Egyptian-Palestinian border. 

“ “Israeli” troops re-occupied parts of the Palestinian-ruled Gaza Strip in a deadly attack that left one Palestinian policeman killed, at least 30 people injured and left life in the region in disarray. In the latest sign of hard-line Prime Minister Ariel Sharon's "get-tough" policy, the “Israeli” army invaded the autonomous-ruled area of Beit Hanoun near the Erez crossing between Gaza and “Israel” and vowed to keep its forces there until Palestinian mortar fire against “Israel” comes to a halt. Using helicopter gunships, tanks and bulldozers, “Israeli” forces pounded civilian and security targets. “Israel” took control of part of northern Gaza and sliced the peninsula into three by blocking main roads. Gaza City's main police headquarters and two of Palestinian President Yasser Arafat's elite Force-17 security posts were among the targets of the devastating strike. “Israeli” security officials claimed they were only controlling territory and not re-occupying Palestinian-ruled areas. “Israeli” officials said there is no intention to keep territories ruled by Arafat, who discussed the escalation with Egypt's President Hosni Mubarak in Sharm el-Sheikh. Top Palestinian officials have denounced “Israel’s” reoccupation of parts of Gaza as a "scandalous violation" of peace agreements and called for international intervention. Sharon ordered the assault after five mortars were fired into the southern “Israeli” town of Sderot, the deepest strike into “Israel” since the uprising began. The armed wing of the Palestinian Hamas movement claimed responsibility for the attack, saying it was in retaliation for a weekend assassination of a Hamas activist. In separate bouts of violence, troops shot dead a Palestinian teenager. “Israeli” gunfire also killed a 14-year-old Palestinian boy near the Karni crossing between Gaza and “Israel”. There were no clashes in the area at the time.
