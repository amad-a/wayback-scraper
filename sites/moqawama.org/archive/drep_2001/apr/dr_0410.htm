Suspect collaborator car explodes at Houla…(1)

A booby-trapped car exploded  in the southern village of Houla at dawn on Monday, the second such incident in two weeks.
There were no casualties in the explosion.
The car, which was totally destroyed by the blast, was owned by school teacher Yasser Awad, 45, and had been parked in front of his home.
Police officials said that an investigation had been started to identify the reasons behind the incident.
On March 26, an explosive charge placed in a pick-up truck in Houla blew up, again without inflicting casualties.
A letter had then been found at the site, threatening the owner of the vehicle “and all collaborators and traitors that they should leave the village immediately, or the response will be very harsh and the gates of Hell will be opened on you.”
The letter was signed “Strike Force 513” on behalf of former detainees and victims of the “Israeli” occupation.
Monday’s bombing was the latest in a series of attacks against suspected collaborators with “Israel” during its occupation of the South.
Last week, a house in the village of Hebbarieh belonging to a former “Israeli”-proxy South Lebanon Army militiaman, was fire-bombed. Two weeks earlier, another house in Hebbarieh was similarly gutted.
Residents of Shiite and Sunni villages in the former occupation zone have expressed deep unhappiness and anger at the sentences handed down to the collaborators, arguing that the judiciary has been too lenient.

 

Public asked not to touch mine signs…(2)

The army cautioned the public Monday against removing land-mine warning signs in the former occupation zone.
“We urge people not to approach military posts that used to be formerly manned by “Israel” or its South Lebanon Army militia,” an army statement said.
It also advised people “not to touch any unfamiliar objects and to use only paved roads” instead of taking shortcuts across the open countryside between villages.
“Anyone who spots any suspicious object, ammunition or weapon must immediately report their findings to the local police,” the army said.

Mortar Bomb Attacks on “Israeli” Settlements Continue As Occupation Troops Shot Dead Another Palestinian…(4)

In the latest intifadah operations, a mortar bomb fired struck a Jewish settlement in the southern Gaza Strip on Monday afternoon, an “Israeli” army spokesman said , adding that no-one was injured.

However, an electricity pylon was damaged inside the settlement of Atzmona in the Gush Katif settlement bloc, the spokesman said.

The occupation army spokesman said it was the 28th mortar bomb fired on “Israeli” targets in Gaza and nearby “Israeli” territory in three days.

Meanwhile, fierce clashes spread around the West Bank after a Palestinian man was killed overnight by “Israeli” gunfire near the village of Beitunia, witnesses and medical sources said.

Palestinian witnesses said four “Israeli” soldiers were wounded in an exchange of fire as they tried to enter Beitunia, which lies between Ramallah and occupied Quds.

The occupation army said a military jeep had been shot at in the area and that the army had returned fire.

“Israeli” tanks and troops closed in on Beitunia  and two Palestinian schools in the village were evacuated during a three-hour gunfight between armed Palestinians and “Israeli” troops, witnesses said.

On Sunday, Taysir al-Amuri, a 45-year-old bedouin, was shot dead by “Israeli” machine-gun bullets in the Beitunia area.

Residents described the man as psychologically disturbed and said that he had not been involved in the gunfight.

The occupation army also reported that Palestinian gunmen also opened fire towards the Ofer army camp near Beitunia and a military post in the village. The army responded but no injuries were reported.

In the West Bank town of al-Khalil (Hebron), occupation  troops fired rubber bullets and tear gas at dozens of stone-throwing Palestinian youths, witnesses said adding that no one was injured.

On the other hand, a high-level “Israeli”-Palestinian security  meeting originally scheduled for tonight  has been cancelled.

Later on an “Israeli” official  said on condition of anonymity," a meeting  is expected to take place Monday night," declining to give further details.

But the Palestinians said nothing was  confirmed. Abdel Razeq al-Majeida,  general security chief in the Gaza Strip, told  Voice of Palestine radio,"There are no  preparations to hold an “Israeli”-Palestinian  security meeting today, especially after the  assassination attempt on the security  delegation, referring to last week's “Israeli” shooting at a Palestinian convoy at  the Erez checkpoint.

Last week's meeting was  the first of the so-called higher security coordination committee to be held since “Israeli”  Prime Minister Ariel Sharon took office a  month ago aiming at halting what is referred  to as the cycle of violence.

The “Israeli”  public radio said Monday's meeting  would take place at Erez following an  agreement reached during separate  weekend telephone conversations US  Secretary of State Colin Powell had with  “Israeli” prime minister Ariel Sharon and  Palestinian Chairman Yasser Arafat.

 

Palestinian mortars hit settlement-”Israeli” army…(5)

“The “Israeli” army said Palestinians fired three mortar bombs at a Jewish settlement in the Gaza Strip on Tuesday, despite efforts by the United States to help “Israel” and the Palestinians resume security talks.

In a token gesture, “Israel” slightly eased its six-month-old blockade of Gaza to let a few Palestinians come to the Jewish state to work. But only about 300 made the trip and tens of thousands more remained unable to work because of the blockade.

An army spokeswoman said three mortar bombs were fired near the Jewish settlement of Gush Katif in Gaza and that one landed in greenhouses and one in a nearby factory. No one was hurt and the army was looking for the third mortar bomb, she said.

There was no immediate comment from Palestinian officials.

They have denied having mortars although militant groups have claimed responsibility for an increasing number of mortar attacks in the last few weeks of the six-month-old uprising against “Israel’s” occupation of Gaza and the West Bank.

Efforts to arrange a meeting between “Israeli” and Palestinian security officials on Monday failed, but the U.S. State Department said it was trying to arrange more talks to follow up a security meeting last week.

"They've agreed, in principle, to continuing those discussions and we will continue to work with the parties to try to see if we can help them do that," State Department spokesman Richard Boucher said.

Neither side said there was any breakthrough at last week's talks but Jordan's King Abdullah, who was due to meet U.S. President George W. Bush in Washington on Tuesday, cited reports.

 

Workers trickle into “Israel”…(6)

At least 370 Palestinians, 13 “Israeli” Arabs and 71 other “Israelis” have been killed since the Palestinians began their uprising last September after peace talks became deadlocked. The toll includes four Palestinian suicide bombers.

“Israel” has blockaded Palestinian areas in what it says is a measure needed to prevent attacks on “Israelis”. Palestinians say the blockades are a collective punishment for the uprising and are choking their economy.

The blockades have barred more than 100,000 Palestinians from jobs they had in “Israel”, and has been criticised by the United Nations, the United States and the European Union.

Yarden Vatikay, spokesman for the military-run Civil Administration for the West Bank and Gaza, said “Israel” had issued permits to 3,200 labourers and expected up to 1,000 to cross into “Israel” from Gaza.

But a Reuters correspondent saw only about 300 pass through the Erez Crossing from Gaza early on Tuesday. They found few buses waiting to take them to work, and many said they would simply return to Gaza.

The slight easing of the closure of Gaza fell into line with a policy announced by “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon of punishing Palestinians responsible for attacks on “Israelis” but easing hardships for other Palestinians.

Saed al-Mudallal, director-general of the Employment Department at the Palestinian Labour Ministry, said many Palestinians thought they had poor chances of finding employment in “Israel” or feared retaliatory actions by “Israelis”.

U.N. Secretary-General Kofi Annan said on Monday that all parties wanted to ease the economic situation for the Palestinians and renew peace talks, and opposed Sharon's view that negotiations cannot resume as long as violence continues.

"I personally disagree with that. I think that (the violence) is one more reason to talk and it underscores the urgency of bringing the parties together," Annan told reporters in New York.

 

Dahlan won't participate in security talks…(7)

Mohammed Dahlan, head of the Palestinian Authority's Preventive Security in Gaza, said yesterday that he won't attend any more talks with “Israeli” representatives after the incident last week in which he and other top Palestinian security officials were shot at by “Israeli” soldiers at the Erez junction. The Palestinians were returning to Gaza from an American-sponsored resumption of the security coordination talks in “Israel”.
Yasser Arafat, called him and asked him to go to the next round of security talks. Dahlan responded, "You know I've never told you no, that I've never turned you down, but this time I must refuse." He told Foreign Minister Shimon Peres, who called him a few minutes later, "you've gone crazy. That wasn't the mistake of a lone soldier. Everyone's lying to you. There were 72 bullets fired at me. They didn't shoot a missile because then they'd have to take responsibility, they couldn't blow up my car, so they made it look like a mistake. I invested six years in peace, I paid a heavy price, but not so that something like this would happen to me. Sharon's son, Omri, was with us in Ramallah. What if we had kidnapped him. What would the world have said? What would “Israel” have said?"
Dahlan says that he was saved by his bodyguards. Abu Khaled, his chief bodyguard, covered him with his own body and ordered the jeep to maneuver to block the bullets from hitting the Mercedes, until Dahlan managed to get through to "the White House" from Erez Junction, starting a round of phone calls that eventually had an angry U.S. Secretary of State Colin Powell waken Prime Minister Ariel Sharon .
"They shot at me for half an hour. They fired from three positions where usually there aren't any soldiers, and when the “Israeli” Army did man those positions, they always let us know in advance. This time they didn't. Half an hour after a meeting that everyone said was excellent, we found ourselves in an ambush. The state of “Israel” should be ashamed."
Dahlan says that Sharon "doesn't want us alive. He wants security, settlements, to let us have 40 percent of the West Bank and we should say thank you very much."
