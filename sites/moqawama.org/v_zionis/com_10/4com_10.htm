4) THE POLICY OF EXPANSION: 

The provisional Zionism moved its path from the epoch of appealing for a refuge to the call for an entity, and then a homeland throughout the period of half a century (1897-1942). Palestine was exactly this refuge, or entity or homeland. But which Palestine? Or what kind of borders should Palestine be identified with?

As we have previously recognized, Zionism is a setting expansionist movement, and it is firmly forbidden to separate between the movement and its settling characteristic, nor between this settling characteristic and the policy of expansion itself, as properly revealed in the forthcoming 5th Commandment of the 10 Commandments list announced by the Zionist movement at the end of the infamous Bazl congress which the Zionists celebrated its 100th anniversary back in May 1997.

In fact, the issue of Palestines borders was, still and will remain, a substantial subject deeply rooted in the Zionist thinking and practices; it is on this groundwork that the expansion will thrive by size, place, time, condition and nature means.

The Zionist expansion policy in Palestine is based on an unshakable concept, meant to never accept any concession or debate regarding it, and expressed by the saying that Palestine is only a first step and an initial base toward creating the ultimate Zionist homeland, which should be poised to expand and never decrease, and which border never allowed to move backward, to the inside of the Zionist entity precisely; thus, each discourse about the stability of "Israels borders" is a superficial one, only confined to a specific time or era. Moreover, there are no borderlines for "Israel" from the geographic point of view, as this concept is widely accepted by the Zionist movement through practical and de facto means. Hence, time and circumstances are the main elements able to draw these borderlines, to create and manipulate them as well. Borderlines have, accordingly, the capacity dimension, not the spatial one.

From this stage, we totally condemn all those who constantly reiterate the Torah saying that "Israels" borders, according to the alleged divine promise given to the prophets of Jewish people, stretch from river Nile to the Euphrates; undoubtedly, many Jewish elements echoed this claim as well, particularly during the first quarter of the current century, most of them traditional religious elements, or even those others who never adhered to the deep religious practices and basis, tending to use these aspects in order to gain the religious elements back to their own camp, especially when the Zionist principles have been lacking the overwhelming support of the world Jewry opinion at that stage of time.

However, those who reiterated this slogan found in it a special attraction, outstandingly relished by the Jewish ordinary man, wherever he might be, and whatever his political orientation might endeavor, because it brought to him the remembrance of a transmitted saying and a prevalent promise (a factitious one), attributed to God of Jewish people only, which bear in its folding the pure explanation of all the other commandments that Jewish people is an exquisite and selected people, a people who possess the divine right of owning the land of Palestine where all the Jews of the world should gather around and establish their presence.

However, it should be noted that the first retraction of the theory mentioning "from the Nile to the Euphrates" took place at the aftermath of World War 1, as the victorious countries began their search deep into the Jewish issue, and therefore in creating a national entity for the Jews in Palestine following the announcement of "Balfour promise", the collapse of the Ottoman Empire and the British occupation of Palestine.

The Zionist leadership was opposed at that time to an upheaval problem:  it was compelled within is persistent attempts before the European countries to gain their approval of the mentioned promise and start to establish the foundations of the targeted entity, to reveal its notion regarding the geographic borders of this desired and most acclaimed homeland. In order to achieve this task, the Zionist leadership was forced to deal with many hurdles; from one side, Herzl always reiterated the word "Entity" in an open and general manner, never being able to define it properly, this situation was amply expressed within the list of announcements released at the end of the first Zionist congress (Bazl, August 1897), and afterwards.

On the other side, there was no definitive agreement between all the Zionists (Leadership and parties, as well as proposals) about the quiddity of these borders, as the final outcome of the mentioned congress heavily lacked the strict drawing of the borderlines; moreover, this issue was left stretchable, subject to many different explanations and opinions. While the concrete dream of fulfilling the "From the Nile to the Euphrates" slogan as the proper borderlines of "Israel" was tickling the deep emotions of many Jews, there have been on the opposite side many European reservations unwilling to adhere to the concept of this expanded new entity, as it will symbolize the Judaization of the super powers regional interests in the Middle-East area, particularly affecting both Britain and France who supported the Zionists claims during the initial stages in Palestine, as both European nations have been contemplating their own strategic interests at the first place, and concluded that the Zionist claims could reveal very positive in order to enhance these interests, not the opposite.

Under the heavy pressures exerted by these operatives, Zionist official memorandums submitted by the "World Zionist Organization" to the victorious nations, collectively and individually, during the "Versailles Convention" (Paris 1919) and afterwards, contented with lesser than the formerly proclaimed slogan of "From the Nile to the Euphrates"; additionally, the Zionist organization adopted a "Theological" study set up by rabbi Isaacs, which called for the definition of "Biblical Palestine". This study settled for borders map stretching from the Lebanese City of Sidon and the riverheads of Litani (North) to the Southern spot of Sinai desert. In contrast, France, having to protect its traditional greedy aspirations in Lebanon following its official agreement with Britain (the famous "Sikes-Picot" accord of May 1916), maintained its upper hand over Southern Lebanon and all the territories of this country, by means of the mentioned accord with its British ally, and sought to reduce the Northern borderlines of the Zionist proclaimed entity, ending up in establishing the map of Palestine, the one we all get familiar with, entering the modern history as the official map of Palestine since 1922; hence, the international approach to any Zionist entity was easily restricted toward this portion of land, forcing all the Zionists to comply reluctantly.

Granting the Jews only a part of the Palestinian land didnt came, in fact, to contradict the official Zionist claims; the 4th Commandment called upon for a foothold in Palestine, leaving to the Zionists of the future the task of claiming wider portions of lands, in conjunction with their own possibilities (either militarily, economically or internationally). This was the reason behind the temporary consent of the Zionist leaderships toward these projects, until the chances arise to violate the defined borderlines recommended by these projects, in order to expand to Zionist entitys sovereignty over new regions surrounding it.

The Zionists, for example, expressed their satisfaction toward the United Nations resolutions of
November 1947, but stunned the whole world after less than 2 years, following their acquisition of large territories of Palestinian lands previously granted to the Arab population by these very same resolutions, to add them to their granted territories! In 1973, the Zionists consolidated their presence even much deeper, and between 1978 and 1982, spread their direct occupation over Southern Lebanon and the Western parts of the Beqaa valley. Yes, there are no stable and steady borderlines for "Israel": these borders are only poised to expand and never retreat, they are very flexible; this is why "Israel" was one of the fewest countries in the world never having a pure constitution, in order to never being forced to officially draw its borderlines like any normal country; "Israel" is also one of the fewest countries insisting on never announcing its official borders, passing over this issue by talking about "Political borders", "Security borders", "Historical  Biblical borders" or "International borders".

Transportation roads, trading convoys, emigration activities, pilgrimage and war conquests frequently crossed this river through both its banks, as a natural watercourse based in a single country. Even a small town like Teberiya located in NorthEastern Palestine was the capital or the base of Jordans district during many long periods of time.
