 August 9
It was reported that an Israeli explosive blew up by a herd of goats, owned by the herdsman Malek Mohammad Ismail, at the outskirts of Zawtar Al-Sharqieh village, slaughtering 40 goats. Ismail and his son were not, miraculously, injured or killed. 

In Eastern Sector of Southern Lebanon, an Israeli tank, Mirkava, fired, at night, eight cluster shells at Mimess area. This was followed, also, by firing fifteen mortar missiles at Bir Al-Azhr heights, surroundings of Ain Fjour Farm and Mimess area. 

However, the security sources reported that one missile of "unknown source" struck the SOS village orphanage in the village of Sfarai, wounding one woman and two children. 

The security sources added that the missile looks like the bombs put by the Israeli forces to kill the civilians inside the occupied zone. 

In this context, an Israeli bomb exploded inside Markaba village within the Israeli-occupied zone, killing one woman and her two children.

 August 18
Daily star /August 19-1997 

In the bloodiest day of violence since last year's Israeli onslaught, nine Lebanese civilians were killed and up to 41 wounded, catapulting the south once again to the center stage of the Middle East conflict. 

The first shell exploded behind the Hammoud hospital in the Sitt Nafiseh neighborhood at 2:15 pm destroying several cars in a nearby car park. 

The second, five minutes later, fell in the Hay al-Zouhour neighborhood, killing a baby, Rami Khattab, and wounding six people. 

Five people were killed when a third round crashed into al Bawabeh Al-Fawqa near the sea castle at 2:45 pm. 

The dead included Ramzi Youssef Youssef, a Syrian kaak seller who was decapitated in the blast, and Hanan Basheer, whose husband, Mohammed Rahal, suffered serious wounds. Their month-old baby lost his right foot. 

The other three dead were identified as Ramez Abu-Hamdan, Mahmoud Sleem and Faten Aziz. 

The remaining shells landed on Martyrs Square, the Daher petrol station and Riad Solh street, damaging cars and shattering windows in several offices. 

Doctors and nurses struggled to find space for the wounded as dozens of anxious relatives converged on the hospital. 

Speaking from his hospital bed, his arms and legs swathed in blood-soaked bandages, Safi Gamlouche, 37, said he heard two explosions and decided to close his jewellery shop, located in a narrow alley behind Riad al-Solh street. 

"I heard two shells explode some distance away and I was not expecting any near me. But then a third shell landed a few metres from me as I was closing the shop," he said. 

The shell that injured him and two other civilians appeared to have been designed to explode in the air a few metres above its target. Airburst shells are intended to inflict maximum casualties among personnel rather than destroy buildings or vehicles and were used to deadly effect in the Qana massacre last year. 

Seventy-year-old Ibrahim Shaaban, the concierge of a building in Serail street, was leaving the building as the shelling began. 

"After the first explosion I tried to get back inside but was caught by the second shell,"he said. The left side of his face was soaked with blood and a relative explained that the sound of the explosion had burst his ear drum. 

The streets emptied during the bombardment as panic-stricken residents sought shelter in their homes. Dozens of car fled the city and headed towards Beirut. 

Villages east of Sidon, including Joun and Kfar Milki, were also targeted by the SLA. 

Hana Salban, 28, was in the kitchen of her house in Kfar Hatta, when a shell exploded nearby. She suffered shrapnel wounds. 

Several houses were damaged in Kfar Milki and shelling sparked brush fires in nearby valleys. Joun was hit by ten shells but no casualties were reported. 

On the other hand, the Israeli aggression did not confine on Saida city, but it spread to another parts of South and Western Beqaa. 

The Israeli-occupied Zaffata and Dabsheh posts fired Zahrani River, the eastern heights of Nabatieh Al-Fawqa and Kfar Romman and the perimeters of deserted Lebanese Army barracks of Nabatieh with several shells. This was accompanied by heavy machine gun rounds. 

The Israeli heavy shelling, also, extended to the highway of Kfar Fila-Jibaa and Kafra valley where a number of fires flared up.
