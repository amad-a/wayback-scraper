Let's just get the hell out
 
    "When the IDF* was stuck in Lebanon in 1982 as a non-victor and did not know how to extricate itself, our soldiers sang a macabre tune that began, "Descend from the sky, little plane, take us to Lebanon," and ended "And we will return in a coffin." The rest of the song is not important.The prime ministers who promised us that ""Israel" will enjoy peace for 40 years," "the defense zone will protect our northern communities," etc., are no longer with us. Our defense ministers have come and gone.

Even the enemy has changed. Palestinian "terrorists" have been replaced by the Hizbullah. The "peace treaty" with Lebanon has been replaced by a hair-raising, humiliating "memorandum of understanding" with the Hizbullah that allows them to kill our soldiers but limits the scope of our reactions."

Only one thing has not changed: "Israel" is still stuck in Lebanon - or, to be more precise, Lebanon is stuck in our throat, while our leadership has been suffering from collective paralysis on everything connected to the topic.

"This week, we will be asking the two-decades-old question: What more must happen, how many more soldiers must be killed, how many more families must join the "circle of bereavement," before our leaders can free themselves from their idee fixe on Lebanon and understand that our only option is to get the hell out? Common sense tells us, or should tell us, that we can never win the guerrilla war in South Lebanon."

"First, no army in the world has ever been able to defeat guerrilla fighters. Second, the Hizbullah knows the territory better than even our best soldiers and, as an army with a routine and set procedures, the IDF is more of an observable target than an observer; its actions are more predictable than the Hizbullah's. Third, the fact that the Hizbullah operates within a friendly population gives it an edge and limits our ability to react. Fourth, the South Lebanon Army** has degenerated from an army on our payroll charged with the task of doing our dirty work to a broken-down military machine that is useless without our protection and presence".

"Fifth, our physical presence on Lebanese soil has transformed the Hizbullah in the world's eyes from a terrorist organization to legitimate freedom fighters and has thereby freed Lebanon - and, chiefly, Syria - from responsibility for the bloodshed. "Israel's" presence in South Lebanon is a godsend for Syria, enabling Assad - by means of a war conducted by a "messenger" - to pressure "Israel" to surrender the Golan Heights. Assad has rejected Rabin's and Netanyahu's attempts to initiate negotiations with Syria using a "Lebanon first" agenda. As our deterrent capacity diminishes and our weakness becomes more prominent, "Israel's" presence in Lebanon is playing into his hands".

"Lebanon is Syria's success story: a prosperous country in which law and order have been restored, which provides a livelihood for a million Syrians and which is an integral part of Syria's military superstructure.

"Assad is in no hurry to plunge into a direct military confrontation with "Israel". Right now, he has the best of both worlds: as he sheds our blood, he enjoys the protection of our status as an "occupier."

Assad gets up in the morning without a worry in the world and with the knowledge that his interests are being protected, while we find ourselves going from one funeral to the next. This is an illogical situation. Not only is our departure from Lebanon imperative, it will change the rules of the game that is serving Assad's interests".

"We did not ask for permission to invade Lebanon and we do not need it to leave. We do not need to make our departure conditional upon suitable security arrangements or upon the presence of American or French peacekeepers"

""Israel" must defend itself from within its own borders, like other sovereign countries, without paying a weekly price in blood and without stomping about in the quagmire known as the security zone. The moment we leave and others can no longer use the alibi that we are occupiers, responsibility for future developments will be placed in the hands of Lebanon - and chiefly Syria. The security zone died long ago; it exists only in our imagination."
