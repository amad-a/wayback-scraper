"Israel" begins clearing outposts in occupied southern Lebanon

""Israel" has begun clearing army outposts in south Lebanon in preparation for its full withdrawal from the region in July after two decades of bloody occupation, the army said Tuesday.

In low-key operations overnight, the army removed equipment from two positions inside "Israel's" self-declared "security zone" across the border in Lebanon.

An army spokesman told AFP that communications equipment was dismantled at Tzipporen fort and ammunition removed from Olesh, although the positions near the "Israeli"-Lebanese border had not yet been evacuated.

Security services in southern Lebanon said that for the past three days "Israel" had also been evacuating one of its seven main positions, the Shraiki fort near its military headquarters in the town of Marjeyoun.

Trucks had transported at least 14 prefabricated reinforced concrete shelters back to "Israel", they said.

An AFP correspondent also reported that the army was removing equipment from a fortified position at Parag straddling the "Israeli" border and some members of the artillery unit have left the site.

"The best-selling Hebrew newspaper Yediot Aharonot quoted soldiers packing their bags and equipment containers with unconcealed glee.

"Since it has been decided that we're leaving, then the best thing is to be first, without wasting time," said one member of the elite Golani unit. "More outposts will be evacuated in the coming weeks and I am waiting for my friends also to leave."

The "Israeli" security cabinet began discussions with military chiefs this week on the preparations for their retreat from the occupied zone, an 850 square kilometre (340 square mile) swathe of land covering nearly a tenth of Lebanon.

"Israel" has pledged to withdraw its troops from southern Lebanon by July, ending a 22-year occupation that has cost the lives of hundreds of "Israeli" soldiers, most of them killed in attacks led by Hizbullah.

The government of Prime Minister Ehud Barak said the withdrawal will take place whether or not it has reached a deal with Lebanon and Syria.

Negotiations between "Israel" and Syria broke off in January with both sides at loggerheads over the fate of the strategic Golan Heights plateau which "Israel" occupied in the 1967 Middle East war and which Damascus wants returned as the price of peace.

"Public radio reported Sunday that "Israel" had begun building a new fence along its border with Lebanon, while work has also started on improving "Israel's" defenses in the area.

On a recent visit to the area an AFP reporter noted the building of bunkers, road widening, the laying of communications and the erection of steel nets designed to catch incoming mortar shells and missiles.

An "Israeli" official said the preparatory work also involved introducing large amounts of hi-tech equipment to the area(1)

"Meanwhile, an "Israeli" newspaper reported on Tuesday that the Jewish states Air Force will fully respect Lebanese sovereignty after "Israel's" withdrawal from the occupied zone.
Even in the event of a war or conflict situation, the Yediot Ahronot daily said, "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak has ordered the military not to violate Lebanese territory without instructions from the government.
The announcement, meant to discourage attacks on "Israeli" territory by Hizbullah, was hailed by a high-ranking Lebanese political source.
The great military and psychological defeat that Lebanon caused the enemy as a result of the unity of its people, its resistance and its united position with Syria, is what made the enemy admit defeat and finally submit to UN resolutions, the source said.
The source added that "Israel's" decision reveals its complete surrender to the Lebanese conditions set by President Emile Lahoud in a memorandum to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan.
The first condition was that "Israel" commits itself to the pullout through a written document, which is what it did when it presented a document to the UN stating its intention to implement Resolution 425, he explained.
The conditions that remain, particularly those related to the right of the Palestinian refugees to return to their homeland, have begun to appear in international debate, he added(2)
