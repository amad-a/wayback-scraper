Tenders for Lebanon pullout said imminent(1)
 "After more than a month of delays, the Defense Ministry expects to get the go ahead this week to issue tenders for moving and building new positions and other preparations for an IDF* pullback from Lebanon.

"Defense sources said that Chief of General Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaul Mofaz is currently studying the new plan, dubbed New Horizon, and is expected to approve it in the coming days.

Once the approval is given, the Logistics and Technology Branch adds its recommendations for military installations and the Home Front Command adds its for the civilian settlements. It is then passed on to the Defense Ministry, which is responsible for issuing the tenders.

"[The approval] is supposed to happen very soon, in the next couple of days," said one Defense Ministry source. "We expect work to begin within a week of issuing the tenders."

The tenders will be to dismantle most of the IDF outposts in the security zone, moving others, and building new ones. Roads and other infrastructure are to be built to support the new deployment. A new electronic border fence is also supposed to be erected along the international line.

Tenders will also be issued to complete the "protection package" for border settlements and others in the region. This will entail erecting security fences, lighting, electric gates, and guard posts. Some will have new approach roads paved to them so they are not exposed to the border, which in some cases is a few dozen meters away.

Sources said the list of contractors has already been drawn up and priority will be given to those from the confrontation-line area.

Some NIS 300 million has already been allocated to the Defense Ministry for the initial stages of the project, defense sources said. Senior IDF officials estimate the total cost of the redeployment to be about NIS 1 billion.

The tenders are already over a month behind schedule. Senior defense officials said late last year that, according to the original schedule, tenders should have been issued in January and work begun in February. But military sources said that the IDF was prepared to channel added manpower to the front if it looked like work would not be ready by the declared pullback date.

Over the years the border fence has been moved, sometimes into Lebanon and sometimes into "Israel" along more defensible venues. According to the plan, the new fence will demarcate the exact border. Bypass roads will be abandoned.

The IDF outposts in the security zone will be dismantled and some straddling the international border will have to be moved.

But according to Channel 1, if a unilateral withdrawal is implemented, the IDF will not pull back completely along the international border, but maintain a presence in outposts just over the border.

The reasoning behind this would be to preserve some bargaining chips, but it would not eliminate the motive behind the Hizbullah guerrilla war to oust "Israel" completely from Lebanon, Channel 1 said.

A very senior IDF commander said that the IDF has submitted to the government requests for some changes along what the army calls the "purple line," or international border. These include the line around outposts located at key geographical locations.

Mofaz has said that he still holds fast to his position against a unilateral withdrawal. But because Prime Minister Ehud Barak has already and repeatedly declared that the troops will be out by July 7, Mofaz could find himself being asked not if he agrees with the decision to get out, but if he can carry it out".

"Israel's" Army readies Lebanon psychological-training plan(3)
" In the midst of public doubts over its continued presence in the security zone, the IDF* is drafting a program to mentally prepare soldiers for service in Lebanon.

The program, titled "Making Sense," will be implemented at the Guerrilla Warfare School in the IDF base at Elyakim. It will be integrated in the bush training combat soldiers go through there prior to their deployment in the security zone.

The program is being put together by the IDF's Behavioral Sciences Department and is mainly a framework for commanders to use to hold a discussion and deliver a message.

According to a senior officer familiar with the program, its aim is to help commanders relay to their soldiers an understanding of their mission's realities. As the officer explained, it is based on story telling, leading to a lesson or moral.

"Once the reason for being [in the security zone] was to defend the northern settlements; now they are also being told that their job is to allow the government the breathing space it needs to make decisions. Well, how do you convey that? It's difficult and this program is to help soldiers understand the realities of their mission," he said.

The program is in its final stages of preparation and is expected to be implemented shortly.

The senior officer also took issue with the name-calling of soldiers by senior commanders, who called soldiers "crybabies" who said they were afraid to serve in Lebanon and that the IDF had nothing to do there now that the government declared it was pulling out anyway in the summer.

"I'm against commanders calling soldiers names. They shouldn't pick on soldiers' weaknesses and fears. They need to consolidate them, create a framework to talk about it. You have to speak to a soldier, not call him names," said the officer.

Still, he said the media blew out of proportion the comments made by other commanders, whose style of motivating their troops was seen by some as offensive.

He was referring to Golani Brigade commander Col. Shmuel Zakai, who referred to the Four Mothers as "the Four Rags." He compared him to a coach trying to fire up his players before a big game.

"I very much understand Zakai and what he did. It is the style of some commanders, but he too shouldn't have used name-calling," the senior officer said.

The IDF has not released any studies regarding the impact the recent hostilities and public outcry has had on troops in the security zone. But the senior officer said he does not believe IDF soldiers in general suffered from a lack of motivation.

"It's very simple: "Israeli" youth today are very conformist. They have a very strong concept of security, they still believe there is an existential threat on the state and that war could break out one day," he said.

"But it also is based on individual motives for self-fulfillment. All this talk about collectivism being replaced by individualism is, excuse my French, bullshit. Men have always fought for reasons of individualism and self-glorification."

More "Israelis" back unilateral Lebanon withdrawal: poll(1)
""Israeli" public support for a unilateral troop withdrawal from south Lebanon continues to grow, according to an opinion poll published Friday.

Almost two-thirds of the population, or 61 percent, support a pullout without an agreement with Lebanon or Syria, according to a Gallup poll published in the Maariv newspaper, up from 56 percent previously.

Thirty-one percent are opposed, down from 34 percent previously, according to the survey of 670 people.

Prime Minister Ehud Barak has pledged to bring the troops home by July, even without a peace deal with Syria, but has warned that a unilateral withdrawal could be more bloody.

Calls for a pullout have mounted following the death of seven "Israeli" soldiers this year in guerrilla attacks led by the Hizbullah.

The poll, conducted on Wednesday, the very day Barak lost a crucial first round parliamentary vote on his peace plans with Syria.

But the poll showed "Israelis" split 50-50 on whether they would support a peace agreement in a promised referendum on a return of the occupied Golan Heights to Syria.

And 69 percent said they did not believe there was a chance of the Syrians agreeing to a peace deal that does not include a withdrawal from the Golan to the lines in place before the June 1967 war when "Israel" captured the plateau.

Barak's coalition government suffered a blow on Wednesday when parliament supported a bill that would make it harder to win public support for a Golan handover, by requiring a majority of all registered voters rather than just those who vote.

The Gallup poll carried a margin of error of 4.5 percent".

"Israel's" human sandbags see the writing on the wall(2)
Nicholas Blanford concludes his report on the crumbling morale of Antoine Lahds renegade force as the military withdrawal pledged by Ehud Barak draws ever nearer

"The fate of the militiamen serving with the SLA* could differ drastically depending on whether "Israel" withdraws unilaterally or as part of an agreement with Lebanon and Syria.
Despite assurances from the "Israelis" that the SLA will not be abandoned in the event of a unilateral troop pullout, the question of what to do with the estimated 1,500 militiamen is posing a considerable dilemma for the "Israeli" government, military sources say.
On the other hand, a coordinated withdrawal should lead to an organized dismantling of the SLA and the deployment of peacekeeping forces into the former occupation zone. The militiamen will be given the option of leaving the country or remaining behind, probably to face minimal terms of imprisonment.
In the event of a unilateral withdrawal, the 700 to 1,000 "Israeli" troops remaining in the zone could probably pull out of Lebanon in the space of two or three days at most, security sources say. The retreating soldiers would likely receive constant air support as well as a blanket artillery bombardment north of the zone to provide covering fire against any last offensive by the resistance.
The "Israeli" Army would then take up positions along the frontier while Barak repeats his warning that any cross-border attacks will meet with severe reprisals.
The spanner in the works of this otherwise relatively straightforward scheme is the fate of the SLA.
It would be politically unacceptable for "Israel" to simply abandon their allies after more than two decades of cooperation and inter-dependence. Many "Israelis", including members of the government such as Deputy Defense Minister Ephraim Sneh, insist that the Jewish state owes the SLA some degree of protection.
If it hadnt been for the SLA, the "Israelis" would have left a long time ago, said a teacher from the Maronite village of Rmeish in the western sector. The "Israelis" have a guilt complex about how to deal with them after a withdrawal. Every time Barak says hes going to leave, the "Israelis" meet the SLA to calm them down.
The SLA mans the frontline posts on the edge of the zone while most "Israeli" positions are to the rear along the border. As such the SLA have been described as "Israel's" sandbags, the first layer of defense against the resistance. If the order for a unilateral withdrawal is given, then the logistics of disbanding this first layer of defense while still under fire from the resistance would be formidable.
The "Israelis" would have to arrange the removal from Lebanon not only of those militiamen who wish to leave the country but their families as well. Furthermore, assuming that the Lebanese Army and UNIFIL are not deployed in the areas unilaterally vacated by the "Israelis", the resulting security vacuum could prompt many militiamen, who would have stayed in the event of a coordinated withdrawal, to abandon their homes as well.
A Western military source said the fate of the SLA in the event of a unilateral withdrawal is one of the key unanswered questions.
The "Israelis" keep telling the SLA not to worry, but I dont think they know what to do with them, the source said.
To defer this hazardous and huge logistical undertaking, it has been speculated that the "Israelis" will create a temporary solution for the militia by deploying them in a buffer strip along the frontier.
Before he was assassinated in January, Aql Hachem, the imposing SLA commander of the western sector, was touted as a possible leader for the post-withdrawal buffer force.
Antoine Lahd, the former army general who commands the SLA, has to all intents and purposes quit the militia. Although no formal announcement has been made ­ nor will be made given the likelihood that the militia will be disbanded in the near future ­ Lahd is a leader in name only, spending more and more time with his family in France.
With or without Lahd, the prospect of a continued SLA presence is welcomed by some of the more resolute supporters of the militia. Of course, this is the best solution, said Elias, a resident of Qlayaa who left the militia three months ago after 15 years service but still retains close links.
We have the will to continue but it would be difficult unless the "Israelis" supplied us with weapons.
Even with the support of the "Israelis", however, an SLA buffer force is an impractical solution. Seventy percent of SLA personnel are Shiite and Druze and most would probably abandon the militia the moment the "Israelis" retreat to the border.
Even Lahd has said that if theres a unilateral withdrawal then the Shiites in the militia will drop their arms because they dont really feel theyre members of the SLA, said the teacher from Rmeish. Without an enormous influx of new recruits, the SLA would have considerable difficulties manning a zone which ­ to accommodate "Israel's" security needs ­ would have to stretch from Naqoura on the coast all the way to the tri-border area with Syria and "Israel", a distance of 73 kilometers.
The demography of the border area also would hamper an efficient defense of the zone. Much of the strip is composed of pockets of Christian villages ­ such as Rmeish and Ain Ibl in the western sector and Marjeyoun and Qlayaa in the eastern sector ­ surrounded by Shiite villages.
Even in the unlikely event that all the Christians opted to remain in the militia, their strongholds would be surrounded by an unsympathetic and potentially hostile population of Shiites.
It doesnt make sense to leave the SLA behind as a fighting force, said the Western military source. There are more than 1,000 Shiites in the militia. Theyll all resign when the "Israelis" leave. This scheme of a buffer zone is just wishful thinking from some of the old (Saad) Haddad crowd.
Another suggestion being aired is the establishment of a cordon sanitaire along the border manned by UNIFIL peacekeepers and possibly French troops as an interim measure until peace is concluded.
I think the "Israelis" want this buffer zone. It would be to protect the residents in the south as much as the settlements in "Israel", said Michel, a militiaman from Rmeish.
Ali, a Shiite former militiaman from Aita Shaab, also believed that a multinational force would have to be deployed in the south if the "Israelis" withdraw unilaterally like Kosovo or East Timor.
However, UNIFIL, and any other foreign force, can only deploy in the vacated area at the request of the government. Most observers believe that if "Israel" withdraws unilaterally, UNIFIL and the Lebanese Army will remain in their present positions north of the occupation zone. The zone will remain a security vacuum until a comprehensive peace deal is concluded.
UNIFIL spokesman Timur Goksel admitted that the role of the peacekeepers following a unilateral withdrawal remained unclear.
This is a pertinent question that many people would like answered, but no-one seems to know. Nobody is talking about it, he said.
If Ehud Barak achieves his goal of securing a troop pullout within the framework of an agreement with Lebanon and Syria, the prospects for the former occupation zone are considerably brighter. With the goodwill of all parties, it should not be difficult to arrange the replacement of "Israeli" troops with UNIFIL peacekeepers, French troops and the Lebanese Army.
The presence of foreign peacekeeping forces may encourage many junior ranking members of the disbanded SLA to stay in Lebanon. Although the government refuses to offer any guarantees of immunity from prosecution for SLA members, it is likely that most militiamen ­ those who were pressganged or joined to earn a living ­ will receive a token punishment.
This could fall along similar lines to the prison sentences handed down to the scores of SLA men who turned themselves in to the authorities following the militias retreat from the Jezzine enclave in June.

"Israeli" Cabinet backs plan for July troop withdrawal
Increasing pressure is being applied on all sides for a negotiated withdrawal  (M.O.)

"An end to "Israel's" 22-year occupation of the south drew a step closer on Sunday with the "Israeli" Cabinet voting unanimously in favor of Premier Ehud Baraks proposal to withdraw the troops by July.
Prime Minister Salim Hoss welcomed "Israel's" decision but said he would prefer to see a pullout accompanied by a comprehensive peace settlement.
The "Israeli" Army will deploy on the border with Lebanon by July 2000 and from there will ensure the safety of the northern towns and villages, "Israeli" Cabinet secretary Yitzhak Herzog said after the meeting. The government will act to ensure that this deployment will be carried out in the framework of an agreement.
If no agreement was forthcoming by July, Herzog said the Cabinet would meet again to weigh its options, suggesting the July deadline would still stand.
In the event that conditions arent conducive to an Israeli redeployment in the framework of an agreement, the government will convene at an appropriate time to discuss the method of implementing Cabinet decisions, the statement said.
The announcement came at the end of a two-session debate on consecutive Sundays in which "Israeli" ministers discussed the occupation of the south.
Hoss welcomed the "Israeli" Cabinet vote but voiced suspicion over "Israel's" intentions if a unilateral withdrawal was carried out.
Whether or not the "Israeli" Cabinets withdrawal decision is a mere ploy, we still welcome an "Israeli" pullout from Lebanon that takes place in accordance with UN Resolution 425, at any time, Hoss said.
We would have preferred that such a withdrawal be implemented within a peace agreement. But we do not trust "Israel's" intentions if a unilateral pullout does take place.
In any case we welcome any withdrawal that liberates our land and reinstates the lifeline with our brethren in southern Lebanon. We have demanded a pullout as long as 22 years ago, when UN Security Council Resolution 425 called on "Israel" to withdraw unconditionally from Lebanon to the internationally-recognized border.

Two militiamen killed, one wounded in IR operations
At least two members of the Israeli occupation's proxy militia were killed and a third was seriously wounded in two Islamic Resistance (IR) operations against the occupation's outposts in Houla and al-Hardoun Tuesday morning.

Near the borders with occupied Palestine, IR's squad of Martyrs Ahmed Fouani and Jihad Hijazi at 9:00 a.m. Tuesday detonated a powerful roadside bomb against a Lahdi patrol that was moving along the Houla road. The militiamen in the patrol were killed or wounded, an IR communiqué read.
Earlier, IR's squad of Martyr Khalil Saad and Radwan Rasheed ambushed at 7:40 a.m. Tuesday another Lahdi patrol at the entrance of al-Hardoun outpost.

IR men peppered the militiamen with machine-gun fire, an IR communiqué said.

In the meantime, squad of Martyrs Ali Harmoush and Mohammed Faqih of IR's Artillery Reinforcement Unit (ARU) pounded the Hardoun outpost and barred it from backing the targeted patrol, while squad of Martyrs Moussa Salman and Hassan Salman paralyzed the al-Bayyada outpost and squad of Martyrs Wael Anani and Mahmoud Srour dealt with the Shamaa outpost scoring hits in its ramparts and fortifications. The confrontations went on for over two hours, the communiqué added.

Moreover, squad of Martyrs Ahmed Shamas and Ali Khalil shelled in a violent and concentrated manner a Lahdi force that tried to inspect the ambush site in al-Hardoun.
At dawn, squad of Martyrs Khodor al-Hajj Ali and Moussa Hamdan attacked the Beaufort outpost with rocket propelled grenades following IR's operations at night that had targeted the occupation's outposts in Baraasheet and Haddatha.

Security sources in the occupied zone affirmed that IR's operations killed three militiamen in Houla and al-Hardoun while the occupation's radio acknowledged that one agent was killed in Houla and another in al-Hardoun adding that a third was seriously wounded in Hardoun.(1)

For its part, Future TV reported that the two were to be reported in southern Lebanon since the Israeli cabinet decided to pullout occupation forces by July with or without a peace treaty. Thirteen militiamen have been killed so far this year. The fate of the militia, which is being trained, financed and controlled by Israel is up in the air. Israel's high court of justice late yesterday rejected a request by three militiamen for asylum. Israeli lawyers representing the men denounced the decision, saying their clients faced reprisals from the Lebanese authorities as well as Hizbollah resistance fighters in the event of an Israeli pullout. The Israeli cabinet has promised to guarantee the safety of the roughly 2,500 members of the militia but gave no details. Press reports have alleged for months that countries such as Australia, Canada and Turkey would grant political asylum to militiamen, particularly senior officers. But Hizbollah has urged these states not to take the Israeli-backed and financed troops, who are responsible for numerous massacres against Lebanese civilians. Hizbollah also declared that its offer to pardon repentant collaborators no longer stands after Israel's decision to pullout. Hezbollah had offered to pardon collaborators if they surrendered before the announcement of an Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon, in an attempt to encourage desertions among crumbling militia ranks. The militia has been deteriorating, with many defecting to government-held territories. Israeli allied militiamen collaborating with Israel face military court trials in Beirut. The head of the militia, Antoine Lahd, has been sentenced to death in absentia by a Beirut military court.(2)

March Friday 11 -2000:

Israel against taking in proxy militia after promised Lebanon pullout  (1)
Israel is strongly opposed to granting asylum to members of its proxy militia and their families following the pullout from south Lebanon which it has pledged to conduct by July, Deputy Defence Minister Efraim Sneh said Friday.

"Bringing the members of the israeli-proxy militia and their families to Israel and transforming them into refugees is no solution either for those who come here or for those who stay in Lebanon," said Sneh, who is a key confidant of Prime Minister Ehud Barak.

"We are not talking about a handful of collaborators here but a force of 3,000 men who is drawn from a community of 150,000 Christians, Shiites and Druzes in south Lebanon which has been allied with Israel for more than 20 years," he told public radio.

Sneh warned that Israel "will react if the militia members are the victims of revenge attacks by Hezbollah," without elaborating on the nature of the reprisals he was threatening.

The israeli high court of justice Monday rejected a request for asylum from three militiamen.

The israeli lawyer representing the three, Zvi Rish, said he was "disappointed" by the court's decision and called on the israeli authorities to "grant asylum to the members of the militia."

Rish argued that his clients faced reprisals from the Lebanese authorities as well as Hezbollah guerrillas in the event of the withdrawal from south Lebanon which the israeli cabinet agreed early last week to carry out by July with or without a peace deal with Beirut and Damascus.

The Beirut government insists that all israeli`s allied militiamen and other residents of the border zone accused of "collaboration with the enemy" be prosecuted.

The israeli cabinet has promised to guarantee the safety of the roughly 2,500 members of the militia but given no details.

israeli Transport Minister Yitzkak Mordechai called on fellow ministers to "meet urgently to agree contingency plans for granting asylum to our allies in the militia."

Press reports have alleged for months that countries such as Australia, Canada, France or Turkey would grant political asylum to militiamen, particularly senior officers, if they request it.

Two collaborators were killed in separate attacks by Hezbollah guerrillas on Tuesday, bringing the militia's losses so far this year to 13, according to an AFP tally.
