Barak threatens end to talks unless Syrians restrain pace of attacks in the south(2)
"Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak declared on Monday that the peace talks with Syria will be suspended unless Damascus halts Hizbullahs attacks on "Israeli" forces in the south.
The stern ultimatum came hours after Hizbullah fighters killed three "Israeli" soldiers, including an officer, and wounded four others, one seriously, in an ambush beside the Izzieh outpost in the central sector.
"Israel" will not be able to negotiate peace as long as the Syrians dont restrain the Hizbullah from acting against the "Israeli" Army in the security zone, Barak said in a statement following an urgent session of his security Cabinet on Monday night.
The absence of a climate of peace will make peace talks with Syria difficult, the statement added.
The fatal ambush came less than 24 hours after Hizbullah guerrillas blew up Aql Hachem, a senior "Israeli" allied militia commander, outside his house in Dibel. Last Tuesday, an "Israeli" soldier was killed by an anti-tank missile at the radar station in Bayada.
Our boys are killed with their (the Syrians) permission. This pattern is unacceptable, said Deputy Defense Minister Ephraim Sneh.
The ministers attending the Cabinet meeting were given a series of retaliatory options by senior military personnel, including the chief of staff, General Shaul Mofaz. Among the options was targeting Lebanons infrastructure in a series of raids similar to the air strikes last June in which power stations and bridges were destroyed.
However, as in similar situations in the past, "Israel's" military options are limited. Hizbullahs ambush fell within the terms of the April Understanding, the loose set of rules that governs the conflict in the south and is designed to protect civilians.
In such circumstances, the option of destroying bridges or power stations in Lebanon is an unacceptable response. Retaliation is theoretically reserved by the "Israelis" for Hizbullahs cross-border Katyusha rocket attacks into northern Galilee.
Barak described the violence in the south as a grave intensification  that "Israel" will not allow to continue. We have known in the past and we still know to strike at the place and time of our choosing.
Baraks bellicose language paraphrased that of his predecessor, Benjamin Netanyahu, who frequently vowed revenge for military setbacks in the occupation zone or cross-border rocket attacks. Yet Netanyahu failed to fulfill his threats until last June when, with only 10 days left in office, he ordered the air raids against infrastructure targets.
The problem that Netanyahu faced in the past in finding an appropriate and justifiable military response is the same facing Barak today: the lack of credible Hizbullah targets.
The secrecy that cloaks Hizbullahs movements presents the "Israeli" Army with few, if any, available targets. This has long been one of the most frustrating elements of the conflict for the "Israelis".
In the absence of the military option, Barak appears to have played the diplomatic card.
A Western observer predicted that Syria would not yield to Baraks ultimatum.
I dont believe hes serious, the observer said. Barak knows that Syria wont go for it, especially as he has made the announcement publicly rather than through private channels.
The observer said that if Barak intends to stand by his ultimatum, then the peace talks are over.
Syrian-"Israeli" peace talks have remained frozen since a second round in the US which ended on Jan. 10 with no tangible results. The third round, scheduled for Jan. 19, was postponed indefinitely.
It is believed that before peace talks restarted between Syria and "Israel" in December, Barak made no serious attempt to press Damascus for quiet in the south as a condition for the talks to resume.
During earlier peace talks between the two sides, Hizbullah continued to attack "Israeli" forces in south Lebanon. "Israel's" seven-day air and artillery blitz in July 1993 came while "Israel" and Syria were still negotiating.
Communications Minister Benjamin bin Eliezer, who is close to Barak and took part in the Cabinet meeting, said he did not think suspending talks would be helpful. I doubt that suspending talks would make much of an impression on the Syrians or bring about changes on the ground in southern Lebanon, he told Channel One television.
The deadly ambush was a significant achievement for Hizbullah, which in the space of a week has reversed several months of declining fortunes.
Observers noted that the ambush occurred in an area not usually prone to resistance assaults. This could explain why the "Israeli" patrol was in an exposed position given that soldiers are now rarely seen in the open in broad daylight. The ambush also suggests that Hizbullah had acquired good intelligence to set up the assault at the opportune moment.
Shortly before the ambush, Hizbullah fighters mounted a concerted effort to overrun the Sojod outpost in the northern sector. The fighters came close to scaling the ramparts, forcing the "Israelis" to drop artillery rounds within 100 meters of the post, security sources said.
Observers believe that Syria has raised no objections to the Hizbullah attacks, in which
the resistance is inflicting mounting casualties against "Israeli" forces, so long as they do not spill over into northern "Israel", thereby jeopardizing the peace talks.


Deadly ambush catches enemy patrol by surprise in safest part of the zone(3)
Mondays deadly ambush staged by Hizbullah guerrillas occurred beside a position that "Israeli" officers long considered one of the safest postings in south Lebanon.
The Izzieh outpost, located on a conical hill above the village of Deir Mimes, is very rarely attacked, even by long-range mortar fire. Traditionally, the posts safety and its proximity to the border with "Israel", 2 kilometers away, made the post a popular viewing point for "Israeli" officials visiting the zone.
From accounts by Hizbullah and local residents as well as the geography of the area, it would appear that the two resistance squads that staged the ambush were hidden in dense undergrowth on a ridge just east of the village of Yohmor overlooking the 200-meter deep Litani River Gorge. The Izzieh outpost lies on the other side of the Litani in clear view of the guerrillas, 1 kilometer away.
The ambush was carefully coordinated. From the footage of the ambush broadcast by Al-Manar television, the "Israeli" patrol was walking near the Izzieh position at 11.15am when Hizbullah unleashed at least three Sagger anti-tank rockets at the soldiers.
Moments later, Hizbullah mortar teams began pounding the Izzieh post, as well as the "Israeli"-manned Beaufort Castle, 1 kilometer north, and the Dabsheh outpost overlooking Nabatieh, 8 kilometers north.
Residents in Yohmor said they heard three anti-tank missiles being fired by Hizbullah and the sound of the explosions seconds later.
The "Israelis" began shelling the area around Yohmor in an effort to hit the resistance squads as they stole away from the scene of the ambush.
Three 155mm shells exploded in a row less than 10 meters from Rabia Allaqs house.
When I heard the shells, I began screaming to my neighbor for help, she said. I thought my house would fall on my head. It was very frightening.
Another resident of Yohmor, Linda Qassem, 26, fainted when she heard the explosions as the ambush was launched. She was admitted to hospital in nearby Nabatieh before being sent home. Ive always been afraid of shelling, she said quietly, this time more than usual.
Several phosphorous rounds were fired at the outskirts of the village and "Israeli" warplanes raided the outskirts of Khorbet Selem and Shoukine opposite the central sector, but no casualties were reported. At least one building was damaged in an air raid on Ain Bousoir in Iqleem al-Toffah.
Allaq said that she rejoiced at the news that three "Israeli" soldiers had been killed.
After all the frightening things that the "Israeli" put us through every day, she said, it was right that the resistance was able to kill some "Israeli" soldiers.
Wajih Barakat, a shopkeeper in the village, said he feared the "Israeli" proxy militiamen occupying neighboring Arnoun would blockade the single road leading out of Yohmor.
They might put up a checkpoint at Arnoun and stop us from leaving, he said.

Paralyzed against Hizbullah, "Israel" must restrain its allied militia
News analysis

Hizbullahs recent deadly offensive, which in the space of 10 days has left four "Israeli" soldiers and two SLA* militiamen dead, including senior SLA commander Aql Hachem, is raising fears that the South Lebanon Army may ignore "Israel's" policy of restraint and retaliate unilaterally.
The death of another militiaman in a roadside bomb blast on Wednesday night near the Baraasheit compound will have done little to ease the SLAs anger over the lethal operations.
"Israel" radio reported on Thursday that the militia is increasing pressure on the "Israeli" Army to respond to Hizbullahs attacks. The radio said that the SLA fears its activities could be undermined if "Israel" continues to react with caution.
The problem facing "Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak in the wake of Hizbullahs deadly attacks is one that used to haunt his predecessors in similar circumstances: Hizbullah acted within the rules. The targets were military, away from populated areas and the fighting did not spill across the border to threaten civilians in "Israel".
Consequently, Barak appears to believe that the option of retaliating against Lebanons infrastructure ­ as reportedly recommended by the "Israeli" Army ­ is unwarranted. The unwritten rules of the conflict dictate that this level of response could only be condoned if Hizbullah staged an unjustified cross-border rocket attack.
In the present circumstances, bombing Lebanons infrastructure would not only receive the opprobrium of the international community, including the United States, but more pertinently would grant Hizbullah legitimacy to launch salvos of Katyusha rockets into "Israel".
