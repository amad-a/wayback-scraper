Military Operations

July Friday 14 -2000:


Hizbullah sets up observation posts on Lebanon's border with Israel(1)

 Hizbullah has set up some 20 observation posts on Lebanon's border with Israel, an AFP correspondent observed Thursday, as it tries to step up control over border traffic.

Hizbullah, which spearheaded Lebanon's armed resistance to Israels 22-year occupation, has put up posts along the barbed wire stretching from the coastal town of Naqoura to the outskirts of Kfar Shouba on the western flank of Mount Hermon, near Syria, the correspondent saw.

The group, according to a Lebanese security official, is looking to control entries and departures at Naqoura, the UN-operated border post that is the only remaining crossing between Lebanon and Israel.

Hizbullah wants both to stop any potential Israeli infiltrators from entering Lebanon and block anyone who collaborated with Israeli occupation from leaving, the official said.

"Hizbullah is apparently looking to avoid any possible movement at the border," the official said.

The Hizbullah fighters, organized in small groups of three or four and armed with assault rifles, set themselves up at former points of passage between the two countries or in posts left behind by the Israelis.

The Hizbullah members patrol the area with motorcycles and automobiles and keep an eye on anyone looking to cross at the border posts, which have been closed since the end of Israels withdrawal May 24.

In Naqoura, Hizbullah set itself up in two former seaside resorts held by Israels proxy militia in the area.

Hizbullah launched the measures the day after five Lebanese left the former occupied zone for Israel, the first known to leave since Israels withdrawal, when 6,000 former militia members and their family members headed south.

July Tuesday 18 -2000:

Hizbullah increases presence at Lebanon-Israel border(1)

 Hizbullah, which spearheaded armed resistance against Israeli occupation of southern Lebanon, bolstered four of its positions Monday along the border with Israel, an AFP correspondent observed.

Using tractors, the militant group put up soil embankments five to six meters (16 to 20 feet) wide and two meters (six and a half feet) high, along with blocks of reinforced concrete and battered containers at four positions along 15 kilometers (nine miles) of the eastern border section.

Hizbullah fighters have also stepped up motorcycle patrols of the border zone.

Hizbullah, which takes credit for forcing Israel to end its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon in May, put up 20 posts last week from the Mediterranean coast to Mount Hermon near Syria.

The moves come as UN troops wait to deploy into the area until Israel rectifies alleged violations of the border. Lebanon has also refused UN calls to send its army into parts of the southern zone where Hizbullah has set up.

Hizbullah, founded in the 1980s, enjoys wide support in Beirut and in Syria.

1-       AFP, 17-7-2000.

July Wednesday 19 -2000:


Hizbullah is patrolling the border (1)

Armed Hizbullah men clad in army fatigues and riding motorcycles were again patrolling the border on Tuesday.
Were patrolling and surveying their (Israeli) movements, said a Hizbullah guerrilla, staring at an Israeli patrol just a few meters away.
The group had begun fortifying several posts near the border on Monday. Cement blocks were put up on hills located in the eastern sector of the border zone specifically near Kfar Shouba.
Lebanese security sources said Hizbullah fortified four of its positions along a 15-kilometer stretch of the barbed-wire border fence in the past two days. Hizbullah men also pitched a tent and flew their yellow flag near an area which overlooks the settlement of Metulla to serve as an observation point.
Hizbullah has set up some 20 observation posts on Lebanons border with Israel since last week. The movement took the measures after five Lebanese civilians quit the former occupied zone for Israel on July 11. They were the first known to have left since Israels withdrawal on May 24.
According to Hizbullah sources, the guerrillas are patrolling the area with motorcycles to keep an eye on anyone looking to cross at the border into Israel.
The government has pledged to send 1,000 troops and Internal Security Forces personnel to take charge of the former occupation zone after the UN troop deployment.
