"Israeli" agents withdraw from Kfar Houna outpost(1)
The "Israelis" have once more tightened the northern perimeters of the occupation zone by pulling back the SLA militia from the Kfar Houne outpost to a new frontline 4 kilometers further south at Aramta.
The isolated outpost, located 1 kilometer south of Kfar Houna, was dynamited by militiamen on Wednesday night. The militia began redeploying from the outpost about a month ago when its civilian responsibilities were transferred to Aramta.
The Kfar Houna position was the most northerly of the six entry points into the occupation zone. Visitors were required to check in with the militia at Kfar Houna and replace their Lebanese license plates with the red and white tags issued by the SLA to all vehicles in the zone. A new checkpoint has been established at Aramta for this purpose.
The redeployment was viewed as necessary due to the vulnerability of the exposed Kfar Houna position to resistance attacks when it became part of the frontline following the SLAs southward retreat from Jezzine seven months ago.
Between January 1998 and the Jezzine withdrawal last June, only one militiaman was killed in the sporadic resistance attacks on Kfar Houna.
Since then, four militiamen have died, all in roadside bomb ambushes. The pullback has also extended the no-mans land between the zone and the first Lebanese Army checkpoints at Bater and Kfar Falous near Jezzine to around 24 kilometers.
The only remnants of the outpost were light poles and barbed wires that surrounded it.
Many people who usually travel through this area expressed their pleasure over the SLAs retreat. Hassan Kheir, from Hasbaya, said he was shocked to see the abandoned outpost when he passed by Thursday morning. I hope the situation becomes calmer in this area now, he said.
Although the road directly overlooking the outpost is still blocked by barricades, travelers said they hoped the Lebanese Army would remove them and clear the road of land mines.
If that were done, the journey between Hasbaya and Jezzine would take 15 minutes. It now takes motorists one hour to travel that distance, and they do so while risking being shot at.

" The Kfar Houna post lays deserted. Hizbullahs mortar fire against outposts is heavier and more accurate. Mortar shelling this week alone has wounded three "Israeli" soldiers in Taibeh and three allied militiamen in Baraacheit.

After months of reversals, Hizbullah needed some successes to boost morale among the fighters and to prove that the resistance can still reach "Israeli" troops even if they do spend most of their time sitting tight in their hill-top bunkers.
"With five months left before Baraks July deadline for a withdrawal, Hizbullah will likely maintain pressure on the occupation forces. Resistance takes out top "Israeli" agent stalwart
Hizbullah bomb team carries out Gods sentence
"Aql Hachem, the South Lebanon Armys* top commander in the western sector of the occupation zone and one of the militias most resolute leaders, was killed in a massive bomb blast on the edge of his home village of Dibel on Sunday ".
Hizbullah announced that it had carried out Gods sentence on Hachem as part of the resistances determined style to carry on the struggle until the occupation is over.
A statement from Hizbullah said that a special-forces squad planted several bombs near Dibel, a small village 5 kilometers west of Bint Jbeil.
A source with Hizbullah said that the operation had taken several weeks to plan and that Hachem had been placed under close observation. His movements and habits were studied before a plan of action was drawn up, the source said.
Hizbullah broadcasts a video tape of the assassination on Al-Manar TV. From the footage and local sources in the occupation zone, it appears that the bombs were planted at the entrance of the driveway to Hachems house on the eastern edge of Dibel. The resistance squad probably penetrated the zone via a wadi running between Yater and Rshaf toward Dibel.
The squad lay in wait until Hachem left his home. The footage shows Hachem with his dog, accompanied by two other people who entered a Hummer all-terrain vehicle. Hachem climbed into his white Mercedes car and the bombs exploded moments later in a huge billowing cloud of smoke which covered the vehicle. Hachem was killed instantly in the blast. After the explosion, Hizbullah mortar teams north of the zone laid down covering fire on nearby SLA outposts in Rshaf, Jamoussa, Qawzah and Hemayed allowing the bomb squad to retreat from the zone.
Hizbullah urged the remaining militiamen to defect or else death will be their certain fate.
Security sources said that SLA commander Antoine Lahd was flown to Dibel in an "Israeli" helicopter to pay condolences to Hachems family.
The operation was Hizbullahs most successful in months. In Beirut, Tyre and Sidon, Hizbullah supporters handed out sweets to passing motorists to celebrate the attack. The death of Hachem, who was widely touted as a potential successor to SLA commander Antoine Lahd, is a severe blow for the militia, which is already facing an uncertain future as the occupation enters its final months.
Hachem, born in Dibel in 1952, was a sergeant in the Lebanese Army before joing Saad Haddads Army of Free Lebanon in 1976. When Haddad died and Lahd was appointed as commander of the SLA in 1985, Hachem was promoted to colonel and given the command of the western sector. Two years later, he was placed in charge of the militias intelligence wing in the western sector and tasked with recruiting agents.
Hachem, who was fluent in Hebrew, was one of three colonels who served directly beneath Lahd. The other two are Karamallah Said, the nominal deputy commander of the militia, and Nabih Rifai, the commander of the eastern sector.
Hachem was widely liked by the militiamen of the western brigade, which is split equally between Shiites and Christians.
"His popularity, the fact he was trusted by the "Israelis" and a persistent rumor that he would replace Lahd should the militia commander resign, sparked resentment from his colleagues, particularly Rifai and other senior officials in the eastern sector, according to sources in the zone. Hachem is expected to be replaced by his deputy, Fares Qassis from Alma Shaab, also a veteran member of the militia"(1)

Commander's death is a nail in SLA coffin(4)
" The Hizbullah's stunning killing of Aql Hachem is expected to make a strong impact on the operational abilities of the South Lebanon Army, but it is expected to damage morale all the more.
Hachem was known as "Cat" to his men because of Hizbullah's many failed attempts on his life, but his death, caused by a well-timed roadside bomb that killed him near his home, may be one of the last nails in the SLA's coffin. If it could happen to the "Cat," it could happen to anyone.
With his broad smile and expansive self-confidence, Hachem seemed a permanent fixture in the scenery of South Lebanon and its bloody war.
"He had seen two SLA commanders, six commanders of the Northern Command and nine IDF battalion commanders come and go, as well as countless presidents and prime ministers in Lebanon and "Israel". He had seen the enemy change from Palestinians to Shi'ites. Only Hachem and his massive presence remained on through it all as commander of the western battalion and head of the security apparatus in the area.
"Hachem, 47, was born in the village of Dibel in South Lebanon to a family of farmers. He joined the Lebanese army in 1970. He served in a variety of positions in the infantry and was later stationed in the intelligence section. When the civil war broke out in 1976, Hachem set out at the head of a force of 600 men from South Lebanon to defend their families at home. The Lebanese army appointed Major Saad Hadad as the supreme commander of South Lebanon.
"In 1979 Hadad retired and, supported by his men, announced the establishment of the "Free Lebanese Army," later to become the SLA and strongly allied with "Israel" and the IDF. From 1985 until yesterday, Hachem served as the commander of the western battalion and head of the security apparatus. In fact, Hachem was the longest-serving and most experienced soldier in the SLA. He was apparently the designated successor of General Antoine Lahad after his upcoming retirement.
"Much of the deep admiration Hachem aroused among the local population stemmed from his close involvement in civilian affairs. "The SLA is involved in everything," he explained in an extensive interview in Ha'aretz Magazine last October. "I have to be a bit of a social worker with all the people in my region. If there is a problem between two neighbors in a village, who can they go to? Lebanese law isn't really enforced here. So they come to me to work out a compromise between them."
Hachem did not hesitate to air his strong opinions against the Grapes of Wrath understandings and "Israeli" policies in South Lebanon in general.
"I think that if an SLA position is shot at, we should send some serious fire in the direction of a Lebanese army position. If an IDF position is shot at, it is unthinkable that the Syrian army should finish off the day peacefully. They must pay for the freedom they allow the Hizbullah.
"Write this down: "Israeli" democracy is what killed the IDF and SLA in Lebanon, far more than the Hizbullah, Syria and the Lebanese government put together. All the talk in your papers about withdrawal made it impossible for me to recruit agents. They would laugh in my face. You have to understand that you are surrounded only by dictatorships. Stop talking about what you plan to do all the time so freely.
"What would I do if I were the chief of staff of the IDF? If the Jewish people, with all they have suffered throughout history, is willing to abandon the people who helped them and paid in blood, a lot of blood, then I say to you that from a military aspect, the international border with Lebanon is the right solution and the IDF can withdraw. In such a situation, Mofaz will find it very easy to order the annihilation of a village every time the north of "Israel" is shot at from a village."
His 23 years of close ties with "Israel" taught Hachem a great deal about us, from Hebrew to our internal politics. He never stopped smiling and never seemed to take anything to heart or lose his self-confidence, even in the face of bad news from Lebanon, including Prime Minister Ehud Barak's announced plan to withdraw from Lebanon this summer.
"I don't think we are losing to the Hizbullah," he said. "It is hard to fight terror. Despite this, in recent months we have thwarted dozens of attacks by the Hizbullah in our area. Ninety-five percent of the Hizbullah's actions in the last year have involved shooting from afar, usually from within villages near United Nations and Lebanese army outposts. That shows that we have learned how they work and that they have been unable to get inside.
