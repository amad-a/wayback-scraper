Enemy shocked at IR's qualitative operation in Markaba

"A few hours after the Islamic Resistance's qualitative operation, two days ago, which took the lives of three occupation soldiers, "Israeli" enemy's Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu claimed that his government wants to pullout its occupation troops from Lebanon but "with the right conditions".

"Netanyahu called the incident a "tragedy" and repeated that the "Israeli" government was making continuos efforts to enable ""Israel"" to withdraw from the occupied zone. However, he explained that the enemy government is looking for a way out of Lebanon, but it "must first receive the proper guarantees pertaining to the security of the northern area (of occupied Palestine) residents".

"On the other hand, a special commission headed by an "Israeli" general was ordered to examine how IR's Mujahedeen were able to plant the powerful bomb only 30 meters from an "Israeli" occupation post and less than 300 meters from occupied Palestine's border."

"Three "Israeli" occupation soldiers, of the elite Golani brigade, were killed and four others were wounded in the IR attack Monday in Markaba (central sector), deep inside the area ""Israel"" occupies in southern Lebanon."

"At the time of the explosion, the enemy troops were carrying ammunition and explosives which increased their casualties. The bomb which was planted by resistance fighters near the border town was detonated by remote control in the path of an "Israeli" foot patrol. It brought to 20 the number of "Israeli" occupation soldiers killed by resistance men in south Lebanon this year."

"IR showed videotaped footage of the patrol walking into the ambush. The seven troops were shown lying on the ground having been hit."

""Israeli" Television's first channel said "Filming has become a major element for the Hizbullah cells, for they know that injuring a soldier affects him alone, whereas filming such an operation will affect thousands of "Israelis" who will see the footage."

""Israeli" newspapers quoted senior military officials expressing astonishment and outrage over the failure to spot the IR men, who after planting the bomb filmed the subsequent explosion."

"A senior "Israeli" military official said the operation needed skill and high training, adding "there was a big foul up".

"Hizbullah is scoring more victories over the "Israeli" army which appears to be in a continuous state of retreat, "Israeli" newspaper Ha'aretz (the Hebrew edition)" said.

""Israel" Knesset's "defense and security" committee met to discuss the operation, and summoned the enemy's chief of staff Shaul Mofaz who talked about ideas to decrease the size of the occupied zone".

"Roadside bombs have been the most successful weapons for the resistance in south Lebanon so far this year. Resistance sources in Beirut were quoted as saying the attack was a "big blow" because fighters were able to infiltrate deep into the occupation zone and return safely despite increased "Israeli" air raids on front-line areas. In a statement, the resistance stressed the attack was carried out by a "special forces" unit in response to daily attacks on southern Lebanon, especially the Iqlim al-Tuffah mountain ridge, just north of the occupation zone. The resistance warned "Israel" it is capable of attacking its occupation troops whenever and wherever it wants. Mounting casualties and pressure from "Israelis" to withdraw occupation troops from southern Lebanon, prompted "Israeli" Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Monday night to calm criticism by claiming his government wants to pullout its troops from Lebanon but quote with the right conditions. "Israel" earlier this year said it would withdraw its forces from occupied Lebanese territories if the Beirut government grants it security guarantees, which include amnesty to the "Israeli" allied militia in the south which has been supporting the "Israeli" occupation. Lebanon denounced the "Israeli" offer calling it a ploy aimed at countering any possible implementation of UN Security Council Resolution 425, which calls for an immediate and unconditional "Israeli" withdrawal from Lebanese territories".

For its part, the Daily Star, a daily Lebanese newspaper, reported the following:
"A day after the deadly roadside bomb ambush in which three of its soldiers were killed and another four wounded, the "Israeli" army Tuesday began investigating how Hizbullah guerrillas could have planted the device so close to a major outpost."

"The bomb was placed at the junction of a road and a dirt track, 35 meters from the front gate of the Markaba post, which is located just 300 meters from the "Israeli" border. The Markaba post is a familiar landmark in the south because of its prominent radio antenna."

We have to examine how they succeeded in penetrating such a relatively secure distance from an "Israeli" army outpost to plant a bomb. Its not impossible, but it is complicated and demands a high level of training, and this is apparently what happened in this instance, said Brigadier-General Erez Guerstein, the "Israeli" armys liaison officer in the south.

"He claimed the patrol was spread out but that the bomb had been of sufficient strength to strike all seven soldiers."

"A video of the blast released by Hizbullah, however, indicates that although the targeted "Israeli" patrol had been moving in single file, they had been bunched together, making them an easier target."

"In the video, the bomb explodes on the opposite side of the track from the patrol as the first soldier turned off the road. As the smoke clears, one of the soldiers is seen lurching off the road for a few paces before falling to the ground".

"The lack of security could have occurred due to over-confidence on the patrols behalf, not expecting to come under attack in what was believed to be a secure area of the zone."

"Coming seven weeks after the Hasbaya bomb attacks in which two soldiers were killed and three wounded ­ also in an area the "Israelis" believed was secure ­ Mondays ambush reconfirmed that the roadside bomb is unquestionably Hizbullahs deadliest weapon in its war against the occupation forces".

On the "Israeli" side, the Jerusalem Post covered the event by saying: "As "Israel" yesterday buried the three soldiers killed in Lebanon, an inquiry led by a brigadier-general was trying to determine how Hizbullah guerrillas penetrated so close to an IDF outpost in the security zone and planted a bomb."

"Chief of General Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaul Mofaz made the unusual step of personally appointing the inquiry into the incident."

"The General Staff inquiry no doubt undermines the authority of the Northern Command, but its commander, Maj.-Gen. Gaby Ashkenazy, said what was important was to "leave no stone unturned" to find out how the guerrillas were able to place a powerful bomb just 35 meters from the outpost of Tel Kaba."

"The inquiry is expected to take two weeks to complete its investigation, Mofaz told reporters in Jerusalem."

"Mofaz, who vowed upon taking command of the IDF this summer that he would install the value of victory in IDF soldiers and take the offensive in Lebanon, said the IDF needed to learn the lessons from the Hizbullah ambush, which killed three Golani soldiers and wounded four".

"The committee will investigate and reveal any faults and whether the incident could have been prevented," Mofaz told reporters following a meeting with the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee. "I hope we will be able to reveal everything about the incident... in order to be able to say to ourselves that we checked all avenues and we have the lessons for the future. We won't whitewash anything and we will expose every fault in this incident."

Family, friends and comrades-in-arms in Jerusalem, Kiryat Shmuel and Gan Yavne buried Nahum Elah, Yosef Ben-Muha and Raz Promovitch. All were 19-year-old sergeants from the 51st Golani battalion.

Meanwhile, President Ezer Weizman visited the wounded at hospitals in Haifa and Safed. Two of the wounded are in serious condition, one suffered moderate wounds and one hit in the chest with shrapnel was considered slightly wounded.

Weizman said the attack, though a difficult blow, should not lead to hasty decisions.

"The situation in Lebanon is not good, but we have gone through much more difficult periods," Weizman said. "I can understand the nervous parents whose sons are serving in the North. But we have to protect ourselves from ourselves."

"The death of the three soldiers, which brought to 18 the number killed in Lebanon so far this year, sparked a renewed debate over the continued IDF presence in the security zone."

"Several MKs, led by Yossi Beilin (Labor), met to discuss ways of furthering the call for an IDF withdrawal from Lebanon".

"Beilin called for reserve IDF generals "to come out of the closet" and publicly state their support for a withdrawal."

"Haim Ramon (Labor) said that former OC Northern Command Amiram Levine had presented maps to a subcommittee of the Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee shortly before he was demobilized and said that the IDF could withdraw within three months, as it had the upper hand on Hizbullah."

"Ramon said, however, that after Levine left, Ashkenazy, the new OC, reversed this opinion and said the IDF had to stay to protect the communities along the northern border."

"Gideon Ezra (Likud) suggested that each IDF position in Lebanon be examined to ascertain whether it is essential".
