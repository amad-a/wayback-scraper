The Markaba operation: Occupation forces in a "State of Erosion"
 

"The results of the Islamic Resistance's (IR) operation on Monday which killed and wounded seven occupation troops in taking its toll on the "Israeli" interior".

"The "Israeli" Northern Command has ordered an investigation into the blast, which claimed the lives of three soldiers and injured four others, three seriously. Senior occupation army commanders are looking for answers as to how the IR Mujahedeen managed to place the large explosive device just 30 meters from an occupation army outpost."

"Reports said the Resistance men managed to incapacitate the enemy's hyper-sensitive radars which are positioned at several points near the military outposts inside the occupation zone, al-Safir newspaper said".

"The daily quoted "exclusive sources" as saying that "The resistance men have worked for a long time on observing and surveying the area during the preparations for the operation. When the Resistance men planted the bomb employing an extremely sophisticated technique, a decision was taken to target as many enemy troops and vehicles as possible. This put off the remote control detonation repeatedly, until the Golani force reached the ambush site."

"All the resistance men returned as planned, without any delays or obstacles bringing with them a detailed filmed report on the enemy troops' movements before, during and after the operation."

""Israeli" analysts noted "a considerable flaw which made it possible to disable systems that can cover all the (occupied) zone and enabled the Hezbollah guerrillas to infiltrate, carry large bombs, plant them, wait for the patrol, detonate the bombs, film the operation and withdraw."

An "Israeli" Northern command noted "the great courage enjoyed by the Hezbollah guerrillas".
"As ""Israel"" yesterday buried the three soldiers, enemy Chief of General Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaul Mofaz made the unusual step of personally appointing the inquiry into the incident."

"The General Staff inquiry no doubt undermines the authority of the Northern Command, but its commander, Maj.-Gen. Gaby Ashkenazy, said what was important was to "leave no stone unturned" to find out how the IR men were able to place a powerful bomb just 35 meters from the outpost of Tel Kabaa."

"The inquiry is expected to take two weeks to complete its investigation, Mofaz told reporters in occupied al-Quds (Jerusalem)."

"Mofaz, who vowed upon taking command of the "Israeli" occupation army this summer that he would install the value of victory in his soldiers and take the offensive in Lebanon, said the "Israeli" army "needed to learn the lessons from the Hezbollah ambush", which killed three Golani soldiers and wounded four."

"The committee will investigate and reveal any faults and whether the incident could have been prevented," Mofaz told reporters following a meeting with the Knesset Foreign Affairs and "Defense" Committee. "I hope we will be able to reveal everything about the incident... in order to be able to say to ourselves that we checked all avenues and we have the lessons for the future. We won't whitewash anything and we will expose every fault in this incident."

"Meanwhile, "Israeli" entity's president, Ezer Weizman, visited the wounded at hospitals in Haifa and Safed. Two of the wounded are in serious condition, one suffered moderate wounds and one hit in the chest with shrapnel was considered slightly wounded. "

Weizman said the attack, though a difficult blow, should not lead to hasty decisions.

"The situation in Lebanon is not good," Weizman said. "I can understand the nervous parents whose sons are serving in the North. But we have to protect ourselves from ourselves."

After discussing the incident with the head of the Northern Command, Major General Gabi Ashkenazi, he could not offer an opinion on what had occurred.

"I have no doubt that what happened is not good, but jump too quickly to conclusions is very dangerous," he said.

"The three dead soldiers, all sergeants, are Nahum Ella, from Jerusalem; Raz Promovitz, from Gan Yavne; and Yosef Ben-Moha, from Haifa".

"The eulogy of Yosef Ben-Moha by Haifa Mayor Amram Mitzna, a retired major general, was interrupted by shouts from the crowd to "keep politics out of it" when Mitzna said it was not a "decree by providence to fight the devils from inside Lebanon." He said, "From this place we urge our leaders to bring about a way to bring our soldiers home as soon as possible."

"In occupied al-Quds (Jerusalem), Mayor Ehud Olmert eulogized Nahum Ella, the son of a well-known building contractor. The funeral of Dov Promovitz was not covered by the media at the family's request."

"Enemy Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called the incident a "tragedy" and repeated that the government was making continuos efforts to enable ""Israel"" to withdraw from the occupied zone. He explained that his government "is looking for a way out of Lebanon, but it must first receive the proper guarantees pertaining to the security of the northern area residents".

""Israeli" daily Hatzofeh said that Netanyahu's statement to the effect that the government is continuing to search for ways to withdraw the "Israeli" occupation army from Lebanon on condition that the security of the north can be assured, "points to symptoms of despair over the "Israeli" army's ability to deal with Hezbollah."

"The incident "points to something deeply negative in the "Israeli" army's method of operations. Perhaps fatigue, perhaps apathy, perhaps a decline in the soldiers' level, their motivation having been hurt after all the sacred cows have been slaughtered," the paper added.

It added that the "Israeli" occupation army needs to "finally carry out a serious house-cleaning, not only in Lebanon, but throughout the army itself."

"The daily concluded that, "the impression is that something is wrong with the "Israeli" army's infrastructure; the new chief-of- staff and the top political and security leadership must immediately examine what can be done to correct the current situation."

Another "Israeli" newspaper, Ma'ariv, noted the "dreary repetitiveness of such incidents" that, "Thus concludes another sad chapter, and we return to our daily routines, putting the latest disaster out of our hearts, until the next one".

"Senior "Israeli" officials are again at loss on how to deal with an increasingly costly occupation of southern Lebanon. But they also insisted they need to maintain their occupation of Lebanese territories in clear violation of international law. War Minister Yitzhak Mordechai and Industry Minister Nathan Sharansky yesterday inspected the occupied village of Markaba where the resistance carried out an operation on Monday that killed three elite "Israeli" soldiers and wounded four others. Mordechai was updated by "Israeli" military officials details of the Hizbollah operation, which has infuriated the "Israeli" government and baffled the "Israeli" army. The roadside bomb was detonated only 30 meters away from an occupation outpost and about three hundred meters away from the international border." "Mordechai stressed the operation was quote dangerous adding there are lessons to be learned. He also announced new measures will be taken to avoid such high "Israeli" casualties. But the resistance has warned "Israel" its occupation forces are never too far from its operations. "Israel's" President Ezer Weizmann said the resistance attack was a difficult blow, but added the occupation of south Lebanon is necessary."

"Weizmann was reported as expressing loss over what to do in the South, saying if he knew of a solution he would propose it. A large-scale military investigation by senior "Israeli" generals is underway to determine the circumstances of the Markaba operation".

For its part, the "Israeli" newspaper Haaretz reported that " A Knesset panel will ask to see the work of the military commission of inquiry that is investigating the deaths Monday of three soldiers in Lebanon."

"Uzi Landau, who is chairing the panel of the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee that is overseeing the investigation, said the panel will study the reports in order to understand whether the incident at Tel Kab'a was indicative of the general behavior of army forces in the South Lebanon." "Defense Minister* Yitzhak Mordechai visited three of the four soldiers who were wounded in Monday's attack and hospitalized at Ziv Hospital in Safed. "The incident was undoubtedly a very serious one, in that we lost three precious fighters, and in that other things were discovered on the ground," Mordechai said.

"He declared that some preliminary conclusions had been drawn about the circumstances of the incident."

"Some things have already been taken care of and others will be taken care of in the future," he said, but refused to explain what he meant?".
