HASBAYA PULLOUT TO START IN JANUARY: SOURCES
 

    ""Israel's" daily Maariv today (yesterday) quoted a high ranking political source as saying the families of five hundred "Israeli" allied militiamen in southern Lebanon will live in "Israel" following a pullout. Security Sources in southern Lebanon told us "Israel" is getting ready to withdraw its troops from the Hasbaya region starting January. We visited villages in Hasbaya that are just outside the zone "Israel" occupies.

" Workers construct a 60,000 dollar toust complex... in the front-line region of Hasbaya... less than four kilometers from the zone "Israel" occupies in south Lebanon. Wahib Madi says inhabitants of this economically devastated region of eastern Lebanon need his project. "This project will not yield the same amount of money here as it would in any other region of Lebanon, but we need it," he says. What if "Israeli" occupation forces withdraw from Hasbaya? "I am not betting on a settlement, but of course I am optimistic, if they withdraw then the entire region would open up for business..." Hasbaya is rife with reports of an imminent "Israeli" withdrawal from this part of the occupation zone. Security sources tell us, "Israel" will pullout its forces and its allied militiamen from this entire region to a newly fortified outpost deep inside the occupied zone, before a complete and a final withdrawal from Lebanon is carried out. Informed sources here told us occupation forces will withdraw from the Hasbaya region starting January next year to Tallet Shreifi, near the central occupation town of Marjeyoun, some half a kilometer from occupied Khiam, close to the border with "Israel". All retreating militiamen and "Israeli" forces who are now based in this mansion would gather, sources added, at the newly expanded outpost of Tallet Shreifi, until "Israel" finally decides to end its 21-year occupation of southern Lebanon. Sources also said some 350 men from Hasbaya serve in "Israel's" proxy militia, adding 150 are ready to turn themselves in to the authorities. They claimed, about one hundred fighters will retreat with "Israeli" forces, some thirty will take refuge in "Israel" and the rest will travel abroad. The eastern sector of the occupied zone was the least volatile region in occupied southern Lebanon. It is strategically located on Mount Hermon on the other side of the Syrian Golan Heights, which is also under "Israeli" occupation. It is also close to the northern "Israeli" Gaillee region. Starting last year, Lebanese resistance fighters infiltrated the area, dealing "Israeli" forces and their trained militiamen severe blows. In February, a roadside bomb near this village of Kawakaba, killed "Israel's" liaison officer in the zone, Erez Gerstein, and two other "Israeli" soldiers. That was followed by a record number of desertions by militiamen. They flee occupation ranks through these luscious hills and valleys and defect to government-held territories. Many have been heeding calls by religious authorities in the region, who issued an edict forbidding any form of collaborating with "Israel". Even "Israel" acknowledges the collapse of its militia in this part of the zone. Behind these gates at the Zimraya crossing point which links Lebanese land under occupation to the rest of the country, the resistance, despite constant observation by forces encamped inside this post, have succeeded in placing a number of roadside blasts killing militiamen here. That has been putting a damp on the morale of the militia, which has helped "Israel" sustain its occupation. A number of senior "Israeli" allied officers, including some who served in this post at Ain Qinya, have been detained by "Israel". They were accused of aiding the Lebanese resistance. "Israel" raids these areas on regular basis now, especially Jabal al-Dahr, where an "Israeli" war jet recently destroyed what was reported to be a military radar it installed, as part of preparations for its withdrawal from Hasbaya. A sense of normalcy in eastern Lebanon ends here at Marj al-Zohour, a village overlooked by a number of occupation outposts. The local population suffers hardships from continued occupation. But villagers in Meimes suffer the most. Meimes is the closest village to areas under occupation, and large plots of farming land are under "Israeli" control. "Israelis" do not allow farmers to cultivate land here. Since the central town in the district is under occupation any legal paper work must be finalized inside the zone. That, according to Hassan Sakr a member of the Kfeir municipal council, makes life more difficult. "The Zimraya crossing point is usually closed during shelling or clashes. That hinders our work," Sakr said. Piles of cars waiting to enter government held territory at Zimraya. Their papers have to be carefully checked. When we approached to ask them about the situation inside the zone, they all claimed there was nothing wrong. We were told they won't risk their lives. But people living outside the zone under the protection of the Lebanese Army are optimistic. Abdel Ghani Abdel Ghani is from Kfar Shouba the first Lebanese village to be totally destroyed by "Israel" in 1970. He sought refuge here, and he believes he will return to his border village. Hopefully the pullout will soon start, we are now optimistic, our resistance heroes are dealing "Israel" painful blows, their militia is collapsing.. I will return home... "Israel" has reportedly informed its militia of the decision to withdraw from Hasbaya. If "Israel" withdraws its troops from Hasbaya soon, Madi will receive more clients in his restaurant and pool when it opens next summer... The hardships endured by people in Meimes and the rest of Hasbaya will also end".
