"Israeli" fury at escape of Hizbullah guerrilla
Fighter strolls around Sojod base, raises flag, punches soldier and gets away(1)

An embarrassed "Israeli" army has launched an investigation to find out how a Hizbullah guerrilla was able to enter the formidable Sojod position on Sunday and wander around at will before escaping from a squad of elite paratroopers.

The incident occurred during heavy fighting on Sunday night between Hizbullah guerrillas and "Israeli" troops near Sojod in the Iqleem Al-Tuffah.

The guerrilla, armed with two grenades and an M-16 assault rifle, apparently scrambled over the compound's fortifications and strolled around the interior of the hilltop bunker undetected.

According to one account in yesterday's "Israeli" press, the fighter raised a Hizbullah flag in the base and may have stolen classified communications equipment.

The guerrilla bumped into a single "Israeli" paratrooper and opened fire at him from close range but missed. The two combatants had a fistfight before another soldier came to his colleague's aid. The guerrilla was shot by the second soldier but was still able to escape, leaving behind his flak jacket and rifle.

An "Israeli" army spokesman said an investigation had been opened but declined to provide further details until the probe was completed.

The incident clearly shocked the "Israeli army, particularly as it had involved an elite paratrooper unit.

"A paratrooper force cannot be surprised in such a way, this could have been a terrible catastrophe," one senior officer from "Israel's" northern command told the daily Yediot Ahronot newspaper.

"It is unacceptable that a unit of this level and size was unable to capture or kill a terrorist who infiltrated its sector," another high-ranking commander in south Lebanon told the daily Maariv.

The Sojod position, on the heights above Arab Salim and one of the most heavily attacked compounds in the zone, is nominally manned by South Lebanon Army militiamen. But "Israeli" troops are often temporarily deployed there, in particular Special Forces units who ambush infiltrating guerrillas on the wooded slopes of Jabal Rafih.

According to the military correspondent for Yediot Ahronot, "Israeli" army investigators were questioning all of the paratroop unit present in the compound at the time to find out how the guerrilla penetrated the base in broad daylight, for how long, why the first soldier to see him did not open fire and how he then managed to escape.

The investigation comes days after a probe was opened into a deadly Hizbullah attack on the same post on July 30 in which a 20-strong patrol was targeted by a road side bomb while on rifle shooting practice. One "Israeli" soldier was killed and four wounded in the blast. The "Israelis" fear that Hizbullah may have had prior intelligence on the unit's movements.

The breach of security in Sojod last Sunday will have seriously alarmed the "Israeli" army. It believes Hizbullah plans to kidnap a soldier to use as a bargaining chip to win the release of Lebanese detainees.

Haaretz daily said the guerrilla fired 15 rounds at a distance of two metres. No trained guerrilla or soldier misses from that range and he may have hoped to subdue the "Israeli" with the aim of kidnapping him.

According to some reports, it was only when other soldiers hearing the commotion reached the scene that shots were fired at the Hizbullah gunman.

Despite being wounded he still managed to evade the paratroopers after throwing down his weapons.

The inquiry being led by Brig -Gen Erez Gerstein, head of what so-called the IDFs Lebanese Liaison Unit, is also concentrating on how the small Hizbullah squad managed to reach the area without being seen.

One of the gunman climbed a short but steep rock face to reach the ramparts of the stronghold and then either cut through or clambered over barbed wire fencing surrounding the position, also without being observed.

The gunman had reached the trench encircling the ramparts before being suddenly discovered by the patrolling paratrooper.

Gerstein yesterday met and debriefed the soldiers and officers who were at post at the time of the incident and his findings will be submitted to OC Northern Command Maj-Gen Gaby Ashkenazi, who will turn them over to Chief of Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaol Mofaz.

Mofaz Sacks the Paratroop Battalion Commander (1)
 "Israeli" occupation's new chief of staff Shaul Mofaz blamed some of his officers and troops for his first "fiasco" in an attempt to cover up the threats he made that his occupation army will continue to confront Hizbullah. Mofaz's threat came hours before the Islamic Resistance's (IR) qualitative penetration in Sojod on Monday.

The "Israeli" occupation's army chief sacked a paratroop battalion commander Friday and kicked two of his men out of their combat unit after they failed to capture or kill the IR man who infiltrated their base in occupied south Lebanon.

"This isolated and exceptional incident showed a serious operational failure because the enemy infiltrator should have been caught or killed," said Mofaz, in announcing his decision.

He said written reprimands would also be put into the records of senior officers judged to have failed to adequately train the paratroopers involved in Monday's incident inside the occupied zone in south Lebanon.

The disciplinary action by Mofaz, who only took up his post as armed forces' chief in July, was considered severe by "Israeli" military analysts; a clear indication of the embarrassment caused by IR's infiltration.

During the incident, an IR fighter penetrated the Sojod outpost on the northern edge of the occupied zone. The outpost is normally manned by the collaborators of the so-called South Lebanon Army, but a unit of "Israeli" paratroopers was also present on Monday.

The IR man, armed with an assault rifle and grenades, fired shots at "Israeli" troops he encountered in the base and fought hand-to-hand with one of the disciplined paratroopers, who failed to use his gun against the intruder, the occupation army said.

Other soldiers then arrived and fired at the Hizbullah man, who was wounded but still succeeded in fleeing the base, leaving behind his gun and a bullet-proof vest, according to the occupation army's account.

The incident caused an uproar in the military and Mofaz personally took part in a three-hour reconstitution of events at Sojod before announcing his disciplinary actions.

As a result of the investigation, Mofaz dismissed the battalion commander of the paratroop unit which was present at the Sojod outpost at the time and ordered that two soldiers involved in the encounter with the IR man be barred from combat units.

Those who received written reprimands included senior officers, one of them a general, responsible for forces in the occupied zone in south Lebanon.

The statement said an officer involved in the incident was removed as platoon commander and two enlisted men could no longer be combat soldiers in their paratroop company.

The enemy army chief issued reprimands to a battalion commander, the head of the regional brigade and the chief of the Lebanon liaison unit, saying they failed to prepare the force properly.

"In contact with an enemy there are no compromises," Mofaz wrote in a statement released by the army.

The decision not to instruct the residents of the communities along the northern border to enter public bomb shelters or the security rooms in their homes on Tuesday was made after a situation appraisal by Northern Command concluded that there was "low probability" that the Shi'ite organizations in Lebanon would fire Katyusha rockets into "Israel".

But Maj. Gen. Gabi Ashkenazi, who heads the Northern Command, said yesterday that "we made a decision in accordance with the information we had, according to our best judgment, and when all is said and done we acted correctly."

The deliberations in Northern Command began shortly after a single missile fired by an air force helicopter killed al-Amin in his car. Northern Command expected that Shi'ite groups would make do with firing at "Israel" Defense Forces (IDF) outposts along the northern border (the "Purple Line").

That thinking derived from the accumulated experience of the past year in the wake of several episodes of high tension in the north. IDF intelligence noted that Amal and Hizbullah had opened fire at outposts near population centers but not at them.

Armed with this assessment, Ashkenzi briefed senior officials from northern communities but did not issue instructions about getting the population into shelters. In the early evening the decision was made to order security coordinators in the communities to open the public shelters "just in case," but again nothing was said about getting people into them. Shortly after the order was given to open the shelters the Katyushas began to fall on Kiryat Shmona and in Western Galilee.

Sources in Northern Command explained yesterday that the intention was to avoid damaging tourism to the north, and the effort to try keeping life running normally as long as the probability of a Katyusha barrage was considered to be low.

Military sources were willing to say that "since the attack ended with only a few people being lightly wounded, the calculated risk paid off in retrospect. If we send the population to the shelters every time the danger of a Katyusha attack looms, the impact on these communities will be extremely harmful."

Ashkenazi noted that "from our point of view the easiest thing is to get the population into the shelters time after time, but that will be very difficult for them... in my best judgment we did not take a risk," Ashkenazi added. "We acted based on the information that was in our possession." Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz gave Ashkenazi full backing.

Government property assessors say that direct damage of at least NIS 1 million was caused by more than two dozen Katyusha rockets that slammed into northern "Israel" late Tuesday night in retaliation for "Israel's" killing of an Amal militia commander earlier in the day.

A preliminary estimate by government property assessors who visited the region yesterday found that about 100 apartments, 4 public institutions and 10 vehicles were damaged, and that minor damage was caused to farming areas in the Galilee Panhandle and in Western Galilee. Few buildings took direct hits. Most of the damage was due to the shockwaves that shattered windows.

Less easy to gauge is the scale of indirect damage, such as lost workdays and cancellations by tourists who had planned to spend the final weekend of the summer relaxing up north.

One hotel manager, Reuven Weinberg, said it was unfortunate that the army did not wait until the end of the tourist season.

Two volleys of Katyusha rockets landed on the northern edge of Nahariya, a coastal resort town, and to the north, about 20 minutes apart. Vacationers who were just bedding down for the night in hundreds of rural "simmers" threw their belongings together and headed south.

A few minutes' drive north of Nahariya, Katyushas hit the farming community of Lehman, named for U.S. Senator Herbert Lehman, where many residents rent out rooms during the summer. The rebuilt home of Moshe Cohen - his former house was destroyed by a Katyusha four years ago - was struck by fragments of rockets again yesterday.

Another Lehman couple who rent out rooms, Yardena and Yair ben Eliezer, said they told their guests, who were unfamiliar with the characteristic whistle of an incoming Katyusha, to leave at once. "Of course in all the hullabaloo we did not sit down to work out their bill," said Yardena, adding that she hoped the guests would contact her to pay what they owe.

Residents who were forced to scramble for bomb shelters after the rockets had already landed were critical of the security authorities for not sounding the alert earlier.
