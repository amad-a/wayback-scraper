Wild goose chase after Israels boar battalions
    Israels occupation of south Lebanon has generated unusual stories in the past but surely none more bizarre than a report that the Israeli Army employs wild boar to track down resistance fighters entering the zone.

The tale, carried by the respected London-based Foreign Report weekly, lends weight to the recent disclosure that the Israeli Army has been planting scare stories about Hizbullah and Iran in the foreign, Arabic and Israeli press to justify the continued occupation of the south.

Some imaginative frontline villagers have long suspected the Israelis of deliberately training wild boar to destroy their crops.

But even they doubt that Israeli troops have been scampering around the steep wadis on the edge of the zone to catch the pigs and deploy them against infiltrating guerrilla squads.

Foreign Report, admittedly with a raised eyebrow, quoted Israeli security sources as saying that captured pigs are being equipped with collars containing an array of electronic equipment.

The boar are then released back into the dense thickets along the edge of the occupation zone.

The Israelis are able to track the movements of the pigs electronically. The clincher, however, is the mini-microphone attached to the collar.

Wild boar, apparently, give a distinctive grunt when detecting the presence of humans. Thus, if Hizbullah guerrillas on an operation in the zone bump into a member of the boar battalion, the Israelis pick up the warning grunt on the microphone, check the location of the animal on the monitor and act accordingly.

In a setback to the normally well-informed Foreign Report, the publication was cited last week, along with Paris-based Al-Watan al-Arabi newspaper, as having been duped into running falsified stories by the Israeli Armys psychological warfare unit.

According to Israels Haaretz newspaper, the unit has planted stories in these publications, among others, to back military intelligence assessments that Iran is the greatest threat to Israel.

Attempting to prove the veracity of the wild boar story is a little more than a wild goose chase. Nonetheless, it neatly coincides with tales circulating in some southern villages of Hebrew-inscribed ear tags being found on wild boar killed by local farmers.

Ive heard about metal tags on their ears but nothing about collars, said Sabah Mansour, 34, in Qabrikha on the edge of the zones central sector.

Qabrikha lies beside Wadi Salouqi which is home to numerous wild boar that prey on the crops of local farmers.

A group of old men nod sagely at the mention of Israeli-trained boar destroying crops. The Israelis do this to harm our livelihoods. They leave the tags on the animals because they dont care if theyre recognized, said one man.

Although all had heard of the ear tags, none had seen them. As for the porcine patrols, it was Mohammed Darwish, who injected some reality into the yarn.

The pigs came from a farm in Israel, he said. Darwish, 70, recalled how dozens of boar had escaped from a farm in northern Galilee years ago and crossed the border into Lebanon. They bred with the local wild pig population and their offspring continue to roam the wadis of the south.

Maybe the original animals had metal tags but they must have all died by now, he said.

A reasonable explanation perhaps, but for one old man it was a little too convenient.

The Jews dont eat pork. So what were they doing rearing pigs? he asked pointedly. The plot thickens.
