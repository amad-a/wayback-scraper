Ambush of naval squad 'no accident'
 
By Yossi Melman 
Ha'aretz Correspondent 
Thursday, August 13,1998.
 Enemy radar detected the force of naval commandos that was ambushed and badly hurt in an operation in Lebanon last September, according to the father of one of the soldiers killed in the failed raid.

Moshe Rudovsky, whose son Gal was killed in the raid, says he learned from highly credible sources that the force was detected by enemy radars and walked into a well-planned Hizbullah ambush.

A commission of inquiry appointed to investigate the failed raid concluded last October that the force was hit after encountering a chance enemy ambush.

Rudovsky claims, however, that following the Hizbullah discovery of the "Israeli" force, the enemy declared a 'radio silence," refraining from using any communications equipment that might disclose its presence, all the while tracking the IDF fighters.

Twelve "Israeli" soldiers were killed in the ambush. The body of one of them, Sgt. Maj. Itamar Ilya, was left behind and was only returned to "Israel" in June in a swap with the Hizbullah.

Rudovslry asserts that once "Israeli" intelligence learned that the enemy had declared a radio silence, Navy Commander Maj. Gen. Alex Tal, who was overseeing the raid from the supreme command post in Tel Aviv's Kirya military headquarters, should have aborted the operation and ordered the force back to safe ground.

In an interview yesterday on Channel One's Erev Hadash evening news program, Rudovsky claimed that several of the other bereaved parents from the incident support his call for T~ to resign.

Rudovsky added that Navy deputy commander Brig. Gen. Moshe Horev confirmed that the decision to carry on with the operation was a "mistake."

Rudovsky further said that according to Horev, in the first minutes after learning of the scope of the disaster, in which 12 of the force's 16 soldiers were killed Tal was in a state of shock and not able to function.
