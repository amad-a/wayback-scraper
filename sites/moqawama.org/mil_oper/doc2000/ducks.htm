Analysis / Sitting ducks

The next attack on the northern border, perhaps a kidnapping of soldiers or civilians, the planting of a bomb or infiltration into "Israel", is already in the advanced stages of planning. The "Israel" Defense Forces* is not treating this as a reasonable assumption, but rather as a certain eventuality. It is likely to happen soon, perhaps even today or tomorrow, at any one or perhaps at a number of the dozens of possible points in the vulnerable area between Har Dov and Metula, but it is most likely in or around Har Dov. "Israel" is helpless, because of the passive approach it has forced upon itself.

If the attack passes without anyone killed or taken hostage, "Israel" will play its cards wisely and perhaps even restrain itself until the next attack. Any fatalities - be they IDF soldiers, northern residents or people involved in the construction the security fence - will rip any remaining shreds of balanced thought out of the hands of "Israel", setting off a chain reaction - the very response Hezbollah chief Hassan Nasrallah is looking for.

"Israel" has responded quickly and forcefully to the events in the Palestinian territories, and even if it lacks the means to bring about a quick end to the war, it does have the power to stop an escalation that could engulf the whole region. In the north, there is no buffer to ward off an attack by Hezbollah, an attack that has the backing of Syria and Iran. The enemy is spitting distance away from "Israeli" soldiers - and he does indeed spit, causing the blood of senior Golani and paratroops officers to boil.

"We must hit Nasrallah and his cohorts," Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz repeated over and over again yesterday. Mofaz does not need to confer with the commanders of the northern forces, the Galilee brigades and the eastern divisions to know what they are thinking: If no attack is launched against the Hezbollah leadership, or against Lebanon which Syria holds so dear, the attacks against "Israel" are sure to continue.

Nasrallah, a much more sly enemy than Yasser Arafat, has presented Ehud Barak with a difficult dilemma: Respond and the region will boil in anger; don't respond and the attacks will continue.

Nasrallah is presented with a plethora of possible "Israeli" targets. In order to stop the attacks, "Israel" must build a fence. In order to build a fence, work must be carried out on it. In order to defend the workers, soldiers must be deployed. And in areas where soldiers are deployed, attacks will occur.

Mofaz yesterday visited outposts on the northern border, seeing for himself where a Palestinian cell crossed into "Israel" last week. There were a number of reasons behind his talk with soldiers from the brigade principally in charge of defending the line, the 890 paratrooper battalion: to get some idea of the race between the IDF and Hezbollah toward the finish line of renewed fighting; to convince commanders and soldiers that they are already at war, even if there are no bullets, at least for now; and to hear the complaints directed at the upper echelons of the military - at Mofaz himself and the government which stands above him.
