SLA* still in denial on IDF leaving southern Lebanon

More import to them - Hizbullah will lead a hostile attitude to the SLA and will strike against those cooperating with "Israel".
 The tone of SLA members is becoming sharper as the phrase "withdrawal following an agreement" is being uttered with increasing lack of conviction by Barak and his cabinet.
But despite the worries, the IDF** believes that the residents have not reached a "decision-making crossroads," regarding their next move. As long as they see a chance that the IDF withdrawal will follow an agreement, many continue to view cooperation with "Israel" as advantageous.
In view of this, they are in no hurry to abandon their homes. This is a fateful decision, and it is still early. For the time being, it is difficult to give up possessions and income in either the security zone or in "Israel", at a time when Hizbullah can only offer immunity (temporary and uncertain) following the withdrawal.
A man with 20 years of SLA service will not rush to risk his benefits and pension. Paradoxically, many SLA men for a breakthrough in talks with Syria. For years they opposed an "Israeli" withdrawal, but in their current view a withdrawal following an agreement is the best of several worse alternatives.
For its part, the Beirut government has intensified its pressure on the residents of southern Lebanon and intelligence officers are busy trying to recruit among them.
 The killing of SLA deputy commander Akl Hashem a month ago was considered a turning point. Senior members of the SLA are seriously concerned for their personal safety. They move about in as much secrecy as possible, using diversionary routes and telling few of their plans.
"If they got to Akl, who had protection, they can get to us," they tell their IDF counterparts. The limited "Israeli" response to Hashem's killing (a failed attempt on the life of a senior Hizbullah member, Ibrahim Akil, in early February), did not impress them.
""Israel" does not know how to avenge and does not understand the Middle East," SLA officers said again as they did after a Hizbullah bomb killed Brigadier General Erez Gerstein two years ago.
What did please the SLA troops were the threats leveled by Foreign Minister David Levy last week that pledged to burn Lebanon in retaliation for attacks on "Israel". "I am willing to kiss David Levy," said a senior SLA man at his battalion HQ at Hasbaya last week. In southern Lebanon, Levy's words are regarded as an expression of reliability and force, regardless of what lies behind them.
Unlike the IDF, which is making intensive preparations for the withdrawal, the SLA is making none. But its men have many requests. Foremost among these is the cancellation of long prison sentences, passed against them in absentia by the authorities in Beirut.
More than 10,000 such sentences have been handed out by Lebanese courts.

The great majority want to stay in the country. "We did not sacrifice so much here for so many years to run away to Canada or Switzerland," they say. Apart from some among the senior officers of the SLA and the security services, few will even consider leaving.
Economics (foreign bank accounts) and personal safety (liable to become targets for revenge attacks) are the main incentives for leaving. There is no substantive movement to set up villages of refuge inside "Israel". Open discussion of compensation is not heard yet, although all are expecting "retirement" packages.
Matching Barak's hints, the key date for southern Lebanese is the beginning of May. When it becomes absolutely clear that "Israel" is indeed pulling out, each will worry about his own. Some will flee to the north or abroad, others will try to purge themselves in the eyes of Hizbullah.
Of course, there are those who will continue to cooperate with "Israel" until the very last minute. The risks for IDF soldiers will increase accordingly. In the public debate on the issue of a unilateral "Israeli" withdrawal from southern Lebanon, one crucial unknown is not being mentioned: How will the South Lebanon Army (SLA) conduct itself in the coming months?
The importance of this in determining the future situation in southern Lebanon cannot be overstated. The abandoning of outposts en masse - or worse, the turning of some SLA men against "Israel" - will require a sharp change in IDF's strategy in southern Lebanon. Currently this is based on "freezing" the situation and holding out against Hizbullah attacks until decisions are reached at the political level. "A dramatic change in the way the SLA conducts itself will also force the IDF to reconsider the SLA's bitter opposition to a unilateral withdrawal," says a senior officer at the General Staff.
"The SLA withdrawal from Sujud earlier this month was, for the present, an unusual event. It was done, under instructions from above, to avoid the disorderly withdrawal of SLA men under heavy Hizbullah shelling (and especially while Hizbullah cameramen were filming).
However, during the last few weeks signs of concern and uncertainty are increasing in south Lebanon.
"Residents in the security zone, not directly linked with us, are beginning to distance themselves from the IDF and the SLA," says an "Israeli" officer with many years of service in southern Lebanon. "In the south people are beginning to grasp that Prime Minister Ehud Barak means what he says and that it looks like the IDF is pulling out in July, even without an agreement."
Since last December's renewal of negotiations between "Israel" and Syria, the withdrawal scenario has become a reality for residents of southern Lebanon. In talks with IDF officers, both civilians and SLA members express concern and bitterness. "Who will take care of us, who will protect us? What will happen to the families of dead or injured SLA soldiers who were hurt because of you? We did not fight on your side for 23 years for this." "Israelis" are having a difficult time convincing their allies there is no cause for concern.
In southern Lebanon, a unilateral IDF withdrawal will be portrayed as a resounding victory for Hizbullah.
