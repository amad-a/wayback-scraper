Paralyzed against Hizbullah, "Israel" must restrain its allied militia
News analysis

   
    Hizbullahs recent deadly offensive, which in the space of 10 days has left four "Israeli" soldiers and two SLA* militiamen dead, including senior SLA commander Aql Hachem, is raising fears that the South Lebanon Army may ignore "Israel's" policy of restraint and retaliate unilaterally.
The death of another militiaman in a roadside bomb blast on Wednesday night near the Baraasheit compound will have done little to ease the SLAs anger over the lethal operations.
"Israel" radio reported on Thursday that the militia is increasing pressure on the "Israeli" Army to respond to Hizbullahs attacks. The radio said that the SLA fears its activities could be undermined if "Israel" continues to react with caution.
The problem facing "Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak in the wake of Hizbullahs deadly attacks is one that used to haunt his predecessors in similar circumstances: Hizbullah acted within the rules. The targets were military, away from populated areas and the fighting did not spill across the border to threaten civilians in "Israel".
Consequently, Barak appears to believe that the option of retaliating against Lebanons infrastructure ­ as reportedly recommended by the "Israeli" Army ­ is unwarranted. The unwritten rules of the conflict dictate that this level of response could only be condoned if Hizbullah staged an unjustified cross-border rocket attack.
In the present circumstances, bombing Lebanons infrastructure would not only receive the opprobrium of the international community, including the United States, but more pertinently would grant Hizbullah legitimacy to launch salvos of Katyusha rockets into "Israel". Baalback-Hermel MP Ammar Mousawi, a member of Hizbullahs Loyalty to the Resistance bloc, warned "Israel" on Thursday that if Lebanons bridges and power stations were attacked, Hizbullah would respond with Katyusha rocket strikes into northern Galilee.
We would like to remind the enemy that our Katyushas are always ready and capable of terrorizing his settlers in the same way that the enemy terrorizes our people, he said on Hizbullahs Al-Nour radio.
Katyushas raining down on Kiryat Shmona would certainly hamper Baraks efforts to persuade the "Israeli" public that it is in their interests to hand back the Golan Heights to Syria. An escalation at this level could set the peace process back by months.
The other option ­ hitting Hizbullah targets ­ is equally problematic. There are few, if any, worthwhile targets for the "Israelis" to attack. If suitable resistance targets were available, the "Israelis" would not wait until their soldiers were being killed in the occupation zone before hitting them.
A source in the occupation zone said that the mood in the SLA has been one of frustration and anger since the assassination of Hachem in a bomb attack last Sunday. Hachem was well-liked by the men serving under him and was trusted by the "Israelis" as a capable commander. The source said there is concern that if Barak is seen to do nothing, then the militia may decide to take matters into their own hands. Such a scenario is not without precedent and in the past has brought the south perilously close to disaster.
In August 1997, the killing of the nephew and niece of the SLA commander in Jezzine in a roadside bomb blast prompted him to order the immediate shelling of Sidon. Nine civilians were killed and 42 wounded in the bombardment. In response, Hizbullah fired Katyushas into northern "Israel", the first such attack since the end of the 1996 April war.
More recently, indiscriminate SLA mortar fire wounded 18 schoolchildren in December in Arab Salim. Hizbullah refrained from replying with the customary rocket strike into Galilee due to the lack of fatalities among the children and "Israel's" apology for its allys behavior. The most persuasive reason for Hizbullahs restraint, however, was the presence of Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa in Washington, where he was about to relaunch peace talks with Barak.
Random and unprovoked mortar shelling by SLA units occupying frontline outposts ­ where the "Israelis" have little immediate hands-on control ­ is a regular occurrence. This is especially true in the western sector ­ Hachems former fiefdom ­ where the militiamen are presently yearning to revenge their slain commander.
The possibility remains high that if the SLA suffers further casualties in the short-term, the "Israelis" may not be able to stop the militiamen in frontline posts from lashing out indiscriminately and causing civilian fatalities, forcing Hizbullah ­ willingly or not ­ to resort to the Katyusha option.
