Parents of perished soldiers claim: Unauthorized explosives caused helicopter crash

 

By Michal Yudelman 
 "One of the two IDF** helicopters which crashed in February 1997 near the northern border crashed because explosives which should not have been on board went off, parents of the 73 soldiers killed in the disaster said yesterday".

"The parents are demanding the attorney-general order an investigation into the disaster so that those responsible can be prosecuted".

"At a press conference yesterday, the parents' representatives rejected the findings of the Ivry Commission, which the defense minister appointed to investigate the accident, and accused the committee of hiding the truth. The parents have just finished reviewing the commission's protocols, which they obtained following an petition to the High Court of Justice".

"We demand the investigation of the disaster and the prosecution of the persons responsible for it, persons with blood on their hands who got away scot-free. These are the people who killed my son, and some of them weren't even questioned," Yoram Alper, one of the representatives of the bereaved parents, said yesterday.

"The parents said that the crash on February 4, 1997, actually consisted of two different accidents: one helicopter fell because it hit the other, but the second one, bound for Dla'at, crashed when explosives went off on board".

"Half of the soldiers killed that night could still be alive if the procedures had been kept. The air force investigated itself, and those to blame are walking around free," Alper said.

"According to documents and testimonies, the Dla'at-bound helicopter did not crash immediately after being hit by the other but continued to She'ar Yashuv, where its landing lights went on and it started circling and lowering for landing, the parents said. When the helicopter was between 25 and 50 meters from the ground, an explosion, which eyewitnesses testified was "louder than a Katyusha," ripped the helicopter in half."

"Sources from the Ivry Commission told Channel 1 the possibility of an explosion causing the crash of one of the helicopters was looked into and ruled out by the commission, as no sign was found to substantiate it."

"Another parent, Yehuda Rothenberg, said pathologist Yehuda Hiss demanded that autopsies be conducted on all the soldiers killed on the helicopter to Dla'at that night, but the air force forbade it".

"This would have revealed the cause of death as the explosion inside the helicopter, not its fall," he said.

"The Ivry Commission ignored all the causes of the accident, and accepted all the conclusions handed down by the air force. They hid the fact - discovered later by the parents - that the explosives weren't taken off the helicopter before the flight as required by regulations, and that the regulations had been violated, as they had been on other, previous flights," Alper said.

"The parents accused the committee of regarding the killed soldiers as "inventory to be struck off the roster" and of contriving to put the entire blame for the disaster on the helicopters' pilots, who also perished, as the easiest way out, with no consideration of their families."

"The parents further charged IDF Judge Advocate-General Brig.-Gen. Uri Shoham with acting as both adviser for the supposedly independent inquiry committee and for the investigated parties (the army and air force), in a blatant conflict of interests".

"[Shoham], who was representing the army, directed and steered the investigation, which should have been independent. He passed on material from commission meetings to the air force and the IDF top brass, so they could prepare themselves in advance for the commission hearings. He and the commission collaborated to close the affair and prevent the investigation from exposing the truth," said Ya'acov Gottlieb, one of the parents.

Gottlieb said the commission's debate focused on how to cover things up and close the affair quickly.

"They never imagined we'd get hold of the protocols, with their arguments with [Shoham] and their fears that someone might appeal to the High Court of Justice. The protocols show they forgot all about the disaster and the victims. All they thought about was how to close it, quickly," Gottlieb said.
"Shoham rejected the parents' charges and said he had never expressed an opinion to the committee in favor of conclusions of any kind, Channel 1 reported".

"The protocols make it clear the investigation was carried out by the air force rather than the Ivry Commission, Alper said, adding that the commission "did everything within its power to shift responsibility downwards, as low as the graves of the pilots."

"They cast false blame on the dead pilots, telling lies about the way they functioned, regardless of the feelings of their families. In reality, the pilots acted as experienced, veteran pilots should and did everything to save their passengers, despite a system of negligence and obvious shortcomings of those who today continue leading their lives as though nothing had happened," Alper said.
