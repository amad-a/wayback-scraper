Netanyahu flounders

"Israeli" patrols now have a new drill before setting foot in south Lebanon. The soldiers each have a number written in felt-tip pen on the back of their hands and shout it out in sequence. The routine apparently comes in useful when they are under attack by the Lebanese resistance as a swift method of determining who is alive and free of wounds, and who doesnt reply because he may be dead or too badly wounded.

Meanwhile their political leader, Benjamin Netanyahu scurried back home Friday from Europe two days earlier than planned, after uttering a variety of confusing, contradictory and illogical statements.
According to "Israel" press reports, Mr. Netanyahu is considering a plan by his foreign minister, Ariel Sharon, for a phased unilateral withdrawal from the south. Mr. Sharon has suggested the idea because he has come to the conclusion that the "Israelis" have failed in their mission to prevent cross-border guerrilla attacks into northern "Israel". If that is how the "Israelis" want to camouflage the real reason for considering a withdrawal ­ the high number of casualties ­ so be it. The facts on the ground are somewhat different.

Since the PLO left Lebanon in 1983 the number of attempts to attack "Israel" from Lebanese soil has been minimal and, other than retaliatory Katushya strikes after "Israeli" shelling of Lebanese civilian areas, none has involved the Lebanese resistance.

At a press conference in London Mr. Netanyahu said Thursday: As soon as we find a way to withdraw our forces without abandoning the need and the ability to defend our northern villages and towns  we shall withdraw from Lebanon.

And very reasonable it must have sounded to a British audience ignorant of the fact that "Israel's" northern towns and villages are not now under attack and essentially havent been for the past 15 years.

Speaking to a domestic audience on "Israel" Radio a day later, Mr. Netanyahu said: The various ways to implement Resolution 425 will be examined by the Cabinet on Sunday. Certainly we have to think that as long as we dont have an alternative path, we will remain in Lebanon to protect northern "Israel".

It would be interesting to discover if even the "Israeli" prime minister understood the whole of that one. The truth is that Mr. Netanyahu is floundering. Domestically he is at the mercy of the Labor Party, whose leader, Ehud Barak, would rather have Mr. Netanyahus job than sustain the present incumbent for too long. On the Palestinian front, the prime minister faces the humiliating prospect of a Clinton-driven advance in the peace process which Mr. Netanyahus non-stop rhetoric of fear actively impeded. And on Lebanon his best advice comes from the father of one of the seven soldiers killed in the past 11 days. We must defend the countrys borders from within "Israeli" territory and not from any other country, said Moshe Cohen, whose son, Staff Sergeant Ayal Cohen, was killed Thursday. We dont have anything to look for in Lebanon, added Mr. Cohen Sr. Precisely.
