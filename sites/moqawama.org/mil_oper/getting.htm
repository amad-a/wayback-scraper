Getting out of Lebanon

 "The deaths of seven "Israeli" soldiers killed in action within only ten days by Hezbollah ambushes in southern Lebanon have generated feelings of profound grief and great frustration. The "Israeli" public identifies with the bereaved families and prays for the safety of those soldiers who are serving in the outposts beyond our northern border. It is only natural that the need for our continued presence in Lebanon and our present policies regarding Lebanon have once again become the chief topics of discussion in our national agenda."

""Israel" has no territorial claims on any part of Lebanese soil, so the question is what is the best way, from the standpoint of national security, for "Israel" to get out of Lebanon. Previous proposals such as widening the security zone or intensifying the "Israeli" occupation of southern Lebanon have no relevance in the present discussion. Nor should we allow ourselves to even contemplate such suggestions as broadening the confrontation to include Syria (which is assisting Hezbollah in its war with "Israel") or a massive attack on Lebanon's economic infrastructures".

"An immediate solution would be a unilateral withdrawal in the hope that the guerrilla forces won't transfer their struggle against "Israel" to our border with Lebanon in order to strike out against the communities there. Should this hope prove to be a pipe-dream and we are unable to reach any agreement with a responsible government, the price of the withdrawal could be civilian casualties and the total disruption of life in all of northern "Israel". Another possibility, which involves a slow and difficult process, would be to carry out a withdrawal within the context of negotiations and treaties with both Lebanon and Syria. It is obvious, however, that the second option would involve a withdrawal from the Golan Heights as well, and not only from southern Lebanon"

"A peace treaty is unquestionably preferable to an unpredictable unilateral withdrawal. But the prospects for a treaty are dependent on the good will of the Syrians as well as the determination of the present government to continue the path to peace that was begun by the Rabin government. The current regime, which so far has not worked very hard at advancing the negotiations with the Syrians, will now have to focus considerable energy in order to renew those talks".

"A unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon is a solution based on a balance of fear, in which either side could use excessive force in the event of a flare-up, even one of local dimensions. That kind of solution could be a last resort, if concerted attempts to reach an agreement with Syria and Lebanon fail."
