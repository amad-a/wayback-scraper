Two plans on table for new shape to occupied zone
 
    "With the occupation of the south dominating the electoral agenda in "Israel", the sudden decision by the "Israeli" government to order a reappraisal of the military situation in the zone reinforces the belief that a partial withdrawal from Lebanon is imminent".

"Reacting to the recent escalation in the south in which six soldiers, including a general, were killed within a week, "Israeli" Defense (War) Minister Moshe Arens has tasked the armys working group with re-assessing the territory and the rules of the game.

"With Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu facing a chorus of criticism over his south Lebanon policy
and facing a tough fight in the May 17 polls, observers believe that he may order a vote-winning tactical redeployment, which would have the support of the "Israeli" Army".

I think were coming very close to a partial withdrawal, said a senior security source. Netanyahu has a sharp brain and hes gauging public opinion. I think they will make some moves on the ground.

"Mr. Arens has made known in recent days his dislike of the 1996 April Understanding, which he believes hampers the ability of the "Israeli" Army to combat Hizbullah.

"But Monday he hinted for the first time at the possibility of altering the shape of the zone.

We have a serious problem in Lebanon, he said. The current situation, in terms of the structure of the security zone and the nature of the Grapes of Wrath understandings, must be compared to other alternatives. We must ask ourselves whether this is the optimal configuration. To me its clear that this is not the best alternative.

"Mr. Arens decision comes only three months after the "Israeli" Army was asked to provide suggestions on south Lebanon by former Defense (War) Minister Yitzhak Mordechai.

"The "Israeli" Armys broad view then was to maintain the status quo. But that assessment may no longer be politically viable with the looming elections and a public grown weary of military casualties in Lebanon".

"The army is hard pressed to come up with a suggestion that is substantial enough for the government to woo liberal voters, while not undermining the operational requirements of the troops in the south nor the security concerns of the right wing.

A partial redeployment could satisfy all parties".

"Continuing to maintain the status quo is politically risky. Further "Israeli" casualties in the short-term will merely augment opposition to the occupation and cause a backlash on Mr. Netanyahu.

"But a unilateral withdrawal is considered too drastic an alternative and is still opposed by the "Israeli" government.

"Two redeployment options are reportedly under consideration.

"The first is to develop a new security belt two to three kilometers north of the border. This would involve building an electric fence from the coast to Mount Hermon, and planting land mines and
installing surveillance equipment along the length of the new zone".

"The aim of the scheme, which is effectively an expansion of "Israel's" northern border, would be to limit the number of targets for Hizbullah.

"The proposal has reportedly gained the support of a large number of "Israeli" ministers.
"Its greatest pitfall is the logistics involved in building a fence across Lebanon. Hizbullah will not sit back and allow the electric fence and mine fields to be established unhindered".

"The second, and less drastic, scheme is to return the Jezzine enclave to the government and
withdraw "Israeli" troops from Kfar Houne to new positions on the south bank of the Litani River. Such a redeployment would reduce the front line by about 40 kilometers, and relinquish some of the most heavily hit outposts in the zone, including Sojod, Bir Kallab, Rihan and the positions overlooking Nabatieh. Beaufort Castle, due to its strategic position, would become the only "Israeli" position on the north bank of the Litani".

I think the Litani option is very much on the "Israeli" Armys cards at the moment, the security source said.

"The greatest obstacle facing such a plan is the fate of the South Lebanon Army in the Jezzine enclave.

"The only reason the "Israeli" Army still occupies the area north of the Litani is to provide a life-line to the SLA in Jezzine".

"But it is unlikely that the "Israeli" government will forego the political and military gains of redeploying to the Litani for the sake of the 200-300 militiamen remaining in Jezzine, who no longer serve a useful purpose defending northern "Israel"".

"But the "Israelis" will have to tread carefully. The militiamen further south will be closely scrutinizing
what happens to their comrades in Jezzine. The "Israelis" cannot afford to simply dump the SLA and their families in Jezzine, as it could encourage the remaining members of the militia to throw in their lot with the government before it is too late".

"The "Israelis", by nature, are reluctant to give something for nothing, which may also have deterred the implementation of the Litani pull-back".

"Experience, however, should have taught the "Israeli" government that Lebanon is in no position to offer concessions anywhere in the south".

"Mr. Netanyahu is a shrewd operator and is morally flexible enough to go with the prevailing mood in "Israel" ­ exhaustion with the south Lebanon quagmire and demands for action, whether offensive or defensive".

"As such, a partial withdrawal would help silence detractors and win votes, thus remaining the most favorable option for "Israel's" embattled premier".
