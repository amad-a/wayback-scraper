Hizbullah Targets Israelis
with U.S.
Anti-Tank Missiles
 
 
New weapon swings balance of power in south forcing IDF to rethink tactics 

Nicholas Blanford  
 Daily Star Staff 
19/11/1997 
 

 
Hizbullah guerrillas have acquired dozens of sophisticated US-made TOW anti-tank missiles and are using them against Israeli armour in the south, a move that poses a huge new threat to the beleaguered Israeli military in the zone.

A seasoned military observer has told The Daily Star that the guerrillas have obtained the missiles and are firing large number of them at Israeli targets in the occupation zone.

The source said that a recent Hizbullah video of one of its attacks clearly indicated the use of a TOW.

For those who know about these kind of missiles, the manner of its trajectory and the size of the explosion showed that Hizbullah was firing a TOW. It was unmistakable, the source said.

The source added that members of the Observer Group Lebanon (OGL), a military force assisting Unifil in the occupation zone, have found traces of the thin, wire filament used on TOWs to guide them to their targets.

There has been considerable speculation that Hizbullah fighters have employed the missile in the zone, but it has never been confirmed by an independent source until now.
The Tube-launched, Optically tracked, Wire-guided missile (TOW), employed by 40 countries around the world, is one of the most accurate anti-armour missiles available.
The first version went into service with the US army in 1970 and was used in Vietnam from 1972.

The Israeli army was also equipped with the TOW on the Golan Heights in the 1973 war.

The missile can be fired from fighting vehicles and helicopters as well as from ground units and can even target warplanes. The missile is guided by an optic wire controlled by the gunner.

Hizbullah guerrillas are believed to have already obtained the AT-4 Spiggot missile, which has largely replaced the antiquated Sagger projectiles. The TOW is a more advanced missile than the Spiggot and the confirmation that the resistance has deployed them in the south will have major repercussions on the Israeli occupation forces.

At least three Merkava Mark Ill tanks have been destroyed in recent weeks, which has seriously alarmed the Israeli army and has led observers to believe that prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu may be contemplating a massive bombardment of the south.
His options are limited and none appear to be favourable to the Israelis. A withdrawal of all tanks from the zone - which is reportedly being considered - would provide a severe blow to Israels military prestige and a massive morale and tactical boost to Hizbullah. Leaving the tanks in the zone would simply incur further losses.
