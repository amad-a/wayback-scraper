February Thursday 01-2001:

Sayyed Nasrallah, Muslim delegations discuss Muslim's condition…(1)

Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah received in the secretariat a number of delegations and personalities, who participated in the Quds Conference that was held in Beirut.

Sayyed Nasrallah discussed with the delegations ways of supporting the intifadah in Palestine, including the condition of Muslims in the world, and the responsibilities of the Islamic and National Movements.

The Chief of the Islamic Jama'a in Pakistan, Hussein Ahmad, who headed a delegation, said after the meeting that he congratulated Sayyed Nasrallah for the great triumph of the Islamic Resistance over the “Israeli” enemy, asserting that this victory brought pride to the Islamic Nation.

Other guests of Hizbullah Chief was Ahmad al-Shami, Secretary-General of the Yemeni party, Haqq, and the Algerian Speaker Abdel-Qader Samadi who headed a delegation of the Algerian Peace Society Movement.

Samadi said to journalists in Hizbullah's General-Secretariat, "The entire Islamic Nation is honored by the victory realized by Hizbullah, in the scope of the historical conflict between the nation and the Zionist Entity project. We consider this experience to pioneer a new route to regain the rights of Arabs and the Muslim Palestinian people. By God's will, this (victory) will be a positive overture to recover Beit al-Maqdes and the Aqsa Mosque. We truly value the efforts of Hizbullah and its Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah."

 

Delegates tour South in show of support…(3)

Two separate delegations from the Al-Quds Foundation conference toured the South on Wednesday, paying tribute to resistance fighters whom they said should serve as examples to liberate all occupied holy lands.
“It is resistance which will liberate Palestine and, with God’s will, holy Al-Quds,” Iranian MP Ali Akbar Mohtashemi, who headed one delegation, told reporters.
The MP, who was greeted by Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, Hizbullah’s commander in the South, and other party leaders, visited Fatima Gate on the border and toured Khiam prison. He was briefed by former inmates on torture methods used by their captors.
Mohtashemi described the prison as “the fierce Zionist enemy’s detention camp,” and said resistance against the occupation “should serve as a lesson to all Muslims and to the Palestinian people.”
Addressing “our brethren in occupied Palestine and all Palestinians,” Mohtashemi said Arabs and Muslims who gathered in Beirut for the conference on preserving the Arab character of Al-Quds were unanimous in declaring they “will not leave you alone, but will be with you in liberating Palestine.”
Qaouk paid tribute to Iran’s aid, noting that Mohtashemi had joined the resistance movement in its early stages and was wounded. “The Lebanese people will not forget Iran’s huge sacrifices and role in the liberation, this great achievement,” he said. “Iran nowadays, standing on Syria’s side, has considerably changed the existing equation and “Israel” will have to take the Iranian role into consideration in any possible war.”
Mohtashemi later visited Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, whom he invited to attend an international parliamentary conference in support of the Palestinian intifada, to be held in Tehran in April.
“If the new (US) administration wants to follow the same course adopted by the previous one, it will inflict harm on the national interests of the American people and state more than hitherto,” Mohtashemi told reporters afterward.
He said the “enmity between our people and American rulers will not change unless the American administration changes its policies.”
Meanwhile, another delegation comprising dozens of Arab and Islamic participants in the Al-Quds conference also visited Khiam and Fatima Gate, arriving in mini-buses.
They included the leader of Pakistan’s al-Jamaa al-Islamiya, Hussein Ahmad, and his counterpart in Kashmir, Abdel-Rashid Tourabi.
The delegation visited the former “Israeli” outpost of Dabshe, accompanied by the vice-president of Hizbullah’s political council, Mahmoud Qmati, and the head of the party’s politburo in the North, Mohammed Saleh.
““Israel’s” defeat in the South proves that we can liberate all occupied land sacred to us,” said Ahmad.
“We’re very proud of the resistance and Hizbullah’s accomplishments in the South,” Tourabi said, adding that the people of Kashmir supported the resistance in both Lebanon and Palestine.
Accompanying them, Algerian MP Hassan Araybi said Hizbullah’s resistance showed that “strength comes from faith, not weapons.”
“It is an honor to every Arab and Muslim to visit places which gave birth to heroes who accomplished the greatest victory in our modern history,” said Syrian historian Ahmad Houeis.
Houeis suggested that political decisions should be made by the mujahiddin, rather than the Arab League.
Those “who have sacrificed their lives should decide the Islamic nations’ fate,” he said.
However, the visitors shouted anti-”Israeli” slogans, such as: “death to “Israel”,” “we will return,” and “Palestine is ours.”

February Saturday 03-2001:

Hizbullah ready to step up attacks on “Israel”: Haaretz …(1)

Hizbullah has deployed Katyusha missile-launchers in southern Lebanon, sparking fears of an upsurge in violence at the “Israeli”-Lebanese border, the “Israeli” daily Haaretz said Friday, quoting a military officer.

According to the newspaper's military source, Hizbullah has set up a strategy enabling it to launch an attack "within a few minutes", firing from all along the border on “Israeli” targets deep inside “Israel”.

The “Israeli” army officer added the new long-range missiles used by the movement could reach the outskirts of the northern “Israeli” port city of Haifa.

The “Israeli” army had taken measures to prepare for such an attack, Haaretz said without elaborating.

The “Israeli” air force flew at low altitude over the sector Thursday , including the eastern tip of southern Lebanon, near the Shebaa Farms area, Lebanese correspondents said.

Hizbullah, which spearheaded armed opposition to “Israel”'s 22-year occupation of southern Lebanon, has led several attacks on “Israeli” positions in the Shebaa Farms, capturing three “Israeli” soldiers October 7 in the area seized from Syria by “Israel” in 1967 and now claimed by Beirut.

Caretaker Prime Minister Ehud Barak has often threatened to retaliate with large-scale attacks on Lebanese infrastructures and Syrian positions in Lebanon, but has refrained from carrying out heavy military operations against Lebanon since the May 24 “Israeli” pull-out.

His right-wing rival and frontrunner in the February 6 election for prime minister, Ariel Sharon, has taken a much firmer stance against Hizbullah's attacks, accusing Barak of being weak.

Soueid supports resistance…(1)

Jbeil MP Fares Soueid has offered his full solidarity with Hizbullah in its confrontation with “Israel”, the party said in a statement on Thursday.
Soueid, who met the party’s deputy secretary general Sheikh Naim Qassem, was quoted by Hizbullah as saying his visit with Qassem was “to get to know the party’s leadership, and Sheikh Qassem especially, in order to express my full solidarity with the party’s position concerning its confrontation with “Israel”.”
Souied thanked Hizbullah for its “good conduct” in the immediate aftermath of last year’s liberation and for “its respect for the idea of coexistence, and the rights of communities.”
The statement said Soueid and Kesrouan MP Mansour Bone met with Qassem at the party’s headquarters in Haret Hreik, where they discussed a range of topics, such as national reconciliation, the image Christians have of Hizbullah and ways of creating permanent channels of dialogue between Hizbullah and Christian leaders.
In their meeting, the leaders  discussed the Shibaa Farms, as well as ways of discussing Syrian relations with all sects.

February Monday 19-2001:

Jewish state debates retaliation…(1)

“Israel” responded to Friday’s deadly attack with mixed signals over the weekend with calls for attacks on Lebanon’s infrastructure balanced by a desire for restraint.
The chief of staff, Lieutenant General Shaul Mofaz, blamed Lebanon and Syria for Hizbullah’s ambush of an “Israeli” convoy, in which one “Israeli” soldier was killed and at least two others wounded. Mofaz hinted that “Israel’s” policy of restraint may not last much longer.
“In the days to come we will hold deliberations on the circumstances surrounding the attack, and when we reach the conclusion to act, we will act,” Mofaz warned.
“Israel’s” outgoing premier, Ehud Barak, said: “Anyone who thinks “Israel” will not retaliate for the Hizbullah missile attack on Har Dov (the Shibaa Farms) is mistaken.”
“Israeli” Deputy Defense (War) Minister Ephraim Sneh blamed Iran for Hizbullah’s deadly ambush but said that “Israel” “had no intention” of responding to Iran’s “provocative” efforts to create a conflict along the Lebanese border.
“(With) the situation that exists today, our retaliation a massive one, not a limited one like we did Friday afternoon is liable to lead to a widening of the conflict, including a limited conflict with the Syrians,” Sneh told “Israel” Radio.
“Israel” limited its retaliation for the ambush to shelling aimed at hitting the retreating guerrillas. Hizbullah has threatened to rocket settlements in northern “Israel” if “Israel” escalates.
“This is the reality. Therefore … we don’t have any interest in opening another front, in addition to the fronts we already have today,” Sneh added.
An “Israeli” military source was quoted in the Haaretz daily as saying that “Israel’s” retaliatory shelling on Friday was “not intended to cause a deterioration in the situation along the northern border.”
Senior “Israeli” Army officers have criticized the  lack of response to Hizbullah’s operations in the Shibaa Farms, which began with the kidnapping of three soldiers last October. Since then, Hizbullah has staged three ambushes in the disputed area, killing two soldiers and wounding five others.
Avigdor Lieberman, an “Israeli” MP, argued that the Hizbullah ambush “is worthy of a significant attack on Beirut’s infrastructure.”

February Thursday 22-2001:

“Israeli” parents ask army not to deploy their children in Shibaa…(1)

She was the home-grown scourge of the “Israeli” Army during the occupation of Lebanon, and now she’s back: the
“Israeli” mother.
Nine months after “Israel’s” unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon, the bane of “Israel’s” military establishment has set her sights on the Shibaa Farms.
“Israel’s” daily Maariv newspaper reported Wednesday that parents are demanding that their children not be deployed to the area, the scene of renewed fighting with Hizbullah since October.
Maariv quoted a military source as saying that the renewal of hostilities along the border with Lebanon and the outbreak of violence in the Palestinian territories has “created anxiety among parents of new recruits as well as among the recruits themselves.”
During the occupation of the South, mothers of serving “Israeli” soldiers formed a powerful lobby group called the Four Mothers which demanded an immediate troop withdrawal from Lebanon. The Four Mothers was established in February 1997 following a mid-air collision between two troop-carrying helicopters en route to southern Lebanon
in which 72 soldiers and airmen were killed.
The Four Mothers spawned similar pro-unilateral withdrawal groups such as the Red Line and the Three Fathers.
Two “Israeli” soldiers have been killed and another 12 wounded in Hizbullah’s four assaults against “Israeli” targets in the Shibaa Farms.
In the first attack on Oct. 7, Hizbullah captured three “Israeli” soldiers from across the south of Shibaa village and wounded seven soldiers in a simultaneous mortar bombardment of surrounding outposts.
Maariv said that new artillery corps recruits were deployed to the Shibaa Farms sector to help construct the new security fence along the Blue Line.
Others were dispatched to the West Bank and Gaza to help guard Jewish settlements.
The military source told the newspaper that when the parents of the recruits found out that their sons were being sent on tasks of this kind, “a flood of phone calls began to the base.”
“Some of them simply asked not to send their children on operational missions because they were anxious and worried.
“Others used the excuse that their children were suffering from unstable emotional heath and that therefore they should not be sent, and others simply asked: ‘What for?’” the source was quoted as saying.
He said that “both the parents and the recruits exhibit anxiety.”
To calm them, the parents were invited to the base and their children’s duties were explained to them.
But some parents were not persuaded and continued to demand that their children remain on the base.
Mental-health officers were called in to help quell anxiety and increase motivation among the recruits.
“When the soldiers exhibit calm you can feel it with the parents too,” the source said. “Thus it was important to talk with them so that they could overcome their anxieties.”
Eventually, the recruits went on their operational tasks and even earned praise by their commanders, Maariv said.

Rain of Rockets Frightens “Israel” into Inaction Over Shibaa Attack…(2)

The specter of long-range Katyusha rockets raining down on the entire Galilee district and such major cities as Haifa, Tiberias, Nahariya and Safad has frightened “Israel” into withholding a massive response to Hizbullah's latest missile attack, which killed one “Israeli” soldier and wounded two in the Shibaa farms, a Tel Aviv newspaper says.
The newspaper Hatsofe said Hizbullah had set up a belt of mobile multi-barreled rocket launchers and truck-mounted missiles along “Israel’s” northern flank ready to go off the moment “Israel” launches a large-scale military offensive against Lebanon.
The Japanese-made Isuzo truck launchers carry Fajr-3 (Arabic for dawn-3) projectiles, a third generation of Katyusha rockets with a 60-kilometer range manufactured by Iran's air force industry specially for Hizbullah in Lebanon.
Large consignments of these rockets were air-lifted to Hizbullah in Lebanon months before “Israel” withdrew its army from the southern Lebanese border enclave last May, terminating a 22-year occupation, said Hatsofe, which speaks for “Israel’s” Mifdal religious party.
These rockets were originally meant to be used against troops manning the occupied zone before the May 24 withdrawal. From their current positions, fixed or mobile, they can cover the entire Galilee which houses one third of “Israel’s” industry, Hatsofe said. Its report was highlighted by An-Nahar.

Haifa preparing for possible Katyushas…(3)

” Local authorities from Haifa northward have received instructions to make appropriate preparations for the possibility of Katyusha rocket attacks from south Lebanon, OC Home Front Command Maj.-Gen. Gaby Ophir said yesterday.
"Regarding Haifa and the bayside suburbs, for the past year, Home Front Command generally and the district commanders in particular have visited the various local authorities and have given instructions regarding the preparedness of bomb shelters," said Ophir.
"Whether the bayside suburbs, Acre and so on, might be the target of Katyusha attacks - I certainly hope not. For the time being, south Lebanon and the [border] line is quiet." Ophir noted that since the completion of the “Israeli” Army’s withdrawal from south Lebanon on May 24 last year, there had not been any rocket attacks, and expressed the hope that this would remain the case.
He spoke to reporters following the inauguration in Kiryat Shmona yesterday of a new electronic control and monitoring system which enables the opening of all the city's public bomb shelters at the press of a single button.
The innovative system, which cost NIS 5 million to install, also relays information to the central control about the state of the shelters. A check carried out earlier this week revealed that 20 of them were flooded with rain-water.
The “Israeli” Army has been aware for a long time that Hizbullah has acquired the long-range Fajar version of the Katyusha rocket which would be capable of reaching as far south as Haifa and its environs.
Furthermore, the conventional 107 millimeter and 122 mm rockets also have a greater range now than previously due to the absence of the security zone. The fact that these Katyusha rockets can now be stationed closer to the international border instead of deeper inside south Lebanon when the zone existed means they would probably be able to hit places like Safed, Karmiel, Acre, Hatzor and Rosh Pina, which had previously been out of range.
The Haifa municipality spokesperson said all public bomb shelters and those in schools in the city and the neighboring suburbs of Kiryat Haim and Kiryat Shmuel were kept maintained and ready for instant use, should the need arise.

Failed “Israeli” Army Operation at Ansarieh Still Haunts Soldiers…(1)

Three years have gone by since the failing “Israeli” commandos operation in the Lebanese southern town of Ansariah,and the surviving “Israeli” soldiers have still not gotten over it.

The Zionist magazine(Haear) reported that the “Ansariah nightmare” controls the “Israeli”  soldiers’ minds.

The soldiers who pre planned this operation were utterly affected by it, and have also filed law suits against the Army demanding compensation for their illnesses ,mainly cancer, diabetes and insomnia.

One of the officers who was in Ansariah said, “All those who participated in this operation go through the shock of the battle...  no one was able to get out clean.”

Others said the “Israeli” Army failed in curing anyone of the affected soldiers.

Ansariah remains a traumatic experience to every “Israeli” soldier.
