Martyr's Day, 17 years after blowing up Israeli military governor's HQ in Tyre
    Hizbullah and the Lebanese mark Martyr's Day on November eleventh, the day on which, 17 years ago, a young man, Ahmed Qassir, carried out the martyrdom operation that killed over 150 Israeli officers and soldiers including the Israeli military governor and other senior officers.

In 1982, Qassir, who hailed from the town of Deir Qanoun al-Nahr, drove a Pegout car full of explosives and bombs after he hugged two of his comrades whose mission was to watch the details of the operation.

Moments later, a horrible explosion blew up the headquarters of the Israeli military governor at the Azmi Building (in Tyre) which was chosen by the Israeli command as a center along with a camp built near the building.

The date for the operation was set on November 10, 1982, but unexpected circumstances emerged and postponed the operation till the next day.

It was seven o'clock; the rainy weather forced the occupation troops and the Israeli intelligence men to take cover in the building. On the previous day, the sky was clear and the troops were deployed in the near camp. This raised the number of the troops killed. The delay also led to the addition of an Israeli intelligence official, who spent the night in the building, to those killed by the explosion. Moreover, a number of the Lebanese and Palestinian detainees were moved, on November 10, to another building.

The 8-story building included offices directly belonging to the Israeli intelligence. One of the floors was headquarters for the Assistance Unit of the Israeli command in the area. The fourth floor was a place were a number of Israeli officers and sergeants slept when they were assigned to perform certain intelligence or liaison missions.

Ahmed Qassir, the pioneer of martyrdom operations, stormed the building's square; moment later a huge explosion was heard and the building crumbled. There was a hellish fire and smoke billowed within horrible moments that witnessed the Israeli troops crying while sporadic shooting was heard.

The site turned into an arena full of torn bodies that were inspected by Israeli generals and commanders, including the northern area commander Gen. Amir Drouri, arriving by cars and helicopters.

As for the losses, the Israeli spokesman then acknowledged that 74 Israeli officers and soldiers, including the military governor, were killed; 27 others were considered missing. Israeli newspapers days later said 141 were killed, and ten were missing.

As the Israeli cabinet announced a state of morning, the Israelis were puzzled in explaining the operation, saying that the reason was a building defect and ruling out the bombed car possibility; Israeli officers', including chief of staff Rafael Elan, later said that bombs were planted at the building's shafts and this led to the collapse of the building.

The name of Ahmed Qassir remained unknown along with the side that carried out the operation until May 19, 1985 when the Islamic Resistance marked the anniversary of the Deir Qanoun al-Nahr's martyrs and unveiled the hero of the first martyrdom operation.

The announcement was made by Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah who was not Hizbullah secretary General then.

His Eminence announced that the original plan included another martyrdom operation that was to rock another Israeli command HQ in Sidon. Field circumstances emerged and canceled the second operation.

1- Al-Manar TV, 11-11-1999.
