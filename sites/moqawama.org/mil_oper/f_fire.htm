Israeli occupation sergeant killed by "friendly fire"
 

An Israeli occupation soldier was killed and two others wounded in a "friendly fire accident" in south Lebanon overnight.

The incident happened when two parts of Israeli troops exchanged fire after they mistook the other side for resistance men near Taibe in the central occupied zone in south Lebanon.

The death raised to 24 the number of the Israeli occupation soldiers killed in south Lebanon since the start of this year, most of them in Islamic Resistance operations.

"As a result of a split in the troops, the rear part of the troops saw the front part and mistook them" for resistance men, according to Israeli media.

However, the fact that the Islamic Resistance has launched an attack on occupation troops in the same area cast doubt on the Israeli story.

The occupation command usually claims that certain "incidents" happened in the occupied zone to explain the heavy casualties in the ranks of Israeli troops -- due to resistance operations -- to an angry Israeli public.

An Israeli Givati Infantry soldier, Eli Bovlil, was killed and two others were wounded two weeks ago "in a training accident" by a fellow soldier.

In another "incident", a soldier taking part in a training exercise sustained serious injuries when the vehicle he was traveling in "flipped over".

Last month , an Israeli Merkava tank "mistakenly fired two shells at another tank after a sergeant on board of the armored vehicle saw a suspicious image and decided to open fire".

In all cases, the occupation army holds investigations and learns more lessons.

Another explanation can be based on the constant state of panic and confusion which the occupation troops live as they expect resistance attacks any minute.

The killed soldier was identified as Staff Sgt. Ohad Zach. His mother told "Israel radio" that "army commanders had ignored warnings from soldiers that there were problems with path-finding and orientation within the unit".

"If soldiers warn about a problem, they know what they're saying, and commanders have to pay attention," she said. "The front shot the back, the back shot the front."

Today's shooting was the second case of injuries from "friendly fire" this month.

The occupation army said it was looking into whether the soldier was mistakenly killed by fellow troops. One of the two was wounded moderately, the other lightly, the occupation army said.

Parents blame army after son killed by friendly fire
 The parents of Staff-Sgt. Ohad Zach, 19, blamed the army yesterday for the death of their son in a friendly fire incident in the security zone, citing the senior command's refusal to replace a company commander despite repeated complaints by his unit that he was unqualified.

Zach, of Nofit village, near Kiryat Tivon, was killed and two others wounded when a unit of Golani Brigade troops opened fire on their comrades from the same company, believing they were Hizbullah gunmen.

The tragedy occurred near the Taibeh outpost in the central sector of the zone around Tuesday midnight, after the two units lost contact with one another while en route to carry out an ambush.
Initial inquiries revealed that the advance squad, led by Sec.-Lt. Assaf Lapid, 21, the company commander, made a navigational error and took a wrong route down into a wadi instead of to the ambush site.

Haim Zach, Ohad's father, pointed a finger at Lapid, the son of prisoner rehabilitation activist Herut Lapid.

Lapid said his son, who was also wounded and hospitalized at Sieff Hospital in Safed, also blamed himself for Zach's death and would not hear otherwise. But he called for a thorough investigation of the incident, saying such accusations play into the hands of Hizbullah.

Haim Zach said "the entire establishment knew about this problem [of Lapid] and the matter was investigated, but he was allowed to continue to act [as the unit's commander]. I blame the [army] establishment directly for Ohad's death.

"The tragedy is everyone's responsibility. Everyone knew about the company commander and I blame them. The company commander is only 21 years old, and the direct responsibility in this matter belongs to the battalion commander or maybe even the division commander, if they gave that officer command."

"This should not have happened," said Zach's mother, Naomi. "If the soldiers warn about a certain problem, they are not totally stupid, and they know what they are talking about.

"The battalion commander and the ones above and below him should pay attention to what [the soldiers] say and not wait until there's a collection of X-number of errors and then clear the arena [to make way] for somebody else," she said.

"What is even worse about it is that it seems that this accident was caused by a navigational error that the [senior command] was aware of. The soldiers in his unit had told him time and time again about this navigational problem and asked that it be fixed, but nothing was done. It seems that they were waiting for more and more errors before they took a step to fix it. This should not have happened."

The family's anger came to the fore after a team from Ohad's unit, accompanied by senior officers, came to their home yesterday morning, spread out a map of the region where the tragedy occurred and explained to them exactly what happened.

Zach was critically wounded in the clash and died on the way to the hospital. The first IDF* fatality from the small hilltop community was laid to rest at the military cemetery in Kiryat Tivon yesterday afternoon. Nofit itself does not yet have a cemetery.

Another soldier in Zach's squad, Sgt. Guy Nissim, was moderately wounded after being hit by shrapnel in the face, and was blinded in his right eye. He was taken to Sheba Hospital at Tel Hashomer.

Lapid was lightly hurt and was taken to Sieff Hospital.

Zach is survived by his parents, grandparents, an older sister, Adva, 23, and a younger sister, Aviv, 12.

MK Yossi Beilin (Labor), a leader of the national movement for Lebanon withdrawal, said the incident shows that "this zone is not a security zone. It's an insecurity zone."

Beilin was speaking during a debate in the Knesset on the recent killing of Lebanese civilians and the subsequent Katyusha attacks on the North.

Expert calls for public body to oversee IDF accident probes
The late prime minister Yitzhak Rabin once said there is no solace for families that lose their sons in military accidents.

Whether such mishaps are caused by miscalculations or bad luck is irrelevant in their aftermath, if one is left with the thought that perhaps not everything is being done to avoid their recurrence.

Prof. Stuart Cohen, a senior research fellow at Bar-Ilan University's Begin-Sadat Center for Strategic Studies, suggests establishing a parliamentary or public watchdog body to oversee results of IDF inquiries into all forms of accidents.

The main tasks of such a body would be to ensure that the recommendations stemming from such inquiries are implemented, he said. However, he added, the IDF is best equipped to conduct the actual inquiries into accidents.

Tragic accidents are often caused by exhausted troops operating under constant pressure, said Col. (res.) Moshe Givati on Israel Radio yesterday. The IDF constantly carries out investigations and reassessments of situations whether there are mishaps or not, he said.

Despite the sophisticated technology the human factor cannot be ignored. Scores of operations are carried out by troops in Lebanon daily, he said, adding that the soldiers are on constant alert and often have to make quick decisions whether something they saw is the enemy.

Finally, someone to blame in Lebanon
On May 25, 1993, Staff Sergeant Dvir Mor-Haim and three other soldiers from the Paratroop Brigade were killed by "friendly fire" as they lay in ambush in the central sector of the security zone in southern Lebanon.

The head of Central Command at the time, Yitzhak Mordechai, now the defense minister, and the chief of staff at the time, Ehud Barak, set up a General Staff probe of the incident but refused to establish a commission of inquiry. Mor-Haim's parents fought the army over the issue for five years, until three months ago the High Court of Justice threw out their petition.

"The High Court has now received another regrettable illustration of why they should have accepted the parents' petition," attorney Shlom Tzippori, who represented Mor-Haim's parents in the case, said yesterday.

The General Staff team that investigated the incident failed to establish its causes but noted that there had been no manifestations of negligence - and therefore no disciplinary or other action was taken. The team did recommend that the orders for opening fire be "clarified and assimilated" with an emphasis on "obeying them even in the most dangerous situations."

There are differences between the 1993 incident and the "friendly fire" death of Staff Sergeant Ohad Zach in Lebanon early Wednesday morning, but no lessons were learned from the earlier event because its causes were never discovered. This week, however, sources in the Northern Command rushed to judgment of a 21-year-old platoon commander.

According to Ruth Mor-Haim, Dvir's mother, the top brass took that unusual step in order to fend off media criticism. She maintained that if a proper commission of inquiry had been established after the 1993 incident, this week's tragedy might have been averted. "The cover-up of the 1993 incident cried out to the heavens," she said.

Since April of this year there have been four incidents in which IDF soldiers were involved in firefights among themselves in Lebanon. On April 11, an IDF tank opened fire at an Israeli force near the Karkoum outpost, having mistaken them for guerrillas. On July 2, soldiers in the Reihan outpost shot at an IDF convoy. On August 25, IDF soldiers exchanged fire with militiamen from the Israeli-backed South Lebanese Army, and on November 25 an IDF force fired a few shells at another Israeli force near Reihan.

No one was hurt in these incidents, and no disciplinary action was taken against anyone involved.
