On the second anniversary of 1997 helicopter collision the bereaved parents are still holding "Israel's" Army the accountable: (1)
"The "Israeli" entity today (yesterday) marks the second anniversary of the deaths of 73 servicemen in the collision of two IAF transport helicopters ferrying troops to Lebanon. In a memorial ceremony last night near the site of the crash".

After making their way north in a long convoy, parents and family members of the 73 fallen gathered at Kibbutz Kfar Blum. Chief of General Staff Lt.-Gen. Shaul Mofaz said at the ceremony that the IDF* would continue to implement the lessons learned from the crash.

On February 4, 1997 two CH-53 Sikorsky (Yasur) helicopters filled with combat soldiers heading to the Beaufort and Dla'at outposts in the security zone collided over Moshav She'ar Yashuv in the Upper Galilee, killing all aboard. It was the worst accident in the IDF's history and the defense establishment took the unusual step of appointing an outside commission of inquiry, which blamed the crash on human error and confusing flight orders.

"Last night's ceremony came amid a reported conflict between bereaved parents who still hold senior IAF officers accountable and others who feel the crash itself was punishment enough for the IAF".

"Yesterday one parent, Prof. Meir Shalit, whose son Omer was killed in the crash, called for OC Air Force Maj.-Gen. Eitan Ben-Eliahu and other senior IAF commanders to resign. But another bereaved parent, Eitan Peretz, father of Eran, said bereaved parents are no longer sacred cows and the country no longer has to "stand at attention" and accept anything they have to say".

"Parents have become increasingly involved in the IDF over the past decade and are no longer readily willing to accept official versions of their children's death or injury, said Prof. Abraham Friedman, head of the Jerusalem Institute for "Israel" Studies think tank. This phenomenon has raised new questions for society to answer", he said.

"Once, the army said it was taking care of you and people didn't ask questions," Friedman said. "Today we have three questions without answers. One, to whom does the body of a soldier belong? Two, to whom does information on a soldier belong? And three, to whom does the grave of a soldier belong? In the past they all belonged to the IDF. But today the public is no longer willing to accept this." Today memorial services will be held for the fallen. The Four Mothers organization, which demands a unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon, announced it is erecting a memorial to the 73 at the Defense Ministry on Friday afternoon".
