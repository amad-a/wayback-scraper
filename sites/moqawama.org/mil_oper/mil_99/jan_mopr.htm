17 Israeli allied militiamen have defected (1)
"17 Israeli allied militiamen have defected and taken refuge in Sfarray's police station and requested the Lebanese government to provide them protection. The 17 are from Annan. The defections came two days after Israeli allied militiamen redeployed in the occupied Jezzine enclave. The militia played down the significance of the redeployment but reports said the move may be part of a rebellion. The militia has been in disarray and its militiamen are suffering from low morale after successful resistance operations".

"Two days ago, the Israeli allied militia redeployed its forces in the occupied south Lebanon enclave of Jezzine moving them closer to the town. Militia personnel evacuated the frontline Dahr al-Mashnaqa and Annan positions east of the port city of Sidon after 30 militiamen serving in the two positions refused to stay unless they received reinforcements. The militiamen reportedly complained to a regiment commander Joseph Karam, known as Allouche they can no longer defend their posts. The commander refused their request and told them to stay. But the militiamen refused and Karam decided to evacuate the positions after consulting with militia commander Antoine Lahd. The militia played down the redeployment saying it was part of a military plan which could be implemented at any time and not a withdrawal. Reports here said the withdrawal followed a series of bomb attacks by the resistance on militiamen travelling to and from the post of Annan. The 2,500 strong militia has lost 35 men killed and 42 wounded this year. It has also suffered a number of defections in recent months particularly from its intelligence services. Their morale is low since their fate is unknown once Israeli forces pull out of occupied areas. As a result of the withdrawal, the villages of Annan, Sfaray, Bisri, Mazraat al Mathaneh and Khirbet Bisri are no longer part of the militia controlled region and the most forward militia position is now in the village of Roum. The militia has reportedly reinforced its presence in Roum and was establishing a new crossing point to replace Kfar Falous. Roum is a strategic position because it overlooks the regions east of Sidon and Iqlim al Kharooub. The source said the militia did not destroy the fortifications in its evacuated positions as it would in the past and did not remove the obstacles and earth mounds from Kfar Falous Roum road. The militia closed all roads leading to Annan in a move apparently aimed at punishing village residents for not forcing their sons to stop the rebellion. It was the second outpost abandoned in the Jezzine region in past few months following the opening earlier in the year of the Kfar Falous. Lebanese newspapers said the redeployment might be the first step that will end militia control over the area".

"In September, the militia pulled out of the Toumat Niha position a strategic location south of Jezzine.

The Islamic Resistance had described that as a major victory and further evidence of the weakening resolve of the militia. Hizbullah said the repeated painful blows struck against Toumat Niha are the main cause of the collapse in the militiamen's morale. Toumat Niha was a hilltop position that had a commanding view over the western Bekaa to the east Jezzine to the west and the central sector to the south. Its primary role was to act as an observation post to provide early warnings of resistance activity to Israeli troops stationed in Kfar Houna five kilometers southwest of the Jezzine. Reports in Beirut said following the rebellion, the militia command is taking measures to fill the vacuum. There are also unconfirmed reports the Israelis may be pondering the option of replacing the militia's commander Antoine Lahd. Lahd has been in Paris with his family and some reports say he was angered by a statement by an Israeli deputy from the Likud party who questioned whether Israeli troops were in the south only to protect their local militia. Lahd demanded a formal apology but so far one was not made. Some reports said Israelis are studying the possibility of replacing Lahd with someone not from the ranks of the militia due to internal fighting".

Hizbullah Warns "Israel" (1)
"The Islamic resistance (IR) group of Hizbullah warned "Israeli" enemy's prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu Saturday against striking Lebanon to gain election votes, saying the country had been the "graveyard of many "Israeli" governments.''

"We warn Benjamin Netanyahu against using the south Lebanon card for electoral gains,'' Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, the leader of Hizbullah and the Islamic Resistance that is battling to oust ""Israel"" from occupied south Lebanon, told Reuters in Beirut.
""Israel"" is due to hold early elections on May 17 after Netanyahu's government failed to win broad support for his settlement moves with the Palestinians.

"The lives of people of the south should not become votes to stuff the ballot boxes. We want to remind Netanyahu that Lebanon was the graveyard of many "Israeli" governments and that betting on Lebanon to win the elections will backfire.''

"Sayyed Nasrallah was referring to the 17-day aggression launched by former "Israeli" prime minister Shimon Peres against Lebanon a few weeks before the 1996 election".

"The "Grapes of Wrath'' operation resulted in the deaths of 200 Lebanese people, mainly civilians, and Peres lost his bid for reelection after "Israeli" Arabs declined to vote for him in a close-run race."

"South Lebanon is the last active Arab-"Israeli" battle front. Twenty-four "Israeli" soldiers were killed there in resistance operations during 1998, prompting the "Israeli" entity to review its occupation of the area."

""Israel"" has occupied parts of the south for 20 years, including a 15 km (nine-mile) deep occupation zone it carved out in 1985.

Last week Hizbullah lobbed scores of Katyusha rockets across the border after a mother and her six children were slaughtered during an "Israeli" air raid around Baalbek, near Lebanon's eastern border with Syria.

""Israel's"" cabinet vowed Thursday to hit targets within Lebanon to avenge the rocket attacks, which wounded 16 "Israelis".

Sayyed Nasrallah said such threats would not spare the "Israeli" entity or halt the Islamic Resistance's war of attrition against the occupation.

"Words won't do them any good, they must take action. They must withdraw unconditionally, and if they really want to avoid the Katyushas, they should not attack civilians,'' his Eminence said.

"We only launch the Katyushas at occupied northern Palestine to revive the understanding which protects civilians.''

"Under the "April Understanding'' which ended the 1996 "Israeli" aggression, the resistance men and the "Israeli" occupation army are barred from striking civilian targets, but not military ones.

Despite the understanding, the "Israeli" aggressions claimed 28 Lebanese civilian lives last year"

January Monday 4 -1999:

A frustrating year for occupation troops
"A spate of activity, both military and political, in the last few weeks of 1998 has cast the debate over the future of south Lebanon wide open".

"Rising "Israeli" military casualties in the south, growing opposition within the Jewish state to the occupation, a security Cabinet divided over the best way to respond to Hizbullah and ­ most worrisome of all ­ a looming general election have ensured a very uncertain start to the new year".

"The present climate contrasts with the beginning of 1998, when the "Israeli" government offered a political solution that was perhaps the first serious attempt to extricate the troops from the blood-soaked wadis and hills of the south".

"Israeli" (War) Defense Minister Yitzhak Mordechai dropped a bombshell in a Jan. 3 interview with Paris-based Al-Watan Al-Arabi newspaper by stating that "Israel" was ready to comply with UN Security Council Resolution 425. It was the first time that a senior "Israeli" official had recognized 425, which was adopted following Israels 1978 invasion".

"But this apparent magnanimous gesture was tempered by "Israel" stating that Resolution 425 would be fulfilled only if Lebanon agreed to deploy the Army in the former occupation zone, dismantle the resistance, absorb members of the SLA into the Army, and maintain security in the south through or with Unifil".

"Lebanon and Syria rejected the proposal outright, declaring that Resolution 425 was unconditional. In the face of blanket opposition from Beirut and Damascus there was little "Israel" could do, although it tried hard to garner the support of the U.S., France and the UN".

"Washington was cautious in its response to the "Israeli" move. Privately, U.S. diplomats expressed doubts as to "Israels" sincerity, noting that the original offer had been made through the media rather than through normal channels. There was little UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan could do without the support of Washington, although it is that clear he would like to see 425 fulfilled, be it conditionally or unconditionally".

"France and the European Union sided ­ at least publicly ­ with Lebanon and Damascus".

"The "Israeli" Cabinet, amid much fanfare, formally adopted as policy the conditional acceptance of 425 April 1. Since then little more has been heard on the subject".

"Meanwhile, the fighting continued in the south unabated, with 1,302 attacks recorded against the occupation forces, the highest number since 1985."

"Hizbullah made greater use of their deadly roadside bombs, which claimed 16 of the 23 "Israeli" soldiers to die in the zone during the year".

"The multi-faith Lebanese Resistance Brigades, the brainchild of Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, launched their first operation in February, attacking the Haddatha post in the western sector. The Brigades, which are trained and led by seasoned Hizbullah fighters, staged attacks through out the year but their effectiveness remains limited".

"The "Israeli" Army adopted a defensive policy in 1998 after the offensive tactics of 1997 helped fuel the largest annual fatality rate in the south since 1985, with 39 killed. Foot patrols were reduced and millions of dollars were spent reinforcing the fortifications of their hilltop bunkers".

"With fewer open targets to strike, Hizbullah increasingly took to long-range mortar attacks, with negligible results against "Israeli" troops sitting in their underground bunkers. But the new "Israeli" tactics allowed Hizbullah greater freedom to roam the zone, with fatal consequences in November when seven "Israeli" soldiers were killed in the space of 10 days. All seven were killed in roadside bomb attacks, two of them in almost exactly the same place and all three very close to the "Israeli" border.

"Hizbullah launched two mass Katyusha strikes into northern "Israel" during the year, both in response to civilian casualties caused by "Israeli" attacks".

"The first attack Aug. 25 provided a crucial test for the "Israeli" government. The "Israelis" had to ask themselves whether they were willing to escalate the conflict by responding heavily or whether discretion was the more prudent option".

"The "Israelis" chose discretion and gave Hizbullah a significant psychological victory. In the past, anyone who launched rockets into "Israel" could count on a counter-strike  nowadays Hizbullah has the last word. The primitive Katyusha rocket has become, in the Israeli-Lebanese arena, a strategic weapon in Lebanese hands, wrote Zvi Barel, a commentator for "Israels" Haaretz newspaper".

"The second attack came Dec. 22 and was in response to the killing of seven members of a family in an air raid on the Bekaa. Again "Israeli" ministers strutted up and down the length of the border making all manner of bellicose threats. But, as in August, the "Israelis" had little choice but to back down".

"Israels" military knows very well that if civilian targets are attacked in Lebanon, Hizbullah would immediately retaliate with Katyusha attacks on Galilee thus, for Israel, defeating the point of the occupation in the first place. Clearly, the "Israeli" Army is itching to hit Hizbullah directly but it cannot find the targets. Such is the dilemma facing the "Israelis".

"Israels" frustration was indicated by the decision to hit the radio antenna in Janta in December in which the seven civilians were killed. One might be justified in asking how bombing the antenna of a minor radio station over 80 kilometers from the "Israeli" border serves to protect the population of northern Galilee. Especially as the bungled raid led the residents of Kiryat Shimona to spend the night in their bomb shelters".

"As if the tit-for-tat escalations were not enough, "Israels" general election scheduled for May 17 has cast a very unpredictable element into the debate. For the time being, the Israeli government is taking the advice of its military to maintain the occupation at its present level. Partial or full withdrawals have been ruled out as have serious escalatory measures".

"But this could change either way at a moments notice. Netanyahu has reportedly lent tacit backing for a staged pullout as advocated by Foreign Minister Ariel Sharon, which would apparently include the return of two villages on the Golan Heights to Syria".

"But he has also warned that further Katyusha strikes into "Israel" will meet with severe reprisals. Hizbullah, in turn, has warned "Israel" that Lebanese civilian casualties will prompt Katyusha attacks".

"With typical morbid timing, five civilians were wounded Sunday in an air raid on the radio antenna in Janta, a copycat attack that led to the last cross-border rocket strike just 13 days ago".

"Unifils political adviser and spokesman, Timur Goksel, acknowledges that the "Israelis" have finally begun a genuine debate over how to leave the south. Weve started the inevitable process of leading to a withdrawal, he said. Were not seeing the light at the end of the tunnel but we are seeing the tunnel.

Hizbullah repulses "Israeli" commando raid on Shaqra
""Israeli" troops and Hizbullah guerrillas clashed on the edge of Shaqra village early Friday morning when a commando unit attempted to penetrate north of the zone, possibly with the intention of staging an ambush or laying roadside bombs."

"The operation came as further evidence that the "Israeli" Army is initiating an offensive campaign against the resistance, in which raids north of the zone by special forces units and air strikes deep into Lebanon will play a major role".

"The "Israeli" Army confirmed Friday that a commando squad acted against a cell that plants bombs near hostile villages.

"There was no mention of the specific aim of the operation.
Hizbullah said, however, that the "Israeli" unit had been spotted approaching Shaqra and was then ambushed by guerrillas.

As soon as the "Israeli" patrol reached the outskirts of the village, a resistance unit detonated a roadside bomb against a unit of the infiltrating "Israeli" force, scoring certain casualties, a statement said.

The statement added that "Israeli" helicopter gunships had been driven off by ground fire from the fighters.

Security sources confirmed that a gunbattle had occurred at Shaqra at around 1.30 A.M., but were unable to say who initiated the clash.

The "Israeli" incursion had all the hallmarks of an operation staged Dec. 17, when members of the Flotilla 13 naval commando unit shot and killed a 17-year-old boy on the outskirts of Shaqra.
Despite Shaqras strategic location on the edge of the zone, above the notorious Wadi Salouqi and facing several "Israeli" outposts, the vicinity of the village is rarely used as a staging ground for resistance attacks.

This raises the question why "Israeli" commandos have sought on two separate occasions to penetrate the surroundings of the village.

The target of the fatal Dec. 17 raid remains unknown, although it is believed the commandos were planning to lay roadside bombs in the area to kill guerrillas. The operation failed after they were spotted by Mohsen Khallaf, who was shot dead before he could raise the alarm. Security sources said that Fridays operation could have been a second attempt to lay roadside charges.

Regardless of the aim of the operation, it is apparent that the "Israeli" Army is now adopting last months decisions by the "Israeli" government to take an increasingly offensive approach in dealing with the resistance.

"These have included increased air raids on areas facing the zone, the two deep-penetration air strikes on a Hizbullah radio transmitter near Janta in the Bekaa and the planting and detonation of a roadside charge near Baalbek which killed a Hizbullah bomb expert".

""Israeli" special forces units, such as Flotilla 13 and Egoz, are expected to return to the frontline by staking out infiltration routes used by the resistance to penetrate the zone.

The aggressive tactics come in contrast to last years policy of static defense in which millions of
dollars were spent beefing up the defenses of outposts while foot patrols were reduced."

"The defensive strategy was adopted following record military fatalities in 1997, which, ironically, were partially caused by the same offensive policy that the "Israeli" army has chosen to reintroduce. Such is the cyclical nature of the war in south Lebanon" (1)
