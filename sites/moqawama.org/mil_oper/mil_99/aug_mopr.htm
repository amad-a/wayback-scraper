August Tuesday 10 -1999:

IR scored new victory "Israeli" allied militia redeploys deeper into occupied zone(1)
"Battered by months of resistance assaults, the SLA* militia has abandoned its frontline position at Qantara in the central sector and is preparing to redeploy to a new post deeper inside the occupation zone.

"The militias departure from the outpost comes two months after the SLA vacated the old Beit Yahoun compound in the western sector. The move is part of a re-organization of the militias frontline outposts during the unpredictable final stages of the occupation.

"The pullout began Saturday evening and by dawn Sunday the estimated 10 militiamen departed having dismantled the post and removed all weapons, ammunition, surveillance equipment and electrical fittings.

"The decision to redeploy, however, has left the SLA post at Qsair, 3 kilometers north of Qantara, completely isolated. The SLA withdrew from the Alman position, 2 kilometers east of Qsair in 1994. "Now, with the departure of the militia from Qantara, Qsair is the only remaining outpost in the area.

A security source admitted that the decision in this respect was puzzling.

Qsair has been left extremely isolated. There will be no reinforcements in case of attack, the source said. The militiamen in the Qsair outpost ­ which is located in the corner formed by Wadi Hojeir running into the Litani river gorge ­ cannot expect reinforcements if they come under full frontal attack as the nearest post at Salaah is 5 kilometers to the east, the source said.

"During the past two months, the resistance has concentrated its attacks on Qantara and the "Israeli"-manned outpost at Taibeh. Even during Julys lull in military activity, Qantara was still subject to almost daily mortar attacks.

"However, SLA casualties in Qantara were not particularly high given the number of assaults. In the past two years, three militiamen have been killed and seven wounded. One "Israeli" soldier was also wounded in Qantara.

""Israel" Radio confirmed the withdrawal of the militia from Qantara and said the unit would man a new position which is easier to defend and better equipped. The occupants are expected to redeploy to a new outpost under construction on a small hill just east of the "Israeli" Taibeh post. The new SLA position will have views of several villages facing the central sector as well the open grassland between Qantara and Taibeh.

"The success of the resistance in forcing the militia to abandon the Qantara post will likely encourage increased attacks on Qsair in the next few weeks in the hope of forcing a further SLA withdrawal.

Hizbullahs commander in the south, Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, described the withdrawal as a new victory for the resistance.

By forcing the enemy to pull back from its position in Qantara, which was turned into a real hell-hole, the resistance has scored a new victory, he said in a statement.

This headlong flight is the inevitable result of the heroic actions and sacrifices by our fighters who have managed to paralyze the enemys military force and its agents and prevent them holding this position, he said.

"The redeployment from Qantara is the most significant development so far as the SLA militia undergoes a reorganization program for the final round of the south Lebanon conflict.

"Many of the most heavily hit frontline positions manned by the SLA are being refortified with the addition of underground bunkers and reinforced concrete roofs over previously open areas.

"The move is an attempt by the "Israelis" to bolster the flagging morale of the militia. At the end of 1997, the "Israeli" Army launched a multi-million dollar program to beef up the defenses of its outposts in the occupation zone but neglected the SLA positions".

August Wednesday 18 -1999:

Three "Israelis" killed in fierce south fighting Resistance keeps its vow to avenge killing of chief(1)
"Fulfilling its pledge to avenge the assassination of a senior resistance commander, Hizbullah fighters on Tuesday engaged "Israeli" forces in the most intense and prolonged battle of the year, killing three soldiers and wounding five.

""Israel" denied that it was behind Mondays bombing in Sidon in which Ali Deeb, a Hizbullah security officer, was killed.
But the government and the resistance remained adamant that only "Israel" would benefit from the assault, which jolted Sidon while still reeling from the assassination of the four judges in June. Despite the "Israeli" denials, politicians in Beirut have linked the two events to a purported "Israeli" scheme to destabilize the country.

"On Tuesday, gun battles raged in the south as mourners in Beiruts suburb of Haret Hreik turned out in the thousands to bury Deeb. The fighting was the first serious outbreak in the south since "Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak took office last month.
""Israeli" warplanes staged at least three raids on areas facing the central sector Monday night and Tuesday morning as Hizbullah mortar and short-range Katyusha rocket teams traded blows with "Israeli" artillery for most of the day. Four Lebanese civilians were also wounded in the clashes.

The fighting began with a series of standard mortar attacks overnight on the Salaah, Taibeh and Mheisbat outposts at the northern end of the central sector. "Israeli" warplanes fired missiles at midnight into Wadi Qaissieh between Majdal Selem and Khorbet Selem. "But the fighting markedly escalated at about 7.30am when a Hizbullah squad ambushed an "Israeli" commando patrol in Wadi Slouqi. A statement from the group said that fierce battles broke out as the fighters hurled hand grenades and fired machine guns at the "Israelis".

""Israeli" artillery pounded the area and fired smoke rounds in an attempt to provide cover while extracting the dead and wounded. A second Hizbullah team attacked "Israeli" reinforcements, preventing them from reaching their beleaguered comrades who were pinned down in the wadi by heavy mortar fire.

"An "Israeli" Merkava tank which was shelling the areas opposite the zone came under fire from the resistance. The statement said that the fighters forced the tank to cease firing.

""Israeli" helicopter gunships began strafing the area while Hizbullah guerrillas targeted "Israeli" soldiers retreating toward Wadi Istabl on the outskirts of the Houla position.

"Heavy shelling continued in the area accompanied by up to six air raids as "Israeli" warplanes fired missiles into Wadi Qaissieh as well as the area around Jmaijmeh and Shaqra, 2 kilometers to the south.

"At 11.35am, Hizbullah said that six Hummer and Rio all-terrain vehicles, transporting a large number of "Israeli" soldiers to the old Houla position, were targeted with rocket-propelled grenades. At the same time, resistance mortar teams stepped up the shelling of the "Israeli" troops trapped in Wadi Slouqi.

The cries and screams of the Zionist soldiers were heard clearly, the statement said.

It was unclear when the "Israeli" casualties occurred. They raised the "Israeli" death toll this year to 12, with another 44 wounded.

"The "Israeli" casualties were the highest in a single incident this year since three officers were killed and five wounded in a similar ambush near Maidoun in February.

Barak, who has pledged to withdraw "Israeli" troops from Lebanon within a year, suggested that Tuesdays fighting will be what makes it possible in the end to remove the "Israeli" Army from Lebanon by agreement and deploy it on the international border.

Security sources, remarking on the intensity of the fighting, said that more than 200 artillery rounds were fired into the Shaqra area by the "Israelis" with Hizbullahs mortar teams responding every few minutes with salvoes of between 10 and 15 rounds each. The sources said that 139 high explosive and smoke rounds of 155mm caliber alone hit the area around Shaqra and Wadi Slouqi.

Theres a genuine battle being fought on military lines out there, a security source said.

"Israeli" troops were spotted carrying three stretchers in Wadi Slouqi at one point"(1)

"For its part, the Islamic Resistance's (IR) issued a statement in which it announced that the mujahedeen Monday night and Tuesday dawn staged a series of operations against the "Israeli" occupation forces and their collaborators inflicting definite casualties in their ranks.

IR once again affirmed that it possesses the initiative and that the martyr leaders' blood only adds up to the Resistance men's resolution, strength and efficiency in the grounds of Jihad and confrontation.

At 11:30, the enemy brought additional reinforcements that included six Hammer and Rio vehicles that carried a large number of troops to the vicinity of the old Houla outpost. "ISRAELI" MINISTER CALLS FOR IMMEDIATE WITHDRAWAL FROM Lebanon(2)
""Israeli" radio today (yesterday) reported that "Israeli" Religious Affairs Minister Yitzhak Cohen of the Sephardi ultra-Orthodox Shas party has called for an immediate withdrawal from Lebanon following deadly clashes between "Israel" and the Hizbullah resistance movement. Cohen said that "Israel" must withdraw unilaterally from Lebanon without further delay. "I think that "Israel" should withdraw from the Lebanon immediately. I am going to raise the issue of the immediate unilateral withdrawal in the cabinet meeting on Sunday," Cohen told the radio. "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak said in response that he will withdraw "Israeli" forces from Lebanon as he promised, within a year after he took office on July 6, in a responsible manner and not in a haphazard manner, the radio said. A spokesman for Barak added that the issue of Lebanon was discussed by the government Wednesday, but she refused to give any substantive information. "Israel's" occupation of Lebanese land is one of the most contentious political issues in "Israel", especially after painful resistance attacks against enemy forces. Renewed opposition to the 21-year occupation of southern Lebanese surfaced when three "Israeli" occupation soldiers were killed in clashes with Hizbullah on Tuesday and seven soldiers were wounded. The toll is the highest in a single incident since eight "Israelis", including Brigadier General Erez Gerstein who was the highest-ranking officer in Lebanon, were killed in Hizbullah attacks in February. The clash came a day after Hizbullah leader Ali Dib, also known as Khodr Salame, was blown up in his car in Sidon, the main city of southern Lebanon. "Israel" welcomed the assassination Tuesday, without claiming the responsibility attributed to it by the Hizbullah. A senior "Israeli" military official was quoted by AFP on Wednesday that he was "100 percent certain" there was no connection between the assassination of Salame and the attack on "Israeli" forces. But Hizbullah is not buying "Israel's" denials. Blaming "Israel" for Salame's death, Hizbullah swore to be avenged on its leaders. The resistance fighters who attacked the "Israeli" patrol on Tuesday called themselves "the martyr Ali Dib" commando", security services in Lebanon said.

"ISRAEL" INCREASES SECURITY WORLDWIDE AFTER DEATH OF HIZBULLAH LEADER(4)
" "Israel" has decided to increase the security in its embassies, consulates and official institutions worldwide, following the assassination Monday of Khodor Salameh, a senior official of the Lebanese Hizbullah organization. Hizbullah has accused "Israel" of being behind the death of Salameh, and the "Israeli" Ma'ariv daily reported Wednesday that "Israeli" security officials believe some groups, including from Hizbullah, will try to take revenge on the Jewish state, either in south Lebanon or elsewhere in the world. Hizbullah, which is the leading group that is resisting "Israeli" occupation of southern Lebanon, immediately accused "Israel" of responsibility and vowed to avenge the death of the official. "Israel" has yet to admit responsibility for the killing".

"The physical and material impacts of the Wadi al-Slouqi confrontations have gone beyond the human and material losses in the ranks of the enemy to reach the "Israeli" troops' morale.

"The enemy media talked elaborately about the feebleness, cowardice, defeatism that was shown clearly in the ranks of "Israeli" troops during and after the confrontations.

"Two "Israeli" dailies, Ha'aretz and Yediot Ahronot, published the details of the clashes, the fact that the three Hizbullah men discovered the Golani force which was lying in wait for them, how the Hizbullah men managed to approach the "Israeli" force silently and shoot and hurl grenades at the elite of the "Israeli" army.

"Yediot Ahronot said the circumstances of the clashes raised a lot of question marks regarding the performance of the elite troops.

"Ha'aretz asked how three Hizbullah fighters were able to take aback the huge and trained Golani force, and how the few defeated the many despite the fact that the battle took place at very close distance in a rocky and complicated terrain. The newspaper noted the precision of Hizbullah's artillery that, in spite of the "Israeli" air raids, was able to prevent the "Israelis" from evacuating their casualties, besides "weakening the efficiency of the "Israeli" aircraft" with the aid of anti-aircraft fire.

"In a bid to contain the repercussions of the Wadi al-Slouqi confrontations, the occupation command formed a panel headed by the occupation commander in Lebanon Gabi Ashkenazi. The committee that also included a number of "Israeli" senior officers is supposed to investigate the details of the confrontations and how three Hizbullah men were able to defeat 15 of the Golani elite troops (later became 75 troops at the end of the clashes) backed by helicopters and warplanes besides the most sophisticated weaponry.

"The panel aims also to attempt to spare the "Israeli" army more accusations and mockery by the "Israeli" press which is talking about the "cowardice and feebleness of the troops" and the inability of their command to urge them to perform their duties in the batterfield".

Jewish state faces storm after the calm(2)
"The killing of two "Israeli" soldiers in fierce clashes with Hizbullah fighters this week has provided a rude awakening for "Israel" which had been lulled into a false belief that Syria had instructed the organization to tone down its resistance activities in preparation for a resumption of the peace process.

"Tuesdays close-quarter battle in Wadi Slouqi was a clear indication that Hizbullah has no intention of de-escalating its drive to oust the "Israeli" Army from the south.

""Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak has committed himself to a withdrawal by June next year by first striking a deal with Syria. This policy has received the endorsement of a new report undertaken by the Tel Aviv Universitys Jaffe Center for Strategic Studies, which concludes that the occupation must be maintained until a peace treaty is signed with Syria. The report, entitled "Israel" and Southern Lebanon, was submitted to Barak on Wednesday.

"According to the Haaretz daily, it examined all options that have been raised by previous "Israeli" governments. These include expanding the zone to the Litani River ­ a drastic measure advocated by the former "Israeli" agriculture minister Rafael Eitan in the mistaken belief that such a move would place northern "Israel" beyond the 22-kilometer range of Katyusha rockets.

"Other measures include the perennial favorite of bombing Lebanons infrastructure every time an "Israeli" soldier was killed in the south, hitting Syrian military targets in Lebanon, and a unilateral withdrawal following security guarantees from Beirut.

"The report acknowledged that a unilateral withdrawal would reduce military casualties but at the increased risk of danger to the residents of northern "Israel". It assumed that Syria would encourage Hizbullah to continue attacking northern "Israel" to place pressure on the "Israeli" government to hand back the Golan Heights.

"The most startling recommendation is that if Barak intends to abide by his pre-election withdrawal pledge ­ and peace has yet to be agreed with Syria ­ "Israel" should establish a channel of indirect communication with Hizbullah to assess the organizations post-withdrawal plans".

Another 29 surrendered collaborators sentenced(2)
"A military court sentenced 29 former pro-"Israeli" militiamen Thursday to prison terms ranging from six months to two years for collaboration with the enemy.

"The sentences raised to 92 the number of former South Lebanon Army* member who have stood trial since "Israel" withdrew from the Jezzine area in June.

"They are among 220 militiamen who voluntarily surrendered to the authorities after the withdrawal, preferring to stay in their hometowns rather than retreat with the SLA to new positions in the "Israeli"-occupied zone further south.

"Of the 92 militiamen put on trial, 83 have received jail terms of three months to two years. Another nine have been acquitted or
had their cases dropped because they were elderly.

"The cases are heard by the Military Tribunal, which holds weekly sessions in Beirut under Brigadier General Maher Safieddine, the presiding judge.

"The trials have usually opened on a Wednesday afternoon and ended around midnight with the handing down of verdicts and sentences.

"Relatives of the militiamen have complained that the sentences are too harsh.

Defense lawyers have argued that the defendants were victims of circumstance, not traitors, who joined the militia under duress or financial straits.

"But the sentences imposed on the convicts have been considerably more lenient than the seven-year sentences proposed by the indictments.

"Meanwhile, a Lebanese soldier and a former bodyguard of Speaker Nabih Berri was accused of spying for "Israel" along with members of his family.

"First Adjutant Hussein Alayyan, who was arrested at his home in the South on Aug. 9; his mother Khadijeh Hussein Mroue; and his wife Rola Ali Hassan, are in custody.

His brother Hassan; father Ali Mustafa Alayyan; and a friend, Youssef Siblini, are still being sought by the authorities.
"They have all been accused of contacting "Israel's" Mossad foreign intelligence service and providing information on the activities of resistance guerrillas, as well as the Lebanese and Syrian arm.
