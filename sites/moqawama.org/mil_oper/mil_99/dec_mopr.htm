Israeli military warns of war with Syria after south pullout(1)
Israeli Army intelligence appears to have strengthened its opposition to a unilateral withdrawal by warning Premier Ehud Barak on Tuesday that pulling the troops from the south would increase the chances of direct conflict with Syria.

The assessment came as the European Unions Middle East envoy, Miguel Angel Moratinos, on a visit to Beirut, said that Baraks priority was to order a withdrawal within the framework of a peace accord with Lebanon and Syria.

Israels Maariv newspaper quoted an intelligence assessment which argued that Syria would seek to destabilize the Lebanon-Israel border in the event of a unilateral withdrawal.

A scenario was presented to Barak and Israeli Chief of Staff General Shaul Mofaz envisaging how an escalation could occur between Israel and Syria in the event of a unilateral withdrawal. If Israel staged such a pullout, the study argued, Syria would lose a vital bargaining chip in future negotiations over the Golan Heights.

Syria will not allow Israel to enjoy all worlds, the assessment said. There will not be a situation in which Israel will live in peace and quiet on the Lebanese border while it continues to control the Golan Heights.

The report predicts a swift escalation of violence in which armed groups in the south stage cross-border assaults, sparking retaliatory air strikes against Lebanese infrastructure targets.

In response, the groups would launch Katyusha rocket attacks as far as Haifa, Israels third most populous city, the scenario suggests.

Israels counter-strike could spark an escalation that could lead to a direct confrontation with Syria, the study said.

Barak has pledged to pull the Israeli Army out of Lebanon within the framework of an agreement with Syria by July 2000. He has refused to declare his course of action should a deal with Syria fail to materialize but has not ruled out staging an unconditional withdrawal.

Israeli military intelligence, in its regular briefings to the Knesset, consistently warns of the perils of staging a unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon, arguing that guerrilla groups would continue to launch attacks into Israel. However, the scenario presented to Barak and Mofaz is the first known time that the military has suggested that a unilateral withdrawal could lead to a direct conflict with Syria.

The assessment contradicts comments made by Mofaz two weeks ago in which he stated that the operational situation in south Lebanon had changed and that a unilateral withdrawal was not as risky as in the past.

Mofaz comments were hardly a ringing endorsement for staging such a withdrawal but came in line with several Israeli officials openly declaring that a unilateral pullout should be conducted.

Three Israeli troops wounded in IR operations
Three Israeli soldiers were wounded on Thursday when Hizbullah fighters attacked the clifftop radar station at Bayada with mortars and rockets.

The casualties were the first suffered by Israel since the beginning of October.

An officer and a soldier were moderately wounded and another soldier was lightly hurt in the attack, according to an Israeli Army statement.

So far this year, 12 Israeli soldiers have been killed and 57 wounded in south Lebanon.

Three months ago, the Israeli Army adopted a strategy of reducing troop movements and instead relying more on surveillance technology to monitor the perimeter of the zone while responding to resistance attacks with air strikes.

The new tactic has had mixed results. While it has failed to reduce resistance attacks ­ October recorded the highest number of assaults against the occupation forces since 1982 ­ Israeli casualties have dropped. Given that a withdrawal from the south seems now to be only a matter of months away, keeping casualties to the minimum while not compromising the defense of Galilee is the primary concern of the Israeli military. Launching offensive operations against Hizbullah fighters and other resistance groups is no longer a priority.

Although Hizbullah is having no problem staging long-range mortar and rocket attacks, it is experiencing difficulties in finding occupation forces to kill now that almost all Israeli soldiers spend their tours of duty hidden beneath reinforced concrete that protect their outposts.

Even the SLA* militiamen, traditionally a softer target than their better equipped, trained and protected Israeli allies, have received morale-boosting defensive measures. The Israelis have improved the fortifications of the militias outposts, issued new equipment and, most importantly, given them a pay raise.

The SLA is painfully aware that the end is approaching. But given the lack of alternative employment in the impoverished zone, the lure of monthly salaries coupled with the advantage of family members augmenting their incomes by working in Israel will probably ensure that most continue serving until the Israelis leave.

With the Israeli Army in a defensive and fairly safe holding pattern in the south and the Jewish states air force gaining plenty of combat practice in the skies above, there is little public pressure on Barak to withdraw unilaterally anytime soon.

This does not alter the fact that in the event of peace talks with Syria failing to resume by July next year, Barak will have little choice but to honor his promise to his electorate and disengage the Israeli Army from south Lebanon, even without an agreement.

But if Hizbullah is able to break the status quo and suddenly inflict serious casualties among Israeli troops, the whole debate in Israel over a unilateral withdrawal will take on a new urgency. Barak may find that public pressure will not allow him the luxury of keeping the Israeli Army inside Lebanon until next July(1)

Barak says he wants out(1)
Saying that Israel must decisively sever the Gordian knot that binds it to Lebanon, Premier Ehud Barak said on Friday that he would wait until April before deciding if a unilateral withdrawal were necessary.

Barak repeated his vow to withdraw Israeli troops from the south by July 2000 in the framework of an agreement with Beirut and Damascus.

He listed seven issues which needed to be negotiated with Syria: Lebanon, a territorial compromise over the Golan Heights, terrorism, water, diplomatic normalization, the opening of borders and the establishment of an early-warning system.

Barak refused to be drawn out on what would happen to his withdrawal pledge if he failed to reach an agreement with Syria.
It would not be reasonable to discuss such an eventuality today, he said. We shall wait until April to see.

He reiterated his contention that a proper agreement with Syria is preferable, in order to ensure the future stability of the border with Lebanon.

However, he justified his decision to set a time limit for the withdrawal without being assured beforehand of having an agreement.

Without a time limit, nothing ever happens, and weve been bogged down in Lebanon for 17 years, he said.
