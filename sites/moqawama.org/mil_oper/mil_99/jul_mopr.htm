July Wednesday 21 -1999:

Hizbullah vows no halt to operations ...(1)
Hizbullah said Tuesday there would be neither a halt nor a decrease in military operations, regardless of the peace negotiations, pledging the resistance would continue with casualty-inflicting attacks against Israeli troops and their militia allies.

Mohammed Raad, head of Hizbullahs politburo, also denied reports that Syria had told the organization to dismantle its military wing. He insisted that Syria viewed Hizbullah as a useful bargaining tool in the forthcoming negotiations with Israel over the fate of the Golan Heights.

The Syrians will never ask us to stop resisting the occupation of south Lebanon, Raad said in an interview with The Daily Star. The Syrians do not trust Israel because they failed to honor so many agreements.

Raad, who is also a Nabatieh MP, said that Israeli premier Ehud Barak is asking Syria to curb Hizbullahs military activities for domestic propaganda purposes.

Barak is not being realistic in asking for quiet in south Lebanon. Can Syria ask Israel to cut ties with the United States? Raad asked.

 Hizbullahs operations against the occupation forces fall into two general categories: quality and quantity.

Quality operations, which include roadside bomb attacks, close-quarter ambushes in the occupation zone and missile strikes on armored vehicles, claim the bulk of Israeli and collaborator casualties.

Quantity operations consist of daily long-range mortar bombardments of Israeli and collaborator outposts, which tend to harass the occupation forces rather than kill or wound them.

 The party has gained a high profile and nationwide respect for its role in fighting the occupation and, as such, risks losing face if it is seen abandoning its resistance activities before peace.

But could Hizbullah offer a compromise in which it would cease quality operations but carry on, albeit at a reduced level, to give the appearance of continuing the resistance?

This is not an option, said Raad, ruling out any compromise. Instead, he hinted that the conflict could escalate soon.

This period could be the calm before the storm. I know something has to happen, he said. Quality attacks that inflict casualties will continue regardless of (peace) negotiations. Expect all types of operations as long as there is occupation, Raad added.

He said the present calm was nothing out of the ordinary.

There has been no political decision to stop or decrease military operations. Its happened before. It allows us to study (Israeli) movements on the ground, Raad said.

The MP emphasized that the military operations are decided by the fighters alone, with no outside interference.

Iran and Syria have no say in decisions over our operations. Not even our political leadership interferes. There is one political aim between Hizbullah, Iran, Syria and others and that is to oust Israel from south Lebanon. The military wing carries out this aim, he said.

Israeli occupation restructured collaborating militia ... (2)
Following the pressure and confusion experienced by the collaborating militia during the pullout from Jezzine and afterwards, which caused the dismantling of the militia, the Israeli occupation's command in south Lebanon lately restructured the militia according to a new system that replaced the previous one.

The Israelis divided the militia into three sections, taking into consideration the religion, sectarianism and the geography of the occupied zone.

Three phalanges are now in charge of the zone. One in the Hasbayya-Rashayya area controlled by a battalion of Druze collaborators and backed by some Muslim Sunni agents.

A second in the Marjeyoun area controlled by Christian Maronite collaborators and backed by other Christian agents.

The third one in the Bint Jbeil area controlled by Muslim Shiite collaborators.

Security sources said the Israeli occupation's command decided to assign Israeli officers to command the three battalions.

The Israeli soldiers will be gradually pulled out from the areas controlled by these battalions, but the Israeli will keep on providing artillery and aerial coverage when needed, the sources stated.

Furthermore, the sources warned that such a religious structure will cause problems among the three battalions members.
