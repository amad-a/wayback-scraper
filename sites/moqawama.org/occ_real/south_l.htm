Introduction
Since the creation of the state of Israel in 1948, South Lebanon has been bending beneath the yoke of aggressions, targeting southern people, land, and waters, as well as other Lebanese territories.

More than once, The International Community has intervened , maintaining Lebanons right to sovereignty, security, and stability. Nevertheless, Israel has been using force to impose the type of solution it needs, a solution which undermines Lebanons peace and sovereignty, and embraces hidden expansionist plans that Israel never dares to voice.

Lebanese people, security and economy have always been mugged. In fact, in 1978, 1982, 1993 and 1996, Israel launched brutal military ventures, aiming at subjugating Lebanon.

The real Israeli nuisance is currently the occupation of 1250 km2 in South Lebanon, i.e. 1/10 of the Lebanese territory. Yet, Israel keeps insisting that the occupied zone is a security strip, ignoring the Resolution 425 which calls on Israeli troops to withdraw from all the Lebanese territories.

Each one of us knows that the occupied zone has soon become a battle-field for Lebanese resistants and occupation troops. But what we do not realize is that Israel, through numerous pervasive ways, has always been seeking to judaize the region.

Our document deals with the goals which Israel has been eager to realize in Lebanon throughout History.

It also treats the various pains stemming from the direct Israeli attacks to Lebanon, as well as its fervent efforts to change the occupied zones nature.
The aim is, no doubt, to take advantage of the zones resources, before annexing it to Israeli territories, in one way or another.

1 - South Lebanon: A General View
South Lebanon (2011 km2) reaches more than 1/5 of the Lebanese overall area (10452 km2).

According to statistics, this region embraces 800,000 people, or around the quarter of the overall Lebanese population (3.6 to 4 million people).

South Lebanon encompasses 456 city and village, divided between two provinces: Nabatieh and the South. Unfortunately, the region was formerly neglected because of its instability, and mainly since the Israeli aggressions against Lebanon had begun in 1948. In fact, social and economic changes obliged southern people to emigrate and eke out their living either inside Lebanon or abroad. The emigration rate ranged between 40 and 45 % in the 1970, especially that the threatened security was making life conditions worse. Among emigrants were businessman and traders, as well as highly educated people and free professionals.

Despite all the odds , southern people has always been reputed for his pride, ambition, and attachment to land. Thats why a huge number of emigrants finally came back to invest in their own land, mainly farmers who still export cereals , citrus , tobacco, bananas, vegetables, oil, figs, and olives.

Furthermore, the occupation has crippled many centers and roads, and weakened the productivity of the whole region.

In 1989, it became evident that the 78.455 people who live in the occupied zone (which Israel names the security strip( are only 3 % of the officially registered population (204.445 people). In fact, a lot of southern emigrated, mainly the young whom the occupation ruined on both economic and security-wise. As we have already mentioned, some of them left the south for other free Lebanese lands, while others went abroad. As for the majority of the actual residents in the occupied zone, they are Lebanese resistants who fight Israeli occupation.

2 - Israeli plans throughout History.
Every now and then, Israeli officials confirm that they have no goals in Lebanon. Yet historical facts prove the contrary and highlight the Israeli determination to spoliate some Lebanese some Lebanese territories, or to lay them indirectly beneath its hegemony.

A - Before the Creation of Israel.
According to the memoirs of Theodor Hertzel (1), the first Zionist leader, Zionist pioneers- who have set the bases of the State of Israel at the beginning of the century - had mentioned South Lebanon and Cheikh mountain (in East Lebanon) in their writings. These two Lebanese spots are important both economically and militarily, and are rich in water, necessary to develop economic and social life in Palestine (before Israelis named it Israel). This importance was mentioned in the land of Israel, a book written by two Zionist leaders, David Ben Gorion and David Ben Zevi, before the Belfore promise was made in 1917, allowing the creation of a Jewish State in Palestine.

According to the book, the provincial government (moutasarrafia) of Mount Lebanon, which included neither the south nor other Lebanese parts at that time, is considered as the northern border of the Jewish State.

Moreover, the memorandum presented by the Zionist movement to the Supreme Council of the Peace Conference in Europe on the 3rd of February 1919, demarcated the Jewish State as follows:

    It starts in North Lebanon at a certain point near the city of Saida on the Mediterranean. It follows the junctions of water on the hills of Lebanese mountains, until Karaoun bridge in East Lebanon.
    It moves then towards Beira (a locality in West Beqaa), and follows the line separating the basin of Karn Valley from that of Teim Valley at Hasbaya.

    Then it joins a southern line separating the eastern versants of Cheikh mountain (also known as Haramoun mountain) from the western ones, till neighboring Beit gin, 

This memorandum entitled the declaration of the Zionist organization concerning Palestine considered that Cheikh mountain is Palestines true father of water.

It called on for an international convention which protects all the rights of the people living in South Litani, i.e. Jews in Great Palestine. For if these water resources were taken care of, they would serve both Lebanon and Palestines development. (3)

At Saint Remo conference in April 1920, the mandate of France over Syria (and Lebanon), and that of Britain over Palestine and Iraq were decided, without solving the border problem.

In the same year, France separated Lebanon from Syria so that they become two separate countries. It also enlarged Mount Lebanon Moutasarrafia to encompass other large lands such as South Lebanon, North Lebanon, and the Beqaa. As a result, the hegemony of a Lebanese pro-France minority was enhanced, along, with the discontent of other large Lebanese categories. Yet the Zionist movement was not satisfied.

It endeavored to lay the south and other Lebanese lands rich in water resources under the British mandate. That way, such lands would be annexed to the awaited Jewish State.
That Zionist aspiration didnt came true, because of the differences between French and British plans in the region.

Haim Waismen touched on the annexation plan through many letters to European States. In one letter addressed to the Italian delegation in Saint Remo Conference, he wrote:

    The French believe that we consider Litani River and Cheikh mountains as our northern borders only because we are promoting British colonialism of course, this thinking is a great mistake 
    (This Letter was published in the Israeli daily Adiot Ahronot on the 21stg March 1978). (4)
Meanwhile, the Zionist movement asked the high commissioner in Beirut to allow Jew settlers to live permanently in South Lebanon.
The French refused, and said:
      when you get Tyr and Sidon, you will ask for a new demarcation.
    The same claim was voiced again in 1924, and addressed to the French commissioner who reacted with much reservation (5).
In 1925, the French consul in Jerusalem Gaston Magras sent a report to the French prime minister. He warned him for the expansionist policy adapted by Zionists who seek to realize their aspirations.
Zionists desire to claim for Tyr and its suburbs. They also desire Litanis waters which provide them with water energy needed to implement electric plans.

Undoubtedly, Jews hold a grudge against Tyr and Sidon since old times. These two damned cities (as Jews believe) ought to be destroyed so thats prophecies would came true. Nonetheless, hatred and need do not create any right for Jews in Tyr and Sidon (6).

The editorial of the bulletin Palestine, published by Zionists at those days, called for the annexation of Litani River to Palestine, soon after hogging Palestine itself thanks to the British mandate.
In November 1920, it was mentioned in the bulletin that the Litani provides Palestine with irrigation and electricity, and if any obstacle is opposed, momentarily, to its direct annexation, we insist that at least Palestine must take advantage of its waters.

The access to the waters of the Leontes (Litani) is not territorial possession, but only the right of storing and using its waters (Palestine Vol 3 n 8, October 1920, p.59)

In 1934, mandatory British authorities cooperated with the Zionist organizations, allowing Jews to get the privilege of dehydrating the Houla river. They were also allowed to invest its lands instead of Lebanese who abandoned their rights and sold them to Jews. (8)

In 1943, a joint study was made by the engineering bureau of Afred Nakkash, with the cooperation of Jewish engineers. It highlighted that Lebanon does not take advantage of more than 1/9 of Litanis water and, therefore, recommended the reconversion of a great amount of these waters at a certain point near the lake of Ibl El-Saqi (in Hasbaya, Lebanon). This way would flow into a tunnel in Palestine, allowing Lebanon to take advantage of the whole electric energy produced thanks to the falling of waters from all mountainous Lebanese regions, of some of them, towards the valley of Jordan River (9).

B - After the creation of the Hebraic State.
After the creation of Israel in 1948, many projects concerning the annexation of Litanis water were made. There were Banjar project, Baker-Herza project, and Coton project.

David Ben Gorion, who was then prime minister, said on 14 May 1945:

Jews and Arabs are engaged in the water battle. The entity of Palestine depends on the results of this battle" (10).

In response to a letter sent by the French president Charles Degaulles in 1967, Ben Gorion said:

In 1964, the first Israeli chief of Staff Egal Eloun, who commanded the war of 1948 against Arabs, said echoing Ben Gorion:

And if we have resumed fighting, we would have freed our whole country (12).
Any way, Ben Gorion confirmed in 1948 that the State of Israel was created only on one piece of the land of Israel which is much bigger.

Ben Gorion said according to p.419 of the above mentioned book: We dont intend to maintain the sto-tus quo. We have to create a moving and dynamic state, prone to expand (14).

As for Shimon Peres, he refused in an interview with Reucelters agency on 27/2/1971, to present a map showing the borders of Israel. He said: Israel is not in a hurry to make such a map. In fact, even if this map showed the minimum of our borders, they (Arabs) would accuse us of expansionism. Therefore, we well prepare the map as soon as Arabs accept to negotiate directly with us (15).

Manahim Begin had the same opinion on 10/3/1971: Israel ought not to publish a map of its borders, since the minimum of borders will become during the negotiations a maximum which we call be asked to abandon. So, in all cases, such a map will do no good to us (16).

3 - Start of Chafe and Evolution of the Conflict

A - attacking and Subjugating Lebanon.
After the declaration of The State of Israel on 14 May 1948, battles erupted along the Arab borders with Palestine. Arabs had decided to help their Palestinian brothers who were expatriated, or whose rights in their own land became dependent on the Israeli settlers will.

During these battles, more particularly on 30 October 1948, Israelis occupied between 12 and 19 Lebanese villages, among which: Meis El-Jabal, Blida, Mohaibib, Markaba, Taibeh, Deir Siryan, Kfarkila, Yaroun,

Moreover, Israeli troops constructed settlements over the detriments of other villages like Naameh, instead of which they installed the Jewish farm of Naameh, and Khalsa which soon became Karyat Shmouna.

They also constructed the settlement of Meskav Aam at Odaisseh hills, and confiscated the villages and farms of Dwar, High Zouk, Low Zouk, Mansoura, Zweye`, Lazaza, Salhiye`, Khan Dabr, Chawke`, Ikret, and Kfarber om. (1)

Land seizing
Despite the announcement of the Israeli withdrawal from occupied Lebanese villages, - after Lebanon signed the truce in March 1949 - Israelis retained large lands of borderline villages (2). And during the following years, Lebanon endured Israeli attacks.

In fact, an Israeli military airplane downed a Lebanese civil airplane above Lebanese territories on 24 July 1950, two persons were killed - and eight wounded. Nine years later, on 18 May, 27 May, and 19 November 1959, three Lebanese airplanes were obliged to land in Israel. Not to mention 27 August 1965, when 2 women were killed, 2 houses and 3 bridges destroyed during the breaking into Lebanese Southern territories.

Then flowed air, sea, and land Israeli attacks. The most brutal was on 26 December 1968, when an Israeli commando attacked Beirut airport, destroying 13 civil planes. They pretended that an Israeli passenger was killed, and an air host wounded in the Israeli plane (Al), during an attack launched by 2 Palestinians fedayeens in Athena.

Finally, on 2 December 1975, 75 people were killed, and about 150 wounded in an Israeli raid against the 2 Palestinian refuge camps Badawi and Bared in North Lebanon (3).
The formation of a Collaborating Militia and of a Borderline Petty State.

Prior to 1976, Israel used to attack Lebanon, pretending that it targeted Palestinians. These lathers were given the right to hold arms in Lebanon, according to an agreement between the Palestinian Liberation Organization (PLO) and the Lebanese, in Cairo in 1968.

After 1976, Israel voiced another pretext: pretending to supply with provisions the habitants of borderline Lebanese villages which were besieged during battles, and intending to protect these villagers security, Israelis opened the first passages between Lebanon and Israel. And named them the Good wall.

That step was an introduction to the installation of a training camp for Lebanese who chose to collaborate with the Israeli army, and who were known later as the army of free Lebanon, commanded by the separatist major in the Lebanese army, Saed Haddad.

Meanwhile, the confessional flame was being stirred up, along with the civil war in Beirut and other Lebanese regions. The number of pro-Israeli militiamen amounted to hundreds belonging to the same Lebanese confession (4). Then, with full Israeli cooperation, the State of Free Lebanon was declared in 1979, to cover 800 km2 already under Israeli occupation (5).

The uncontrolled Palestinian arms might have indirectly been a good pretext which Israel needed to interfere in Lebanon and to dominate many of its territories. Of course, the aim of this interference was not to solve Lebanons interior problems.

In fact, on the 21st of July 1977, The Lebanese State and the PLO signed in Chtaura an agreement, staging the withdrawal of the Palestinian troops from the regions they occupy, and their standing in by the Lebanese army. This agreement aimed at hindering every Israeli interference in Lebanon. As a reaction, Saed Haddad held a press conference in the Israeli borderline colony of Mtolleh on 2 August 1977, and expressed his opposition to any Lebanese army deployment. Later on 9 November 1977, Israeli air forces destroyed the village of Azieh near Tyr. 65 persons were killed, and 68 injured (6).

Yet the most brutal aggression ever launched against Lebanon was on 14 March 1978. That days almost 30,000 Israeli soldiers, upheld by tanks, aircrafts and gunboats attacked large southern sectors. They even neared Tyr and Nabatieh, although the Israeli secretary of war Azar Weizman had confirmed that his troops wouldnt go 10 km beyond the security strip.

Almost 1100 km were occupied, 1168 persons killed and injured, and 80 % of southern villages and cities (358 city and village) damaged. According to the international Red Cross, around seven villages were totally destroyed, among which Khiam, Abbasieh and Ghandourieh .

moreover, so schools were destroyed, ten health clubs ruined, water, electricity, and telephone systems damaged, as well as other farms and worshipping places.

Meeting Lebanons request, the UN Security Council pronounced the Resolution 425 which avoided the condemnation of Israel. It called for the respect of Lebanons borders, sovereignty and political independence within its internationally acknowledged borders.

The Security Council also summoned Israel to stop its military operations in Lebanon, and to withdraw from its territories. As a reaction, Israel held that the international decision was invalid, since it didnt condemn what Israel called terrorism.

After their retreat, Israeli troops maintained almost 800 km2, which became in 1979 known as the State of Free Lebanon, headed by the separatist Saed Haddad. According to the Resolution 426, multinational units were deployed both in freed and occupied territories. They were supposed to supervise a comprehensive Israeli withdrawal and to assist Lebanese army in maintaining security.

Yet the comprehensive withdrawal from Lebanon never took place, and the units stayed in Lebanon. Their mandate is renewed every six months, and their role is still insignificant . any way, many were wounded or killed during attacks by Israeli of pro-Israeli troops.

Between 1953 and 1982, the General Assembly of the UN and the International Security Council pronounced a series of resolutions denouncing Israeli Aggressions against Lebanon, among which:

    Resolution 270 on 26/8/1969; Resolution 280 on 19/5/1970; Resolution 313 on 28/2/1972; Res.316 on 26/6/1972; Res. 332 on 21/4/1973; Res. 337 on 15/8/1983; Res. 347 on 24/4/1974; Res. 3331 on 17/12/1974; Res. 425 on 20/3/1978; Res. 426 on 20/3/1978; Res. 508 on 20/6/1982; Res. 509 on 20/6/1982; Res. 524 on 12/7/1982; Res. 516 on 11/8/1982; Res. 520 on 17/9/1982; Res. 521 on 19/9/1982; Res. 515 on 29/9/1982; Res. 517 on 4/8/1982.
We notice that in 1982 only, ten consecutive resolutions were pronounced by the Security Council (8). Yet all of them did not prevent Israel from launching the most barbaric attack against Lebanon in 1982.

Israeli soldiers arrived in Beirut, Mount Lebanon, the south, and parts of the Beqaa 73.000 casualties were signaled, among whom 17825 dead, and 6000 arrested (the majority were civilians). Half a million people left their houses under the pressure of air, land, and sea shelling which wrecked havoc in houses and in infrastructures.

(2258 houses were destroyed totally, 9168 partially, not to mention numerous schools, hospitals and other damages on the industrial level. These damages were estimated to around 700 million dollars, while physical ones amounted to 1.9 billion dollars, according to a study made by the Development and Reconstruct Council.

For 83 days, Beirut was besieged, shelled by tens of thousands of bombs, and smitten until its premises fell on peoples heads. The invasion ended with a horrible slaughter , which caused the death of 1500 man, woman and child in the camps of Sabra and Chatila. The military general prosecutor laid the whole responsibilities over Israeli troops, for the camps were under their control. Get Israelis held responsible a local militia collaborating with them (9).

B - Fiasco and Fallback towards the Security Strip
Because of Israels crushing military strength. Lebanon was damaged economically, politically, and militarily. Agricultural and industrial production dropped off, and both interior and exterior commercial transactions slowed.

Moreover, pretending to protect themselves from the ambuscades which the resistants planted after the Israeli invasion, the occupation forces ruined the irrigation systems, and turned large lands of Citrus, bananas, and other vegetables into military roads.

In these conditions, huge amounts of Israeli goods invaded the Lebanese market at relatively law prices. That was a heavy blow to local products.

The convention of the 17th of May
The direct Israeli presence in Lebanon obliged Lebanese headed by Amin jumayel to sign, on 17 May 1983, a convention allowing the occupation forces to:

    - Impose the retreat of the Syrian and other foreign forces from Lebanon, as a condition to implement the convention (which stipulates the Israeli retreat from Lebanon).

    Lets note here that Syria entered Lebanon in 1976, at a Lebanese official request, in order to end interior confrontations.

    - Integrate the local forces collaborating with Israel into the Lebanese army.

    - Form a brigade of soldiers close to the occupation forces and others collaborating with them. This brigade is to assure security in the south.

    - Reduce the number of Lebanese soldiers in the south, and prevent them from possessing heavy arms, land-air missiles, and air forces.

    - Develop economic and cultural relations between Lebanon and Israel, without taking into account if Lebanese people was equal to the Israeli in these areas on not (10).

Undoubtedly, the convention aimed at reducing Lebanons real sovereignty over many of its territories, and at relating ifs security with Israels. (Israel was allowed to deploy common patrols with the Lebanese army). It also aimed at detaching Lebanon from its Arab surroundings, by forbidding Lebanese from making alliances of blocs with any other Arab country which is still in war with the Hebraic State.

The convention was never put into effect, on the one hand because of the Israeli inflexibility, and, on the other, because of the Islamic Resistance strengthening. The Lebanese government announced the annotation of the convention in 1984. That step was made to satisfy the opposition, as well as Syria which had refused the accord.

The Resistance imposes a partial retreat
Israel could bear no longer its daily losses in Lebanon. The most flagrant were on 11 October 1982, when a Lebanese suicide, Ahmad Kassir, 18 years old, set off the residence of the Israeli military governor in Lebanon. According to an Israeli official declaration, 75 soldiers were killed after the explosion of the 8-stage- headquarters located in Tyr. The public mourning lasted three days in the Hebraic State.

One year later, a similar attack look place at the headquarters of the Israeli Intelligence in Tyr, killing 29 Israeli soldiers.

In other terms, the resistants hunted the occupation soldiers and patrols by bombs, rockets, machine guns, and mortars. That was why the Israeli government decided to retreat from some territories in 1985. It retained only 1250 km2 in South Lebanon, and placed passage gates between the occupied and free zones, in order to reduce traffic. The passages were the following villages:

    Beit Yahoun (the Caza of Bent Jbeil)
    El Bayada (the Caza of Tyr)
    Kfar Tibbnit (the Caza of Nabatieh and Marjeyoun)
    Zemraya (Hasbaya)
    Bater (Jezzine - Chouf).
On the other hand, Israel opened many border gates in order to enhance the normalization with the occupied zones residents. Moreover, the occupation forces installed military positions at the hills, to control important roads in the free zones.

When the major Saed Haddad died, Israel chose the major general Antoine Lahd to resume the mission. It also reinforced the equipments of the militia which became the army of South Lebanon instead of the army of free Lebanon (11).

This nomination highlights the lessening of the occupation troops leverage in Lebanon, on the one hand, and its unilateral implementation of the 17 May convention, on the other hand.

The Occupied zone today.

A- Means of repression and recruitment.
The occupied zone in South Lebanon includes 19 villages following the province of Tyr, 23 villages and 7 farms following the province of Marjeyoun, 18 villages and 4 farms following the province of Bent Jbeil, 13 villages and 7 farms following the province of Hasbaya, and the majority of the villages and farms following the province of Jezzine (1). The word farm means in this context a zone widely built but almost deserted.

200,000 persons form the registered population, among whom the Chiites are the most numerous, then come the Christians, the Druze, and the Sunni. A large number of villages is half empty because of the terrorist policies adopted by the occupation forces, and enhanced by the obnoxious economic situation .

the Israeli occupation forces have used manifold ways of recruitment in the occupied zone. After the
partial retreat in 1985, Davis Rogers, Rustler's reporter, wrote from Marjeyoun: Israel is launching, in the border zone, a campaign to raise fears as well as interests, to win the collaboration and assistance of the local villagers an Israeli colonel once said our policy is to threaten and help in the same time. The local villagers have to help us hinder terrorism, or revenge will be their reward. A Senior Official directly implicated in the Israeli campaign also declared to the same source: our goal is to make people realize secretary of war-Itzac  Rabin at that time-added: the Israeli army will only defend Israel; thus, it will never jeopardize the life of a single Israeli soldier in protection of our ex-allies, the Christians.

Inept to carry on the expense of that occupation, Israel retreated from widespread zones, and decided to guillotine its snarled relations with some Christian militias dominating East Beirut. It confiscated a part of the Lebanese territories and planted guards everywhere.

They were local villagers, lured by the Israeli who premised to collaborate with them, and to safeguard their interests. But in fact. Israel was only safeguarding its own interests, abusing of the villagers collaboration to shield its army and strategy.

To establish relations with the local villagers, the occupying forces adapted the following approaches in the occupied zone and in the security strip.

B - Terrorism and Repression:
Israelis pretended to sustain the security of northern Israel (the security strip) from the operations of whom they called the devastators, and they meant the Islamic Resistants . it is widely known that the economy of the occupied zone lies upon agriculture, which was gravely marred by the military maneuvers taken by the Israeli Army right after every resistant operation. In fact, the crops were fully damaged with the bullets and grenades fired during Israeli patrols, not only to respond to the dissidents operation, but also because they needed some room to built their garrisons, as well as roads to get there (through the planted fields). Peasants were strictly forbidden to go near their properties that happen to be close to the Israeli garrisons. Thus, Israeli went on annexing hundreds of hectares to their territory.

To terrorize people, the occupying forces practiced detention. They put hundreds of people under arrest, no distinction made between sexes of ages, and took them to Al Khiam concentration camp and to other camps. There, one could find about 250 to 300 prisoners enduring physical and psychological torture. This was the yield one from refusing the occupation of the enlistment in its loyal militias. One could also be exiled from the occupied to the free zone. Talking about punishment, and beside everything we have already listed , villagers were punished collectively by being deprived from water, power and provisions. Israelis also imposed curfews for long hours during the night, and planted checkpoints to interrogate people.

The Lebanese authorities highlighted these transgressions in a pleading sent by the Lebanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs in 1988 to the head of the Lebanese delegation in the UNESCO, M.Adel Ishmael. It said that Israel was not paying any respect for the Lebanese cultural identity: Israel had detained teachers who refused the creation of a civic administration, and transgressed the spiritual morals of the community by exiling the Imam (spiritual leader) of Sheba with other officials in the municipal council. Add to what was previously related the detention of a public school director and of two private school directors. Israel had also forced the villagers to offer their help, and opposed their believes. Moreover, the occupying forces had tempted the villagers materially by offering 25$per month to single collaborators, and 40$ to the married ones; otherwise, terror would be their reward.

The names of those who refused the collaboration were listed, then they were interrogated. Israel had supplied the collaborating schools with oil, unlike the dissident schools which were totally deprived from oil. According to the pleading, this terrorism was firmly rejected by Lebanon.
Faced with this hard reality, many students and teachers left the villages and national institutions they had loyally served. It is also noticeable that the infrastructure was seriously damaged because of the incessant raids (especially in 1978-1982), leaving an unbridgeable gap in the process of development. This gap was the weapon the occupying forces used to get the villagers to collaborate. This was what we call the stick and carrot policy.

C - Previnding Help!
After the opening of the borderlines in 1976, Israel started forming special committees within its army, called the Israeli army committee for humanitary help and the Civil Administration formed at the damn of the raid of 1982. Israel also accorded great facilities in many domains among which trade and internal tourism. A campaign was also launched to tighten the rope round the neck of the few Lebanese institutions.

After its retreat in 1985, Israel assigned a budget of some millions of to dig new roads and to spend over development projects as water, electricity, and other facilities.
The majority of the money spent over these projects was collected from taxes over the cars crossing the borderline, and from the trade in Nakoura Harbor.

D - the second factor
that helped in jeopardizing the Lebanese identity was the competition created by the Israeli products, cheaper but more imposing. In June 1982, two weeks after the great raid, the Israeli products started flowing through the gates of the good wall, and the Nakoura Harbor was totally paralyzed.

The Lebanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs has asserted the UNESCO that the production of national artifacts is suffering from Israeli competition, thus people working in this domain were obliged to give up their work and to earn their living in Israeli factories.

E - Sponging the Labor Force by:
1 - evacuating the occupied territories from young workers who were sent to work in America, Europe or Africa. They were instigated by the economic crisis, and by the Israeli presence that only conferred them fear and poverty.

2 - Investing the labor force in the Israeli factories, especially after the shortage that occurred in the Palestinian Labor force. The economic crisis was the Israelis major weapon to abuse of 2500 Lebanese workers in the Israeli sectors and militias. At a certain time, some sources estimated that the income of the Lebanese workers in Israel reached 150,000$, which constitutes a complete economic cycle in the occupied territories i.e, a total dependence on Israel through the civil administration. The cycle was organized in a very methodic, although dangerous way.

3 - Recruiting young men and enlisting them in militias by using every possible means, until the number of the enlisted reached 3000-and it is never enough.

Means and Domains of Recruitment.

- The concept of Israeli Security:
In all their declarations, the Israeli officials emphasize on the concept of security and peace in their eventual relations with Lebanon. And to concretize this fact, the former Israeli Prime Minister, M. Itzhak Rabin, had invited the Lebanese authorities, after his visit to the southern occupied zone, to establish a comprehensible peace through knitting mutual diplomatic relations, and by opening the common borders.

While the Israeli spokesmen focus on the necessity of having security guaranties before any retreat, the former leader of the North Front in the Israeli Army M. Yousi Sareed gave other definitions of the word Security. He said it means, not only assuring security and military guaranties, but also providing the security strip with the daily subventions. Accordingly, the aim of Israel is to annex the Security strip militarily and economically to the rest of the Israeli territories, and to make it the gate to the rest of the Lebanese territories, as well as the door allowing them to interfere occasionally in the Lebanese affairs, and to abuse of the water and land.

- Replacing the Lebanese Public Institutions:
 For that purpose, the Israeli authorities organized the civil Administration, whose role was to normalize the relations with the people of the occupied zone through supervising all kinds of services in the region. From this Administration derived numerous local councils which replaced the former municipality and assured the management of the economic affairs. The occupying authority designated a budget for the occupied zone.

The civil administration is a hierarchy. It large base is formed of Lebanese and Israeli lieutenants, and its summit is an Israeli lieutenant assisted by two others whose task is to supervise directly the Eastern and Western banks.

The administration is not centralized. It has different branches throughout the occupied provinces (The main branch is in Marjeyoun Garrison that was once a headquarters of the Lebanese army). The lieutenant observes that everything is on the right track in the local committees. These latters are endowed with large authorities over the villagers affairs, and can abide to responding to all their needs according to the commands of the civil administration in each province.

The administration carries on the burden of choosing the committee members among the collaborators, widely known for their social performance. The committee embraces high personalities who do not adhere by their own will, but after being threatened and blackmailed. It worked on uprooting the frontier villages in the economic and development structure of Israel. The civil administration policy for spreading the Israeli goods consisted of imposing high taxes over the Lebanese goods. One of the main missions of this administration was to collect taxes imposed over the Lebanese goods through a special chamber built on the borderlines. And this was not all; they collected taxes over houses, stores, vans, productive institutions, and car registration. (Every car has two matriculation cards: one sealed by the Lebanese specialized authorities and the other by the civil administration). The revenues of Nakoura harbor, and of the public hospitals in the occupied zone - managed nowadays by the administration - were also charged. Some villagers preferred paying large amounts of money instead of being enlisted in the Israeli army. All these revenues were spent either over development projects of as militias wages.

The projects are innumerable:
Normalizing services: These services were based on water, electricity and roads. In this domain, the civil administration put generators in 22 villages (in the occupied zone) supplied by the electricity system of the Palestinian territories . the Israeli radio station explained that these measures were the sequels of some major damages in the Lebanese electricity system.

When the Lebanese authorities obstructed the occupying forces from organizing the power matters in the villages of the provinces, the leader of the collaborating militias, Antoine Lahd, claimed that taxes were being collected by members of the committees in Bint Jbeil.

Israeli also seized the water system in Jabal Amel (Taibeh, Baraasheit, Bint Jbeil) and turned it to a pomp near the casino of Ayn Ebel, connected to the tanks of Shtoula Colony near Eataa al-shaab. There, the Israeli water company (Micorot) relied a system of tubes to the water systems in 24 villages in Tyr province  and Bint Jbeil; the external communication was assured by an Israeli central.
The occupying forces have also dug more than 250 km of new roads in the occupied zone for military and civil purposes.

Sanitary normalization:
There are three public hospitals in the occupied zone: In Bint Jbeil, in Marjeyoun and in Jezzine. But they are all managed by the civil administration following the Israeli occupation. It offers doctors high wages beside the ones given by the Lebanese ministry of health. And still, we must not forget that Lebanese Doctors are always in touch with the Lebanese ministers.

There are also some health centers following the UN forces working in south Lebanon, as well as the centers of the Red Cross, and local committees annexed to the committees formed by the occupation to enhance the role of the collaboration. The civil administration has also created a health assurance system in the occupied zones, in return of a monthly participation of 4$ for each individual.

Educational normalization:
Dangerous facts are unveiled in this domain because the hegemony on the educational and scientific sector is a main prelude to a perfect normalization operation. Thus, it was said that the high council for Galilee (an Israeli educational institution) supervised the amendments of the educational system in the occupied zone, deleting every single information related to the Palestinian cause, and replacing Palestine with Israel in all expressions and maps; it is noticeable that Lebanon did not recognize Israel officially. That was never enough for Israel. It opened the borders and organized meetings between Lebanese and Israeli children in different Palestinian regions; the Israeli identity was the most prevailing one: the teachers were obliged to learn Hebrew.

The Lebanese Ministry of defense had asked the public prosecution to investigate in analogous cases which occurred in Naharaya colony in Tel Aviv in 1985. The Israeli integration unit, responsible for organizing relations between the Israeli Army and the local parties, acknowledged the students that it was permissive to pursuit their studies in Hebrew Universities located in Haifa, Tel Aviv, Jerusalem
and Beer El Sabee. First, they had to fulfill to the criteria decreed by those universities.

Add to all this the establishment of educational clubs and of school projects on the borderlines of the occupied zone to unfold the normalization process.

Social normalization:
It embraces the entire society and especially children. The occupying authorities focused on organizing touristic excursions for schools on both sides of the borders, mainly from South Lebanon to Palestine.

These excursions began in 1984. They ungloved thousands of children of both sexes, and were molded and framed according to untrustworthy conditions. In 1982, the people to people Israel-Lebanon was established. It had the mission of strengthening the relations between both sides of the borders. It also offered great facilities in the permits, and abused of great temptations on the religious, touristic and commercial sides.

The Israeli leaders were keen on grabbing every single opportunity to pay congratulation visits to some important personalities in the region.

Mediatic normalization :
It lays upon what is broadcast by the Israeli (Arabic speaking) radio station the voice of hope, and by the Middle East television following the loyal militias. They broadcast from the occupied territories. They were established thanks to the members of the Protestant Church in the USA, among which is George Otiss, whose followers -so called the congregation of the big adventure - believed in the infallibility of the Torah, and in the role of Israel in expediting the Messiahs arrival.
They defended blindly the Israeli policies. Otiss had said after establishing the radio station that backing Israel up was a miracle, for nobody had ever dreamt that Israel would give way to the establishment of a Christian radio station.

Economic normalization:
It proceeds by submerging the Lebanese markets with Israeli goods, and by abusing of Lebanons geographic position to merchandize the Israeli goods in the Arab world. there is also the falsification of the goods producing papers in the frontier harbor of Nakoura.

Both realities engendered major problems to Lebanese Economy. The former caused a competition to Lebanese goods, and the latter an embargo (Arab) on the importation of Lebanese goods. Arabs thought these were Israeli goods - knowing that the Arab world absorbs 90 % of Lebanese exportations of agricultural and industrial goods-.

The economic normalization started in 1982, when the Israeli minister of economy Jacob Mirdor was named to supervise the marketing in Lebanon.

The land and Water Resources:
Israel has a full dossier in the domain of seizing Lebanese land and water resources.
The former secretary of war Mr. Mosheh Dayan declared after the war of 1967 and the occupation of the west band, Gaza strip, the Golan and Sinai that the modification that occurred on the frontiers
with Lebanon was a necessity.

Confiscating Lands.
In the name of modifying the borderlines, Israel confiscated hundreds of hectares of agricultural lands situated near the line of truce established between 1982 and 1967. It is noticeable that in 1923, the English colonization- to the Zionist movement. Thus, Lebanon lost 23 thousands of hectares of land.

despite Lebanons neutrality in the war of 1967, Israel seized a large landscape in Cheikh mountain, and confiscated 23 farms in Chibaa. these lands reached 25 sq. km after evacuating them from villagers, destroying their houses, and killing their cattle.

The Lebanese ministry of foreign affairs informed the Lebanese spokesman in the UN on 20/4/1989 that Israel is putting the landlords in the Wazzany source and the Khyam valley under pressure. The confiscated land was estimated to 64 villages and farm of 130 sq. km.

The spokesman of the UN forces affirmed that the Israeli forces are willingly confiscating large lands of the Lebanese territories. They are fencing those lands and sustaining them with water and electricity. They are also forbidding every soul to go near these lands, they say its for their own security and for the security of the northern territories.

Confiscating water:
According to a report exposed in the ASCWA meeting in Amman in May 1994, Israel abused of 2150 million sq. meters of water in 1991, driven from the Jordan, Yarmook, Litani, and Wazzan rivers. This report also noticed that a tunnel of 18 km long was dug to connect the Litani River to the Israeli territories. The expert, Mr Storck, said in and essay published in Merib report in 1983 that Israel had started pumping water from the Litani river into its projects since 1978. Some locals say that this organized theft of water is pushed by the Israeli need for water. Israel is suffering from a water deficit that reached 950 million cubic meters in each fine. Bearing in mind that the storing capacity of both Litani and Wazzani River is 6 to 7 billion sq. feet per year, the operations of driving water are set to be done under ground. On 10/6/1983, the American intelligence said to Herald Tribune that Israel was intending to dig a tunnel through which the Israeli forces can reach the Lebanese territories located near the Khardaly Bridge in al land high of 240 meters of the water sea surface. This tunnel of 10 km long is a real disaster to the zone using the water of the Litani River, for it will be totally deprived from water in case the tunnel was dug.

Official Assertions:
On 15/12/1993, Mr. Nabih Berry (speaker of the Lebanese parliament) related some information asserting that the Israeli occupying forces put water pipes under the Khardally Bridge.

Mr. Timor Goksile, the spokesman of the UN forces in South Lebanon, declared that the UN did not ear any sort of the former constructions on both sides of the Litani and Hasbani rivers; but he said that there might be some clandestine operations of water theft under ground, although we still have no proof.

It is noticeable that in 1990, the Lebanese government required, from the supervisors of the truce, to halt the maneuvers of the Israeli occupying forces. But the Israeli opened fire on the group of experts from a nearby Garrison, and forbade them from investigating.

The subject of water is very crucial in the Israeli strategy. Mr. Shimon Perez, the former Israeli Prime Minister, said after the meeting he had with the vice prince of Jordan and the German Chancellor Helmut Khol in Bonn on 15/3/1995: We need weapons for war and water for peace.

The prime minister Isaac Shemir launched, on the papers of Wall Street Journal, an invitation to Arab people amercing limited negotiations and saw that such accord could help to establish trust between Israel and the Arab countries. Israel had declared on 11/5/1991 that it wont retreat without guaranties giving Israelis the right to have a share of the water of Litani river.

On 25/3/1991, the newspaper al-Hayat published a declaration of a Jordanian personality who said that Washington is trying to convince Amman that while Israel is canceling all the security arguments keeping it from retreating from the west bank, Gaza strip and South Lebanon, it is creating new arguments standing upon the need for water. So the water problem should be solved to give Israel the confidence it needs, and to contribute to solve the Palestinian Problem.

Mr. Thomas Nayf, the American searcher, says that it Israel is not ready to give up the Security belt in south Lebanon without having a share of the water.

In return Nayf affirmed before the American congress that Israel is robbing the water of the Litani River.

Conclusion:
Israel and the Zionist movement have interests in the land of South Lebanon and in its water resources. Their argument was either inspired of the Torah saying that God promised to endow the Israeli people with this territory (although the biblical texts say it is between Dan and beer al-Sabaa south Lebanon is then not included); or by Israels economic need for fertile land, water and most of all, for security.

England manifested sympathy in the early twenties to the Zionist movement. It welcomed the Zionist demands to annex South Lebanon to their territory. But the French colonization hindered this danger because it saw in Lebanon a center of the French policy in the Middle East.

Lebanon had suffered from Israeli transgressions since the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948. Neither the truce nor the successive international resolutions succeeded in stopping these transgressions. And if Lebanon had allowed since 1968 the Palestinian armed resistance , Israel did not respect the life of the civilians, whom it made its targets. It started operations of massive destruction to revenge for the little operations lunched by some groups against it, although Lebanon was not always the responsible for these attacks. In addition, Lebanon started taking in 1977 steps to stop fightings on the south border. Lebanon wanted to give Israel a cause to retreat, but the latter refused and forbade the Lebanese army from deploying its troops, topin by that to make new benefits.

Israel established the security strip in 1985, three years after the great occupation of Lebanon. It was obliged by the Lebanese resistance operating until now to retreat from a large land of the occupied territories.

Israel is trying to deprive the Security strip from its Lebanese identity. This reality was put into words by the Secretary General of the UN Mr. Perez Decuillar in a report handed to the Security Council in 1991: Recently, Israel was making roads in the occupied territories. If is annexing the water and telephone webs of the occupied territories to Israel. It is also multiplying the number of administrative bureaus. Hebrew registration cards are obligatory, and passports are given for internal circulation in the Security strip that is depending economically and administratively upon Israel, directly through Lahed forces. The Lebanese authority is asking for an unconditioned retreat from the entire occupied zone.

As for the collaborators, they should be left to the Lebanese legislature. However, Israel wants to integrate them into the Lebanese army, bearing in mind two aims: the first is to keep its hegemony over the occupied zone, the second is to knit maladjusted relations, and put a term to every opposition, even the journalistic one. And last but not least, to share Lebanons water. Lebanon responds that his water is not a subject of division because it will barely satisfy the Lebanese future needs.
