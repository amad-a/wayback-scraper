Human Rights groups call on UN Body to condemn "Israel" (1)
An alliance of human rights groups called Tuesday for the UN Committee on Human Rights to condemn "Israel" for torture, detention without trial and other violations of International law.

The Committee, made up of 18 experts, is meeting in Geneva until July 31 and will consider "Israel's" case on Wednesday and Thursday. It is the first time that "Israel", which ratified a 1991 international pact on civil and political rights, is presenting a report before the body charged with upholding the agreement.

Several non-governmental organizations, including Human Rights Watch, the International Human Rights Federation and "Israeli" groups, have called on the Committee to condemn "Israel". They cite the use of torture to get information from Palestinian detainees suspected of preparing attacks.

"Israel" Supreme Court has already been condemned twice by the UN Committee Against Torture for legalizing the use of physical pressure when trying to uncover terrorist plots. The groups are particularly critical of what they call the "hostage-taking" of some 20 Lebanese, who have been held for eventual exchange against "Israeli" soldiers missing in action.

The "Israeli" Supreme Court authorized the measure in a judgement made public in March, while acknowledging that those held were not a threat to state security. The group has also denounced "Israel's" practice of administrative detention for up to six months imprisonment without trial. They also accuse "Israel" of sending agents to assassinate extremist Palestinian leaders based abroad.
The most recent case made public was the failed attempt last September in Jordan against Hamas leader Khaled Mishal. The groups also denounce "Israel" for making no mention in its 286-page report of the situation in the Occupied Territories, which the Committee, chaired by French lawyer Christian Chanet, is due to discuss.

The Committee's conclusions are normally made public at the end of their session, but preliminary reports can be made after each country has been questioned.

The pact on civil and political rights, to which 140 countries now adhere, first came into force in 1976. "Israel" dismissed Tuesday a UN Security Council declaration against its plans to create a Greater Jerusalem, while the Palestinians regretted the lack of any concrete action to stop the project.
