Facts in Lebanon
Some 200 detainees are currently held in Khiam detention centre situated in South Lebanon. Khiam was set up as a permanent detention centre in early 1985 by the South Lebanon Army* (SLA) with the obvious "Israel's" assistance and supervision. Most of the detainees are Lebanese suspected only of belonging to armed organisations hostile to "Israel" and the SLA, or having been involved in attacks against "Israel" Defence Force**. Many of them have been tortured or ill-treated during the endless interrogations carried out either by "Israeli" militaries or SLA officers (further called 'lahdis'-from the general Lahad, the commander of this faction). They appear to be held outside any legal framework and have no access to the outside world.

Testimonies from detainees who had been released already indicated a systematic pattern of torture including beatings  with electric cables, dousing with water and electric shocks and much more. Detainees told of the direct involvement in interrogation and torture of "Israeli" personnel.

Interrogation and torture One detainee taken prisoner at the end of 1986 recalls: " After hours of continuous beatings the "y" ("Israeli" interrogator) stopped. Another detainee, held between 1985 and 1990 reported: "I was tortured very badly being accused in the disappearance of two "Israelis". They used stronger voltage when giving me electric shocks. Once an "Israeli" interrogator came into room just after I had been beaten  with a steel rod over the head, and the blood was coming down over my eyes. After hours of beating he decided I had not been involved and the interrogation ended.

TORTURE METHODS

1) Electric Shocks
This method has been described as follows: while the person is seated or lying on the ground, handcuffed and hooded with a blindfold, an interrogator attaches electrodes to two fingers(one on each hand) or other parts of the victim's body(such as tongue, ear lobes, noses, toes, penis and nipples at women) crank of an apparatus producing electric current. Detainees who said they saw this machine said it looked like an old telephone, or a military field telephone, attached to a battery. The faster the crank is turned , the higher the voltage that streams through the wires.

The following is the sad-but-true testimony of a detainee held between 1988-1991 in Khiam. "The wires were about two meters long. He (the "Y") put them on my index fingers and began turning the crank, slowly at first, then faster and faster. Then he said , "this is not enough for you". He kept beating me. Then he took off my trousers and underwear and he put one wire on my penis, and the other on my finger. He started turning the crank, faster and faster.

I was jumping up and down. In the violence of this, the wire slipped off my penis. He repeated the electric shocks two or three times over a two hours period. All the time he was pouring water over the areas where he had put the wires. After this I was taken to an isolation cell."

2) Beating with an electric cable
Most former deatinees have reported being beaten repeatedly with a thick electric cable (kurbaj kahraba). Typically, an interrogator beats a detainee lying prostrate on the ground, hitting all parts of the body.

The following is another testimony of a man held between 1987-1991. He says he had already been interrogated for one day, given electric shocks and spent the night in isolation.

"The "Y" took me to a room with  an interrogator. He then told me to lie face- down on the floor. He brought an electric cable and began to hit me. He was hitting me on my back and legs. This went on for an hour."

Another detainee recalls: " An interrogator started hitting me with an electrric cable. He said "I am going to hit you 100 times. Keep count, and when I ask you how far I have got  and you get it wrong, we will start from the beginning." After 20
lashes he asked me how many I had had. When I said 20 he said "you lied, there were only 19" and continued to hit me about 120 times." Sometimes detainees are beaten with an elecric cable on the soles of their feet, a practice known as 'falaqa'.

3) Suspension from an electricity pole.
Between interrogation sessions detainees are frequently suspended from an electricity pole ('amud kahraba) located in an outside yard between sections. Most commonly, a detainee's handcuffs are slung through another pair of handcuffs, or a chain or rope, hanging down from a cross-bar attached to the pole. Detainees are usually suspended so that their toes barely reach the ground. They may be kept like that for several hours, exposed to the elements. In addition, they may be stripped off their
clothes and doused with water at frequent intervals. They may also be beaten with electric cables.

4) Dousing with water
Detainees are frequently doused with water when they are in the outside yard, for example while they are suspended from the electricity pole in between interrogation sessions. Sometimes hot and cold water is used alternatively. The detainees may be clothed or unclothed, even during the freezing weather in winter.

5) Painful positions
Several detainees have described the pain and the physical exhaustion they suffered by being forced to keep their arms raised while standing or kneeling, or by being made to perfom other forms of physically demanding activity.

6) Deprivation of sleep, food and hygiene
During the initial  period of interrogation detainees are usually deprived of sleep and food , clearly in an attempt to exhaust them physically and mentally. This is done by forcing detainees to stand in between interrogation sessions or by beating them or pouring cold water over them at regular intervals while they are kept in a corridor or in one of the yards.

During this period detainees are usually further weakened by the provision of insufficient food.

Frequently detainees are not allowed to use the toilet for many days, and must relieve themselves in their trousers, if they are clothed, or anywhere on the ground.

We recall to those who are not yet aware of such methods that they are to be considered classical in both former communist and nazis' prisons.

7) Hoods and handcuffs
Upon arrival in the Khiam detention centre and during interrogation, detainees are normally hooded with thick dark canvas sacks, described as dirty and smelly. To make sure that they cannot see, a blindfold is usually placed over the sack. In addition, detainees's hands, and sometimes feet are placed in steel or plastic cuffs. The plastic cuffs can be particularly painful when tightened because they can cut into flesh.

The sacks and cuffs are apparently removed  when a detainee is put in an isolation cell or held in a cell with suspected "collaborators". The main reason for the hooding seems to be to disorient detainees and induce fear, rather than to prevent victims from identifying interrogators. Detainees have reported that their hood was removed towards the end of their
interrogation in the presence of their interrogators. Some detainees wearing hoods are hit, exploiting their inability to expect the blows and thus increasing the distress and degradation caused by the blows.

8) The isolation cell
Throughout the period of interrogation detainees'only contacts are their interrogators and possibly one or more collaborators. Detainees under interrogation are often kept in isolation cells, said to measure less than one meter per side and to be so dark that detainees are sometimes unable to discern the food in their plates. One detainee reported having spent 60 consecutive days in such a cell in 1985, sleeping in a sitting position. Sometimes, to increase the distress, two detainees would be placed together
in the same isolation cell for several days. In these cells they may be able to hear the moaning and screaming of other detainees.

9) The "chicken coop"
Until early 1988, a very small cell called  "chicken coop" ('al-qin') by detainees was in Section I  at Khiam. The "chicken coop" was apparently 50 cms long, 50 cms wide and 70 cms high. A detainee, hooded and bound, would be pushed in at a sitting position with the head on the raised knees.

The detainee would be kept in this cramped position for two or three hours, enough to cause severe back and neck pains. One detainee held between 1987-1991 related his experience of the "chicken coop":
"After five days in isolation, and I was not talking , they put me in what is called 'al-qin'The first time I was in there for three hours. After 3 hours, you feel like you are about to explode  They put me in the "coop" three times, each time for about 2 or 3 hours".

10) Insults and threats
Insults, especially of a detainee's religion or female relatives, are common. Threats, including death threats, are often directed at detainees or their realtives. "If you do not confess, we will bring your mother here and torture her in front of your eyes". "If you  do not confess, we will blow up your house ". "If you do not confess, you will not get out of here alive".

Some such threats appear to have been carried out, or refer to actions that detainees know have actually been carried out against others.

A woman detainee held between 1987 and 1988 described other threats and a mock execution:

" During my interrogation the 'lahdis'(SLA members) threatened to blow up my house  and my entire family. They also said they would pull my cloths off and rape me. After 5 days, they started using electricity. They put wires on my two index fingers, poured water over me, and shocked me. I screamed.Then they put a rope around my neck and threatened to kill me."

11) Torture of relatives
To put additional pressure on detainees, one of their relatives may be detained in Khiam for short or long periods and tortured or ill-treated with earshot of the deatinees. Mothers, wives, daughters, sisters and fiancees of the male detainees are beaten or raped under their relatives'terrified eyes. The method of psychological torture appears to be highly effective. Deatinees say that once they heard the anguished voice of their mother or father, for example, they could no longer resist their interrogators.

This is a testimony of a detainee taken prisoner at the end of 1986. He said had already been repeatedly and severely tortured when they brought his mother:
"Then (the interrogator)  told me  "I brought your mother here". And sure enough she was in the next room. I heard her scream. She is 50 years old.

They kept her for 3 months, I found out latter, and they tortured her to put pressure on me. Eventually when I heard her scream (the detainee was crying while telling), I confessed whatever they wanted me to confess".

12) The interrogation of women
Women detainees in Khiam detention centre are reportedly interrogated and tortured by male interrogators. In general, the same interrogation methods used against men appear to be used against women. However, women detainees have been reportedly been subjected to rapes, and forms of sexual abuse.
