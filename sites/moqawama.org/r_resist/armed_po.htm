     The Israeli enemy launched the war against Lebanon in June 1982, and succeeded after more than 2 months of fierce battles in penetrating deeply to the heart of the Lebanese capital, forcing the Palestinian gunmen to evacuate the country.

     Under the shadow of the Israeli oppressive occupation, in the midst of the horrific frustration, and against the Arab dreadful silence, the Lebanese popular resistance (later restricted to only the Islamic Resistance) rose like a lightning dawn after a long night. Following only a short spell of time, the resistance managed to achieve many remarkable and extraordinary victories which forced a state of confusion and exhaustion in the enemy's ranks, and inflicted a heavy blow to its military supremacy myth, ultimately leading to various and serious political rifts deeply established in its entity.

     Ever since that date, a question presented itself at the public international law's level, concerning the resistance subjects and the civilians right to resort to the weapon's option in order to counter and resist the occupation forces by all available  means and methods. It would be wise, before answering this question, to demonstrate some remarks and clarify the importance of this resistance from the enemy's point of view.

1) QUICK REMARKS REGARDING THE POPULAR RESISTANCE.

A- The armed popular resistance against the extortionist enemies is a phenomenon which overwhelmed the history of all peoples; the peoples' elements urged themselves forward to support the regular armies when their countries have been facing foreign military assaults and aggressions. In the past, it was very difficult to accurately compare between the regular armies and the volunteers coming from the ranks of the peoples themselves.

B- The importance of the armed popular resistance has been constantly increasing during the 20th century, particularly in World War II and throughout the following decades. Albeit this phenomenon posed imminent legal problems, the concerned parties in the general international law didn't consecrate the deserved attention and consideration in order to deal with this subject.

C- The international doctrine gives an extensive interpretation to the  term "The Resistant People", which is the people who uses the arm alternative to face its enemies. What is really meant by this people, according to the broad interpretation by the international law doctrine, aren't the individuals who are connected to each others by political or legal ties directly with their country, since popular resistance could proceed forward without the presence of a given country, or it could also be initiated in order to establish the country itself; furthermore, the presence of the people precedes the presence of the country. Thus, what is purely meant by "The Resistant People" is that group of individuals whose members belong to a single country, either living on its soil or outside it, and who enjoy a deep common link consisting of various aspects such as the language, history and legacy, in addition to many other national aspects.

D- The popular resistance doesn't stipulate (in order to establish itself and acquire the full legitimacy) the whole people to initiate it, since it could be proceeded at the hands of some factions or specific members of the people. However, the bottom line is the people's sympathy with this specific group which decided to bear this national obligation; this sympathy could be translated (a fact happening in most cases) into a firm support or popular backing. Historic events confirm that popular backing to the resistance is a main prerequisite in order to secure the ultimate success and continuous survival.

E- The contemporary international law accords to the resistance militants the right to resort to all available means and methods able to exhaust the occupation forces, including the armed force, initiating sabotage activities and proceeding guerilla warfare; this resistance could be also a civil one, nor just a military resistance, thus preventing violence acts.

F- The armed popular resistance is always targeting a foreign enemy who is invading a country, or violating its independence status, or threatening the country's dignity. Hence, the armed resistance differs from the civil war which is launched within the country, between the people (or a faction from it) and the reigning regime, or between the citizens' factions challenging each other in order to secure the local power over the country, or defending a specific trend, or imposing a particular philosophy. From this point of view, the armed resistance also differs from the wars of separation led by citizens of a given region from the country in order to detach away from it or to establish a new state (or even different states).

G- The armed resistance is characterized by a national incentive that is appropriate to the highest national interest and embodied in the defense of the ancestors' land against the hostility acts, occupation or colonialism. If this incentive is one of the main popular resistance characteristics, it is also designed as a standard to differentiate it against the acts of violence committed by a given group in order to rob and plunder, even if these actions have been targeting a foreign enemy, and to differentiate with  the military operations launched by gunmen against a foreign country for the benefit of political objectives and purposes for a specific governor or party.

H- The martyrdom operations implemented and carried out by the resistance heroes in Lebanon following the enemy's forces arrival to Beirut have inflicted deep panic and fear in the hearts of the Israelis, forcing them to withdraw to the southern spots of the country and to be convinced that their advanced and destructive weaponry systems are failing to maintain their hostile acquisitions just when the resistance heroes decide to transform their bodies into time bombs.

I- The adoption of resistance as a mean to struggle against the enemy imposes the necessity of toppling all kinds of settlement policies for which some Arab governors continue to adhere to, as well as to all kinds of traditional regular warfare which could generate many problematic and interfering issues we can't afford at present time. Arab regimes have long tried, since 1948, the regular warfare against the Zionist enemy, but to no avail. After their failure in this kind of war, the same Arab regimes resorted to various kinds of settlement process, calling on the United Nations to provide help, accepting to participate in the international conferences, and approving the involvement in direct and indirect negotiation rounds, openly and secretly, with our direct enemy. After many insulting and bitter experiences, those regimes have found (without admitting it) that settlement logic is a pure failure, and the fate of negotiations with the occupier or extortionist will only result in a direct fiasco; hence, the Lebanese  resistance rose to the expectations and proved the liability of the principle saying that "The liberation will only be achieved through the armed popular struggle".

I- The popular resistance idea against the Israeli enemy was presented in the past, but faced clear opposition from some factions arguing that the soil and geographic nature of our country aren't suited to this kind of struggle. When the resistance in Vietnam and Algeria set the example, it was said that geographic nature in both countries (including vast woods, swamps and mountains) has been the main reason behind the successful achievement of the local resistance; yet, the Lebanese resistance succeeded despite the lack of woods, swamps and mountains in southern Lebanon. This setting proves that the resistance is capable to get into action under all circumstances and given times, and can also adopt itself under any geographic situation. If there were a necessity to adopt a criterion to the resistance work, it would be the will, coupled with pure faith and the preparation to provide martyrs and sacrifice lives for the sake of virtues and values. The successful achievements of the resistance in southern Lebanon present a sound condemnation to  the propounding of yielding figures who predicted the absurdity of initiating a popular resistance in the occupied Arab territories.

Perhaps, the biggest challenge facing the Arabs who oppose the surrounding policy would be the ability to maintain and protect this nucleus of Arab resistance ingrained in southern Lebanon, and to secure its survival and proper growth reasons able to transform it into a fully-adopted Arab method facing the occupation and hostility operations led by the Zionist enemy.

2)THE ENEMY's RECOGNITION OF THE LEBANESE RESISTANCE IMPORTANCE.

     The Lebanese resistance, which aroused the deep admiration and stimulated the feelings represents, according to the attestation of all involving sides, the most remarkable phenomenon and greatest event brought to life throughout the history of the Arab- Israeli conflict. It would be enough for us to refer to what the Israeli media wrote about it to fully understand its importance and huge impact on the evolution of current events.

A- Ze'ev Shif, one of the most prominent Israeli military analysts, has recognized (after a few months period of the Lebanese resistance inception), that merely one single day passes, since the occupation of the Lebanese southern region, without experiencing a serious attack targeting the Israeli forces at the hands of the Lebanese resistance members, and that no single week passes without suffering many casualties, either killed or wounded troops in the ranks of the Israeli occupation forces as a result of these operations. Shif also found that the war which is countering the Israeli army in Lebanon is completely different from all the previously experienced ones from the military point of view, since the relatively high number of casualties in Israeli human lives indicates that this army has lost the appropriate and suitable response to the imminent problem facing it. The Israeli military analyst refuted the claimed voiced by the then Israeli war minister, Ariel Sharon, that the "saboteurs are cowards who never dare and attack the Israeli army", adding that "what is happening in Lebanon is exactly the opposite, since they are only attacking the Israeli army, and never hesitate to confront it directly in daylight the prisoners held by the saboteurs represent one of the evidences regarding this subject." (HA'ARETZ daily newspaper-- 15th April, 1983).

B- Another Israeli military analyst, Gabi Zohar, depicted the resistance activities against the Israeli army as taking shape of the guerilla warfare which adopt the "Strike and Run" style, as well as mining the front axis, entering bombed cars to the Israeli occupied zone and opening fire from the ambushes toward the Israeli army's mobile patrols, checkpoints and main headquarter in southern Lebanon. Zohar also referred to the fact that this resistance "enjoys the support of local civilians who are fed up with the Israeli army presence in south Lebanon", predicting the imminent increase of the resistance operations level to the extent of initiating a real war of attrition turning out into a fait-accompli. Finally, Zohar warned about the forthcoming guerilla warfare inception, which is "the worst kind of warfare suffered by any country, but what if launched against Israel?". (DAVAR daily newspaper-- 4th February, 1983).

C- Under the title "1000 faces of death in Lebanon", a third Israeli military analyst, Eitan Hafer, tackled the subject of "the death which is lurking in every corner against the Israeli soldiers in Lebanon", portraying the horror situation experienced by these troops, and saying the following: "Nobody goes to a place unless it is very urgent, and nobody does anything unnecessary the Israeli army deliberately entered the blockadethere is a constant protection for transport activity, and it isn't recommendable to walk one single meter without the close escort of yet another military vehicle, coupled by communication devices and iron cap. It is an extremely dangerous situation the Israeli army is confined all day and night inside its bases like a hedgehog each bullet shot, or a flashing light, makes the Israeli army troops shiver". (YEDIOT AHRONOT daily newspaper, Saturday supplement--25th February 1983).

D- Along with the increasing level of the operations led by the Lebanese resistance militants, a fourth Israeli military analyst, Tili Zlinger, warned that time doesn't work in favor o Israel, because the "saboteurs" succeeded, by means of small local groups backed by local citizens, in regaining the power following the first strike, but also because the hostile feelings against us, accumulated throughout the past months "are revealed into a phenomenon indicating in itself to the role played by time against us those who welcomed us in June 1982 with rice are ready right now to welcome us with hand-grenades and light machine gun fire".  Furthermore, the analyst approached the efficiency of the resistance operations against the Israeli army and concluded that the guerilla warfare adopted today in Lebanon "is pushing this army to the situation of reaction against surprises, not to the situation of the initiator". The same analyst discussed the psychological situation of the Israeli soldiers, describing "their difficult, rather impossible circumstances they are enduring, and the day-night accompanying panic situation". (DAVAR daily newspaper-- 5th July, 1983).

E- A fifth Israeli military analyst, Menachem Horowitz, tackled one of the "intolerable situations" aspects in southern Lebanon regarding the Israeli army stationed there, and related the Israeli betting failure to lure the loyalty of the local southern citizens, or eliciting the sectarian religious rifts between their communities; he also depicted the anxiety situation overwhelming the daily life of the Israeli troops thanks to the growing number of  resistance operations, stressing the fact that they are taking no more risks, "from their point of view, every human being, each road and each speedy passing car is a suspected thing". (HA'ARETZ daily newspaper, 13th January, 1984).

3)THE ARMED RESISTANCE LEGITIMACY IN THE INTERNATIONAL PUBLIC LAW.

Both complementary and interactivity deeply link between the social phenomenon and legal positions; without the peoples' confrontation, since early history, to the assailants, resistant thinking wouldn't be able to define the relationship between the occupation authorities and the citizens of the occupied territories on the basis of belonging relationship continuity to the country of origin and the lack of any commitment toward complying to the occupation country. The various kinds of resistance trends initiated by peoples enduring the foreign occupation in order to protect their pure presence and dignity have generated deep impacts and efficient role in the evaluation of the military occupation law rules in the field of prohibiting both annexation and seizure, maintaining the sovereignty of the state, and the liability of self-defense in order to secure liberty and protect the independence.

     If we take a close look at the contemporary international law rules, we wouldn't notice any close preventing the occupied territories' citizens from launching the national resistance activities, either armed or unarmed ones; the modern international doctrine didn't lay behind in this field, according to those citizens the full right to launch the revolution against the occupation authorities, as their national duty orders them to adhere to the resistance activities. Some legislators even went far beyond this explanation after finding that they have the obligation to revolt, and that this obligation is deeply ingrained in their spirits according to the constant loyalty relationship linking between them and their occupied territory.

     This trend had enjoyed the approval and support of many countries, including both Great Britain and the United States. The latter referred in its military legislation to the War Revolutions as "War Rebellions". For instance, we note that the 10th article of the American terrestrial war law stipulates that "The fighter has no right to announce that he will treat every person arrested from the ranks of the armed forces belonging to the insurgent people against the enemy by means of the same treatment reserved for a participant of a thieves gang, or the treatment of an armed thief".

     Every legal treatment of the national resistance legitimacy in the occupied territories requires a word in the Martial Occupation Law, a discourse about the various trends of resistance and a presentation of the international efforts seeking to support this legitimacy.

I) The Martial Occupation Law and Its Imperative Characteristic:   A part of a district belonging to the state of Lebanon is succumbed during many years to the Israeli occupation. The occupied regions, mainly southern Lebanon, are witnessing an armed resistance and consecutive attacks targeting the enemy's forces. Battles and confrontations are occurring almost everyday. With the admiration to the resistance bravery generated by the militants operations initiated against the occupation forces, the aforementioned question pertaining to the right of the local citizens under foreign occupation to resort to the resistance movement and activities by all means in order to confront this occupation, is forcing its way at the level of the international conventions, laws and covenants.

Before answering this question, we note that a substantial change occurred in the modern era, to the nature of war, particularly after the international law's condemnation to the use of force, considering it as an illegal act. The 4th section of the second clause within the United Nations convention clearly stipulates the necessity of refraining the member countries, within the frame of their international relations, from threatening each others by the means of use of force against the sovereignty of a given state of its political independence, or the use of any other mean which never complies with the United Nations purposes.

Still, some great or small countries resort, from time to time, to violate the principles of the nations' convention and force the occupation of other territories; this fact implied in the past the presence of the Martial Occupation Law. Why this law was created? What are the most important phases undergone by this law before approving it? And if this law enjoys an imperative characteristic, what does it specifically mean?

a- The Martial Occupation Law: The international doctrine differentiates between both the war and the martial occupation status: the first one is the confrontation and combat phase at the battleground, while the second is the transitional phase that follows the armed aggression.

     Each of these situations has its own law. As for the rules of war law, they were shaped and manifested prior to the occupation law. We note in the list attached to the "Lahaye Accord" pertaining to the terrestrial war, and in the 4th "Geneva Accord" regarding the protection of civilians, the codification and registry of regulations pertaining to the Martial Occupation Law.

     The clause 42 of the Lahaye Accord (1907) attached list had identified the occupied district as "The district under strict authority of the enemy's army". There is no difference in this context between a military occupation achieved as a result of the defeated country surrender, or as a result of its armed forces abandonment to their weapons, or as a result of a truce or cease-fire uncoupled with the abolition of war situation.

     The bottom line of the occupation is the submission of a given state's district, or just a part of this district, materially or militarily, to the effective foreign occupation, and the necessity of the occupation authority's compliance to the Martian Occupation Law which organizes the occupation status and defines the relations between the occupying state's authorities from one hand, and the occupied state's authorities and the civilians of the occupied territories from the other hand.

     The Martial Occupation Law was created in the past century, particularly after the intensification of Napoleon's wars which proved to be a disaster for the civilians in the occupied territories, only to find a harmonious path able to meet the peoples' aspirations and put a definitive end to the brutal procedures used by the assailants armies against the peaceful civilians.

     Indeed, it was the United States, which has been subject to the most brutal crimes during the civil war, the first country that codified or registered in 1863 the war rules, and organized the conduct of combat forces which prohibited the plundering and stealing acts, and stipulated sanctions against members of these forces when confirming their commitment to related crimes.

     The American codification was handed to the delegations attending the 1874 Brussels Conference, which witnessed the participation of most of the European countries. As the Russian government submitted its plan regarding the Terrestrial War Law, the conference was instantly divided to 2 sides: the first one included the leading great nations and called for forcing the citizens of the occupied territories to comply to the occupation authorities and to never resort to violence in order to expulse the occupiers from their land, while the second side consisted of the small nations which called for the non-recognition to the rights and claims of the occupation countries in the occupied territories, as well as calling for the people's legal right to revolt against the occupation forces until their ultimate expulsion.

     At the end, the conference succeeded to overcome many hurdles and reached a planned agreement which includes the rules of Terrestrial Law emanated from the martial rules which have been prevalent in the past. Despite the fact that the participating countries didn't approve this plan, its impact was clearly manifested in the military instructions ordered by the countries to their armies in order to implement it in the battles.

     The first Lahaye Conference, held in 1899, succeeded in its efforts to set up many accords, notably the list of Terrestrial War emanated from the Brussels Conference plan, which was annexed to the second accord. The second Lahaye Conference, held in 1907, reviewed some of the accords and statements issued at the first conference, and managed to initiate an accord pertaining to the Martial Occupation Law. The rules of this law were violated during both World War I and World War II, as all sides became convinced that the clauses pertaining to the protection of civilians during the occupation period weren't enough in the full-scale war cases.

     In 1949, the Swiss government (insinuated by the International Red Cross Committee) called for a diplomatic conference that was held in Geneva and attended by delegations of 64 countries. On the 12th August 1949, following 4 consecutive months of intensive and fruitful deliberations, the participating nations signed 4 accords, with the fourth one pertaining to the civilians protection during war time and martial occupation. These accords came to complement or modify or codify the texts of previous accords. The 4th Accord  is considered as one of the most significant achievements peoples managed to fulfill in the field of international and human law. Both Lebanon and "Israel" were among the conference participating countries, and both approved the 4 accords, and later ratified them without reservations.

     Thus, the rules of the Martial Occupation Law consist of the texts mentioned in the list attached to the Lahaye Conference, pertaining to the Terrestrial War and stated in the 4th Geneva Accord, in addition to the general principles of human rights and protection means.

b- The imperative characteristic of the Martial Occupation Law: The rules of this law are peculiar to their imperative characteristic governing all countries, either those adhering to this law or non-adhering to it. The clause 53 of the Vienna Accord (1969) pertaining to the Law of Conventions, defined the imperative rule in the International Public Law as "The accepted and recognized rule by the international community as a rule never permitted to be breached and unable to be modified except being replaced by a new rule of the international law rules and possessing the same characteristic".

     If it is completely right that there is some international jurists who believe that the international law is consisting of potential rules and that the countries have right, at all times, to ratify any kind of treaties albeit being contradicting the public rules of the international law, the contemporary international doctrine adopts a completely different situation.

     There is a side who believes that the imperative rules in the international law are those making the nucleus of the international public system, while another side defines those rules as the ones which include the minimum level of necessary requested conduct to guarantee the international relations; a third side argues that the imperative rules are those who express some of the social and political needs. Still, there is another different trend which considers the rules mentioned in the United Nations convention as imperative rules which shouldn't be violated nor should they be opposed to an initiated agreement contradicting them.

     Among the rules that general opinion insisted on considering them as "imperatives" in the international law are those rules in close touch with many aspects such as the countries' rights, the rules pertaining to the human rights and substantial freedoms, the basic principles of the nations' convention pertaining to the prohibition of using force threatening or using the force within the international relations frame, the rule regarding the peaceful solution of international conflicts, the rule concerning the support provided to the United Nations in each displayed activity (by this body) which adheres to its convention, and the refraining of backing aggressive countriesBecause these laws have been incepted for the sake of humanity.

     During World War II, Germany refused to implement the Lahaye accords and its regulations, arguing that they include a prerequisite stipulating that the adherence to its clauses is only restricted to the countries that signed these accords, but the doctrine rejected this pretext submitted by the occupation countries who sought the refrainment from implementing their legal rules of imperative characteristic in the occupied territories.

     As for the Nuremberg court, it has discussed the extent of the obligatory characteristic pertaining to the Lahaye accords and its annexed regulations over the sides who didn't sign them, and considered these international documents binding Germany regardless of the prerequisite general participation stipulated in the second clause of the Lahaye  Accord (1907). Thus, the court had confirmed the imperative characteristic of the Lahaye regulations regarding all the countries, as an endorsement work of a custom that was prevailing long before the inception of these regulations,
and not a work initiating forthcoming new rules. Hence, the Lahaye Accord should be implemented by all countries, including the participating sides and the refraining ones.

     The national justice approved this trend, as the Greek Court of Appeal had ruled in a case pertaining to the Bulgarian occupation of Greece during World War II, that the occupation authorities are compelled to implement the principles stated in the Lahaye regulations concerning the occupied territories, and that the rules included in the Lahaye Accord represent the international martial law principles binding all the countries of the world (even those who didn't participated in the Lahaye Congress), as it was the case of Greece.

     If we take a close look at the 4th Geneva Accord, we would find that it is one of the collective treaties that enjoys a world characteristic, even resembling at a very close extent to the nations' convention binding all the countries in the world, including those unaffiliated with the convention itself. Doesn't the 6th section of the second clause stipulate the United Nations work towards forcing the unaffiliated countries within its body to adhere to the principles included in the convention? Isn't the protection of generations from war calamities, the protection of human rights and freedoms, and the prohibition of resorting to the use of force are included in these principles? Does the 4th Geneva Accord really aim to secure another thing beyond the protection of these rights?

The principle stated in the aforementioned 6th section "reflects a period of evolution that the international community was subjected to, where the resources of the international law have been no longer able to set up the rules capable of maintaining the international peace and protecting it, in all the situations and cases, and in every region of the world, in order to achieve the security and stability in this society; thus, it is clearly natural to recognize the lack of this principle basis within these resources; it is also natural the necessity of launching a search campaign in order to find this basis within the revolutionary transformation occurred in the methods of legal rules production in the international order".

    If the United Nations derived its statutory specialization from its status as the authority created by the widest majority of the international community in order to preserve both peace and security, and from its standing as the center which organizes and coordinates the suitable and appropriate efforts leading to these goals, "the will of the international community's majority, at the present period of international law's evolution, has acquired the capability of initiating the legal impact not only in the ranks of the participating sides of this will, but also in the camp of those who didn't participate in its creation".

     In addition to this proof, the overwhelming majority of the countries in the world has ratified the 4th Geneva Accord and included it within its internal legislation. Most of the resolutions announced by various United Nations apparatuses have also indicated this accord, and called upon the necessity of honoring its regulations by all countries. Even the last section of the 158th clause stipulates that the withdrawal of any country from the accord doesn't exempt it from the commitments beard by the conflict sides according to the principles of the "Law of Peoples", derived from the traditions established between the civilized peoples and from the laws of humanity and the requirements of public conscience.

     In this context, we are only interested in the group of basic principles and rules pertaining to the martial occupation, as defined by the 4th Geneva Accord; therefore, we can sum up these principles and rules as following:

1- The occupation is a temporary situation lacking the prominence characteristic and any direct impact on the country's constant survival.
2- The occupation won't lead, from the legal point of view, to the transfer of sovereignty right from one country to another; thus, it isn't capable of providing the occupying country the right of sovereignty over the occupied district, since the sovereignty remains deeply attached to the civilians who have the full right, during the occupation period, to reject the foreign invasion and resort to the resistance by all available means.
3- The occupation entitles the occupying country with only the competence of practicing an effective and practical authority aiming at maintaining both peace and security in the occupied district and managing the affairs of the local citizens.
4- The Law of Martial Occupation is the body that defines the legal and executive framework under which the occupying authority has the right to proceed with its activities. Any change established by this authority within the occupied district affairs should be initiated in the narrowest possible scope and restricted on meeting the daily life needs of the local citizens.

     As an occupier country in Lebanon, "Israel" has definitely exceeded the proper limits of its violation of all the aforementioned principles, pushing a faction of the Lebanese people to resort to the weapon alternative in order to expulse this invading country from its own territory.
II)The Various Methods of Armed Resistance: The armed resistance boasts multiple kinds of practical methods; some of the international accords tried to submit its practice to defined prerequisites. As the armed resistance substantially gained ground after World War II, the attention has been drawn towards it within the international circles, while the will of organizing its practice and attributing the legal peculiarity over its members witnessed a remarkable increase.

In the past, the traditional rules of the Law of War have been characterizing 2 factions of the people: the "Warriors" who represent the bulk of the armed forces of a given country, and the "non-Warriors" who simply represent the civilians.

     A discord took place over the recognition of the "Warrior" peculiarity pertaining to the irregular fighters, in both following cases:

     The first one concerned the "Levee en Masse" (of the people) against the enemy. This wording has 2 different meanings: it could means a revolt initiated by the civilians against the invading enemy, following an official call from the government, leading to the participation in military organizations established by this government in order to defend the country. But also it could means the deliberate handling of weapons by the civilians to confront the invading enemy.

     The 2nd clause of the regulations attached to the Lahaye Accord decided to attribute the "Warriors" peculiarity on the civilians who embark upon resisting the enemy's presence on their home soil, after lacking the appropriate time enabling them to organize their ranks. However, this clause stipulates to enjoy  the acquisition of the "Warriors" peculiarity the following aspects:
- To publicly hold their weapons, to comply to the laws and traditions of war, and the non-occupation of their district.

The second case is related to the "Volunteers Squads" or "Forces of Liberation" (named as "Corps Francs" in French) or "the Snipers"; these factions consist of individuals who deliberately participate in war operations without being members within the corps of any unit affiliated to the regular army units. The status of these individuals is still undefined, as the international doctrine remains divided towards this issue.

     The 1st clause of regulations attached to the 4th Geneva Accord of 1907 awards the militia members and volunteers the "Regular Warrior" peculiarity if the following 4 prerequisites are provided by them:
 - To be under the guide of a responsible person.
 - To hold a stable and significant mark which should be clearly seen from a far distance.
 - To publicly hold their weapons.
 - To comply to the laws of war when proceeding with their resistance operations.
 - Normally, the volunteers' squads are consisted of the warring country's subjects; however, there should be no particular reason prevention the inclusion of volunteers from other countries' subjects. In this case, the rules of war are applied upon them, like the other volunteers, and they should be treated as "Prisoners of War" should the enemy have captured them.

     During the confronting operations launched against the occupying forces in World War II, two phenomenon were revealed:
 
The first one is the phenomenon of "Free Forces" ("Forces Libres" in French). In 1940, some of the European governments, following their military defeats suffered in battleground against the assailing Nazi forces, approved to halt the war operations. But the national elements of their peoples rejected to comply with this matter and decided, accordingly, to continue the armed struggle. This is exactly what the "French Free Forces" proceeded to achieve, under the direct command of General Charles de Gaulle. The French law doctrine had even equaled between the members of these forces and the regular warriors.

     The second case concerns the phenomenon of  "Resistance Movements"; World War II was also characterized by the crucial role played by the "Partisans" groups in the territories occupied by the "Axis Pact" countries, especially in France. In 1939, there was no legal organizational system pertaining to this phenomenon. As for the Lahaye regulations, they were only interested in the organized resistance movements, and never included anything pertaining to the status of the individual resistance member. Nazi Germany capitalized on this clear gap and considered the resistance members as "Terrorists", and executed everyone caught under its grasp.

     The "French Interior Forces", one of the resistance movements, succeeded to acquire their legal status only following the terrestrial landing of the Allied Forces on the French Western shores in Normandy, and the release of an act regarding this issue by the "French Committee for National Liberation" on June 9th, 1944. On the other hand, the national law doctrine in the European countries which witnessed the resistance movements phenomenon didn't linger towards ratifying the equivalence in general, and the activities between these movements' members and the regular warriors in particular. In this context, the Dutch doctrine was very clear when approving the legitimacy of the clandestine resistance movements which were very active during the Nazi occupation of the Netherlands, particularly when it considered that these movements constituted "a legal force of warriors against the occupier's acts which have been completely contradicting the international law".

     Since the end of World War II, some various kinds of irregular resistance movements were established once again, and have been extremely active in many African and Latin American countries, a fact which forced a lot of people to call for the implementation of appropriate amendments to the laws of war, which should be capable of coping with the new current evaluations in the scope of confronting the assailants or oppressors, and to contain the resistance activities inside or outside the occupied territories, either the 4 mentioned prerequisites are fully available or no.

     If we proceeded into a quick comparison between the rules of the Lahaye Accords and those related to the 4th clause of the Geneva Accord pertaining to the "Prisoners of War", we would notice the main differences restricted in the following aspects:

1- The Lahaye rules were restricted to the protection of militia members and those affiliated within the volunteer forces, which are the combat forces in connection with the fighting armies. However, the second section of the 4th clause had embraced within its protection, in addition to the militia, armed forces, volunteer units members and the workers with armed forces, the members of other volunteer units, including the movements of organized resistance; thus, the aforementioned section had embraced within its protection all kinds of resistance, even if they have been active in total independence from the regular army.

2- The Lahaye rules have attributed the "Warriors" peculiarity on the factions who fight outside the occupied district. On the other hand, the 4th clause had included the regular resistance members working inside or outside the occupied territories, if the 4 prerequisites are fully available within their frame of activities.

3- The Lahaye rules didn't subjugate all kinds of material war and armed conflicts to the rule of law. As for the Geneva Accords, they have been subjected to the rule of law, the official war or the unofficial one, the legal or illegal one. And if these accords have recognized the organized movements and provided them with the full rights of the fighting forces, the same accords have subjugated them, at the very same time, to the rules of the laws of war.

5- The Lahaye and Geneva rules have authorized the people's members who are confronting the enemy to benefit from the "Warriors" peculiarity; this text covers the unorganized resistance operations outside the occupied territories. However, the mentioned rules didn't clearly address the status of the irregular resistance members, or the situation of the people's members who are involved in individual resistance acts inside the occupied territories. In other words, the text didn't discuss the status of the civilians' position that resort to the weapon against the occupying forces inside the occupied territories.

III)The International Efforts Backing the Resistance Legitimacy: When the diplomatic conference which emerged from the Geneva Accords was held, many delegates found it hard to harmonize between both following issues: The announcement of the war's illegitimacy or prohibiting it from one side, and the request of the occupied district citizens for respecting the rights of the occupying country and the compliance to its authority and will from the other side, or prohibiting the civilians from the right of resorting to any kind of rebellion or resistance against the occupation authority. Thus, some people relied on the people's right of self-defense against the occupation, and accordingly submitted suggestions aiming at establishing the clear recognition of the individual resistance operations, that is the "Organized Resistance".

It seems that the conference tried to sympathize with this trend following its ratification of the 5th clause in the accord, which is related to the Prisoners of War; this clause ruled that "If there is any doubt about the affiliation of persons who are involved in warring acts and captured by the enemy in any category of the categories stated in the 4th clause, then, these persons enjoy the protection approved by the accord, while a specialized court will have the task of defining their status".

     However, we are aware that this text is filled with gaps, as it only recognizes the organized resistance, and doesn't provide the suitable and appropriate protection for the resistance when it embodies the individual profile, launched by individual members and having the unorganizational status. This text also leaves for the occupation authorities the issue of judging in the doubts and suspects pertaining to the fighters' affiliation, and in the issue of appointing the specialized court that has the mission of defining their status. Hence, the text doesn't cope with the evaluation of events and current changes witnessed at the international community scene throughout the last years, and isn't pursuing the containing process of the armed resistance phenomenon which enjoys a widespread presence in all the regions of the word, and which claimed a huge impact on the international relations' tensing and the collapse of many political regimes. Finally, this text doesn't comply nor interact with the modern trends and situations revealed in the international practice and expressed by the international law doctrine, which calls for implementing a fair formula able to secure the international recognition of the resistance acts, either the organized or unorganized resistance.

     In accordance with the wishes expressed by the "International Conference for Human Rights", held in Tehran in 1968, the General Assembly of the United Nations announced the resolution 2444 (19th DECEMBER, 1968) in which it called the Secretary General to hold deliberations with the Red Cross International Committee and other related international organizations in order to discuss the appropriate procedures that should be adopted to create a suitable implementation process concerning the international treaties and accords of human profile, and which should be displayed during the armed conflicts.

     The resolution also urged the Secretary General to initiate new international accords of human profile, or to set up specialized legal documents aiming at providing better protection for the citizens, prisoners and fighters in any armed conflict.

     In 1970, and basing on the request submitted by the General Assembly of the United Nations (resolution 2957, announced in 1969), the United Nations Secretary General provided the General Assembly with his second report in which he tackled the issue of respecting human rights during the armed conflicts. The General Assembly discussed this issue very carefully, and announced in 9th December 1970 five resolutions pertaining to it.

     We are only interested in the resolution 2674 in which the General Assembly reiterated its official position towards the necessity of preventing aggressive wars and armed conflicts, and also condemned the conducts of countries who are still involved in launching aggressive and assailant wars, in total violation of the United Nations covenant, and in full defiance of the international conventions' principles like the Geneva Protocol of 1925 and the Geneva Accords of 1949. The very same resolution announced that the countries that violate the immunity of these conventions should be condemned and recognized as fully responsible of these committed acts before the international community; the resolution also affirmed that the individuals who are involved in resistance movements, and the militants for freedom should be treated, when captured by the enemy, as Prisoners of War.

     A conference of governmental experts was hold in Geneva (May-June 1971) and discussed the issue of promoting and reshaping the humanitarian international law liable to be implemented during the periods of armed conflicts.

     The third report submitted by the United Nations' Secretary General to the General Assembly tackled, in particular, the work achieved in the mentioned conference.

     In 20th December 1971, the General Assembly issued a resolution (number 2625) under the title of "The Announcement Related to the International Law Principles, Pertaining to the Friendly Relations and Cooperation between the Countries, According to the United Nations' Convention". This resolution stated that "Each country should refrain from resorting to any coercive procedure able to deny the peoples from their right of self-determination, and from their freedom and independence. When these peoples rush forward to resist, within their right of self-determination, they have the right to solicit and receive the support which is appropriate to the convention's goals and its own principles".

     If we review the list of resolutions issued by the General Assembly, we would notice that since 1975, there was at least 1 text reiterated each year, which includes the reaffirmation of the General Assembly towards the "Legitimacy of peoples' struggle for independence, regional safety, national unity and the liberation from the colonial and foreign domination, and from foreign control, by all means disposed by these peoples, including the armed struggle".

     The most encouraging and positive aspect, admittedly, when focusing on these rules, is embodied in the increasing tendency of the countries who announce their approval of this text; in 1975, for instance, the text secured the approval of 99 countries, against only one (Israel), while 18 other countries abstained from voting on it. As for 1976, the proponents number increased to reach 109 countries, as this trend continued in 1977 (113 endorsing countries) and in 1980 (119 ratifying countries), respectively.

     The "Human Rights Committee" of the United Nations' "Economic and Social Council" emulated the steps taken by the UN General Assembly, and adopted the same text in its latest announcements.

     Thus, we can consider the International Public Law's endorsement of the peoples' right for armed and unarmed struggle, in order to defend their freedom, sovereignty and self-determination, as one of the major achievements and acquisitions recorded by these peoples within their continuous battles in direct confrontation against the oppression and injustice they are submitted to.

     The human societies' history could be summed up through the phenomenon of the violent conflict, the bloody one in general, opposing peoples who are struggling to claim their national right in free living against the brutal oppressing forces (internal and external ones) who are seeking to exploit these peoples and submit them to their own control in order to achieve their sole goals and maintain the supremacy of class or sectarian privileges they enjoy.

     However, the peoples succeeded, thanks to their firm determination of proceeding with the struggle trend and their full preparation to bear all sacrifices throughout many eras, to seize from their oppressors the endorsement of their own right of countering both injustice and oppression, and to grab from the international organizations various treaties, accords and conventions ratifying their right of self-determination, their sole choice of own suitable systems and the free disposal of their natural assets and resources.

     Today, we can confirm that the contemporary international public law rules endorse, in general, the right of all peoples in confronting the aggressive enemy. The national resistance legitimacy, or the liberation war legitimacy against the occupation is not an emergency issue that emerged for the complete surprise of the international law, it is rather an issue which was initiated and put forward on the table for serious discussion in the second half of the last century, when the very first attempts for codifying the rules of war have been directly initiated.

     These are the leading accords and conventions that establish or support the principles pertaining to the resistance right and legitimacy:

I- The Accords of both Lahaye Councils: during the mentioned two councils, the superpowers tried to restrict the legitimacy of war in their own armies and in the wars involving them only, arguing that war is a legitimate means out of many political ones, pertaining only to the sovereign countries. However, the small countries, regularly submitted to both aggression and occupation, rejected this attempt and insisted on the necessity of ratifying the legitimacy of the national resistance and also the necessity of providing it with the full protection within any process of codifying the law of war. Accordingly, the superpowers were forced to comply and announce that the codification of the war's rules doesn't mean, in any way, the derogation from the legitimacy related to the other defense means.

Despite the fact that the purpose of organizing both conferences was to ease the atrocities of wars, supplying protection for human rights and finding peaceful solutions for the international conflicts, the accords emanated from both mentioned conferences have approved the legitimacy of national resistance against the occupation and aggression, and ratified for the resistance members the same rights accorded to the regular army members, volunteer forces and peoples individuals who rush to fight the enemy.

The 2nd clause of the Lahaye resolutions (1907) had defined "the people's rebellion against the enemy" as the group of citizens from the occupied territories residents who take up arms and advance to fight the enemy, either by their own government's order or by a deep incentive emanated from their own obligation or pure nationalism. This clause decided that these fighting civilians are considered as having the status of regular forces, and the peculiarity of warriors should be applied to them, should they be able to provide both following prerequisites: holding  publicly their weapons, and complying to the war laws and rules.

     The "Volunteer Forces" and the "Rebelled people against the enemy" have been generally considered until the end of World War I as movements of organized popular resistance, whose members were eligible of holding the peculiarity of "Warriors". Accordingly, the international law doctrine fully adopted this trend within its procedures.

II)The United Nations' Convention: this convention not only contented with the prohibition of war and use of force, it did even goes beyond that in the fourth section of the 2nd clause, prohibiting the mere threatening of using them. Thus, the United Nations had abolished the principle of legal warfare in the international law, and considered any attacking warfare as an aggressive one.

     The convention approved the war only if it was launched in a legal state of self-defense, and within a frame of defined restrictions. The 51st clause of the convention states that the countries, individually or collectively, have a natural right to defend themselves if submitted to the armed aggression.

     Albeit this major achievement, the colonial countries insisted on their own interpretation of the issue of self-defense, in total contradiction of reality, as they claimed that this issue is only restricted to countries not peoples, and therefore rejected the principle of liberation warfare, guerilla warfare and all movements of the popular resistance.

     However, most of legislators have been convinced that this logic is totally rejected on basis of the international and human reality, since the small countries or the newly-incepted ones aren't able to face the aggression launched by the big countries, by means of regular warfare and on traditional military fronts. The total supremacy of the big countries in weaponry is compelling the small ones to practice their own right of legitimate defense through all kinds of resistance means, primarily the popular resistance and guerilla warfare. In this context, peoples of Europe have put this method at work against Nazi and Fascist military aggression without regarding their resistance as an act contradicting the principle stated in the 51st clause of the United Nations' convention.

     Political governing regimes were crashed by the invading Nazi forces, hence the people, or at least a part of it, surged forward and took place of its government in practicing the right of defending the national soil and independence. During that period of time, the popular resistance was adopted and emerges as a prevalent aspect; therefore, the resistance had been displaying magnificently, and claimed the major role of exhausting the enemy before annihilating its presence on national territory.

     The major superpowers have recognized the legitimacy of this resistance, and dealt with it as the sole legal representative of its people, provided it with weaponry and financial aid, and also allowed it to establish official offices and training camps on their own territories. The United States, for instance, didn't hesitate to secure various means of backing to the resistance movements, materially and morally. In this context, the French resistance enjoyed wide support directly from the United States.

     In fact, all kinds of resistance in Europe have gained the sympathy and support of the United States; in August 1943, the United States, the Soviet Union and Great Britain have all recognized the French resistance. In January 1942, the US Secretary of State made an announcement in which he supported the Albanian resistance against Italian occupation, and added that "The efforts of all various resistance groups displayed in Albania against the common enemy are eliciting our respect and appreciation". As for the peace treaty in Italy, signed in 1947, it also recognized the Italian resistance. If the different legal philosophies have established the people's right to rebel against its government, the logic leads us, primarily, to recognize its right to revolt against a foreign force that is occupying its national soil.

III) The International Accords Pertaining to the Human and Peoples' Right: there are different international accords dealing with this issue, these are some of the most famous and interesting themes:

"The World Declaration of Human Rights", released in 1948 by the United Nations' General Assembly; this declaration considers, in its introduction, that it is necessary to establish a legal system able to secure the protection of human rights, in order to prevent the people from resorting to face the oppression and autocracy.

     Besides the aforementioned "Declaration", 1996 witnessed the release of 2 accords: the accord concerning the economic, social and cultural rights, and the accord pertaining to the human civil and political rights.

     In total compliance with the human rights principles and peoples' right of self-determination, the United Nations General Assembly had ratified (14th December 1960 - resolution 1514) "The Special Declaration of According the Independence to Colonized Territories and Peoples", in which it stated that "The submission of peoples to foreign subjugation, occupation and exploitation consists a repudiation  to the basic human rights, contradicts the United Nations' convention, and exposed world peace and cooperation to danger", and that "Any attempt, partially or collectively, aiming at undermining the national unity and regional safety of any state, is contradicting the purposes of the United Nations' Convention and principles".

IV)Defining the Term "Aggression":  on 14th December 1974, the United Nations' General Assembly issued the long-awaited resolution of defining the "Aggression" term, in which the 1st clause defined it as "The use of armed force by a country against the sovereignty of another country, or against its regional safety, or its political independence".

     The 3rd clause identified the acts that constitute the "Aggression" as following: the invasion, the military occupation, the shelling of another country's district, the blockade of coastline and ports. The 7th clause stipulated that the definition doesn't hold any aspect threatening the right of freedom, independence and self-determination enjoyed by the peoples, or any other aspect which could undermine their right of struggle in order to achieve this goal, and appeal for gaining appropriate support from other sides.

     The "Aggression", according to the 5th clause, couldn't be justified under any given consideration, be it a political, economic or military one, since the aggression warfare is a pure rimed committed against world peace that generates an international responsibility; thus, all acquisitions and privileges emanated from the aggression couldn't be considered legal, and therefore couldn't be able to claim recognition towards its effects.

V)Geneva Accords of 1949:  when initiating these accords, the colonial countries insisted on including the expression "Organized Resistance Movements" in order to tighten the grip over the armed rebellions against the occupation authorities. However, the reality has proved the lack of presence pertaining to an armed resistance capable of providing within its status the prerequisite of "Organization" and "Assemblage" aspects under the predominance reign of he occupation authorities, alongside the previously mentioned 4 prerequisites. If this fact were fully established, it would end in complete breakup and weakness.

     The resistance movements are, in general, clandestine organizations who don't hold a distinctive mark, nor display its members' presence publicly and in military uniform. Moreover, the holding of arms in public is no more a practical aspect in the modern warfare, since the resistance members show off their arms only in combat and military missions.

     Many legislators have found that both prerequisites pertaining to the "Distinctive Mark" and the "Condition of Holding Arms" consist of restrictive chains which couldn't be bore, and that the insistence on complying with all the prerequisites lead to "Depriving an increasing section of resistance movements around the world from this peculiarity, since the international accords laws are in direct opposition (in most of their components) with the guerilla warfare's demanding aspects, based on both clandestine and surprise elements, and with the nature of modern warfare which relies on rapidity and technical advanced weaponry.
 
     In this regard, the courts established at the aftermath of World War II have comprehensively understood these difficulties, thus adopted a flexible position towards the resistance movements which didn't fully adhere to the Lahaye rules' prerequisites (totally complying with the Geneva Accords). These courts have also insisted on the necessity of treating the captured resistance members as Prisoners of War, even if their trial ruled that they aren't eligible to hold the peculiarity of "Legal Warriors".

     Despite the fact that most legislators didn't find in the Geneva Accords an explicit text preventing the people of the occupied territories from announcing the armed revolution or civil rebellion against the occupation authorities, and albeit the fact that a section of these legislators had decided that guerilla warfare and resistance movements initiated by the occupied people in order to defend its country and freedom are "Legal Warfare" protected by the international law, despite all of this, the position regarding the unorganized resistance movements and general spontaneous revolutions against the occupiers remains unstable.

     The reason behind his fact is related to the superpowers' position towards the issue of the resistance legitimacy, since this position is still submitted to their own interests and disparity of positions. In this context, a great nation would support the legitimacy idea if only submitted to occupation, but rejects it when transformed into a real occupation country.

     Great Britain was totally in support of the legitimacy idea in the "War of Boer" witnessed in the Republic of South Africa, the United States was also a firm opponent to this idea during the "American Civil War", but both countries announced their complete backing and assistance to the resistance movements against the Nazi occupation throughout World War II.

     Nazi Germany remained until the end of World War II one of the main enemies of national resistance. Their leaders didn't refrain from issuing clear orders to kill the resistance members without any mercy. However, Germany was forced, due to the harsh military circumstances and through the self-interest incentive, to change its position towards the resistance idea when the Allied forces invaded its territory, including the occupied and unoccupied regions.

     Furthermore, we should not forget the position of the UN General Assembly towards the national resistance movements all over the world, as the Assembly succeeded to establish the full presence of these movements and enhanced their position after rewarding them with the "Observer Member" status.

     To sum up this study, we notice that the citizens of the occupied territories began to see in the international conventions and positions what really justify their resorting to hold the arms in order to defend the honor, human beings and national territory. However, the complete reliance on the laws and conventions is not sufficient.

     We, despite our glorifying feelings towards all the remarkable humanitarian achievements recorded by the contemporary international law in the field of providing protection to the resistance members, remain convinced that the Lebanese resistant doesn't fight the occupation forces because of the supportive position of the international laws, nor because the Geneva Accords are capable of providing him with comprehensive and reliable protection, but because he learned through the experience of his nation and other nations' experiences that the door of liberty and dignity couldn't be knocked at without the bloodstained hands.
