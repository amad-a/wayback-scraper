"Israelis" reel from Hizbullah attacks
 

"With two-thirds of "Israels" fatalities in the south this year caused by Hizbullahs roadside bombs, "Israels" Army intelligence chief has acknowledged that the ambushes posed a serious threat to the occupation forces, who constantly demand new methods of detection and prevention."

"Major General Amos Malka added that Katyusha missile attacks into northern Galilee are now an exception and are usually provoked when the "Israelis" attack civilians."

"He acknowledged that Hizbullah is continually updating its tactics to overcome improvements in "Israeli" defenses in the zone, which he described as a contest of technology and a contest of brainpower.

In the past year, we have noticed Hizbullahs attempt to identify our weak points and increase activities that caused us the most damage, he told the Maariv newspaper. But in their opinion, most of our casualties are caused by explosive devices and therefore theyre trying to increase its use.

Of the 18 "Israeli" troops killed in the zone in 1998, 12 have been from roadside bomb attacks.

We are continuing to invent ways of discovering explosive devices, General Malka said.

The roadside bomb which killed three soldiers and wounded four others Nov. 16 caused the highest "Israeli" casualty toll in the south in over a year. Hizbullah guerrillas had planted the bomb 30 meters from the Markaba post beside the "Israeli" border.

Their ability to reach the perimeter of this position was daring but weve seen it also in previous years, he said. In the past two years, weve noticed that there has been a change from planting devices on roads to planting devices on routes used by infantry on patrols and ambush duties.

"In November 1997, the "Israeli" Army was growing concerned at Hizbullahs use of anti-tank missiles which had knocked out several Merkava tanks. That month at least three Merkavas were destroyed by guerrillas wielding newly-acquired AT-4 Spigot anti-tank missiles or possibly more advanced U.S.-made TOW rockets."

"General Malka noted that their had been fewer attacks involving anti-tank missiles in the past year but we might see this again in the future.

"Hizbullahs anti-tank squad disabled two Merkavas Monday in separate attacks on Dabshe and Bir Kallab."

"There has been a marked decline in the use of the larger Katyusha rocket, which before the April war in 1996 was regularly launched across the border at "Israeli" settlements in Galilee."

Katyusha rockets fired on northern communities are more rare and have turned from being tactical weapons into strategic ones. Firing occurs only when Hizbullah believes that the IDF* has breached the (April) Understanding or has changed the rules of the game, he said.

"Perhaps Hizbullahs greatest strength as a guerrilla organization is its commitment to total secrecy in operational matters. This is one reason why the "Israeli" Army has had such little success in targeting the partys leaders in the south and predicting when and where attacks are likely to occur."

"The last successful "Israeli" operation against Hizbullah was in Kfour, near Nabatieh, in August 1997 when helicopter-borne troops killed five guerrillas, three of them local commanders. That was a temporary lapse of judgment on Hizbullahs part, said a senior security source. Since then, the "Israelis" have been scratching their heads looking for further opportunities.

"Hizbullah was intent on capturing an "Israeli" soldier to use as a bargaining chip to secure the release of detainees."
