Delegates tour South in show of support…(3)

Two separate delegations from the Al-Quds Foundation conference toured the South on Wednesday, paying tribute to resistance fighters whom they said should serve as examples to liberate all occupied holy lands.
“It is resistance which will liberate Palestine and, with God’s will, holy Al-Quds,” Iranian MP Ali Akbar Mohtashemi, who headed one delegation, told reporters.
The MP, who was greeted by Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, Hizbullah’s commander in the South, and other party leaders, visited Fatima Gate on the border and toured Khiam prison. He was briefed by former inmates on torture methods used by their captors.
Mohtashemi described the prison as “the fierce Zionist enemy’s detention camp,” and said resistance against the occupation “should serve as a lesson to all Muslims and to the Palestinian people.”
Addressing “our brethren in occupied Palestine and all Palestinians,” Mohtashemi said Arabs and Muslims who gathered in Beirut for the conference on preserving the Arab character of Al-Quds were unanimous in declaring they “will not leave you alone, but will be with you in liberating Palestine.”
Qaouk paid tribute to Iran’s aid, noting that Mohtashemi had joined the resistance movement in its early stages and was wounded. “The Lebanese people will not forget Iran’s huge sacrifices and role in the liberation, this great achievement,” he said. “Iran nowadays, standing on Syria’s side, has considerably changed the existing equation and “Israel” will have to take the Iranian role into consideration in any possible war.”
Mohtashemi later visited Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, whom he invited to attend an international parliamentary conference in support of the Palestinian intifada, to be held in Tehran in April.
“If the new (US) administration wants to follow the same course adopted by the previous one, it will inflict harm on the national interests of the American people and state more than hitherto,” Mohtashemi told reporters afterward.
He said the “enmity between our people and American rulers will not change unless the American administration changes its policies.”
Meanwhile, another delegation comprising dozens of Arab and Islamic participants in the Al-Quds conference also visited Khiam and Fatima Gate, arriving in mini-buses.
They included the leader of Pakistan’s al-Jamaa al-Islamiya, Hussein Ahmad, and his counterpart in Kashmir, Abdel-Rashid Tourabi.
The delegation visited the former “Israeli” outpost of Dabshe, accompanied by the vice-president of Hizbullah’s political council, Mahmoud Qmati, and the head of the party’s politburo in the North, Mohammed Saleh.
““Israel’s” defeat in the South proves that we can liberate all occupied land sacred to us,” said Ahmad.
“We’re very proud of the resistance and Hizbullah’s accomplishments in the South,” Tourabi said, adding that the people of Kashmir supported the resistance in both Lebanon and Palestine.
Accompanying them, Algerian MP Hassan Araybi said Hizbullah’s resistance showed that “strength comes from faith, not weapons.”
“It is an honor to every Arab and Muslim to visit places which gave birth to heroes who accomplished the greatest victory in our modern history,” said Syrian historian Ahmad Houeis.
Houeis suggested that political decisions should be made by the mujahiddin, rather than the Arab League.
Those “who have sacrificed their lives should decide the Islamic nations’ fate,” he said.
However, the visitors shouted anti-”Israeli” slogans, such as: “death to “Israel”,” “we will return,” and “Palestine is ours.”

February Saturday 03-2001:

Hizbullah ready to step up attacks on “Israel”: Haaretz …(1)

Hizbullah has deployed Katyusha missile-launchers in southern Lebanon, sparking fears of an upsurge in violence at the “Israeli”-Lebanese border, the “Israeli” daily Haaretz said Friday, quoting a military officer.

According to the newspaper's military source, Hizbullah has set up a strategy enabling it to launch an attack "within a few minutes", firing from all along the border on “Israeli” targets deep inside “Israel”.

The “Israeli” army officer added the new long-range missiles used by the movement could reach the outskirts of the northern “Israeli” port city of Haifa.

The “Israeli” army had taken measures to prepare for such an attack, Haaretz said without elaborating.

The “Israeli” air force flew at low altitude over the sector Thursday , including the eastern tip of southern Lebanon, near the Shebaa Farms area, Lebanese correspondents said.

Hizbullah, which spearheaded armed opposition to “Israel”'s 22-year occupation of southern Lebanon, has led several attacks on “Israeli” positions in the Shebaa Farms, capturing three “Israeli” soldiers October 7 in the area seized from Syria by “Israel” in 1967 and now claimed by Beirut.

Caretaker Prime Minister Ehud Barak has often threatened to retaliate with large-scale attacks on Lebanese infrastructures and Syrian positions in Lebanon, but has refrained from carrying out heavy military operations against Lebanon since the May 24 “Israeli” pull-out.

His right-wing rival and frontrunner in the February 6 election for prime minister, Ariel Sharon, has taken a much firmer stance against Hizbullah's attacks, accusing Barak of being weak.

February Saturday 16-2001:

Resistance expects status quo to go on…(1)

The timing of Friday’s fatal ambush appears to be a clear signal to the incoming Israeli government of Prime Minister-elect Ariel Sharon that Hizbullah has not abandoned its bid to eject the Israeli Army from the Shebaa Farms, despite the lull of the past two months.
Since Hizbullah recommenced military operations against the Israeli Army last October, a “balance of terror” has existed between the two. It theoretically enables Hizbullah and the Israeli Army to trade blows in the Shebaa Farms without the conflict escalating beyond control.
Hizbullah has deliberately confined its attacks to the Shebaa Farms and to military targets only in an attempt to weaken Israeli justification for harsh retaliation. The party leadership is aware that retaliatory raids against Lebanese infrastructure would likely result in nationwide opprobrium against them.
Hizbullah doesn’t want to sacrifice the plaudits it won for its role in ousting the Israeli Army from the south last year.
Similarly, Ehud Barak has refrained from ordering massive attacks in retaliation to previous incidents in the Shebaa Farms to prevent escalation. Hizbullah has warned that it will fire rockets across the border if Israel responds to its attacks disproportionately. Syria appears to be content to allow Hizbullah to dictate the pace of operational activity. Despite Sharon’s past military record, Hizbullah seems to believe that the “balance of terror” will continue to shackle the next Israeli government.
“If Barak was able to do anything in the South, he would have done so,” Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, Hizbullah’s southern commander told The Daily Star recently. “The reality is that they’re in a weak position. The coming of Sharon doesn’t change the reality that northern Israel is within (our) range.”
But there are complications threatening to upset the balance. The first is that Sharon was elected on a “security” rather than “peace” platform. Hizbullah’s deadly ambush may allow Sharon to make good on his electoral vow and restore the deterrence factor that many Israelis believe was lost by the unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon.
Punishing Lebanon for attacks on the Shebaa Farms which would have the understanding of the international community would also serve to deflect some attention from the crisis in the West Bank and Gaza. In such a scenario, the residents of northern Israel would certainly suffer from Hizbullah’s Katyushas, but Lebanon would fare considerably worse.
Secondly, since Israel’s withdrawal last May, Hizbullah has concentrated on fulfilling more of a regional role as exemplar to  Muslims, especially Palestinians.
Hizbullah may thus care a little less about the opinions of the divisive Lebanese when it knows that its military operations allow the party to maintain a high profile in the Islamic world.

Israelis justify draconian strategy by creating false links to Hizbullah…(2)

There has been a noticeable tendency in recent days for Israel to widen the circle of blame for planned acts of violence in the Jewish state, beyond that of the Palestinians, to include Hizbullah and by extension Iran.
The first incident was the announcement last weekend that Israel has been holding since Jan. 5 Jehard Shuman, a Lebanese-born British national, on suspicion that he was planning guerrilla attacks inside Israel on the orders of Hizbullah.
Hizbullah has refused to comment on Shuman’s arrest, neither confirming nor denying that he was working on behalf of the party.
The second incident was the assassination of Massoud Ayad, a Lieutenant Colonel in Force 17, the personal security unit of Palestinian President Yasser Arafat.
Ayad was killed in Gaza when his car was struck by a missile fired from a helicopter gunship.
The Israeli Army promptly issued a list of accusations against Ayad, charging him with having planned and carried out bomb and mortar attacks against the Netzarim settlement in Gaza, smuggling arms and drugs, and heading a Hizbullah cell in the Palestinian territories.
The Israeli Army said that Ayad visited Lebanon last summer for training and to receive instructions from Hizbullah.
“He maintained continuous contact with Hizbullah officials in Lebanon and was activated by them to carry out terrorist attacks inside the Gaza Strip in the name of Hizbullah,” the Israeli military said. “In addition, (Ayad) was involved in the planning and attempted kidnapping of Israeli soldiers,” it added.
The third, possibly related, incident were comments from the US State Department aired on CNN regarding its unease at a meeting of anti-Israeli and anti-American militant groups in Beirut last month.
In particular, one US official said that the presence of Iranian delegates to the meeting was a “cause of concern.”
Since the Al-Aqsa intifada began last September, Iran has “stepped up its terrorist activity … and stepped up its planning for terrorist attacks,” one US official maintained.
The meeting in question was a well-publicized conference in which some 200 Arab and Muslim figures met to discuss measures to protect Jerusalem from Judaization.
The gathering was organized by Hizbullah, Hamas, Palestinian Islamic Jihad, and the Islamic National Conference. Among the participants was Ali Akhbar Mohtashemi, an Iranian MP, and a former Iranian ambassador to Damascus who played a major role in the founding of Hizbullah in the 1980s.
Ironically, the flurry of accusations against Hizbullah comes at a time when the resistance has been noticeably quiet on the Shebaa Farms front. The last operation carried out by Hizbullah in the area was a roadside bomb ambush on Nov. 26, in which an Israeli soldier was killed.
“There’s been too much mentioning of Hizbullah lately,” said a senior Western military source. The source suggested that the Israeli military was deliberately implicating Hizbullah and Iran in planned attacks in the Palestinian territories and Israel to serve as a pretext for any military action against the resistance in Lebanon. “They are setting up the stage, just in case,” the source said.
Neither outgoing Israeli Premier Ehud Barak nor his hawkish successor, Ariel Sharon, are expected to launch pre-emptive raids into Lebanon for the time being. Given the sentiment in the Arab world, Israel is unlikely to exacerbate hostilities by provocative actions outside the main theater of conflict in the West Bank and Gaza.
However, linking Hizbullah and Iran to acts of violence in the Palestinian territories and Israel planned or executed serves to defuse any misgivings that exist over the Israeli government’s controversial policy of assassinating Palestinian activists.
Ayad may or may not have had links with Hizbullah, but that is beside the point, said a European diplomat.
“Palestinians come in all shapes and sizes, from those you negotiate with in five-star hotels to stone-throwers,” the diplomat said. “But by renaming them collectively as Hizbullah terrorists you are discrediting them to the fullest and gaining a 100 percent acceptance rate among the Israeli public” for the Israeli Army’s actions.
Prime Minister Rafik Hariri echoed that view in his dismissal on Thursday of Israel’s claim that Ayad was working for Hizbullah.
“It’s easier for the Israelis to say that they have assassinated a Palestinian belonging to Hizbullah than an aide to Arafat,” he told reporters in Paris.
Hizbullah and Iran are regarded in Israel, correctly, as being implacably hostile towards the Jewish state. References to Hizbullah and Iran lend the impression that the Israeli Army is not only contending with the Palestinians whose plight draws sympathy domestically and internationally but also a broader, more sinister, pan-Islamist front.
The Israeli Army has long used Hizbullah as a “bogeyman” to its own advantage usually to win increases to the defense budget.
This point was recently illustrated by an Israeli columnist, Reuven Pedatzur, in an unprecedented opinion piece in the Haaretz daily newspaper.
“Where’s the wisdom in the commanders of the Middle East’s strongest and most advanced army glorifying the threat of the Hizbullah, broadcasting to the public a sense of fear in the face of a threat from such a tiny organization?” Pedatzur asked. “The absurdity of attributing to the Hizbullah a strategic threat is symptomatic of a certain routine on the part of the senior command. It can’t operate without identifying and nurturing new threats.

February Monday 19-2001:

Nasrallah slams US Ambassador for denouncing Hizbullah’s attack
Qassem we shall liberate Shibaa Farms by armed force…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, accused the United States on Sunday of blocking foreign investment to pressure Lebanon into signing a peace agreement with “Israel”.
Nasrallah made the comments at a memorial gathering for the late president of the Higher Shiite Council, Sheikh Mohammed Mehdi Shamseddine, who died last month.
The Hizbullah leader’s remarks came one day after US Ambassador David Satterfield spoke of the harmful economic effects of Hizbullah’s attack.
“This is not a question of the Shibaa Farms,” Nasrallah said. “Only when Lebanon gives in and signs a settlement agreement based on the Zionists’ conditions, the American master might, just might, extend a helping hand.”

Also, Hizbullah’s deputy secretary-general, Naim Qassem, rejected “Israeli” and UN charges that the Friday attack violated the Blue Line, which was drawn after “Israel’s” May withdrawal from the South.
“We have never heard, in our entire history, of a ‘Blue Line’ being regarded as an international border,” Qassem said during a ceremony in Beirut’s southern suburbs.
“This (line) was written and made public to serve Zionist interests,” the resistance leader argued. “The Shibaa Farms are Lebanese and we shall liberate them by armed force.”
Replying to Satterfield, Qassem said: “Are the “Israeli” crimes against the Palestinians not provocative? And were the “Israeli” attacks against Lebanon not provocative? As for the Shibaa Farms, is their continued occupation by the “Israelis”, and the continued detention of Lebanese political prisoners in “Israel”, not provocative?”
He stressed that the attack was “in defense of our land and it was a legitimate right to the resistance forces to launch it.”
He dismissed what he said was linkage between economic aid for Lebanon and halting resistance actions in the South.
“We are not among those who seek economic aid through selling their pride and independence, even if this costs us assistance from donor states,” he declared, adding that “the Americans are the main source of trouble for Lebanon. Their economic aid is nothing but a political and cultural trap. Thus, we should continue with our struggle.”

Information Minister Ghazi Aridi said Saturday that attacks against the Jewish state “had not stopped,” and that the resistance would make the decision to halt its operations. In an interview with Egyptian radio, Aridi noted that “Israeli” warplanes had violated Lebanese air space in recent weeks “on an almost daily basis.”
Also on Saturday, Speaker Nabih Berri visited Damascus, where he conferred with Assad on the latest local and regional developments. Berri later lunched with Syrian Vice-President Abdel-Halim Khaddam.

February Thursday 22-2001:

Rain of Rockets Frightens “Israel” into Inaction Over Shibaa Attack…(1)

The specter of long-range Katyusha rockets raining down on the entire Galilee district and such major cities as Haifa, Tiberias, Nahariya and Safad has frightened “Israel” into withholding a massive response to Hizbullah's latest missile attack, which killed one “Israeli” soldier and wounded two in the Shibaa farms, a Tel Aviv newspaper says.
The newspaper Hatsofe said Hizbullah had set up a belt of mobile multi-barreled rocket launchers and truck-mounted missiles along “Israel’s” northern flank ready to go off the moment “Israel” launches a large-scale military offensive against Lebanon.
The Japanese-made Isuzo truck launchers carry Fajr-3 (Arabic for dawn-3) projectiles, a third generation of Katyusha rockets with a 60-kilometer range manufactured by Iran's air force industry specially for Hizbullah in Lebanon.
Large consignments of these rockets were air-lifted to Hizbullah in Lebanon months before “Israel” withdrew its army from the southern Lebanese border enclave last May, terminating a 22-year occupation, said Hatsofe, which speaks for “Israel’s” Mifdal religious party.
These rockets were originally meant to be used against troops manning the occupied zone before the May 24 withdrawal. From their current positions, fixed or mobile, they can cover the entire Galilee which houses one third of “Israel’s” industry, Hatsofe said. Its report was highlighted by An-Nahar.

Haifa preparing for possible Katyushas…(2)

” Local authorities from Haifa northward have received instructions to make appropriate preparations for the possibility of Katyusha rocket attacks from south Lebanon, OC Home Front Command Maj.-Gen. Gaby Ophir said yesterday.
"Regarding Haifa and the bayside suburbs, for the past year, Home Front Command generally and the district commanders in particular have visited the various local authorities and have given instructions regarding the preparedness of bomb shelters," said Ophir.
"Whether the bayside suburbs, Acre and so on, might be the target of Katyusha attacks - I certainly hope not. For the time being, south Lebanon and the [border] line is quiet." Ophir noted that since the completion of the “Israeli” Army’s withdrawal from south Lebanon on May 24 last year, there had not been any rocket attacks, and expressed the hope that this would remain the case.
He spoke to reporters following the inauguration in Kiryat Shmona yesterday of a new electronic control and monitoring system which enables the opening of all the city's public bomb shelters at the press of a single button.
The innovative system, which cost NIS 5 million to install, also relays information to the central control about the state of the shelters. A check carried out earlier this week revealed that 20 of them were flooded with rain-water.
The “Israeli” Army has been aware for a long time that Hizbullah has acquired the long-range Fajar version of the Katyusha rocket which would be capable of reaching as far south as Haifa and its environs.
Furthermore, the conventional 107 millimeter and 122 mm rockets also have a greater range now than previously due to the absence of the security zone. The fact that these Katyusha rockets can now be stationed closer to the international border instead of deeper inside south Lebanon when the zone existed means they would probably be able to hit places like Safed, Karmiel, Acre, Hatzor and Rosh Pina, which had previously been out of range.
The Haifa municipality spokesperson said all public bomb shelters and those in schools in the city and the neighboring suburbs of Kiryat Haim and Kiryat Shmuel were kept maintained and ready for instant use, should the need arise.

February Friday 23-2001:

Hizbullah Leader, PM held a meeting…(1)
Nasrallah: Resistance is the people’s right
Hariri: I’ll support operations in Shibaa Farms

A rare meeting between Premier Rafik Hariri and Hizbullah’s secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, was described on Thursday as “positive and successful,” having apparently ended in an agreement to maintain constant cooperation.
But Nasrallah is believed to have insisted that no restrictions be imposed on Hizbullah’s operations in the Shibaa Farms, despite Hariri’s reported concerns.

Hariri met with Nasrallah for two-and-a-half hours at the Hizbullah leader’s home in Haret Hreik late on Wednesday night. The meeting, which was apparently called by Hariri, came amid reported tension between the prime minister and Hizbullah stemming from last Friday’s deadly ambush of an “Israeli” convoy in the Shibaa Farms in which one “Israeli” soldier was killed and three others wounded.
Hariri and Hizbullah agreed to cooperate with each other, they said, but the resistance said it would not agree to “a joint operations center with the government.”
Hizbullah cloaks its activities amid tight secrecy to prevent details of pending operations being leaked to “Israel”.
According to Hizbullah sources, Nasrallah insisted that the resistance was “the people’s right,” and should as such be supported by the government. In exchange, Nasrallah said that Hizbullah was ready to help the authorities in any way possible. Nasrallah said that Hizbullah had no intention of allowing its military operations to impact negatively on Hariri’s government. Hizbullah’s target was not Hariri, but “Israel”, the resistance leader stressed.
Nasrallah argued that it was not the resistance that was threatening investments in Lebanon, but the “Israel’s” occupation of the Shibaa Farms.
US Ambassador David Satterfield had described last Friday’s fatal attack as “provocative” and warned that Lebanon could suffer economically if such actions continued.
Nasrallah had responded by condemning what he described as attempts to submit Hizbullah to “Israeli”-US conditions and provoke a rebellion against Syria and the Taif Accord.
Nasrallah explained to Hariri during their meeting that his comments had not been directed at Hariri but were an attack against the US.
It was only the second time that Hariri has met with Nasrallah one-on-one. The first occasion was three months ago, when the two talked for four hours while paying condolences to Hajj Hussein Khalil, Nasrallah’s political adviser, on the death of his father.
Sources close to Wednesday’s meeting said Hariri had assured Nasrallah he was not yielding to pressure from the US.
“He came to say: ‘Look, I’m not surrendering to American pressure. I still support you and support operations in the Shibaa Farms,’” the source said.
Hariri said he wanted to maintain contacts with Hizbullah to develop a “unified political language” between the government and the resistance.
The source said that Hariri and Nasrallah agreed to hold further meetings.
A Hizbullah spokesman said that military operations would continue in the Shibaa Farms.
“This is a red line for us,” the spokesman said.
“Israel” has reportedly asked Egypt to help persuade Hizbullah not to open a second front on the Lebanon-”Israel” border.
““Israel” asked Egypt to ask Syria and Lebanon to stop the activities against “Israeli” soldiers by Hizbullah,” an “Israeli” official source said on Thursday.
Egypt so far has supported Lebanon’s attempts to regain the Shibaa Farms through military force.

Sharon Warns Powell: Hizbullah Can Paralyze One Third of “Israel”…(2)

The “Israeli” media said Sunday Prime Minister-Elect Ariel Sharon and chief-of-staff Lt. Gen. Shaul Mofaz gave "this gloomy report about “Israel’s” security in separate overnight meetings they held with U.S. Secretary of State Colin Powell in Al-Quds.
"Iranian supplies of upgraded medium-range Fajr-3 rockets are currently deployed in dozens of multi-barreled launchers along the northern “Israeli” border, which is effectively controlled by Hizbullah's fighters," state-run radio “Israel” quoted the two Jewish leaders as telling Powell.
