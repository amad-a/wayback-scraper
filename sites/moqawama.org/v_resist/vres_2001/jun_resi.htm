Views in the Resistance

June Thursday 07-2001:

 

Nasrallah warns “Israel” not to invade…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general said Wednesday that “Israel” should not “imagine” it can again invade the country as it did in 1982.
Speaking at a ceremony to honor the Lebanese Units to Resist “Israeli” Occupation, an event held on the occasion of the first anniversary of the liberation of the South, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah urged constant readiness, warning that “the battle is still on.”
A statement issued by the group’s information department said Nasrallah pledged that resistance will continue until all “Israeli”-occupied territory, including the Shibaa Farms, has been liberated.
“Zionist escalation and threats make it imperative to remain active as we are committed to protecting our country and defending our people,” the statement quoted Nasrallah as saying.
The statement also said Nasrallah praised the Units’ “steadfastness” and that he backed their demands to join in operations to liberate Shibaa Farms.
The Units’ commander presented Nasrallah with a symbolic shield in appreciation of his support.

 

Muslim Community in Ukraine Celebrates Days of Liberation…(2)

The Ahlul Beit Association in Odessa and Sanfarubble in Ukraine held in both cities exhibitions and ceremonies to mark the days of liberation and resistance against the “Israeli” occupation in southern Lebanon. On the occasion, a number of Arab and Islamic organizations representatives stressed on the need to support the Palestinian intifadah, and not yield to Zionist and American pressure. They also said Hezbullah’s resistance, which defeated the Zionist enemy, is to be taken as a model.

June Monday 11-2001:

 

‘Resistance is tribute to Assad’
Hizbullah leader vows to liberate ‘every inch’ of occupied territory…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general lavished praise on late Syrian President Hafez Assad on Sunday, vowing to continue the struggle against “Israel” until all occupied land is liberated
Speaking at a memorial service in Qordaha commemorating the first anniversary of Assad’s death, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah said his group’s members “will bring to your place of rest olive branches from Palestine, and they will plant them only for you.”
“We have nothing for the enemies except blood and rifles,” said Nasrallah, who then called on Arab leaders to follow the uncompromising policies of the late Assad, who refused to make peace with “Israel” unless it first returned occupied Arab territory.
“One year after you left us,” Nasrallah continued, “we come back to your side because we promised to live up to your ideals. A promise is a promise and we have to be faithful to you … Today the resistance forces, which you sponsored, protected and supported with all your strength, have now become an example to be followed by the entire Arab world.”
The resistance leader then thanked Assad for “unifying” the Lebanese, saying that the country owes him a “debt” which it “will not forget.”
He also lauded Assad for refusing to give in to either American or “Israeli” pressure during peace negotiations, saying both nations “were wrong again when they took (Assad) for granted. He refused to budge and give up one inch of land or of the national rights.”
“He acted wisely and courageously,” Nasrallah added. “He was a man for whom land was not available for rent, sale or exchange. He was a man for whom land was not a matter of meters or inches. It was a matter of national pride over which there was no compromise.”
Syrian President Bashar al-Assad, who assumed power after the death of his father last year, presided over the ceremony, which was also attended by Arab and Western envoys.
Hizbullah, whose resistance efforts ended a 22-year-long occupation of the South last May, enjoyed strong backing from Assad’s regime. He regarded its campaign against the Jewish state as a crucial asset in Syria’s conflict with “Israel” to regain the Golan Heights, seized from Syria in 1967.
After last May’s withdrawal, Hizbullah stepped up its attacks ­ with Syria’s blessing ­ along the border of the Shibaa Farms, saying “Israel” was still occupying it.
“We will not give up one inch of the Shibaa Farms … We will regain it with blood, holy struggle and resistance. We will not also give up the Golan for many reasons because Syria supported Lebanon and helped it liberate its land,” said Nasrallah.
“We will not forget Palestine and Al-Quds will remain the focal point for our strugglers,” he said to applause.
Assad first assumed power in 1970, after which he ruled for nearly 30 years. On Sunday, Syrian newspapers published pictures of the deceased Assad framed in black. The streets of Damascus were almost empty as many flocked to Qordaha.
The memorial service was also attended by a large number of politicians from Lebanon.

Hizbullah Wages War of Nerves Over 16th Century Border Tomb…(2)

A large billboard showing six dead or wounded “Israeli” soldiers appeared Saturday on a hill overlooking the tense Lebanese-”Israeli” border, erected by the Hizbullah activists.
On the other side of the barbed-wire border fence, a few meters (yards) away from the 3- meter high and 3-meter long image, “Israeli” troops made no attempt to intervene. Neither did any of the U.N. peacekeepers from the 30-man Ghanaian unit manning a post nearby.
Beneath the reproduced photographs, Arabic and Hebrew writings read: " Sharon, don't forget that your soldiers are still in Lebanon."
The “Israeli” soldiers on the billboard were not identified or recognizable, but they are thought to have been killed or wounded in clashes with Hizbullah fighters during “Israel”'s occupation of south Lebanon, which ended 13 months ago.
Hizbullah, which has vowed to continue fighting the “Israeli” army until it vacates the Shibaa Farms area, is holding three “Israeli” soldiers and a colonel and wants to exchange them for Lebanese and other Arab prisoners in “Israeli” jails.
On Thursday, Hizbullah leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah warned, that the guerrillas may kidnap more “Israeli” soldiers for a possible swap.
"If we reach the point where we consider that the four we have are not enough and that more are needed, then this matter will become a priority for us," Nasrallah said.
In October, Sgt. Adi Avitan, Sgt. Avraham Binyamin and First Sgt. Omar Sawayid were detained by Hizbullah in the Shibaa Farms area.
Another “Israeli”, Elhanan Tennenbaum, a reserve “Israeli” army officer described by “Israel” as a private businessman and by Hizbullah as a spy, was lured to Beirut and seized one week later.
Besides a dispute over the U.N.-drawn Blue Line on the Lebanese-”Israeli” border, the two countries are also arguing over a few inches of land that bisects a 16th-century tomb sacred to both Muslims and Jews.
Shiite Muslims contend the tomb, near the village of Houla, 74 kilometers southeast of Beirut, that contains the remains of a 16th century religious scholar known as Sheik Abbad. “Israelis” insist it is the burial place of Talmudic scholar Rabbi Ashi, a contemporary of Sheik Abbad.
Jewish and Muslim believers occasionally make the climb to the hill to pray at the tomb, and demonstrations on the Lebanese side have often resulted in warning shots from “Israel”.
In the last such incident on May 24, the anniversary of the “Israeli” withdrawal, two Lebanese men were shot in the leg.

June Tuesday 12-2001:

 

Hizbullah: ‘no Iranians on border’…(1)

Hizbullah on Monday denied “Israeli” press reports claiming that Iranian intelligence forces had deployed along the border to gather information in preparation for guerrilla attacks against the Jewish state.
“Israel’s” Yediot Ahronot daily reported Monday that dozens of Iranian Revolutionary Guards arrived in Beirut a month ago for briefings with members of Hizbullah.
The Iranian intelligence men, being more professional than their Hizbullah counterparts, decided to check the situation on the ground for themselves and devise possible plans of action, Yediot said, quoting a report on “Israel’s” Channel 2 television.
The newspaper claimed that the Iranian agents had established an observation post on Sheikh Abbad Hill, facing a new “Israeli” outpost.
“From time to time the “Israeli” press works on fabricating baseless news,” Hizbullah said in a statement. “We have learned from the “Israeli” enemy that whenever they want to pave the way for an aggression they document such lies.”
Security sources in the South also dismissed the report.
“If anything, it’s a case of Hizbullah feeding the Iranians information, not the other way around,” said a UNIFIL officer who spoke on condition of anonymity.

June Thursday14-2001:

 

Hizbullah Fields Strella Missiles Against “Israel’s” Air Force…(1)

Hizbullah has fielded a new air defense system to confront “Israel’s” unrelenting 'sonic war' in advance of U.N. Secretary-General Kofi Annan's visit to Lebanon.
A Hizbullah communiqué highlighted by the Beirut media Wednesday said Party of God's air defense unit had fired twice on intruding “Israeli” fighter jets over Bint Jbeil and Tyre Tuesday.
No hits were claimed in the communiqué that reported the first Hizbullah interception of “Israeli” fighter jets since the Jewish state terminated its 22-year occupation of south Lebanon nearly 13 months ago.
An-Nahar said two surface-to-air missiles were shoulder-fired on the planes over Zibkin near Tyre in addition to automatic fire from anti-aircraft guns.
The Soviet-designed Sam-7 and Sam-9 missiles are the only heat-seeking anti-aircraft rockets that are shoulder-fired. They are known as Strella missiles.
“Israeli” pilots have a standard practice of releasing heat balloons to deflect ground-to-air projectiles.
Media reports said the development indicated that Hizbullah fighters would henceforth be firing at intruding “Israeli” warplanes and navy gunboats in Lebanon's territorial waters hostilities with “Israel” well beyond the Shabaa farms.
Media commentators said the aerial hostilities were expected to figure high in Annan's talks with President Lahoud, Premier Hariri, Speaker Berri and Foreign Minister Mahmoud Hammoud on Friday and Saturday.
Annan's personal representative Steffan de Mistura disclosed on Tuesday that the secretary-general would not be visiting the UNIFIL peacekeeping force in south Lebanon or inspecting the Blue Line as the local media had widely anticipated.

June Tuesday 26-2001:

 

Resistance will ‘extract our security’…(1)

Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, Hizbullah’s commander for the South, said on Monday that unleashing anti-aircraft gunfire against “Israeli” warplanes violating the Lebanese airspace emphasized the resistance’s commitment to defend the homeland against the ““Israeli” hegemony and provocation.”
Qaouk was commenting on Sunday’s “Israeli” overflights, which were confronted by ground fire.
He criticized the silence of the world community toward “Israel’s” disregard for international law, “which is increasingly taking the shape of a conspiracy” with “Israel” against the Arabs.
As such, he said, “we are not going to beg for our people’s security or the sovereignty of our country. We shall extract our security,” he said during a ceremony in Kounine to lay the cornerstone of the Imam Hussein complex.
Qaouk called on the government to “shoulder its responsibilities” and provide the development needs of the population in the liberated villages, insisting that inhabitants “were not seeking rewards for their past sacrifices.”
