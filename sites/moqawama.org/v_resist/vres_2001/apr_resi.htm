Views in the Resistance

April Wednesday 18-2001:

 

Hizbullah's Constant Rule Is Resistance Operations Shall Continue Until Liberation Is Achieved…(1)

Responding to the “Israeli” enemy leaders words about changing the rules of the game following the “Israeli” air raid on a Syrian position in Lebanon, Hizbullah deputy Secretary-General Sheikh Nahim Qassem asserted that the resistance has one unchangeable rule, which is the continuation of its activities for the sake of liberation- of the occupied Lebanese Shibaa Farms and detainees in “Israeli” prisons.

Sheikh Qassem affirmed the military operations aim at liberation and are not carried out for political uses, nor are the operations directed against any side but the Zionist occupation.

 Commenting on Lebanese daily al-Mustaqbal's words, belonging to Premier Rafiq Hariri, that Hizbullah's Shibaa Farm operation on Saturday was ill-timed, Sheikh Qassem said the daily's comment was ill-timed.

He also said that the recent “Israeli” pounding of the Syrian position shows the extent of the Zionist irritation from the Lebanese-Syrian coordination and cohesion.

 

Southern Lebanese, not scared by renewed “Israeli” raids…(2)

“ “Israeli” air strikes Monday did not scare the people of south Lebanon who lived with the dual attrocities of both the country's civil war (1975-1990) and a 22-year “Israeli” occupation that ended last May.

Although “Israeli” fighter planes bombarded a Syrian radar station east of Beirut, killing at least one Syrian soldier and wounding four more, in the first direct “Israeli” attack on the Syrian military since 1982, many south Lebanese residents appeared surprisingly calm in the aftermath.

Lebanon's turbulent history with “Israel” of ground invasions and pulverising air raids seemed far away.

"The attacks and counter-attacks were 'messages' and the issue is over now. I don't think that “Israel” is now interested in getting into another long-term military adventure in Lebanon," said Georges Lahoud, 31.

Lahoud is well placed to know about armed conflicts in the region. He lives in the Christian town of Marjayoun, the main locality in the border zone occupied by “Israel” for 22 years until its troop pullout in May 2000.

"It is true, as “Israeli” leaders said yesterday, that these are new rules for the game. During the occupation, we were constantly awaiting the next round of “Israeli” shelling. Now they responded with a very tactical hit," said Ahmad, a resident of Shaqra located just outside the former occupied zone.

But while this usually sleepy southern region was even more quiet during the long Easter weekend, the Lebanese and Syrian army troops, as well as Hizbullah guerrillas, remained on a state of high alert.

"It is all an issue of messages being sent, and this one was for Syria, more than for Lebanon, this is why the general situation is rather normal," said housewife Samia Ayoub, referring to Syria's status as Lebanon's chief powerbroker.

"This is why people are generally not scared, although there is always a fear that “Israel” would want to escalate the situation," she added.

Jiryes Nohra, sitting behind the counter of his pharmacy in the town of Qlaiaa, said "the “Israeli” direct strike against a Syrian target was meant by “Israeli” Prime Minister Ariel Sharon to defuse internal tensions inside “Israel” over the Intifada, or Palestinian uprising."

"We, in the south, are not concerned and we are not scared, because “Israel” is very much worried about Hizbullah's presence on the border, a few yards away from villages in northern “Israel”," said Nohra, 38.

"“Israel” had already been worried about the resistance guerrillas during the occupation when they were relatively far away, so we can imagine how it is now when “Israeli” towns are just a stone's throw away from the arms of the resistance," he said.

Kamal Khreis, 40, a baker from the nearby village of Khiam, said "we are not scared from “Israel” and not from its reactions because it has recently suffered more dangerous blows and it did not respond."

"“Israel” is now scared of the resistance guerrillas and I do not think that “Israel” would dare bombard civilian regions in southern Lebanon because it knows that the resistance's response will be harsh."

But if the Lebanese have their views about Hizbullah, Syria's continuing 35,000-strong military presence in the country and the latest “Israeli” attack, all breathed a sigh of relief when they woke up to see that, this time at least, the country's power grid was spared.

"The “Israelis””, when they couldn't hit back at Hizbullah, they used to unleash their anger at our power stations," said a woman named Fatima, sitting behind the counter of her candy store.

"At least, this time our summer is saved," she said, handing over ice cream cones to a group of young girls.

April Friday 20-2001:

Resistance: “Israel” will pay for attack
South commander warns that “Israeli” PM ‘miscalculated’ with radar strike…(1)

Hizbullah’s southern commander suggested Thursday that “Israel’s” air strike against a Syrian military radar base earlier this week had not been unexpected and warned that the organization would hit back in a “specific way.”
Sheikh Nabil Qaouk accused “Israeli” Premier Ariel Sharon of “pushing the region toward a large-scale war for which all the Arab countries should prepare.”
Qaouk said that Hizbullah had been studying Sharon closely since the former “Israeli” general took power in February.
“We knew he was a very careless man and does not calculate well.
“We were ready for all possibilities and probabilities … (The air raid) did not go beyond what we had calculated Sharon capable of,” Qaouk told The Daily Star in an interview.
“Israel’s” air raid against what was the largest Syrian Army radar installation on Lebanese soil has raised the stakes in the tit-for-tat exchanges between Hizbullah and the “Israeli” Army in the Shibaa Farms.
“Israel” appears to have set a new precedent by which Syrian military positions will be targeted in response to Hizbullah attacks in the Shibaa Farms or elsewhere along the border.
Faced with this prospect, will Syria pressure Hizbullah to curtail its military operations?
“This has never happened in any former period and will not happen in the future,” Qaouk said. “Syria fully supports our right to resist.”
The last time a Syrian position of any importance was attacked was during “Israel’s” invasion of 1982 when “Israeli” warplanes destroyed Syria’s anti-aircraft missile system in the Bekaa. The only deliberate attack against Syrian troops since then was on the second day of the Grapes of Wrath operation in April 1996, when “Israeli” helicopter gunships fired missiles at a Syrian anti-aircraft position near Beirut Airport.
Could Damascus really stand back and allow “Israel” to destroy its military positions so that Hizbullah could continue attacking “Israeli” troops?
“Sharon and his government will discover that Syria is able to resist and maintain steadfastness,” Qaouk said. “Hizbullah is determined not to allow the “Israelis” to impose any equation. Sharon committed a very stupid action. Syria has said through the comments of President Bashar Assad that it will defend itself and it will not be long before the “Israelis” discover they have created their own tragedy.”
Qaouk argued that the air raid had backfired on Sharon because it had strengthened Syria’s resolve and rallied the Arab world against “Israel”.
“The “Israeli” people will soon discover that they have put their faith in a very stupid man,” he said.
The deadly anti-tank missile attack on Saturday in the Shibaa Farms which killed an “Israeli” soldier and prompted the retaliatory air raid was indirectly criticized by Prime Minister Rafik Hariri through an editorial in his Al-Mustaqbal newspaper. The editorial ­ which questioned the timing of the operation ­ underlined the continuing rift between the premier and Hizbullah.
Qaouk conceded that Hariri “had opinions” on the wisdom of Hizbullah’s operations before the “Israeli” air strike, but after the raid “the issue is different.”
“He has trust in our operations,” he said, “and all Lebanese have the same opinion in Hizbullah’s ability.”
Should we expect another unfavorable editorial in Al-Mustaqbal if Hizbullah was to stage a further operation?
“Wait and see,” Qaouk replied.
Hizbullah appears determined to proceed with its hit-and-run operations against the “Israeli” Army in the Shibaa Farms, regardless of whether Hariri or the Lebanese in general approve.
Qaouk said that Hizbullah has “decided to make the “Israelis” very sorry for what they did (by attacking the radar base).”
“Our (military) program was not affected by the “Israeli” raid … Saturday’s operation was definitely not the last. We will deal with this raid in a very specific manner,” he warned without elaborating.
He would not be drawn into giving a time-frame for any response to the air raid, but his comments suggested Hizbullah will continue to confine its actions to military targets in the Shibaa Farms rather than expand the theater of conflict elsewhere along the border.

 

Lebanon rejects “Israeli” offer in return for constant Syrian presence
Lahoud: Resistance will continue…(2)

President Emile Lahoud said Thursday “Israel” should know “clearly and once and for all” that it would not achieve security by using force and disclosed “secret” offers to barter protection for “Israel” in exchange for a permanent Syrian presence in Lebanon.
Speaking at the opening of a weekly Cabinet session, Lahoud dwelt on recent developments, especially “Israel’s” air strike against a Syrian radar installation in the Bekaa on Monday.
“All this will not prevent us from adhering to our firm position based on doing away with “Israeli” occupation of territory, a return of Palestinian refugees to their homes and releasing prisoners held in “Israeli” jails,” he said.
“Without all of these rights and without resorting to the principles of a comprehensive and just peace in the region, “Israel” should know clearly and once and for all that it will not achieve security by force, regardless of its aggression and escalation.”
The president said “Israel” would be held responsible for its “aggression and escalation in occupied territory as well as for its attempts to introduce new formulas which will be rejected by us, through resistance and diplomatic and other legitimate means.”
Lahoud said that more than two months ago, both Lebanon and Syria had rejected offers through diplomatic channels proposing a quid pro quo ­ “a permanent Syrian presence in Lebanon in exchange for permanent Lebanese and Syrian protection for the security of northern “Israel”.
“We rejected these offers and said at the time that such a presence is linked to the strategy of confrontation, not with an exchange between “Israel’s” security and Syria remaining here.
“We say the same thing about an offer made openly by “Israeli” Foreign Minister Shimon Peres two days ago and affirm that the resistance is continuing and the exchange is rejected,” the president said.
““Israel’s” interior will not be safe if our own interior is subject to any aggression,” he warned.
In turn, Premier Rafik Hariri said that since Ariel Sharon came to power this year, “Israel” was following a “suicidal policy that would not lead to a solution.”
He added that the “Israeli” air strike Monday had been “denounced by everyone” and that “Israel” was trying to escalate tension and violence in the region.
Such a policy will have “negative effects” on “Israel”, he said.
Hariri reiterated the government’s support of the resistance as long as “Israel” occupied Lebanese territory and committed “aggression” against it.
The session was preceded by a separate meeting grouping Lahoud and Hariri lasting about 30 minutes.
Ministerial sources said Economy and Trade Minister Basil Fuleihan told the Cabinet that he was notified during a visit to Tehran earlier this week of Iran’s approval to place a deposit with the Lebanese Central Bank, earlier requested by Hariri.
No amount was set, but some ministers felt the figure would be “a considerable one.”
It also approved a German government grant to help remove mines from the South.

April Saturday 21-2001:

 

Qassem pledges resistance will continue until Shibaa is freed…(1)

Hizbullah’s deputy secretary-general said Friday that the campaign to evict the “Israeli” Army from the Shibaa Farms would continue regardless of political considerations.
“We will strive to liberate the Shibaa Farms without taking into consideration the political situation or regional, local and international considerations,” Sheikh Naim Qassem said. “We will not succumb to political blackmail whether from the United States, “Israel” or the United Nations.”
But Qassem added that Hizbullah’s operations would not be the cause of a deterioration in the region, blaming instead the actions of the United States and “Israel”.
“The “Israeli”-American decision in the region can deteriorate or calm the situation,” he said, adding that everybody would support the resistance.
Qassem said that Lebanon’s economy was ailing because of its political positions and not because of the resistance.
He dismissed the United Nations-delineated Blue Line as merely a “creation” by the UN for “Israel’s” benefit.
This would allow “Israel” to keep the Shibaa Farms under its occupation to “pressure Lebanon to take what it (“Israel”) couldn’t take when it pulled out defeated,” said Qassem.
Meanwhile, Agriculture Minister Ali Abdullah said that the “Israeli” attack on the Syrian radar position in response to Hizbullah’s operation in the Shibaa Farms was a cycle which could not be imposed on the Lebanese.
“Resistance is the result of the occupation, it will continue until all the land is liberated ... the Lebanese and Syrian governments have asserted that the Shibaa Farms are Lebanese,” Abdullah said in Amal’s office in Hermel.
The minister said the
debate on the Syrian presence had stopped “after everybody was assured that such a presence was an insurance for Lebanon and was costing Syria a lot of martyrs.”
He said the issue should be discussed by the governments of both countries.
Also on Friday, Higher Shiite Council vice-president Abdel-Amir Qabalan urged the Lebanese to unify ranks and resort to dialogue to prevent “Israel” from taking advantage of their disagreements.
“Lebanese should not allow the “Israeli” enemy to profit from their disputes and debates about national or political issues,” said Qabalan during Friday prayers. “They should always resort to dialogue as the right way to reach an internal consensus.”
He said “Israel’s” attacks on Lebanon, Syria and Palestine would not achieve their goals and that the Jewish state would be unable to separate the Lebanese-Syrian track.
He also called on the Arabs to fully support Lebanon and Syria by boycotting commercial and diplomatic ties with “Israel” and pressuring the international community to force it to comply with Security Council resolutions.

April Monday 23-2001:

 

Tehran calls for more attacks by resistance
Imam Khamenei hails Hizbullah’s outstanding role

Iran’s supreme leader claimed over the weekend that Israel has been weakened, urging Hizbullah to continue attacking the Jewish state’s troops in the Shebaa Farms.
“Hizbullah, which has a heavy responsibility, must take the initiative against the Zionist enemy,” state-run radio quoted Ayatollah Ali Khamenei as saying.
Hizbullah killed an Israeli soldier on April 14 in a missile attack, the third to die in the Shebaa Farms since the party resumed military operations against Israel last October.
 Khamenei saluted the dead Syrian soldier as a “martyr of the intifada,” the nearly seven-month-long Palestinian uprising against Israel.
Iran’s spiritual leader paid tribute to Syria for “its resistance faced with the Zionist threats.”
Hizbullah should also strengthen cooperation with other Lebanese groups “to stand against the plots of the Zionist regime,” Khamenei said.
He described the intifada as “a direct result of the devoted efforts of Hizbullah and is proof of the awakening of Palestinian Muslims.”
Nasrallah and other Hizbullah officials are in Iran to take part in a conference Tuesday and Wednesday on the intifada….(1)

 On the other hand, Iran will host a two-day major  international conference from  Tuesday to support the Palestinian  intifadah against the Israeli enemy.

Hezbollah Secretary General Sayyed  Hassan Nasrallah along with other  Hezbollah officials will attend the conference. Some 35  Arab and Islamic nations, as well as all  Palestinian opposition groups are expected  to take part in the conference, which  was entitled “The International  Conference of Support for the  Intifadah and the Islamic Revolution in  Palestine”…(2)


Hizbullah MP vows to extract steep price if Israel launches new attacks…(3)

Baalbek-Hermel MP Hussein Hajj Hassan said Sunday that Hizbullah will attack Israeli settlements in Northern Palestine if the Jewish state continues to target Lebanese infrastructure.
“The resistance is ready to turn the Israeli settlers’ lives in northern Palestine into hell,” said Hajj Hassan, who said Hizbullah, not the Israelis, would “choose the time of the attack.”
The Hizbullah MP made the comments while attending a UNESCO Palace ceremony marking the 23rd year of the detention of Samir Qantar.
Qantar, the longest-serving detainee still in Israel, was given a 542-year sentence in 1979 after being convicted of killing five Israelis in the town of Naharya.
More than 400 attended the ceremony, which was organized by the Follow-Up Committee for the Support of the Lebanese Detainees in Israeli Prisons.
“We are equipped with the right weapons,” said Hajj Hassan, vowing that the resistance would liberate the Shebaa farms.
Hajj Hassan also praised the Palestinian resistance, especially Sunday morning’s suicide (martyrdom) bombing which killed one person and wounded dozens in Tel Aviv.
He also warned US President George W. Bush to be wary of the resistance’s capabilities.
“We say to President Bush ­ you should read and analyze the resistance well. We are neither weak nor afraid.
“Sharon should also understand that resistance will struggle for the return of the Palestinian refugees in Lebanon,” he continued, pointing out that “Sharon is afraid of their return.”
To relatives of detainees, Hajj Hassan said: “I can assure you, it won’t involve too much time before you see your detainees arriving ... (home) because the rules of the game have changed.
“In the past, liberating our detainees was a tenuous dem­and,” he added, “but today it has become an attainable possibility since our enemy has detainees, as we do.”
He also admonished authorities here for poor treatment of ex-detainees, saying Israeli prisons “would be better than living with unfulfilled promises.”
Aley MP Akram Chehayeb echoed the call, arguing that the state should treat the liberated detainees as heroes.
“The state should take good care of them,” he said, “so they don’t feel they were first imprisoned by occupation and then by their own country’s negligence.”
In a letter to his brother, Qantar said the detainees issue should not be separated from peace negotiations, saying the two were linked by “necessity.” The letter also urged all Arabs to defend the Palestinian intifada.
“We have to support the Palestinians ... and defend Palestinian refugees’ human rights in Lebanon,” it said.
Social Affairs Minister Asaad Diab, who spoke on behalf of President Emile Lahoud, said Israel did not respect international agreements and violated them at will, accusing the international community of failing to “distinguish between an attacker and its victims.”
Diab also promised to continue working to free both the detainees and all remaining Lebanese territory.
Mohammed Safa, the head of the follow-up committee, said he hoped that UN Resolution 425 contained provisions for the release of all detainees, rather than leave the issue up to international “promises.”
Safa spoke of the families of three Israeli soldiers captured by Hizbullah last fall, saying they are traveling the world in an attempt to enlist support for their cause, asking Lebanon, Syria and Iran to allow International Committee of the Red Cross delegates to visit their relatives.
“I ask the government to resist this campaign,” said Safa, urging the authorities to create their own campaign to seek the release of Lebanon’s 19 detainees.

 

Damascus supports the resistance against Isreali enemy..(4)

 Syria’s  Baath Party said Saturday that Damascus continued to support Hizbullah’s operations.
Syria “stands by the Lebanese national resistance to liberate the occupied Shebaa Farms and free the detainees in Israeli jails,” the official Syrian Arab News Agency (SANA) quoted  Suleiman Qaddah, the party’s assistant regional secretary, as saying.
Qaddah’s statement came in the wake of a request from Washington for Syria to use its influence on Hizbullah to restore calm in Lebanon.
Qaddah, who was speaking at the opening of a conference on information technology and economics, also renewed Syria’s support for the Palestinian uprising and called on the “international community and United Nations bodies to check Israel’s bloody drive.”
He said that “the escalation of Israel’s aggression” would do nothing “but increase the Arabs’ determination to stick to their rights, make more sacrifices and give more martyrs.”
Syrian President Bashar Assad was quoted Thursday by SANA as telling his US counterpart, George W. Bush, that “calls for restraint have become useless, as it is necessary to diagnose the problem,” meaning Israel’s occupation of Arab lands, “to find effective solutions.”

April Wednesday 25-2001:

 

Nasrallah puts “Israel” on notice…(1)

Hizbullah’s secretary-general warned “Israel” on Tuesday to expect attacks from Palestinians, a declaration that won applause at a conference in Tehran.
“The fully armed Zionist military should wait for surprise attacks by Palestinian resistance groups,” Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah told an audience that included Iran’s top leaders as well as Khaled Meshaal, a leader of the Hamas movement, and Abdullah Ramadan Shalah, the head of Palestinian Islamic Jihad.
“Victory is ours. I confirm to you that the resistance in Lebanon will continue its operations,” Nasrallah added, asserting that Hizbullah would not submit to pressure.
He said his group would prove that Palestinians were not alone in their fight against “Israel”. “I say to the Zionists ­ and I mean it ­ wait for us, we’ll come to you from everywhere,” he warned.
Parliamentary leaders from over 30 Islamic countries are attending the two-day conference Iran is hosting on the intifada. Iran’s supreme leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, opened the conference Tuesday by declaring that combat, not dialogue, was the language understood by “Israel”.
Khamenei called on the rest of the Muslim world to support the Palestinians, even if that meant defying the United States and the West.
“Supporting the Palestinian people is one of our important Islamic duties,” Khamenei said.
Also in attendance was Speaker Nabih Berri, who cautioned that “Israel” was still determined to occupy parts of Lebanon, notably the South.
““Israel” is working hard to re-enter parts of the South that it left after 22 years of occupation,” he said. ““Israel” has launched a substantial provocation campaign aimed at blaming the Lebanese for any attacks  launched on their territory.”
“Israel” claims Lebanese resistance forces have received a fresh batch of long-range rockets from Tehran and other weapons aimed at undermining the security of northern “Israel”.
“The recent air raids against Dahr al-Baidar weren’t a reply to the Lebanese resistance activities,” the speaker argued, “but were aimed at intimidating both Lebanon and Syria.”
Berri added that “the resistance is a consequence of the “Israeli” occupation of our land. Hence, this resistance will remain as long as the occupation continues. The resistance forces have already launched attacks against the Shibaa Farms and will continue to do so.
““Israel” also aimed, during its latest air raid on April 16, to cut the supply route for the Lebanese resistance from Syria,” he said, “and prevent further attempts to normalize relations between Damascus and the Palestine Liberation Organization.”
He stated that “the raids were also aimed to achieve another “Israeli” objective ­ halting the Syrian peace initiative in Lebanon after it successfully stopped fratricidal bloodshed in our country. “Israel” won’t end its attacks on Lebanon until it succeeds in breaking Lebanon’s solidarity with Syria and launching an international diplomatic campaign to force the Beirut government to deploy the Lebanese Army to the southern border.”
He predicted that “Israel’s” next objective was to “disarm the resistance forces as a prelude to making south Lebanon a free-for-all once again.”
Berri and Nasrallah were chosen to be part of the conference’s presiding committee, along with five other members.
Former Prime Minister Salim Hoss told the conference that supporting the intifada was “an historic responsibility,” adding that the uprising’s failure would be a “disaster” similar to that of “Israel’s” 1948 creation.

April Thursday 26-2001:

 

Iranian FM praises Hizbullah's role in anti-"Israel" battles…(1)

" Iranian Foreign Minister Kamal Kharrazi here Thursday highlighted the Lebanese Hizbullah's instrumental role in driving "Israeli" troops from southern Lebanon after an 18-year occupation, and said it ushered in a new atmosphere for regional combats.                                                             

    "The Lebanese victory in southern Lebanon has made the regional states more determined to obtain legitimate rights (long) denied them," Kharrazi told the visiting Lebanese Hizbullah leader Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah.                                                    

    "Iran has always supported the brave nation of Palestine and is in favor of driving the occupiers from all occupied territories," he noted.                                                               

    For his part, Nasrallah said that the recently-concluded Tehran conference to support the seven-month-old Palestinian Intifada (uprising) would further encourage the Muslim nations to support Palestinians in their anti-"Israeli" combats.                          

    "The Zionist regime is trying to sow terror in the region to achieve its own goals," he said, and "to this end, it is seeking to tarnish the real image of regional combats (against "Israel")."        

    "But," he noted, "the Lebanese nation has found the key to victory against the occupiers after driving "Israeli" troops from southern Lebanon."                                                   

    On Wednesday, he told the Tehran conference in support of the Intifada, "I tell you...we in Hizbullah and the Islamic resistance in Lebanon will not stand idly by...We will prove to them (Zionists) that the Palestinians are not alone in this battle."                  

    The two-day conference--attended by the Palestinian group Hamas, Hizbullah of Lebanon and delegations from 30 Arab and Muslim states--

was aimed to help attract financial and other support for the Palestinian cause and to give a fresh impetus to its policy of "no compromise with "Israel"."                                             

    Iran has repeatedly called for the destruction of "Israel" as the only solution to the conflict in the Middle East.                    

    At least 387 Palestinians, 13 Arabs and 74 other "Israelis" have been killed in what is now known as the second Intifada against "Israeli" occupation which began in late September.
