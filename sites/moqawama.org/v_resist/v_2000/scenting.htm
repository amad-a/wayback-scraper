Hizbollahville is scenting victory

By Robert Fisk in Jibsheit, southern Lebanon

18 May 2000

"If Hizbollahville exists, it is Jibsheit. Its "martyrs cemetery" holds the bodies of 72 fighters, including that of Sheikh Ragheb Harb, the village imam hated by the "Israelis" and shot in the back in 1985, at the age of 32. When "Israel" bombarded Jibsheit in 1993, a helicopter fired a missile straight into his grave.

On the other side of the stubborn hill on which Jibsheit stands, is the home of another priest much loathed by "Israel": Sheikh Abdel-Karim Obeid, kidnapped by "Israeli" occupation troops in 1989 and now languishing as a hostage  against all international law  in "Israel".

And when "Israel" withdraws from southern Lebanon, there are few here who believe that peace will bless the streets of Jibsheit. Celebrations there may be; the Hizbullah are promising victory flags atop "Israel's" abandoned artillery batteries and a few captured pro-"Israeli" militia tanks to show to the people of southern Lebanon. But as the late Sheikh Ragheb Harb's brother Ismail says, Jibsheit is a special place.

"The "Israelis" despise us specifically because of our fighters and our martyrs," he says. "We were the first to hurt them so it is natural that they will seek revenge. There could be intelligence and covert operations: the history of Zionism shows us that "Israelis" use assassinations, the killing of civilians with explosives.

"But for the rest of Lebanon, I think there will be some tranquillity after a total "Israeli" withdrawal because the resistance that has defeated the "Israelis" will remain in place here.

"The "Israelis" will have concerns for the safety of their settlements and their civilians [in northern "Israel"] and will not want them harmed."

Talking to the resistance organisation in Jibsheit, it's obvious that it scents victory. I've never seen so many Hizbullah men joking and smiling, promising that Christian villages in the occupied zone will be protected and Obeid's son Sajed, 18 years old and a student at the Lebanese University in Beirut, believes he will see his father very soon. "We have spent 11 years waiting to see him and hoping every day that we would see the sheikh. Now it doesn't matter if it's before or after the withdrawal."

In fact, there is no agreement to allow Obeid his freedom. Indeed there is no agreement by the Arabs for an "Israeli" withdrawal. Obeid is a hostage, held for the release of a captured "Israeli" airman, Ron Arad. Why should the "Israelis" let him go now?

Sajed Obeid is plastering his website with the story of his father's abduction and imprisonment in "Israel". The website, www.geocities.com/sajedz, is his little resistance cell on the internet, a technology far removed from the old winding roads around the hill villages of southern Lebanon  and from the dire warnings that the Hizbullah issues daily against "Israel's" proxy militia, led by Antoine Lahd. "They have now been given one last chance by Hizbullah," Sajed Obeid says. "They must kill an "Israeli" or a Lahd officer  but this is the last chance. What they do after that is their concern."

Privately some Hizbullah members say they are worried that car bombs may explode in the aftermath of "Israel's" withdrawal in an attempt to provoke the Hizbullah into a war across the border. "Israel" might be blamed for this even if they were not responsible. Syria has already insisted that 18 farms around the village of Chebaa - farmland which "Israel" refuses to consider part of its occupation area  are in Lebanon. So does the war really end if Lebanon and Syria claim "Israel" has not withdrawn from all Lebanese territory?

And does hatred end? A conversation with Sheikh Ismail Harb produces some chilling remarks; quotations from that ghastly old Tsarist forgery, The Protocols of the Elders of Zion, and blatant racist material about the "horrific nature of the Jews" and "their desire to sacrifice children's blood". When I challenge the sheikh and tell him that this is nonsense, he merely repeats the same terrible statements over and over.

Several Hizbullah men who caught my eye were clearlydisturbed. But Sheikh Harb is the imam of Jibsheit. And who in Jibsheit dare challenge an imam on the eve of victory?

Independent, 18-5-2000.
