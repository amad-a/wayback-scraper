Hezbollah -- a perennial thorn in "Israels" side
 

 The Lebanese Hezbollah resistance movement, whose latest attacks forced Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu Friday to cut short a European tour, has defied repeated Israeli efforts to crush it since its birth in the aftermath of Israels 1982 full-scale invasion.

Bloody Lebanese campaigns by successive Israeli governments of both right and left have failed to dent the militia's ability to mount a sustained guerrilla war against "Israels" 20-year occupation of south Lebanon.

In July 1993 a seven-day Israeli onslaught left 132 Lebanese dead, most of them civilians, without diminishing Hezbollah's military effectiveness. In April 1996 the then Labor government of prime minister Shimon Peres launched another, 17-day offensive in the middle of an election campaign, killing 175 civilians.

In the end, as international condemnation mounted, the Israeli government was forced to agree to an internationally monitored understanding with its bitter foe to avoid further civilian casualties. But Hezbollah attacks against Israeli military targets in the 850 square kilometer (340 square mile) border strip occupied by the Jewish state continued to multiply.

In 1997, the Israeli army sustained a record 39 soldiers dead, not counting a further 73 soldiers killed in a nocturnal collision between two helicopters heading for south Lebanon.

Including the attacks Wednesday and Thursday in which four more Israeli soldiers were killed, a total of 23 have been killed so far this year.

Even Israeli officers grudgingly acknowledge the increasing effectiveness of the militia's war of attrition. Its tactics have become more complex over the years, ranging from suicide attacks and a wave assaults to sophisticated radio controlled bombs.

Hezbollah fighters have even taken video footage of some of their more spectacular raids against Israeli positions denting the Jewish state's image of invincibility.

Hezbollah now has some 3,000 full-time fighters at its disposal with a further 4,000 held in reserve. Led by Sheikh Hassan Nasrallah since the death of his predecessor Abbas Mussawi in an Israeli air raid in 1992, the group is now firmly implanted in Lebanon with nine MPs in parliament and its own TV and radio stations.
