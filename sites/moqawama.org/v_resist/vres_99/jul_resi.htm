  July Friday 2 -1999:

HIZBULLAH VOWS TO CONTINUE RESISTANCE EVEN IF PEACE TALKS RESUME(1)
" The Lebanese resistance movement Hizbullah has warned there will be no let-up in its armed campaign to oust "Israel" from Lebanese territory even if there is a revival of long-stalled peace talks.

"Resistance operations will continue until there is a full "Israeli" withdrawal from south Lebanon," said the Hizbullah official for south Lebanon, Nabil Qauq. "There will be no guarantees (of a halt in operations) during negotiations," he said in an interview with Magazine, a French-language weekly due to appear on Friday. Qauq said there were US efforts to relaunch peace talks between Lebanon and "Israel" that have been frozen since 1994. "But no matter what the result of these efforts, that does not change the reality of the resistance. Hizbullah will force "Israel" to pull out in disarray, under fire, to ensure it does not think of carrying out other attack," he said. Quaq said he was "skeptical" about the intentions of incoming "Israeli" prime minister Ehud Barak, suggesting his current stance was different from the one he adopted during the campaign for the May elections. Barak, a former army chief of staff, has pledged to withdraw "Israeli" troops from Lebanon within a year and resume negotiations with Syria. But Qauq said he believed there would not be a full "Israeli" withdrawal "but a redeployment with the aim of reducing losses." Hizbullah leads the armed campaign to oust "Israeli" troops from Lebanon".

Obeid and his bodyguard were abducted by Israeli commandos from Jibsheet, near Nabatieh, in July 1989. The Israelis captured them to gather information on the fate of Ron Arad, an Israeli navigator whose plane was shot down in 1986 during an air raid.

Hizbullah says high casualty rate will force Israelis out ... (1)
Hizbullahs secretary-general said on Monday that the resistance intends to inflict a high number of casualties among the occupation forces to bolster the Lebanese-Syrian negotiating stance in the imminent peace talks with Israel.

Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah vowed that this policy was irrevocable.

The Hizbullah leader said that a partial Israeli withdrawal from the occupation zone was possible before the end of 1999 and certain within a year, regardless of progress in the peace process.

Our decision is to carry on with the resistance and to inflict the largest amount of casualties in the enemys ranks because we think that this continuous pressure will eventually lead to a withdrawal from the south and the western Bekaa, Nasrallah said during an interview with Melhem Karam, the president of the Journalists Union.

Nasrallah said that Syria and Lebanon have been subjected to very large military and political pressure to stop the resistance operations.

What the Israeli forces were unable to do with their large arsenal, the U.S. administration is trying to do politically today through political pressure and peace promises and through the activation of peace negotiations, he said.

The Hizbullah leader insisted that resistance could not stop simply on the promise of a resumption of negotiations. He said that Barak was bringing the implicit threat of millions of dollars of U.S.-supplied weapons to the negotiating table. The resistance, Nasrallah argued, sought to provide a balance.

Lebanon and Syria, the Arab side, are badly in need of the resistance because they have to gather all the power and pressuring cards they have, he said.

Nasrallah attributed Hizbullahs reticence on stating its intentions toward Israel following a troop withdrawal from the

south as a negotiating tactic to benefit Lebanon and Syria.

We think that remaining silent and not talking in detail about our role following the settlement gives Lebanon and Syria strength as they continue on the tracks they believe in, he said.

Asked whether he expected partial Israeli withdrawals from the zone, Nasrallah noted that the Israeli Army is examining the possibility of pulling out of its two positions north of the Litani river, at Aishiye and Rihan.

I believe that within a year, regardless of the negotiations, whether they fail or succeed, there will be partial withdrawals to reduce the size of the occupation zone A partial withdrawal in the zone is possible before the end of the year, he said.

Responding to speculation that the resistance has decreased its operations in reaction to the peace overtures between Syria and Israel, Nasrallah said that any reduction in activity is in response to changes by the Israelis in the zone.

If (recent resistance activity) has been lukewarm, this does not have any political significance and must not be interpreted politically. It must be interpreted through the factors on the ground, he said.

Israeli forces occasionally change their dispositions in the zone which requires studying and appraisal by Hizbullahs fighters before they can recommence operations, he said.

Our brothers sometimes go into the occupied zone and wait for days, (but) their movements inside the zone have become subjected to strict measures (recently). There is a review (by Hizbullah) of the Israeli tactics which effects (the number of) casualties (inflicted by the resistance) or targeting them as easily as it used to. But regarding the resistances actions, initiative, vitality and presence, they are not effected, he said.

Nasrallah was confident that an end to the occupation was imminent. If the Israelis get out of the south unilaterally we will consider it a victory, he said.

HIZBULLAH VOWS NEVER TO RECOGNIZE ISRAEL  (2)
Hizbullah has vowed never to recognize Israel. The group also dismissed reports that Syria has put pressure on them to halt resistance operations.

At a ceremony commemorating the tenth anniversary of Israel's capture of Hizbullah leader Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid at the Unesco Palace in Beirut yesterday. Hizbullahs Secretary-General Sayyid Hassan Nassrallah pledged at the ceremony, which was attended by Prime Minister Salim Hoss and top Lebanese officials, never to recognize Israel which he described as a quote cancer, racist and terrorist entity with plans to dominate the Middle East. Nassrallah said "even if the Syrian Golan and southern Lebanon are returned by Israel there will still be a great national and Islamic problem to resolve... the restitution of Palestine to its true owner... " And the return of Palestinian refugees. The Hizbullah Chief, said "American officials started putting pressure on Syria and Lebanon to stop resistance actions because (Israeli Prime Minister Ehud) Barak will refuse to negotiate" under such pressure. Nassrallah said that is totally unacceptable, adding Damascus and Beirut refuse Barak's demands as long as Israel's occupation continues. He explained that there is a risk that Barak will fail to keep his promise to withdraw from southern Lebanon if Hizbullah freezes its action. Hezbollah spearheads a resistance war to force Israel to end its 21-year occupation of southern Lebanon. So far this year, Israeli attacks have left 13 Lebanese civilians dead. Hizbullah resistance operations have also killed ten occupation troops in the south in the same period
