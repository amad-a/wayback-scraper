Views in the Resistance

  March Tuesday 2 -1999:

Let's just get the hell out (1)
"When the IDF* was stuck in Lebanon in 1982 as a non-victor and did not know how to extricate itself, our soldiers sang a macabre tune that began, "Descend from the sky, little plane, take us to Lebanon," and ended "And we will return in a coffin." The rest of the song is not important.The prime ministers who promised us that ""Israel" will enjoy peace for 40 years," "the defense zone will protect our northern communities," etc., are no longer with us. Our defense ministers have come and gone.

Even the enemy has changed. Palestinian "terrorists" have been replaced by the Hizbullah. The "peace treaty" with Lebanon has been replaced by a hair-raising, humiliating "memorandum of understanding" with the Hizbullah that allows them to kill our soldiers but limits the scope of our reactions."

Only one thing has not changed: "Israel" is still stuck in Lebanon - or, to be more precise, Lebanon is stuck in our throat, while our leadership has been suffering from collective paralysis on everything connected to the topic.

"This week, we will be asking the two-decades-old question: What more must happen, how many more soldiers must be killed, how many more families must join the "circle of bereavement," before our leaders can free themselves from their idee fixe on Lebanon and understand that our only option is to get the hell out? Common sense tells us, or should tell us, that we can never win the guerrilla war in South Lebanon."

"First, no army in the world has ever been able to defeat guerrilla fighters. Second, the Hizbullah knows the territory better than even our best soldiers and, as an army with a routine and set procedures, the IDF is more of an observable target than an observer; its actions are more predictable than the Hizbullah's. Third, the fact that the Hizbullah operates within a friendly population gives it an edge and limits our ability to react. Fourth, the South Lebanon Army** has degenerated from an army on our payroll charged with the task of doing our dirty work to a broken-down military machine that is useless without our protection and presence".

"Fifth, our physical presence on Lebanese soil has transformed the Hizbullah in the world's eyes from a terrorist organization to legitimate freedom fighters and has thereby freed Lebanon - and, chiefly, Syria - from responsibility for the bloodshed. "Israel's" presence in South Lebanon is a godsend for Syria, enabling Assad - by means of a war conducted by a "messenger" - to pressure "Israel" to surrender the Golan Heights. Assad has rejected Rabin's and Netanyahu's attempts to initiate negotiations with Syria using a "Lebanon first" agenda. As our deterrent capacity diminishes and our weakness becomes more prominent, "Israel's" presence in Lebanon is playing into his hands".

"Lebanon is Syria's success story: a prosperous country in which law and order have been restored, which provides a livelihood for a million Syrians and which is an integral part of Syria's military superstructure.

"Assad is in no hurry to plunge into a direct military confrontation with "Israel". Right now, he has the best of both worlds: as he sheds our blood, he enjoys the protection of our status as an "occupier."

Assad gets up in the morning without a worry in the world and with the knowledge that his interests are being protected, while we find ourselves going from one funeral to the next. This is an illogical situation. Not only is our departure from Lebanon imperative, it will change the rules of the game that is serving Assad's interests".

"We did not ask for permission to invade Lebanon and we do not need it to leave. We do not need to make our departure conditional upon suitable security arrangements or upon the presence of American or French peacekeepers"

""Israel" must defend itself from within its own borders, like other sovereign countries, without paying a weekly price in blood and without stomping about in the quagmire known as the security zone. The moment we leave and others can no longer use the alibi that we are occupiers, responsibility for future developments will be placed in the hands of Lebanon - and chiefly Syria. The security zone died long ago; it exists only in our imagination."

In guerrilla warfare, small is great (14)
"Hizbullah yesterday refrained from retaliating with Katyushas on the Galilee after the IDF hit bases throughout Lebanon. But that shouldn't fool anyone about the inevitable continuation of the fighting in the "security zone". Hizbullah is avoiding a full-scale conflict with the IDF that could hurt Lebanese civilians - and by doing so, it is forcing the IDF to drop its plans for the "rolling operation" announced by Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz. The IDF operation rolled backward to a stop".

"Hizbullah is following the classic rules of guerrilla warfare: After hitting a strong regular army, guerrilla fighters should go into hiding, avoiding the army's anger and direct confrontation. The guerrillas may later return to the field, where they are much more successful. Thus, Hizbullah dictates the rules of the game in the fighting by focusing on guerrilla attacks in the "security zone" using land mines and ambushes"

"That's been its strategy from the start. After the Grapes of Wrath operation of 1996, and the written understandings reached between "Israel" and the Hizbullah, the guerrillas returned to operation very quickly, and within a short time managed to kill nine soldiers".

"From the IDF's point of view, the main difficulty is that it has neither a full answer to guerrilla warfare, nor even a reasonable one. The IDF cannot force a decisive showdown with the Hizbullah even if the guerrillas suffer heavy casualties. In such a situation, "Israel" can expect a repeat of this week's events."

"The targets struck this week by the air force were mostly destroyed, but it appears the Hizbullah suffered few casualties. Even if they suffered more casualties than they published, it's clear the Hizbullah will do all it can to avoid showing its wounded - and not only as a strategy of psychological warfare, which it emphasizes in its strategy. The top priority of the guerrilla group is not to reach a situation in which it is forced to respond by landing Katyushas on "Israel" because it's now doing everything possible to avoid direct confrontation and return to the "little fighting" of the "security zone".

The security leadership of "Israel" faced a problem yesterday. On the one hand they could tell the residents of the north to leave the shelters, but on the other hand it's clear to all that there will be new rounds of violence in the "security zone". And that's the way it will go until a better way to protect the northern border is found".

SYRIAN PRESS WARNS "ISRAEL" ITS STRONG-ARM TACTICS IN LEBANON WILL FAIL (1)
" Syrian newspapers hit out on Tuesday at "Israel's" "strong arm" policy of occupation in southern Lebanon, and warned the Jewish state against any attack on its northern neighbor. ""Israel" will be committing a serious new mistake if it attacks Lebanon ... It will achieve nothing, for Lebanon has always resisted all the previous savage attacks launched against it," wrote the ruling party newspaper Al-Baath. "Israeli" Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced Monday a "new policy" of
harsher reprisals for attacks by Lebanese guerrillas on "Israeli" forces in south Lebanon, following the killing of a top general and three other "Israelis" on Sunday. ""Israel" has been trapped ever since it occupied the border zone (of southern Lebanon) which has become a deadly quagmire destroying the Zionist theory of security based on expansion and occupation," the paper continued. "Israeli" policy in Lebanon has collapsed, said the government paper Tishrin, "since what "Israel" calls a security zone has become one of the least secure regions and a trap which is draining the energies of the "Israelis"." "Netanyahu's attempts to improve his position for the elections through military adventures will turn against him, as such adventures have already brought down three "Israeli" prime ministers before him," the paper said. "Last week's heroic actions in southern Lebanon deepened the crisis in "Israel" and showed that the policy of might and occupation will not solve the problems and that the only possible way is an immediate and unconditional withdrawal of Lebanon from southern Lebanon," said Tishrin.

Yes to unilateral withdrawal (2)                          By AVRAHAM SELA
"The vociferous public debate over Lebanon of the last two weeks has revealed two main developments. The first is the continuously descending threshold of "Israeli" society's
willingness to further tolerate the toll of continued military presence in south Lebanon. The second is the unmistakable awareness of the government that the growing public unrest can no longer be ignored".

"Indeed, the days when the IDF's* presence raised no questions, reflecting "consensus," are over. The deepening involvement of grass-roots groups in the debate and the ever-strengthening
voices in support of a unilateral withdrawal represent a widening consciousness of the undeniable failure of the "security zone" concept in terms of cost-benefit calculations.

"The public awakening came after years of denial and hibernation. It was the disaster of last year's helicopter tragedy, which claimed the lives of 73 soldiers, that gave birth to the "Four Mothers" organization. Similarly, the fall of 12 more in the abortive naval commando raid that September triggered the establishment of The Movement for Leaving Lebanon Peacefully, headed by MK Yossi Beilin. Yet for a society overwhelmed by issues of national security, peacemaking with the Palestinians, and economic depression, the attempts to pressure the decision makers "from below" remained ineffective".

"The recent toll of seven soldiers within two weeks brought the issue back to center stage, showing that despite long intervals between disasters, we have grown ever more impatient with the
high military and political echelons. We are less and less tolerant of the explanations, apologies, and promises for reassessment and more creative tactics, all of which proved to
be empty words".

"To put it bluntly, "Israeli" decision-makers in this government, like their predecessors, prefer to preserve the security zone concept regardless of its undeniable failure rather than taking the risk of
post-withdrawal attacks on "Israel" from south Lebanon for which they may be held responsible".
"No doubt, the preferable policy is to reach agreement with Lebanon that would ensure "Israel's" security interests before returning to the international border. In the absence of such a
possibility, however, what is the calculated risk of a unilateral withdrawal?"

"The question is no longer whether the IDF should pull out of Lebanon, but how to minimize the risks of the proposed alternatives. In other words, should "Israel" withdraw without full
proof guarantees, and in their absence, how would Syria and Hizbullah, the main actors on the other side, respond.

"The opponents of a unilateral withdrawal argue that Syria would continue to encourage guerrilla attacks on "Israel", because it would be the only way to bring its government to resume the
negotiations over the Golan Heights. In this effort, they claim, Hizbullah would play a leading role because of its own interest in eliminating "Israel" and liberating Jerusalem."

"These arguments ignore Syria's interest in maintaining Lebanon's stability, continued economic prosperity, and rehabilitation following 15 years of civil war. That these developments have been in force since the early 1990s was due to Syria's efforts since the mid-1970s, during which it has
paid dearly in material and human losses. Syria would have to consider carefully the damage that "Israel" could cause to Lebanon as a Syrian asset, should there be repeated attacks on
"Israel's" northern territory from Lebanon".

""Israel's" presence on Lebanese soil makes it easy, even legitimate for Syria to encourage guerrilla attacks on the IDF and the South Lebanese Army**. But given Syria's interest in
advancing a settlement on the Golan, it would have to consider the impact of such attacks on the US administration, whose involvement would be necessary for Syria in any case. And
finally, Syrian President Hafez Assad has shown impressive prudence regarding violation of "Israel's" sovereign territory, even when proxies are involved".

"As to Hizbullah, the picture of a mercenary group presented by the opponents of a unilateral withdrawal is far from accurate.
Hizbullah is deeply rooted in the social and political reality of Lebanon, competing for power and access to resources like any other political movement. That Iran has been its patron and
financier, and Syria is allegedly supporting it militarily or approving its activities, does not mean that these two countries own it".

"Unlike the Palestinian guerrilla organizations that dominated south Lebanon and were expelled by "Israel" in the war of 1982, Hizbullah's intentions have never been to liberate Palestine, but
to advance domestic social and political goals, in which the armed struggle against "Israel" has played a major role. Despite occasional announcements that it intends to fight "Israel" until
Jerusalem is liberated, Hizbullah leaders have also said the opposite - that their war is not with "Israel" as such and that they would cease fighting once it fully withdraws from Lebanon".

Another striking piece of evidence is that in over 15 years of fighting, Hizbullah's guerrillas have never attempted to cross the border into "Israel", even though they have repeatedly demonstrated the permeability of the security zone by physically reaching it".

"Finally, unilateral withdrawal is not meant to create a rosy situation in which peace prevails in the North. Given that the "security zone" is unable to prevent Katyusha rockets from hitting "Israel", our northern border can be defended from inside "Israeli" territory. Our continued presence in Lebanon is a liability more than an asset, and it is about time to bring to an end the anomaly of protecting one's own territory by occupying a neighbor's."

The writer is a senior lecturer in the Department of International Relations at the Hebrew University and a former member of the "Israeli" delegation to the Nakoura talks.

"Israel": Hizbollah sharpens up its tactics"(3)
 David Gardner, the Financial Times' Middle East Editor
"Intelligence is a commodity in which "Israel" has long believed, not without reason, that it had a ruthless edge sharp enough to outmanoeuvre all its adversaries. It has come as a real shock to learn that Hizbullah - the Lebanese Shi'ite Islamist movement long disdained by "Israelis" as ragamuffin suicide bombers from the slums of south Beirut - has developed an intelligence capacity which can outsmart "Israel's" finest".

"This was made brutally clear on Sunday when Hizbullah guerrillas ambushed a convoy ferrying Brigadier-General Erez Gerstein through the "security zone" "Israel" has occupied in south Lebanon since 1978.

"Gen Gerstein, in charge of liaison with the South Lebanon Army (SLA), "Israel's" mercenary force in south Lebanon, was the most senior officer killed on "Israel's" northern battle-front since the full-scale "Israeli" invasion of Lebanon in 1982.

"But Hizbullah's ability to identify and attack vital "Israeli" occupation targets has been evident since roughly October 1995. It was then that the "Israeli" Defence Forces** (IDF) began noticing a qualitative change in the Shi'ite movement's tactics - in particular its ability to anticipate the movements of senior "Israeli" intelligence officers and elite units.

"This was not because the guerrillas had acquired better weapons. Nor was there any change in the motivation of Hizbullah volunteers, who between 1982 and 1985 had forced "Israel's" invasion force to retreat from Beirut to the southern buffer under attrition spearheaded by suicide bombers. It was primarily because the "Party of God" had immeasurably improved its intelligence gathering.

It acquired this capacity not only because it was fighting on home soil - the treacherous mountains and ravines of Shi'ite southern Lebanon - but because it had penetrated the crumbling SLA.

"Israel" originally built this force out of a predominantly Christian faction of the Lebanese army which had disintegrated during the 1975-90 civil war. It then enticed local Shi'ite volunteers with substantial salaries in an occupation zone which offered almost no alternative employment.

But successive "Israeli" offensives after the retreat of 1985 failed to dent Hizbollah, which grew into a popular social movement and legitimate resistance organisation commanding support across the sectarian spectrum of Lebanon. "

"Along with military stalemate in Lebanon, the prospect of regional peace also eroded the morale of the SLA. Dim though this prospect now is, nobody in south Lebanon has forgotten the lynchings of "Israeli" collaborators that accompanied the IDF's retreat in 1985.

"As the SLA's ability to police the buffer diminished and its members turned to collaboration with Hizbollah, IDF units were thrust into the front line, and have been sustaining growing casualties.
From June 1985, when "Israel" finalised its withdrawal to the "security zone" to June 1996, the IDF lost 159 men in Lebanon; from mid-1996 to today it has lost 162."

"These bald figures, however, are inadequate to express the wounded pride of the legendary IDF at this rare defeat by Arab arms, which has led former senior officers to question the wisdom of "Israel's" involvement in "the Lebanese quagmire". Whereas in the past, "Israeli" commandos used to hit targets in Lebanon with ease - including Sheikh Abbas Musawi, the former Hizbollah chief they assassinated in 1992 - many of its recent ventures north of the "security zone" have been fiascos.

Inside the zone itself, the IDF is often pinned down in fixed positions, and no amount of air superiority can compensate for this weakness on the ground. "

"In this kind of conflict, many "Israelis" believe their vaunted regional power is that of a giant with feet of clay. This sentiment, along with popular disquiet at the growing casualties, is fuelling calls for a unilateral withdrawal from Lebanon."

"Eitan Haber, a former aide to Yitzhak Rabin, the late "Israeli" prime minister, summed up this sense of powerlessness in yesterday's Yediot Aharanot newspaper.

"We, whose arsenal is full of atom bombs, who extricated the hostages at Entebbe [airport], who snatched radar systems from under the noses of drowsy Egyptians, who killed terrorist chiefs in their beds [in Beirut] with soldiers dressed as women, and we - we have no solution?" he asked, concluding: "There is none. We have tried everything in Lebanon."

Time to go
A senior officer speaks frankly about the need for the army to get out of Lebanon...(1)
                                                                                                                    By Amos Harel
"The time has come to stop mincing words: We have no business staying in Lebanon. The army always has a ready excuse for why it's not the right time to discuss it. Either that it's too early - we haven't even buried the dead yet - or that we should wait until the investigation is complete, because it's still not clear what really happened up there. Then too much time passes by, and aside from the soldiers and their families, everyone loses interest in the situation. But in Lebanon, we're always between one incident and the next, between a terrorist raid and some other tragedy. So there's no choice but to talk about it now, while the soldiers are being killed."There is something symbolic about the death of Erez [Brigadier General Erez Gerstein, commander of the IDF's* liaison unit in Lebanon]. Reality has thrown us a tragic slap in the face. Of all people, Erez was a guy who was wholeheartedly in favor of staying in Lebanon and taking a tough stance, a fellow who always belittled the Hezbollah. He considered them third-rate terrorists. Erez would always say there was a military solution in Lebanon, that "we've gotta pull the finger out of our - and start ripping them to shreds." He was a very brave guy who always seemed as if nothing could happen to him. His death is a slap in the face to the military doctrine in Lebanon. It once again proves how the IDF has fallen victim to those same patterns of action that repeat themselves, in a sort of recurring ritual.
"There is also a symbolism about the way Erez was killed, after having paid a condolence call at Kafr Shab'a to the family of a fallen South Lebanese Army security officer. This in itself was the embodiment of the idea that we can rule over the security zone as if it were ours to rule over, and not as a military force that has seized the high ground in order to protect the valley below. This is what's happening to us: We act like colonial rulers, and then we're brought down to earth. This is the place to mention the advice that Charles de Gaulle gave the Americans about getting involved in Vietnam. Don't fall into the trap of being colonial occupiers, he told them, but they didn't know what he was
talking about. They had never considered themselves as such.

"Hezbollah isn't a terrorist organization. It's a guerrilla organization. It's an national liberation organization. Show me a single case in history in which an army managed to occupy territory over time and defeat a guerrilla organization that benefited from massive support and political backing from the local population. Even if a unilateral withdrawal is interpreted as weakness, that doesn't mean it ought to be ruled out. There are plenty of nations that have already done it: the Americans folded in Vietnam, and the Russians ran as fast as they could from Afghanistan. We can also get out. There are means of doing this gradually, without having to play games of honor and dignity. When he decided to pull out of Algeria, de Gaulle didn't try to sell his people any stories. Basically, he told the French: 'They deserve independence, and it's costing us too much.' And there it was a matter of tearing away at the flesh of France. Nobody in "Israel" considers South Lebanon to be an inseparable part of the Galilee. I don't think there's one sane Frenchman who is now sorry about withdrawing from Algeria.

"Again, allow me to refer you to the history books. General William Westmoreland (the commander of the American forces in Vietnam) was no idiot. He was a prisoner of the concept: Give me another 10,000 soldiers, and I'll beat the Vietcong. Afterward, when they gave him 8,000 soldiers, he argued - If you'd only sent me another 2,000, everything would have come out differently. Study his biography and you notice how he slowly begins to confuse the end (victory) with the means (dispatching more troops). In certain respects, this is also happening to the IDF, which has its own requests: Give us money for more means - weaponry and protection - so that we can defend our soldiers there.

"Have you been to South Lebanon recently? Have you seen what kind of outposts we've built there in the last year? We are sitting in these huge armored fortresses, which of course invite enemy shelling, and we make the convoys leading to them into easy targets. Little by little we're becoming Crusaders who primarily guard only ourselves. We are so stubborn about putting ourselves out there as bait, that it's frightening. What sort of successes can we be proud of? Discovering the explosive charges planted at the roadsides? But finding those charges is a defensive action; it doesn't cause any real damage to the other side.

"We don't have any way of winning in Lebanon, since such a goal does not exist. Our goal there is survival: protecting the residents of northern "Israel", and suffering as few IDF losses as possible. In Lebanon, the IDF has a sum total of two types of action. One is completely defensive - fortifications, convoy safety procedures, unearthing explosive roadside charges. The entire purpose of the other type of activity - taking the offensive - is to prevent terrorist raids in the security zone. Something along the lines of 'let's be waiting for them when they come.' At its foundation, this is also a form of defensive activity. It doesn't have any genuine, attainable objective.

"Killing Erez was a moral victory for Hezbollah, because this time it was close to home, very close to the highest echelons of the IDF and the Northern Command. In the army, we got the feeling that the Northern Command had stopped functioning for a moment, that things had gone out of control.

Erez's death was an immense emotional blow to them, partly because he really did radiate this sense of invulnerability. But there was another shocking event that took place in Lebanon this week - the demonstration by the residents of Beirut, who broke through the fence [constructed by the IDF] in the village of Arnoun. Either we still haven't figured out what happened there, or the Lebanese themselves haven't figured out what they achieved. Can you imagine what would happen if 10,000 residents came and simply marched up to the Beaufort? What would we do? Shoot them? For the Arabs, the Intifada was a victory over the "Israelis" brought about by sending out the masses to march and demonstrate. As soon as the Intifada is employed in the Lebanese context, we're going to have an even more difficult problem.

"The truth is that there isn't a hell of a lot more the IDF can do against the Hezbollah. We can drop tons of bombs on them, as we did in Operation Accountability and Operation Grapes of Wrath. The truth is there simply isn't any action we could take that would vanquish them, because there are no real targets to attack. I don't believe the government will do very much, beyond issuing proclamations and shows of force. You have to remember that the elections are at hand, and more dead soldiers will not bring Netanyahu any extra votes. I wasn't surprised that the Hezbollah did not react to the air force attack. They're not interested in any real escalation. The Grapes of Wrath understandings are comfortable for them, because on the one hand, they safeguard their civilian population, and on the other, allow them to continue striking at IDF soldiers.

"If there was parity between the populations, the IDF would be able to do more. But while the residents of the Galilee are not indifferent to katyushas, the Hezbollah has a population base that for the most part identifies with it, supports it and is ready to bear the brunt of the shelling and attacks.

Compared to the conflict with the Palestinian organizations in Lebanon, we have a huge problem here. Fatah had no roots in the population, so it had to build itself military strongholds, which were targets that the IDF could attack. But the Hezbollah feels at home in South Lebanon. Aside from a few security patrols of its own, it doesn't have any real targets that can be attacked.

"In my opinion, Hezbollah will not chase us into the Galilee if we leave the security zone and settle down along the international border. They are a liberation organization that has built itself on the goal of forcing the IDF out of South Lebanon. I doubt whether Jerusalem really interests them. It's rhetoric. Nor do I believe that Hezbollah would shoot at the bus at Avivim [a northern settlement; the speaker is referring to a PLO attack from Lebanese soil that occurred in the 1970s.] It doesn't serve their interests. Palestinian terror in the '70s had an objective - weakening the "Israeli" opposition - but what object would the Hezbollah have after the withdrawal? I have only one reservation: the mere fact of our prolonged stay in Lebanon sets into motion a process in which Hezbollah becomes so committed to hating "Israel" that in the end, the indoctrination of its fighters is liable to induce them to commit terrorist raids even after the withdrawal. Right now, this trend that can be forestalled by withdrawing from Lebanon.

"Regarding Syria, I'm in the minority. I have a hard time believing that Assad will still be able to control Hezbollah following an "Israeli" withdrawal. Hezbollah has its own agenda, its own very distinct social and religious background. I doubt whether Assad would be able to force it to continue its attacks following a withdrawal.

"There is only one real reason for staying, and that is our obligation to the SLA and the local populace. This is a troublesome problem, but I can't see how it can justify the continued bloodletting of our soldiers in Lebanon. We have an incomprehensible situation here: What would happen if every country took an extra 20 kilometers for itself to defend its border? Deep down, "Israel's" refusal to withdraw is a very strong defeatist statement - as if we are incapable of protecting our own settlements from the border.

"There is a real political problem here. The people of "Israel" are losing their patience and their ability to absorb more losses. Someone once calculated that the "Israelis" would be willing to accept 25 deaths a year in Lebanon. Now it turns out that the losses have to come in a certain scatter, and not include senior officers, in order for the public to be able to come to terms with "Israel's" continued stay in Lebanon.

"The political-diplomatic ranks are avoiding taking any decisions affecting the army, preferring that the army come up with the solutions first. The politicians are looking for excuses not to do anything. If the army says that we can't withdraw, then the politicians can seize on that as an excuse. Golda Meir used the same argument: 'My generals told me...'

"Lots of officers know that it's folly, but few of them say so out loud. Here and there, you hear it in internal discussions, but it's not easy to express your opinions when the leaders of the army feel differently. Few army officers would express any doubts about staying in Lebanon in front of a guy like Erez, who was so sure of himself. I'm always amazed by the immense gap between what is said at meetings and what some of the officers say afterward, when they head outside with the disposable coffee cups. It's only then that opinions are expressed a lot more openly. Lots of people who live and breathe Lebanon understand what's going on up there. Of course, there are others, who think otherwise. This is another cognitive dissonance. If you're already there, and doing the job with all that madness around you, you have to believe there is justification for the risks you are taking on.
"The saddest thing about the military mantras that officers are always offering up in interviews with the media is that many of them no longer believe them to be true. Army officers are expected to demonstrate determination and a winning facade, so that's what they do. So why don't you generally hear the other side? Because nearly every officer will be on his guard when talking with a journalist, even if it's off the record.

"Amnon [Lipkin-Shahak] was clearly revolted by these kinds of debates. He considered them a waste of time and energy. [Shaul] Mofaz is a very decent person. His recent statements - about how there are differences of opinion on the issue within the army - were made with the intention of sending up a message. I think that, in a very delicate way, he's hinting to the political echelons that they have to rethink everything that's happening in Lebanon. He's not going to come out and say 'we have to get out'; what he's doing is throwing the issue out there for the public to discuss it.

Associated Press on south residents' support to Resistance(3)

" Just days after the front porch of Muslim Fadel's house was shattered by shellfire from "Israeli"-allied militiamen, workers turned up on what was left of his doorstep, ready to fix the damage".

"These weren't just any handymen, though. They were from the "jihad al-bina," a name best translated as "construction for the holy struggle's sake." And they were sent by the Shiite Muslim guerrillas who are waging war to drive "Israeli" troops out of south Lebanon.

The workers quickly plastered gaping holes in the front stairs and repaired a smashed sewer pipe.

They couldn't fix a decades-old grapevine whose gnarled roots were severed by shrapnel, but they did shore up the trellis its branches were entwined in.

"I'm grateful, of course," said Fadel, an olive farmer in his early 70s. "But what matters isn't them fixing my house. It's them sacrificing their lives ... I can't pay back what I owe them for that."

"Such sentiments are among the reasons it has proved so difficult for "Israel's" powerful modern army to prevail against a relatively small and lightly armed band of guerrillas in south Lebanon.

In the 14 years since "Israel" carved out a 440-square-mile enclave to try to guard against cross -border attacks, the guerrillas have enjoyed an important edge: The backing of local people.

"A case in point is the front-line village of Hadatha, six miles north of the "Israel"-Lebanon border, and only a few hundred yards from "Israeli"-controlled territory.

"The village is faced on two sides by outposts of "Israel's" allied militia, the South Lebanon Army.

Guerrillas from Hizbullah and the pro-Syrian Amal movement fire on these outposts -- just across a steep, rocky valley from the village -- whenever they can.

"And the SLA fires back -- even though the guerrillas are usually long gone, since their standard method is to fire a few quick rounds and escape before their position can be pinpointed.

This hit-and-run style puts Hadatha directly in harm's way. But rather than resenting the guerrillas, the villagers _ overwhelmingly Shiite Muslims, like the fighters themselves _ express enthusiastic support for the guerrillas' cause.

"On the main road through the village, 7-year-old Hassan Nasser and his friends played in the afternoon sunshine, waving wooden sticks they pretended were guns.

"Turning his eyes toward the SLA post on the opposite ridge, Hassan piped up, "Someday I'm going to go up there and get them!" The watching adults beamed approvingly.

"I'm 80 years old -- I'll join the resistance if they'll take me!" called out a woman drinking tea nearby.

"All the "Israelis" have to do is get out of our country," said Zeinab Taha, 56, clad in a headscarf and long, enveloping robe. "We won't chase them all the way back to Haifa" -- a northern "Israeli" coastal city -- "but we will drive them out of here."

Of course, in a climate of such vocal support, any villager who disagreed with the guerrillas' goals or tactics would be unlikely to speak up. But there are other, quieter gauges of the bedrock popular backing.

"All the villages along the northern edge of the "Israeli" zone have elected town councils and mayors affiliated with Amal or Hizbullah. All are communities that have taken the brunt of fighting.

"It's been an experience that was bound to make them more militant," said Commandant Kieran
McDaid of the Irish U.N. peacekeeping contingent that has its headquarters in nearby Tibnin.

U.N. forces can sympathize with the villagers' plight, because in some respects they share it. An Irish-manned outpost in Hadatha was hit by nine artillery shells last month in the same barrage that
damaged Fadel's house. Two peacekeepers were wounded.

No one is supposed to target the peacekeepers, but U.N. officials believe "Israeli"-allied militiamen took deliberate aim at them, perhaps because they blamed U.N. troops -- and villagers -- for the guerrillas using Haddatha to fire on an SLA outpost.

"Within the "Israeli"-held zone itself, opposition to the "Israeli" presence is a little less pronounced.

"Israel" has 2,500 SLA militiamen who support their families with "Israeli"-paid wages. Other villagers in the enclave benefit from the "Israeli" presence because they are allowed to cross into "Israel" and work. And suspected guerrilla sympathizers face arrest and expulsion by the "Israelis".

But inside the zone, the help of local informants is key to guerrilla efforts. Such inside information about "Israeli" movements helps Hizbullah carry out attacks like the devastating roadside bombing that killed an "Israeli" brigadier general and three other "Israelis" last month.

In Haddatha, villagers say their determination to back the guerrilla cause has only grown as the long conflict has dragged on.

"Hizbullah is not alone -- they have the support of the people," said school principal Ali Soumi. "We teach our children to fight the enemy."
