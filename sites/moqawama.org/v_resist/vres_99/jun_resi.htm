  June Wednesday 2 -1999:

Jezzine delegation hosted by Hizbullah: Jezzine public backs Resistance(1)
"Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah today (yesterday) received a delegation of Jezzine notables, headed by Pierre Serhal, Philip al-Khouri and George Naameh. The meeting was also attended by Hizbullah Political Council Member Sheikh Mohammed Kawtharani.

"During the meeting Sayyed Nasrallah underlined Hizbullah's guarding the well-being of the Jezzine residents, "that (the Resistance) has offered precious bloods to rescue from occupation and to recover to the homeland and freedom."
"Regarding the collaborators, the national interest and the security in Jezzine requires that they be handed over to Lebanese judiciary, as some may be linked to the collaborating militia's security and Zionist intelligence services, his Eminence said.

"For their part, the delegation members commended the straightforward positions released by the Secretary General in the press conference he held two days ago. They stressed the Resistance's major role in the liberation process, along with the people's steadfastness during the years under occupation.

"The relations of the Jezzinites with the Resistance is old and good, Serhal said, adding that liberating Jezzine is a step on the road to liberate all the occupied zone.

Serhal said no one must claim to represent the people of Jezzine in the media or in other spheres.

"There is no reason for fear, especially as other areas had been liberated and no problems occurred, Serhal added.

"For his part, Attorney George Naameh stressed that the people of Jezzine and East of Sidon are united in backing Hizbullah and the Resistance.

Visiting Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah is to refute what some have been saying or spreading through the media, Naameh said.

"The hands of the Jezzinites and the Resistance are united to defeat the occupation and forces ""Israel"" to completely withdraw from the occupied zone, he added.

"The Jezzine public opinion backs the Resistance, the voices that are heard here or there are fueled by private interests and electioneering, he concluded.

"The Hizbullah Secretary General later received the head of the Mashmoushe Monastery Abbot Boulous Khawand who reviewed with Sayyed Nasrallah the situation in Jezzine and conveyed to him the joyful sentiments of the enclave's residents for returning to the homeland, thanking the Secretary General for his sincere positions.

"Following the meeting Khawand denied that he had opened the monastery for Lahd collaborators.

We do not intend to protect the Lahd militiamen from the state. They must be tried by the Judiciary, Khawand said.

As for what would follow a full-fledged withdrawal, the occupiers will have to wait for answers until they return to their own side of the border".
"The Jewish state is using the ever-present threat posed by its strategic military superiority in a cynical effort to cow Beirut and Damascus into ending the activities of the resistance, whose tactical superiority seems to increase with each passing day".
"Specifically, the "Israelis" have found no way to deal effectively with Hizbullah.
Their intelligence on Sayyed Hassan Nasrallahs group is increasingly murky and their willingness to come out of their bunkers and face the guerrillas on the ground is evaporating".
"Even the high-tech warplanes supplied to them by Washington are of little use these days beyond the infliction of new tragedies on civilians, the impotent destruction of rocks and shrubs in Iqleem al-Toffah, and the rattling of windows in Beirut".
"The "Israelis" are therefore trying to go around what they have consistently failed to go through. Faced with such an effective and implacable foe, "Israel" ­ and the United States ­ would like nothing better than to have others neutralize Hizbullah. "But ludicrous demands that others must make concessions to preserve "Israeli" security are nothing new. Accordingly, it is incumbent on all Arab countries ­ including and perhaps especially those who have already made peace with "Israel" ­ to join their voices to Lebanons and make it unmistakably clear that the ball remains very much in the "Israeli" court".

  June Friday 4 -1999:

Hoss hails steadfast Jezzine(1)
"Prime Minister Salim Hoss on Thursday congratulated Jezzine residents on their liberation and expressed hopes that the same will happen to the residents of the "Israeli"-occupied zone.

The steadfastness of Jezzine residents in their land and the heroic operations of the resistance have forced the "Israeli"-allied militiamen to withdraw without any conditions or security arrangements, Hoss said.

"He told reporters he had learned that the SLA had finished its withdrawal from the Jezzine enclave and that police patrols had entered the area to take charge of security.

Im sure that the awareness of our compatriots who live in the liberated areas and their national sentiments are capable of foiling any "Israeli" attempt to disturb their happiness with their liberation, he added.

"A source close to Hoss said that SLA members who hail from Jezzine and have refused to leave the area after laying down their arms must place themselves under the disposal of the government, which will look into each of their cases separately.

Parliaments Foreign Affairs Committee chairman, Tyre MP Ali Khalil, said after meeting Hoss earlier in the day: The liberated areas are now subject to the 1996 April Understanding, which bans the targeting of civilian areas or their use as launching sites for attacks.

"He said that the "Israeli" enemy has failed to implement a plot to instigate national strife in Lebanon and separate the Lebanese and Syrian tracks of the Middle East peace process.

Khalil called for caution and awareness in this critical stage following Ehud Baraks election as "Israeli" prime minister. We will not return to the negotiating table unless the talks resume from the point they reached before the 1996 deadlock.

This means that before the talks restart, the "Israelis" must admit that a just and comprehensive peace cannot be achieved if they dont withdraw from the south, the western Beqaa and the Golan Heights, he added.

"A delegation from the National Bloc, headed by party official Ibrahim Estephan, also held talks with Hoss on Jezzine. Our view is that the region should be controlled fully by the government and that the head of the SLA, Antoine Lahd, should be tried for treason, Estephan said.

We also told the prime minister that SLA members who have deserted their militia should be given 10 days or so to put themselves at the governments disposal".

"The National Bloc, led by Raymond Edde from his self-imposed exile in Paris, had called for deploying the army in the area evacuated by the SLA.

Hoss told us that sending the army to Jezzine could be a trap for the armed forces, Estephan said, referring to the governments refusal to send troops to the area. But we still believe that Jezzine should be run exclusively by the government.

"A delegation from the Syrian Social Nationalist Party, headed by the partys president, Ali Qanso, said that the SLA dissidents should not be pardoned because theyre traitors.

"During the first visit of Hizbullah to Jezzine since the town was liberated earlier this month, Nabatieh MP Mohammed Raad reassured residents that the government was responsible for security and pledged that no party members would impede the state in fulfilling its duties toward the area.

Were here in Jezzine today to confirm that all the care and security of the region and its residents are under the supervision of the Lebanese government and there will be no security measures by the resistance within any liberated inch of Lebanese territory, said Raad, who heads Hizbullahs politburo.

The delegation arrived in the town, escorted by Internal Security Forces, and met with several Jezzine notables, including Father Boulos Khawand, the head of the Mashmousheh monastery.

But the absence of Jezzine MPs was noticeable.

Raad insisted that Hizbullahs visit was to congratulate its people on the liberation and to witness the great accomplishment by the resistance.

The Zionist enemy wanted to show the people of Jezzine that members of the resistance were their enemy when the fact is that the resistance is a nightmare to the "Israeli" enemy, he said.

Raad said his presence at the city Serail was to show the people that Jezzine was dear to the resistance.

We hope you receive the proper care from the government and whatever we can offer will be at the disposal of the people of Jezzine, he said.

Raad said that Hizbullah had presented a legislative proposal to Parliament more than 18 months ago, recommending amnesty to "Israeli" proxy militiamen.

The aim was not to give free amnesty to collaborators but an organized and scheduled amnesty with the national aim of retrieving our sons and giving them a second chance, he said.

Khawand said that Jezzines wedding began when it was liberated and is complete with Hizbullahs presence.

He called on "Israel's" militia to defect and surrender themselves to the government.

However, Khawand stressed that there were 203 young men who have placed their trust in the cleric when they resigned from the militia.

I want to reiterate that these men have fulfilled their promise and are at the governments disposal, he said.

Khawand added that the fate of these men would be watched closely by the 1,500 people still working for the militia.

The priest said that the families of Butros Wehbe and Najm Habib, two militiamen captured by Hizbullah earlier this year, wanted to see them.

Im speaking not only as a religious man but as a human being and I would like to arrive at a solution for the detainees, he said.

In response, Raad said Wehbe and Habib were known collaborators who would be used to free Khiam detainees.

Theyre with Hizbullah and the resistance and we have proposed to Antoine Lahd to swap them with detainees in Khiam. I can assure you there are no civilians kidnapped by the party but only collaborators, he said. "Afterwards, the Hizbullah officials had a rare opportunity to inspect the handiwork of resistance fighters up close when they examined the remains of an armored personnel carrier destroyed in an attack during the "Israel's" militia retreat along the road south of Jezzine"

  June Monday 28 -1999:

Lahoud: "Israeli" enemy displayed barbaric activities(1)
"With the south remaining calm over the weekend, the government reaffirmed its commitment to the resistance while diplomatic efforts gathered pace to prevent against a repetition of last weeks severe escalation".

With the drive to repair the damage caused by the air raids well underway, President Emile Lahoud praised the army and the government for its response to the offensive.

Addressing a delegation of 26 Central Bank governors from Francophone countries, Lahoud said Lebanon would demand financial compensation from "Israel" with the help of international bodies.

The president complained that some of the foreign media had reported "Israel's" attacks were in response to Hizbullahs cross-border Katyusha raid.

But the media overlooked the "Israeli" shelling in the south which had wounded civilians, Lahoud told his visitors.

The "Israeli" enemy displayed barbaric and monstrous activities. After shelling, destroying and burning the Jamhour power plant, the television screened several firefighters and civil defense troops who had come to put out the fire and save those trapped inside. "Israel" deliberately launched its second raid on the plant killing and wounding several of them, he said. Lahoud said what was important was the fact that the aggressions did not paralyze everyday life, adding that Lebanon was strong through the unity of its people".

"Prime Minister Salim Hoss affirmed Lebanons support for the resistance for as long as "Israel" is occupying Lebanese territories.

Iranian Foreign Minister Mohammed Kharrazi flew to Damascus for talks with his Syrian counterpart Farouq Sharaa on the "Israeli" air raids.

"Israel's" outgoing prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, maintained his belligerent tone, threatening further raids on Lebanon if cross-border rocket attacks continue.

Hoss told French Middle East envoy Aubin de la Messuziere that Hizbullahs firing of Katyusha rockets on northern Galilee Thursday afternoon, to which "Israel" retaliated with the evening air raids, was in response to earlier "Israeli" action against civilian targets in stark violation of the April Understanding.

In principle, we are against cross-border military operations. But what are "Israeli" troops doing inside our borders for more than 21 years? Isnt this military aggression? Hoss demanded.

The premier said that the five-nation Monitoring Group, which is due to convene Tuesday in Naqoura, should take the adequate measures that will restore its authority.

Stressing his visit to Lebanon was an affirmation of Frances solidarity with Lebanon, De la Messuziere said he believed the repeated escalations in the south would soon end.

The Monitoring Group is to meet now under Frances direction and I am confident that the prevailing aggressive policy will end soon, he said.

Speaking during an interview with a local radio station, Hoss affirmed that Lebanon is not willing to sign security agreement with "Israel". "Israel's" wishes to drag Lebanon to individual negotiations and separate the Lebanese and Syrian paths but we will not accept such an alternative, he said.
