  August Monday 16 -1999:

Goksel : Lack of Israeli casualties is connected to the very little Israeli movement around ... (1)
Israel cannot expect security along its northern borders following the signing of peace with Lebanon and Syria if the Palestinian refugees are prevented from returning to their original homes and are forced to remain in Lebanon, according to Unifils special advisor, Timur Goksel.

Goksel said that if there is no satisfactory outcome to the fate of the estimated 350,000 Palestinian refugees in the country, then they will become a testing problem for Lebanon. Aside from the humanitarian issue, leaving those 350,000 angry Palestinians here in Lebanon isnt going to contribute to Israels security, he said in an interview with The Daily Star.

There are a lot of youngsters in the population here, frustrated and angry youngsters, and an increasing religious influence (among them). If you leave them here, unchecked, uncontrolled and unwanted by the Lebanese its not going to contribute to the security along the borders. They could be a problem that the Lebanese and others will find difficult to control. During a trip to Washington in July, Israeli Premier Ehud Barak suggested that a resolution of the Palestinian dilemma be found in their host countries. Baraks comments triggered alarm bells in Beirut where the government adamantly rejects the possibility of settling the Palestinians permanently in Lebanon.

If the Israeli governments policy toward the Palestinian refugees is vague, its plans for the occupation of the south are very clear now, said Goksel.

They want to get out of here on the basis of an agreement. Thats there number one priority, he said. But I dont think that they have completely wiped out the possibility of withdrawing unilaterally if the peace process doesnt work. Anything radically longer than the promised 12 months (for a withdrawal) will become an internal problem in Israel.

 Goksel argued that the Israelis overcame a psychological barrier by unleashing warplanes against infrastructure targets at the end of June following Hizbullahs Katyusha attack into northern Israel.

The government of Benjamin Netanyahu had found itself impotent to respond to earlier cross-border rocket attacks because of the fear of incurring further salvoes from Hizbullah. But Goksel believed that Hizbullah has not abandoned the strategy of firing Katyushas into Israel in reprisal for Lebanese civilian casualties.

They (Hizbullah) have a promise to the people and Hizbullah is determined to keep that. For them, this is very important. If civilians get killed, the Israelis take the rap. The deterrence has not gone, its still there, he said.

Despite Israels belief that Syria ordered Hizbullah to de-escalate its operations against the occupation forces after the June infrastructure raids, Goksel argued that the lack of Israeli casualties recently was connected to the troops spending most of their time in underground bunkers. The fact that nobody is getting hurt, I dont think can be attributed to anybody restraining anyone... I think that there are certain military constraints on the resistance side because there are fewer targets on the ground... finding targets has become much more difficult than before. There is very little Israeli movement around, he said.

He pointed out that there had been some serious roadside bomb attacks since the June raids, despite the general downturn in the number of operations. The Israelis are taking new precautions against roadside bombs ­ still the deadliest weapon against the occupation forces.

A very important element is the scraping of the roadsides along all the major roads of the occupied area which will make the planting of roadside bombs that much more difficult, he said.

As part of the defensive measures underway, Goksel said he expects some further relocations of troops in the coming months similar to the SLAs abandoning of the Qantara outpost last week. But the much speculated withdrawals from the northern sector to the Litani and from Hasbaya in the east appears to be on hold, he said.

I dont expect a major revamping of the situation because of the political developments, he said. I think what we are seeing is the military requirements being put into action. We might see more adjustments to the frontline, the relocation of some of the more vulnerable posts, we are already seeing the hardening of some SLA posts, and new SLA positions.

  August Wednesday 18 -1999:

Hizbullah SG at martyr Deeb's funeral: There is no place for the occupation in Lebanon
"Thousands of Hizbullah partisans and sympathizers, chanting Death to "Israel" and America is the Great Satan, crowded Haret Hreik on Tuesday for the funeral of Ali Deeb, a resistance officer slain in a roadside bomb attack in Sidon a day earlier.

"The crowd assembled in front of Hizbullahs Shura Council headquarters in Haret Hreik to honor Deeb, alias Abu Hassan.

"The partys secretary-general, Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah, led the mourners. In a brief address he repeated Hizbullahs pledge to keep up the jihad (holy war) until "Israel" is ousted from Lebanese territory.

We are not wasting time. We are leading a huge war to liberate our land, declared Nasrallah.

The crowd silently repeated after him as he recited the prayer of the dead in front of Deebs coffin, draped with a yellow Hizbullah flag. Death to America; death to "Israel"! "Israel" is the enemy of Islam, they shouted.

Finally, Abu Hassans body has rested and his soul ascended to meet that of his son who also died a martyr several months ago, Nasrallah said. It is not surprising that Abu Hassan was martyred. It is more surprising that he remained alive until now, he said.

Deeb joined Hizbullah when the party was established in 1982. He was the target of at least six assassination attempts. While no group has yet to accept responsibility for his death, Hizbullah has blamed "Israel" for the assassination.

The procession passed through the blocked-off streets of Haret Hreik, causing a traffic jam that lasted two hours. Deeb was buried in Beirut at the Rawdat Shahidein cemetery because his body could not be taken to his native village of Kounine in the occupation zone.

The procession included a van draped in Hizbullah flags that was covered with posters of Nasrallah and late party leader Sayyed Abbas Musawi, as well as a stream of sheikhs who carried the coffin.

Deebs murder was widely condemned by government officials, with President Emile Lahoud paying condolences to Hizbullah. This crime, committed by the enemy, can never falter Lebanons determination to defend its territory and its rights, he said(1)

Nassrallah said: "Our open war against the occupation will remain as such; we have started this war with full awareness, realizing whom we are fighting, what road we are taking, whom we are confronting, the injustice, estrangement, killing. displacement, perils, accusations, intimidation and threats that await us, he said.

"Let no one think that the falling of martyrs and killing our leaders, cadres, Mujahedeen, brothers, and families may reduce our resolve in this battle, Sayyed Nasrallah said, adding "Abu Hassan (Martyr Ali Deeb), was martyred, so did Sayyed Abbas al-Musawi and Sheikh Ragheb Harb, along with their sons and brothers. Others will be martyred, for we are not playing games here, nor are we wasting time; we are engaged in a real war, the only real war fought by the Ummah. We will proceed with all the strength, firmness, resolve that the battle requires."

"The enemy will understand, as we have made it understand in the past months and years, that there is no room for the occupation in Lebanon, the Secretary General concluded(2)

"Prime Minister Salim Hoss also condemned the attack, saying it not only targeted the resistance, but every Lebanese.

"Tyre MP and Amal Movement member Mohammed Abdel-Hamid Beydoun accused "Israel" of murdering Deeb, saying it was an attack on Lebanons sovereignty and a violation of all international laws.

"Beydoun called on the government not to let the crime go unpunished especially on the legal level.

We heard that "Israeli" Prime Minister Ehud Barak has prepared a long list of names of resistance fighters he wants dead, he said(3)

  August Tuesday 31 -1999:

Hizbullah vows to avenge further attacks(1)
"The threat of imminent cross-border Katyusha rocket attacks was rekindled Monday in a blistering speech by Hizbullahs deputy secretary-general, Naim Qassem, who warned "Israel" that the resistance would not tolerate further civilian casualties in south Lebanon.

We announce with no reservations that the Katyusha rockets are ready for launching the minute we think suitable. These rockets will be launched by our brothers and will fall on the settlements (in "Israel") in retaliation for the Zionist attacks, Qassem said during the funeral of a Hizbullah fighter, Karim Daqdouq, killed in an air raid on Sunday.

Qassems fiery speech was prompted by the wounding of five civilians on Sunday, including a three-year-old child and a nine-month-old baby, in "Israeli" and SLA* shelling. Several houses were also damaged in the bombardments.

Whats happening in the south is very dangerous from our point of view. We have registered what happened (Sunday) and its for you to remember so that no blame can be cast when the retaliation is launched at a time we will decide from now on. No one must assume that we will remain silent, he said.

Qassem insisted that the resistance would not be intimidated by the prospect of further attacks similar to the wide-ranging "Israeli" air raids on June 24.

If we practiced self-restraint (on Sunday), it was not in preparation for (U.S. Secretary of State Madeleine) Albrights visit or because were scared that the infrastructure may be bombed ... Let everyone know that our finger is on the trigger. Let them beware of the resistance fighters anger and their response at the right time and place, he said.

The "Israeli" Army has said on several occasions that the June air raids are the reason why Hizbullah has refrained from firing Katyushas across the border. However, Hizbullah usually only launches mass cross-border rocket assaults in response to a spate of civilian fatalities over a short period of time. Since June 24, the circumstances have not arisen for Hizbullah to unleash the Katyushas.

Qassem hinted that Syria has placed no restraints on Hizbullahs military operations and warned the "Israeli" inhabitants of northern Galilee to prepare their bomb shelters.

No one has spoken to us and no one will accept "Israel's" arbitrary and aggressive acts. We have not remained silent because were scared, but because were weighing matters to decide whats best to protect our people. But I tell the "Israeli" government, army and especially the settlers, as of today prepare to be in the shelters whenever one civilian is wounded, he said.

Qassems remarks are certain to alarm the "Israeli" government at a time when the arrival of Albright to the region on Wednesday is expected to inject a new impetus into relaunching the peace process.

"Hizbullah has consistently declared it will continue fighting the occupation forces until there is a full withdrawal from the south, regardless of progress in the regional peace talks. But Qassems speech was the most bellicose uttered by a senior member of Hizbullah since the election of "Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak in May raised hopes for a peace settlement and an end to the occupation. It was also the first time since June 24 that the threat of Katyusha attacks against northern "Israel" has been made so forcefully".
