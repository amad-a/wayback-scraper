Lebanon responds to "Israel's" accusations (2)
"A barrage of criticism and bellicose threats fired at Lebanon from senior "Israeli" and U.S. officials has led Prime Minister Salim Hoss to suspect that the Jewish state is preparing the ground before unleashing a military offensive against the south".
This campaign indicates that "Israel" is preparing something against Lebanon and that it is trying to justify it by distorting the
facts, Mr. Hoss said in a statement Friday.
"The premier was responding to a letter delivered Thursday to UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan by "Israel's" ambassador to
the international body, Dore Gold".

"In the letter, Mr. Gold blamed the Beirut government for the violence along the border and warned that Lebanon would be held accountable".

"Israel" reserves the right to take whatever measures may be necessary to assure its own self-defense, he said.
"The south has been quiet lately and there was no apparent reason for the timing or the pugnacious tone of the letter. But the implicit threat conveyed by the letter prompted Mr. Hoss to question the logic behind the "Israeli" claims".

Its most extraordinary that the aggressor should make accusations against the victims of aggression and blame them for acts of resistance which is a legitimate right of all peoples whose land is being occupied, he added.

"Mr. Hoss also lashed out at remarks made Thursday by U.S. Assistant Secretary of State Martin Indyk who openly
supported "Israel's" claim to be willing to abide by Resolution 425".

"In response to the U.S. officials comments, Mr. Hoss said that if "Israel" had genuinely accepted Resolution 425, why has it not already pulled out from south Lebanon?"

The truth is that "Israel" is still maneuvering and linking its withdrawal to the fulfillment of several conditions that Lebanon has rejected and will always reject because they are in total contradiction with the resolutions text and its purposes, he added.

  February Manday 15 -1999:

PM makes clear strong support for resistance (1)
"Hizbullahs growing influence in the political arena was apparent Sunday when Prime Minister Salim Hoss headed a diverse list of officials attending a rally at Unesco Palace to commemorate the assassinations of Hizbullah leaders Sheik Ragheb Harb and Sayyed Abbas Musawi".

"The gathering was the first major Hizbullah occasion to be held outside Beiruts southern suburbs, the partys traditional stronghold. Hizbullah Secretary General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah paid tribute to the two leaders, addressing an audience of some 1,200 supporters and a crowd outside who watched the event on television".

With the martyrdom of these two great men, we now possess important elements of power. The most important of these is credibility, because these leaders were living the life of every other person who suffers because of the occupation, he said.

Mr. Hoss praised fighters killed in the battle against "Israel", stressing resistance was the only path to victory.

This (resistance) is the only language the cowardly enemy understands, the enemy that shields itself with dominant international forces to practice crimes against our country, people and land, he said.
The presence of Mr. Hoss highlighted the governments closer links and greater sympathy with Hizbullahs political aspirations. Although former Prime Minister Rafik Hariri paid lip service to resistance efforts, relations between the his government and the party were often strained.

Mr. Hoss described Hizbullah as the greatest resistance movement in the history of Lebanon. It has perfected the use of weapons and changed "Israel" from a feared force to one that is continuously afraid.

Also in attendance were representatives of the Syrian Baath Party, the Palestinian Islamic Jihad and the Iranian Embassy.

Nawwaf Musawi, a member of Hizbullahs politburo, said that holding the rally at Unesco Palace, rather than a location in the southern suburbs, indicated Hizbullahs growing profile.

There is no doubt that choosing a place in the heart of the capital is symbolic. It was also the largest
place we could find, he said.

With party supporters filling the hall in a matter of minutes, hundreds of others who arrived in buses arranged by the party sat outside watching the proceedings on a large television set. Hizbullah has moved from the level of popular support to that of an official and regional nature, Mr. Musawi said, stressing the diverse representation at the rally.

The head of the Maronite League made a point to be at the event, in addition to representatives from Palestine, Syria and Iran, he said.

Such developments mean more efficient coordination between Hizbullah and the Army and Hizbullah and security forces, said Mr. Musawi.

The fact that our president was an army commander makes our coordination with the Army better, he said.

He also noted that the prime ministers support for the resistance has a long history. Mr. Hoss was one of the first to present condolences when Sayyed Abbas Musawi was assassinated in 1992. Sayyed Musawi was killed on the outskirts of Jibsheet along with his wife and a son when his car was destroyed by an "Israeli" helicopter gunship.

Sheik Harb, one of the founders of the resistance, was shot and killed 15 years ago in Jibsheet while leaving a mosque. Sheik Nasrallah recalled that Hizbullahs first major use of Katyusha rockets against "Israel" came a day after Sayyed Musawis assassination.

During an incident of "Israeli" aggression against the villages of Kafra and Yater, Hizbullah fired hundreds of Katyushas on Zionist settlements in northern Palestine, initiating a new tactic of confrontation, he said.

  February Wednesday  17 -1999:

Hizbullah offers rare glimpse of activities (1)
"Usually shrouded in secrecy, Hizbullahs military wing Tuesday allowed reporters a rare glimpse into the world of the resistance fighter, his weapons and tactics at a remote front line location in Iqlim al-Touffah".

"Some 70 representatives of local and foreign media were herded into coaches for the trip, organized by Hizbullahs Central Information Office, to the frontline village of Ain Boussoir, 6 kilometers north of Nabatieh."

"The reporters were led through olive groves just outside the village to an isolated area by camouflaged Hizbullah fighters who seemed to know the location of every stone and tree along the slippery and rocky path".

"The fighters showed their weary visitors to designated chairs facing a makeshift platform overlooking the villages of Ain Qana and Jibaa".

"Impatient photographers and cameramen were told when and of whom they could take pictures by the polite but security-conscious resistance officials."

"Press officer Atallah Hammoud introduced a local Hizbullah commander as Ammar.
Clad in a camouflage uniform, with a black keffiyeh around his neck and sporting sunglasses, Ammar said that the chosen area was secure enough for visitors but was nonetheless classified as a military zone."

"Indicating a map with a laser pointer, he said that his unit was responsible for targeting the South Lebanon Army militiamen based in Rihan.

Ammar informed the reporters that the strategic "Israeli" positions at Bir Kallab and Qassaret Aaroush in the northern sector of the occupation zone were protected by SLA outposts dotted along the frontline.

The SLA positions are used as barriers to protect the "Israeli" soldiers. Recently, the SLA positions have been rebuilt with reinforced cement roofs and equipped with 24-hour radar surveillance, he said.

Ammar added that several of the "Israeli" Armys state-of-the-art Merkava Mark III tanks had been deployed to sensitive military areas.

But despite all these reinforcements, resistance fighters have been able to break through them on numerous occasions, he said.

Ammar said that the number of SLA soldiers had halved to 900 from 1,800 in 1995, but he refused to specify the number of Hizbullah fighters.

Describing the effect of the psychological war against "Israel" through videotapes of Hizbullah operations, Ammar said each film was scrutinized by a special committee before broadcasting to
ensure no secret military tactics were included.

Listing the steps fighters go through before staging an operation, Ammar said that when an outpost or ambush was chosen, an operations committee picked the best way to target it and prepare a back-up plan.

We then pray and look forward to Gods blessing and head to our positions, he said.
Reporters were then shown three different weapons commonly used by the guerrillas, including a Katyusha rocket launcher, a Sagger anti-tank missile and a 122mm mortar.

One Hizbullah fighter, his face daubed with camouflage paint, said that the mortar was a favorite weapon for the resistance because it could be dismantled into three pieces and easily transported across difficult terrain.

We can fire around 15 shells a minute. We can also use fragmentation and phosphorous shells, smoke rounds and flares, the fighter said, as an "Israeli" pilotless reconnaissance plane could be heard buzzing above our position.

"A plan to allow journalists to witness an actual operation by Hizbullah was canceled at the last minute as Ammar received information that security conditions were unstable".

"As reporters mounted the two coaches rented by the party on the return trip, several Hizbullah members were praying under an oak tree."

"The press officer said that they were on their way to stage an operation".

Cat and mouse in Lebanon (2)
A combination of political error and military weakness is causing the continuing casualties in Lebanon. The army is imprisoned by outmoded concepts and the politicians are doing all they can to avoid a settlement

"At the Systems Analysis Center of the IDF* General Staff Planning Division, life, like death, is a computer game. Software developed over the last decade allows unit and formation commanders, commanding generals and general staff chiefs - and if they want to, defense ministers and prime ministers - to move corps around and destroy them, courtesy of a standard, army-issue mouse. The developers of the software, which has also been purchased by the armies of Switzerland and Singapore, say that although the officers sit in front of the computer and are not in any physical danger, they still experience anxiety, surprise, resourcefulness and confusion.At the Systems Analysis Center, they are still wary of analyses that lead directly to political conclusions, although seemingly innocent research also tends to lead in that direction. For budgetary reasons, the IDF must keep maintenance costs down and maintain a ceiling on stocks of ammunition, spare parts and fuel (and also training exercises, which require engine, sea and flight hours).

"The IDF, for lack of choice, recognizes the weakness of standing power, and tries to make up for this weakness by honing its striking power. The clash of paratrooper reconnaissance troops with a small Hezbollah force this week demonstrated, once again, that our striking power is not infallible. One could easily imagine the public's response if the numbers had been reversed and three "Israelis" had defeated a much larger enemy force. In the 1950s, Unit 101 and the paratroopers were celebrated for achieving victories like these".

"Times have changed and our enemies are different, but the earth shakes when operations go wrong and casualties are suffered by elite frameworks like Sayeret Matkal, paratrooper reconnaissance troops, Flotilla 13, the Mossad and the Shin Bet. The naval commando has never really discovered what happened at Ansariya a year and a half ago. Without knowing what went wrong, it is difficult to fix things. Mossad agents are still locked up in Cyprus. The security guards at the "Israeli" consulate in Berlin bravely fought off the Kurdish mob that stormed the building, but not before some of them infiltrated the "holy of holies" - the code room. This time, the codes were safely locked away in a safe, but one cannot be sure that "Israel's" state secrets were not leaked to a certain Western country - one that is friendly toward us and angry with us by turns - after the discovery of a surveillance system installed at one of "Israel's" foreign embassies".

"Brigade commander Col. Gershon (Gerry) Yitzhak, who was authorized this week to speak to the media about paratrooper reconnaissance operations, will soon be appointed to another senior post, although his affiliation with airborne combat divisions will continue. Until then, the paratroopers and rest of the army have little choice but to go on playing cat and mouse in Lebanon."

"A fiasco that costs human lives is the joint product of political error and military weakness. "Israelis", who have no patience for a prolonged fight, are always crying for a cease-fire or an escalation that will end in a cease-fire. Without one or the other, the situation seems like an endless nightmare. If the IDF enjoys a technological advantage over its enemies, this does not seem to show itself on the battlefield in Lebanon (although without this advantage, the number of casualties would perhaps be higher). What is particularly astonishing is that when "Israel" initiates a strike in Lebanon, there is no on-the-spot intelligence, despite all the apparatus - carried, transported or airborne - which is available for this purpose and could be used to keep tabs on the situation every step of the way. The Hezbollah does not have such intelligence either, but it does have good instincts for analyzing the moves of the troops it encounters. The game is a dangerous one: a Hezbollah force sits in ambush for an "Israeli" force that sits in ambush for another Hezbollah force. Some day it may become known that "Israel" scored hundreds of successes, proclaimed or unproclaimed, for every failure that ends in military funerals. But that scorecard has no value because it does not advance a political settlement (which the politicians are doing all they can to evade)".

"Although the refusal to move forward with negotiations with Syria is the major reason why "Israeli" blood is being shed in Lebanon, the IDF senior command cannot evade responsibility for military failures. A recent issue of the IDF journal Ma'arakhot contains an article that is unusually frank for an officer in active service and an official army publication. Col. Yehuda Wagman, an instructor at the Command and General Staff College and the former head of the armored corps, writes about the "decline in the performance graph of the IDF since the Yom Kippur War," and a "chain of failures" which he attributes to "patterns of thought and action nourished by an anti-academic military ethos."

Wagman finds "serious flaws in an organization that perceives itself as a professional body," among them a lack of professional training and a mindset that prevents it from operating as an efficient war machine. Mistakes made in 1973 were repeated with even greater severity in 1982, and no lessons were learned because the army clung to the simplistic belief that the outcome of the Yom Kippur War was determined chiefly by the fact that "Israel" was surprised and did not have enough armored formations. The mistaken credo of IDF land forces (as opposed to the air force) was "If force doesn't work, apply more force."

"Until 1967," Wagman writes, "the average officer grew up in an atmosphere in which the importance of studying the military profession was scoffed at." The successes (or what were believed to be successes, thanks to propaganda and censorship) appeared to justify the system, "and improvisation and fighting experience were sanctified as the solution to every problem on the battlefield."

"The early founders of the state were a special breed, and that made up for their lack of military expertise. The fact that the standards of the other side were so low was also crucial in the series of successes "Israel" chalked up during the first two decades of the state. Over the next three decades, "Israel's" military failures were a product of "the unwillingness of the intellectual elite to join the career army, the continued disregard for professional military education and the steady improvement made by the Arab guerrilla and terrorist organizations." Military operations in Lebanon like operations "Accountability" and "Grapes of Wrath," says Wagman, have proven that the primitive "more force" credo is still alive and well in the IDF".

"In Lebanon, this plodding army, with its declining performance graph, now confronts an organization whose performance graph is soaring upward. "Hezbollah has never been so well off," states a report of the Foreign Ministry's Political Research Center. "It has expanded its social, economic and political activities all over Lebanon; the character of the new Lebanese administration and Syria's new operational strategies have increased support for the organization's resistance activities; it sees itself as winning in the battle against "Israel" and the public debate in "Israel" is growing. At the moment, say the researchers, Hezbollah believes that it is abiding by the "April understandings" (a tacit agreement reached between "Israel" and this organization in 1996) and working to prevent an undesirable escalation of tension".

The policy it plans to adopt after "Israel" withdraws from Lebanon is being kept intentionally vague, "although there are signs that the struggle will not end here, and will continue through other means."

During a visit of Hezbollah leaders to Iran last November, the organization stepped up its operations against the IDF and decided, after consultation with its Iranian hosts, against joining the Lebanese government .Not that the term "Lebanese government" means much when it comes to foreign affairs and defense issues. It might be more correct to talk about a regional council of southwest Syria - a body that is about as independent as the Upper Galilee regional council is in "Israel". Hezbollah will be faced with a real dilemma if "Israel" decides to withdraw in an orderly fashion, as part of an agreement with Syria on the Golan Heights. If that happens, the organization will have to resign itself to being dictated to by Syria - or confront it head on, but none of this seems likely in the near future.

Over a period of three years, ending this June, Benjamin Netanyahu has been responsible for making the above scenario a remote possibility. His refusal to renew talks with Syria at the point where they were broken off may be in line with international law, but the bloody game in Lebanon is played by different rules. Yitzhak Rabin's choice of the Palestinian channel over the Syrian channel prolonged the battle in the north. But Netanyahu made things even worse: He managed to dry up both channels.
In the five years since the Oslo accords, the IDF has lost 135 soldiers in Lebanon, not counting the 73 who died in the Yasur helicopter crash".
