December Wednesday 01 -1999:

Government releases list of demands to be fulfilled before resistance ends(1)
Lebanon unveiled on Monday a seven-point list of demands which Israel must fulfill before a peace agreement can be signed between the two countries, warning that a troop withdrawal from the south was insufficient to warrant the end of the resistance.

The release of the demands, outlined in a statement released by Parliaments Defense and Foreign Affairs committees, was intended to allay the view that Lebanon opposes an Israeli withdrawal from the south for the benefit of Syria.

Speaker Nabih Berri convened a special session of the committees which gathered Prime Minister Salim Hoss and Defense Minister Ghazi Zeaiter and another 44 MPs.

The basis of any just and comprehensive settlement is Israels commitment to implement UN Security Council resolutions 242, 338 and 425 that call for Israels complete withdrawal from the Lebanese and Arab territories it occupies, the statement said.

The seven-point list of peace demands went much further than the required Israeli withdrawal. It included the rejection of a permanent settlement of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon, the return of Lebanese territory annexed by Israel, ending alleged water theft, the release of Lebanese prisoners and the right to bring South Lebanon Army* militiamen to trial.

These issues should show the whole world that Israels pledge to pull out its troops from Lebanon is simply a pullback that will not lead to any settlement or be a step toward peace, the statement said.

The committees stuck adamantly to the governments policy of rejecting a permanent settlement of Palestinian refugees in Lebanon.

Lebanon rejects solving the case of Palestinian refugees at its expense and considers any direct or indirect attempt to permanently settle the refugees here a plot against their national identity and an attack on Lebanons existence and sovereignty, the statement said.

The statement said another condition for a peace agreement with Israel is the release of Lebanese prisoners, detainees and hostages held in prisons run by the occupation forces and their collaborators. It said that the sentences imposed on some of the 200 Lebanese taken by Israel or its proxy, the SLA militia, are illegitimate and that their conditions of imprisonment violate the Geneva Convention.

The fate of the SLA militiamen must be decided by the Lebanese judiciary and not as part of a pre-withdrawal agreement with Israel. Lebanon rejects any Israeli attempt to use these people in the future for cross-border purposes, it said.

Lebanon wanted compensation for the mass murders, massacres and destruction of public and private property resulting from Israels occupation, invasions and attacks.

Lebanon also requires the return of the seven villages and all the other areas  seized from Lebanon before and after 1949 and during the June (1967) war, including the Shibaa farms and the border areas in the qadas of Marjeyoun, Bint Jbeil and Hasbaya, the committees said.

The so-called seven villages, which also include more than 25 farms, were annexed in the 1920s to British mandate Palestine from French mandate Lebanon under an agreement by the two European powers.

Their residents, who fled after the creation of Israel in 1948, were given Lebanese passports under the 1994 Naturalization Decree.

The other areas claimed by Lebanon were seized by Israel during the 1949 and the 1967 Arab-Israeli wars. After Israels invasions of Lebanon in 1978 and 1982, the barbed-wire fence separating the two countries was moved deeper into Lebanon.

Lebanon, which accuses Israel of stealing its water, wants this practice to end, especially at the Wazzani and Hasbani Rivers and the cross-border underground water, the statement said.

The committees affirm Lebanons adherence to the simultaneity of the Lebanese and the Syrian tracks and to the heroic resistance movement until Israel pulls out fully and unconditionally from Lebanon under Resolution 425, recognizes the international border and respects Lebanons national resources, mainly water, it said.

After the meeting, Berri said he had released the statement to counteract the international view that Lebanon is opposed to Israels pullout from the south and that this opposition is the result of Syrian pressures.

LEBANON SAYS RESISTANCE ATTACKS TO CONTINUE UNTIL ALL LEBANESE TERRITORY INCLUDING 'SEVEN VILLAGES' ARE LIBERATED(2)
Views in resistance
"The head of parliament's foreign relations committee, Deputy Ali Khalil, today (yesterday) denounced "Israel's" new reported annexation of Lebanese territory, saying such moves reveal "Israel's" "real intentions". Khalil confirmed that "Israel" has dispatched technical teams to southern Lebanon to plot a new border with Lebanon in a way "which does not reflect reality". DPA yesterday quoted UN sources as saying "Israeli" soldiers have fenced in and annexed the outskirts of four Lebanese villages on the border with "Israel" over the past few years, including areas in the villages of Odaisseh, Maroun Al Ras, southeast of Marjeyoun, and Alma Al Shaab. The "Israelis" claim the fences were erected for practical reasons and to apprehend infiltrators, the UN sources said. They added that the original fence demarcating the common border line is still in place and is monitored around the clock by the United Nation's 55-member Armistice Observer Group, who were deployed in 1948 to monitor the Lebanese-"Israeli" armistice pact. Lebanon will continue to launch resistance attacks in southern Lebanon, Khalil added, until "Israeli" occupation troops withdraw from all Lebanese territories, including the Shibaa farms and the seven villages, which lately raised controversy here. Prime Minister Salim Hoss yesterday denied reports that Beirut will drop its demands for seven villages which were annexed to Palestine in 1923 during the drawing of the border agreement of the French and the British. He was quoted by the Daily Star as saying the villages were part of Palestine when France and Britain carved up their protectorates in the Levant. But Hoss yesterday stressed that the return of the villages will be "a Lebanese demand that will be on the agenda of the future peace talks with "Israel"". Lebanon is expected to resume peace negotiations with "Israel" following the second round of talks between Syrian and "Israeli" officials in Washington on January 3".
