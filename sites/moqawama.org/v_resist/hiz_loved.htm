Hizbullah: Loved by The People, Respected by The
Military and Feared by The Enemy
 
 
By Munif Al-Haj
Monday 5 Jan. 1998
 
 

At first glance one would think that the above title is exaggerated to some extent. But not to a seeker of truth who reads a lot and looks with an open eye at what is always clouded by the Jewish controlled media of our time.

If we look closely at David Hirsts article Lebanon security zone snares Israel we can find the answer to the above title as to whether it was exaggerated or not.
The following excerpt shows the love of the people:

In the ensuing Israeli bombardment, the people of Nabatiyah took to their basements while inhabitants of the zone went about their business as if they belonged to one world and the bedlam of outgoing fire to quite another.

Israeli protection did not account for their sense of security; it was their knowledge that they need not fear Hizbullah. Under the US-brokered rules of this conflict, neither side is allowed to attack civilians. Hizbullah shows far more respect for them than the Israelis. You hear of dozens of civilian casualties over there, but there are hardly any here, said a Christian resident.(1)

Not only does it show the love of the people towards Hizbullah, it also shows how little US-brokered rules mean to people.

As for respect by the military, lets see what he has to say:

    A regular army can fight with raw recruits, said an officer of the UN force in Lebanon, Unifil, if it has good commanders, but these guys are their own commandersand they are really good.

    It shows in the casualty figures. In the past, against Palestinians, the Israelis got used to inflicting disproportionate losses. But Hizbullah has narrowed the gap to one Israeli killed for as few as 2.7 of their own. That is a good ratio for an attacking force and it is being achieved by fearlessness, planning and patience.(1)

Regarding, feared by the enemy:
    ISRAELI columnists call it Israels Vietnam, that cursed place, that Moloch devouring its young manhood. They are referring, to South Lebanon, the last violent frontier of the Arab-Israeli conflict. Last months helicopter collision took their anguish to new heights. It was an accident, but an all but inevitable one.

    But perhaps the strangest thing about the zone is that you rarely clap eyes on this enemystrange, that is, until you grasp that its very invisibility is a measure of the Islamic resistances effectiveness. For Israeli soldiers now do all they can to reduce their exposureby such means as helicopterwhen move they must.

    The few Israelis I saw in a two-day visit were visible only because of Hizbullahs latest operation(1)

Finally, I would like to end by also referring to Mr. Hirsts article:
    (One weapon Hizbullah usually takes into battle is a video camera. In boosting its supporters morale and lowering the enemys, it is possibly the most effective weapon of all. To General Lahd, films of Israeli soldiers being blown apart by mines or the Hizbullah flag being planted atop the Dabshe outpost are cheap showmanship. But not so for Hizbullah. Its motivations, religious and patriotic, and the vitality of its fighting machine, are reason enough why it will not go away, and why the Israelis are deluding themselves if they think it will.(1)
 

(1) The Guardian Weekly Volume 156 Issue 11 for week    ending March 16, 1997, page 3.
