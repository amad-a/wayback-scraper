Keep up the good work

    Lebanon celebrates Independence Day on Monday, and few people deserve more thanks for guarding that independence than the men who risk everything by confronting our oppressors each and every day.

Shaul Mofaz can beat his chest all he likes, but the Lebanese resistance has shattered the myth of Israeli military invincibility like no other force ever confronted by the Jewish state.

Somehow, a relatively small but highly devoted group of fighters has managed to keep the vaunted Israeli soldier holed up in concrete bunkers, his nervous leaders cowed into confusion and inaction by their increasingly obvious inability to solve the riddle posed by what may be the worlds most effective guerrilla force.

Mofaz is the chief of staff of Israels military, so he more than most is well aware of his sides embarrassing failure to achieve victory despite its easy access to some of the best weapons in Americas arsenal ­ and massive US aid to help pay for them. There is little that anyone in Lebanon can do when the Israelis use their high-tech warplanes

to lash out, but even these episodes have utterly failed to weaken the resolve of Hizbullah and other groups.

Apart from the transitory successes of the past few days, the Israelis have several problems that can only get worse with time.

Domestic public opinion is increasingly against the occupation in south Lebanon, and every Israeli casualty only heightens that opposition.

There is also the matter of international perceptions: despite their admitted expertise at public relations, the Israelis are steadily losing this battle as well because more and more people around the world ­ and their governments ­ are discovering the despicable nature of the occupation.

On the ground, things are even worse.

Morale in the ranks of the South Lebanon Army* militia has never been great, and everyone from Antoine Lahd on down has got to realize that its difficulties today, although legion, are nothing compared to what awaits it in the future.

Even Israels own soldiers have got to be asking themselves the kinds of questions that undermine the cohesiveness of any fighting force. Why are they here? What benefit does the occupation serve for the Jewish state? Who wants to be the last Israeli soldier to die in south Lebanon before the inevitable withdrawal?

Our side may not have the resources to force the invaders out militarily, but it can and has created conditions in which the political costs of staying here are approaching prohibitive levels.

Unlike previous wars, when Israeli leaders had little trouble convincing their people that the military was acting solely in self-defense, the long misadventure in Lebanon has been far more difficult to pass off as anything but an exercise in bullying, especially over the past few years.

Mofaz has little choice but to display the kind of bravado he feigned this week by alleging stinging reversals for the resistance. The need for Israeli military leaders to claim untouchable superiority on the battlefield is almost as deeply ingrained as the obsession with dressing up aggression under the guise of legitimate self-defense ­ and just as fatuous.

1- The Daily Star, 20-11-1999.
* It is an "Israeli" nomination we absolutely reject for it contradicts with Lebanon's independence and sovereignty. This army is not but an "Israeli" agent working for realizing "Israel's" goals in Lebanon.
