Hizbullah gains
moral high ground

    "Amid indications that tension in the south is easing after some of the worst violence in months, foreign diplomats in Beirut suggested on Wednesday that Hizbullah has gained an enhanced reputation from the latest confrontation with "Israel" by refraining from launching Katyusha rockets across the border.
My impression is that "Israel" has been acting outside the April Understanding and Hizbullah has been acting within the understanding, said a European diplomat. Hizbullah has gained the moral high ground by acting with restraint. I think someone needs to stand up and say this on the record.
Another diplomat said: All foreign diplomats I have spoken to are in agreement that Hizbullah has been very wise to confine its operations to the south.
"Israel's" devastating aerial assault on Lebanons power grid backfired, diplomats say, while Hizbullah gained the upper hand.
Hizbullahs threat to retaliate was alone sufficient to achieve complete paralysis in northern "Israel" as the entire population of 300,000 civilians was ordered to flee or sit tight in bomb shelters. Around 80 percent of Kiryat Shmonas population was reported to have left by Wednesday, with economic losses estimated at $2.4 million per day. All this without a single Katyusha rocket crossing the border.
Hizbullah did a better job by not firing the Katyushas and ironically I think the "Israelis" are very upset by this unexpected restraint, said a Western military source.
"Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levys bloodcurdling threat on Wednesday that if Katyushas cross the border Lebanons vital interests will go up in flames sounded particularly hollow. It has become evident now that Hizbullah has no intention of granting "Israel" justification for further air assaults and, instead, is content to retaliate by killing "Israeli" soldiers in the occupation zone.
Some form of "Israeli" military response to the surge of casualties in the south was anticipated, but few expected "Israeli" Premier Ehud Barak to target Lebanons infrastructure. With three power stations lying in ruins and the "Israeli" war machine poised to strike again, Hizbullahs decision ­ presumably encouraged by Syria ­ to refrain from a Katyusha barrage was well-judged.
There was, in fact, only one appropriate and viable response that could restore the upper hand to Hizbullah. It came at 2.50pm on Tuesday, 13 hours after the air raids began, when an anti-tank missile fired by Hizbullah fighters struck an observation turret in the Dabsheh outpost above Nabatieh, killing an "Israel" soldier.
Barak was left in an awkward position. He had justified the air strikes on Lebanons power plants by claiming that Hizbullah has continually broken the understanding by launching attacks from populated areas.
Although both sides routinely flout the understanding, Hizbullahs four attacks since Jan. 25, in which six soldiers were killed, provoking "Israel's" retaliation, were all conducted within the rules.
Hitting Lebanese infrastructure was a decision the "Israeli" premier might have regretted on Tuesday afternoon when he was informed that another soldier had been killed in south Lebanon. Barak appeared to have exhausted his political currency with Mondays air raids against the power plants. Battering Lebanons infrastructure for a second night in a row risked angering the international community, including the US, particularly as Hizbullahs deadly attack on Dabsheh had once again remained within the rules.
Instead, Barak pursued the softer option of retaliating with a helicopter gunship strike against a Hizbullah communications center in Tyre.
By Wednesday morning, "Israeli" warplanes were back to hitting regular, everyday targets in frontline areas.
The conclusions to be drawn from the escalation ­ if it really is over ­ are three-fold.
First, it is evident that it will take some major civilian casualties in Lebanon for Hizbullah to seriously consider firing Katyushas across the border. Clearly, Syria is coordinating closely with Hizbullah on this issue and remains unwilling to risk a complete collapse of peace prospects with "Israel".
The second point is that Syria will not interfere with Hizbullahs campaign against the occupation forces ­ so long as the fighting is contained within Lebanon. Growing calls in "Israel" for Barak to demand a ceasefire in the south as a condition for resuming peace talks are likely to be ignored by Syria.
Thirdly, Hizbullah is taking ­ and will continue to take ­ full advantage of this leeway. The fighters have received TOW anti-tank missiles, with which they have successfully exploited a chink in the armor of "Israel's" heavily-fortified compounds in the south. TOWs were used in both Tuesdays attack on Dabsheh and the Jan 31. attack on the Izzieh position in which three soldiers were killed. The US-made missile has a range of 3,750 meters, more than 1 kilometer further than the Soviet Sagger rocket traditionally employed by Hizbullah. The TOWs improved range and superior accuracy over the Sagger has allowed the resistance fighters to guide the missiles directly into the slit windows of observation posts in "Israeli" positions, killing and wounding anyone inside.
Hizbullahs adoption and skilled use of the TOW has had, and will continue to have, serious ramifications for the "Israeli" Army during its final months in the occupation zone. This suggests that the south is set for further violence in the coming weeks as the pattern that defines the conflict continues its cyclical nature of escalation and lull.
