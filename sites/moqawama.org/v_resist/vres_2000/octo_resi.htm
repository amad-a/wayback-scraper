Views in the Resistance

 

October Tuesday 10 -2000:

Ferzli: air strikes wont sap Hizbullahs support(1)

Deputy Speaker Elie Ferzli affirmed on Monday that any Israeli attacks on Lebanon would not succeed in stripping the resistance movement of the popular support it enjoys.
Israel may believe that by attacking civilian infrastructure in Lebanon it can turn the countrys public against the resistance movement or at least split the public over the legitimacy of resistance attacks against Israel, he told the Voice of Lebanon radio. This is impossible. I also think Israel knows that such a scheme will be fruitless and will only intensify the Middle East crisis. Tension mounted across the Lebanese-Israeli border on Saturday after Hizbullah captured three Israeli soldiers in the disputed Shebaa Farms.
The operation was launched in retaliation for the killing of two Palestinian demonstrators and the wounding of 20 others by Israeli gunfire at Kfar Shouba in the eastern sector of the border area earlier on Saturday.
Tyre MP Ali Khalil, the head of Parliaments Foreign Affairs Committee, hailed Hizbullah for snatching the three Israeli soldiers. This operation will force Israel to consider releasing the Lebanese detainees it holds, he said. Lebanon says Israel is detaining 19 Lebanese, without trial in many cases, while Israel, which says it is keeping the detainees as bargaining chips to learn the fate of Israeli troops who went missing in Lebanon, puts the number at 15.

 

The Daily Star, 10-10-2000.

 

October Thursday 12 -2000:

Lebanese have right to fight for Shibaa Farms, says Assad(1)

 The Lebanese have the right to "fight" to retake the disputed Shibaa Farms from Israeli control, Syrian President Bashar al-Assad said Wednesday.

"The Shibaa Farms are Lebanese land occupied by Israel. The Lebanese people have the right to fight against the occupiers," he said in a meeting with Iran's Foreign Minister Kamal Kharazi, the official SANA news agency reported.

Kharazi also met his Russian counterpart Igor Ivanov and leaders of eight Palestinian groups in Damascus opposed to the autonomy deals with Israel, before leaving for Lebanon.

Israel withdrew its occupation forces from south Lebanon in May, but held on to the Shibaa Farms, located at the meeting point of the Lebanese, Syrian and Israeli borders, as they were conquered in 1967 and not in 1978.

Hizbullah captured three Israeli soldiers in the disputed border area on October 7, and now wants to swap them for 19 Lebanese held in Israel.

Israel seized the farmlands from Syria in the 1967 Middle East war but the area is claimed by Lebanon with the support of Damascus.

October Friday 13 -2000:

 

Damascus supports Hizbullah attacks on Shibaa Farms(1)

Official newspapers in Damascus have denounced the US and other world powers for working to release the Israeli soldiers held by Hizbullah but turning a blind eye to the Palestinians' suffering.  In a clear reference to UN Secretary General Annan, al-Baath said it is strange quote these circles get taken by Israeli allegations that the soldiers' capture constitutes a violation of Resolution 425. Syrian President Bashar al-Assad said Hizbullah operations against Israeli troops in the Shibaa Farms were legitimate because the area belongs to Lebanon and remains under Israeli occupation. Presidential spokesman Joubran Kourieh quoted Assad following talks with Iranian Foreign Minister Kharazzi in Damascus, as saying international law guarantees the right to resist occupation, including in the Shibaa Farms. Israel has held Damascus responsible for Hizbullah operations in Shibaa Farms and threatened what it described as "decisive action" against Syria and Lebanon. Both have warned Israel against any military attacks. A string of foreign diplomats, including Russian Foreign Minister Igor Ivanov, have been visiting Damascus to reduce tensions and help mediate the release of the Israeli soldiers. European Union foreign policy and security chief Javier Solana and British Foreign Secretary Robin Cook are both discussing regional developments in the Syrian capital.

October Thursday 19 -2000:

As you sow

According to press reports, US Secretary of State Madeleine Albright wants Damascus to calm tensions on the Lebanese-Israeli border by using its influence to rein in Hizbullah. In a nutshell, Albright told Syrian President Bashar Assad what he has already heard from Israeli leaders themselves: the Jewish state will hold both Lebanon and Syria responsible for the fate of four Israeli servicemen captured by the resistance in the past couple of weeks.
The approach that Albright and her boss, President Clinton, have taken to this latest set-to has been an extension of general US policy in the region for decades, one of unthinkingly reiterating Israels positions while ignoring those of the Arab world.
This time around, the imbalance could have easily been avoided if US policymakers had asked themselves a simple question: why has Hizbullah chosen the course of action in question? The answer is that the White House gave Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah and his advisers almost no choice in the matter because no help was forthcoming when various Lebanese and Arab voices demanded the release of more than a dozen Lebanese whom the Jewish state has held in prison for years.
Nasrallah expressed his grievances in a conventional fashion shortly after the Israelis pulled out in May: in talks with UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan, he warned that the resistance would not consider Lebanon to have been totally liberated unless and until, among other things, Israel set the detainees free. No UN, US, or EU officials came galloping into the Middle East to press for their freedom, so Nasrallah could not help but presume that his strategy had failed.
Unaccustomed to sitting on its hands, Hizbullahs reaction to this international intransigence could not have been more natural. After all, it is a professional guerrilla organization, so when its attempts to obey the niceties of diplomacy were rebuffed, it reverted to what it knows best: speaking to the enemy ­ and his powerful allies ­ in the only language to which they respond.
Lo and behold, it worked ­ Washington has finally taken notice of Lebanons existence again. Now if we can just convince Clinton and Albright of the radical notion that gross human-rights violations committed against Lebanese are matters worthy of their consideration, the situation might actually be resolved satisfactorily for all concerned ­ including the four Israelis, whose families now have a tiny taste of what some of their Lebanese counterparts have endured for more than two decades.
Lebanon is a peaceful country. Its people seek no quarrel with others, and its military is too small for anything but a minimum self-defense capability. Now Israeli warplanes are plying our skies again, sometimes frightening women and children by blasting through the sound barrier, sometimes lackadaisically ­ and arrogantly ­ over-flying our capital at an altitude that our meager air-defense weapons cannot hope to reach.
This was the last thing that the Lebanese public wanted to see, but the decision was not in its hands, and nor was it in Hizbullahs: the choice was taken in Washington when Americas leaders decided that their expensive ally did not deserve to be scolded for adopting an approach to the detainee issue that would have made Burmas ruling junta proud.
This goes to the very core of Washingtons unconditional (and paradoxical) support for whatever boorish policy Israels government happens to trot out on any given day. When Israel does something for which any other government would receive a tongue-lashing from the White House, the Jewish states neighbors are accused of provocation; when Israeli occupation forces treat Palestinian civilians far worse than British colonial troops ever did Americans, the successors to the worlds first popular revolutionaries warn the victims of oppression to stop making trouble.
China and North Korea can rely on America to remind them when they treat human beings like farm animals. Unfortunately for a few Israeli families, their sons and husbands are now in captivity because America would not do the same for Israel(1)
