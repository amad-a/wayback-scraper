Sayyed: Resistance would continue until all Lebanese lands are liberated(1)

The head of parliamentary Bloc of Loyalty to Resistance vowed on Wednesday that Hizbullah would reject rewards in the form of government positions after spearheading the resistance against "Israel", and instead demanded economic justice on a national scale.
Addressing an extraordinary session of Parliament in Bint Jbeil, MP Ibrahim Amin Sayyed also announced that the resistance would not lay down its arms, vowing to continue targeting "Israel" until all of the Shibaa Farms were returned to Lebanon. The Baalback-Hermel MP lambasted what he said was the governments uneven social and economic development and its failure to begin rebuilding recently liberated areas. Its clear that the governments program is merely fit for a village or town beset by domestic conflict, he said. We want to talk about the greater notion of justice, namely development in Lebanon.
What we want is a comprehensive development plan for all Lebanon, beginning in the resistance South and reaching every resistance village and town in the north, the South and Mount Lebanon, he demanded. Every home in Lebanon is part of the resistance, every village is part of the resistance.
Its completely inappropriate to speak of charitable overtures and mercy when some villages have been completely destroyed, Sayyed responded to government promises of aid to the former "Israeli"-held areas.
We dont want medals placed on the tombs of the resistances martyrs, or on the chests of our fighters. What we want is Lebanon ­ its government, state, people, institutions, parties, free political forces ­ to rise to the level of this victory.
We wont turn our backs to the enemy, and we wont seek out state positions, he vowed.
Sayyed also said that the government was insufficiently protecting Lebanese water sources, saying the solution doesnt lie in providing cisterns.
The government hasnt taken any step toward giving the liberated detainees top priority, he added, claiming that freed prisoners had not been given posts in state-run institutions. And the wounded, who number between 10,000 and 15,000, who is responsible for them? he asked.
Sayyed also said that the resistance would carry on launching armed jihad until the last meter of Lebanese land, including the Shibaa Farms, are liberated.
The resistance movement is not concerned with UN rulings. The Shibaa Farms will be the resistance movements last battleground as long as the Lebanese government continues to claim ownership over the territory, he said, adding that Hizbullah would continue its resistance activities as long as "Israel" occupied Palestine and other Arab territories.
Sayyed also cautioned against showing leniency toward former collaborators.
We hope the state and its bodies will ... deal with this matter not with mercy, but justice. In this case justice is served through punishment, he contended.
Sayyed thanked Syria and Iran for their support for the resistance and attacked Hizbullahs political opponents who, he claimed, tried to undermine the resistance groups victory.
There are political forces that  didnt want victory but rather sought defeat for Lebanon. There is no place for such people after this victory.


Fadlallah overjoyed by absence of predicted chaos(2)

"Israel's" frenzied retreat from south Lebanon last week has ended an era of war and tragedy that is not likely to recur, despite the stalemate in the Middle East peace process, according to Sayyed Mohammed Hussein Fadlallah.
The leading cleric is basking in the glory of victory. As the spiritual father of the Islamic Resistance, he is proud of his sons, who crushed the myth of "Israel's" might, sending the occupation troops scurrying across the border as their militia allies crumbled.
But something is coloring Fadlallahs euphoria. It is the perpetual suggestion that the South would plunge into civil strife, even though the past week has proved wrong all those who had gambled on such negative developments.
Fadlallah spoke in an interview with The Daily Star at his home in Haret Hreik a week after the liberation of the south.
Lebanon has stunned the world. The country which has been branded as a base of terrorism has proved that even in the most sensitive of times, when it is very possible for people to lose their balance, the most civilized humanitarian conduct is being observed by all, he said.
"Israel's" hurried withdrawal, completed on May 24 and coupled with the disintegration of militia, could be attributed to the joint efforts of the people and the resistance, he said.
The resistance pressured the enemy militarily and the people turned up the heat on the political and humanitarian levels, he said. This is why I believe that it was the people of Lebanon who sealed the liberation after the resistance had prepared the atmosphere for it.
The shooting on Sunday in which a Christian in Rmeish was killed was a one-off incident, he said. The ability of both the resistance and locals to swiftly contain its potentially dangerous repercussions was proof of the high degree of discipline among both groups.
I wonder, if there was a similar crush of people in Washington or London, would we have seen the same degree of discipline? Fadlallah asked.
The withdrawal, he said, had come as a surprise to all. But the personal initiatives of senior government officials countered some of the problems that could have surfaced, even though filling the security vacuum proved to be complicated.
Fadlallah was not against sending the security forces, including the army, to the South, but not until certain political conditions had been met to avoid turning the Lebanese into a border police for the Jewish state. The Lebanon experience would make "Israel" think hard before embarking on another adventure, he stressed, even though there were no signs of an early resumption of the Middle East peace process.
This process, he said, was launched to achieve decisive results and would eventually do so. But I dont think the time is ripe for resuming decisive negotiations between Syria and "Israel" and ultimately Lebanon. Not even on the Palestinian track.
This is partly due to the US preoccupation with the upcoming elections and also because the stature of  the enemys prime minister has been shaken by problems within his ruling coalition and as a result of a mounting opposition onslaught, he said.
Ehud Barak rode to office carrying the peace card, Fadlallah continued. His chances now appear dim. But no one really knows whats happening behind the scenes.
Would Lebanons victory serve as an incentive for the Palestinians to revive their own intifada against "Israel"?
Its natural that this formidable collapse of the "Israeli" enemy and its agents has created among the Palestinians a new sentiment that the chances of success for their intifada are no longer as poor as they were before, replied Fadlallah.
But, he cautioned, the Palestinians needed to change their strategy to mobilize public opinion against the "Israeli" government.
It is possible to revive the intifada with new planning and a new strategy following the example of the Lebanese resistance. This would mean staying away from all events that could provoke an international outcry such as explosions in public places, he said.
As for the future options of Hizbullah, Fadlallah decried efforts to always force the resistance show all its cards, without chasing the enemys designs. Its as though were obsessed with weakness. As though we want to rest at any cost. Let the resistance be.
Let the resistance make its threats and let it say it will not disarm until full liberation, he said. Man has to be politically clever and disregard all the political analyses that mislead the weak and spread fear.


Who needs honor?(3)
" Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz is taking measures to support and exonerate himself and his high-ranking comrades. We didn't abandon the South Lebanon Army, he has declared. We left Lebanon honorably, we didn't flee, the IDF's* deterrent capabilities haven't been impaired. So the coopted media has started to praise, exult and glorify this great escape, executed "without a scratch.". None of these praise-makers stopped to ponder whether soldiers and their commanders, who were compelled to behave contrary to the way they were trained, fleeing in circumstances of panic with a sense of abandonment, scratched morale or values.
A few days went by before Hamas leader Ahmed Yassin declared that the Palestinian resistance must deploy Hizbullah methods if it is to succeed. And it was just a matter of hours before a powerful explosive, triggered in a manner reminiscent of the methods of the Lebanese victor, went off near Netzarim, though the blast fortunately didn't hurt anyone.
Yasser Arafat, who was not surprised by the collapse in Lebanon, chimed in. Speaking to a boisterous, celebrating audience, he declared that the adage that Arab lands must be liberated "meter by meter" has been proven anew.
Arabs from "Israel", no less gleeful, traveled to the Lebanon border to wave Hizbullah flags and make "V" for victory signs to their brethren on the other side. They thus furnished further proof of the Chief of Staff's assessment that "Israel's" deterrent capabilities were not impaired.
Major General Amos Malka, Director of Military Intelligence, took an unusual step, issuing a public warning about pan-Arab euphoria that might possibly lead to acts of violent terror. How can this be so, if one accepts his superior's assessment that the IDF's deterrent ability remains intact? Disciplinary action should be taken against the intelligence officer, for rank insubordination (in Major General Malka's favor, it can be noted that politically, he's not in a bad position; about two months ago when the prime minister desperately needed support for a total withdrawal from the Golan Heights, Malka stood by his side, albeit in the guise of a "high ranking officer," declaring "what is the Hermon, after all - it's just a mountain").
As it turns out, justice is on the Chief of Staff's side. The panic-stricken flight did not harm the army's deterrent capabilities in the least. Those capabilities were already gone - first, during the Intifada, and then because of the situation in Lebanon - years before last week's panic-stricken (though honorable) escape.
People who know the Chief of Staff and respect him as a soldier and officer are incredulous. Is this really Shaul? Could it be that the exultation heaped upon him by the Four Mothers group and other defenders of the withdrawal has turned his head, made him lose his balance? Why, those with a clear view of events ask, did the IDF decide to hand over Taibeh, the key army outpost, to the weakest SLA** unit? And why, once it was clear that a withdrawal was in the offing, did the IDF not prepare an orderly pullout, which included the collection of property and equipment? Why were IDF soldiers forced to withdraw by foot, running away as shells blasted along their escape route, prohibited from taking along elementary personal equipment (one soldier was forced to leave behind tradition teffilin phylacteries which had been passed down from fathers to sons in his family for five generations), not to mention more sophisticated and classified army property, and "Israeli" flags?
Who, apart from Ra'aya Hernik, cares about symbols today? Writing in Ma'ariv, Hernik, one of the first to raise the banner of opposition to the involvement in Lebanon, and so someone who would have been expected to join the post-withdrawal celebrations, lamented the indignity of those surrendered flags, and mourned the erosion of national solidarity that the recent events reflected. Hernik recalled a bygone era in which "Israelis" were prepared to give their lives for a cause.
Beyond the role it plays in cultural configurations, "honor" is a strategic asset. An army respected by its enemies has deterrent power. A people, represented by rabbis, learned men, political leaders and even military commanders, who display contempt for honor, generally, and for the national army in particular, evince confusion about their own values, and show doubt about the justice of the course they have followed.
And now, amid cheers from people who only yesterday demanded that he be tossed behind bars, Rabbi Ovadia Yosef himself has risen, declaring, "honor isn't important. It doesn't matter." And the Chief of Staff is being advised to appoint Rabbi Ovadia's son to the IDF Chaplain General post (how could we have failed to think of this earlier?). With all his virtues, Yosef fittingly comes to embody the courageous spirit, the ethos of integrity and truth-seeking, that now animates the IDF.
A few people responsible for the moral and operational fiasco have informed confused, troubled associates that their hands were tied. Maybe that's true. But any man of honor and dignity who anticipated this scenario should have stood up and resigned before it actually came to pass. The fact that the prime minister is ultimately responsible for whatever ominous strategic repercussions stem from the tactical failures does not exempt these officers from any share of the burden for the disgrace and the humiliation.
Somewhere deep in their hearts, some of those foolish, or coopted, supporters publishing praise for Barak know that it was the IDF, not the SLA, that collapsed. This fact must generate some concern among them, as they consider the prudence of the group they so adore

Hizbullah criticizes UN bias, demands just trials for collaborators(1)

Hizbullah Secretary General his Eminence Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah charged that "the UN attitude seems to reek of bias in favor of Israel," trying to pressure Lebanon into making concessions towards "Israel" on border questions.

Speaking at a rally in Haret Hreik to commemorate the martyrdom of 11 resistance fighters killed in operations against the "Israeli" occupation forces during their three-day withdrawal last week, Sayyed Nasrallah said, "These martyrs are the luckiest because they witnessed both the honor of the victory and the honor of martyrdom," drawing loud cries of Allahu Akbar (God is Great) from tens of thousands who had gathered for the event.

The fight against "Israel" will continue so long as one inch of the Shibaa Farms remains occupied, even if the United Nations rules that the "Israeli" entity has indeed implemented Resolution 425, Sayyed Nasrallah declared on Friday.

Hizbullah will "make no compromises," he added.

"As far as Shibaa or any other patch of Lebanese land is concerned, we, Hizbullah, are not interested in the UN resolutions. We have fought to liberate our land unconditionally," he said.

"Pressure, both open and hidden, is being put on Lebanon... but we shall not give up one grain of sand," Sayyed Nasrallah said.

"All Lebanese officials taking part in the negotiations must know that we are with them, that the whole of Lebanon backs them," he added.

"We have been saying the same thing since 1982, that as long as there is occupation, there will be resistance. As long as one inch of land remains occupied, there will be resistance," he said.

"Anything we do not obtain now, we will never obtain. What we have obtained now, we obtained thanks to the resistance. We will not obtain anything later by way of negotiations," he added.

"The situation is calm," said Sayyed Nasrallah, but warned it might not remain so.

"Can the United Nations, the United States or the whole world imagine that we in Lebanon could remain silent about part of our land remaining under occupation on the pretext that that was the result of the line" drawn by UN cartographers? he asked.

While he said it was the government's prerogative to either accept or repudiate the border demarcation by United Nations experts, Sayyed Nasrallah advised people to carefully monitor the process and support the government in its struggle to recover Lebanese territory.

Hizbullah's secretary-general also cautioned against leniency toward militiamen who served in the "Israeli" occupation's militia during the occupation, saying they would have to either be locked up in rehabilitation centers or sent abroad, but not allowed back to southern villages.

He added that the resistance had forced the "Israelis" to change their withdrawal plans, transforming what was supposed to be a "strategic pullout into a chaotic rout."

He warned against postponing critical issues for future negotiations with the "Israeli" entity, including the Shibaa Farms and the fate of detainees in "Israeli" prisons.

"We don't care about the political translation of Resolution 425 or 242, what we care about is that we want the Shibaa farms, which are part of our land," he said.

He advised the Palestinians that they should not count on negotiations if they want to compel "Israelis" to return their land.

"I assure you that negotiations will not lead anywhere," he said, adding that "Israeli" prime minister Ehud Barak "always says that al-Quds (Jerusalem) is the eternal capital of Israel and is not willing to give up one street of it."

Jerusalem, he said, lay at the heart of the Arab-"Israeli" conflict.

He noted that 1967 and 1982, the dates of the "Israeli" occupation of al-Quds and Beirut respectively, were years that were engraved in the memory of all Arabs.

"We're even with the "Israelis" on the second count because our land has been liberated, and we're still waiting for the liberation of al-Quds to erase the memory of 1967," he said.

Sayyed Nasrallah said the issue of the defunct "Israeli"-allied militiamen was delicate and was creating a culture of fear among some pockets of the community. He added that it was the government's duty to assuage these fears.

"The president, the premier, the speaker, MPs and ministers ­ all of them tried to tell these people not to worry," he said.

"But we should be careful not to go to the other extreme, though, and start promising that the collaborators will be released soon," he added.

To do this, he continued, would be a slap in the face to those who had known militia oppression and were counting on justice to be served.

"We've heard of a group of Lebanese lawyers who are planning to defend militiamen members before the Lebanese courts, and it's shameful to find in Lebanon people willing to defend them," he said.

"All militiamen, no matter what their religion, are collaborators in the crimes that occurred, and they should get the punishment they deserve," he added, though he assured his listeners that Hizbullah members would not seek revenge.

"Whenever one of us sees a collaborator, he has orders to inform either military intelligence or the Internal Security Forces," he said.

"We're counting on the judiciary to make sure that no mother sees the killer of her son walking free in the village."

Sayyed Nasrallah cautioned politicians against trying to pressure the judiciary into freeing the militiamen, and said that if any of the collaborators were released they should be barred from returning to their villages.

"Let the government send them abroad, or put them in a rehabilitation center. But don't allow them to go back to their homes," he said.

The spoils of war were on display Monday as Hizbullah fighters toured the country atop Israeli-proxy militia armored vehicles to the cheers of hundreds of thousands.

For Asaf, like thousands of Lebanese of all religious and political stripes, the slow procession of victorious fighters  was both a cause for celebration and unity. Shortly after nightfall, the procession arrived in Achrafiehs Sassine Square, where a brief candlelight vigil was held.

A statement by the Islamic Resistance, Hizbullah's military arm, said the group's guerrillas will carry out a similar parade with the war booty in the port city of Tripoli and other regions of northern Lebanon Tuesday.

The Islamic Resistance had spearheaded the guerrilla war against "Israeli" occupation troops before the Jewish state pulled out its troops from southern Lebanon.
