Report: Hizbullah deploying Katyushas, mortars(1)

 Hizbullah has positioned scores of Katyusha rocket launchers and mortars throughout southern Lebanon recently, according to a report in yesterday's English-language Lebanese Daily Star. The newspaper said some of the weapons had been deployed in outposts abandoned by the IDF* and its now disbanded ally, the South Lebanese Army**, in the May 24 withdrawal from Lebanon.
Hizbullah made clear from the outset that it has no intention of disarming, maintaining that Israel has not fully withdrawn from Lebanon and that it has to remain on guard to "defend the people" in case of "renewed aggression by the Zionist enemy."
If correct, the reports indicate the organization is prepared for cross-border attacks on Galilee communities and, possibly, even as far south as Acre and Haifa's bayside suburbs.
Because there is no longer a security zone, Hizbullah can position its weapons closer to the border, enabling them to hit targets deeper inside Israel.
It has already been widely reported that the organization possesses upgraded versions of the Katyusha rocket, with a smaller warhead but longer range. Such rockets would have been capable of reaching Acre and the bayside suburbs even with the security zone in south Lebanon.
There have also been reports that Hizbullah has continued to receive large shipments of arms and ammunition since the IDF pullout.
Hizbullah established positions and observation posts on the Lebanese side along the entire length of the border following the withdrawal, from where its gunmen have been closely monitoring IDF movements and procedures and civilian traffic.
Coming after the October 7 kidnapping of three IDF soldiers in a Hizbullah ambush on the Israeli side of the border, an abortive infiltration attempt nearly two weeks ago, and a series of other incidents, the reported deployment of rockets and mortars has further raised tension along the northern border.
The Daily Star report follows comments by OC Northern Command Maj.-Gen. Gaby Ashkenazi earlier this week that the IDF is preparing for a possible resumption of hostile activities.
Similar remarks were made by Deputy Defense Minister Ephraim Sneh, who stressed that Israel would hold the Lebanese government and Syria, as the controlling power in Lebanon, responsible for any deterioration in the security situation.
Despite the rumors of war, UNIFIL sources noted that the border has been enjoying its quietest period in 20 years. "It seems that there is an attempt to artificially create an atmosphere of tension. We don't know what's behind this, but it certainly does not help in maintaining stability in the region and along the border," said a senior UNIFIL officer.
Israel Radio last night quoted security sources as saying they doubted the authenticity of the Daily Star report. The sources noted that Hizbullah had developed methods of camouflaging its activities and the fact the alleged deployment of rockets and mortars was being quoted in the press was probably intended to be more of a warning signal.
Western diplomatic sources said last night, however, that there appear to be more than a grain of truth in the Lebanese newspaper report.

Jewish states troops fire warning shots to warn stone-throwers away from frontier(1)

Israeli soldiers fired three warning shots into the air on Sunday to disperse supporters of Hizbullah who were throwing stones at the soldiers from Fatima Gate in Kfar Kila.
No casualties were reported, and the nearly 60 youths dispersed after chanting anti-Israeli slogans, waving Hizbullah banners, and shouting pro-Palestinian slogans for almost an hour.
The crowd also burned US and Israeli flags.
The demonstrators went to the border demonstration in a ceremony during which MP and Hizbullah official Mohammed Raad announced that the Kfar Kila-Aadeisseh road along the border would be renamed for Abdullah Atwi. Atwi launched a martyrdom operation in front of a group of Israeli soldiers at the fortified gate in 1988.
The stone-throwing, which has occurred since Israels May 24 withdrawal from the South, has prompted Israeli soldiers to fire at protesting crowds on several occasions. Since October, three Palestinians have been killed and 16 others wounded by Israeli gunfire.
Mohammed Sobhi, the renowned Egyptian comedy actor and director, was among those at Fatima Gate who threw stones at the Israeli soldiers on Sunday.
Sobhi, who is visiting Lebanon to participate in the International Book Fair, said the resistances achievements should teach Arabs that what was taken by struggle should be recovered by struggle.
Sobhi added that solidarity with the Palestinians was felt among the Palestinian and the Egyptian publics and should exist with their governments.
He said he had honored Hizbullah for three years with his 2000 Road to Peace play, which was performed in Cairo and revolves around the resistances struggle.
Each night after Israels pullout from the South, I gave a special salute to Hizbullah on stage, Sobhi said.
He added that the audience would warmly applaud the special salute to Hizbullah, expressing their solidarity with the resistance.

 

Golan settlements prepare for possible Hizbullah attacks(2)

 Security procedures at northern Golan Heights settlements have been beefed up in response to the kidnapping by Hizbullah of three Israeli soldiers at Har Dov and to reports about the intentions of the Shi'ite organization to continue its hostile activities along the northern border.
Exemplifying the trend, a security team of residents of Moshav Neveh Ativ has been re-assembled, two years after it was dismantled; in addition, bomb shelters in the settlement are staying open and are being kept well stocked in accordance with orders issued by the Northern Command of the Israeli Army.
More stringent security procedures on the Golan have included the shutting down of the Mount Hermon site, although the Northern Command has promised to reconsider this policy as the winter progresses and the ski season begins.
In addition, Golan settlements have been asked to take various steps in the name of added protection. Some have balked; residents of the Nimrod settlement, for example, refused to allow soldiers to stand guard at the entrance to the community.
Ha'aretz has learned that the Northern Command has ordered the residents team at Neveh Ativ to prepare itself along the lines of the emergency squads in communities in the Upper Galilee, along the Lebanon border.
Some 10 residents of Neveh Ativ, most of them newly-discharged from the Army, volunteered to join the squad and will receive M-16 rifles and other types of defense equipment.
Two weeks ago, the members of the squad trained at a firing range and were briefed by Israeli officers.
The four families which live in Nimrod, formerly an Israeli Army outpost that became a civilian settlement two years ago, opposed recommendations for the deployment of guards and the implementation of other stepped-up security procedures.
Since the Har Dov kidnapping, the residents of Nimrod said yesterday, "the army has been on the go. There are more soldiers in the region now."
None of the residents of Nimrod volunteered to be issued with rifles. The IDF has stated that if the need arises, soldiers would be quickly dispatched to provide defense for the small settlement.

Nasrallah opens exhibition to honor fallen fighters(1)

An exhibition honoring 1,280 fighters who died for the resistance was inaugurated by Hizbullah Secretary-General Sayyed Hassan Nasrallah Tuesday.
Entitled Men of Glory, the exhibition features large-scale models of the resistances accomplishments since its beginning in 1982.
Spread over 6,000 square meters in the Ghobeiri Municipality Garden, the exhibition, organized by Hizbullahs central information office, also presents art works by Arab painters under the theme of the Sacrifices of Resistance Fighters for Lebanon.
The exhibitions entrance is a memorial strewn with flowers for people to use in prayers for the souls of the fighters. Next lies a model of a village that was deserted after being subject to constant attacks by Israel. Along with it is the model of a mother clutching her sons bloody body.
One of Hizbullahs military achievements, the storming of the Sojod post in 1998, is also depicted, with the model of a fighter carrying the Hizbullah flag and planting it on top of the former Israeli post.
Ahmad Qasir was the first resistance fighter to stage a martyrdom operation against the Israelis, in Tyre in November 1982. His operation, which killed and wounded about 100 Israelis, is also depicted.
The date of his operation, Nov. 11, has since been dubbed Martyrs Day by Hizbullah and is celebrated annually.
Nasrallah, who toured the exhibition site accompanied by Information Minister Ghazi Aridi, stopped at the model of a former Hizbullah secretary-general, Sayyed Abbas Musawi, who was assassinated by an Israeli helicopter in 1992.
The car Musawi was riding in during the assassination, which is usually on display in his Bekaa village of Nabi Sheet, was also brought in for the exhibition.
The head of the partys central information office, Hajj Mouwafaq Jammal, explained that a team of 80 people planned and set up the exhibition over a period of three months.
Jammal insisted that the only money spent on the exhibition was for materials. Ghobeiris municipality offered Hizbullahs exhibition space for free. Everyone volunteered for the work.
Lebanese, Syrian, Jordanian, and Egyptian artists, upon Hizbullahs request, also donated their work, which was exhibited in five wings.
One stand displays Israeli paraphernalia Hizbullah confiscated following the liberation, including machine guns, bullets, and uniforms.
A painting of Al-Aqsa Mosque in Al-Quds is set behind bars, and another piece on display shows a drawing of a hand attempting to release it.
The exhibition also allowed people to view the Ghobeiri Municipality Gardens for the first time.
Ghobeiri Mayor Mohammed Said Khansa said he was honored that the first exhibition in the area was of Hizbullahs war dead. The Hizbullah exhibit will be on display until Nov. 24.

Lebanon comes to Gilo(2)

The head of preventive security in Gaza, Mohammed Dahlan, and the secretary-general of the Tanzim, Marwan Barghouti, have learned the lessons of Israels glorious withdrawal from Lebanon.
Just a reminder: On the day after the hasty retreat and the irresponsible surrender of the security zone, Chief of Staff Shaul Mofaz and the commander of the escape, Brigadier General Benny Ganz, proclaimed in the face of whatever television camera they happened to come upon, We have not lost a fraction of our deterrent power.
After learning the lessons, Dahlan and Barghouti have explicitly declared that their goal is to emulate Hizbullah and to turn the lives of Israeli soldiers and civilians into pure hell. Barghouti added (speaking on Israel Army Radio, of course) that this campaign will continue until the mothers of Israeli soldiers drive them out of the Al-Quds suburb of Gilo just as they drove them out of Lebanon.
Mofaz was asked on Tuesday why the soldiers who were killed in a shooting incident north of the settlement of Ofra near Ramallah had not been traveling in a bullet-proof vehicle. We do not have a large enough budget to bullet-proof all our vehicles, the chief of staff replied. The commander instinct in him did not lead Mofaz to issue the kind of statement that all combat soldiers need to hear from their supreme commander: Why should we bullet-proof our vehicles? Our role is to pursue and totally defeat the enemy. An army that takes the initiative and launches offensives forces the enemy to flee, to seek protection, to use bullet-proofing.
Instead, the chief of staffs apologetic response and the way the army has conducted itself and operated over the past seven weeks, are conveying a crystal-clear picture: The Lebanese mentality has taken root in our soldiers. From the top brass right down to the lowest ranks. And the dybbuk of bullet-proofing graphically indicates that an entire operative philosophy (or, to be more precise, an entire anti-operative philosophy) has slid down from the mountains of Lebanon and has ascended Mount Gilo.
Israels prolonged presence in Lebanon has generated a number of very negative results. It eroded the will of the nation and castrated its army. The Israel Defense Forces* hamstrung itself and deliberately suppressed the unique fighting spirit that characterized it in its formative years. Instead of attacking and letting the initiative slip through its fingers, the IDF has put most of its energies, especially over the past few years, in defensive actions: Protecting its troops and securing the transport routes leading to its fortified outposts.
This is how the IDF developed its culture of bullet-proofing, of learning how to absorb the enemys blows without losing personnel and of constantly going on the defensive. A few hundred Hizbullah warriors managed to inflict on the IDF the severest kind of damage possible: the transformation of its unique collective personality. Now, because that personality has been transformed, the IDF has reached the point where the few armed bands under Barghoutis command  armed bands that cannot match Hizbullahs prowess  are able to lead the IDF into a perpetual maze, instead of vice versa. Meanwhile, Barghoutis armed bands, not IDF troops, are calling the shots.
Although the IDF has been in a perpetual state of war for the past 18 years, an entire generation of commanders and soldiers has grown up without any real combat experience of the nature of military initiatives, of large-scale offensives, or of decisive battles. All that these commanders and soldiers have been doing has been to drill these three areas of military specialization. Some of these commanders, especially those who have had the most media exposure, seem to never tire of giving the same old explanation  a number of them know how to give it in a highly polished, impressively rhetorical manner  on the advantages of being on the defensive. After all, they know where the glory lies buried and they know that they need this glory in order to go up the ranks.
Is it any wonder that Israeli soldiers  I have heard them express their fears personally  are afraid to knock out the sniper who has been firing directly at them? And why are they afraid? Because they have been warned that, by knocking out this sniper, they could end up setting the entire Middle East on fire. A fair number of top-ranking officers in the IDF deny that the Lebanese syndrome is still alive and kicking. Yet, facts are facts, and even the commission headed by Major General Yoram Yair acquitted senior IDF commanders who had been blamed for the abandonment of a wounded soldier at Josephs Tomb in Nablus  Madhat Yusuf, a Border Patrol officer, who subsequently died of his wounds  and for the rescue episode on Mount Eval. However, the Yair commission did find out who the real culprits were: The government (at Josephs Tomb), for not having evacuated the site two days earlier, and the organizer of the Mount Eval field trip, who deviated from the itinerary that the IDF had authorized. Very few of those who were present at the conference of senior combat officers that was held earlier this week  and that conference in itself is clear evidence of the blockheadedness and callousness that has taken hold of some of the top brass  had the courage to stand up and declare that the findings of the Yair commission are irrelevant. When wounded persons are slowly dying  as was the case in Nablus and on Mount Eval  the commander in the field must assign top priority to getting them out of danger. Only after the rescue has taken place, should time be spent figuring out who was to blame, if blame exists, for the wounding of those persons. Here two questions must be asked: 1. What business is it of the officers serving on the commission to determine when the politicians should have ordered the evacuation of Josephs Tomb? 2. How can the IDFs chief education officer, Brig. Gen. Elazar Stern, explain the differences between, on the one hand, the report on which his own signature appears as a member of the commission and which, in effect, justifies the abandonment of the dying soldier at Josephs Tomb and, on the other, the painful things he wrote in his open letter to IDF troops? And why was the letter placed so quickly in the archives?
During the Mount Eval incident, there were many senior officers in the field, including the divisional commander, and they had a large number of troops from various corps, including armor, plus helicopters, at their disposal. Yet instead of assigning blame to the commanders  this should have been the inevitable outcome of a professional analysis founded on the IDFs value system  for the IDFs badly flawed performance on Mount Eval and certainly for the intolerably protracted process in which the wounded were rescued, the members of the commission heaped the bulk of responsibility on the shoulders of the civilian who had organized the field trip and placed little responsibility on the shoulders of the brigade commander who had authorized the excursion.
Here is yet another failure that can be added to the fiascos at Josephs Tomb and on Mount Eval. Give the IDF the opportunity to win was the wording on the posters at the demonstration that was held after the murder of the two soldiers and two civilians early this week. Unfortunately, the truth is a little different. Although the politicians have hamstrung the army, the army is outdoing the politicians in hamstringing itself  even when there are soldiers and civilians to be rescued.

Qaouk: Hizbullah is a source of stregth for all Arabs & Muslims(1)

Hizbullahs new resistance campaign against Israeli troops in the Shibaa Farms appears to be based on the perceived reluctance of Israel to open a new front in Lebanon, by staging limited and periodic assaults against Israeli troops in the Shibaa Farms, Hizbullah is seeking to sap the resolve of the Israelis without forcing the government of Ehud Barak into launching harsh military reprisals.
In an interview with The Daily Star, Sheikh Nabil Qaouk, Hizbullahs southern commander, said that the resistance did not intend to start a large-scale conflict.
When we took the decision to continue the resistance, we took into account all possibilities, he said. If Israel stages a large-scale attack it will not be to its benefit. Our aim is not to have a wide-scale war. Our aim is to liberate our land and free our detainees.
Qaouk acknowledged that the rules of the past still apply. A severe Israeli retaliation to attacks in the Shibaa Farms which saw the infrastructure targeted would undermine Hizbullahs standing in the country. But it would also guarantee an escalation in the conflict, with settlements and towns in northern Israel coming under mass rocket attacks by Hizbullah. It is this balance of terror that Hizbullah is exploiting.
By resorting to military operations in the Shibaa Farms, Hizbullah is treading a very fine line, in which war or the continuation of the status quo rests on its ability to predict the reactions of the Israeli government.
We know very well the enemys weak points, Qaouk said. The Israelis know that the cost of a war would be very great, more to them than others.
When we stage an operation in the South, this makes Israel weaker and the intifada stronger. When domestic politics in Israel are weak we become stronger. The enemy is very aware that it cant stage a wide-scale attack on the Palestinians or Syria because Hizbullah is (in the picture).
Today Hizbullah is a source of strength for all Arabs and Muslims. (It) is a factor one cant ignore in the regional balance of power. When Israel studies its power and capabilities, it not only examines the armies of Arab states it also has to take Hizbullah into account.
Israel has said that retaliation for cross-border attacks could target Syrian troops stationed in Lebanon. But such a response would only bring the prospect of war with Syria that much closer and still not keep Hizbullah from attacking northern Israel.
Professor Nizar Hamzi, the head of the political science department at AUB, said that attacking Syrian forces would be a futile gesture.
What would the Israelis have to gain by hitting the Syrians in Lebanon? They did that in 1982 but the Syrians came back even more powerful, he said.
The balance of terror remains in the South, in which the weakest is equal to the strongest, Hamzi added. If Israel retaliates to attacks in the Shibaa Farms area by assaulting Lebanons infrastructure then Hizbullah will respond with rocket attacks into Galilee.
The main difference between the resistance efforts against Israels occupation of the South and the new campaign in the Shibaa Farms is the perception of legitimacy.
The Israeli occupation of the South flouted UN resolutions and was internationally recognized as illegal. Even Hizbullahs opponents could not deny the partys right to engage in legitimate resistance against an illegal occupier. For Hizbullah,
such legitimacy was a useful propaganda tool.
But the Shibaa Farms is a different matter. The state was unable to convince the UN that the area was Lebanese and the Shibaa Farms were subsequently separated from the requirements of Resolution 425 by the Blue Line.
As far as the UN and the international community are concerned, attacks against Israeli forces in the farms is an act of aggression, not resistance. Theoretically, this grants Israel justification to respond militarily, with the endorsement of the international community.
But Hizbullah no longer has any use for international legitimacy.
The view of the international community is no indication of whether we are right or wrong. It doesnt change the truth of what were doing, Qaouk said. The international community is a partner in the crime of what is happening to the Palestinians. It adopts double standards. We believe that the only way to regain occupied land is through resistance.
However, Qaouk hinted that Hizbullah was aware of national sentiment and would act accordingly.
Our people are confident in our decisions on their behalf. When we resisted in the South, we resisted both wisely and courageously, he said.
Qaouk stressed that last weeks roadside-bomb attack in the Shibaa Farms against Israeli military vehicles was conducted on land seized in 1970 rather than 1967, when the Golan Heights were occupied. Qaouk hinted that attacks would be confined to the Shibaa Farms.
Hamzi said that Hizbullahs actions in Shibaa were made possible by the intifada in the West Bank and Gaza, the reluctance of Israel to escalate the conflict and the polarization of Lebanon regarding the debate over the Syrian presence.
The peace process has collapsed and the intifada has claimed over 200 lives. In Lebanon, the country is increasingly polarized over the divisive issue of a Syrian withdrawal, he said. Polarization in Lebanon allows Hizbullah to get away with attacks against Israel. If the country were unified then it would be different.
Hizbullahs role as exemplar to Arabs and Muslims in general and the Palestinians in particular means that the party cannot be seen to do nothing while the intifada rages on.
Doing nothing, Hamzi argued, is as dangerous as doing too much.

Hizbullahs South Lebanon commander pledged Tuesday to continue attacks against Israeli as long as Lebanons territory remains under occupation and its citizens are held by Israeli.

Qaouk also said Hizbullah did not recognize the UN-drawn Blue Line along the Lebanese-Israeli border.
We acknowledge neither blue nor green lines, he said. We believe only in lines and decisions that bring back our prisoners and return our land.
Qaouk slammed those calling for Beirut to deploy its troops along the border in the South, saying their intent was to provide protection for the Israeli soldiers.
They want the Lebanese army to act as a police for the occupation on the borders of the Zionist entity, he said.

A grouping of parties insisted Tuesday that resistance operations in the Shibaa Farms would continue, saying any comments otherwise came from suspect quarters.
The parties, among them Amal, Hizbullah and the Syrian Socialist Nationalist Party, met at Hizbullahs Information Center. They said recent aggressive campaigns against the Syrian presence had increased by daring to call for a cessation of resistance operations to liberate the remaining occupied territory, while asking that the UN Security Council help recover it.
Those who support these campaigns, they said, are trying to exploit the socio-economic crisis to implement American-Israeli designs.
A statement issued by the group said the gathering, while strongly denouncing moves undertaken by these suspect parties, reaffirms that it abides by resistance operations as a means for liberation.
Those calling for abandoning resistance operations, it claimed, are in fact advocating keeping the Shibaa Farms under occupation.
The statement affirmed the unity of struggle between Lebanon and Syria, labelling the issue not a subject for argument. It also urged the Arab people to give their continued support to the intifada.

Kfarshouba inhabitants support Resistance action for soil liberation(2)

Inhabitants of the Lebanese southern village of Kfarshouba asserted that they will not renounce demanding for the liberation of a big portion of their village still under Israeli occupation.

During a meeting with Abdel Raheem Murad, MP for the western Bekaa region and Minister of Education, Kfarshouba inhabitants maintained that the only way to regain their land is through resistance operations.

MP Murad, on his side, said that the Israeli threats will not keep the resistance fighters from playing their role, stressing that Shibaa Farms will only be liberated through resistance.
