In the end, they just skulked away. But can the war really be over?(1)

By Robert Fisk in Beirut

23 May 2000

So what was it all for? The almost quarter century of occupation, the two decades of guerrilla war, the dispossession and the thousands of deaths? In just 24 hours, the whole history of southern Lebanon has been shown up as a fraud.

Three of the returning villagers were killed when an "Israeli" helicopter pilot opened fire on a convoy of civilians, another of "Israel's" notorious south Lebanon "mistakes"  like the massacre at Qana, the attack on the Mansouri ambulance, the destruction of an entire family in Nabatieh in 1996, the slaughter of a shepherd's family two years ago.

But the village graveyards are already full of "martyrs" and death is a familiar character in the stony hills of southern Lebanon.

Death has come to Hizbullah guerrillas and "Israeli" soldiers with equal ferocity over the past 18 years. The Hizbullah welcomed death, the "Israelis" feared it. Which is why the Hizbullah have won and the "Israelis" have lost.

And it was all so predictable. The yellow and green Hizbullah banners on the abandoned SLA positions, the dire warnings of "severe reprisals" from the "Israeli" Prime Minister, Ehud Barak, the claims  all too real  of victory by the guerrillas who have been fighting the "Israelis" since 1982.

In just four days, 375 SLA men, perhaps 18 per cent of the active SLA force that has bombarded its fellow citizens, threatened them, cajoled them and  in Khiam prison  tortured them, have defected to the Hizbullah or handed themselves in to the Lebanese authorities.

Yet yesterday, oddly for such a Lebanese war, there was a kind of tolerance about it all. No SLA men were murdered. No Hizbullah army moved en masse into the Shia villages liberated from the "Israelis". The guerrillas were there, of course, often with their guns.

Some say only a handful of "Israeli" soldiers remain; perhaps 140, perhaps 500. The great keep of Beaufort Castle, besieged by Saladin during the crusades, besieged by the Hizbullah for the past 15 years, was rumoured to have been abandoned.

There was gunfire, of course; "Israeli" tanks vainly firing into the wadis and on to the roads to stop the army of civilians from returning to long-lost homes, helicopter gunships firing into the wadis. And three more "martyrs" to the cause of south Lebanon, as innocent as the dead of Qana.

But by last night, almost every Shia Muslim village in southern Lebanon had been liberated from "Israel's" occupation. Only the Christian villages remained.

And there lies the rub. Will they be "freed" as easily as the Muslim hamlets? Will their Christian SLA men be so keen to give themselves up? Is the war really over?
