"Israeli" shelling fuels fires
"A yellow haze hung over Akkar, Mount Lebanon and the south as tinder-dry undergrowth and a strong breeze fuelled dozens of brush fires, destroying huge amounts of cedar and pine forests and olive and citrus groves."

"As if the natural elements were not enough to exacerbate the nation-wide fires, "Israeli" gunners made matters worse. In a calculated attempt to denude the densely forested slopes that provide cover for resistance fighters infiltrating the central sector of the occupation zone, "Israeli" gunners went into action firing phosphorous rounds into Iqleem al-Toffah, sparking a huge blaze."

"Despite the absence of resistance activity in the area, at 2.15pm, "Israeli" guns began shelling the eastern slopes of Wadi Zahrani at the southern end of Arab Salim, with an average of one phosphorous shell exploding on the heights in a ball of soft, white smoke every five minutes. Within a quarter of an hour, a huge fire had broken out, carried northwards by the stiff breeze and casting a thick yellow pall over Iqleem al-Toffah."

"Similar shelling on Monday had already turned vast areas of the hillside into a charred expanse of rock and earth, almost reaching the "Israeli" Sojod post, clearly visible on the ridge above Arab Salim."

Theyve burned the lower slopes and now theyre concentrating on the upper part of the wadi, said "Arab Salims mukhtar, Mustafa Moussa, as he watched the fire spread. They know the resistance uses the undergrowth to creep into the zone so theyre deliberately burning it all, he added.

"Further south, "Israeli" phosphorous shells had ignited the dry brush around Kafra and Yater. The blaze spread rapidly to Majdal Zoun, 10km further west. Hundreds Tyres residents gathered on the beach to watch the blaze on the hills to the east as Nepalese Unifil soldiers struggled with local villagers to contain the fires which, by nightfall, had got out of control."

"By nighfall, brush fires had spread to Mansouri, facing the western sector of the "Israeli" occupation zone, trapping some 4,000 inhabitants."

"The blaze quickly spread to Unifils post in the village, which was promptly evacuated by its Nepalese occupants."

"Looking east from Jbaa in Iqleem al-Toffah, dozens of brush fires were visible as civil defence personnel and soldiers rallied to contain the blazes."

"In Wadi Jenneh, just south of Kfar Melki, a huge fire had erupted out of control, destroying hundreds of olive trees as soldiers looked on helplessly. Several fires had broken out in Qraiye, with one threatening a roadside petrol station. The owner hosed his fuel pumps with water but the fire petered out before reaching the station" (1)
