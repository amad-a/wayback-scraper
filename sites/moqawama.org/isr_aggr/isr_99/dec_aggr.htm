Who owns the northern border?(2)
Last week the army, very late in the day, reached out toward a withdrawal from Lebanon. The chief of staff, the headlines said, agrees to a unilateral pullout.In two or three months' time, and certainly as the date for the withdrawal nears, it will be difficult to remember that the army, or more accurately the chief of staff, was the chief hindrance that caused the decision on a withdrawal to be inordinately delayed.

It is difficult for the army to give up Lebanon, just as it would be difficult to give up any occupied territory. Few armies anywhere have had a comparable opportunity to conduct a war that was cut off from the media and from public criticism for so long. What other army has been given a large tract of land and unlimited budgets to try out all types of combat? From minor tactical warfare to cooperation between armor, infantry and air power to the trial of all of the Air Force's sophisticated weaponry, from the latest warplanes to precision-guided munitions.

Over the course of 17 years, three generations of technology have succeeded one another, and in Lebanon the IDF* has tried them all. Lebanon also provided a free hand for every military man with an idea to try it out in the field. Evacuating villages; damaging the infrastructure; in-depth operations, broad-scale operations and target-specific operations; subcontracting for a local militia; village associations and more. Lebanon, far more than the West Bank and the Gaza Strip, is a deluxe laboratory without settlers, without holy places, without an international commitment to the occupied population - because Israel declared that there was no occupation - and with the entire package wrapped neatly in an agreed motive: protection of the northern settlements. There could be no greater luxury for an occupying army, which need only take care that its soldiers not get killed.

The result is that the war in Lebanon became a habit that is hard to break. And because in Israel the army is the sole arbiter of what is necessary to determine security, anyone who demanded a withdrawal was perceived as a busybody who understood nothing about security, at best, and as a traitor in the worst case. The army thereby acquired the ownership of assets to which it had no title: It determined the consensus on the Lebanon question, it gave orders to the government and thus became not only a polic-implementing body but also an integral element of the policymaking apparatus, and it had the final say on the fate of the settlements in the north.

In an ongoing display of political negligence, successive governments permitted a strategic asset to be whittled down into tactical fragments. Over the course of many years, Israel did not succeed in turning the war in Lebanon into a threat against Lebanon or Syria or in using it to generate momentum for a diplomatic dialogue. Israel squabbled with the Shi'ite villages, used missiles against the cars of Hizbullah leaders, destroyed a few transformers and turned the northern border and its settlements into a no-man's-land, available as a target for every random Katyusha rocket. The fact is that the army, too, now understands that the results of the balance between us and Hizbullah are at best a draw, meaning a loss, and there is no reason to think that a redeployment will be any worse than the current situation.

The IDF, which was unable to impose its will during the past 17 years and therefore did not provide the government with an effective strategic instrument, is now at long last permitting the government to make a policy decision and initiate a move of its own.

 This is precisely the time for Israels next strategic move, one that will translate the war in Lebanon and the withdrawal from that country into political and diplomatic terms. The army will also benefit in no small measure: It will be able to restore its deterrent.

At the time, the coordinator of activities in Lebanon, Uri Lubrani, rejected the Lebanese government claims as "nonsense."

However, some days later, after reexamining Lebanese claims, defense establishment sources admitted that ""Israeli" citizens stole soil from within Lebanese territory without obtaining permits from the army." A South Lebanon Army** officer confirmed the theft of earth from an area within Lebanon, near the border fence. According to a UN spokesman, ""Israeli" trucks regularly transport topsoil from the Adassiya area to "Israel". It is inconceivable that "Israeli" soldiers don't know about it. We are talking about an area in which the "Israeli" presence is not negligible" (Ha'aretz, November 8, 1998).

The chairman of the Metulla local council, Ya'akov Katz, when asked to explain the official process that enabled the transfer of earth from South Lebanon to "Israel", including Metulla, responded, "I am not addressing this issue. Go to the authorities that deal with South Lebanon. I know nothing about it."
Both the IDF and Defense Ministry spokesmen responded laconically when faced with a detailed list of questions and asked to respond to the claims made by "Israelis" that they were involved in transporting soil from Lebanon with the knowledge or permission of the IDF and the defense establishment, as well as claims by Kilah residents regarding the relocation of the border fence westward. "We are not addressing your questions, and all border issues will be discussed in the future between us and the Lebanese," said the Defense Ministry spokesman, while the IDF Spokesman said "all issues connected with the "Israel"-Lebanon border will be discussed in the future."

Yossi Goldberg, a native son of Metulla who headed the local council for 20 years (1978-98) and served in the Knesset from 1988-92, was considerably more forthcoming than his successor and the Defense Ministry and IDF spokesmen.

Goldberg declared decisively that "the IDF never prevented the transfer of earth from the Ayoun valley because it was private land owned by Metulla residents for a century, who have the kushans [a Turkish term for property registration certificates] to prove it." To reinforce the historical connection, Goldberg added that Ayoun is mentioned in the Bible (Kings I and II and Chronicles). The Arabs call the valley Marjeyoun.

According to Goldberg, there used to be no border fence separating "Israel" and Lebanon north of Metulla. The cease-fire agreement signed with Lebanon in March 1949 stipulated that the cease-fire line would run along the international border between Lebanon and Palestine-Mandatory Eretz Yisrael demarcated in 1923 by France and Britain. (Lebanon was under the French mandate, while Palestine-Eretz Yisrael was under the British Mandate.) Goldberg says the border was not defined precisely, and from 1949 until the early 1970s there was no fence.

Then, in the wake of infiltration incidents from what used to be called Fatahland, "Israel" built a fence - the "old fence" - where it was convenient security-wise, but not necessarily along the exact lines of the border. After a truck explosion in March 1985 in which 12 IDF soldiers were killed, "Israel" moved the border fence westward and northward - the "new fence."

"The future location of the border will be determined in the expected negotiations between "Israel" and Lebanon," Goldberg pointed out. "There is no argument that soil was taken from the Ayoun valley, both by Lebanese from areas away from the border and by Metulla residents and others," he emphasized, "but that does not determine the border. I repeat, the earth that Metulla residents took is earth owned by them throughout 100 years of [Jewish] settlement. With the beginning of negotiations between "Israel" and Lebanon, the inhabitants of Metulla will demand their private lands in the Ayoun valley, some 4,000 dunam (1,000 acres) north of the fence. This is land given by the "Israeli" government in 1949 to the Lebanese after signing the cease-fire agreement. The land given in the 1980s as a temporary substitute for agricultural purposes to the farmers of Metulla in the Galilee and the Hula valley cannot replace our lands in the Ayoun valley, and we will not give them up. The border between the two countries must be clearly marked out again".
