"Israel" condemned by truce panel(1)
"The five-nation Monitoring Group condemned "Israel" on Monday for violating the April Understanding by shelling the Western Beqaa last week, killing two civilians and wounding three others.

"The group also accused "Israel" of violating the understanding on six other counts, all for inflicting civilian casualties or damaging property.

"The group, comprising delegates from Lebanon, Syria, France, "Israel" and the U.S., convened in Naqoura at 10am to assess 10 complaints.

"Lebanon lodged seven, all dealing with civilian casualties or damage to civilian property.

The final statement from the group said that two civilians were killed, three others wounded and 11 houses, a shop, two cars and a watertank were damaged when "Israeli" and SLA* militia artillery units shelled Libbaya.

The group considered this action a violation of the understanding and condemned it, the statement said.

"Israel's" protests consisted of two allegations of the resistance staging attacks from civilian areas and one for a mortar round that exploded south of the border two weeks ago.

The group noted that the mortar shell had overshot its target, the Blat outpost, and, although unintended, failed to conform to the provisions of the understanding.

Significantly, the "Israelis" failed to lodge a complaint for the cross-border Katyusha and mortar barrage last week. The "Israeli" Army claimed to have found no evidence that Katyushas had landed in "Israel", although several mortar rounds did explode across the border. However, Saturday, the "Israelis" conceded that one Katyusha rocket had been fired but crashed inside the occupation zone damaging the border fence.

"In conclusion, the group expressed its concern over these recent incidents and urged that the highest priority be given to the protection of civilians and civilian property, as provided in the understanding.

Israeli air aggression targets deep into Lebanon(1)
Israeli warplanes flew 125 kilometers north of the occupation zone on Tuesday to fire two missiles at a cluster of farm buildings beside the Orontes River in Hermel.

The raid, the most northerly staged by the Israelis in at least two years, comes as the Israelis have significantly reduced the amount of shelling against front-line areas in the south over the past few days.

Four F-16 fighter-bombers circled a small hilltop 5 kilometers north of Shouaghir village at around 11.30am before two of the planes swooped low over the Assi Valley and fired a pair of missiles. Several houses were damaged in the blasts and a high-tension power cable was severed. No casualties were reported. An Internal Security Forces patrol inspected the site afterward for unexploded munitions.

The intended target of the raid was unclear. Local residents said that there was once a Hizbullah base in the area but it had shut several years ago. The banks of the Assi, with its numerous riverside restaurants and fish farms, are popular with tourists.

Hassan Hajj Hassan, a local resident, said the raids aimed at intimidating civilians and scaring their children.

Imad Hajj Hassan, the mayor of Shouaghir, condemned the attack and said the district was an agricultural area.

On June 16, the Israelis staged a similar raid a few kilometers south at Ras al-Assi, wounding two civilians. The previous air strike on the area occurred in March 1997.

In 1994, Israels air force struck a training camp in the mountains near Baalbek, killing up to 50 recruits.

Further south, there has been an unusual decline in the number of Israeli bombardments of front-line areas in the past few days. Although resistance attacks are continuing at a steady rate, security sources have noted that the Israelis and the SLA* militiamen in front-line outposts are not responding to attacks with the customary handful of mortar or artillery rounds.

Typically, a resistance attack against a front-line SLA position of three or four long-range mortar rounds will be met with a few rounds of counter-mortar fire from the occupants of the outpost, as well as several 155mm shells from Israeli artillery guns positioned along the border.

Security sources said that on Monday, despite six or seven attacks being staged against both Israeli and SLA posts, no counter-artillery fire was recorded, even though the resistance firing was in most cases from very open terrain.

The decline began following last weeks escalation when the deaths of two civilians in Libbaya led to a cross-border mortar and Katyusha barrage. The two civilians died as Israeli and SLA gunners pounded the western Beqaa following the killing of two SLA militiamen in a double roadside bomb ambush.

Israeli and SLA counter-bombardments regularly cause civilian casualties in Lebanon. Given Hizbullahs repeated warnings that it will retaliate with Katyusha rockets into northern Galilee in reprisal for civilian casualties, the Israeli Army may be considering a more judicious approach to the use of artillery. Certainly, the Israeli Army has been alarmed at the behavior of their militia allies who tend to be less prudent when retaliating against resistance attacks. Furthermore, the June Katyusha attack into Israel was largely provoked by SLA mortar fire wounding three civilians in the same village on two consecutive days.

The Israeli Army may have ordered the militia to sit out resistance mortar attacks, thus preventing the prospect of renewed cross-border Katyusha attacks in the event of further civilian casualties caused by reckless SLA counter-bombardments. The militias front-line posts have been recently re-fortified with ample quantities of reinforced concrete, providing sufficient protection against 81mm and 120mm mortars.

The security sources admit being puzzled at the change of pace, but believe it is too early to say whether the Israelis have altered their policy with regard to the use of artillery.

Flechette shell wreak havoc on Lebanese civilians: Unifil(3)
" "Israel" is still periodically using flechette shells in southern Lebanon, despite the deaths of several civilians and one UN worker from the deadly darts that UN forces in Lebanon (UNIFIL) believe are implicitly banned by international conventions. The shells, each containing between eight and 12,000 five centimeter (two inch) long steel darts, are not expressly named in any weapons convention, a UNIFIL officer who asked to remain anonymous told AFP Friday. "However, the fact that they are not aimed at a specific target but scatter over a wide area, inflicting serious damage, including damage to civilian facilities, puts them indirectly into the category of banned weapons," he said. The 120 millimeter shells are fired by tank cannon and explode at a height of 500 meters (1600 feet), scattering the darts over an area of about one square kilometer (half a square mile), he said. A Norwegian member of UNIFIL was killed in southern Lebanon in 1993, when he was struck in the head by one of the darts. "We protested via the "Israel"-UNIFIL liaison committee. The "Israeli" army suspended their use for a time, but still uses them from time to time," the officer said. Often it is Lebanese civilians who are the victims of the shells, he added. In 1995, three members of one family were hit by darts when a shell exploded over the field they were ploughing in a village outside the "Israel"-occupied zone of southern Lebanon. One son was killed, the father was seriously injured and a second son was also hurt. Last year another man was injured as he stood at the door of his house, and his seven year old daughter was killed. In May this year, two workers with the Lebanese water authority were injured in an orchard. The doctor who operated on one of them in the hospital in Tyre told AFP that it took five hours to remove two darts which had lodged in the man's skull. The victim is still undergoing treatment, and suffers from constant headaches. Since that attack, UNIFIL forces have found holes from the darts riddling the walls of countless houses in the region".

"ISRAEL" ADMITS INVOLVEMENT IN NOTORIOUS KHYAM DETENTION CAMP(1)
"For the first time ever, "Israel" has acknowledged that its security services interrogate, instruct and cooperate with "Israeli" allied militia interrogators in the Khiam detention camp. " At least 140 Lebanese are detained behind these walls in the infamous Khiam detention center in occupied southern Lebanon. Amnesty International and a growing number of local and international human rights groups say torture and maltreatment are systematic here at the hands of "Israeli" occupation forces and their allied militia. Lebanese are held in the center without trial or charge, in clear breach of international law and UN human rights conventions, to which "Israel" is a signatory. The latest arrival to Khiam was Cosette Ibrahim, a young journalist who was kidnapped on September 2 from the occupied village of Rmeish. Reporteurs Sans Frontiers, the Paris-based journalists defense group, said Cosette had reportedly undergone torture and physical and mental abuse at Khiam. Friends and family of Cosette regularly hold protests in Beirut to highlight her plight and the suffering of Lebanese detained by "Israel". Some are suspected of aiding the Lebanese resistance fighting to oust "Israel" from southern Lebanon. A protest was held by an "Israeli" civil rights group yesterday on behalf of Cosette. "Israel" had previously denied any involvement in the notorious Khiam practices, blaming its allied militia for maltreatment. But "Israel's" Jerusalem Post today (yesterday) unveiled that the head of "Israel's" army operations, Dan Halutz, admitted in an affidavit which he submitted to the High Court of Justice last week in response to a petition on behalf of four Khiam prisoners by two "Israeli" civil rights groups, that "Israel" is involved in Khiam. Activists asked the court to allow them to visit Khiam or to release the prisoners immediately. The ministry of defense first claimed it did not run Khiam and was quote not in control of occupied southern Lebanon. Human rights groups did not accept that response and pushed for more explanation. Last week, "Israel" reportedly provided details never before acknowledged, of links between its security services, "Israeli" forces in Lebanon, and the Khiam detention center. "Israeli" army generals said their relationship is based on intelligence sharing and interrogations meant to foil resistance attacks in the south. The head of "Israel's" army operations stressed, "Israel" guides and instructs its allied militiamen, but claimed security agents do not participate in face to face interrogation of Lebanese detainees. That contradicts numerous accounts by freed detainees who say "Israeli" security agents are present throughout their interrogation periods. The senior army officer also contradicted earlier "Israeli" reports that "Israel" did not pay the salaries of jailers at Khiam, saying salaries to "Israeli" allied militiamen in Khiam are directly funded by the "Israeli" army.
