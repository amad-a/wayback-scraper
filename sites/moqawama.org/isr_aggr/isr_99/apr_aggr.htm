Amnesty International: "Israel" Flouting UN Obligations in the Name of Security(1)
"In a statement read on "Israel" and the Occupied Territories today (yesterday) before the Commission on Human Rights in Geneva, Amnesty International reiterated its concerns that over the past year, "Israel" has, in the name of "security", flouted its obligations in human rights treaties it has freely ratified and has ignored recommendations made by United Nations treaty bodies".

"Although the number of Palestinians imprisoned or administratively detained by the "Israeli" security services has decreased in recent years, more than 1,600 Palestinians are still arrested each year.

They have been routinely tortured or ill-treated during interrogation. At least 80 of them are held in
administrative detention without charge or trial".

"In addition, about 130 Lebanese nationals remain detained without charge or trial for up to 13 years in Khiam detention center in "Israeli"-occupied south Lebanon.

"In July 1998, the Human Rights Committee examined "Israel's" human rights record including its use of administrative detention. The Committee found that the present application of administrative detention is "incompatible with articles 7 and 16 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights". These articles prohibit torture and ill-treatment and guarantee the right of every person to recognition before the law.

"The Committee expressed specific concern that Lebanese held in administrative detention "do not personally threaten state security" but are kept as "bargaining-chips". This fact, which constitutes a violation of human rights, has been acknowledged by the "Israeli" Supreme Court.

Moreover, the Working Group on Arbitrary Detention concluded last year that the detention of the 21 "Lebanese held as "bargaining chips" was arbitrary. Nevertheless the "Israeli" government renewed their administrative detention".

"Examining "Israel's" second periodic report in May 1998, the Committee against Torture again concluded that interrogation methods, such as violent shaking, or hooding and shackling detainees to low chairs with loud music playing, constituted torture or cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment and thus contravened Article 1 of the Convention against Torture. Nevertheless, in January this year, "Israel's" State Attorney stated before "Israel's" High Court that these methods were legal.

Many of these human rights violations are officially authorized at the highest level and indeed effectively legalized".

"Without human rights there can be no genuine security and no sustainable peace. Amnesty International requests the Commission to call on the "Israeli" government to immediately cease violations of basic human rights and to apply fully United Nations human rights treaties, as well as the Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949, in the Occupied Territories".

"Israel's" quiet border invasion in the south(1)
""Israel's" repeated claim that it has no territorial ambitions in Lebanon will be put to the test when a withdrawal is eventually carried out, as the "Israeli" Army has quietly shifted the international border northward in six separate locations since 1978".

"While attention has focused on the military occupation of the south, the "Israelis" have built self-styled technical fences inside Lebanon to a depth of several hundred meters each".

"The new boundaries are similar to the genuine border, consisting in most cases of dirt tracks between 3-meter-high fences. The incursion zones are well documented and are described by the United Nations as Permanent Violations of the 1949 Armistice Agreement.

"They stretch from near Yarin in the western sector of the zone to Jabal Rbaa Teben between Shebaa and Khiam in the east.

"The aim of all but one of the incursions is to allow the "Israeli" Army to dominate the high ground which falls just inside the Lebanese border.

But in the case of a small incursion at Wazzani, the "Israelis" have seized the numerous springs that gush out of the hillside east of Khiam and run into the Hasbani River. The water is reputedly pumped across the border in tunnels to "Israeli" settlements in northern Galilee and the occupied Golan Heights.

"The strategic use to which the "Israelis" have put these incursion zones is clearly demonstrated at the southern end of al-Marj, the fertile plain between Marjayoun and Khiam.

"The "Israelis" have built an outpost on a small hill beside the village of Sarda. The outpost has views over much of al-Marj to the north and west and the Hasbani River valley to the east. The international border runs around the southern end of the hill. But the "Israelis" have created a new
boundary around the northern side of the hill.

"The technical fence continues eastward across the southern section of al-Marj, just above the old
British airfield, before ending at the road between Kfar Kila and Marjayoun.

If the "Israeli" Army withdrew to the international boundary, the topography of the border area would obstruct the views presently gained from the Sarda position.

"This is even more pertinent in the incursion zone the "Israelis" have constructed on a range of hills from Adaisseh to just south of Houla. Five hilltop posts have been built with unobstructed views of south Lebanon. The technical fence runs along the upper slopes of the western ­ or Lebanese ­ side of the hills. The international border, however, follows the steep and wooded eastern side of the hills.

"In the event of a withdrawal to the border, the "Israeli" Army would not only lose the strategic high ground to Lebanon but face ­ for them ­ the unpalatable prospect of having Lebanese troops overlooking the whole of northern Galilee.

"Consequently, if a general withdrawal to the border is ordered, some observers believe that the "Israeli" Army will argue strongly for the retention of the incursion zones to protect the northern settlements.

The border is still there. No one can hide it. But I cant imagine that the "Israelis" wont attempt to hang onto the technical fences if they think they can get away with it, a security source said.

"Israeli" Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu hinted at the possibility of maintaining the incursion zones last month when outlining to the Knesset the provisions for a pullout.

Security arrangements that are required by us for the purpose of a withdrawal from Lebanon would... have to include arrangements on the ground and along the border line that would protect us, he said.

According to Mr. Netanyahu, the "Israelis" could not rely on promises from Lebanon or any other party to secure the border area following a withdrawal.

Therefore it would be necessary to carry out physical security arrangements along the border line, he said.

"These physical security arrangements would include electric fences, minefields, surveillance equipment, foot patrols and outposts.

But the military effectiveness of such arrangements would be weakened if the "Israeli" Army relinquished the strategic incursion zones.

"Last month, the "Israeli" Armys intelligence branch assessed that Hizbullah would continue attacking the military after a unilateral withdrawal because the organization claims that outposts built by the "Israel" Defense Force* along the international border are located on Lebanese territory.

"But most of them are on Lebanese territory and have long been recognized as Permanent Violations by the Observer Group-Lebanon part of the UN Truce Supervision Organization.
Indeed, the groups observation post in Markaba is located beside the technical fence of the
Adaisseh-Houla incursion.

"The findings of the intelligence assessment suggests that the "Israeli" Army has already decided to retain the incursion zones after pulling out from the rest of the south.

As far as Hizbullah is concerned, the "Israelis" can expect no respite from guerrilla attacks if it decides to hold on to the incursion zones.

Our position is clear. Not one "Israeli" soldier is allowed to remain on Lebanese soil, said Sheik Nabil Qaouk, commander of Hizbullah forces in the southern sector".
