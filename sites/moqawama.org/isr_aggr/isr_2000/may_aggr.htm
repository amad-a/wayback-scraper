Israel backed off from further aggression against Lebanon Friday despite a new Hezbollah rocket strike on the north of the country.

Israel's public radio said the security cabinet had decided not to launch further attacks on Lebanon following the raids early Friday, but reserved the right to hit back at the appropriate moment.

 

Israel did not want to unleash a military escalation in Lebanon, after its air attacks on power facilities in the wake of a Hezbollah rocket strike Thursday, an unnamed senior official was quoted as saying.

 

The radio said members of the inner cabinet chaired by Prime Minister Ehud Barak were satisfied with the air raids, considering they had served their purpose of "punishment and dissuasion."

Thursday's Hezbollah rocket attack, itself in retaliation for deaths and injuries of civilians in southern Lebanon in air raids and shelling by Israel and its militia allies, killed one soldier and wounded another and injured 26 civilians in the northern town of Kiryat Shmona.

 

Friday's strike, which targeted Kiryat Shmona and the northwestern locality of Shlomi, caused one slight injury in the latter place.

The raids were the third against power plants in a year.

Three transformers at the Bsalim power station, northeast of Beirut, were completely destroyed while three others sustained serious damage but might be repairable pending further reports, according to Electricite du Liban.

Meanwhile, Israeli bombers blasted the Deir Ammar power station in Baddawi, north of Tripoli, to rubble, leaving Akkar, Baalbek and Hermel in total darkness.

Its still premature to estimate the extent and cost of damage before the submission of a comprehensive report (of Bsalim and Deir Ammar), Electricity and Water Resources Minister Suleiman Traboulsi said during an inspection of the Bsalim plant. The station is now non-operational and has been isolated from the network, he added.

Meanwhile, Public Works Minister Najib Mikati confirmed that as in the past, reconstruction will take place immediately, bringing things back to normal.

He also said that generators would be supplied to ensure the distribution of drinking water through the networks.

Even by midday, smoke and steam jetted out of the wrecked transformers, leaving repairmen wary of starting their work.

They could go up in flames at any moment, said one, pointing out that the wreckage still contained boiling oil.

Beirut makes case for Shebaa (8)

Lebanon submitted documents and maps on Friday to prove to UN experts that the Shebaa Farms were part of Lebanese territory before they were captured piecemeal by Israel over the past two decades.

The second half of the meeting between UN experts and government officials at Baabda Palace was attended by President  Emile Lahoud, Premier Salim Hoss and UN Middle East envoy Terje Roed-Larsen. The presidents participation was said to have contributed highly to consolidating the Lebanese stand.

As Israel plans to withdraw its troops from the south, Beirut is demanding that the Jewish state relinquish the fertile Shebaa Farms.

The Lebanese delegation included the head of the Surete Generale, Major General Jamil al-Sayyed, and one of Hoss foreign-affairs advisers, Yehya Mahmasani. Among the UN delegates was topography expert Miklos Pinther, to whom the Lebanese presented seven arguments, supported by documents, to counter UN claims:

All ownership deeds in the various Shebaa Farms are Lebanese, issued and stamped by the Lebanese government.

A Cabinet decision was issued in 1948 saying that the state owned 3 million square meters in the farm of Machhad al-Tayr. The decision was made following judicial discord between Lebanon and the Sunni religious court. The decree contradicted a decision by the Sunni religious authority that described the land as religious endowment property. Proving that such a rift actually existed, the mufti of the republic, Sheikh Mohammed Qabbani, asserted on Friday that the Islamic authorities own 3 million square meters in the Shebaa Farms.

The Lebanese delegation refuted the Israeli argument that Shebaa was occupied in 1967 ­ that is, during the Arab-Israeli war ­ which means that a settlement on the issue would have to be part of UN Resolution 242. Beirut argued that the area of Bastara, within the Shebaa Farms, was invaded much later, in 1989, leading to the displacement of more than 30 families.

Lebanese officials asked that if the Shebaa Farms were considered Syrian or were the subject of conflict between Syria and Lebanon, why hadnt UN experts questioned Syria about it?

According to political sources, Syrian Foreign Minister Farouk al-Sharaa asserted to Hoss that Syria was convinced that the Shebaa Farms were Lebanese territory. However, Syria would only express this stance if asked about the ownership of the disputed farms, the sources said.

The Lebanese delegation argued that even though Beirut did not make its claim until recently, that fact did not alter the fact that it was Lebanese land.

The Lebanese officials said that during the occupation, Lebanon avoided raising matters related to the Israeli border. These issues were left for when Israel would plan to fully withdraw, which did not occur until this year. When Israel started saying that it would pull out in accordance with Resolution 425, it became Beiruts right and duty to ask for every inch of Lebanese territory, the delegation argued.

This point was raised following remarks made by a senior official from UN forces in the Golan, who attended the meeting and argued that UN forces had always dealt with the Shebaa Farms as Syrian territory.

The delegation presented a military map in which the Shebaa Farms were included, contradicting maps UN experts have submitted that did not mention the farms. The military map was printed in 1966 on the basis of an earlier one published in 1945.

The delegation argued that a Lebanese-Syrian committee, formed in 1949, had officially recognized the Shebaa Farms as Lebanese territory. The delegation supported its account of the committees findings with official notes from the Syrian Foreign Ministry at that time.
