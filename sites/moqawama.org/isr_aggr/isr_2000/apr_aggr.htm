Israel premier backtracks on pullout plan (1)
He decides to redeploy and keep parcels of Lebanon
Under pressure from the Israeli Army and residents of northern Galilee, Ehud Barak has reportedly reversed an earlier decision to stage a full troop withdrawal from the south to the international border and now intends to retain key parcels of land on the Lebanese side of the fence.
If true, the Israeli premiers about-face comes despite having received clear warnings from international observers that such a policy would be considered by the Lebanese as a redeployment rather than a withdrawal and would invite continued resistance assaults.
Baraks new assessment on the extent of a withdrawal reflects growing unease in the region following the failure of the Geneva summit to resuscitate direct peace talks between Syria and Israel.
Last weeks mood of optimism has turned into a gloomy expectation of violence in south Lebanon and has even raised the specter of war between Israel and Syria ­ factors that could have contributed to Baraks reported change of mind.
His volte-face was reported on Friday by Nahum Barnea, a respected and influential columnist in Israels Yediot Ahronot.
According to Barnea, the sections of the boundary fence which have been pushed northward and westward to capture the strategic high ground and provide a buffer for settlements that abut the border will be kept in place. Military outposts which were established on the fence and a little deeper into Lebanon will also stay.
The proposal to withdraw to the military boundary ­ the so-called Purple Line ­ was the cornerstone of the Armys Morning Twilight plan for a unilateral withdrawal. Barak reportedly vetoed this component of the plan when it was presented to him last month on the basis that it could provide justification for Hizbullah to continue targeting the Israelis.
But the Israeli Army believes that Hizbullah ­ or other Lebanon-based groups ­ will continue to mount operations against Israel, even if a full withdrawal to below the international border is conducted. Therefore, the reasoning goes, if the fighting is to continue, why not maximize the strategic opportunities offered by redeploying to the Purple Line and at the same time defer the considerable cost of moving the fence, outposts and security equipment to the genuine border.
Furthermore, some houses on the western edge of Misgav Am have actually been constructed on Lebanese soil. The residents of these houses would have to be evacuated if the Israelis pulled back to the international border. Hizbullah, the argument continues, could hardly justify attacking Israeli troops over a paltry few meters of land especially when Barak had declared Resolution 425 fulfilled and pulled out of the rest of Lebanon. A firm retaliation would consequently be warranted if Hizbullah was rash enough to continue attacking Israeli troops.
A final withdrawal to the international border could be carried out in line with security arrangements reached in any future peace deal between Lebanon and Israel.
This argument has apparently won Barak over.
The entire concept, however, is based on a dangerous fallacy.
Although Hizbullah has traditionally remained tight-lipped over its designs toward Israel following a withdrawal, observers believe that the party has no intention of carrying the conflict across the border. Recent statements by its leaders suggest that the party is preparing for a less bellicose campaign against Israel: resisting normalization in the post-peace era. Hizbullah supporters are more likely in the future to be found picketing the Israeli Embassy in Beirut rather than shelling Kiryat Shemona.
However, this scenario is dependent on Israel staging a total withdrawal from Lebanon. Hizbullah has clearly stated on many occasions that it will continue attacking Israeli forces as long as they remain on one inch of Lebanese soil.
Therefore, the Israeli Armys desire to keep parcels of Lebanese territory simply guarantees bringing the conflict to the border itself. The Israeli government has warned that it will
respond severely to any attacks on its forces or civilians following the withdrawal ­ possibly directing its retaliation to Syrian military targets in Lebanon.
A forceful Israeli retaliation to cross-border assaults probably would be considered justifiable in the eyes of the international community. But the same cannot be said if Israeli troops still remained in Lebanon, albeit on a few meters of land.
The US, the UN and the rest of the international community agree that Resolution 425 entails a withdrawal to the international border. It will not suffice for Barak to claim 425 has been fulfilled and expect UNIFIL to deploy in the vacated areas and secure Israels northern border when Israeli troops are still manning outposts in Lebanon.
One can only speculate about why the Israeli Army is so adamant on adhering to the Purple Line when the move is such an obvious recipe for a serious escalation of the conflict which could drag Israel back into the quagmire it created in Lebanon.
Given the Israeli Armys reluctance over the whole issue of withdrawing from Lebanon one could almost believe that the defense establishment is deliberately engineering this scenario so it can continue running its private fiefdom in Lebanon for a little while longer.

Levy tries to sell Annan on "Israel's" 425
Defiant Sneh: We will decide what is the border(1)

""Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levy said on Tuesday that the Jewish state was prepared to cooperate fully with the United Nations over a withdrawal of forces from south Lebanon in line with Resolution 425.
But a unilateral withdrawal will be conducted according to "Israel's" interpretation of 425. This entails retaining a few military outposts on Lebanese territory along the border, according to deputy defense minister Ephraim Sneh, a move that is unlikely to win the support of either the UN or the international community.
Levy traveled to Geneva on Tuesday to hold a meeting with UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan to formally declare "Israel's" intention to withdraw in one go from Lebanon by July.
"Israel" is ready and willing to cooperate with the United Nations in order to set up together a mechanism in the field itself that will bring about the implementation of the resolution, Levy said after the 90-minute meeting.
He added that "Israel" was acting in its own interests and would continue to protect itself.
"Annan said that the UN and "Israel" were in agreement that cooperation was necessary during the withdrawal phase to prevent a possible deterioration. The minister informed me that the withdrawal will occur in one go by the end of July, Annan said. I will discuss this with the Security Council. We are doing our own contingency planning to determine if the force needs to be reinforced, and how.
"Israel's" Haaretz newspaper reported on Tuesday that the UN, France and "Israel" were attempting to update Resolution 425 to clarify the role of UNIFIL following an "Israeli" troop withdrawal.
But UNIFIL spokesman Timur Goksel rejected the possibility of any tinkering with the 22-year-old resolution.
There are no efforts to work on a new resolution because it would bring untold complications, he told The Daily Star.
The present wording calls on UNIFIL to confirm the withdrawal of "Israeli" forces, restore international peace and security, and assist the government of Lebanon in ensuring the return of its effective authority in the area. Lebanon and Syria would object to any changes in 425 that could grant "Israel" leeway in not staging a full withdrawal to the international border, as is presently demanded by the resolution.
"But it is becoming increasingly apparent that "Israel" will not fully withdraw to the international border if a unilateral pullout is ordered.
Instead, seven outposts will be kept ­ three straddling the border and four on Lebanese soil. The outposts are located in areas where the border fence has been pushed northward and westward into Lebanon, allowing the "Israelis" to dominate the high ground and establish a narrow protective buffer for settlements that abut the frontier. The de-facto frontier is known as the Purple Line.
The move was confirmed on Tuesday by Sneh, who said that "Israel" reserved the right to keep the seven posts. He said that in the absence of a peace treaty with Lebanon, there is no party that can judge what is the border ­ we will decide from where to withdraw.
The "Israeli" Army ­ which has consistently opposed a unilateral withdrawal ­ fears that abandoning these parcels of Lebanese land will jeopardize the safety of the border settlements if no prior security arrangements are reached with Lebanon and Syria. But retiring to the Purple Line would not constitute a full withdrawal as ordained by 425, only a redeployment.
Hizbullah has repeatedly said that it will continue attacking "Israeli" troops until the last soldier has departed the country. In this event, the UN would probably announce that 425 had not be fulfilled by "Israel" and UNIFIL would not deploy in the areas vacated by the "Israeli" Army.
But Sneh downplayed the likely backlash from the international community, arguing that countries willing to support "Israel's" pullout would not be deterred by "Israel" remaining inside a tiny border area.
Not every "Israeli" minister shares Snehs confidence.
The Europeans told me very clearly that a precondition for their assistance  is the withdrawal of "Israel" to the international border and not to any other line, said Justice Minister Yossi Beilin, a long-standing supporter of a unilateral withdrawal".

Lebanon asks UN if it will disarm Palestinian camps(1)

Lahoud: It is Resistance that forced "Israel" to withdraw

 views in resistance

"Lebanese President Emile Lahoud Wednesday set out to the United Nations his country's concerns about its security in the aftermath of an "Israeli" withdrawal from the southern border strip.

In a "presidential memorandum" addressed to UN Secretary General Kofi Annan he asked if UN peacekeepers would be prepared to disarm the Palestinian camps in Lebanon before deploying on the border, and how they would prevent further "Israeli" attacks.

The three-page document contained eight points dealing with the proposed international force that is to help the Lebanese government restore its authority in the border strip that has been occupied by "Israel" for 22 years.

Lahoud made it clear that Lebanon wants all the questions answered before it is ready to discuss in detail the conditions for the pullback which "Israel" has pledged to carry out by July.

"If certain Palestinian groups tried to carry out operations across the border in the context of their right of return and in the absence of any solution for their future, do you think the international force will be able to carry out daily small-scale military operations at the border???" the document asked, underlining Lebanese concern by using three question marks.

It said that the 1978 "Israeli" invasion was "provoked" by Palestinian refugees, and asked whether, in the light of "previous experience," Annan would not consider it in Lebanon's interest "for the international force to disarm the Palestinian camps, or to take part on the ground in such an operation ... before deploying at the border."

Some 350,000 Palestinians live in Lebanon, nearly half of them in the 12 camps scattered through the country. The Lebanese constitution forbids their permanent settlement.

The document pointed out that UN resolution 425, which calls for an "Israeli" withdrawal, also states that the task of the international force is to "assist the government of Lebanon in ensuring the return of its effective authority in the area."

"Is it possible to recover this authority without disarming the camps?" it asked.

The document also raised the issue of possible future "Israeli" "aggression" against Lebanon, or violations by it of UN resolutions, and asked whether the UN force will have the means to deter "Israel" attacks by land, sea or air.

The document also put the case for war reparations, which Lebanon has estimated at 70 billion dollars for loss of life and moral and material damage.

"Who has decided to exempt "Israel" from the compensation due to Lebanon and its people? ... On what grounds has it been forgiven?" it asked, pointing out that "Israel" has only decided to withdraw in the face of attacks by the Lebanese resistance forces.

Lahoud presented the memorandum to Annan's envoy Terje Roed-Larsen, who came to Beirut to brief him about Annan's meeting in Geneva Tuesday with "Israeli" Foreign Minister David Levy.

Copies of the memorandum were also distributed to the press.

Roed-Larsen described his meeting with Lahoud as "excellent." He said he had given the president a written message from Annan about his talks with Levy.

He is scheduled to meet parliamentary speaker Nabih Berri and Prime Minister Salim Hoss Thursday before going on to Damascus.

"Mr. Annan wanted to sent Mr. Roed-Larsen quickly not only to inform Beirut and Damascus about "Israel's" position on its withdrawal from southern Lebanon, but also to find out the Lebanese and Syrian reactions to the question," a top UN official told AFP, asking not to be named.
