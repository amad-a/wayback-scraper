"Israeli" group acknowledges occupation's "war crimes" in Lebanon(1)
An ""Israeli" human rights group" accused Israel on Sunday of rights violations that "constitute war crimes'' in its occupation zone in south Lebanon.

The B'Tselem group 67-page report said Israel "flagrantly and continuously violates fundamental human rights of Lebanese civilians'' either directly through its own armed forces or indirectly through its so-called South Lebanon Army (SLA) militia allies.

Among alleged violations it cataloged were:

-- the extra-judicial detention and torture of Lebanese by the SLA at the militia's notorious al-Khiam jail

-- the abduction of Lebanese and their imprisonment without trial in "Israel" where they are held as "bargaining chips'' for information on the fate of missing "Israeli" soldiers

-- the expulsion of residents from the zone by the SLA and its practice of forcible conscription, including of minors

-- indiscriminate firing in violation of the rules of war and the use by the "Israeli" army of weapons such as phosphorous shells and flechette shells that fire thousands of darts in circumstances that have killed and wounded civilians

"These violations, whether committed directly by Israel or indirectly through the SLA, constitute war crimes under international humanitarian law,'' the B'Tselem report said.

It noted that the international war crimes tribunal in The Hague had held Yugoslavia legally responsible for atrocities against civilians committed by Serb militia during the war in Bosnia even in the absence of direct orders.

"Employing the court's criteria to the relations between Israel and the SLA leads to a similar conclusion,'' the report said.

The SLA is armed, financed and trained by "Israel".

B'Tselem said the "Israeli" long-running domestic debate over the military presence in Lebanon had focused primarily on the risk to soldiers' lives and the security of northern residents, ignoring almost completely the suffering of Lebanese civilians.

"Human rights of Lebanese civilians, even recognition that Israel maintains a violent and prolonged occupation, are not part of the collective "Israeli" consciousness,'' it said.

Better late than never: village takes joy in end of occupation(2)
When Sraireh Mukhtar Salim Kiwan locked the swing gate behind him and returned the key to the SLA* militiaman in the adjacent Kfar Houna outpost last Wednesday, he little imagined that it would be the last time he would ever have to perform the onerous chore.
The next morning, residents of the Jezzine village arrived at the gate to discover the outpost in ruins and the SLA gone. It was as if wed been in jail and now were free, said Kiwan.
Sraireh, the only Druze village in the district, is tucked among the eastern folds of Jabal Abu Rashed, 13 kilometers southeast of Jezzine.
For over 19 years, the village remained largely forgotten as the 100-odd residents, most of them members of the Kiwan clan, quietly tended their olive groves, ignoring the battles between the resistance and the occupation forces among the surrounding craggy hills and wadis.
The roads running north into the western Beqaa and east toward Hasbaya have remained blocked by earthen embankments and land mines since 1985. The villages only lifeline to the outside world was via a narrow, crumbling road that terminated at a swing gate beside the Kfar Houna position, overlooking the main route between Jezzine and Marjeyoun.
For the residents of Sraireh, the simple red- and white-painted gate became their greatest source of irritation and a symbol of the strictures imposed by the occupation. It was our main problem, said Kiwan. Every time we wanted to leave the village we had to go up and fetch the key from the militiamen in the outpost, unlock the gate, drive the car through, lock the gate and return the key.
The SLAs decision to abandon the Kfar Houna outpost and redeploy to Aramta, 4 kilometers to the south, came as a complete surprise to the residents of Sraireh. The move also freed the nearby hamlet of Qotrani, a tiny cluster of single-story stone buildings.
When we saw the militia had left, we smashed the lock and jammed the gate open, said Kiwan.
It is perhaps illustrative of Srairehs seclusion that the most irksome element of occupation for the residents was the hassle of having to open and close a gate. Nearby villages in Iqleem al-Toffah and the western Beqaa suffer regular infrastructure damage and the occasional loss of life from the daily bouts of violence. But other than damage to two houses from a mortar shell in 1994, Sraireh has emerged from the occupation remarkably unscathed.
Our village was stuck in the middle of the fighting. The shells always flew over our heads in both directions, said Khalil Kiwan, the villages school teacher.

"The SLA once manned a position in an old quarry at the entrance of the village, but the post was abandoned in 1987, after which the occupation forces were rarely seen
We never saw them here. They were too scared to come as we are far from the safety of their positions, said resident Wael Kiwan.
Srairehs location at the hub of routes between the western Beqaa, Hasbaya and Jezzine once helped it attract dozens of students from neighboring villages. Today, although the village school can seat 500 pupils, only one student attends class, 6-year-old Maher Kiwan.
Khalil Kiwan said construction on the present school finished in 1995 in anticipation of the occupation ending and the school filling with pupils.
Asked if he missed having friends at the school, Maher gave a doleful nod of the head and continued scribbling his lessons on the chalk board.

The prime minister is especially interested in reports of the establishment of a new land register and the drawing of new borders, officials said.
Hoss launched the inquiry after the National News Agency reported that "Israeli" engineers had been seen surveying the villages of Rmeish, Yaroun, Alma Shaab, Maroun al-Ras, Yarin and Ramieh. The NNA said metal-surveying stakes had been seen planted in the ground. The "Israelis" kept civilians away from the surveyed areas, the agency said.
"The report came days after "Israel's" chief of staff, General Shaul Mofaz, spoke of a new border existing between the two countries following a troop withdrawal from the south. His comments were instantly condemned by Hoss, who insisted that the border was inviolable ­ except where it allowed the return of the so-called Seven Villages incorporated into Palestine in 1923.
"The "Israeli" Army subsequently denied that Mofaz had meant the international border would be redrawn. Instead, he had referred to the border once again becoming the frontier between the two countries after the northern limit of the occupation zone had served as the de-facto boundary.
"The Seven Villages cropped up in an interview with Hizbullah deputy secretary-general Nahim Qassem on Friday.
"He said that the villages were Lebanese and their return remains a key demand. We consider them an integral part of Lebanon, he told Radio Monte Carlo. This will be a long struggle which requires all efforts and which we shall follow with all the strength we have.
Qassem said that "Israel's" frustration with Hizbullahs resistance attacks was driving it to retaliate against civilians. He warned that Hizbullahs patience with attacks on civilians was wearing thin. Were committed to the April Understanding. The "Israeli" enemy is the one which repeatedly breaks this understanding, he said.


"Israeli" collaborators sentenced to death for espionage(2)
"A former Amal official was sentenced to death by a military court on Friday after being convicted of carrying out espionage for "Israel" that led to the killings of resistance leaders.
In addition to former army First Adjutant Hussein Olayyan, 37, his wife Rola, 28, and mother Khadija Mroue were sentenced to two years and one year in prison with hard labor respectively. They are all in custody.
"His father Ali, brother Hassan, and cousin Youssef Musa Siblini were each sentenced
in absentia to 20 years with hard labor.
All sentences can be appealed.
Information delivered to "Israel" allowed agents working for the enemy state to carry out a roadside bomb attack that killed Hizbullah official Khodr Salameh in Hlalieh near Sidon in July, said the courts presiding judge, Brigadier Maher Safieddine.
Alayyan also delivered to "Israel" information about other Hizbullah officials, including those who run the groups Nour Radio and Manar Television, said Safieddine.
While Alayyans father, brother and cousin helped collect the information, his wife and mother went to "Israel" to communicate between Alayyan and the "Israeli" agents, the judge said.
Alayyan, his wife and mother were arrested a short time after Salamehs killing. Salameh masterminded Hizbullah attacks on "Israeli" troops and their agent in the occupation zone.
Before the rulings were handed down, the judge had listened to closing arguments by Assistant Military Prosecutor Myassar Shokor and defense lawyers Riad Matar, Fadl Hajj and Hayat Abdel-Khaleq.
While Shokor asked the court to impose the harshest sentences on the defendants, Matar said his client Alayyan did not directly cause Salamehs death, asking for a commuted sentence for his client.
Hajj, who represented Mroue, said his client only carried food to her sons from her village inside the occupation zone.
