"Israel" kept guessing over Hizbullah's capability to launch long-range attack (1)
"Last week's Katyusha strike into northern Galilee has rekindled fears in "Israel" that Hizbullah may possess long-range rockets with double the range of the version currently deployed by the guerrillas", security sources said.

"The sources said that "Israeli" fears of a long-range rocket attack were evident last week when civil defense units were put on alert all the way to Haifa."

"Hizbullah is equipped with the antiquated second world war 120mm Katyusha rocket, which it has used against "Israeli" positions and northern Galilee for years".

"The missile, which is designed to be launched in mass volleys, has a range of 22km, which puts the towns of Kiryat Shmona and Nahariya within range".

"Following the wounding of six civilians in Mashghara and the assassination of Amal official Hussam Amin last week, Hizbullah guerrillas launched 47 Katyushas across the border. Several targeted "Israeli" posts along the border but at least 21 struck Kiryat Shmona and the outskirts of Nahariya."

"The "Israeli" army has long believed that Hizbullah may have acquired the 240mm missile, manufactured in Iran, with a range of 40km."

"This would bring the suburbs of the coastal town of Haifa within range".

"Shiite cleric Sayed Mohammad Hussein Fadlallah suggested that Hizbullah may posses new weapons when he said on Radio Orient a week ago that "Israeli infrastructure" was now within range of the resistance."

"His comments came in reaction to calls within "Israel" to bomb Lebanon's infrastructure every time a soldier is killed in the south".

"Similar reports emerged at the end of the 1996 April war when "Israeli" helicopter gunships abruptly halted attacks on the southern suburbs of Beirut after five successive days".

"Reports suggested that Hizbullah had fired one 240mm Katyusha towards Haifa's outskirts, prompting a cessation of air strikes on Beirut for fear of further long-range salvos."

"Hizbullah, true to form, has neither confirmed nor denied the existence of the long-range Katyusha, in line with the party's policy of keeping the "Israelis" guessing over its armaments".

"Brig Gen Oded Ben-Ami, the former "Israeli" army spokesman, in August last year claimed that Hizbullah was equipped with the 240mm rocket. Two months ago, the "Israelis" asserted that Hizbullah had acquired a 122mm artillery piece and had used it to pound a border position".

  September Wednesday 16 -1998:

Lebanon: Behind the lines, in occupied Lebanon (1)
JIBSHEET, Southern Lebanon, (Sep. 14) IPS - In 1993, "a few weeks after her house was demolished in an "Israeli" air raid, a team of young men from the Hizbullah came to clear away the rubble and rebuild Zahra Fahas' home."

"By day, the men poured concrete and pounded wooden pillars into the ground until the new house was finished. By night, they took machine guns into the surrounding hills to strike at the "Israeli" army installations that occupy south Lebanon."

"Since then, Fahas has watched Hizbullah men descend on dozens of her neighbors' demolished homes in this small village just north of the "Israeli"-occupied zone. Here the battle lines are starkly drawn."

"Since 1985, "Israel" has occupied a sliver of south Lebanon for what it says is protection against guerrilla raids on its northern border communities. Hizbullah, young Lebanese Shi'ites, has been battling since then to free Lebanon of "Israeli" occupation."

"It has also successfully transformed itself from a shadowy band of Islamic extremists to a populist movement supported by regular Lebanese. Its popularity is now so overwhelming, its goal of ousting "Israel" so universally accepted, that guerrillas and ordinary villagers fuse into one, with fighters, supporters and even village women doing their part to aid the resistance."

"Meanwhile, Hizbullah's increasingly sophisticated guerrilla tactics has sapped the morale of "Israel's" army and prompted its leaders to search for a way out of a war that, like the United States' war in Vietnam, seems increasingly impossible to win."

"We are Hizbullah," said Fahas, a 35-year-old mother of five, gathered with her extended family in front of their home. "We will defend our land and our families. We will resist. We will fight until the "Israelis" leave."

"In April, "Israel's" Cabinet accepted a 20-year-old United Nations resolution calling on it to withdraw from Lebanon, on the extra condition that the Lebanese Army controls the south, not Hizbullah."

"Beirut officials won't accept the condition. "It's not the task of the Lebanese Army to protect "Israel"," said Abdel Satar al-Laz, spokesman for Lebanese Prime Minister Rafiq Hariri.

"The stalemate has kept a costly war of attrition going, despite a cease fire arrangement and international monitors in place since 1996, when "Israeli" attacks killed about 200 Lebanese civilians, mostly refugees holed up in a United Nations compound."

'The monitors oversee the arrangement, which bar both sides from launching attacks on the other side's towns and villages."

"But there is no similar ban on attacks against military targets."

"It's an international experiment in conflict management, but not in conflict resolution," said a Lebanese foreign ministry spokesman and member of the monitoring group, who spoke on customary condition of anonymity. "We simply find ways to spare civilians" he said. "This is our only job."

"Since then, the number of civilian casualties has decreased while better trained Hizbullah guerrillas are making the war more costly to "Israel". Hizbullah's improved methods have made it possible for a core of only about 1,000 fighters with limited weaponry to exact a relatively heavy toll against a larger and much better equipped "Israeli" army and its proxy militia."

"So far this year, 11 "Israelis" and 19 militia members have been killed, according to an "Israeli" army spokeswoman. This successful skirmishing has boosted  Hizbullah's morale and snuffed out an unfavorable image of the fighters as undisciplined zealots."

"Members have focused their religious fervor on achieving the Islamic ideal of social justice", say observers.

"They don't see their role as representing the Shi'ites only," said Radwan al-Sayyad, a professor of Islamic studies at Lebanese University. "They want a just political system, a just social system, for all Lebanese. That for them, is the ideal Islamic state, the zenith of all good things."

"Hizbullah leaders see themselves as freedom fighters and refuse to accept the"terrorist" tag. "We are a liberation movement fighting for the freedom of our country," said Ibrahim Musawi, spokesman for Hizbullah. "We don't care what labels they want to put on us," he said. "We know what our role is and we know that what we are doing is right."

Three miles from Jibsheit, in the town of Nabatieh, Hizbullah's presence is strongly felt. Here, black and yellow resistance flags flutter between murals of Imam Musa Sadr, the Iranian-born cleric who disappeared in Libya in 1978."

"Teenagers in black pants and t-shirts usher crowds to a Hizbullah rally commemorating the kidnapping of Sheikh Abdel Karim Obeid, captured by Israel in the 1980s, as members of a marching band and omen draped in black look on."

"At a restaurant nearby, Ahmed, a 37-year-old bearded man works part-time as a baker. Everyone in these villages does his part to liberate Lebanon, he says. Some provide food and shelter to fighters in need. Others collect funds. A few, like himself, are on what he calls "stand-by" volunteers for guerrilla operations."

"At night, I help out Hizbullah," said Ahmed, taking a break from the bakery ovens. "I do jobs to get rid of the occupation. Without support from me and the other villagers, there would be no resistance."

Mofaz admits helicopters threatened by Hizbullah's guns (1)
""Israeli" chief of staff Lt Gen Shaul Mofaz has admitted that Hizbullahs anti-aircraft firepower has become, for the first time, a real threat to his air forces long-unchallenged superiority in the skies over Lebanon."

"He said the "Israeli" military is continually seeking to improve its tactics and performance in the south to counter increasingly sophisticated and effective resistance by Lebanese guerrillas."

"In comments carried by  Haaretz , Mofaz, who was appointed in August, said Hizbullah was making progress both acquiring and using equipment. He noted that the group is now using missiles and mortars and is constantly trying to improve their equipment. This is a legitimate and natural course of action for a large guerrilla group.

"While Mofaz argued that the "Israeli" army is head and shoulders above Hizbullah, he admitted that helicopter gunship crews were being forced to seek new ways to reduce the threat posed by the groups anti-aircraft batteries."

"For the past six months, Hizbullah has been making greater use of its Soviet-made 57mm anti-aircraft guns. The weapon has a horizontal range of 12,000 metres, an effective elevated range of over 4,000 metres and is capable of firing up to 120 rounds a minute."

"The guns, mounted on flat-bed trucks, are highly mobile and when used in groups of three or four have a deadly potential against low-flying helicopters. Mofazs concern comes as aircraft are being increasingly used against the resistance as an alternative to ground forces in an effort to limit casualties."

"His remarks reflect the growing belief that "Israel" is embroiled in an unwinnable war in the south against a fluid, disciplined and determined guerrilla force and that only a political solution will prevent further casualties."

"The "Israelis" have been forced to constantly adapt their tactics to meet initiatives by the resistance. But in the long run, their options are limited, according to a senior security source. All they can do is keep the situation as it is and try to minimise casualties. I dont see what other options they have, the source said.

"He argued that the only way "Israel" can defeat Hizbullah is to occupy the whole of Lebanon, an unrealistic scenario."

"The offensive options open to "Israel" would be to stage another air and artillery blitz on the south, hit infrastructure targets to pressure the government into reining in the resistance, expand the zone, possibly up to the Litani, or conduct pin-point raids north of the zone."

"Israels" (war) minister, Yitzhak Mordechai, appears to have ruled out another mass offensive like the 1996 April war. The political fallout over the Qana massacre, coupled with the failure to curb Hizbullahs activities, seems to have convinced "Israel" that full-scale assaults are costly and ineffective."

There will be no military solution for the terrorism in Lebanon. I didnt think that this or that tactical success would substantially change the situation in Lebanon, Mordechai said earlier this week.
Despite recent calls in "Israel" for Lebanons infrastructure to be targeted, this option remains unlikely. The "Israeli" government knows that hitting power stations, roads and bridges in Lebanon will provoke salvoes of Katyushas into northern "Israel", thus defeating the claimed purpose of the occupation ­ to protect the northern settlements."

Expanding the zone so that northern "Israel" falls out of range of mortars and the Katyushas is impractical and could only be achieved at the cost of many "Israeli" lives, international condemnation and a domestic political backlash."

'The only viable option for "Israel" is to conduct limited strikes against individual resistance targets. This is where "Israeli" technological superiority can be brought to bear, as proved by the assassination of Hussam Amin in a helicopter missile ambush three weeks ago."

"Israel" could rely on its state-of-the-art unmanned surveillance planes, or drones, to gather intelligence data. The drones have sufficient range to cover the whole of Lebanon at any one time, relaying real-time images back to "Israel". A Western defence expert said Amins car had been tracked by a drone, out of earshot and sight, before the two helicopters launched their deadly attack."

'While these operations would not destroy Hizbullah, they would achieve a victory on points, according to Mordechai.

"Defensive options are limited. The "Israeli" army has spent the last eight months beefing up  positions to ward off mortar and rocket fire but troops are still vulnerable once out in the open. Hizbullah has sought to restrict "Israeli" movements by using roadside bombs ­ with deadly results. Of the 11 soldiers killed so far this year, seven were from roadside bombs."

""Israel" may seek to tighten its front line by returning the Jezzine enclave to the government and redeploying south of the Litani River. This would free up some of its most heavily hit posts and reduce the number of targets available to the resistance. But the "Israelis" would have to placate the (so-called) South Lebanon Army before withdrawing. Given that the SLA is reportedly on the verge of collapse, this option would do little for its flagging morale."

'Short of an unconditional withdrawal from the south, of which there is no imminent sign, the "Israelis" can do little but hold their ground and  search for isolated resistance targets. Theyre having problems knowing what to do, the security source said. They dont have an answer and all theyre doing is buying time.

  September Sunday 27 -1998:

Its high time the Lebanese agreed on the identity of the common enemy (1)
 

by Adib F Farha, a Lebanese businessman
 contributed this article to The Daily Star

"Michael Youngs article entitled The pussy-footing in Jezzine has to stop (The Daily Star, September 26) and the divergence of opinion demonstrated in his repeated references to my earlier article, Jezzine dilemma: a tragedy and a trap are prime examples of the controversy Israel seeks to create over Jezzine to destabilise the fragile national reconciliation process. The "Israeli" strategy is to push certain well-intentioned Lebanese to call for the neutralisation of the region based on an attitude marred in deeply rooted sectarian fears. "Israel" is certainly correct in its underlying prediction that such a call would result in a controversy that could fuel disastrous internal problems in Lebanon."

"Young accurately paraphrased that the forceful occupation of the Jezzine pocket by the Lebanese army could place our armed forces in a sudden confrontation with the enemy. However, later in his article, he cynically asked the rhetorical question, isnt that what the armed forces are for? Young ignored the two sentences that followed the paraphrased statement, thus making it sound as though the armys role was in question. Indeed the issue is not whether or not the army is meant to confront the enemy but that the timing of such a confrontation entails a serious political decision that would have to be carefully weighed by the central government in close consultation with regional and international allies.

"Furthermore, Lebanon cannot allow itself to be drawn into such a confrontation  by making premature military moves under the pressure of emotional outcries. The army should take proactive military action toward regaining national sovereignty only when the Lebanese government decides that it is the right time to do. It should not be drawn into a confrontation according to a timetable set by the enemy to achieve any one of a multitude of Israels self-serving objectives."

"Young proceeded to misinterpret remarks that maintained that Jezzine notables who have become self-proclaimed foreign ministers dont have much to gain by pleading their cause to clerics, foreign diplomats or other wailing walls as a call for silence. Quite the contrary. If their pleas were meant to solicit international pressure on Israel to withdraw its support for its mercenary proxy army in Jezzine, the effort would represent a commendable and legitimate public outcry against occupation. However, the gist of their anger is implicitly and accusingly directed against the resistance movement. There is clearly something fundamentally wrong when people complain to foreigners about resistance activities by their own compatriots against the occupation of their own land. They are ready to believe that resistance fighters are either sloppily or deliberately causing the death of innocent civilians sooner than they are prepared to even consider the possibility that Israel could be placing the roadside bombs that cause these deaths. Its purpose is precisely meant to lead them to this erroneous assessment of the resistance. The readiness to be so distrusting of ones own people clearly reflects the depth of national disunity. "

"The comments in my article (September 21) were definitely were anything but a call for silence. They were rather a sincere plea to redirect the noise in the right direction and toward demanding an end to "Israels" proxy occupation of our land. The article gave Jezzine notables the benefit of the doubt by choosing to believe that they were simply misled into unwittingly (playing) into Israels hand. The assumption remains that they are well-intentioned individuals who are eager to see their region liberated. However, their emotions and their energies seem to be channeled in the wrong direction."

"Nobody is suggesting that the people of Jezzine should be instructed to smile even as they are given the shaft as Michael Young put it. The regions notables should indeed speak out. However, their rhetoric should be saved for the right cause. They should speak out to rectify misconceptions, to allay unfounded fears and to encourage people to maintain their ties to their land. This would serve their cause far more than complaining to foreign diplomats about perceived internal threats."

"The liberation of Jezzine requires the steadfastness of the local people, real and sustained support by the central government of that steadfastness of the inhabitants of Jezzine and other occupied Lebanese areas, and rallying around the resistance rather than viewing it with suspicion because of chronic sectarian wounds. Only the parallel combination of armed struggle, steadfastness, and diplomacy will achieve the liberation of our land."

"Young also refers to the fact that the problem (in Jezzine) is also a confessional one. He adds that it is not doing "Israels" bidding to mention that a substantial community of Christians is disappearing from a key part of the south. Although the regrettable and sad fact that this exodus exists is indisputable, it is disheartening that it is precipitated by confessional considerations. There is no reason that the Christians of Jezzine should leave their region in higher ratios than their Muslim counterparts in other occupied regions except for misconceptions precipitated by unfounded sectarian fears and deeply rooted suspicions. The answer to this unfortunate fact would be for Jezzine notables as well as spiritual and political leaders to help rectify these misconceptions and remove these suspicions."

"It is high time that the Lebanese realised that they have one common enemy and should act accordingly. The suffering of the people of Jezzine is no different from the suffering of residents of other occupied Lebanese areas. However, it is the "Israeli" occupation that causes their suffering and not the resistance to it. Of course, there is a tragic collateral price to resistance that is unavoidable, which often includes the loss of innocent civilians. If a nation were not willing to make necessary sacrifices, liberation would be an unachievably lofty goal. Diplomacy from a position of weakness can only lead to surrender. It is important to remember always that resistance activities are a logical and honorable consequence of Israeli occupation and not the other way around. It is only when we lose track of these basic facts that "Israel" is presenteed with the opportunity to divide and conquer.
