Hammering "Israel" with The Cost of
Occupation
 
 Howard Goller
Reuter 4/12/1997
 

JERUSALEM: The Palestinian uprising that erupted ten years ago this week showed "Israelis" the cost of their occupation as never before.

The "intifada" - the Arabic name for the revolt that both sides gave the conflict - etched on the worlds memory an enduring picture of "Israel" soldiers shooting at stone - throwing youngsters.

It also taught "Israelis" lessons which - even after interim peace deals and negotiations gone sour with the Palestinians - they are still trying to unravel.
It shattered the "Israelis" self-image as a benevolent occupier. Instead families grappled with images of their fathers, brothers and sons shooting and beating Palestinian youngsters.

It cost "Israeli" lives and many in the Jewish state learned, possibly for the first time, to view the Palestinian as a people with aspirations of their own.

The uprising began on December 9,1987. A collision which Palestinians blamed on an "Israeli" truck driver killed four Gazans and triggered clashes that spread throughout the lands captured by "Israeli" in the 1967 Middle East war.

"The Palestinian masses, not isolated terrorist groups and not highly motivated individuals, but the masses, were willing to pay a significant price, and the other side of the coin was that it would be quite costly for "Israel" to maintain the kind of control and strong military presence that was suddenly required", said political scientist Gerald Steinberg.

As defense minister at the start of the "intifada", Rabin ordered troops to crush unrest with "might, force and beatings".

The revolt turned the strongest army in the Middle East into a police force and seriously sapped the morale of its troops - a condition that has worsened with "Israels"
occupation of south Lebanon.

Palestinian children hurled stones, bottles and fire bombs at men in uniform, who responded with bullets made of rubber, plastic and metal and with tear gas, percussion grenades, clubs and gravel cannons.

The "intifada" took an emotional toll on the soldiers.

"The deaths they witnessed first hand, and in some cases were responsible for, hardened a lot of them and increased the level of violence in "Israeli" society", Steinberg said.
