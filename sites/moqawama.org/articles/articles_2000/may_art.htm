May Wednesday 24 -2000:

Collborators: Barak betrayed us.(1)

By Mazal Mualem
Ha'aretz Correspondent

"It's hard for me to look him in the eye. Before yesterday, he was a high-ranking officer, with a car and a villa; and now he's waiting in line for food, holding a plastic plate." The speaker was an IDF* officer in the Yakal, South Lebanon liaison unit, yesterday, as he watched a forlorn South Lebanon Army** officer, a comrade with whom he collaborated for years in the security strip.

The sentiment reflected the human tragedy, the chaos and sense of hopelessness, which now grips both SLA men and their families, and also IDF officers who accompanied them yesterday on their way to the "Amnon" retreat on the Sea of Galilee coast.

The SLA soldier in question managed to escape a village near Bint Jbail on Monday. He extricated his wife and ten children from danger, but could not persuade his 75-year-old mother to leave her native village.

The Yakal officer visited this SLA soldier and his family in their home dozens of times in recent years. Standing beside them yesterday, the IDF man couldn't conceal his distress: "I am standing here, and I don't have any answers. I, too, am hurt. I go around with them, they ask questions, and I don't have any answers."

The Sea of Galilee retreat was closed to the public yesterday, and watched closely by the IDF and police. By afternoon hours the 300 rooms at the facility were fully occupied; and another 800 vacated SLA men and family relations waited outside "Amnon," resting on the grass, or waiting on line before entrances to two absorption tent compounds.

An IDF officer who observed the ingathering of SLA refugees at the Sea of Galilee retreat commented: "The attempts to contact family members who remained behind in Lebanon, the fear, the fatigue, the hunger and confusion, and, more than anything, the anger and feelings of betrayal expressed by most of them has caused shame among us. It's a situation which can be described in one word - a disgrace. It's hard to look at two-year-old toddlers walking around and wailing, while still dressed in their pajamas; it's hard to look at their mothers who search for food to feed their babies, because they didn't have time to prepare bottles and food when they escaped."

One SLA officer who served in the army's western brigade, and who arrived at the Amnon retreat yesterday afternoon, agreed to tell his story, on condition that he remain anonymous.

The 47-year-old man left his wife, three children, five siblings, mother and an infant grandson behind at the Rmaich village, in the Bint Jbail region. After serving as an officer in the SLA for 24 years, he hadn't anticipated the army's sudden collapse. When the end came, he became a refugee, trying to save himself from Hezbollah operatives who encircled the village.

Weeping, he provided details of his escape: "I got a phone call in the afternoon from army headquarters at Bint Jbail, telling me to go the border crossing immediately. I left everything behind. I knew that the situation was a mess, but I didn't know how bad it was. I went to my house, and took some money. I told my wife that we had to leave, but she didn't agree to leave our three children who live in Beirut. So I had to go alone. As I was leaving, I saw Hezbollah men entering our village, waving flags, and celebrating. Most residents got in their cars, and traveled to the border.

"When I got to the border, they took my rifle away from me. I crossed into Israel after many hours of waiting. I can't stop thinking about what Hezbollah men will do to our daughters and wives. They know who I am, and they're certain to search for me."

This SLA officer-cum-refugee had bitter words of criticism about Israel and Prime Minister Barak: "Barak betrayed us. If he thinks we'll keep quiet, he's mistaken. We are going to demand that we return to our villages. I don't want to live here. I find myself here without money, without a home - they could have at least prepared us in advance. I have money in the bank. I could have lived here like a human being, instead of being discarded on the Tiberias coastline, not knowing what's happening to my children. I've left my native village behind."

This SLA man is Christian, but many of his comrades are Shi'ite Muslims. Meeting some at the Sea of Galilee retreat, he coordinated plans to demonstrate in Israel, in a vehement, Intifada-like, style.

"Why hasn't anyone come here? Where is Barak, where is Ephraim Sneh? Why aren't they speaking with us? They should come here and see what's happened to the SLA. What's happened to all the talk of hand in hand, and blood ties?
