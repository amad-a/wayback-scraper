Under Siege
Lahd Begs Israel Not to Abandon Him
 
 

In a further sign of the deteriorating morale of the South Lebanon Army, its commander Antoine Lahd said the militia would consider Israel a traitor if it withdrew from the south.

In a letter to Yossi Bilin, a Labour party politician who has formed a new movement calling for a unilateral withdrawal, Lahd urged Israel not to leave before providing security guarantees for the militiamen.

These people fought alongside you and their blood mixed for your blood for over 20 years in order to defend a common goal. The possible withdrawal from Lebanon cannot be considered an internal Israeli issue, as it directly affects the future of the areas inhabitants, Lahd wrote. Otherwise, Israel will be perceived as a traitor.

Lahds letter came in response to a letter from Beilin which told the militia leader not to interfere in Israels internal policies.

Lahds position as head of the SLA is becoming increasingly tenuous. The Israelis consider Lahd as merely a figurehead for the SLA and he retains almost no authority over the militia.

Having been sentenced to death in absentia in Beirut and with growing calls in Israel for an end to the south Lebanon quagmire, Lahd has few options open for him and his men.

Last month, he warned the Israelis that a pull out would result in some militiamen joining Hizbullah and others forming a new anti-Israel militia.

Although his threats were dismissed in Tel Aviv, it was seen as an indication of Lahds crumbling morale. The Israeli army has little faith in its Lebanese allies and has been forced to boost troop numbers from around 1,500 to over 2,000 to counteract a wave of SLA desertions.
