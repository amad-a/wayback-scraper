WOMEN AND LEGISLATION
Palestinians have long suffered from living under a succession of foreign legal systems. Elements of Ottoman law, Jordanian law, Egyptian law in Gaza, and Israeli military orders are still applicable in the West Bank and Gaza. This unhealthy amalgamation of laws affects women through its discriminating and humiliating provisions. Abundance of examples are found in criminal, civil and Ottoman Milleh laws. Developing gender-sensitive legislation which provides for equality and protection for Palestinian women's rights, and setting strategies for their adoption is a matter of priority in the current transitional phase.
The Palestinian Model Parliament provides a democratic platform which addresses human rights issues, specifically those issues related to women. It aims to eliminate all forms of discrimination against women found in the law. The Palestinian Model Parliament succeeded in highlighting the many flaws against women found in the current applicable law. Personal Status law for instance does not provide women with protection against early marriages and divorce. Criminal law discriminates in a most obvious manner against women, Article 340 "crimes of passion" provides mitigation circumstances for men, which are not available for women under analogous circumstances.
Gender inequality is a concern for all progressive members of the society and is not solely a women's issue. Therefore, the women's movement must be certain to maintain a close relationship with them in order not to alienate them. Further the women's movement must take initiatives to mobilize various political, professional, and organizational bodies in the society to ensure their support and guidance for their program. By mobilizing the various social bodies along gender lines the whole issue of developing legislation that is more gender-sensitive becomes a natural development. After that, the task remains to develop mechanisms to ensure that the law is respected.
