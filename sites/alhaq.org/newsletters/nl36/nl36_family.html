KNOW YOUR RIGHTS: Family Reunification
Al-Haq continues to cooperate with Palestinian and Israeli Human rights organizations as a part of the ongoing advocacy program for the reunification of Palestinian families in the Territories.
The right of a family, wife, husband, and children to live together in their country is natural and basic. However, Palestinians in the West Bank, Gaza, and East Jerusalem are not enjoying this right. At best they are given a permanent residency status in the Occupied Territories as a privilege not as a right. Families are forced to live either illegally with their families in the Occupied Territories, or away from their families in some other country.
Al-Haq recognizes the gravity of the situation and the confusion it is creating within Palestinian society. Al-Haq offers basic clarifications on the situation as is, under the administration of the Palestinian National Authority.
Israel and the Palestinian Authority have agreed that Israel will process a certain number of applications per year. The quota is 2000 applications per year; 1200 allotted to the West Bank and 800 to Gaza.
Family reunification applications are filed with Ministry of Interior region offices.

A non-resident spouse:

WHO: A non-resident spouse applying for permanent residency status.

PRESENT STATUS: Entered the Territories on a visit permit.

WHEN: During 1990,1991 until 30 August 1992.
Inquire with the Civil Affairs Office, Ramallah. The Spouse must present either an original or a copy of the visit permit. Spouses under this category are not be subjected to the quota.

A non-resident spouse:

WHO: A non-resident spouse applying for permanent residency status.

PRESENT STATUS: Entered the Territories on a visit permit.

WHEN: Between 31 August 1992 and 30 August 1993.
Inquire with the Civil Affairs Office, Ramallah. The spouse must present either an original or a copy of the visit permit. Spouses falling under this category are subjected to the quota, but are given priority over new applications.
Applicants who applied for family reunification during the Israeli administration may inquire about the status of their application with Civil Affairs region offices. If applications have not been processed, applicants may file for a renewal with the Civil Affairs Office, Ramallah free of charge, provided a copy of the application is presented.

Finally, according to Civil Affairs Officials priority is given to the following applicants:

  1. Applications filed during the Israeli administration, provided that names are found in the Civil Affairs Office files.
  2. Spouses who have been married for a long period of time.
  3. Spouses.
  4. Sons and daughters.
  5. Humanitarian cases.
