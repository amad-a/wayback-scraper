EDITORIAL
On 30 August 1998, the Palestinian National Authority executed two brothers, Staff-Sergeant Muhammad Kamal Abu-Sultan and Sergeant Ra'id Kamal Abu-Sultan. The two brothers who were shot to death, were convicted of the killings of two brothers, Majdi and Muhammad Al-Khalidi.
This was the first implementation of a death penalty sentence by the Palestinian National Authority and as such, it established an alarming precedent of violating Palestinian civil liberties.
World-wide studies show that, when comparing the crime rate in countries where the death penalty was abolished and in those where it is still in force, the threat of the death penalty, or its actual execution, has never been a deterrent to crime. Based on these studies, it is clear that most murders take place between family members or friends. The majority of these murders are carried out in a state of rage and anger. It is clear that the offender does not take into consideration his or her actions or the possible consequences.
In addition, it was concluded at the 1977 International Conference, convened by Amnesty International, in Stockholm, that the death penalty is irreversible. Decided upon according to fallible processes of law by fallible human beings, it can be- and has been- inflicted upon people innocent of any crime. Further, it was concluded that there is a lack of convincing evidence that the death penalty has any more power to deter than for example, a long period of imprisonment. It is even more so in the case of offenders who are mentally ill or who are impelled by violent political motives. Finally, execution by whatever means and for whatever offence is a cruel, inhuman and degrading punishment.
The Palestinian National Authority has expressed its commitment to international and human rights law, and is, therefore, bound to it. The execution of the death penalty, without ensuring the most careful legal procedures and greatest safeguards for the accused, is a violation of international covenants, including Article 14 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, which accords the accused in capital cases the right to appeal to a higher judicial authority or to petition for a pardon or reprieve. The Palestinian National Authority responded to the tense mood of the general public in the Gaza Strip and did not respect the waiting period provided for by international conventions and standards before carrying out the execution.
Al-Haq emphasizes that fighting and deterring crime in Palestinian society is only possible through legislating the powers of the various security apparatuses, regulating the use and ownership of firearms, including those utilized by the various security apparatuses, and through ensuring respect for the rule of law, application of the law, independence of the judiciary and the enforcement of the decisions rendered by the courts.
