AN INTRODUCTION TO ISRAELI MILITARY ORDERS

Since the beginning of Israeli occupation of the West Bank in 1967, Israel has ruled in a legalistic fashion. As a signatory of the Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949 ("Relative to the Protection of Civilians in Time of War"), and the Hague Regulations of 1907 (which was enacted prior to Israeli independence, but which "Israel accepts as binding"), Israel has gone to great lengths to rule over the West Bank in a manner appearing to follow International Law. Thus, the Israeli military has amended and added to existing law by issuing a series of Military Orders. Between June 1967 and June 1998, over 1450 numbered military orders were issued as well as hundreds of unnumbered military orders, manifestos and appointments.

In Occupier's Law: Israel and the West Bank, Raja Shehadeh divided the military orders issued by Israeli military commanders between 1967 and 1985 into four legislative stages. He found that both the goals and effects of Israeli military law over the West Bank fell into four moderately distinct stages as follows.

The first legislative stage, during the first four years of occupation (from 1967 through 1971), established the groundwork for the occupation. "The some 200 Military Orders issued during these four and a half years laid the foundation for the occupation. The orders were not published and were not available even to lawyers."1 During this first legislative stage, Israel assumed responsibility for most land, natural resources, and issued orders giving the Israeli military authority to expropriate land for public use, to control building and use of the land, and to operate banks in the Occupied West Bank.2 The orders regulate all importing and exporting from the West Bank and mandates that Palestinian residents of the West Bank must obtain permits from the Israeli military to travel, obtain a drivers license, or to practice one of several listed professions.3 During this stage identity cards were issued, and meetings of 10 or more people to hear a political speech required a permit. Administrative detention, practiced during the British Mandate, was specifically approved of through a Military Order.4

The second legislative stage covers the orders issued between 1971 and 1979. Mr. Shehadeh explains that this stage was largely responsible for "facilitating Jewish settlement in the West Bank."5

The third legislative stage extends over the two years following Israeli-Egyptian Camp David Accords. During this stage, Mr. Shehadeh points out that more Jewish settlers moved to the West Bank than during any prior period since the beginning of Israeli occupation over the West Bank. Interestingly, the period following the signing of the Oslo Accords also marked an unusually large influx of Jewish Settlers into the Occupied West Bank. According to Shehadeh, the Military Orders issued during the third legislative stage served following three objectives:

"1. Tightening the links between Israeli citizens living in the Occupied Territories and Israel by extending Israeli law to the Jewish settlers and excluding them from the
jurisdiction of the West Bank courts;
2. Organizing the administration of Jewish settlements to make it consistent with the local government in Israel, i.e., extending Israeli regulations regarding regional and local councils to the settlements of the West Bank;
3. Reorganizing the military government in the West Bank and giving some of its functions to the newly established Civilian Administration."6

The fourth legislative stage covers the years between 1981 until the start of the Intifada. In this stage, Military Orders deal extensively with the use of land expropriated for Jewish Settlements, and with control of money flow in and out of the West Bank.7 Value added tax was introduced during the fourth legislative stage, increasing Israel's financial benefits from occupation.8

This paper is an attempt to create a fifth legislative stage from the post-Oslo Military Orders, issued between May of 1994 through June of 1998. The military orders issued during this period document transfer of authority to the Palestinian National Authority in limited areas in the West Bank. As this limited authority over portions of the West Bank with dense Palestinian population is transferred, orders are issued to divide these areas with bypass roads, which remain entirely under Israeli control. Additionally, each time there is any terrorism perpetrated against Israel by a Palestinian, strict closure orders over the West Bank are ordered. Between May 1994 and June 1998, at least 27 orders demanding strict closure of all or part of the West Bank, or canceling any outstanding permits for West Bank residents to enter Israel. Since the signing of the Olso Accords there have been over 300 days of closure in the West Bank and Gaza Strip. Thus, the fifth legislative stage is aimed largely at limiting Palestinian freedom of movement and instituting a policy of collective punishment. This stage's goals and effects secure Israel's hold over settlements in the West Bank, while implementing only select passages and select goals of the Oslo Accords.
