THE PLIGHT OF THE AL-JAHALIN BEDOUINS

The Eviction of the al-Jahalin

The al-Jahalin Bedouins are part of a nomadic Bedouin tribe that have been living in Palestine for hundreds of years. They live in encampments in various locations in the West Bank, largely in the eastern part. Traditionally, however, they lived in the Naqab, from which the Israelis forced them following Israeli occupation of the area in 1948.

The West Bank land, on which they have been living, was owned by Palestinians from the nearby villages of Abu Dis and Al-Izzariya. In 1997, the Israelis confiscated this land, declaring it 'state land,' claiming the Palestinian owners were not able to prove possession. This confiscation left the Al-Jahalin as illegal residents on government property. According to the Israeli authorities, the Bedouin are to be relocated to a piece of expropriated Palestinian land that rests just 500 metres from the central garbage dump of the Jerusalem Municipality. Between 700 and 800 garbage trucks pass by this tract of land each day. The rocky terrain of this land as well as the small plots allotted to each family will make life nearly impossible for the al-Jahalin. Their only source of livelihood, their sheep, will be unable to graze in their new location.

The al-Jahalin were evicted to make way for the expansion of the Israeli settlement, Ma'ale Adumim. The municipal planning boundaries of this settlement are bigger than those of Tel Aviv and already contain some 4,000 housing units. A lawyer hired for the Bedouin petitioned the Israeli High Court in 1997 to postpone the eviction of the Bedouin because the land on which they were living was not in the original confiscation order. The court issued a temporary order until a final decision could be reached. Although the court order specifically forbade any eviction of the al-Jahalin, Israeli contractors continued to build and encroach on Bedouin tents, finally surrounding their encampments with a ring of heavy construction. The Israeli government, intervening on behalf of the contractors, petitioned the High Court to reduce the restriction order on construction so as to avoid any financial penalties in missing the construction deadlines. The court agreed with the government that, indeed, the land of the Bedouin was confiscated and they would have to be moved.

The Israeli authorities had offered to provide alternative housing for the al-Jahalin in their new location. Currently, they are living in discarded railway cars which in contrary to several international laws and conventions that provide for adequate shelter and housing should a nomadic tribe be forced to move.

Objectives of the Israeli Authorities

The movement of Bedouins in this way serves a political objective for the Israelis. The eviction of the al-Jahalin clears the land for the expansion of a very lucrative settlement in the scenic foothills of the Negev Mountains. The Israeli Authorities continue to be intent on the expansion of such settlements in the Jerusalem area (which they regard as annexed) and in the West Bank, despite the existence of the Oslo Accords and in strict contradiction to the Fourth Geneva Convention Relative to Civilians in a Time of War.

The Israeli authorities' treatment of the al-Jahalin could be described as a microcosm of their policy toward the whole of the West Bank and Jerusalem in that it involves the expropriation of Palestinian land to make way for the augmentation of the Israeli populace in these areas.

Domestic and International laws

There are several laws governing the treatment of indigenous people both in local and international laws.

The Fourth Geneva Convention Relative to Civilians in a Time of War states that in the event of an occupation the laws that were in place on the eve of the occupation are the laws that will govern the occupied area. In the West Bank these laws are Ottoman, British and Jordanian. These laws state, those who settle and utilise an area of land for ten years or more are entitled to possession of that area of land. The al-Jahalin lived on this area of land from which the Israelis are forcing them to move for approximately forty years.

The action that the Israeli authorities have taken toward the al-Jahalin is a clear violation of international law.

The Fourth Geneva Convention Relative to the Protection of Civilian Persons in Time of War, Article 47 on civilians residing within occupied territories states that:
"Protected persons who are in occupied territory shall not be deprived … of the benefits of the present Convention … by any annexation … of the whole or part of the occupied territory." Article 49 (1) forbids the deportation or transfer of an occupied population: "Individual or mass forcible transfers … are prohibited, regardless of their motive." Article 49 also recognizes the illegality of Israeli settlements in general, "The Occupying Power shall not deport or transfer part of its own civilian population into the territories it occupies."

The International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR) in Article 1(1), states that:

The State Parties to the present Covenant recognise the right of everyone to an adequate standard of living for himself and his family, including adequate food, clothing and housing, and to the continuous improvement of living conditions. The States Parties will take appropriate steps to ensure the realisation of this right, recognising to this effect the essential importance of international cooperation based on free consent.

Covenant on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (CERD) in Article 5(e) (iii) states that:

The States Parties undertake to prohibit and eliminate racial discrimination in all of its forms and to guarantee the right of everyone, without distinction as to race, color, or national or ethnic origin, to equality before the law, notably in the enjoyment of the following rights: ... (e) in particular ... (iii) the right to housing.

Covenant on the Elimination of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW) in Article 14.2 (h) states that:

States Parties shall take all appropriate measures to eliminate discrimination against women in rural areas in order to ensure, on a basis of equality of men and women, that they participate in and benefit from rural development and, in particular, shall ensure to such women the right ... (h) to enjoy adequate living conditions, particularly in relation to housing, sanitation, electricity and water supply, transport and communications.

Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC Article 27.3) states that:

States Parties in accordance with national conditions and within their means shall take appropriate measures to assist parents and others responsible for the child to implement this right and shall in the case of need provide material assistance and support programmes, particularly with regard to nutrition, clothing and housing.

The UN Committees that monitor compliance with the above human rights treaties have repeatedly affirmed that these covenants and conventions, which Israel has ratified, apply to the occupied Palestinian territories.

Other standards that apply to Israel's treatment of the Al-Jahalin are Convention Concerning Indigenous and Tribal Peoples in Independent Countries (ILO No. 169), 72 International Labour Organization Official Bull. 59, and the UN Draft Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous People.

The Convention Concerning Indigenous and Tribal Peoples states in Article 1,


The peoples concerned shall have the right to decide their own priorities for the process of development as it affects their lives, beliefs, institutions and spiritual well-being and the lands they occupy or otherwise use, and to exercise control, to the extent possible, over their own economic, social and cultural development. In addition, they shall participate in the formulation, implementation and evaluation of plans and programs for national and regional development which may affect them directly.

Article 14 of the Convention Concerning Indigenous and Tribal Peoples states,

(1) The rights of ownership and possession of the peoples concerned over the lands that they traditionally occupy shall be recognised. In addition, measures shall be taken in appropriate cases to safeguard the right of the peoples concerned to use lands not exclusively occupied by them, but to which they have traditionally had access for their subsistence and traditional activities. Particular attention shall be paid to the situation of nomadic peoples and shifting cultivators in this respect.

In this Convention, "the use of the term "lands" ...shall include the concept of territories, which covers the total environment of the areas which the peoples concerned occupy or otherwise use." Article 16 states,


1. Subject to the following paragraphs of this Article, the peoples concerned shall not be removed from the lands which they occupy.

2. Where the relocation of these peoples is considered necessary as an exceptional measure, such relocation shall take place only with their free and informed consent. Where their consent cannot be obtained, such relocation shall take place only following appropriate procedures established by national laws and regulations, including public inquiries where appropriate, which provide the opportunity for effective representation of the peoples concerned.

3. Whenever possible, these peoples shall have the right to return to their traditional lands, as soon as the grounds for relocation cease to exist.

4. When such return is not possible, as determined by agreement or, in the absence of such agreement, through appropriate procedures, these peoples shall be provided in all possible cases with lands of quality and legal status at least equal to that of the lands previously occupied by them, suitable to provide for their present needs and future development...

The Draft Declaration on the Rights on Indigenous People in article 7 states,

Indigenous peoples have the collective and individual right not to be subjected to ethnocide and cultural genocide, including prevention of and redress for:
... c. any form of population transfer which has the aim or effect of violating or undermining any of their rights...

Article 10 of the Draft Declaration states:

Indigenous peoples shall not be forcibly removed from their lands or territories. No relocation shall take place without the free and informed consent of the indigenous peoples concerned and after agreement on just and fair compensation and, where possible, with the option of return.

Article 11 of the Draft Declaration protects Indigenous people specifically under the Fourth Geneva Convention.

States shall observe international standards, in particular the Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949, for the protection of civilian populations in circumstances of emergency and armed conflict, and shall not:
c. force indigenous individuals to abandon their lands, territories or means of subsistence, or relocate them in special centres for military purposes...

Israeli Justification

The Israeli authorities argued in the nineteenth session of the Israeli State's Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (CESCR) that under the Jordanian laws in effect in this area the al-Jahalin never acquired any property rights to the land on which they lived. They claimed that while the al-Jahalin claimed to reside there with the permission of Palestinian owners of the land, the latter failed to substantiate a claim for ownership as required by law and so the land in question was declared "public (Government) land" in 1981.

The Israeli authorities do not recognise their belligerent control over the West Bank or the area of Jerusalem as a belligerent occupation and therefore they deny their de jure obligations under the Fourth Geneva Convention. Moreover, as well as refusing to recognise their obligations as an occupying power they do not recognise any duty on their behalf towards the al-Jahalin under the Covenants outlined above.

The United Nations has recognized on several occasions that Israel is indeed perpetuating a belligerent occupation of the Gaza Strip, Jerusalem and the West Bank and had demanded that they apply the Fourth Geneva Convention as well as other applicable UN conventions to its territories.

The al-Jahalin's Options

The al-Jahalin declare that, as they have been given no choice but to move then they should be provided with the basic infrastructure of water, electricity and sewage, the title to a plot of land of sufficient size for each displaced family, building permits so as to be able to build on these plots and a near-by site on which to graze their flocks. These are provided for under the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR Article 11(1), Article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention, and both the Draft Declaration on the Rights on Indigenous People and the Convention Concerning Indigenous and Tribal Peoples in Independent Countries in articles 10 and 16 respectively.

The above Conventions and international law demand that the Al-Jahalin, wherever they are housed, must be provided with basic facilities as are consistent with what they have demanded. These responsibilities to the al-Jahalin must be observed by the Israeli authorities and, where they are not, al-Haq requests support from the international community to ensure protection for the al-Jahalin.
