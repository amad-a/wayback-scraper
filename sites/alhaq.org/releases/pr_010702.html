July 2 , 2001
Press Release Index

Three Palestinian Activists Assassinated, and Two are Killed In Israeli Instigated Attacks

Three Palestinians were killed late last night in the second assassination carried out by Israeli forces this week. The three were assassinated at 11:00 pm when an Israeli Apache helicopter fired four missiles into their car. This attack is a clear indication that Israel has begun to act upon the cabinet decision taken ten days ago to resume the use of assassination as a preventative measure if it is deemed necessary for security reasons. The assassination occurred on the main road between the villages of al-Zababda and Mesiliya near the village of Qabatya, south of Jenin.  Israeli sources claim that the attack was carried out in order to avert a planned terrorist attack, yet no evidence has been offered to support this claim. The three men killed in the attack were Mohammad Ahmed Basharat (27), Walid Rasmi Sadiq Bisharat (19), and Samah Nur Aldin Abu Hnaish (23). According to information gathered by al-Haq the three were returning home from Jenin to the village of Tamoun where Mohammad and Walid were from. The bodies of Mohammad and Walid were burned beyond recognition causing great difficulty in identifying them. Samah was killed when a missile struck him in the chest. Mohammad Basharat was apparently the intended target of the assassination, although all three are reported to have been wanted members of Islamic Jihad. 

Haaretz reported today that Israeli Cabinet Minister Tzachi Hanegbi acknowledged Israels responsibility for the assassination. He stated that the people who were liquidated were killed in an act of self-defense, and that these things are carried out only when there is no alternative.  Israeli military officials claimed that the attack definitely averted a terrorist attack, stating that Mohammad Basharat was responsible for past bombings and shootings attacks against Israeli targets. Again evidence to back up these charges was not provided.

In a second incident yesterday morning two Palestinians were killed near the village of al-Zababdeh, south of Jenin. According to Palestinian sources the two men who were killed, accompanied by three companions, had stopped to buy gas at a gas station located just beyond the Palestinian checkpoint that marks the entrance to al-Zababdeh, when Israeli forces stationed in a military camp 450 meters away from their location opened fire. After the shooting started the five men retrieved weapons from their car and returned fire towards the camp. During the ensuing exchange of fire Jamal Daifallah Hassan Thlaji (33) and Mahmoud Musa Suliman Khalil (34)
were killed. It is not currently clear whether the other three men fled or were arrested. This case is still being investigated by al-Haq. A statement by the Israeli military about the incident stated that Israeli forces operating on pro-active operational activity in the area spotted the group of armed men and an exchange of fire followed. During the ensuing firefight the two men named above were killed. The statement goes on to say the several explosive devices were found in the area where the men were killed. Both men killed are confirmed to have been active members of the Izi al-Diin al-Qassam Brigades of Hamas. 

These attacks come during a time when Israeli officials are insisting that Israel is doing everything within its power to minimize its use of violence against Palestinians, and are also insisting that Palestinians must bring about an absolute cessation of all violence before any political negotiations can resume. Early yesterday evening Israeli Prime Minister Ariel Sharon reiterated his demand that Palestinians halt their resistance when he told a group of settlers in the southern West Bank that there will not be any beginning of any political process whatsoever before there is an absolute halt not half, nor temporary but absolute halt to terrorism and violence and incitement. 
However, the seven day period of calm called for by US Secretary of State Colin Powell and agreed to by both the Israeli and Palestinian Authorities is not only applicable to the Palestinians. 
Israeli violations of Palestinians basic human rights must stop. Al-Haq reiterates its stance, that in addition to being a blatant violation of the ceasefire, Israels policy of assassination is a blatant violation of international law and must be halted immediately. Israels continued closure of the Occupied Territories and the curfews imposed on various cities and villages also constitute blatant human rights violations and are a more subtle form of violence that must be stopped. 

It is al-Haqs position that there can be no justification for Israels use of assassination. An analysis of al-Haqs documentation shows that the lack of concrete proof that substantiates the charges against individuals who have been assassinated is not unique to this case, but is a reoccurring pattern. This is a clear violation of all international standards of justice that give an accused person the right to defend him/herself in a court of law in order to challenge any potential violations of their rights and ensure against a miscarriage of justice. In ordering assassinations the Israeli authorities have failed to pay heed to this obligation, and have carried out the assassinations without due regard for the basic guarantees provided by the relevant provisions of international humanitarian law and human rights principles. There has been no attempt to arrest and detain assassinated individuals, no credible evidence has been furnished to the public domain for judicial scrutiny, those killed were not made aware of the charges against them, and they were not given an opportunity to refute the allegations that sent them to their deaths. Israeli officials have given themselves the authority to act as judge, jury, and executioner. 

Assassinations such as the one carried out last night qualify as willful killings, which Article 147 of the Fourth Geneva Convention of 1949 prohibits. Article 147 of the Fourth Geneva Convention qualifies willful killing as a grave violation of the convention, which are equivalent to war crimes and must be prosecuted by States Parties to the convention. In this light the international community must take action to stop Israeli aggression against unarmed Palestinian civilians and must pressure the Israeli government to end its policy of assassination. The US government should also take action by demanding that weapons that it has supplied to the Israeli military, including Apache helicopters, not be used against the civilian Palestinian population. 

Al-Haq therefore reiterates its calls for:

1. Dr. Asma Jahangir Special Rapporteur of the Commission on Human Rights on extrajudicial, summary or arbitrary executions to visit the Occupied Palestinian Territories to fully investigate all cases of assassination that have occurred since the outbreak of the Intifada and to report on his findings to the relevant international bodies. 
2. States Parties to the Fourth Geneva Convention to act immediately under their obligations as outlined in Articles 1, 146, and 147 of the convention to provide the Palestinian people with protection from grave breaches of their rights, which qualify as war crimes, and must demand an end to abuses committed by Israeli forces. 
3. The United Nations to send a human rights monitoring force to the Occupied
Territories to document all violations social, economic, cultural, civil and political that result from the continued Israeli occupation. 
4. The United Nations to fully implement all resolutions related to the Occupied Territories passed during the last two meetings of the Human Rights Commission be, and press the Security Council to take action to end Israels systematic violation of international humanitarian law.
