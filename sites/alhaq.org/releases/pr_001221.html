Press Release

December 21, 2000
Press Release Index
Al-Haq wishes to express its disappointment at the failure of the UN Security Council to approve the establishment of an observer force in the Occupied Palestinian Territories

For the last three months Al-Haq has called upon the international community to take action in defense of the civilian Palestinian population who continue to live under Israeli military occupation.  In explanation of their abstention from voting a number of states voiced concern that this resolution was being considered at a sensitive and inopportune time.  With talks beginning yesterday, the day after the vote, between Israeli and Palestinian negotiators in Washington, they argued that it could jeopardize the peace process.  Unfortunately, the Palestinian people cannot wait upon the outcome of yet another round of negotiations.  With each passing day the situation in the Occupied Territories worsens.  To not approve an observer force is to prejudge the outcome of negotiations in favor of the more powerful Occupation Forces.  While it is hoped that justice and peace will soon become realities, there is no guarantee that the negotiations will lead anywhere.  The approval of a UN force to be sent to the Occupied Territories sends a strong message that human rights must be respected, not doing so only serves to support the status quo.  It is al-Haqs opinion that even if this round of Palestinian-Israeli negotiations succeeds in bringing about an end to the current violence, at a minimum an observer force is needed in the Occupied Territories.  As was seen during the Oslo Peace process agreements do not guarantee rights.

Israel continues to act in flagrant disregard of the international humanitarian principles, treaties, conventions and covenants to which it is subject, and the Israeli Military continues to use excessive, disproportionate and lethal force against Palestinian protestors.  This excessive use of force has affected all people without distinction.  At this time nightly bombing, summary and arbitrary executions, religious discrimination, the use of administrative detention and torture all persist.  Civilian Palestinians, who qualify as protected persons under international law, live in a constant state of fear.  As they are unable to protect themselves, the responsibility for their protection falls upon the shoulders of the occupying party and, in light of their failure to offer adequate protection, the international community.  It is in light of these facts that al-Haq and the Palestinian people have requested that a protection force be sent to the Occupied Territories. 

Originally, the UN was requested to provide an armed protection force consisting of 2,000 soldiers to be deployed in the Occupied Territories.  Following negotiations, this was pared down to a force of military and police observers of unspecified size.  Palestinian supporters also agreed to change the resolution from expressly calling for the establishment of an observer force to only stating that the UN Security Council was expressing its determination to establish such a force.  Even in this weakened form the Security Council refused to approve the resolution, which in effect may give legitimacy to the brutal and criminal actionsof the Israeli Occupation Forces, including summary executions, excessive use of force and other war crimes that have been documented by the UN.  The Israeli government saw the lack of action on the part of the Security Council as a huge diplomatic success.   
 
Al-Haq wishes to express its grave concern over the lack of action taken on the part of the international community to combat human rights violations occurring in the Occupied Territories.  In light of the continuous deterioration of the human rights situation in the Occupied Territories al-Haq also wishes to reiterate its call for:    

 

1.     The United Nations Security Council should take the necessary measures to pressure the Israeli governmentto stop its excessive use of force and aggression against Palestinian civilians.

 

  1. 2.  Immediate action by the States Parties to the Fourth Geneva Convention to bear responsibility towards atrocities committed against Palestinians in the Occupied Territories, and to fulfill their legal obligations as High Contracting Parties of the Fourth Geneva Convention under Article 1 of the Convention that states that states must act  to ensure respect for the present Convention in all circumstances.
