EXTRA-JUDICIAL EXECUTIONS CONTINUE DESPITE CEASE FIRE

At 2:45 p.m. this afternoon, Israeli helicopter gunships fired three missiles at an agricultural shed in the Wadi Shaheen area of Bethlehem. Omar Ahmed Saada (43), a father of eleven children, Mohammed Saleh Saada (30) a father of five, Taha Alerouj (35) also a father of five and Issaq Ahmad Saada (51) were killed. Amjed Anan Saada (20) was seriously injured and was taken to Beit Jalla hospital. Both Omar and Taha were known Hamas activists. 

Al-Haq condemns this latest extra-judicial execution in the strongest terms. Though the Law of Belligerent occupation confers upon an occupying power the right to take measures to ensure public order, and the security of its forces, any action under this rubric must be within the parameters of article 27 of the Fourth Geneva Convention Relative to the Protection of Civilian Persons in Time of War, which states protected persons are entitled, in all circumstances, to respect for their persons.They shall at all times be humanely treated, and shall be protected especially against all acts of violence or threats thereof There is no legal foundation under international humanitarian law for killing protected persons on the basis of suspicion or even on the basis of evidence of their supposedly threatening activities or possible future actions. 
Instead, the Israeli authorities are under an obligation to attempt to arrest and detain those who they deem are a threat to their security, and any force that is used, must be proportionate to the harm threatened. 

Extra-judicial executions are willful killings for the purposes of the Fourth Geneva Convention, which are in principle crimes of war. State signatories to the Convention are under a legal obligation to seek out and try those responsible for grave breaches. Those individuals who are involved in the planning, instigation, ordering, or directly committing grave breaches are individually responsible for the crime.
