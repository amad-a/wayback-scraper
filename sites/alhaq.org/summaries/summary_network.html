PREFACE
For at least ten years before signing the Oslo Accords, road construction by the Israeli occupying power in pursuit of Israeli interests provoked concern amongst Palestinians. While the so-called "by-pass roads" are superficially associated with the Israeli-Palestinian agreements, in particular the Interim Agreement also known as Oslo II, Israel's activities in the field of road construction, regardless of labeling or means of execution, continue to pursue the continuation of the road construction plan which began to be executed in the early 1980s. Israel has succeeded in exploiting the current peace process and the Israeli-Palestinian agreements, which maintained the settlements and placed responsibility for their security with Israel, to escalate the execution of this road construction plan. Israel's objectives are to maintain its control over the Occupied Palestinian Territories and even achieve actual annexation of most of the area by altering its character and legal status, in particular by destroying the area's territorial integrity and preventing the Palestinian people from exercising its national and inalienable right to self-determination, following the elimination of the occupation.
If by-pass roads are generally associated with settlements and may seem a logical outcome of settlement construction, in reality they are designed to pursue these same objectives by changing the infrastructure of the Occupied Palestinian Territories and linking them ever more closely to Israel. By-pass road construction runs parallel with settlement building and is designed to achieve the same goals.
In this study, al-Haq attempts to examine different aspects of the by-pass roads question and expose their dangers and side-effects, particular in the way in which they extend Israel's physical occupation of Palestinian land, isolate Palestinian population centers from each other, uproot Palestinians from their land, and impose firm military control over the Occupied Territories. Israel's construction of by-pass roads contravenes principles of international humanitarian and human rights law, and, additionally, undermines the current peace process. Al-Haq attempts in this study to discuss various critical issues, many of which are on the agenda of the Israeli-Palestinian final status negotiations, with the aim of exposing how the real intentions of the Israeli occupier are diametrically opposed to what is needed to achieve a permanent, just and comprehensive peace based on the principles of international law.
Riziq Shuqair
Research and Studies Coordinator
Al-Haq
