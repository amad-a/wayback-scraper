Above: Vic Winter assembled this oposite of the best fisheye photographs he took on the morning of November 17th 1998 from Powell Observatory near Louisburg, Kansas. The brightest of the dozen meteors he recorded was a fireball as bright as the full Moon that lit up the sky. Note the Sickle of Leo, which marks the shower's radiant, rising in the east and Orion on the meridian.

These Leonids were photographed from the  4,000-foot elevation of Pine Mountain near Ojai,  California, on the morning of November 17th. Meteors from two 20-minute exposures were digitally composited with thosecaptured in the 5-minute exposure of the northeastern sky. Polaris is the  bright star at upper left. Click on image for larger view. 1998
Above: From his observing site at Monte Romano in Italy, Lorenzo Lovato captured this brilliant fireball behind a thin layer of clouds during the peak  of the 1998 Leonid shower. He used a 16-mm wide-angle lens at f/2.8 for a15-minute exposure on Fuji 800 film. 

At St. George, Kansas, this  Leonid flared with the brightness of the full Moon at about 1:45 CST on November 17th. 1998 Click on image for larger view.
