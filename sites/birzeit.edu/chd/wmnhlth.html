Women's Health

Although the Department always utilized a gender-sensitive approach in its work, its formal engagement in the area of women's health--as opposed to pre- and post- natal care--began in the late 1980's. Beginning by joining hands with Women's Affairs in Nablus--a women's non-governmental organization aimed at improving the skills of women and assisting them in moves into public life--the Department conducted a study, a first of its kind in the country, focusing on women's health perceptions and needs in the Old City of Nablus. Focusing on women's health needs within the urban context, the study yielded a considerable amount of data-- available at the Department for anyone to use--pertaining to, among other things: women's health status, their perception of their own health and the health of others, issues of poverty and health, birth histories and family planning utilization rates, and, significantly, problems in the receipt of health care services, particularly as encountered in the urban setting.
This information was widely used to argue the case of an expanded view of women's health that goes beyond reproduction. Furthermore, the data contributed to the generation of new research schemes, and the inclusion of training schemes for women's health into Departmental training and teaching activities.
During 1994, the Department became one of the founding members of The Palestinian Women's Health Coalition and was voted as the Coalition's institutional coordinator for the coming three years. Thus, the department currently houses the Coalition and its coordinator. Faculty and staff at the Department have joined hands with the Coalition in conducting policy-oriented studies in the area of women's health, focusing, at the moment, on the health needs of menopausal and adolescent women.
Faculty and staff at the Department are also linked to other groups and institutions working on women's emancipation issues. One of the faculty members at the Department is a founding member, researcher and lecturer at the newly developed Women's Studies Program at Birzeit University. Departmental linkages include, but are not exhausted by: membership in the Reproductive Health Group for the Arab World operated by the Population Council, Cairo, Egypt, as well as advisory committee membership in the World University Service in the UK which provides funds for the training of Palestinian women to become researchers. Department members are also involved in women's issues through advisory committee membership in the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics as well as in the United Nations Developmental Program committee which is in the process of planning for the production of UNDP's Human Development Report for Palestine.
