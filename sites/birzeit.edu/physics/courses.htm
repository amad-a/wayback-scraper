COURSE DESCRIPTION

Following is a brief description of all the courses offered bythe Physics Department. The three numbers given in parenthesis against each course title give the number of lecture, discussion and laboratory hours, in that order. One credit hour comprises one lecture hour or two discussion hours or three laboratory hours.The designations (F), (S) and (D) indicate whether the course is normally offered in the Fall, in the Spring or on Demand, respectively.



  • 111 GENERAL PHYSICS LABORATORY I (0.0.3) (F & S)
Survey of the basic laboratory techniques and methods; taking measurements, data evaluation and report writing with application to selected experiments in mechanics, heat, optics and electricity.

Text: University manual.



  • 112 GENERAL PHYSICS LABORATORY II (0.0.3) (F & S)
Experiments in electricity and magnetism including electrostatics, magnetostatics, DC and AC circuits and applied electric circuits.
Prerequisite: Physics 132 at least concurrently and Physics 111.

Text: University manual.



  • 131 GENERAL PHYSICS I (2.2.0) (F & S)
Newton's laws of motion and their application; mechanical conservation laws; motion of rigid bodies; fluid mechanics; oscillations andthe law of universal gravitation.
Prerequisite: Mathematics 131 at least concurrently.

Text: R. Resnick and Halliday, Fundamentals of Physics, John Wiley.



  • 132 GENERAL PHYSICS II (2.2.0) (F & S)
Electrostatics; magnetic field and induction; direct and alternatingcurrent circuits; dielectrics; magnetic materials; introductionto Maxwell's equations and electromagnetic waves and geometricaloptics.
Prerequisites: Physics 131 and Mathematics 132 at least concurrently.

Text: R. Resnick and Halliday, Fundamentals of Physics, John Wiley.



  • 211 GENERAL PHYSICS LABORATORY III (0.0.3) (F & S)
Experiments in mechanics, heat, waves and fluids.
Prerequisites: Physics 111 and 231 at least concurrently.

Text: University manual.



  • 231 GENERAL PHYSICS III (3.1.0) (F & S)
Waves and sound; physical optics; heat, the laws of thermodynamics and the kinetic theory of gases; introduction to modern physics including relativistic kinematics and quantum theory.
Prerequisites: Mathematics 131 and Physics 131.

Text: R. Resnick and Halliday, Fundamentals of Physics, John Wiley.



  • 212 MODERN PHYSICS LABORATORY (0.0.3) (D)
Experiments in modern physics and physical optics including atomic spectra, radioactivity and various quantum phenomena.
Prerequisites: Physics 112 and 232 at least concurrently.

Text: University manual.



  • 232 MODERN PHYSICS (3.0.0) (D)
Wave-particle duality; schrodinger's equation; atomic structureand many-electron atoms; introduction to solid state physics;relativistic dynamics; nuclear physics and elementary particlesand instruments used in their study.
Prerequisites: Physics 231 and Mathematics 231.

Text: Serway, Modern Physics, Saunders.



  • 233 INTRODUCTION TO ASTRONOMY(new course) (3.0.0) (D)

Prerequisites: Physics 131 or General Science 231.

Text: Zeilik, Astronomy, The Evolving Universe.







  • 234 MEDICAL PHYSICS(new course) (3.0.0) (D)

Prerequisites: Physics 132.







  • 235 METEOROLOGY(new course) (3.0.0) (D)

Prerequisites: Physics 131 or General Science 231 (non-science majors).



  • 331 ELECTROMAGNETIC THEORY I (3.0.0) (S)
    Electrostatics; solutions of Poisson's and Laplace's equations;dielectric media; electrostatic energy; magnetic induction andAmpere's law; magnetic energy; electromagnetic induction; Maxwell'sequations and the wave equation.
    Prerequisites: Physics 132 and Mathematics 331.

    Text: J. Reitz, Milford and Christy, Foundations of Electromagnetic Theory, Addison Wesley.



    • 332 ELECTROMAGNETIC THEORY II (3.0.0) (F)
    Slowly varying currents; propagation of electromagnetic waves in infinite and bounded media; microscopic theory of dielectrics; magnetic materials and optical dispersion; radiation emission and electrodynamics; Lorentz covariant formulation of electrodynamics; superconductivity; plasma physics.
    Prerequisites: Physics 331

    Text: J. Reitz, Milford and Christy, Foundations of Electromagnetic Theory, Addison Wesley.



    • 333 MECHANICS I (3.0.0) (F)
    Kinematics and dynamics of a particle in one, two and three dimensions with emphasis on the oscillator and central force problems; Lagrangian formulation and Hamiltonian's equations.
    Prerequisites: Physics 131 , Mathematics 231 and 331.

    Text: Simon, Mechanics.



    • 334 MECHANICS II (3.0.0) (S)
    Motion of a system of particles and rigid bodies; gravitation; waves in continuous media; moving coordinate systems and relativistic dynamics.
    Prerequisite: Physics 333.

    Text: Simon, Mechanics.



    • 335 OPTICS (3.0.0)
    Electromagnetic waves; matrix method in geometrical optics; reflection, refraction and propagation of light; waveguides; interference, diffraction, and polarization; Fourier optics; coherence theory and an introduction to lasers.
    Prerequisites: Physics 231 and math 231.

    Text: E. Hecht, Optics, Addison Wesley.



    • 336 ANALOG ELECTRONICS (F)

    Prerequisites: Physics 112 and Physics 132.



    • 337 DIGITAL ELECTRONICS(new course)

    Prerequisites: Physics 112.



    • 338 COMPUTATIONAL PHYSICS(new course) (D)

    Prerequisites: Mathematics 331 and Computer Science 234.







    • 339 AUTOMATION USING COMPUTERS(new course) (D)

    Prerequisites: Computer Science 234.



    • 411 PHYSICS SEMINAR (F & S)
    Papers and talks on selected topics in physics prepared and given by the students under the supervision of the Physics staff. The students are introduced to physics journals and specialized texts.
    Prerequisite: Approval of the Department.

    Text: No text.




    • 430 ADVANCED PHYSICS LABORATORY (1.0.6) (S)
    Selected advanced level experiments from various fields of physics. The course aims at giving the student more experience in experimental work and exposing him to the methods and techniques of modern experimental research.
    Prerequisites:Physics 212 and 336.

    Text: No specific text.



    • 431 THERMAL AND STATISTICAL PHYSICS (4.0.0) (F)
    Statistical description of systems of particles; statistical thermodynamics with application to macroscopic thermodynamics; foundations of statistical mechanics with applications; quantum statistics and ideal gasses; kinetic theory and transport phenomena.
    Prerequisites: Physics 232 and Mathematics 331.

    Text: Kittel/Kroemer, Thermal Physics.



    • 432 MATHEMATICAL METHODS OF PHYSICS (3.0.0) (F)
    Coordinate systems; determinants, matrices and group theory; conformal mapping and calculus of residues; Frobenius' method Green's function solution of the differential equations of physics; Sturm-Liouville theory; Hilbert space and orthogonal functions; Bessel, Legendre, Hermite and Laguerre functions; integral equations and the calculus of variations.
    Prerequisites: Physics 232 and Mathematics 332.

    Text: G. Arfken, Mathematical Methods for Physicists, Academic Press.



    • 433 QUANTUM MECHANICS (3.0.0) (S)
    Foundations and basic postulates of the quantum theory; Schrodinger's wave equation with applications to one-dimensional problems; coordinate and momentum representations; the formal structure of quantum mechanics; angular momentum; central forces and matrix representation.
    Prerequisites: Physics 232 and 333.

    Text: R. Liboff, Introductory Quantum Mechanis, Adison Wesley.



    • 434 ATOMIC AND PARTICLE PHYSICS (3.0.0) (D)
    Methods of approximation in quantum mechanics; angular momentum coupling schemes in atoms and molecules; hyperfine structure of energy levels; vibrational and rotational spectra; Stark effect and experimental methods in spectroscopy.
    Prerequisites: Physics 433 .

    Text: R. Liboff, Introductory Quantum Mechanis, Adison Wesley.



    • 435 NUCLEAR PHYSICS AND ELEMENTARY PARTICLES (3.0.0) (D)
    The deuteron; nuclear forces; nuclear models with applications; theories of alpha and beta decays and gamma transitions and decayconstants; nuclear reactions and elementary particles.
    Prerequisite: Physics 433 or concurrent.

    Text: A. Das and Ferbel, Introduction to Nuclear and Particle Physics.



    • 436 SOLID STATE PHYSICS (3.0.0) (D)
    Crystal structure and diffraction; phonons and lattice vibrations;the free electron model; energy bands in solids; semi-conductors;Fermi surfaces; magnetic properties of crystals and superconductors.
    Prerequisites: Physics 433 or concurrent.

    Text:Kittel.



    • 437 SPECIAL STUDY (D)
    Independent study or research conducted by the student under the guidance of a staff member. A written report is required.
    Prerequisite: Approval of the Department.

    Text: No specific text.



    • 438 SELECTED TOPICS IN PHYSICS (3.0.0) or (1.0.6) (D)
    Study of a specified topic in physics selected according to the interests and needs of students and availability of staff.
    Prerequisite: Approval of the Department.
