Arab Culture: Chalenges of the 21st Century

 Amman 19-21 January 2001


Department of
Philosophy and
Cultural Studies
Call for Papers

The need to get together for Arab intellectuals is an urgent one. The re-shaping of the world in the age of Globalization is affecting the Arab world to a great extent. The Arab Israeli conflict is also being reshaped into a "peaceful" conflict. Changes are taking place in most Arab countries, and the question of the future is yet to be faced. Arab intellectuals are dealing with these issues in relative isolation (at least that is what one can comprehend while living in Palestine).

Debate among Arab intellectuals and thinkers has declined due to factors that emerged during the last three decades. The first was the tendency on the part of the Arab world to focus on the Palestinian problem; the second was the nature of political alliances that forced them selves during the cold war, the third was the Gulf war and its implications on inter-Arab relations

The relative weakness of media, and other information dissemination means in the Arab World, made the task of Arab intellectual interaction yet more constrained.

The challenges that the Arab world is facing today, further prevented the interaction of Arab intellectuals regarding the provision of a role in a [post] modern global world, the provision of a future for the region, a positive role in the East  West, and South - North relations reached a verge. Choices have to be made.

Popular reaction to current changes can be, to a great extent, described as politically undefined and rather indifferent, i.e. apathetic.

To our concern this might lead to a politically more unstable situation where cultural development may not be a priority. It might also lead to an environment which might be described as an anti-civil one, where citizens lack citizenship; a situation where cultural diversity leads to clashes rather than being a source of wealth.

After many years of practical isolation from the Arab world, at a time when restrictions on Palestinian  Arab interaction slightly decreased, we see such an event as a breakthrough in inter-Arab intellectual interaction.

For these and for other relevant reasons we see an immediate need to organize a three-day conference for Arab intellectuals under the title "Arab Thought: Challenges of the Twenty-First Century". It is planned to take place in January 2001 in Jordan so as to give the opportunity for more people to participate, given the fact that many Arab intellectuals will be reluctant to attend a conference in Palestine at the moment. The Center for Strategic Studies at the University of Jordan agreed to host the event.

The faculty of the Department of Philosophy and Cultural Studies are involved in teaching several courses that deal with these matters. One of these courses is a University requisite titled "Modern and Contemporary Arab Thought". All department members teach this course. The nature of the course engages faculty and students in reading and discussing texts of many of the potential participants.

Although we intend to develop a concept paper for this conference based on our vision and on extensive consultations with potential participants in this conference, several preliminary themes for this conference can be defined:

 

  • The role of culture in facing the challenges that encounter Arab world in the 21st century.

  • Globalization and the Arab State: The relationship between Globalization and the nation state in the Arab world. The cultural and economic impact of Globalization in relation to the public consciousness. How does this phenomenon affect central issues such as identity, history, modernization, and post modernization phase in the Arab world.

  • Citizenship in the Modern Arab Context.

  • Changes in the structure of Arab political systems that raise new issues concerning citizenship, spreading out new practices related to civil society that results from the decline of patriarchy in the Modern Arab Societies.

  • Secularism and religiosity in the Arab Context in 21st century. The role of religiosity in the Arab culture, state political structure, the contrast between state secularism and religiosity of people. The impact of Globalization on both public and official religiosity.

  • The role of civil society in the Arab Context conceptually and practically.

  • Gender issues in the modern Arab Context.

  • Democratic Transitions in the Arab Context. The choice of Democracy; Democracy and Liberalism in the Arab Context. Democratic transitions and the role of economic and social conditions for such transition.

This conference is expected to become one the epochal intellectual gatherings in the Arab World. It will involve as part of the participants prominent Arab thinkers, many of whom have regional, and international reputation. 

The other part of the participants will be of the younger generation of Arab intellectuals. The combination of two generations at this even is intended to make the necessary link between generations and promote younger intellectuals at the same time. It is hoped that a good part of the innovative approaches and ideas will come from the younger generation.

Apart from the main participants who will deliver papers, there will be a group of participants who will participate in the discussions and debates that will exhaust the larger part of the event.

The participants will reflect to the possible extent both the geographic, and ideological diversity in the Arab World. The conference therefore will serve as a well-organized forum for debate amongst a group of diverse specialists of the Arab World. It will be organized in a manner that will lead to the emergence of debate among Arab youth in particular and Arabs in general.

The aims of this conference can be formulated as follows:

 

  1. On the level of the Department of Philosophy and Cultural Studies in Birzeit University the aim is to overcome the intellectual isolation from Arab intellectuals the was imposed by political circumstances. This workshop will serve to help build long-term relations and set basis for future cooperation with other Arab participants and their institutions.

  2. On the Palestinian level, conveying a sense of diversity and modernity in thought and culture that exists in "bigger portions" in some places in the Arab world will be an aim for the organizers.

  3. On the general academic level, it aims at encouraging the exchange of ideas amongst Arab intellectuals, as well as to define common ground and diversity amongst them. Another related aim is to create a forum for the promotion of intellectual participation in designing the future of the region.

  4. On the public level, it will aim at involving Arab societies in reflecting upon the common future. It should also help involve wider groups in inter-Arab debates on vital issues of national, regional, and global characters. It should facilitate the process of self-reflection amongst targeted audience. Intellectual debate will widen scopes and options within the existing trends in Arab thought reducing the effect of recently popular extremist trends.

The achievement of such aims is visualized as a multi step procedure. The first is to brainstorm on selected topics, then to formulate positions and develop arguments and reflect on them collectively. This part needs a closed atmosphere, where the effort of participants is not directed towards a wide audience, and not bound by the types of restrictions that accompany public sessions. The last step is to deliver this debate to the public in an intriguing manner.

We do not exclude the idea of holding a last session in the presence of the media (satellite TV for example) and selected public.

A book will be published based on the proceedings of this conference. Such a publication will ensure the follow up of the debate that the conference will create. At the same time this should not hinder the publication of papers in different journals in the Arab world.

Please send suggestions to the Department of philosophy and cultural studies at Birziet University or to the Centre for Stratigic Studies at the   University of Jordan no later than the first of September 2000. The final papers should be submitted before the 10th of December 2000.
