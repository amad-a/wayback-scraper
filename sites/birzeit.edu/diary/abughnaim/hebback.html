Discovering Oslo 2 Diary
22 March 1997
Abu Ghnaim Diary: "Back through Wadi-Al-Nar"

Back to work

Despite it being Saturday, we’re back at work today. Birzeit University (right) has its weekend on Friday and Sunday, for the Muslim and Christian staff and students. Personally, I always hope that when we finally make peace with Israel we’ll celebrate Shabbat on Saturday as well. In Palestine, the four-day working week is a distinct possibility.

Rumors that the police of the Sulta ("authority" - the shortened version of Sulta Wataneeyyah Falistineeyah, "Palestinian National Authority") have opened fire at Israeli troops in Hebron, like last September. Adli (left) - who also works in the PR office at Birzeit - asks if I want to go back tonight with him and crash over. I agree. I have visited Hebron with Adli many times before, the last during the redeployment on 17 January 1997. Seeing the clashes in different parts of the country gives a good overview of the situation. I felt it was going to escalate for sure. I was later to find out - unfortunately - how true this was.

Wadi Al-Nar...again

The drive through Wadi An-Nar affords me the sixth or seventh opportunity to photograph this amazing road, with the compiled results available on the next page, with enlargements. I'm still not - unlike Adli - sick of it. U2 are playing "Helter Skelter" on the car stereo. Adli likes music a lot and knows every word of the songs he likes, something that makes me smile.

Adli gives me a guided tour of the settlements on the way - Pisgat Ze’ev (above) and Ma’ale Adummim (below). Both are actually whole towns in their own right and roll on for miles, Israel’s castration of northern Jerusalem from the West Bank. Abu Ghnaim is the last hole to be plugged.

The closer we get to Hebron, the foggier it gets. We pass through Sureif, the village from which the Tel Aviv cafe bomber originated from. It is, of course, under curfew, and the next few weeks would see massive Israeli actions on the village. Tonight, a jeep sits at the entrance, its blue light shedding eerie beams on the road. This is one of the coldest areas in the West Bank. A last checkpoint before we arrive, which we drive around to save time, and we arrive at Adli's home.
