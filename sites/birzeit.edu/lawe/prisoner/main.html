No Peace Without Freedom

Urgent Action - Administrative Detainee on Hunger Strike
November 14

Today, over 4000 Palestinian political prisoner, from ages 14 to 73, languish in Israeli jails. Despite the signing of the Oslo Accords, which promised the release of all these prisoners, their number continues to grow. Lives are torn apart and families separated by this illegal detention.

Under international law and the Oslo Accords:

  • It is illegal for Israel to detain these prisoners in Israeli jails
  • All of these political prisoners should have been released following the Oslo Accords

According to the 4th Geneva Convention (to which Israel became a signatory in 1951) "Protected persons accused of offences shall be detained in the occupied territories, and if convicted they should serve their sentences therein" Article 76, Paragraph 1, IV Geneva Convention, 1949

"..deportations of protected persons from occupied territory to the territory of the occupying power or that of any other country are prohibited" Article 49, Paragraph 1, IV Geneva Convention

"Persons who have been accused of offenses or convicted by courts in the occupied territory shall be handed over at the close of occupation to the authorities of the liberated territory." Article 49, Paragraph 1, IV Geneva Convention

"Israel will release or turn over to the Palestinian side, Palestinian detainees and prisoners, residents of the West Bank and Gaza Strip" Article XVI, Oslo Accords.

Many families have lost their main means of support and are prevented from visiting their loved ones in prison. A general closure has been imposed on the West Bank and Gaza Strip since 1993, preventing Palestinians without special permits from entering Jerusalem or Israel. The number of permits issued depends on the severity of the closure at the time. Palestinian attorneys must also follow the same procedure as other Palestinians wishing to enter Israel.

Sha'ban Barham from Ramallah was detained in December 1995. According to testimony he provided to the human rights organization B'tselem, his family appointed Attorney Samara from Ramallah to represent him. However due to the closure, the attorney could not reach him for his hearing and his appeal was refused.

Requests to visit prisoners must be filed through the International Committee of the Red Cross, which is forwarded to the Israeli authorities. As of July 1996, there are two categories of Palestinians permitted to visit prisoners:

  • Parent, spouse, child or sister
  • Brothers aged under the age of 16 or over the age of 30

In addition, any visitor may be denied access on the basis of security and it is almost impossible for prisoners from Gaza to receive visits from their families due to the permanent closure of the Gaza Strip.

Over 500 of these prisoners have never been charged with any crime or faced any trial. These administrative detainees never know when or even if they will be released. For most of these prisoners their only crime is a critical view of the Oslo Accords - ironically a view also held by the Israeli government. Palestinians oppose the occupation of their land, the apartheid laws, and the daily humiliation of their people which has continued after Oslo. They seek only an alternative - and for that they are silenced. As in every struggle for freedom, the occupying power is frightened most by the thoughts and words of those who do not accept their occupation.

When the ANC was negotiating with the white apartheid state, ANC leader Nelson Mandela refused to sign before the release of his comrades who had struggled with him. He knew that any agreement without their release, would be a betrayal of their struggle.

This web page aims to show the real story of these prisoners. LAW, The Palestinian Society for the Protection of Human Rights and the Environment, has continuously campaigned for the rights of prisoners in both Israeli and Palestinian jails. LAW provides free legal services as well as publicizing the plight of these victims of the peace.

Now, more than at any time, international support is needed. Many of these prisoners are sick or elderly and face a long cold winter in confinement. Their families have lost a main means of support and are often prevented from visiting their loved-ones in jail. Join LAW's Solidarity Campaign.There can be no peace without freedom.
