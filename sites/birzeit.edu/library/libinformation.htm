INTRODUCTION:

The library is the intellectual heart of the university. The mission of Birzeit University Library is to provide access to information and research materials for all members of the University Community and the Palestinian people.

Guided introductions are provided at the beginning of each term, or by appointment. Tours cover the general use of the library and location of material. For more information on when tours are offered, or to schedule a tour, please contact the reference services staff.

The library also plays a leading role in information provision in Palestine and beyond. Users from other Palestinian universities, schools, other institutions may have limited access to the library to assist them in locating the information required for their researches and studies. Birzeit University Library welcomes all users, however, the collection and resources are primarily for the use of the Birzeit library community, and outside users may face restrictions on the level of services they may receive.

BUILDING:

The BZU library was established in 1924. The new "Yusuf Ahmed Alghanim" building of the current library was constructed in 1985 through a generous contribution from Alghanim Industries in Kuwait.

The library consists of four floors:

Ground Floor (1st floor): contains the periodicals, microfilms and microfiche room, media room, and staff offices for the Collection Development, Acquisitions, and Periodicals department.

Main floor (2nd floor): contains the entrance to the library, Circulation desk, Reference division, reference collection, reading hall, computer laboratory, and the staff offices for the Cataloging department.

Third floor: contains the Library Director's office, meeting room, reading hall, special collections, seminar room, books in science and engineering, LC classification Q - Z.

Fourth floor: contains books in arts & humanities, LC Classification A - P,  reading areas, study carrels, seminar room, training classroom, and staff areas.

COLLECTION:

The Birzeit University Library has a large collection of books and materials in English, Arabic and some other languages.  Approximately 60% of the materials in the library are in English. 

Collection Specifics:

  • Approximately 100,000 books (Arabic & English)
  • 850 periodical titles, of these 200 are current subscriptions (Arabic & English).
  • microforms: microfilms & microfiche on a variety of materials including indexes and back issues of older major Arabic newspapers
  • documents and special collections on Palestine affairs.
  • ten years back issues for four major Arabic newspapers: al-ayyam, al-Hayat al-Jadida, al-Quds, al-Ittihad
  • audio-visual aids: projector, television, video and microforms monitors.
  • The library is a depository library for the UNESCO publications since 1979. The first UNESCO collection were cataloged and classified as the UNESCO classification and are in the storeroom in the forth floor. If you need one of these older documents please ask the staff for assistance. The Library has started classifying these documents using the Library of Congress system, and newer documents can now be found in the general collection.

    SPECIAL COLLECTIONS MESSION STATEMENT

    The Special Collections unit of the Birzeit University main library has as its mission to provide research access to unique collections. These collections include materials that, because of rarity, source, condition, or form, are best handled separately from the regular collection. Access to the materials is provided by the OPAC, and the label SPC precedes the call numbers. The collections comprise of materials in the five languages presently approved for materials housed in the BZU main library: Arabic, English, French, German, and Hebrew, and they are interfiled within the collections. A knowledgeable staff is available to assist the patrons. The Special Collections room is located on the second floor of the Library, through the door near the photocopier.

    HOURS

    The Special Collections unit is open from 8:00 a.m. -- 4:00 p.m. Monday -- Thursday and Saturday. (Hours in the summer and during Ramadan may vary.)

Palestine Collection

This comprehensive collection is the largest of the collections. It includes, but is not limited to, published materials of the Palestinian National Authority, local research institutions and universities, communiqués from various Palestinian political groups and public records or historical documents that are related to Palestine. Heavily used items from this collection comprise the various statistical series published by the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics and reports issued by the Ministry of Planning and International Cooperation on specific Palestinian industries, e.g., stone cutting. Copies of the Oslo Accords, Hebron Protocol, Wye River Memorandum and Sharm el-Sheikh Memorandum are available here. Among the notable items in this collection are census reports published by the Government of Palestine during the Mandate period, and a vast majority of communiqués issued during the Intifada by the United National Command. The materials in this collection have the designation PAL after the call number.

Rare Books

The Rare Books collection consists of books that are scarce, uncommon or unique thus must be provided more attention and care. These include early imprints, volumes with exceptional bindings or illustrations, and landmark works. Over 20 volumes of the Author’s National edition, published between 1899 through 1918, from the series The writings of Mark Twain are placed here. The Rare Books collection contains many books of historical interest in Palestine, religion and travel. The oldest book in the collection is an 1804 publication of The Holy Bible. All rare books have the designation code RBK at the end of the call number.

BZU Archives

This collection houses selective material published by Birzeit University and bestowed to the library. Publications found here are copies of Birzeit University yearbooks, and communiqués from the Student Council and other student political groups. These materials have the code BZU attached to the call number.

Pamphlets

Pamphlets are defined as short printed publications unbound or with a paper binding. Their purpose is often to inform on a specific subject or address a controversial issue. This collection contains pamphlets that are not related to Palestine (pamphlets on Palestinian issues are found in the SPC Palestine collection). Topics addressed include economics, education, travel and tourism, art and literature, religion, technology, and medicine. The majority of the materials, however, deal with historical, political, and legal issues. A monthly newsletter published by Amnesty International is located here, as are several issues of VOICE: Intelligence Report. Each issue from this series concentrates on a particular region, i.e., Middle East. In addition, this collection contains the complete papers presented at two separate conferences: the 2nd Conference (1978) on the History of the Greater Syria, and a 1982 conference of Arabization. Several Unesco and United Nations publications are also housed here. The designation code for pamphlets is PAM.

CIRCULATION SERVICES:

Students must obtain a University identification card in their first semester. All students must validate their card each semester of enrollment. This card is necessary to borrow any reserve or circulating materials. The cards should not be lent to others. Faculty and staff may borrow materials after presenting a valid University ID card.   The library staff reserves the right to refuse to circulate materials if the proper identification is not presented.

All circulating materials are checked out and returned at the circulation desk.

The loan period varies between two and four weeks depending on the type of material and the status of the borrower. Materials can be renewed if no one has requested them. Students are expected to return materials when due.

The fine for overdue circulating materials is ½ NIS per day for regular material and 1NIS for reserve material. Students with overdue books or unpaid fines may not be able to register for classes or receive grades. Lost materials should be reported and the borrower is responsible for the replacement cost of the item or fined another copy.

RESERVE SERVICE: Faculty often put materials on reserve shelf for specific class assignments. These items are shelved at the circulation desk and are checked out with a valid library card for use in the library. Reserve books or materials are of two kinds:

  • Open reserve - meaning books may be checked out of the library for one night and are due back by 9 am the next working day.
  • Close reserve - meaning books must remain in the library at all times

Non-University users may be allowed to check out certain materials with presentation of proper identification and a monetary deposit.  However, the Library staff reserves the right to refuse to check out materials if they feel it is not in the best interest of the library to allow the materials to circulate.

REFERENCE SERVICES:

Reference books are those materials always available for use in the library. They include encyclopedias, dictionaries, atlases, documents, indexes, and other specialized sources. All reference books are listed in the computer, and have call number begins with a location note REF. These books do not circulate. Questions concerning how to locate legal materials and how to develop useful research strategies should be directed to the librarians working at the reference desk.

COMPUTER SERVICES:

The computer room is located on the main floor of the library, right next to the stairs.  In addition, there is a MINISIS terminal located on each floor.

  • The library uses the MINISIS computer system to provide library users with access to the catalog.  Records may be searched in Arabic or in English.  The catalog covers all books and Arabic journals in the library and will provide you with the call number (location) of the book.  Circulation information is not yet available.
  • MINISIS Databases - The MINISIS system has four different databases to allow you to search for different types of materials:
    • db user - searches all of the books in the system
    • db article - searches all Arabic journals in the system
    • db document - searches the special collection
    • db bzu per - bibliographic listing of all English periodicals available in the library
  • The computer lab contains 10 MINISIS terminals and 6 PC's for Internet and database access.
  • Library users may print from MINISIS or the Internet for a fee of 1 NIS for 3 pages, and they can scan any (image or text) for a fee of 1.5 NIS for each file (if they want to scan {save on disk only} without colored printing), or a fee of 5.0 NIS for each file (if they want to scan and print colored images).
  • Reference Staff is available to assist users with the computers. 
  • Due to the heavy demand, the Internet access is limited to one half hour per user.   Anyone wishing to use the Internet must sign up at the Reference Desk.
  • Library users may download information to floppy disks for free.
