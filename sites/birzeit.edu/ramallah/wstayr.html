Where to Stay

With at least three hotels presently under construction the number of places to stay in Ramallah is set to continue expanding.The ratings (as expressed by the little green faces) represent both the atmosphere of the hotel and the general treatment I received when visiting them, since this may well be representative of how customers will be treated.

Al-Hajal Hotel
(Lutheran St., tel. 02 998 6759)

Singles/doubles 120 NIS/150 NIS, including breakfast. All rooms have a telephone, television, and bathroom as well as a communal lounge area and kitchen.

Al-Wihdeh Hotel
(26 Main St., tel. 02 995 6452)

Recently refurbished, this hotel is bright and fresh. All the rooms are pleasant and most have their own bathroom, television, and telephone. Some have balconies, and private lounges. There is also a communal lounge with a breakfast area, chairs, sofas, a television and video machine and a view over the bustling streets of Ramallah. A selection of food and soft and alcoholic drinks are available. This hotel has a friendly and relaxed atmosphere and no curfew. Prices range between 80-150 NIS per night according to the type of room.

The Miami Pension Hotel-Restaurant
(Jaffa St., tel. 02 995 6808)

Singles/doubles 120 NIS/150 NIS including breakfast. Each room has a bathroom, telephone and television. The management brought their chef with them when they moved here from Jordan, apparently a wise move as the Italian restaurant attached to the hotel is excellent.

Plaza Hotel and Restaurant
(Jaffa St., tel. 02 995 2020)

Singles/doubles/triples at 60 NIS/120 NIS/140 NIS including breakfast. All rooms have a telephone, satellite television, bathroom and some have balconies. There is an attractive outdoor as well as an indoor restaurant serving a variety of western and Arabic food in a beautiful flower filled environment.



Ramallah Hotel/Motel
(tel. 02 995 3544, fax. 02 995 2559)

This hotel is set away from the town centre in a quiet suburban area with a view of the surrounding hills. While quite a trek to reach, its a nice walk through one of the more affluent suburban areas of the city however if you are carrying bags it would be better to take a taxi. The hotel has singles/doubles/triples at $50/$75/$90, suites (bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, lounge area) for between four and five people at $100 and a honeymoon/deluxe suite (lounge, kitchen, bedroom, balcony) at $150. All rooms are equipped with a telephone, satellite television, fridge and bathroom. Breakfast is included in the price, however tax will be added. With a restaurant, bar, lounges on each floor, an outside dining area and a planned coffee shop the hotel is tasteful designed and equipped to make your stay comfortable. The owner also runs a travel agency and so can assist in any touring plans you might have. To get here walk down Jaffa road past the Ramallah Muntaza, and follow the sign.

Star Mountain Guest House
(tel./fax. 02 995 6151)

Between Ramallah and Birzeit is this walled Moravian church compound which runs a school for mentally disabled children. The recently expanded school has a small farm with ducks, chickens, rabbits and a donkey which you will sometimes see wandering through the forested part of the compound. It also has a number of guest rooms looking over the beautiful terraced countryside in what would be a tranquil area to stay in.
