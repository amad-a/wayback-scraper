Background and Objectives

Based on our commitment at the Development Studies Programme/Birzeit University to spreading and deepening concepts of sustainable human development in Palestine and giving them concrete meaning, and based on our belief that there is an interconnection between research and theoretical work on the one hand and tangible practical work in the community, on the other, the Programme established on 3/1/1999 a new unit called “community outreach unit.” This is an action based unit,  inspired by the studies and research conducted by the Programme. It specializes in carrying out development projects, especially ones concerned with the development of the local community and corresponds to the concept of sustainable human development. The unit works by attracting the largest possible number of activists and local people and by incorporating them in sustainable local development.

The first project that has been carried out by this unit is “The Project for Developing Local Initiative in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip,” in cooperation with the United Nations Development Program “UNDP”, and with funding from the government of Japan. It is for one year from 3/1/1999 through 9/1/2000.

The basic idea for this project is to support local Palestinian groups in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip by promoting local initiatives and small local projects. Local groups are provided with necessary training and some funding to carry out their serious initiatives relating to a development field. In this way, their self-confidence is boosted since they are in a position to determine their needs and priorities and are capable of working to meet these pressing local needs.

What distinguishes this project from others is that it reinforces local development but without determining in advance people’s needs in this or that site. No one is acting as a substitute or a representative for the local community. The people are the ones who come up with the concept, they are the ones who are required to put a plan to execute their idea, and finally they are the ones who actually carry out the project. They also play a central role in the evaluation process through a series of monthly and quarterly meetings and final conferences etc. Therefore, the project is not only expected to come up with a group of small local projects, but also with a large number of a sustainable local cadre with both theoretical and practical development experience.

Finally, the project will be executed by a team consisting of: project coordinator, project coordinator field assistant for the Gaza Strip, field coordinator for mid and southern West Bank, field coordinator for the northern West Bank, media coordinator, and a trainer.

Project’s Objectives

Based on the above, the project aims to achieve the following objectives:

    • To execute 15 local projects in the West Bank and Gaza, which will concretely benefit thousands of people and make up for an obvious shortcoming not covered by other institutions, both governmental and non-governmental, especially in distant locations and among marginalized groups.

    • To develop the role of local institutions in serving the local community and to reinforce people’s support for these institutions and improve attitude to them.

    • To establish a network of relations between local institutions and to upgrade cooperation and coordination among them.

    • To train a sustainable cadre of institutional workers with practical experience and adequate training to play an effective role in local development.

    • To attract large numbers of local residents to participate in the development process and to integrate the concept of sustainable human development in the local development thinking through local initiatives.

    • To spread the concepts of sustainable human development as widely as possible among the public and in a tangible way.

    • To encourage local initiatives in the local Palestinian society by highlighting these initiatives and publicizing the successful ones.

How Sites Were Chosen

The fifteen sites to be covered by the project were chosen according to the following criteria:

  • An advisory committee was established consisting of 15 people widely knowledgeable in the field of local development and  representing a number of governmental and non-governmental organizations.

  • The advisory committee has established a set of criteria for choosing the sites to be included in the project.

  • 75 sites have been chosen in the West Bank and the Gaza Strip and information has been gathered from these sites through a questionnaire. On average, five sites were chosen from each region.

  • The advisory committee met again to choose 15 sites out of the 75 recommended sites.

Training Workshops

A training Programme for Unit Members

Three training workshops were organized for the field coordinators and other unit members. The aim of these workshops was to deepen the participants’ understanding of this project and to develop their abilities to reach and deal with local groups. The first day of training, conducted by Dr. Nader Said, focused on concepts of human development and the way they inform the philosophy and objectives of the project. The second day, conducted by Dr. Leila Al-‘atshan, focused on means of communication with people and ways of empowering them. The third day was about strategic planning and was conducted by Badr Al-A’raj.

A Training Programme for the Sites

The Programme organized ten training workshops in the locations that were chosen by the advisory committee. The training was done by Dr. Leila Al-‘Atshan.  Some of the locations of these workshops are:

North Gaza Region—The Bedouin Village

On 7/14/1999 a training workshop was held in the Bedouin Village. It was attended by more than 300 residents of the village, among whom was the engineer Ahmad Tawfiq Baroud, head of the Environment Department in the north Gaza region. The workshop was held in a house under construction. The training commenced at about 9:30 a.m. and lasted until 3:30 p.m.; it was conducted by Dr. Leila Al-‘atshan, who is a project consultant as well. She noticed that there was a communication problem among the trainees; therefore, she taught them some means of effective communication, then some exercises were done. A discussion followed about the available resources in the village,  the obstacles facing the residents, and the activities they had carried out to improve their conditions. The session culminated in dividing the trainees into groups; each group designed a plan to execute a project in the village. After that the needs and priorities of the village were discussed.

The participants were representative of different groups in the village, including representatives from the Village Development Society including the chairperson; the head and members of the local committee, which has become a follow up committee for the water issue; and village notables, intellectuals, enthusiastic youth, a woman activist, and a female engineering student. All emphasized that this was the first time that they had ever taken part in such a training and that this is the first time that men and women work together, since the villagers are so conservative that the elections of the society were limited to men only.

The day prior to the workshop, a meeting took place with the engineer Jamal Baghdadi, who is prominent in the Ministry of Housing, which is the ministry supervising the process of transferring the Bedouin residents from their original place of residence to the Bedouin Village, established in 1997. He provided us with useful information that helped us understand the “story” of this village.

On 7/22/1999 a meeting was held, attended by a small number of people, to discuss the work plan in the village. The following suggestions were made:

establishing a vocational center for girls. The society and a number of women will present a written detailed plan about the importance of such a project, the possibilities of its execution and success, and its costs. This kind of center can arrange for a number of vocational courses. It can play an important role in strengthening women’s economic power and preparing them for work and increase the income of many families etc. The center can also be a base to activate %50 of the village residents (the women’s sector), especially that it is a completely neglected sector,by organizing a number of educational courses for women.

Holding an open meeting between the residents of the Bedoin Village and the Ministry of Housing and the Ministry of Local Affairs. The former has given its preliminary consent.

Holding an open meeting between the residents and the Ministry of Environment to discuss the issue of sewers and environmental pollution.

Organizing a training workshop for those responsible for the society and the local committee about effective communication and the way to work as a team, strategic planning, and evaluating and promoting the successes they have had so far.

Helping the residents reach funding institutions, both governmental and non-governmental, and working on attracting these institutions (as much as possible) to the Bedouin Village to study its needs and provide for them.

Continuing the organized media campaign about the problems and needs of the Bedouin Village.

Involving village representatives in the quarterly meeting of the Gaza Strip area and inviting various institutions to attend the meeting to help in the networking process.

Following up on the preliminary promises made by the society’s and local committee’s representatives regarding the representation of women in the administrative board of the society and the local committee.

The Hebron Region—Al-Samou’

A training workshop was held on 7/26/99 on the premises of the Samou’ Charitable Society. It was attended by 26 people, 19 of whom ……

The majority work in the Samou’ Charitable Society and in the traditional rug project sponsored by the Society. The workshop was attended by the head of the Samou’ Society, and representatives of CPR program for the rehabilitation of the disabled. The discussion focused on the needs of Al Samou’s local institutions and on the traditional rug project.

Al-Samou’s Charitable Society has received a grant from the Welfare Association to support the traditional rug project. Provided that the Programme is satisfied with what is going in Al-Samou’, the following suggestions were made:

contributing to solving the problem of marketing the traditional rug, whether by organizing a course on marketing or a media campaign about the distinctive qualities of the traditional rug and the importance of supporting it.

activating the center for the rehabilitation of the disabled and contributing to solving the administrative and financial problems faced by the center.

organizing workshops about ways to cooperate with and to help local institutions and about ways to overcome family obstacles and seasonal work.

organizing a workshop about early marriage.

developing the parents’ council in Al-Samou’

helping the local club overcome some obstacles, including financial ones, by seeking aid from concerned organizations or by developing the skills of local members.

supporting the efforts to solve the environmental problems facing Al-
Samou’, where there is a high percentage of cancer caused by the chemical radiation from the Demona reactor and the Israeli chemical factories in Al-Naqab. The residents have initiated some steps that need to be supported.

supporting the efforts of residents to solve the problem of overcrowding in schools. It is possible to address the Ministry of Education and to encourage the residents to do so.

developing the agricultural sector by encouraging agricultural societies to pay attention to Al-Samou’, especially that agriculture is considered a main source of income for that region.

The Bethlehem Region: Al-Khas and Nu’man Village

A training workshop was held between 10:00 a.m and 3:00 p.m on 7/30/1999 on the premises of Al-Fardous School in the village. It was attended by 20 people, including the head of the village council. Based on this workshop, the following suggestions were made:

Supporting the project “Center for Rural Development” since there is much enthusiasm and readiness among the residents, who are willing to contribute money, effort, and expertise.

Organizing a child festival, donations will be the main source of funding. This will be the first activity for the center.

Organizing courses and campaigns to raise awareness concerning the early marriage phenomenon.

Inviting the Ministry of Agriculture and other agricultural institutions to develop the agricultural sector in this fertile region, which is threatened by the Israelis.

Organizing a literacy course.

Using the proposed center to organize vocational courses for women.

Inviting the agricultural relief committees to open agricultural roads in the region.

Inviting the medical relief committees to activate the proposed health center since Pecdar has approved the project provided that there is someone who would be responsible for the center since the ministry of health is incapable of that right now.

The Toulkarem Region: Nour Shams Camp

A training workshop was held on Friday 8/7/1999 at the premises of the women’s center in the Nour Shams camp. It started at 10:30 a.m. and lasted until 4:00 p.m. It was attended by 23 of the camp’s residents: 8 women and 15 men. The first part of the workshop was attended by representatives of local newspapers and the Voice of Palestine.

Based on this workshop, the following suggestions were made:

Obviously Nour Shams Camp is not exactly a marginalized locale (especially compared to other places). Therefore, it was suggested that our work in the camp should be with a marginalized group, especially the disabled, the elderly, or children (in that order of priority). It was suggested that we help the residents implement the project of equipping and developing the disabled center. For this purpose, the residents were asked to prepare a clear plan about this proposed project.

Supporting the residents in the campaign they have started months ago to solve the problem of pollution and disease caused by Wadi Al-Zoumer. In this context, it is possible to push towards developing coordination between the service committee in the camp and the rest of municipal and local councils hurt by the wadi (such as ‘Anabta Municipality, the municipal council of Beet Leed, and that of Bal’a).

Supporting the residents in their attempt to receive funds to implement some of the vital projects mentioned above.

Organizing a campaign in the camp to face the problem of dropping out of school.

Organizing a training course for those active in various local institutions about administrative skills, development, planning, and lobbying etc.

Nablus Region: Beet Hasan Village

A training workshop was held on Friday 8/13/1999 at a school in Beet Hasan village, which is located in the Nablus region. The workshop started at 10: 00 a.m. and lasted until 3:30 p.m. It was attended by 25 men.

Based on this workshop, the following was suggested:

The main project we can support is that of reviving the Charitable Nasreyeh Society because it used to carry out a variety of activities such as training courses for women, kindergartens, cleaning etc.

Another option is that of establishing a youth cultural center or club. What is required of us is to help the residents obtain a permit by going to the Ministry of Sports and Youth, and to help them rent a place and supply it with the necessary equipment.

Considering the high costs required by the project of establishing a health center, the participants agreed that it would be better to develop the existing Medical relief clinic so that it has a resident doctor all the time (as before). We can coordinate with the Medical Relief to encourage them to improve their services in the Nasareyeh area.

It is possible to open a communication channel with the Ministry of Agriculture and encourage them, either by direct invitation or through media pressure, to focus on the Beet Hasan area by providing the farmers with the services they need.

Work can be done to vitalize the women’s sector in Beet Hasan, whether by asking women’s groups to work there, by organizing lectures and training workshops, or by using the society or club we are going to support to activate women (by, for instance, appointing one or two days a week for women’s activities).

It was suggested that the Development Studies Programme organize a training workshop on the problem of marketing agricultural produce or the Palestinian farmer and marketing and competition problems. This is a problem suffered by many villages whether in the north such as Beet Hasan, or in the middle such as Beet Daquoa, or in the south such as Al-Samou’.

Pressuring the Ministry of Public Works to carry out its responsibilities in the region and help its residents to face winter.

The Advisory Committee

An advisory committee for the project was established and it consists of 15 people experienced in the fields of development and local communities. Eight of them are residents of the Gaza Strip and seven reside in the West Bank. Five work as general directors in various Palestinian National Authority ministries, the other ten work in non-governmental organizations. Some are university professors and specialists.  

The first meeting of the advisory committee was held in the West Bank on 3/5/99 in the Development Studies Programme - Ramallah, and in the Gaza Strip on 5/26/99 .  The project was met with much enthusiasm by the members, who have high hopes for it. According to them, this project represents a new experiment which can be developed through bigger projects in the future. These projects will have far-reaching implications since they will raise the level of people’s initiative and encourage local institutions to cooperate among themselves to develop the local community. This will not only win the people a much-needed local project, but will also give them a unique development experience that they will gain through active involvement. Another meeting of the advisory committee was decided will take place in Gaza on 6/26/1999 to choose the sites for the project.

Questionnaire

Projects are chosen by designing a questionnaire according to criteria established by the unit’s advisory committee. A nine-page questionnaire was designed, which included 30 questions focusing on the demographics of the suggested location; information about nearby sites; the institutions and local groups present in that location; the economic situation; the available and unavailable services; health and educational conditions; the spirit of initiative and cooperation; the relation among local institutions; service projects and infrastructure projects which have been implemented in the location in the past two years; the political, cultural, and sports activities taking place in the location during the past year; the proposed initiatives and small public projects; and finally the impressions of the field coordinator about the location. The questionnaire was answered during the interviews which was conducted with local activists and those in positions of authority in local institutions in the location that fit the established criteria.

Thus, the field coordinators went to the field armed with the necessary training, with a document outlining the criteria that they need to consider, and with a comprehensive questionnaire which is the basis for any interview to be conducted with a local activist or a local institution.
