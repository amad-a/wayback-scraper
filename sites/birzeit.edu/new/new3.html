  • 22 May 1997 - The "where it's at" section of Complete Guide to Palestine's Websites, found on the homepage, is featuring the website of Palestinian journalist Daoud Kuttab this month, since his arrest yesterday by the Palestinian Authority.

  • 17 May 1997 - The Complete Guide to Palestine's Websites has updated with a few new websites, bringing the total to 64. The Websites About Palestine section of the Complete Guide now includes links to some of the most interesting or significant Israeli websites. In related news, the Israeli Internet Blackout is coming on May 29th, 1997. What has the Israeli Internet got to do with any Palestinian website? Nothing, if you ignore the fact that all Palestinian websites are still part of the Israeli Internet. Confused? See Birzeit and the Web's look at "Palestine and the World Wide Web".

  • 6 May 1997 - The Department of Economics is now online. Slowly but surely, the various academic nooks and crannies in the university are creeping on the Web. Congratualtions to the Economics Department for being the most recent!

    SAFE TO SURF AT BIRZEIT - Yesterday, we rated our website with the Recreational Software Advisory Council, an organisation that enables parents to filter out content they do not wish their children to see. Basically, the whole website is completely acceptable with regards to sex, nudity, language and violence, but we did mark two subsections as being potentially disturbing for children. These were On the Ground in Ramallah: Reports from a Town Become Battlefield, our visual and textual record of the September 1996 clashes, and A Personal Diary of the Israeli-Palestinian Conflict, which also includes some images and text relating to "the death of human beings" and a few "mild expletives". Users of Microsoft Internet Explorer version 3.0 and above can enable the built-in RSAC filter very easily.

    It should be noted that none of the material on either website could be said to be gratuitous by any stretch of the imagination. A Personal Diary has been chosen as a Norton Publishers resource for secondary school students studying the Arab-Israeli conflict, so one can safely assume that the Diary has a sound educational value and - after all - sometimes being a little disturbed is a good thing.

    In the future, we will be re-rating the On the Ground website on a page-by-page basis, as only one or two pages (related to the intensive care ward in Ramallah hospital) actually include disturbing images out of a total of about 50. The Personal Diary website will be a little more difficult as it is already due for a directory restructure and comprises of around 4 times as many pages. But again, we are only talking about 5 or 6 pages out of a potential 120 or more.

    Why go to all this trouble? The advantage of rating your websites is that some parents or guardians may choose to block access to all sites which are not rated, a reality which would make the whole Birzeit website unavailable. Also, the filter has gradiations, and while some parents may understandably not wish their children to see "graphic sexual violence", they may believe it is a good thing for them to be exposed to existing realities of "violence in a social context".

The Birzeit Webmaster and webteam will be on holiday from Wednesday 16th until Wednesday 30th (inclusive). E-mails to webmaster@birzeit.edu will be answered after this period. Have a nice holiday!

  • 7 April 1997 - It's election time! The Birzeit Web Team have put up a website to cover the current student council elections. The site offers the election results from the last two years, background to the proportional representation system, photos from previous years, details of the competing blocs, and daily photos and updates on the developing campaigns. This year's results will be posted on Wednesday 9 April 1997, at 8:00pm Palestinian Time (GMT+3hrs). All in all, a useful website.

  • 2 April 1997 - 20-year-old Birzeit student Abdallah Khalil Abdallah Salah was shot dead on Saturday. The university sent out a press release and the Abu Ghnaim section of A Personal Diary was updated with reports and photographs. The university community has spent the last three days in mourning. Regular readers of What's New? will notice that it is only 51 days since our last student died as a result of Israeli violence, on 6 February 1997.

    The university's Press Release Archive has been reorganised and a missing release from 1996 added along with all of the press releases from 1997. A new feature includes a link to whatever the latest release is, on the Press Release Archive index.

  • 27 March 1997 - The Center for Research and Documentation of Palestinian Society's website is now up and running, in time for the coming conference at Birzeit for Deir Yassin's 49th anniversary. The website - like the Center - is partly dedicated to recording the history of Palestinians who lived in towns and villages which were in territories that were annexed to become modern day Israel. Also, Birzeit News covered the clashes yesterday in Ramallah.

  • 18 March 1997 - A Personal Diary gets a surprise award and goes to Abu Ghnaim. Also, the Human Right Action Project's homepage gets a facelift.

  • 25 February 1997 - The Academic Freedom First website is gearing up for a 3rd March petition drive. Check out their website and support the Gazan students in their quest for the "right" to education.

  • 17 February 1997 - Birzeit News has a few interesting new entries that shed light on student life here.

  • 6 February 1997 - Birzeit University's Complete Guide to Palestine's Websites has been updated. New additions can be found in the "academic & research", "government & services", "human rights" and "internet services" sections. Palestine now has a grand total of 61 websites. As you tour the website, you will also notice advertising, a new feature. Before you hit 'send' in your e-mail programme, note that this will bring in revenue for the university's web activities and fear not - you will not be seeing advertising spread all over the university website.

    Also new, check out the MARHABA! directory of Palestinians with e-mail worldwide. Sign up today, if you are a Palestinian! You can search for fellow wired Palestinians by name, present location, home town, employment, and others, an excellent service brought to you by Jack Rabah, in cooperation between Palnet, Birzeit University and Baraka. It is hoped that the new directory will put Palestinians directly in touch with the approach of the final status talks, when the future of Palestine will be decided and will need discussion amongst Palestinians everywhere.

    Unfortunately, we just heard that Yasser Abdul Ghani, a Birzeit student injured in the clashes at end-September 1996, passed away today after almost 4-and-a-half months in intensive care. The Birzeit University community would like to take this opportunity to say "Lahoor rahmeh walakum min ba'adahe toolal-bakaa'" ("Mercy upon him and for you a long life") to Yasser's family. The university community will be having a memorial rally when we return from the 'Eid al-Fitter break, on Wednesday. For those who have not seen the website yet about the clashes, and want links to the sections that mention Yasser, here they are: On the ground in Ramallah website | Yasser's mother in Ramallah hospital | Yasser in the Intensive Care Unit | Yasser's father in Ramallah hospital. Some images will be disturbing.
