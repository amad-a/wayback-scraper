Professional Experience:

         Feb. 99 : Professor at the computer department of Birzeit University. My main field of interest turns
              around deduction and inference for Goal-Based Agents.

        Nov. 94 - Nov. 98: Graduate Student at LAAS-CNRS, Robotics and Artificial Intelligence Group (RIA),
              Toulouse - France.

       Dec. 93 - June 95: Participation in the European MARTHA project (Mobile Autonomous Robots for
              Transportation and Handling plications).

        The project objectives are to study and to develop the systems necessary for planning and executing
        tasks for a fleet of cargo transport robots. The purpose of my thesis is to enable several robots to plan
        and coordinate their activities in a distributed way. The proposed approach is based on a generic and
        incremental process, called the Plan Merging Paradigm, that allows a large number of mobile robots to
       plan and execute simultaneously several kinds of tasks while sharing the same workspace.

        The paradigm involves a deliberative and a reactive aspect. The deliberation allows plan construction,
        detection and reasoning on plan interactions while reactivity ensures their synchronization and
        execution failure treatment.

        The implementation of the proposed approach has lead to a realistic coordination scheme and a great
        number of experiments has reinforced our conviction about the validity of the operational performance of
        our system. About thirty simulated robots and three real ones in a partially structured environments
       have shown the efficiency in terms of autonomy and robustness of our system.
