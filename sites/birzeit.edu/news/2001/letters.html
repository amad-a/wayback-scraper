The BZU March 2001 Blockade: Letters of Solidarity

Posted: March 20, 2001

INTERNATIONAL SOLIDARITY AGAINST ISRAELI OCCUPATION AND SIEGE

Birzeit University has been receiving scores of messages by email, phone and fax, expressing solidarity with the University and the Palestinian community in these trying times. Solidarity messages have also included copies of protest letters sent to the Israeli government, in support of the Birzeit calls to open the roads and remove checkpoints.
Following is a small selection and quotes from the solidarity messages that have been received mainly by the public relations office. We are very grateful to all who stood with us including those whom we did not get copies of their messages.

Press Release
Motion of the Free University of Brussels (ULB) Approved Unanimously by the Board of Directors on Monday March 26, 2001, Concerning the Events Occurring Around the University of Bir-Zeit.
The board of directors of the ULB,

Finding:
That the Arab-Israeli peace process has been blocked since the visit of Ariel Sharon, the leader of the Likud party, to Haram-al-Sharif (Temple Mount) on September 28, 2000;

That this visit, regarded as an 'act of provocation' by the UN Security Council (Security Council Resolution 1322 dated October 7, 2000, paragraph 1), triggered the violence which ensued in the territories occupied by Israel and that the Security Council has condemned this violence, 'in particular the excessive use of force against Palestinians' (ibid. paragraph 2);

That it is in the context of this violent cycle that the Israeli army destroyed, on March 7, 2001, portions of the only road connecting the University of Bir-Zeit to the city of Ramallah, seriously undermining the educational and research activities of this university, comprising 5000 students and 700 academic and administrative staff;

Recalling that the ULB has maintained co-operative relations with the University of Bir-Zeit since 1990, and concluded on June 3, 1993, a formal co-operation agreement for five years, which it renewed in 1998,
1. Considers that Israel has not properly justified the measures taken against the University of Bir-Zeit;
2. Observes that these measures are contrary to the most fundamental rules of international humanitarian law, inter alia the rules of the 4th Geneva Convention of August, 12, 1949 on the protection of civilians in times of war, in particular article 33, which prohibits collective punishment as well as reprisals against protected persons;
3. Observes also that by denying the inhabitants of the village of Bir-Zeit access to relief and supplies, the destruction of aforementioned road is incompatible with the spirit of articles 55 and 56 of the 4th Convention cited above;
4. Finds that the Security Council has in many instances ascertained that this Convention applies to the occupied territories and must be "scrupulously" respected by Israel (e.g. SC Resolution 1322 of October, 7, 2000);
5. Condemns the measures taken by Israel against the University of Bir-Zeit, on grounds of their international illegality and brutal and vexatious posture towards the local population;
6. Declares that these measures also affect the Free University of Brussels through its co-operative arrangement with the University of Bir-Zeit;
7. Requests that Israel bring to an end these measures and repair the road to Bir-Zeit;
8. Urges the Belgian government, in accordance with article 1 common to the four Geneva conventions of 1949, on the one hand to exhort Israel to conform with its obligations under these instruments, and on the other, to encourage the European Union to launch initiatives obliging Israel to comply with its responsibilities and to accept necessary compromises in order to reach a just and equitable peace.

We, members of French universities and faculty and researchers of the University of Paris, express our deep indignation at the unacceptable conditions of surveillance and obstruction to free movement, which are imposed by you by military means upon the students and faculty of the Palestinian University of Birzeit.
This constraint and this violence are in violation of the international laws that regulate the behavior of an occupying army.
We are asking you to put an end to this situation, which is revolting to all those who cherish the most fundamental rights to education.

(This was also published in daily papers Al-Ayyam and Al-Hayat al-Jadeeda)

To Foreign Minister Shimon Peres
Your government must end the siege warfare against Palestinian civilians immediately. The siege is a form of collective punishment, prohibited under international law, and it is contrary to the most basic and elementary principles of international morality. You must not continue to place hundreds of thousands under virtual imprisonment denying them access to medical facilities, to education institutions, jobs, and markets.
As a Nobel Peace Prize holder, you are undoubtedly aware that the use of excessive force by your military and settlers against civilians and children and your current attempts to smother the people and starve them into submission represent the worst form of cruelty and inhuman behavior.
Even your own generals are on record stating that that the siege has not and will not deter attacks against Israeli soldiers and settlers. In fact, contrary to your thinking this siege will only increase the people's resolve to resist injustice and the illegal military occupation. The siege must end and must end now,
Mr. Foreign Minister.
Let the people live.
