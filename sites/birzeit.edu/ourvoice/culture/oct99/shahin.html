Notes on Palestinian Arabic with Speech Samples

By Kimary Shahin, Birzeit University and University of British Columbia.

The material below provides a taste, so to speak, of Palestinian Arabic, the colloquial Arabic spoken by Palestinians. This vernacular is actually a collection of many colloquials that vary with social group (urban, rural/villager, or bedouin) (see, e.g., Cadora 1992) and geographic location.1 Three aspects are highlighted here: the consonantal inventory, lexicon (vocabulary), and intonation. The speech samples are from men aged 20-25, from Khalil (Hebron), Nablus, Rafat, and Gaza.

Lexicon

They also differ with respect to certain words. For example, the word meaning 'here' is one syllable for some speakers, two syllables for others, and the quality of the (initial) vowel varies in the following sentence "What are you doing here laughing?":

Khalil Urban Lexicon - "What are you doing sitting here laughing?"

Nablus Urban Lexicon - "What are you sitting here doing? Are you laughing at us?"

Rafat Urban Lexicon - "What are you doing sitting here laughing away?"

Intonation

There is also variation in intonation, that is, the raising or lowering of voice pitch over sentences and phrases. For example, while most speakers use a fall or fall-rise pattern for wh-questions, Khalil speakers frequently use fall, fall-rise: "How much is a kilo of fish today?"

Khalil Urban: Fall, Fall-Rise

Nablus Urban:Fall

Rafat Rural: Fall-Rise

Gaza Urban:Fall
