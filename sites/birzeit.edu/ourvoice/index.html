What's New


  • Taken from the new Journal of Palestinian Archaeology, we reprint an articleKhirbet Birzeit 1996, 1998-1999: Preliminary Results Khirbet Birzeit is a multi- period site with an occupation extending from the Iron Age to the Ottoman period. The evidence from the excavations indicates that, after the early phase of the Ottoman period, the site was exploited by the inhabitants of Birzeit mainly for agricultural purposes.This article details results from 3 seasons of excavation. Added 26/4/2000.


  • Samia A. Halaby, a free lance artist from New York contributed
    an article titled: THE LANDSCAPE OF PALESTINE IN ARABIC ART published in Birzeit University publication The Landscape of Palestine: Equivocal Poetry (pp. 175-190), a volume that sums The Fifth International Conference convened by Birzeit University under the theme Landscape Perspectives on Palestine, which took place during November 12-15, 1998. Added 15/02/2000.


  • Albert Aghazarian head of the Public Relations Office in Birzeit University writes an article: Would Jerusalem soften its heart of stone? for a publication called "Christian Voices From The Holy Land Bethlehem 2000 Palestine Year One?" published by the Bethlehem 2000. Added 16/12/99.


  • Palestinian Arabic has a rich variation depending on location. In this article, Kimary Shahin from Birzeit University and the the University of British Columbia analyses different speech samples from Gaza, Rafat, Nablus and Hebron. With Audio. Added 16/10/99.



  • A new article by Professor Roger Heacock entitled Between Systems and Salami: US Policy Towards the Palestinians and the Middle East in the Late Twentieth Century. The article traces the successive policies of US administrations towards the Middle East and proposes that sovereign states tend to range in their foreign policies between two strong alternative poles, and that this pendulum-like movement often occurs between successive administrations. Added 9/10/99.



  • Professor Roger Heacock examines the evolution of the Palestinian national movement with a particular focus on the "inside" and the "outside" or locals and returnees, in his article "Locals and Returnees in the Palestinian National Authority (PNA): A Historical Perspective". The article is taken from the book (pictured right) "The Becoming of Returnee States: Palestine, Armenia, Bosnia" published by the Graduate Insititute of International Studies, Birzeit University 1999 Added 4/10/99.



  • George Giacaman, Dean of Graduate Studies at Birzeit University, discusses the features of the Algerian situation that might be relevant to the Palestinian context in his article Order and Disorder After Oslo: Some Parallels Between Algeria and Palestine Added 27/9/99.



  • George Giacaman, Dean of Graduate Studies at Birzeit University, discusses the Oslo Accords and their affects on Palestinian society, the PLO and civil society in his article "In the Throes of Oslo: Palestinian Society, Civil Society and the Future". The article is taken from a book edited by Giacaman, entitled "After Oslo". To order the book, click here. Added 27/4/99.



  • Palestinian humor has passed through several stages since the beginning of the negotiations with the Israeli government. Sharif Kanaana examines these different stages and what they reveal about Palestinian attitudes towards the "Peace Process" in this article, "Palestinian Humor and the Peace Process". Added 30/1/99.

Journal of Palestinian Archaeology (JPA) is a new journal, published by the Palestinian Institute of Archaeology of Birzeit University.

Editor: Khaled Nashef.

JPA will appear in two issues per year (January and July), between 96 and 128 pages each. The first issue (112 pages) is scheduled for January 2000, and will appear in February.

JPA focuses on the archaeology of historical Palestine, its geographical horizon, however, encompasses countries of the Eastern Mediterranean, including the Arabian Peninsula. It includes articles in archaeology, ancient writings, ethnography, Old and New Testament. JPA prefers studies exhibiting new primary material, such as fieldwork reports, collections of artifacts, inscriptions, or individual finds or texts. JPA also seeks articles furnishing innovative concepts, interpretations, and new directives in the archaeology of Palestine and the Arab World.

In addition to the specialists, JPA also addresses readers who view their interest in archaeology and history as a crucial step towards shaping Palestinian historical consciousness. It encourages participation of non-specialist or graduate students, when their contributions enrich the substance of the Journal.

JPA is the platform of the Palestinian Institute of Archaeology (PIA) of Birzeit University. It expresses the ambition of the Institute to enhance among Palestinians an interest in archaeology as an integral part of their history. JPA is the concrete outcome of a policy aimed at promoting fieldwork, research, publication and the development of a graduate program. Click here for more information. Also available in Arabic.
