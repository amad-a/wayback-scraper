1999 Annual Report

Accomplishments

Data Transmission, Fixed and Mobile Telephony

National Transmission Links

The year 1999 was one of accomplishment for the actual construction phase of the national transmission network. Focusing on this strategic goal, the company executed the following essential projects in 1999:

Executing excavation operations besides fitting, connecting and testing about 400km fiber optic cables. Thus, total working cables currently number 540km.
Installing, testing & operating 52 SDH devices of different levels (sub-STM-1, STM-1, STM-4).Installing digital distribution frames (DDF) in about 25 sites (switch/RSU).
Operating a large number of switches and RSUs through the utilization of the SDH over fiber technology
Concluding a huge part of the backbone network project and thus preparing for the disconnection from Israeli counterpart.
Making available high bandwidth capacity on the Palestinian network sufficient for many years ahead, including multi-utilization (voice, data, video).
Preparing a unique qualified cadre capable of dealing with the various technical aspects of the company.

The national backbone network constructed during 1999, makes the vision of total network independence from Israeli counterpart to be implemented in stages, more tangible. The first stage towards comprehensive disconnection has been executed with national traffic being routed currently through transmission links. The second stage of the load, sharing/protection, has been underway since the end of 1999.

Digital Microwave Network

Microwave-Interconnection between the West Bank & Gaza

This project is seen as the "backbone" for the Palestinian national independent telecommunications network. Its purpose is to interconnect the transmission links of the West Bank with the transmission links of the Gaza Region. This will ameliorate the need for traffic between the West Bank and Gaza to run through the Israeli Telecommunication Company network.
With microwave interconnectivity projects executed and in actual service, all the main cities of the West Bank are interconnected together with the Gaza Region. These projects are as follows:

Hebron - Gaza link
Expanding radio links of the cellular network.
The Jordan Valley Area Project; work is proceeding on connecting the Jordan valley area (Jehricho) with Southern and Mid West Bank areas through another project of which 50% is completed.
Microwave interconnection; Sinjel - Ramallah.
Ar-Ram Microwave project.
Radio links in various areas upon securing telephony connectivity.

Data Services

Expanded data transmission services is a component of PalTel's strategy. Commercial service is expected to be launched by the end of April 2000. At both national and international levels, PalTel's data transmission service will roll out, offering Frame-Relay technology at first, followed shortly by the introduction of ATM services. At completion, all computer centers will be connected with new digital transmission networks. These offerings serve the vital needs of the Palestinian academic and other institutions.

Current Data Services Projects:
Digital Leased Lines

In preparation for the Palestinian information transmission project, digital leased-line circuits are currently deployed to multiple economic institutions for inter-branch and private network communications. A total of 325 leased circuits are currently serving 75 national institutions for monthly fees approved by the Ministry of Post and Telecommunications. A 100 percent completion record was accomplished for the 1999 plans as follows:

All remote areas have been connected with the leased digital lines as part of the Paltel private network.
Frame Relay service has been initiated and offered to the main cities throughout Palestine. This service will expand following the deployment of the multi-services network, expected to be placed in service by the end of May 2000.
The network has been expanded to include extra numbers. By the end of 1999, there are about 325 leased lines compared with 123 at the end of 1998.

Internet Project:
Installation of needed equipment for the backbone interconnectivity and construction of an Internet site have been completed. Equipment has been installed and programmed. Plans call for Internet services to be provided throughout Palestine by the beginning of 2000 offering connectivity for all ISPs, institutions and companies.

Recognition of the New Palestinian Telephone Gateway 970

Palestine received its 970 independent telephone code from the International Telecommunications Union in 1999. Until recently, all telephone calls to Palestine had to go through the Israeli code 972. Currently, 33 countries are utilizing the new Palestinian international code 970 as follows: Algeria, Australia, Bahrain, Belgium, Brazil, Canada (Teleglope), China, Denmark, Egypt, Finland-Sonerra, Greece, Hungary, India, Indonesia, Ireland, Jordan, Kuwait, Morocco, Netherlands, Oman, Peru, Qatar, Romania, Saudi Arabia, Singapore, Sudan, Sweden, Tunisia, Turkey, UAE, UK (CWC), USA (MCI),and Yemen.
Work is in progress to sign further mutual agreements with other countries to recognize the 970 telephone code.

Palestine Cellular Phone (Jawwal)

Paltel has been granted a license from the Palestinian National Authority to operate, maintain and manage the telecom sector in Palestine. This license covers both voice and non-voice, fixed and cellular networks.
The company has initiated GSM cellular services by offering the latest GSM technology. GSM technology was chosen by PalTel because it is the most widely accepted, fully digital, wireless technology available in the world today. The Ericsson GSM system exceeds current industry standards and provides unique state-of-the-art services compared to other technologies.
In 1996, Jawwal brand name was chosen for the PalTel GSM service. The Company was established as the first GSM operator in Palestine. The system was fully constructed in six months. Commercial service commenced on August 15, 1999 in Gaza, and October 2, 1999 in the West Bank. Two mobile switching centers (SMC's) have already been constructed, the first in Ramallah City and the second in Gaza City. Both were inaugurated and became operational on June 21, 1999. Currently, the number of installed base stations in the West Bank and Gaza is 102. The initial capacity of this project amounts to about 70,000 numbers, expandable to 120,000. Within the next year there will be 90% indoor and 95% outdoor network coverage. The Palestinian cellular network is equipped with market oriented value-added services which enable Jawwal customers to enjoy a modern, handy and flexible network. Some of these services are roaming, speech security, clear messaging, short messaging services (SMS), voice mail, fax/data service, call transfer and connected line identification numbers. The project provides an appropriate technical and commercial alternative for the Israeli service providers.

Activating Roaming Agreements

Jawwal Company has international agreements concurrent with local ones, since the company is dedicated to offering roaming services in other countries. For example, Jawwal Company has finalized the technical issues related to operating the Palestinian Jawwal with Partner (Orange) operator in Israel, enabling Palestinian subscribers to use their mobile phones inside Israel.
Besides Israel (Orange), further international roaming agreements have been signed with over 80 countries, and are activated in 24, with further activation initiatives underway.
The countries Jawwal has roaming agreements with are:
Switzerland (Swisscom), Jordan (Fast Link), UK (Vodaphone), Turkey (Telsim, Turkel), Greece (Panaphone), Australia (One), Norway (Netcom G.S.M.), Hong Kong (Hutchsson), France (France telecom),Poland (Centertel), Belgium (Belgacom Mobile), Israel (Orange), Germany (E-Plus), Taiwan (Chunghwa Telecom), Sweden (Europolitan), Spain (Telefonica Moviles), Finland (Radiolinja), Czech Republic (Radio Mobile), Russia (Mobile Telesystems), Hong Kong (Cable & Wireless), Morocco (IAM), Egypt (MobiNil), Hong Kong (Sunday), and Italy (Wind).
Jawwal's long term objectives are focused on being number one in the region in terms of regulation, service, quality, customer satisfaction and coverage. Our market research shows that over the next 10 years there will be about 370,000 cellular users in Palestine. However, one of the obstacles to achieving this goal will be the availability of the required frequency spectrum. PalTel has been forced to use additional base stations to meet the coverage and capacity imposed by the Israelis.
