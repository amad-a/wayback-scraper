UNBROKEN  LEGACY

 

I remember my parents in the 1980's talking about how peace would prevail soon and that it was a question of only few years. I remember my teachers at school saying the same thing. I also remember that I was always skeptical. Here we are today 2003 and I still hear the same old saying, "This cannot continue and the peace is coming soon!"

 While photographing in the West Bank and Gaza, images of demolition could be seen in many places. I came across many unemployed people and families who lost their loved ones. There were extreme life conditions, sometimes even lacking the most basic elements of life. I wondered how any human being could even accept to live in such conditions where one was denied his freedom and his life. Long curfews that sometimes lasted for weeks. No schools, work or any external life activities were allowed. Added to that, the forbidden mobility between cities and villages.

I find it hard to live this way as I, as a normal human being, completely resent such restrictions. I resent that somebody dictates my life. But, right here it seems it became the norm of life to live in such environments. Not because people wanted to, but because they were left with no choices. This struggle has been going on for decades. Yet, when I talked to these people, it amazed me how much patient and determined they were. Their endurance and patience to their miserable political psychological and financial situation fascinated me. I saw people who lost their homes and belongings but when I photographed them they stood firm and proud if front of my lens. It is as if they shared a silent demand and message. This made me wonder! Where do they obtain their power from? The answer was simple.  Palestinian family ties are very strong and united. Palestinian culture believes in the family structure. If one member is having trouble, the whole family offers its support and help. They strengthen and console each other. When everything else is gone, the family and its pride that remains. It is a concept of all for one and one for all. This legacy is unbroken and survives from one generation to the next.

Steve Sabella
