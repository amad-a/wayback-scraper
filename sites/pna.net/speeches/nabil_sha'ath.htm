Ladies and Gentlemen both on the table and here in the conference.


Again I salute you with all the friendly and deep rooted belief in me about the European Palestinian and the European Middle Eastern and the European Mediterranean relationships of strategic nature that tie our country with your continent.


I have considered myself a Europeanist for a very long time and this is in no way to diminish my assessment of the importance of the American relation nor certainly the importance of the Arab National Bonds that tie us with our Arab Brothers. But Europe has emerged gradually as our very important partner of troth. And I think that this is based on our needs and also our estimations that we can play an important role in your needs and in your own strategic plans, clearly explained .in your Barcelona approach, of an encompassing relationship of peace, security, political stability, economic prosperity and cultural growth and development. And I think it is this encompassing strategic long term relations that are based on your and our security needs, economic needs, futuristic cultural and educational and technological needs, particularly emerging as a result of the end of the cold war and the end of the Soviet Union as we all knew it and of the emergence of the new visions of a new world order or disorder as many people would like to think of it that needs restructuring. And we feel that you are an island of strength and security for us, a very big island, but in all our calculations, both as Palestine and as part of this region, this calculation of the relationship with Europe counts most particularly in its global strategic long term visions and not only in its tactical needs of a day to day dealing with a very difficult political process with its combatant risks of return of confrontation -and its day to day problems of trade and investments.


It is in that sense that I would also look at Palestine within the regional context. The regional context was a very important one for all those who thought of and dealt with and participated in the peace process. Obviously Palestine and the Palestine problem has a very important primacy, almost centrality, in the regional approach of all countries looking at us as a region in this new world of blocks, I mean the European Block, the American Block, the Pacific Block and the possible emerging new Russian Block. Unfortunately the Arab block has really never been able to act in this new world in a way tl1at would make it a block of the same proportions. But all visions of the peace process, including the expansion of that emphasis of the centrality of the Palestine problem, include an approach to the whole region. And .the question or a region in peace versus a region before peace basically involved Israel. I mean the region as we talked about it before peace with Israel was basically an Arab region. And Palestine participated in that Arab region also as a very central part of the political economic social and cultural aspirations of our region. When that region was expanded, it included on and off some non-- Arab participants such as Iran, Turkey and the Ethiopian South. And then we talked about the Middle East before peace that definitely excluded Israel. And therefore whatever shape we look at, an Arab Natiot1al shape or a Middle Eastern geographical shape, the difference of the pre-peace and the post peace arrangement really meant a role of Israel in that new region m which Palestine is also to play an important role despite of its almost insignificant size in the total economic calculations of that region. Somehow I think Palestine was thought to have leverage far beyond its immediate economic size and immediate political influence. And that leverage emanated partly from the centrality of the political problem of the Palestinian Israeli conflict within the overall Palestinian Arab Israeli
Conflict. But it also has to do with the way Palestinians developed both Particularly in their Diaspora and their expatriate ,status. For a while Palestinians held economic and political posts in most of the emerging independent Arab countries around them, in Jordan, in Lebanon) in the Gulf States in particular and partially in Iraq and Libya. And that presence of the Palestinian entrepreneurial expatriate produced Palestinian interest in trade, investment, even public enterprise development and political developments. Palestinians represented Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Jordan, in the United Nations for quite a while including also Libya at a certain time and Palestinian diplomats were a major force in many of these Arab Countries as they emerged into independence. It was also the influence Palestine had directly over the Arab masses and Arab political opinion and also to some extent the business communities in the Arab world, and that really produced for Palestine a leverage in .the area that was rarely practiced by a country that has not yet emerged into full independence without any of the requisites of sovereignty at a time.

I also tl1ink that Palestine has been endowed with a particularly strong leadership that has been able to create direct personal relations to exert direct political and economic influence far beyond the reaches of Palestine, the like of which we have not seen in many similar emerging countries, but we have seen in people like Mandella, for example, and the leadership of the ANC. I think the particular influence of President Arafat and the Leadership of the PLO has been quite a plus in terms of the
Palestinian leverage in its relationship with the Arab world and also with a much wider area of influence in central Asia, in Latin America, in the -heart of Africa and the South of Africa and in the far East, particularly in the Muslim areas such as Malasia and Indonesia.


So a variety of factors, the political importance of the Palestine issue, the direct involvement of the Palestinian expatriates and the particular political influence of the Palestinian leadership over Arab masses and other populations and the direct effect of that relationship on the relations with the rest of the world, created for Palestine a particular leverage in the  region that we are talking about that really gave Palestine a particular advantage in addressing the problems of that region. Therefore the peace process between Israel and Palestine is really what created the whole interest in the four of five basic approaches to the area as a region
including Israel after the peace process. I mean the multilateral process, particularly REDWG, the US Casablanca process, and I call it that because that's what it is, the Barcelona process and a variety of other processes involved in the region with particular centrality of Palestine but also the particular centrality of Israel.


And I am going to talk about that other leverage because it is rea11y the combinations of these two leverages and the particular emphasis laid on one or the other. that have shaped a lot of the approaches made to the region and therefore the role of Palestine in that region. Because as I have explained about the particular leverage of Palestine in the area, obviously Israel had a very significant leverage on Europe, on the United States in particular as well as many countries far away. of the world's most horrible crimes not only of the century but of many centuries before it and that is the holocaust and. the effect that holocaust created in terms of western approaches to Israel as the inheritor of the population that suffered from that holocaust.
