Palestinian Economy

"An Introduction "

 

One of the strategies form the beginning of the Israeli occupation has been to integrate the Occupied Palestinian Territories (OPT) according to Israel's economic, land and water interests. Thus, the OPT faced a policy which aimed at freezing any economic development, preventing independent economic growth and creating a situation of complete Palestinian dependence on Israel. The OPT became a supplementary market for Israeli goods and services and a source of cheap labor for the Israeli economy. This situation has forced thousands of Palestinians to emigrate and to seek employment in the Arab World. This situation has forced thousands of Palestinians to emigrate and to seek employment in the Arab World or the West (the group most likely to emigrate are non-camp residents, the middle class, mainly Christians and urban dwellers).

 

Popular Economy:

 

Particularly significant during the Intifada was the growth of the popular or informal economy, a wide range of initiatives in small-scale industry, agriculture, crafts and services, linked to popular political and social movements. It included production projects of "grassroots" organization and household economy cooperatives, aiming at strengthening the more marginal elements of the population, to facilitate equitable distribution of resources, including profit-sharing, participation of women, developing human resources, and meeting the development needs of the community.

 

Prospects:

 

Precise parameters of Palestinian economic activity were outlined in an economic agreement signed by Israel and the PLO in Paris on April 29, 1994 . A major role will be played by the donor countries whose input will provide the financial foundation for the reconstruction and development of the Palestinian economy.

 

Closure:

 

Due to the ongoing closure imposed on the OPT by Israel since march 1993, the Palestinian economy is losing $600,000 million a Year (UN estimates, 1995). The closure meant the loss of tens of thousands of jobs that could not be recreated domestically. The return of thousands of Palestinians from exile/diaspora to the homeland has aggravated this situation.
