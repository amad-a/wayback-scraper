A Marketable Market

Nazareth's Old City market, one of Israel's largest traditional bazaars, sprawls over winding lanes. It is comprised of many secondary markets, such as the Vegetable Market (for bridal dresses and other such needs).
Hidden among the shops are public squares, mansions, churches, monasteries, and schools.

For many years, the market's infrastructure was neglected. The drainage and sewage situation was catastrophic, and the area was entangled in a web of electrical and telephone wires. Vendors invaded streets and squares, sometimes completely blocking them with stands built from a hodgepodge of wood, plastic and tin.

The planning of the market's renovation was assigned to architects Eunice Figueiredo and Peter bugod, who worked together with Israel Government Tourist Corporation architect Noga Landwer.

The first stage was to install underground water, sewage, electrical, telephone, and television line. For this purpose, the shops had to be temporarily evacuated, and so the Israel Government Tourist Corporation and the Nazareth Municipality built a large tent beside the city's main street to accommodate them. The Municipality prepared a videotape depicting the changes that were about to take place; other means of persuasion were used as well. The campaign was a success; the vendors of Nazareth moved their shops without objection.
