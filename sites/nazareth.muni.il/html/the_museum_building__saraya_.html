The Museum Building

THE MUSEUM BUILDING (SARAYA):
The Saraya, in which the Nazareth Museum will be located, is a historical building and one of the most important monuments in the city.

   The Saraya Facade and Courtyard
    from Entrance Area

The house was built in the 18th century by Daher El-Omar, the Bedouin ruler who rebelled against the Ottoman sultans of the time, eventually becoming the governor of Galilee. During his rule, Nazareth development from a village to an urban center. He encouraged the Christian population to settle within its borders, and even took one of the local maidens as his wife.

Daher El-Omar manifested the special feelings he had for Nazareth by building his summer villa there and spending one month a year in the residence. The state of security improved greatly in the region during his lifetime, and trade flourished. It was during this period that the Saraya - Government House - was built.

Daher El-Omar turned one of the rooms of the 2-story building into a prayer chamber for Moslems, until such a time in 1812 when the White Mosque was erected next door to The Saraya. The north wing of the building was rebuilt in the middle of the 19th century, at which time a turret was also added. The building erected by Daher El-Omar in the 18th century still stands, and until the 1990s fulfilled its original purpose, serving as Nazareth's Town Hall.

The Saraya will be restored and its original facade conserved. The exhibits will be displayed in the rooms of the original building and the windows will provide the visitor with a scenic view of the city, preserving an architectural link with the nearby market and the urban surroundings of the city.
