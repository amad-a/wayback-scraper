Loosening the Arteries

The Transport Ministry is investing more than NIS 60 million in Nazareth to alleviate the city's overwhelming traffic problems. These stem first and foremost from he narrowness of city's main artery, Pope Paul VI Street. Another contributing factor is the numerous tourist buses that use that street.

  After a through study of the situation, planners decided that Pope Paul VI Street must be turned into a one-way boulevard, with two-way traffic permitted to buses only. Alighting zones will be built the boulevard, and an alternative route has been found for cars traveling in the opposite direction.

  Parking areas for tourist vehicles, however, have yet to be found. A quarter acre of land abutting Pope Paul VI Street is so expensive that its price is sure to discourage any entrepreneur from a building a parking  lot on the site. in any event, it is much more advisable to offer free parking if tourism is to be seriously encouraged.

  Several solutions have been put forward. Most of them put forward. Most of them involve allowing construction of additional stories on existing buildings for shops, residences, or offices, with the condition that underground parking areas are installed.

  The revolution in central Nazareth's transportation system will also include the construction of new roads. A new access road to the center of town is now being built near the Mount of the Precipice, and the main road from the Jezreel Valley will pass through the large quarry on the slopes of the mountain.
