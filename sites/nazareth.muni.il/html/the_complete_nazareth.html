The Complete Nazareth

A real visit to Nazareth is much more than just a tour of the Old City.  One of the basic rules for understanding Nazareth is to get to know its surrounding landscape: a horsehoe-shaped bally whose concave side faces the Jezreel Vallry. At one end of the horseshoe, on the open side, a large cliff steeply drops down to the vally's fields.

Proposal for the creation of a park with natural landscapes on the Nebi Sa'in Ridge. (Planning: Shlomo Arnson)

  Nazareth 2000 is not based on one church, no matter how celebrated it is. Chirstian pilgrims want to visit all of the historic sites in the city and soak up the atmosphere of the site that shaped Jesus' character. They want to walk through the quaint little lanes, visit the markets, and meet the local residents, Proper guiding and portrayal of the city in .

its geographical context are the keys to ensuring satisfies tourists
  The plans for Nazareth 2000 designate a large tourism area that includes green spaces, promenade routes, and lookout points encircling the city, enabling the visitor to tour the city's outskirts and not only its core.

  The planning team identifies ``portals" to Nazareth. These portals are the large intersections at both ends of Pope Paul VI Street. A third entrance junction will be built at the foot of the mount of the Precipice.

  The heart of Nazareth is the Old City, which is comprised of two parts: the nucleus - the historical and religious core - and the ``casing" - the markets, mansions, and houses on the slope of the Nebi Sa'in Ridge. The expansion of the city's  tourism  area will bring the visitors to the top of the ridge and from there to the city and its chrches.

  Most of the work will be centered on creating lookout points atop the ridge and building stepped paths. The planned change will lengthen the minimum stay in Nazareth from two hours to half day.
