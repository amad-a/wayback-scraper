Wide-open Spaces

The old city of Nazareth is comprised of a network of narrow, winding lanes that open onto squares. Along the lanes, and mainly around the squares, stand mansions and various public and religious buildings. But over the years, the beauty of the streets and the squares was obscured by shops that spilled out into the street, with ``modern" additions made of plastic, asbestos, and tin.
Electricity and telephone lines run haphazardly along the walls, and the roofs are cluttered with junk and television antennas.

  The Nazareth 2000 Project proposes a comeback for the area, in which the splendor would be returned to the streets and the charm restored to the squares. Besides the two main squares (the square of the Church of the Annunciation and the square of Mary's Well), the city has scores of little squares, or ``piazzettas," such as the squares beside the Church of St. Gabriel, the Turkish government building, and the Vegetable Market. The upper sketch shows Author's House Square as it looks today and the lower sketch depicts it as it can look after renovation. (Sketches: Arie Rahamimoff, Gene Dyer).

A walk through the city of Nazareth will soon be greatly enhanced by attractive open spaces and lanes that will be blocked off to vehicular traffic.

  The city's new central square is to be built south of the Church of the Annunciation, enabling access to the church's main entrance so that it will no longer be necessary to go in from the side. The square will also serve as a venue for festivals and other outdoor events.

  Its construction requires drastic measures, including the razing of a school building. But the demolition won't take place until the completion of a new school building, which should be ready for use by next year.

  Surrounding the square are a group of nine teeth century buildings, including the Casa Nova Hotel; and old caravansary; and the tomb of Shahab el-Din, a Moslem holy man.

  The area of Mary's Well will also undergo extensive renovations. The flow of water will be restored to the fountain and a square, or piazza, as architect Adeeb Daoud prefers to call it, will be build around it.

  A pedestrain-only walkway will lead from piazza to smaller square (a ``piazzetta"), in front of the Church of St. Gabriel. From there, another pedestrian thoroughfare, lined with restored old houses, will lead through other piazzas to the market and the Church of the Annunciation.
