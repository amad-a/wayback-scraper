The last five short films of the 2nd Millennium

 

Five Palestinian filmmakers were commissioned by the Bethlehem 2000 Project to produce each a short film for December 31, 1999, the last New Years Eve of the millennium. An expression of what is inherently Palestinian, these films bring to life personal experiences and transpose images from the mind into short narratives that reflect the complexity of the Palestinian experience, especially during the last decades.

 

In Out of  Place, each image and each place conjures up spaces that preoccupy the director Azza Al-Hassan; be it heaven or hell or the earth in between, each space evokes the spirits of the past, present and future, and amalgam history, geography and fantasy into a subjective experience that is uniquely intimate and universally appealing.

 

Mai Masris short version of her film Children of Shatilla, which blends elements of the history of the refugee camp of Shatilla and the Nakba (catastrophe of 1948), offers a glimpse into what could potentially be a brighter future for Palestinian refugee children who grow up in camps.

 

Subhi Zbeidi believes that dreams come true for those who wish them and hope is redeeming even in the most abject of situations. Ali and his Friends from the Jalazone refugee camp near Ramallah have a dream and they make it come true. They will take the rest of their dreams one at a time.

 

Makoubeh or Upside Down is the name of a typical Palestinian dish that gives the film its title. Rachid Mashharawi uses the theme of food to explore Palestinian identity, and this disarming humour, his depiction of rural simplicity and the bucolic freshness of this shots help him achieve his objective.

 

Elia Suleiman builds his impressions of Palestine on the religious tradition of the journey of Joshep and Mary before she gives birth to Jesus. Cyber Palestine transposes the story to modern times and uses the metaphor of displacement to ponder over present day realities. Wrought with caustic humour and provocative freshness, Cyber Palestine fine tunes the tribulations of fate while shaking any complacency one might have had about history, whether lived or handed down.
