Bethlehem University Silver Jubilee

A Mosaic of Memories

Brother Vincent Malham, Vice Chancellor

It was a wonderful week of celebration: one of thanksgiving, great joy and pride, one long to be remembered and cherished. The photos and coverage in this special issue will serve to recall some of its special moments. One would have had to be here, however, to truly capture the emotion, the impact, the significance of this milestone in the history of Bethlehem University.

The Silver Jubilee was, among many things, an exciting reunion of many of the pioneers who began this courageous venture in faith, men like Pio Cardinal Laghi, a true Father and Founder; Brother John Johnston, Superior General of the Brothers of the Christian Schools; and Monsignors Robert Fuglister, President of ABU; Herbert Michel, President, Weltmission-Weltkirche; and Robert Stern, Secretary General, CNEWA. It was also a gathering for almost all of the University's past chief administrators, Brothers Joseph Loewenstein, Thomas Scanlan, Anton De Roeper, and Ronald Gallagher, who made the long journey to celebrate with us, as did many others, including Brother Brendan Fitzgerald, one of the first three Brothers to serve here, and former teachers and students living abroad. It brought together as well a wide circle of benefactors, church and civic dignitaries, Trustees and Regents, alumni and friends, noteworthy among whom were Latin Patriarch Michel Sabbah, first President of the University (1981-1988), Apostolic Delegate Archbishop Pietro Sambi, new Chancellor of the University, and Mayor Hanna Nasser, Chairman of the Board of Trustees.

Recalling a twenty-five-year history of dramatic struggle and courageous achievement, the various ceremonies throughout the week gave tribute to many significant persons, both living and deceased. But this Jubilee was not only a remembrance and celebration of the past. It will also be, it is hoped, a sign of encouragement to our students, and to the entire Palestinian community, that Bethlehem University renews its commitment to providing quality education for Palestine's best and brightest. We have been here twenty-five years, but we are just beginning. With God's continued blessing, and with the generous support of our friends and benefactors around the world, this University will continue to serve the young people of this land.

 
Happy Birthday,
Alma Mater

 

Coming Home to Bethlehem

They came from far away places, returning to Bethlehem for Bethlehem University's Silver Jubilee celebrations, a week of events beginning October 4th and ending October 10th. There were founders, former administrators, teachers and students, many benefactors and dignitaries, in addition to the University community of faculty,

staff and students. The celebrations included concerts, campus tours, an all-day picnic, sports events, a photo exhibit and science fair, singing and dancing, and a special time set apart to commemorate deceased faculty, staff and students.

 

 

 

 

The Jubilee opened with a Mass of Thanksgiving. Presiding was His Eminence Pio Cardinal Laghi [in photo left] from Rome, the First Chancellor of Bethlehem University (1973) and the recipient of Bethlehem University's new De La Salle Award . In his homily the Cardinal remarked that the founding of Bethlehem University "was an act of faith," that had "the power to … transform history" and he praised the courage of the three original Christian Brothers who journeyed to the Holy Land to implement the project—Brothers Joseph Neary, Anthony O'Connor and Brendan Fitzgerald. "Theirs was a faith that moved the mountain," he said.

De La Salle Award

His Eminence Pio Cardinal Laghi, the Prefect of the Congregation of Catholic Education is pictured here on the right, accepting Bethlehem University's De La Salle Award from the University's Vice Chancellor Br. Vincent Malham [center] and Christian Brother Superior General Br. John Johnston [left]. Cardinal Laghi was the Apostolic Delegate in Jerusalem at the time of the founding of Bethlehem University in 1973. This new award, created in honor of St. John Baptist de La Salle, the Founder of the Brothers of the Christian Schools, is bestowed on "a person who has distinguished himself/herself in the field of education, or to a person who has contributed in an outstanding way to the good of the University or the community." Accepting the award, Cardinal Laghi made these comments: "The instructions Pope Paul VI gave me were to see to it to commit yourself and the efforts of the church to stopping the exodus of young Christians from the Holy Land. My satisfaction is obvious, at seeing the 25th Jubilee of the university. It is my prayer that Bethlehem University continue to prosper.

 

One of the principal events of the week was the Academic Convocation on October 5th, an impressive international gathering of the Bethlehem University family--past, present and future.

Honorary Doctorate

Bethlehem University presented the Honorary Degree of Doctor of Pedagogy to Brother John Johnston, FSC, the Superior General of the De La Salle Christian Brothers. This is the first Honorary Doctorate ever given by the University and the sixth conferred upon Br. Johnston who has five previous honorary degrees from other Lasallian institutions. The citation of Honorary Doctorate of Pedagogy was read by the Dean of the Faculty of Education, Dr. Jacqueline Sfeir, acknowledging Br. Johnston's forty-eight years of service to the De La Salle community, the past twelve as its Superior General, and his career in education as teacher and administrator. In his remarks of acceptance, Br. Johnston said, "Twenty-two years ago God in his loving Providence called me to accompany in a particular way Bethlehem University. From miles away, at our headquarters in Rome, I have tried to contribute to its development. I am intensely proud of Bethlehem University and all those who throughout its twenty-five-year history have made it a reality. I thank you for recognizing my love for this institution and my efforts on its behalf."

The University Presents the Special Service Award to Five Special Individuals

 

Monsignor Robert Fuglister, AFSC

Monsignor Robert Fuglister is the president of the Bethlehem University Association in Switzerland. He has expressed his uncommon devotion to Bethlehem University over many years through his administrative and development activities and efforts on behalf of the University in the Vatican and the Swiss government and through many outstanding contributions. At the presentation ceremony in the University Chapel of the Holy Child, Monsignor Fuglister stated, "We need this institution. We have a great mission."

 

 

 

Monsignor Herbert Michel, AFSC

Monsignor Herbert Michel, currently serving as Chairman of the Finance Committee of the International Board of Regents, was present in the earliest discussions leading to the choice of the present campus. His encouragement was essential in making the decision to establish a university granting baccalaureate degrees rather than a community junior college. To Monsignor Michel we also owe the name of Bethlehem University. He worked assiduously to bring direct aid from the Archdiocese of Koln in Germany for development projects and academic programs. He said he felt his biggest contribution was the naming of Bethlehem University.

 

 

 

Monsignor Robert L. Stern, AFSC

Monsignor Robert L. Stern, Director General of Catholic Near East Welfare Association (CNEWA), has been one of Bethlehem University's most astute, loyal and influential supporters. He helped to establish the International Board of Regents in 1995 and now serves as its Chairman. He helped to regularized the legal status of the University and was the principal author of the Statutes. "The credit really goes to my predecessor, Bishop John Nolan," he responded. "Sixteen years ago I came along with him to Bethlehem at Christmas time and he introduced me to the university. I am just continuing his work."

 

Brother Joseph Loewenstein, FSC

Brother Joe, as he is lovingly known, served Bethlehem University for 17 years, from 1975 to 1992 in various capacities, including Vice Chancellor. His many accomplishments include opening a dialogue with the Union of Arab Universities and implementing building projects to modernize the campus. But his greatest gift, according to his closest friends, is his personality and charm and his winning smile, which formed many lasting friendships for him and the university.

 

 

 

Brother Jean Manuel, FSC

Brother Jean is the one who was present before the University was founded, who conceived the idea of an institution of higher learning in Bethlehem, helped to make it a reality and continues to serve after twenty-five years. Brother Jean, a Palestinian, was director of the Freres Schools in Jerusalem and Bethlehem. He worked alongside then-Monsignor Pio Laghi, who was Apostolic Delegate in Jerusalem, to found a university in Bethlehem, doing all the necessary details, finding an academic and secretarial staff, and arranging for those Brothers who came to administer the University

Three Faculty Members Receive Prestigious Fulbright Award

Professors Vivian Khamis, Mo'en Kan'an and George Rishmawi, all faculty members of Bethlehem University, are recipients of Fulbright research scholarships for the current academic year. This is an outstanding achievement for each recipient and an unusual occurrence for one institution to have several Fulbright scholars in the same year. Dr. Khamis, currently doing her research at Harvard University, is investigating the best techniques of conflict resolution throughout the world to adapt to the regions of the Middle East, particularly Palestine. Dr. Mo'en Kan'an will implement his research on The Molecular Basis of Deafness in the Palestinian Non-Syndromic Deaf Population at the Medical School of the University of Washington in Seattle. Dr. George Rishmawi will investigate the influences of the Muslim East on Western literature, particularly on American literature at the University of California, Riverside.

Five-Year Strategic Plan Enters Phase II: Implementation

On June 22nd last year a general assembly was held to unveil Phase I of the Bethlehem University "Five Year Strategic Development Plan - The Vision". Work on the plan started in October 1997 with the appointment of a team from the University Community, and two strategic analysts, Monsignor Conor Ward and Dr. Colette Dowling, who are funded by Irish Aid. Phase II: Implementation, the most challenging phase, started immediately following the completion of the October Silver Jubilee celebrations and included an Assembly of the University Community held on November 5th to advance the process of implementation. Vice Chancellor Brother Vincent Malham and Dr. Colette Dowling made presentations, then the assembly broke into groups for discussions on the Vision Plan. Eighty-two proposed initiatives were listed under twelve major themes in the Vision Phase Report. When all the members of the University were consulted about the possibilities for immediate implementation, the proposed initiatives which were most frequently chosen were:
  • Development of a University Institute for Community Partnership and Research.
  • On-going review and updating of undergraduate education to ensure an undergraduate curriculum based on practical needs of students and Palestinian society as a whole. Openness to new fields of study, and procedures for rapid implementation.
  • Consideration of the development of postgraduate programs to meet current demand and projected needs, avoiding unnecessary duplication with those offered in other Palestinian universities.
  • The establishment of an Office of Human Resource Development responsible for devising a Structured Human Resource Development Scheme as a matter of urgency.

Major Grants Provide Funds for New Construction

The United States Agency for International Development, under the auspices of its Office of American Schools and Hospitals Abroad, has granted Bethlehem University, through the Christian Brothers Conference in the U.S., a sum of $503,574 to support the construction of a new classroom/office building. This grant, together with the grant of $250,000 received from the Oil Producing Exporting Countries (OPEC), will enable the University to start construction next summer. This will be the first new construction since the Social and Cultural Center was completed in 1990.

 

 

 

First Individual Endowed Scholarship Received

Breen Bland, MD, AFSC, and his wife Betty Moore Bland, pictured here with their family, are the first individuals to give a $25,000 endowed scholarship to Bethlehem University. Married for fifty-four years, with eight children, fourteen grandchildren and one great grandchild, they recently celebrated this landmark anniversary with a trip to Rome and the Holy Land where, as members of the Equestrian Order of the Holy Sepulcher, they received their Pilgrim Shells from His Beatitude Michel Sabbah, Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem. It was during this visit to Bethlehem that they presented the scholarship to Brother Vincent Malham, FSC, Vice Chancellor of the University.
The Blands, from Millington, Tennessee, have a long history of association with the Christian Brothers. Dr. Bland and his father, brothers, uncles, cousins, his five sons and now two grandsons have all attended or are now attending Christian Brothers Schools. He is an affiliated member of the Institute of the Brothers of the Christian Schools. Both Blands, Breen and Betty, have lived a life of service to others through volunteer work with countless civic, community and religious groups, as well as serving on many boards. Doctor Bland retired in 1997 after practicing medicine for fifty-five years in service to "God's little ones," as he affectionately referred to his patients, young and old, rich and poor. Though both are retired from their active professions, they have never retired, nor will they ever retire, from a lifetime of selfless, loving, living, giving and caring. This generous gift to the University is a shining reflection of the lifetime of Doctor Breen and Betty Bland to which we all aspire and for which we are all most grateful.

First Annual Fundraising Dinner

An annual fundraising dinner was inaugurated in this Silver Jubilee year with proceeds going to the student scholarship fund and tuition aid for needy students. The event is an opportunity for the local community to support University development programs. In October, a Silver Jubilee Annual Appeal was mailed to individuals as a first step in expanding the University's individual giving program on an international scale. The dinner is the first special event locally and will foster local participation in the overall development plan. The dinner was held November 7th in Bethlehem's Nissan Restaurant, attracting 340 guests, including the Mayor of Bethlehem, Hanna Nasser, who is also the Chairman of the Bethlehem University Board of Trustees, and Daoud Al-Zeer, a member of the Palestinian Legislative Council. There was a raffle drawing for donated prizes: a new washing machine donated by Fuad Kattan of the Transjordan Engineering Company, two diamond pieces donated by the Lama Brothers and a gold ring donated by the Saca Brothers, all from Bethlehem. Unexpected special donations received were 10,000 shekels worth of printing services from Issa Habesch and one student scholarship donated by Mr. George Nasser. The Bethlehem University development office is grateful to all volunteers, donors and guests for making our first annual fundraising dinner a successful event
