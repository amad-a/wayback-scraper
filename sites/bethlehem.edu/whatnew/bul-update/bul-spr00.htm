FROM THE LIBRARY DIRECTOR

Change is the word that is most descriptive of the year's activities of the Bethlehem University Library. We welcomed Mr. George Awwad for the position of Reference Librarian and Assistant in Arabic Technical Services. He joined the library staff with a professional background and many years of library experience. 
The University Archives has been relocated to the third floor of the library. The Archives is now included in the structure and organization of the library. Sister Agatha Diaz has joined the staff as Archivist.
Ireland Aid is funding the construction of the new addition to the library building. The new addition, named Turathuna: Center for Palestinian Heritage at Bethlehem University, will open in summer 2000. The special collection - Palestiniana will be relocated in the new center. A special committee has been working to prepare for the opening.
Ms. Rebecca Calaor has departed for a one year study leave to pursue a Master's degree at the University of San Carlos in Cebu City, Philippines. We welcomed Mr. Wasim Zoughbi, Bethlehem University graduate 1998, to replace her in Circulation and Periodicals.
Change has taken place in many other ways also. Hundreds of new books have been purchased and contributed. The new books display is constantly changing. Members of the staff have been concerned with improving themselves. Details of these changes and many others are in the pages that follow. All these changes are important in order to provide better quality service to the library users.

Br. Dominic Smith, FSC


  STUDENTS ASSISTANTS

The student assistants comprise an essential component of the regular library staff. Every semester about thirty students are selected to work in the library within the university work/study grant program. This program entitles a student to a reduction in the tuition fees for an input of six hours of work per week throughout a semester. Students are selected on the basis of financial need, good academic performance (a GPA of not less than 2.50), and willingness to work. First year students in their first semester are not eligible to apply.
The library offers a scholarship each semester to the two best student assistants. They receive an additional reduction of tuition fees.

LIBRARY INSTRUCTION

Many books have been received by the library through generous donations from organizations and individuals. Significant contributions are noteworthy additions to the library collection.
The gift of the Everyman Millennium Library has been made possible by a Millennium Commission grant, Everyman's Library, generous donations from various British charities, and with the help of the British Council. The complete library comprises 250 volumes of the world's greatest classic books written in or translated into English.

AUDIO VISUAL CENTER

In the Audio Visual Center students and teachers can access LINK, an information service created by Springer Publisher for over 400 scientific journals, on-line with full text. In addition, free access to 25 journal titles subscribed through EBSCO is available on-line. Several journals are available by direct access through the publishers.
Meetings of the staff with Br. Carl Clayton, Director of the Office of Teaching Development have discussed the promotion of multimedia technology as a tool of instruction in classrooms. One of the conclusions of a survey among teachers is an interest in the need to offer training sessions to use various technologies in instruction.
A new multimedia presentation using Power Point has been prepared to assist in the orientation of first year students to the proper use of the library. A second version is used in training the new student assistants for their duties in the library.
New computer equipment has improved the services that can be offered. Recent additions include: HP3200 scanner, HP8200 CD-writer, HP695C color ink jet printer, HP2100 laser printer, PII350 MHz computer, and PIII 500 MHz computer.
