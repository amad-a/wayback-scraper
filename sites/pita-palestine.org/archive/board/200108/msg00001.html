On 31 Jul 2001,, AbdelRahman Omar wrote:

> Sir,
> 
> As far as I am concerned, this Draft Working Paper was only a couple of
> months in the making. And it is in Draft form as a courtesy to the other

I believe as far as those who have been working on this issue for a while now, 
history did not start with PALTEL's draft paper. Nevertheless.

> parties to provide their comments and inputs prior to finalizing. It takes
> two to tango. PalTel can easily issue a Finalized Policy unilaterally. But I

In telecom, as you well know, it takes more than two to tango to build a 
sector that secures the interest of the consumer and provides for the 
sustainable development of the sector.  If two suppliers tango and the 
consumer and/or sector pays the price, I for one, would not see that as 
progress.

> personally would not consider this a Partnership and/or a cooperative effort
> between PalTel and the ISP's, which is the objective of this exercise
> 
> It was only fair for it to take the couple of months as it took some time to
> understand the existing situation from all angles, strengthen certain areas such
> as the Data Backbone, Initiate, negotiate and finalize Interconnect Agreements
> with various Telco's to increase and enhance PalTel's Internet Infrastructure
> capacity etc etc. In short, it was prudent to do our homework to ensure PalTel
> can deliver what is stated in the Paper.

While PALTEL avoids any real competition (a natural reaction given its clear 
monopoly position), are we to believe that the sector players are always to 
wait for PALTEL to catch up, technically and commercially, before action 
can be taken on any issue?

> It was equally prudent to ensure and position PalTel such that if the ISP's deem
> it not in their interest to work with and Partner with PalTel, in this very
> important economic and social sector, that PalTel will have the degrees of
> freedom, the capability and resources to go it alone.

Same comment as above.  Please explain "degrees of freedom".

> In short. What is on the table now is an offer from PalTel to the ISP
> community that PalTel will not compete in the ISP business, other than
> providing the required infrastructure at reasonable cost for the ISP's to
> expand and be profitable, in lieu that the ISP's use and only use PalTel
> infrastructure, as well as not to compete in PalTel's core business which is
> Telecommunications including IP Telephony.

I agree fully, expect for the hollow call for users to not use any and all 
available end-user technologies readily available.  Otherwise, we would need 
to ask all Palestinians to refrain from using services like MSN, etc - not too 
practical.  On the other hand, if you speak of IP switching services with the 
goal of re-selling service as an alternative to Paltel services, then I fully agree.

> Very simple and very fair.

True, if Paltel was not already heavily invested in a specific (or more) ISP's 
with no real regulation in place to provide for a level playing field.
 
> I am asking now for the ISP's, as an entity because PalTel will not deal
> with each and everyone separately, to get their act together, meet with us

Hummm, I do not understand the collective action requirement or PALTEL's 
right thereof.

> as a unified Group, and discuss and finalize this blueprint. And the sooner the
> better so as to get this issue behind us and move on.

Those that have been engaged in this issue for years could not agree more 
to want to end this saga, and others for that matter too.
 
> I hope I am addressing the right people. If not, please advise me who to
> address.
> 
> Thanks.
> 
> ARO
> 
> P.S. Instead of dissecting every word and sentence, sometimes out of
> context, it would more productive and constructive to look at the big
> picture and address the issues at hand. This kind of analysis and dialogue
> is simply a waste of time and effort.

Ahh my friend, as you know, "the death is in the details".  Do you not read 
every word and sentence of your interconnect agreements?  This is just as 
important to the ISP's.

If we are to look at the bigger picture, then the issue cannot be said to have 
a 3-month history and we cannot separate the ISP issue from the overall 
telecom sector development or history.  Also, we cannot continue to ignore 
the lack of regulation or missing consumer protection mechanisms.

Again, I agree with your first msg that we meet face to face as interested 
sector parties and first, personally get to know one another, and second, 
agree to which parties have the right to act and in which frameworks.

Salaam,
Sam
